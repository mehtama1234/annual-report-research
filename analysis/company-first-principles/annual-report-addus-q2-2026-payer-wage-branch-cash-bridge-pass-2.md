# Addus Q2 2026 payer, wage, branch, and cash bridge pass 2

## Purpose

This pass advances Addus HomeCare from source-visible home-care evidence to a
company-specific valuation and liquidity object. It tests whether service
revenue can become branch-level cash after caregiver labor, payer rates,
receivables, acquisition cash, technology, debt, and other senior claims.

It does not prove care quality, caregiver adequacy, normalized collections, or
acquisition returns. The correct object is `payer- and labor-constrained
branch cash`, not revenue, adjusted EBITDA, or mechanical OCF-less-capex.

## Filing and period

- Company: Addus HomeCare Corporation (`ADUS`)
- Filing: Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, period ended June 30, 2026
- Primary source: [Addus Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1468328/000143774926025678/adus20260630_10q.htm)

## Operating and cash path

The filing supports this path:

`authorized hours -> caregiver recruitment and scheduled service -> documented
personal-care delivery -> state/MCO billing -> receivable collection -> branch
and corporate costs -> acquisition/technology/debt claims -> common-owner
residual`

Personal-care revenue is based on authorized hours multiplied by contractual or
legislated hourly rates. That makes the key denominator caregiver hours and
wage-adjusted service capacity, not office count alone. Addus also identifies a
tight labor market and caregiver shortages as risks to serving demand.

The H1 operating-cash bridge is:

`net income $52.676M + SBC $9.390M + credit-loss provision $0.759M + AR
$5.834M - prepaid/other $(3.397)M + government advances $0.684M + AP $0.649M
 + accrued payroll $10.893M + accrued expenses $5.782M = OCF $92.376M`

This is a reproducible filing bridge, but Addus states that the increase in
cash was primarily affected by the timing of AR receipts and payroll/AP
payments. Accrued payroll and accrued expenses therefore cannot be treated as
permanent cash conversion without a later-period unwind test.

## Residual and valuation screen

The simple pre-owner screen is:

`$92.376M OCF - $12.182M acquisitions - $3.050M PP&E = $77.144M`

The `$77.144M` figure is only a diagnostic before financing claims and does
not equal owner cash. The filing separately reports `$60.000M` of debt
principal repayment, `$8.257M` of operating-lease cash, `$3.218M` of interest,
and `$5.288M` of income taxes. Lease, interest, and tax payments are already
reflected in operating cash-flow accounting where applicable; they must not be
subtracted again without a defined perimeter. The correct next object is a
same-entity, same-period branch/service collection and required-reinvestment
schedule.

For valuation, test:

- billable hours and caregiver fill rate by service line and state;
- reimbursement rate changes against direct wages, payroll taxes, benefits,
  travel, compliance, and branch overhead;
- personal-care versus hospice/home-health mix and its margin effect;
- receivable days and payer-specific cash collection;
- HomeCourt purchase price, acquired revenue, integration cost, and retention;
- technology and branch maintenance versus growth investment;
- debt repayment, leases, taxes, and diluted common residual.

## QoE and thesis-breaker controls

1. Do not treat the H1 OCF increase as normalized until AR, accrued payroll,
   and accrued-expense timing is tested in the next period.
2. Do not use the Illinois `$30.80` reimbursement rate as a companywide
   reimbursement rate or subtract the `$18.75` wage from it as if the result
   were branch profit. Other labor, travel, benefits, compliance, and overhead
   remain.
3. Keep personal-care, hospice, and home-health margins separate; blended
   gross margin can conceal a service-mix shift.
4. Keep the `$12.182M` HomeCourt acquisition cash and `$3.050M` PP&E/technology
   cash visible; do not treat acquisition-driven revenue or branch count as a
   return until acquired cash contribution is evidenced.
5. Treat the `$60.000M` debt repayment as a financing allocation, not as a
   recurring operating cost and not as proof that all remaining cash was
   distributable.
