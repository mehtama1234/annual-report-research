# Annual Report Cigna Q2 2026 Unpaid-Claims and Cash Bridge Pass 2

## Purpose

This pass tests whether Cigna's Q2 2026 Form 10-Q can move the benefits and
pharmacy-routing case beyond medical-cost ratio and point-in-time payable
figures into a same-period claims-incurred, claims-paid, recoverable, and cash
funding bridge.

## Source and period

- Company: The Cigna Group (`CI`)
- Filing: Q2 2026 Form 10-Q
- Period: six months ended June 30, 2026
- Primary source: [SEC Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1739940/000173994026000065/ci-20260630.htm), Note 7 and Statements of Cash Flows

The filing says the liability includes incurred-but-not-reported claims,
expected development on reported claims, claims reported but not yet paid, and
other medical-care expenses payable. The IBNR plus expected development and
reported claims in process totaled `$5.0B` at June 30, 2026. Favorable prior-year
development of `$268M` was attributed primarily to lower-than-expected medical
service utilization.

## Cash and pharmacy/service funding perimeter

The same filing reports:

- H1 operating cash flow of `$710M`;
- a `$(4.108B)` operating-cash impact from pharmacy and other service-costs
  payable;
- `$26.225B` of pharmacy and other service costs payable at June 30;
- `$1.5B` of accounts receivable sold through receivable facilities during H1;
- `$0.9B` of sold receivables still uncollected at June 30; and
- `$0.3B` of collections not yet remitted to the financial institution.

These are different objects. The unpaid-claims schedule is a Cigna Healthcare
medical-liability bridge. Pharmacy/service costs payable and receivable sales
belong to the broader Cigna/Evernorth cash perimeter. They cannot be pooled as
one medical margin or treated as unrestricted common-owner cash.

## What this proves

Cigna now has a same-entity, same-period claims bridge showing `$15.825B` of
incurred costs, `$14.834B` of paid costs, `$143M` of ending recoverables, and a
`$5.228B` ending unpaid-claims liability. This is stronger evidence than the
prior point-in-time `$5.09B` net medical-cost-payable observation.

The filing also exposes a concrete cash-quality control: H1 operating cash was
small relative to the scale of pharmacy/service flows, and the company used
receivable-sale facilities while some sold receivables remained uncollected or
had been collected but not remitted.

## What remains unproven

- The claims roll-forward is Cigna Healthcare-specific and does not allocate
  cash to each customer cohort, payer, product, or legal entity.
- The `$268M` favorable development is not recurring margin or proof of reserve
  adequacy.
- Pharmacy/service costs, receivable factoring, premiums, risk adjustment,
  rebates, and claims payments are not joined into one after-tax owner-cash
  schedule.
- The filing does not prove that sold receivables represent durable operating
  cash rather than timing support.
- The bridge does not establish affordability, access quality, PBM
  transparency, or patient outcomes.

## Decision

`cigna-unpaid-claims-rollforward-qualified; pharmacy-and-receivables-cash-bridge-open`

Cigna's claims liability and paid-cost roll-forward may be promoted as a
qualified payer-cash diagnostic. The healthcare cohort remains unranked, and
normalized common-owner cash remains unproven.
