# Annual Report DaVita Q2 2026 Treatment-to-Cash Bridge Pass 2

## Purpose

This pass tests whether DaVita's Q2 2026 Form 10-Q can connect recurring
dialysis treatment activity to reimbursement, operating cost, maintenance and
development capital, noncontrolling interests, debt funding, and a disclosed
cash denominator.

## Source and period

- Company: DaVita Inc. (`DVA`)
- Filing: Q2 2026 Form 10-Q
- Period: six months ended June 30, 2026, with Q2 operating inputs where noted
- Primary source: [SEC Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000108/dva-20260630.htm)

## Treatment and reimbursement bridge

| Object | Current filing value | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| H1 U.S. dialysis treatments | `14.256M` | Recurring care-delivery activity |
| H1 average treatments per day | `92,153` | Normalized treatment-density input |
| H1 U.S. dialysis revenue | `$5.954B` | Care-site revenue perimeter |
| H1 average patient-service revenue per treatment | `$416.71` | Reimbursement/revenue-per-treatment input, up 3.6% year over year |
| Q2 patient-care costs per treatment | `$277.40` | Direct care-cost denominator for the quarter |
| Q2 dialysis treatments | `7.2266M` | Quarter-level operating volume |
| H1 operating cash flow | `$811M` | Consolidated cash-flow starting point |

Management attributes the H1 increase in revenue per treatment primarily to
Medicare base-rate and other annual rate increases, partly offset by payer-mix
changes. The Q2 filing also reports Q2 patient-care costs of `$2.005B` and
patient-care cost per treatment of `$277.40`; those are operating inputs, not a
complete treatment-level margin after all labor, facility, corporate, tax,
debt, and capital claims.

## FCF reconciliation and senior claims

DaVita's H1 disclosed free-cash-flow reconciliation is:

`$811M OCF - $150M NCI distributions + $4M NCI contributions - $197M maintenance capital - $75M development capital + $2M self-developed-property proceeds = $396M FCF`.

The filing defines maintenance capital as investments that maintain productive
capacity, including information technology, dialysis-center renovations, and
asset replacements. Development capital includes new centers, expansions,
relocations, and new or expanded contracted hospital operations.

The same filing reports:

- `$150M` H1 distributions to noncontrolling interests;
- `$4M` H1 contributions from noncontrolling interests;
- `$1.561B` of noncontrolling interests subject to put provisions at June 30;
- a `$500M` incremental Term Loan B-2 tranche and `$65M` of revolver draws;
- approximately `$25M` of Term Loan A-2 and `$11M` of Term Loan B-2 principal
  payments; and
- 5.2 million shares repurchased during H1.

The `$396M` FCF figure is therefore a useful disclosed denominator, but it is
not automatically common-owner cash. It is after stated capital and NCI
distributions, but before a complete debt-service, tax, put-settlement,
replacement-risk, and diluted-share residual analysis.

## What this proves

DaVita now has a period-matched care-site bridge from H1 treatments and
revenue-per-treatment to patient-care cost, operating cash, maintenance and
development capital, NCI distributions/contributions, and disclosed FCF. The
filing also makes payer-mix and rate effects visible, rather than leaving
treatment volume as the only operating input.

## What remains unproven

- The filing does not provide collected cash by payer, treatment cohort, or
  legal entity.
- Revenue per treatment is not reimbursement margin after all labor, supplies,
  center overhead, bad debt, and risk-contract claims.
- The `$396M` FCF denominator does not settle debt interest/principal, taxes,
  NCI put obligations, leases, dilution, or common-owner availability.
- Risk-based kidney-care economics and annualized medical spend remain a
  separate risk-contract object from dialysis treatment cash.
- Treatment volume and revenue per treatment do not prove clinical outcomes or
  patient affordability.

## Decision

`davita-treatment-to-fcf-bridge-qualified; reimbursement-and-common-owner-waterfall-open`

DaVita's treatment-to-FCF bridge is now a qualified care-site cash diagnostic.
It remains unranked, and common-owner cash is not promoted until the remaining
reimbursement, debt, NCI, tax, and legal-entity joins are evidenced.
