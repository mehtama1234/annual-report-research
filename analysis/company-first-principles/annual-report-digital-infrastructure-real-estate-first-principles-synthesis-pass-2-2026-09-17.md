# Digital infrastructure real estate: first-principles synthesis — pass 2

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This chapter formalizes the digital-infrastructure lane across Equinix and
Digital Realty. It treats powered capacity, interconnection, and lease
conversion as distinct control points and does not treat AFFO, FFO, backlog, or
parent-level operating cash as completed common-owner cash.

## Force and control point

Cloud, AI, enterprise connectivity, and power scarcity increase the value of
locations that combine available electricity, network adjacency, construction
capacity, and a credible path to customer acceptance. The testable question is
not whether data-center demand is growing; it is whether each company converts
scarce powered capacity into collected rent and durable common-owner cash after
construction, power, leasing, recurring capital, debt, partner, and dilution
claims.

Equinix's control point is interconnection density and network adjacency.
Digital Realty's is powered land and hyperscale capacity converted into
long-term leases. Customers fund rent and connectivity; utilities,
construction suppliers, lenders, partners, and common owners carry different
parts of the capital and operating burden.

## End-to-end economic chain

```text
cloud/AI/connectivity demand
  -> land, power, interconnection, and construction
  -> signed lease or booking
  -> energization, acceptance, and commencement
  -> rent/NOI and operating cash
  -> recurring capex, leasing commissions, power, debt, JV/fund claims
  -> preferred/OP-unit/equity/SBC dilution
  -> common-owner cash per diluted share
```

The denominator must keep recurring operations, growth development, real-estate
acquisitions, partner funding, project debt, and parent cash separate. A signed
lease is a visibility input until the capacity is energized, accepted, and
collecting rent.

## Same-period evidence surface

| Object | Equinix H1 2026 | Digital Realty H1 2026 | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Operating cash flow | `$1.784B` | `$1.595B` | Both have operating cash, but neither line funds all growth claims |
| Property/development capex | `$2.834B` PP&E purchases | `$1.627B` cash-basis capex, including `$1.477B` development | Growth and maintenance definitions are not interchangeable |
| Recurring-capital surface | FY guidance midpoint about `$300M` | `$136M` reported H1 recurring capex | Company definitions require reconciliation |
| Acquisition/investment | `$224M` real-estate acquisitions; xScale equity-method exposure | Approximately `$2.170B` acquisition/asset-investment activity in the current comparison | Acquisition and JV capital can overwhelm the recurring screen |
| Funding claims | `$19.873B` senior notes; `$6.1B` unaccrued capex commitments | `$18.768B` debt; about `$2.0B` pro-rata unconsolidated secured debt; about `$2.5B` ATM proceeds YTD | Parent leverage and partner funding do not describe the same perimeter |
| Cash-after-visible-growth screen | Approximately `$(1.274B)` after H1 OCF, PP&E, and acquisitions | Approximately `$(2.202B)` after H1 OCF, capex, and acquisition activity | Diagnostic screens, not normalized owner cash |

The shared conclusion is that the recurring surface and growth-capital surface
must be modeled separately. Equinix's H1 OCF less recurring-capex screen is
approximately `$1.484B`, while the full PP&E/acquisition screen is negative.
Digital Realty's corresponding recurring-capex screen is approximately
`$1.459B`, while development and acquisition activity drives the full visible
screen negative. These differences describe capital timing and perimeter; they
do not establish superior economics.

## Quality of earnings and financial-shenanigans controls

These are reconciliation prompts, not misconduct findings:

1. Separate GAAP income, FFO/Core FFO/AFFO, operating cash, recurring capex,
   development capex, acquisitions, and leasing commissions.
2. Reconcile signed bookings and backlog to project identifier, energization,
   customer acceptance, commencement, incentives, rent, and collection.
3. Test recurring-capex definitions against physical equipment refresh,
   power, maintenance, and leasing requirements.
4. Allocate JV, fund, VIE, partner, guarantee, preferred, OP-unit, and debt
   claims before treating partner capital as an owner benefit.
5. Test receivables, straight-line rent, lease-up, construction-in-progress,
   capitalized interest, impairments, and acquisition accounting for timing and
   denominator drift.
6. Include ATM issuance, SBC, OP units, preferred claims, and diluted shares
   in the per-share cash bridge.

## Valuation and liquidity handoff

The Damodaran-style valuation object is stabilized common-owner cash from
energized capacity, after total project capital, power and operating costs,
recurring capex, leasing commissions, project debt, partner economics, and
dilution. AFFO/FFO is a useful starting reconciliation measure, not the final
owner-cash endpoint.

The Lyn Alden-style stress path is simultaneous: higher rates raise refinancing
and project-capital costs; power scarcity and construction inflation delay or
reprice capacity; hyperscaler timing can delay commencement; and constrained
capital markets can increase ATM or partner dependence. Backlog can remain
signed while cash conversion and per-share economics deteriorate.

## Promotion ladder and breaker

Current status:

`qualified-control-point; current-period-diagnostic; project-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires project-level total capital, partner/debt funding,
energization and commencement, stabilized NOI/utilization, power and leasing
costs, recurring-capital support, legal-entity claims, and diluted common-owner
cash. The decisive breaker is rising AFFO/FFO or backlog while OCF-to-capex,
commencement, stabilized yield, or per-share cash after financing and equity
issuance weakens.

The next joinable object is a named project or portfolio schedule—not another
backlog headline or company-defined AFFO screen.
