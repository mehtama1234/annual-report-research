This pass reviews the long-form annual-report draft and chooses the best first chapter to polish.

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/annual-report-draft-review-chapter-selection-pass-1.csv`

The question is:

`Which broader annual-report theme should become the first publication-ready chapter, and what does it need before polishing?`

## Short Answer

The first publication-ready chapter should be:

`Power scarcity and grid reliability`

Reason:

It is the cleanest bridge between a big public theme and hard annual-report evidence.

The reader hook is obvious:

`Electricity is no longer background infrastructure; it is becoming a constraint on AI, factories, households, industrial growth, and state competition.`

The evidence is also stronger than most broad themes because it has company metrics and document routes:

- GW and generation fleet data
- PPAs and customer-backed project structures
- data-center ESAs
- rate-base and capital-plan evidence
- tariff/rider/regulatory recovery evidence
- project approval and in-service status routes
- FPL/AEP/Duke as proof paths into recovery and customer-payment mechanisms

Capital allocation/private markets should be the second chapter or a proof-heavy evidence box, not the first chapter, because it is more complex and easier to overclaim.

## Why Power Should Lead

Power has the best combination of:

1. simple hook
2. broad relevance
3. concrete company examples
4. annual-report evidence
5. regulatory proof route
6. public-policy tension
7. direct tie to AI and infrastructure
8. cash-proof discipline through customer contracts and recovery filings

The chapter can open with:

`A data center cannot run on a press release. It needs power that is generated, delivered, contracted, financed, regulated, and recovered.`

That sentence explains the whole mechanism.

## Power Chapter Evidence Stack

| Evidence Layer | What To Use | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Demand pressure | Data-center load, industrial growth, electrification, air conditioning, reliability. | Explains why power becomes scarce. |
| Asset control | Constellation nuclear fleet, Vistra dispatchable fleet, NRG retail/generation, NextEra development backlog. | Shows who controls actual power or development capacity. |
| Regulated conversion | Duke ESAs, AEP load/capital plan, FPL SPPCRC recovery, Exelon wires capital. | Shows how demand enters permissioned utility systems. |
| Customer-payment route | Tariffs, riders, rate factors, billing determinants, ESAs, PPAs. | Separates demand from recoverable cash. |
| Boundary | No customer cash or project return without billing/collection/in-service/return proof. | Keeps the chapter from becoming hype. |

## Proposed Power Chapter Outline

1. Open with the simple claim: electricity is becoming a growth constraint.
2. Explain why a megawatt must be generated, delivered, contracted, financed, regulated, and recovered.
3. Split the power universe into different machines:
   - clean reliable generation
   - dispatchable merchant power
   - customer-backed power growth
   - regulated wires reliability
   - regulated load conversion
   - hybrid utility/development compounding
4. Use the company examples:
   - Constellation for clean reliable generation
   - Vistra for dispatchable fleet value
   - NRG for customer-backed growth
   - Exelon for wires and reliability
   - Duke/AEP for load-to-capital conversion
   - NextEra/FPL for development and regulated recovery
5. Insert the proof box:
   - load forecast is not cash
   - ESA/PPA/tariff is stronger
   - approved recovery is stronger still
   - billing/collections and earned return are the final proof frontier
6. Close with the takeaway:
   - power is becoming permissioned growth infrastructure

## Recommended Second Chapter

The second chapter should be:

`Capital allocation and private markets`

It should follow power because it explains where the financing may come from and why large platforms matter.

The clean transition is:

`Once power and infrastructure become scarce, the next question is who supplies the capital and what wrapper carries it.`

That is where BlackRock, Blackstone, Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic, Brookfield, Ares, and Carlyle enter.

## Safe Claim

`The long-form annual-report draft should lead with power scarcity and grid reliability because it has the strongest combination of reader clarity, company examples, annual-report metrics, and public proof routes. Capital allocation/private markets should follow as the proof-heavy financing chapter. The selection is a writing decision, not new primary evidence or proof that power projects have reached customer-cash or project-return grade.`
