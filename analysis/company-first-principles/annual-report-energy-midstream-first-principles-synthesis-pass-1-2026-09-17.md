# Energy midstream: first-principles synthesis — pass 1

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This chapter compares Cheniere Energy and Energy Transfer as energy-
infrastructure businesses. It is a qualified diagnostic, not a ranking:
Cheniere is LNG export and terminal infrastructure with contracted demand;
Energy Transfer is a diversified pipeline, NGL, storage, processing, and
partnership network with a different legal and capital structure.

## Force and control point

Natural-gas abundance, LNG export demand, energy-security policy, NGL growth,
industrial load, and constrained transport/terminal capacity can create value
at physical bottlenecks. Cheniere's control point is liquefaction, terminal
access, long-lived offtake contracts, and cargo execution. Energy Transfer's
control point is a connected network of pipelines, processing, storage,
fractionation, export, and shipper relationships.

The burden carrier is not just the end customer: projects require permitting,
construction, commodity and basis management, maintenance capital, debt,
counterparty performance, environmental compliance, and sometimes partnership
or minority-interest claims.

## End-to-end economic chain

```text
gas/NGL supply and energy-security demand
  -> terminal, pipeline, processing, storage, and fractionation bottleneck
  -> shipper/offtaker contract and tariff/fee economics
  -> throughput, utilization, cargo delivery, and collection
  -> operating cash after maintenance/growth capex and project costs
  -> debt service, preferred/NCI/partnership claims, taxes, and dilution
  -> common-owner residual
```

Capacity, contracted volume, adjusted EBITDA, DCF, and system throughput are
visibility inputs. They are not proof of project cash contribution or common-
owner cash.

## Quality of earnings and financial-shenanigans controls

1. Reconcile throughput, cargoes, contracted volumes, utilization, and tariff/
   fee rates to invoices, receivables, collections, and cash.
2. Separate long-term contracted demand from take-or-pay terms, actual delivery,
   counterparty credit, price formulas, and realized cash.
3. Decompose OCF for working-capital timing, derivatives, inventory, margining,
   and temporary commodity/basis effects.
4. Separate maintenance, growth, expansion, and capitalized interest; project
   capex cannot be inferred from total PP&E spending alone.
5. Keep acquisitions, asset contributions, dropdowns, partnership distributions,
   minority interests, and related-party transactions in the owner-cash bridge.
6. Test adjusted EBITDA and DCF against GAAP cash, debt service, restricted
   cash, reserve/contractual obligations, and required capital.
7. Reconcile repurchases, dividends, and partnership distributions to cash
   after capex, debt, claims, and dilution.

## Valuation and liquidity handoff

The Damodaran-style input is project and entity cash flow after volume, tariff/
fee, utilization, maintenance/growth capex, debt, tax, partnership/NCI, and
dilution. A DCF or EBITDA multiple based on contracted capacity alone is not
enough; project commencement, stabilized contribution, and legal ownership must
be matched.

The Lyn Alden-style stress path is gas/NGL price and basis volatility,
counterparty credit, refinancing rates, construction inflation, permitting,
export policy, domestic affordability, and funding access. The thesis breaker
is rising capacity or contracted-volume claims alongside weaker collections,
utilization, project returns, debt service, or common-owner cash.

## Promotion rule

Current status:

`qualified-physical-bottleneck; company-level-cash-diagnostic; named-project-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires a named project or legal entity, capital invested, source of
funds, customer/shipper contract and billing, collected cash, maintenance and
growth allocation, debt/partnership claims, and a project-to-common-owner
return bridge. The exact next source object is a project contribution or
settlement schedule, not a larger EBITDA, DCF, capacity, or throughput number.
