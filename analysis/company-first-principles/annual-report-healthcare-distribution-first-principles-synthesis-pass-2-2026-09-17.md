# Healthcare distribution: first-principles synthesis — pass 2

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This chapter formalizes McKesson, Cencora, and Cardinal Health as a
filing-backed healthcare-distribution cohort. It is a sector diagnostic, not a
ranking: the companies have different fiscal periods, segment mixes,
acquisition programs, and legal-entity claims.

## Force and control point

Essential-drug demand, specialty-pharmacy complexity, reimbursement friction,
and provider workflow pressure create a recurring need for trusted purchasing,
regulated logistics, inventory availability, chargeback/rebate administration,
and specialty services. The sector's control point is distribution scale and
workflow access—not ownership of the full value of the drugs moving through the
system.

Manufacturers and payers fund the chain; pharmacies, hospitals, providers, and
patients are end users; distributors carry inventory, receivables, compliance,
recall, labor, technology, legal, acquisition, and financing burdens. Because
product value passes through the distributor, revenue is a particularly weak
owner-cash denominator.

## End-to-end economic chain

```text
essential and specialty-care demand
  -> manufacturer access and purchasing scale
  -> inventory, regulated logistics, rebates, and provider/pharmacy workflow
  -> gross-profit spread and service adjacency
  -> receivable collection, supplier terms, and inventory settlement
  -> operating cash after capex, software, acquisitions, and legal claims
  -> debt, preferred/NCI, physician units, dividends, repurchases, dilution
  -> common-owner residual
```

The proper test is gross-profit conversion plus normalized working-capital
funding and claims—not revenue growth, adjusted EPS, or reported OCF alone.

## Quality of earnings and financial-shenanigans controls

These controls follow Sloan-, Schilit-, and Beneish-style diagnostic logic but
are not manipulation findings:

1. Decompose revenue into product price, volume, mix, specialty services,
   rebates, chargebacks, and acquisitions; revenue pass-through is not value
   capture.
2. Reconcile receivables, inventory, rebates, returns, LIFO, and payables over
   matched periods. A payable release is temporary funding until settlement is
   observed.
3. Join GAAP income to adjusted EPS by isolating goodwill impairment, deal and
   integration costs, LIFO, litigation, remeasurement, and contingent
   consideration; recurring costs cannot disappear through labels.
4. Keep acquisition cash, physician/management units, debt, and integration
   spending in the return hurdle.
5. Treat opioid payments, litigation credits, tariff refunds, and customer
   payback obligations as period-matched legal or temporary items; avoid
   subtracting cash already reflected in OCF twice.
6. Allocate preferred equity, redeemable NCI, VIEs, held-for-sale entities,
   specialty interests, and securitization claims before naming common-owner
   cash.
7. Reconcile repurchases and dividends to post-reinvestment cash, debt, and
   dilution. Capital returns do not prove that preceding OCF was surplus.

## Company-specific control points

McKesson's scale and specialty reach may support a durable gross-profit spread,
but its quarterly cash surface is highly sensitive to receivables, inventory,
payables, rebates, and the Medical-Surgical Solutions preferred/NCI perimeter.
The next decisive evidence is two comparable quarters plus segment cash and
preferred allocation.

Cencora's specialty and international route may create high-value workflow
adjacency, but the OneOncology/RCA acquisition program makes adjusted-EPS
quality inseparable from acquisition cash, contingent consideration, physician
economics, integration, debt, and working capital. The next decisive evidence
is a post-close cohort cash and gross-profit bridge.

Cardinal's distribution and medical-products workflow has a stronger current
OCF surface, but acquisitions, opioid settlement cash, CVS concentration,
Solaris physician-unit economics, debt reduction, repurchases, dividends, and
temporary tariff/refund items compete for the residual. The next decisive
evidence is a cohort-level acquisition/legal/remittance schedule.

## Valuation and liquidity handoff

The Damodaran-style valuation input is normalized gross-profit spread converted
to owner cash after working-capital funding, capex/software, acquisitions,
claims, debt, preferred/NCI, and dilution. A revenue multiple or adjusted-EPS
multiple ignores the pass-through economics and claim stack.

The Lyn Alden-style stress path is: drug-price inflation and LIFO volatility;
reimbursement and payer pressure; customer credit losses; higher acquisition
financing rates; supplier and inventory funding; contract concentration; and
legal/regulatory settlement cash. A distributor can report growth while
working capital, claims, or acquisitions absorb the common-owner residual.

## Promotion ladder and breaker

Current status:

`qualified-distribution-control-point; current-period-diagnostic; normalized-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, comparable-period gross-profit and cash
conversion; normalized receivables, inventory, payables, rebates, and LIFO;
acquisition cohort return; legal and temporary cash; financing; preferred/NCI
claims; and diluted common-owner cash.

The thesis breaker is: revenue and adjusted EPS rise while normalized gross
profit, working-capital settlement, acquisition return, legal cash, debt, or
dilution causes common-owner cash per diluted share to deteriorate. The next
joinable object is a matched settlement or acquisition cash schedule, not a
larger revenue or adjusted-EPS screen.
