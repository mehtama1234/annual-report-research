## Short Answer

The older HTML site and the newer markdown framework were doing related but different jobs.

The older HTML site was better at naming social and cultural pressure points. It gave us phrases like:

- `The Gray Wave Dividend`
- `The AI Infrastructure Toll`
- `The Outage Anxiety Economy`
- `The Trust Market`
- `The Addiction Dividend`
- `The Presence Premium`
- `The Ritual Economy`
- `Membership or Nothing`
- `The Private Capital Colonization`

Those names are useful because they are memorable and human. They describe what people, institutions, and companies are reacting to.

The newer markdown framework is better at proof discipline. It asks:

- who needs something
- who controls the bottleneck
- who pays
- what legal or operating route the money follows
- whether the evidence is source-visible or still candidate-level
- what we still cannot claim

The integration decision is:

`Keep the newer 15 themes as the main framework, and treat the older 20 HTML dynamics as sharper subthemes inside that framework.`

That gives us both things we need: memorable ideas and evidence boundaries.

## What The Crosswalk Changes

Before this pass, the project had two useful but disconnected bodies of work.

The older HTML site could answer:

`What are the big social, cultural, consumer, industrial, and financial dynamics visible across annual reports?`

The newer markdown system could answer:

`Which themes have source-visible support, how does money plausibly move, and where does the proof still break?`

After this pass, the old dynamics become usable inside the current reading system.

That matters because some of the most interesting observations were stranded in the old HTML layer:

- households paying for reliability, comfort, ritual, and regret reduction
- institutions paying for trust, screening, compliance, identity, and control
- companies turning membership, spread, route ownership, and habit into cash
- AI showing up as power, land, cooling, chips, construction, and utility demand
- private capital platforms routing retirement, insurance, credit, real estate, and infrastructure money through wrappers that are harder to see than ordinary bank lending

## What This Means For The Final Annual-Report Story

The old site gives the story more human depth.

The new framework gives the story more evidentiary discipline.

Together they say:

`The economy is not only growing or shrinking. It is being rerouted through bottlenecks. Some bottlenecks are physical, like power, land, labor, materials, logistics, and care sites. Some are legal or financial, like insurance wrappers, private credit vehicles, leases, tariffs, collateral, payment networks, and memberships. Some are cultural, like trust, habit, ritual, presence, identity, and fear of failure. Annual reports are useful because they show which companies sit near those bottlenecks and what evidence exists for how money reaches them.`

## How To Use This

Use the crosswalk in three ways.

First, use it to deepen the existing theme pages. The underdeveloped newer themes now have old-site subthemes to pull from:

- services and cultural consumption gets addiction dividend, presence premium, local news collapse, ritual economy, and membership
- consumer goods and household identity gets ritual economy, pet economy, affordability ceiling, and trust
- broad technology gets AI cultural disintermediation and AI infrastructure toll
- healthcare tools gets screening revolution and gray wave demand
- real estate and scarce locations gets presence, grid landlords, gray wave housing, and private capital
- ordinary finance gets cash harvest, spread machine, membership, affordability pressure, and private capital

Second, use it to keep language vivid without overclaiming. The old names are useful headings, but the newer proof system still decides what can be claimed.

Third, use it to choose the next evidence pass. The best next work is not to invent more themes. It is to take the weakest but most interesting crosswalk themes and add source-visible company examples.

## Boundaries

This page does not prove named cash movement.

It does not show receipts, billing files, debt waterfalls, legal-entity schedules, collateral certificates, or asset-level return models.

It is a synthesis crosswalk. It says:

`Here is where the older HTML trend work belongs inside the newer annual-report framework, and here is the money-movement question each old trend raises.`
