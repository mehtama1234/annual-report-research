# Insurance statutory named-asset income: first-principles synthesis — pass 2

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## The force and control point

Insurance converts policyholder premiums and annuity liabilities into a
regulated balance sheet of bonds, mortgages, private credit, alternatives,
and other named assets. The economic control point is the legal-entity asset
and liability spread—not platform AUM alone.

```text
policyholder / annuity funding
  -> insurer legal entity and regulatory capital
  -> named issuer, CUSIP, borrower, fund, or collateral pool
  -> coupon, sale, maturity, repayment, or other proceeds
  -> liability crediting cost, reinsurance, capital, and servicing claims
  -> statutory surplus / parent distribution / common-owner residual
```

The figures are not a platform ranking. They show why the statutory route is
more informative than AUM: the legal entity, named asset, income/proceeds, and
senior claims become testable objects.

## Quality-of-earnings and financial-shenanigans controls

1. **Income versus cash:** interest received is stronger than accrued income,
   but still does not prove unrestricted cash, remittance, or common-owner
   availability.
2. **CUSIP continuity:** a same-CUSIP appearance across periods does not prove
   lot continuity, purchase cost, sale settlement, or final proceeds.
3. **Disposal economics:** consideration and gain/loss are not interchangeable;
   verify principal, accrued interest, fees, taxes, and settlement date.
4. **Legal-entity perimeter:** statutory investment income, policyholder cash,
   parent distributions, preferred claims, and common cash must remain separate.
5. **Liability cost:** asset yield cannot be called spread or return until
   credited rates, reinsurance, funds-held balances, servicing, capital, and
   other funding costs are joined.

These are Sloan-, Schilit-, and Beneish-style diagnostics, not allegations of
misconduct. A favorable statutory income row is not enough to promote an
asset-level return.

## Damodaran and Lyn Alden lenses

The Damodaran object is a liability-adjusted asset-income and capital-return
range: asset cash yield, credit losses, duration, defaults, realized proceeds,
capital requirements, expenses, and the cost of insurance funding. The model
must not value gross statutory income as common-owner cash.

The Lyn-Alden-style stress path includes higher rates and spread widening,
credit migration, policyholder withdrawals, collateral haircuts, forced sales,
reinsurance liquidity, and parent restrictions. Duration mismatch and a
liquidity event can turn an apparently profitable asset book into a capital or
realization problem.

## Promotion ladder and breakers

Promotion requires:

`legal entity -> named asset/CUSIP -> invested amount -> income/proceeds -> liability cost/funding source -> cash route or waterfall -> return`

The lane remains qualified because the current public evidence does not join
all of those fields. The thesis breakers are:

1. statutory income rises while collected cash, asset quality, or liability-
   adjusted spread deteriorates;
2. named proceeds appear without lot continuity or settlement evidence;
3. asset-manager or parent distributions are assumed to be unrestricted
   common-owner cash despite policyholder, preferred, NCI, or capital claims;
4. credit migration, collateral haircuts, or forced sales consume statutory
   surplus and liquidity.

Status: `insurance-statutory-named-asset-end-to-end-qualified; liability-adjusted-return-open; no-ranking`
