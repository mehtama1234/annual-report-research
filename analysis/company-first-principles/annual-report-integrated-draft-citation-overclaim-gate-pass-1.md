## Public Citation Priorities

For external publication, cite in this order:

1. Power and capital platforms, because these contain the highest-risk money-movement claims.
2. Healthcare access and healthcare infrastructure, because volume, quality, reimbursement, and trust can be easily confused.
3. Broad technology and digital control, because ARR, RPO, AI exposure, and traffic can be overread.
4. Value retail, services, and consumer goods, because cultural claims need company evidence without pretending to prove all household behavior.
5. Basic materials, real estate, energy, and ordinary finance, because they broaden the article and need sector-specific support.

## Required Edits Before Public Release

1. Add public source citations to every named company paragraph.
2. Add source notes for the `15`-theme map explaining that it is a synthesis map, not exhaustive company coverage.
3. Keep the capital-platform AUM boundary.
4. Keep the healthcare quality and reimbursement boundary.
5. Keep the technology durability and cash-conversion boundary.
6. Keep the final named-cash proof boundary.
7. Do not add stock-ranking, buy/sell, or recommendation language.

## Reader Review Questions

Use these questions for human review:

1. Does the article make sense without knowing the earlier research history?
2. Does the `15`-theme map feel broad enough to represent the `519` company folder?
3. Do the eight expansion themes make the article stronger, or do any feel too thin?
4. Is the difference between article-ready theme evidence and named-cash proof clear?
5. Are any company examples too specific to remain uncited in public form?
6. Does the final takeaway sound like economic interpretation rather than investment advice?
