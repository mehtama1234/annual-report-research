# Annual Report Integrated Meaty End-To-End Goal Pass 1

## Purpose

This page states the combined goal for the annual-report project.

It connects two things that can be confused:

1. the broad theme project
2. the named cash proof project

The broad theme project asks what the annual reports reveal about modern life, industry, finance, and investment.

The named cash proof project asks whether a specific money path can be proven from source to use to cash return.

The structured companion table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/annual-report-integrated-meaty-end-to-end-goal-pass-1.csv`

## The Meaty End-To-End Goal

`Use the 519-company annual-report folder to explain what the economy is asking companies to solve, who is getting paid to solve it, how money is being routed into those systems, and which claims can be proven with documents instead of assumed from size, growth, backlog, AUM, capex, or EBITDA.`

In plain words:

`What is breaking, scarce, delayed, expensive, trusted, financed, maintained, or protected; who sits in the middle; who pays them; where does the money go; and what proof do we have?`

This is bigger than one stock idea, one sector, or one capital-flow lane.

The project is trying to turn hundreds of company annual reports into a bottom-up map of the economy.

## The Main Question

The main question is:

`What do annual reports tell us about how society, industry, technology, healthcare, households, real estate, materials, utilities, finance, and big capital are actually working underneath the headlines?`

That breaks into five practical questions:

1. `What is becoming harder to supply?`
2. `Who controls the bottleneck or solves the problem?`
3. `Who pays for the solution?`
4. `How does money move into the company, project, borrower, asset, or legal entity?`
5. `What proof do we have, and what proof is still missing?`

## What We Are Trying To Build

The final output should be a first-principles research package with four layers.

### Layer 1: The 15-Theme Economic Map

This layer explains the big patterns found across the annual reports.

The current integrated map covers:

1. power scarcity and grid pressure
2. capital platforms and private-market money routing
3. industrial uptime and physical work
4. healthcare access and care delivery
5. built-environment upkeep
6. digital control, security, workflow, and infrastructure
7. value retail and pressured household budgets
8. services, travel, restaurants, and cultural consumption
9. consumer goods, identity, routines, and small comforts
10. basic materials and input scarcity
11. real estate, location scarcity, rent, and debt
12. broad technology beyond internet software
13. energy affordability and logistics
14. ordinary finance, credit, deposits, cards, and insurance
15. labor, staffing, trust, and execution capacity

This layer answers:

`What is the economy doing, sector by sector, when we stop reading slogans and start reading operating disclosures?`

### Layer 2: The Company And Sector Evidence Map

This layer ties the themes back to actual company packets.

It should show:

- which companies support each theme
- what operating facts matter
- which sectors are well covered
- which sectors are undercovered
- which claims are article-ready
- which claims need more primary-source citation
- which claims need hard cash proof

This layer prevents the article from becoming a loose essay.

### Layer 3: The Money-Movement Map

This layer answers the user's core capital question:

`Who is investing where, how is the money moving, from where, through what wrapper, into what asset or company, and what cash comes back?`

For each major money path, the row should try to identify:

| Field | Plain Meaning |
|---|---|
| source of money | saver, policyholder, deposit holder, ETF holder, pension, institution, private-wealth client, lender, bond buyer, customer, or operating cash |
| router | asset manager, insurer, bank, BDC, utility, REIT, public company, fund, or financing vehicle |
| wrapper | fund, ETF, statutory insurer, private-credit facility, revolver, ABL, bond, securitization, lease, rate clause, acquisition financing, or project-finance structure |
| destination | company, borrower, project, legal entity, asset, fleet, collateral pool, pipeline, plant, mine, grid program, data center, store base, or healthcare network |
| use | capex, acquisition, refinancing, inventory, working capital, fleet, buildout, maintenance, loan repayment, collateral advance, or asset purchase |
| cash back | customer receipt, billing, tariff revenue, rent, repayment, investment income, proceeds, settlement, cash flow, or asset sale |
| proof level | map, bridge, proxy, hold, or full proof |

This layer is where BlackRock, Blackstone, Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic, Brookfield, Ares, Carlyle, insurers, banks, BDCs, utilities, REITs, and public debt markets belong.

### Layer 4: The Named Cash Proof Layer

This is the hard verification layer.

It is needed only when we want to make an exact claim about a named money path.

Full named cash proof requires:

- receipts or billing records
- settlement ledgers
- debt waterfalls
- legal-entity schedules
- collateral certificates
- borrower cash records
- source/use records
- return models

The standard chain is:

`capital source -> router -> legal wrapper -> instrument -> named destination -> use -> operating output -> receipt or settlement -> debt service or legal availability -> return`

If one of those links is missing, the row can still be useful, but it must be called a map, bridge, proxy, or hold.

## What We Have Already Learned

The simple version is:

`The annual reports show where pressure is building, who is positioned near that pressure, and how money is starting to organize around it. They do not automatically prove that every company earns durable cash returns from that pressure.`

The broad findings are strong:

- electricity and grid capacity are becoming binding constraints
- physical execution matters because plans need labor, parts, equipment, permits, and materials
- healthcare is an access and reimbursement system, not just a demand-growth story
- consumers are managing pressure through value, convenience, identity, and small forms of relief
- technology is increasingly about control, trust, security, workflow, data, and compute infrastructure
- real estate value depends on location, tenant cash flow, debt maturity, and replacement cost
- materials matter because buildout needs copper, steel, aggregates, chemicals, fuel, packaging, and logistics
- finance is not just money supply; it is routing, wrappers, collateral, legal entities, duration, and risk transfer
- big asset managers and insurers are not only investors; they are routers of savings, insurance liabilities, retirement money, wealth money, and institutional capital

The cash-proof findings are more bounded:

- we found visible pipes
- we found several named destinations
- we found strong source/use/cash proxies
- we did not prove a complete named cash loop for every big platform
- no broad theme should be turned into an exact cash-return claim without the hard proof layer

## What The Project Is Not

This project is not:

- a stock recommendation list
- a claim that every named company is good
- a claim that every theme is investable
- a claim that AUM equals deployed cash
- a claim that backlog equals collected cash
- a claim that capex equals profitable growth
- a claim that EBITDA equals cash return
- a claim that traffic, users, patients, members, or customers prove quality
- a claim that public filings reveal every controlled document needed for proof

## The Working Answer Shape

The final package should answer each theme in this shape:

1. `What is the pressure?`
2. `Why does the pressure exist?`
3. `Which companies sit near it?`
4. `How do they get paid?`
5. `What operating metric shows the pressure?`
6. `What money path supports the business?`
7. `What can we safely say?`
8. `What would be an overclaim?`
9. `What document would upgrade the proof?`

For capital-flow rows, the answer shape is stricter:

1. `Who supplied or controlled the money?`
2. `Who routed it?`
3. `What legal wrapper carried it?`
4. `Who received it?`
5. `What did it fund?`
6. `What cash came back?`
7. `Who had legal claim on that cash?`
8. `What return was earned?`
9. `Which document proves each step?`

## Definition Of Done

The broad annual-report project is done when:

1. the 15-theme article is readable by someone with no background
2. every theme has company examples and source anchors
3. every named metric has a public citation or is removed
4. the article clearly separates theme evidence from cash proof
5. the source notes, citation queue, sentence audit, link check, and handoff checklist are complete
6. the final draft does not overclaim investment, quality, durability, or cash-return proof

The named-cash proof project is done only when:

1. at least one selected case joins source, wrapper, destination, use, cash back, legal route, and return
2. every missing link is explicitly labeled
3. the decisive missing documents are named
4. the safe claim and forbidden claim are written side by side
5. the result can be repeated on other platforms or sectors

## Current Practical Goal

The immediate practical goal is:

`Finish the integrated public article as the broad economic synthesis, then keep named-cash proof as an appendix or follow-on proof program rather than letting it delay the entire article.`
