# Integrated oil and gas: first-principles synthesis — pass 1

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This chapter compares ConocoPhillips and Exxon Mobil as energy producers with
different integration and capital profiles. It is a qualified diagnostic, not a
ranking.

## Force and control point

Energy security, natural-gas and oil demand, industrial consumption, refining
and chemicals complexity, geopolitical supply risk, and decarbonization policy
shape the sector. ConocoPhillips' control point is upstream resource position,
reservoir execution, cost structure, and reserve replacement. Exxon's control
point spans upstream resources plus refining, chemicals, trading, logistics,
and integrated project scale.

The burden carrier is the operator and ultimately its owners: commodity price,
decline, reserve replacement, drilling, megaproject, refining-margin,
feedstock, environmental, abandonment, political, tax, and balance-sheet
burdens. Integration can diversify cash flow but also adds capital and
operational complexity.

## End-to-end economic chain

```text
energy demand and supply/geopolitical force
  -> reserves, acreage, wells, LNG/refining/chemical assets, and logistics
  -> production, throughput, realized price, margin, and trading
  -> operating cash after lifting, transport, labor, taxes, working capital,
     maintenance/growth capex, exploration, and decommissioning
  -> acquisitions, debt, preferred/NCI, dividends, buybacks, SBC, and dilution
  -> common-owner residual
```

Production, reserves, guidance, EBITDA, realized price, and reported OCF are
not automatically through-cycle owner cash. Capital replacement and commodity
cycle normalization are mandatory.

## Quality of earnings and financial-shenanigans controls

1. Reconcile production, reserves, lifting/throughput, realized prices, basis,
   and product mix to revenue and collections.
2. Separate commodity-price, volume, acquisition, divestiture, hedging,
   derivative, inventory, and working-capital effects from recurring cash.
3. Distinguish maintenance, development, exploration, LNG/refining/chemical,
   and megaproject capex; a capital program is not the same as paid capex.
4. Test reserve replacement, decline, finding/development cost, impairment,
   abandonment, and asset-sale gains against through-cycle owner cash.
5. Keep acquisitions, project overruns, environmental/remediation, litigation,
   taxes, decommissioning, debt, and preferred/NCI claims in the hurdle.
6. For Exxon, separate upstream, downstream, chemicals, trading, and corporate
   results; integration does not prove each segment's return.
7. Reconcile dividends and buybacks to post-replacement-capex cash, debt,
   commodity-cycle stress, SBC, and diluted shares.

## Valuation and liquidity handoff

The Damodaran-style input is a through-cycle commodity and project model:
production/throughput, realized price, margin, decline, reserve replacement,
maintenance/growth capex, tax, debt, and dilution. Spot-price earnings or an
integrated-company multiple is insufficient.

The Lyn Alden-style stress path is energy inflation, recession, geopolitical
shock, rates, refinancing, reserve replacement, project delays, carbon policy,
consumer affordability, and counterparty/trading liquidity. The thesis breaker
is rising production, revenue, or adjusted earnings alongside weaker reserve
quality, rising replacement capex, debt, environmental claims, or declining
diluted owner cash.
