## What JPMorgan Is Doing In Simple Words

Start with a paycheck, a bank account, a mortgage, a credit-card balance, a small-business loan, a merchant payment, a corporate bond sale, or a trading desk.

JPMorgan touches all of those.

A household may deposit money, borrow on a card, make a payment, save, invest, or refinance. A business may keep deposits, borrow, process payments, raise debt, manage cash, hedge risk, or use investment-banking services. A government or institution may need custody, markets, treasury, or capital-market access.

That is why JPMorgan belongs in ordinary finance.

It is not only a Wall Street bank. It is a daily money machine: deposits come in, loans go out, payments move, fees are earned, losses appear, markets create revenue, capital is held, and regulators decide how much room the bank has to operate.

## Money Path

The safe money path is:

`household, business, institution, or government financial need -> deposit, loan, card, payment, treasury, market, wealth, or banking relationship -> net interest income, fee income, payments revenue, market revenue, investment-banking revenue, asset-management revenue, or card revenue -> provision, credit losses, expenses, capital requirements, and funding cost -> net income, ROTCE, capital return, and balance-sheet capacity`

The pressure path is:

`higher rates, inflation, credit stress, deposit competition, weaker borrowers, market volatility, regulation, or geopolitical risk -> higher funding cost, weaker loan demand, more losses, reserve pressure, or episodic market revenue -> JPMorgan scale absorbs part of the shock -> evidence remains bounded until deposits, loan yields, credit losses, delinquencies, charge-offs, allowances, card metrics, capital, and segment profitability are extracted`

## Why This Matters For Ordinary Finance

JPMorgan gives the ordinary-finance theme its first source-visible anchor.

The theme is about how macro pressure reaches daily life. JPMorgan is useful because it touches several daily and institutional money channels at once:

| Channel | Plain Meaning | What To Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Deposits | people and businesses store cash at the bank | deposit cost, deposit movement, liquidity, funding mix |
| Loans | the bank lends today against future repayment | loan growth, loan yield, charge-offs, delinquencies, reserves |
| Cards | households and businesses spend through short-term credit | spend, balances, repayment, losses, rewards cost |
| Payments | commerce needs authorization and settlement | payment volume, merchant behavior, fees, fraud, competition |
| Markets | clients trade, hedge, issue, and move risk | market revenue, client activity, volatility, episodic gains |
| Capital | the bank must hold enough loss-absorbing capacity | CET1, stress capital, dividends, buybacks, regulation |
