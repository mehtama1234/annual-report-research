# Medical devices and clinical workflow: first-principles synthesis — pass 2

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## The force and control point

Healthcare systems are under pressure to treat more patients with constrained
clinical labor, capital, and reimbursement. The control point is not simply a
device sale. It is the workflow around a procedure or practice: installed
equipment, clinician training, consumables, software, service, procurement,
and the financing that lets a customer adopt the system.

```text
clinical demand / labor constraint -> procedure or practice workflow
  -> device, installed base, consumables, service, or distribution route
  -> reimbursement / provider payment / procurement -> revenue and gross profit
  -> inventory, receivables, R&D, leases, acquisitions, claims, debt, SBC
  -> residual common-owner cash
```

| Company | Control point | Who pays | Burden carrier | Correct denominator | Current grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stryker | Procedure platforms and clinical-device ecosystem | Hospitals and providers through procurement/reimbursement | Acquisition integration, inventory, quality/recall, R&D, debt, dilution | Procedure-linked revenue, gross profit, OCF, reinvestment, acquired-cohort cash | Qualified; Inari return open |
| Intuitive | Installed robotic-surgery base and recurring instruments/service | Hospitals/providers and procedure economics | Manufacturing, service, usage-based leases, R&D, inventory, SBC | Systems, procedures/system, instruments, service, lease collections, per-share cash | Qualified; utilization/collection open |
| Henry Schein | Dental/medical procurement and practice-workflow distribution | Practices, institutions, and patients indirectly | Working capital, securitized receivables, restructuring, software, debt, acquisitions | Gross profit, OCF, receivable collateral, capex/software, post-debt residual | Qualified; funding/owner cash open |

## Current cash surface

The latest evidence is company-specific rather than a pooled ranking. Stryker's
H1 2026 OCF was `$1.842B`, with current investing use around `$824M`, a `$1.000B`
note repayment, and disclosed integration, structural, recall, and regulatory
charges. Intuitive's installed-base evidence includes 11,710 da Vinci and 1,096
Ion systems, procedures up about 16%, H1 instruments/accessories revenue of
`$3.421B`, service revenue of `$906M`, and operating-lease revenue of `$512M`.
Henry Schein reported H1 OCF of `$145M`, property of `$55M`, capitalized
software of `$30M`, acquisitions/equity investments of `$30M`, and `$430M` of
receivable securitization outstanding.

These observations show three different cash problems: acquired-platform
return, installed-base utilization/collection, and thin-spread distribution
funding. None is normalized owner cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

- Stryker: separate acquired revenue, inventory step-up, amortization,
  integration cash, quality/recall cost, debt service, and diluted shares.
- Intuitive: test procedures and instruments per system, service attachment,
  lease receivables and collections, inventory turns, R&D, capitalized SBC,
  and repurchases. System placement is not utilization or collected cash.
- Henry Schein: reconcile restructuring, working capital, receivable
  securitization, borrowing, acquisitions, software/property capitalization,
  KKR-related ownership, repurchases, and dilution.

These are Sloan-, Schilit-, and Beneish-style reconciliation prompts, not
manipulation findings. “One-time” charges remain senior claims when they recur;
tariff refunds, acquisition remeasurement, restructuring, and legal or quality
payments remain period-specific unless repeated evidence proves otherwise.

## Damodaran and Lyn Alden lenses

The valuation question is what durable procedure utilization, consumable pull-
through, service margin, acquisition return, and reinvestment efficiency the
price assumes. Stryker needs acquired platforms to earn above the cost of
capital after integration and quality burden. Intuitive needs installed-base
growth to create recurring usage and service cash after manufacturing, R&D,
leases, SBC, and dilution. Henry Schein needs distribution scale and practice
workflow growth to overcome low margins, securitized-receivable funding, and
restructuring/debt claims.

Higher rates raise hospital capital costs and acquisition hurdles. Reimbursement
pressure can delay procedures and squeeze provider purchasing. Labor scarcity
increases the value of workflow automation but also raises implementation and
service burden. Liquidity tightening exposes receivables, inventory, leases,
securitization, and debt before adoption metrics necessarily fall.

## Conclusion and breakers

This is a qualified end-to-end sector lane, not an investment ranking. The
sector thesis breaks if clinical adoption or practice volume rises while
utilization, collection, inventory turns, cash after reinvestment, or
per-share residual deteriorates.

1. Stryker: Inari growth fails to produce post-close margin and cash after
   integration, quality, debt, and dilution.
2. Intuitive: procedures per system, instrument pull-through, lease collection,
   or per-share cash weaken despite installed-base growth.
3. Henry Schein: restructuring, securitization, acquisitions, or working
   capital consume the residual needed for debt and common owners.
