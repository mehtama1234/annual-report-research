# Annual Report Option Care Q2 2026 Therapy-Collection and Margin Bridge Pass 2

## Purpose

This pass tests whether Option Care's Q2 2026 Form 10-Q can move the
alternate-site infusion case from revenue and operating-cash visibility to a
therapy-mix, payer-collection, working-capital, and reinvestment bridge.

## Source and period

- Company: Option Care Health, Inc. (`OPCH`)
- Filing: Q2 2026 Form 10-Q
- Period: three and six months ended June 30, 2026
- Primary source: [SEC Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1014739/000101473926000023/bios-20260630.htm)

## Revenue and gross-profit surface

| Object | Q2 2026 | H1 2026 | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Net revenue | `$1.442B` | `$2.793B` | Consolidated infusion-services revenue |
| Commercial payer revenue | `$1.237B` | `$2.381B` | Largest payer category |
| Government payer revenue | `$186M` | `$364M` | Direct-government route |
| Patient revenue | `$19M` | `$47M` | Patient-pay component |
| Cost of revenue | `$1.175B` | `$2.264B` | Pharmaceuticals, supplies, direct clinical labor, logistics, and related cost |
| Gross profit | `$267M` | `$529M` | Therapy-level contribution before SG&A, D&A, interest, tax, and capital |
| Gross margin | `18.5%` | `18.9%` | Margin denominator exposed to therapy mix and payer economics |

Management states that Q2 acute-therapy growth was high single digit while the
chronic portfolio was flat. It identifies patient attrition and unfavorable
chronic inflammatory disease therapy mix as gross-profit pressure, with an
expected approximately `$55M` 2026 gross-profit headwind from those CID
dynamics. That is an observed mix risk, not a normalized recurring cost
estimate.

## Collection and working-capital bridge

The filing reports:

- accounts receivable of `$511.507M` at June 30, up from `$473.566M` at
  December 31, 2025;
- H1 accounts receivable use of `$37.941M` of operating cash;
- H1 inventory supply of `$71.544M` of operating cash;
- `$154.9M` of earned but unbilled gross receivables at June 30;
- billing delays ranging from one day to several weeks because payer-specific
  documentation may be required after therapy administration;
- the largest payer representing approximately `14%` of Q2 and H1 revenue;
- government programs representing approximately `13%` of revenue and `13%` of
  accounts receivable; and
- approximately `57%` of pharmaceutical and medical-supply purchases sourced
  from three vendors.

Revenue is recorded at estimated net realizable amounts, and the filing warns
that complex payer arrangements can require later adjustments. Delivered
therapy is therefore not the same thing as final collected cash.

## Cash, reinvestment, and capital allocation

H1 operating cash flow was `$171.485M`, including the `$37.941M` receivable use
and `$71.544M` inventory release. Investing cash included `$20.123M` of PP&E
acquisition. Financing cash included `$170.545M` of common-stock purchases,
`$3.390M` of debt principal repayment, `$30.377M` interest paid, `$34.306M`
income taxes paid, and `$16.160M` operating-lease cash paid.

At June 30, total debt principal was `$1.173B`, the revolving facility had
`$850M` capacity, and net borrowing availability was `$846M` after letters of
credit. The company had `$193.767M` of cash.

The visible mechanical screen is:

`$171.485M OCF - $20.123M PP&E - $170.545M repurchases = $(19.183M)`

before treating repurchases as a residual financing choice. This is a
funding-pressure diagnostic, not a reported owner-cash result.

## What this proves

Option Care now has a same-period bridge from payer-category revenue and
therapy-mix pressure to gross profit, receivables, unbilled revenue, inventory,
PP&E, debt capacity, leases, taxes, and repurchases. It is a stronger
alternate-site infusion cash-quality object than revenue or adjusted EBITDA
alone.

## What remains unproven

- The filing does not disclose therapy-level collected cash, denial rates,
  authorization outcomes, or payer-specific gross margin.
- The `$55M` CID headwind is management's estimate and must not be treated as a
  fully quantified recurring cash burden without later evidence.
- Inventory release may be operational improvement or timing; it is not
  recurring owner cash.
- Repurchases do not prove surplus cash, especially alongside debt, lease,
  tax, clinical-labor, and working-capital claims.
- Patient outcomes, nurse capacity, therapy adherence, and legal-entity cash
  availability remain outside this filing bridge.

## Decision

`option-care-therapy-collection-margin-qualified; payer-and-owner-cash-bridge-open`

Option Care's therapy-mix, payer, and working-capital controls are now
qualified evidence inputs. The model remains unranked and does not promote
reported OCF, gross profit, or repurchases to common-owner cash.
