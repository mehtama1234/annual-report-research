# Annual Report Power/Grid Customer-Cash Proof Work Order Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass executes the top research choice from:

`/cluster/annual-report-theme-selection-work-order-pass-1.md`

The structured companion table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/annual-report-power-grid-customer-cash-proof-work-order-pass-1.csv`

The question is:

`How do we test whether power, grid, and data-center demand turns into actual customer cash, contractor cash, supplier cash, and earned return?`

## Short Answer

The power/grid theme is the best public proof lane, but it is still not finished proof.

The current evidence shows:

- load demand
- tariff and ESA routes
- capital plans
- turbine procurement
- project approval
- regulated recovery
- aggregate customer-revenue proxies
- contractor backlog and cash proxies

The missing evidence is:

- named customer contracts
- billing determinants
- billed revenue
- collected cash
- category allocation ledgers
- contractor project collections
- supplier deposits and delivery cash
- debt waterfalls
- earned-return or project-return models

In simple words:

`We can see that electricity demand is becoming a money path. We cannot yet prove every dollar from customer to utility to contractor to supplier to return.`

## Target Chain

The chain to prove is:

`load request -> customer contract or tariff -> utility capital spend -> approved recovery -> billing determinant -> collected cash -> contractor/supplier cash -> debt service -> earned return`

Most current evidence sits in the middle of that chain.

The next work must push toward the two hard ends:

1. who pays
2. who receives cash and return

## First Extraction

Start with:

`FPL SPPCRC customer receipts`

Reason:

FPL is the closest public route to actual customer cash. The local corpus already has a docket route, factor route, recovery mechanics, aggregate clause revenue, and Distribution Inspection category recovery.

The specific missing bridge is:

`Distribution Inspection category recovery -> billing determinants -> billed revenue -> collected cash -> category allocation -> earned return`

The next artifact should be:

`capital-flow-fpl-billing-determinant-category-receipt-proof-chase-pass-1`

## Second Extraction

Run:

`AEP large-load customer obligation`

Reason:

AEP Ohio has an official large-load/data-center tariff route with customer obligation mechanics. That is the most direct way to test whether data-center load becomes enforceable customer risk instead of only forecasted demand.

The missing bridge is:

`large-load request -> executed or redacted LOA/ESA/application -> collateral/deposit/minimum billing/reimbursement -> project allocation -> billed cash`

## Third Extraction

Run:

`Duke ESA and named project recovery`

Reason:

Duke has both secured ESA load and a named approved generation project. That gives two possible proof routes:

1. customer obligation route
2. named project route

The missing bridge is:

`secured ESA or Anderson County approval -> project cost -> recovery treatment -> in-service date -> billing/earned return`

## Why Contractors Matter

The power theme should not only ask:

`Which utilities own the assets?`

It should also ask:

`Who gets paid to build the assets?`

That is why Sterling and MasTec remain in the work order. They may show whether utility and data-center demand becomes backlog, revenue, margin, working-capital movement, and cash collection.

The proof boundary is strict:

`Backlog is not cash. Revenue is not project-level collection. Adjusted EBITDA is not owner-funded project return.`

## Why Suppliers Matter

AEP and Duke turbine procurement moves the theme toward physical scarcity. But secured turbines are not cash proof.

The supplier proof chain is:

`utility order -> supplier backlog -> deposit or payment term -> delivery -> revenue recognition -> margin/cash`

That is the route needed before the article can say who benefits from turbine scarcity in cash terms.

## Article Use

The power chapter can safely say:

`Power and grid demand is moving into documentable money paths: tariffs, ESAs, capital plans, recovery filings, project approvals, contractor backlog, and equipment procurement.`

The power chapter should not say:

`Data-center customers have already paid for all the grid buildout, utilities have earned the returns, or contractors have collected the project cash.`
