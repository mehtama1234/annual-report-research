# Annual-report real estate and scarce locations deep dive — pass 1

Research date: 2026-09-17

## Scope and first-principles question

Real estate creates value when a location gives another system something it
cannot easily reproduce: power, connectivity, customer access, logistics,
visibility, or a reliable place to transact. The scarce asset is not simply
the building. It is the right to use a location under operating conditions that
make the customer or tenant productive.

The first-principles question is:

> Who controls a place, site, lease, or access right that another system must
> use?

The current repository has deep evidence for digital infrastructure and a
property-based franchise model, but not yet a balanced tower, lodging,
residential, healthcare-REIT, or logistics-REIT packet. This pass keeps that
boundary visible.

## What the annual reports reveal

### 1. Location scarcity is a service, not a building statistic

Equinix and Digital Realty demonstrate that the useful property is a powered,
connected, accepted site. A data center with no power, interconnection, tenant
fit-out, or reliable commencement is not the same economic object as a
stabilized revenue-producing site.

McDonald’s shows the consumer version. A restaurant site is valuable because it
is convenient, recognizable, and integrated into a repeat behavior. The parent
can monetize property and franchise access even when a local operator carries
much of the day-to-day labor and food burden.

### 2. Scarcity can create funding needs before it creates cash

Digital Realty’s H1 development spend and Equinix’s PP&E purchases show that
the scarce-location thesis requires capital ahead of revenue. Signed rent,
backlog, bookings, and project count describe future visibility. They do not
prove that construction is complete, that rent has commenced, or that the
project return covers power, leasing, debt, partner, and equity claims.

The same issue appears in restaurant property. A strong site may require
remodels, technology, rent support, and local labor before the parent sees a
durable return. Property control is valuable, but the burden sits somewhere in
the system and must be assigned.

### 3. Property ownership changes who captures the economics

McDonald’s franchise structure separates parent property and fee economics from
restaurant operating economics. Equinix and Digital Realty separate
consolidated, JV, fund, and unconsolidated project economics. In both cases,
the consolidated income statement can hide who funded the location and who
receives the residual cash.

The key question is therefore not “which company owns the most real estate?”
It is “which legal entity funded the site, who pays to use it, who carries
renewal and financing risk, and what cash reaches common owners?”

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

1. Separate owned property, leased property, JV/VIE property, fund property, and partner-owned assets.
2. Do not treat bookings, backlog, annualized signed rent, occupancy, or project count as collected cash.
3. Reconcile rent commencement, incentives, straight-line rent, receivables, power costs, leasing commissions, and tenant fit-out.
4. Separate development, recurring maintenance, acquisition, capitalized interest, land, and indirect project costs.
5. Attribute debt, guarantees, preferred claims, OP units, partner contributions, and ATM dilution to the property cohort that requires them.
6. For McDonald’s, separate parent rent and fees from franchisee operating margin, remodel support, technology burden, and site-level traffic.
7. Treat property contributions or dispositions as one-time financing or portfolio events until settlement, debt, taxes, ownership, and remittance are reconciled.
8. Do not use FFO, AFFO, rent, or property-level NOI as common-owner cash without the legal-entity and capital-claim bridge.

## Owner-cash and valuation handoff

- **Equinix:** common-owner cash after power, recurring capex, lease-up, leasing commitments, real-estate acquisitions, JV/VIE funding, debt, leases, SBC, and dilution.
- **Digital Realty:** stabilized common cash after development and recurring capital, power, leasing costs, project/fund debt, partner economics, preferred/OP units, ATM dilution, and taxes.
- **McDonald’s:** parent rent/fee cash after property investment, remodels, technology, brand support, taxes, debt, and common-owner claims, while monitoring franchisee health as a leading burden signal.

Damodaran-style valuation must make the market pay for site duration,
commencement, utilization, pricing, reinvestment, and capital structure.
Lyn Alden-style stress must test rates, power and construction inflation,
tenant or franchisee funding, cap rates, refinancing, customer affordability,
and the liquidity value of a location during a downturn.

## Filing-based thesis breakers

| Company | Breaker observation |
| --- | --- |
| Equinix | Project commitments and PP&E rise faster than commencement, collections, or stabilized common cash; power, JV/VIE, debt, or dilution claims consume the network-density premium. |
| Digital Realty | Development capital and signed rent fail to convert into commenced, collected, stabilized NOI after project debt, partner capital, ATM dilution, and recurring capex. |
| McDonald’s | Guest counts remain weak despite site and value initiatives, franchisee economics deteriorate, or property/remodel and technology claims consume the parent residual. |
