# Restaurant franchising: first-principles synthesis — pass 1

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This chapter extends the cross-sector system into restaurant franchising. It is
a qualified cohort diagnostic, not a ranking: CAVA, Restaurant Brands
International, Wingstop, and Yum have different operating mixes, franchise
structures, fiscal calendars, and levels of corporate restaurant ownership.

## Force and control point

Consumers want convenient, trusted, repeatable meals while labor, real estate,
food input, delivery, and affordability pressures make restaurant operations
difficult. The franchisor's control point is the brand, menu system, operating
standard, supplier/procurement interface, technology, and franchise contract.
The franchisee typically funds local buildings, labor, inventory, and execution;
the franchisor captures royalties, fees, advertising-related economics, and
sometimes company-store margin or property income.

## End-to-end economic chain

```text
consumer occasion and affordability
  -> brand and franchise demand
  -> franchisee unit sales and local operating execution
  -> royalties, fees, advertising/property flows, and company-store revenue
  -> collection, refranchising, lease/property, and corporate support costs
  -> corporate OCF after capex, acquisitions, legal items, and working capital
  -> debt, dividends, repurchases, SBC, and other senior claims
  -> diluted common-owner residual
```

Systemwide sales and restaurant count are control-point indicators, not
franchisor owner cash. The correct denominator is the cash generated by the
legal franchisor after support, corporate investment, acquisitions, financing,
and dilution.

The mechanical residual is a diagnostic only. It does not allocate advertising
funds, franchisee support, leases, maintenance versus growth, taxes, debt
service, acquisitions, legal claims, or dilution, and it must not be compared
as if the four businesses had the same franchise mix.

## Quality of earnings and financial-shenanigans controls

These are Sloan-, Schilit-, and Beneish-style prompts for reconciliation, not
manipulation findings:

1. Separate franchisor royalty/fee revenue, company-store sales, property
   revenue, advertising funds, and pass-through amounts. Systemwide sales are
   not consolidated revenue.
2. Reconcile franchise receivables, deferred revenue, advertising-fund assets
   and liabilities, gift cards, rebates, supplier incentives, and franchisee
   support over matched periods.
3. Test refranchising, closures, remodels, impairment, and asset-sale gains;
   a shift from company stores to franchisees can improve reported margin while
   moving capital and operating burden outside the franchisor.
4. Keep acquisitions, integration, contingent consideration, lease obligations,
   litigation, and recurring restructuring in the return hurdle.
5. Reconcile capex to maintenance, remodel, new-store, technology, and
   corporate categories. Total PP&E spending is not automatically maintenance.
6. Join advertising-fund collections and spending before treating advertising
   revenue or expense as available to common owners.
7. Reconcile dividends and repurchases to post-capex cash, debt movement, SBC,
   and diluted shares. Capital returns do not prove surplus owner cash.

## Company-specific read-through

CAVA's corporate-store growth makes its OCF-to-capex conversion especially
sensitive to new-store and remodel intensity; the next proof object is a
same-period unit cohort and maintenance/growth capex schedule.

Restaurant Brands International has the largest visible corporate cash
surface in this sample, but acquisitions, dividends, advertising/property
flows, debt, and franchisee economics must be separated before interpreting
it as surplus cash.

Wingstop's highly franchised model may make royalties and brand control
economically attractive, but the repurchase amount exceeds the mechanical
OCF-less-PP&E screen in FY2025; funding, SBC, support, and franchisee health
must be reconciled before calling buybacks owner-funded.

Yum's scale and franchised system create a strong control-point hypothesis,
while repurchases, SBC, refranchising, advertising/property flows, debt, and
company-specific brand performance remain part of the cash and quality test.

## Valuation, liquidity, and promotion rule

The Damodaran-style valuation input is royalty/fee and company-store economics
converted to normalized common-owner cash after support, maintenance/growth
capital, acquisitions, debt, leases, advertising-fund perimeter, taxes, and
dilution. A systemwide-sales or royalty multiple alone is insufficient.

The Lyn Alden-style stress path is affordability and traffic pressure, wage and
food inflation, franchisee financing costs, real-estate and lease burden,
supplier disruption, delivery-platform economics, and refinancing risk. The
thesis breaker is rising systemwide activity or royalty revenue alongside
weaker franchisee health, higher support/closure costs, capex intensity, debt,
or diluted common-owner cash.
