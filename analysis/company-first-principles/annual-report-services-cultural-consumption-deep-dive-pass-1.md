# Annual-report services and cultural consumption deep dive — pass 1

Research date: 2026-09-17

## Plain-English thesis

People pay service companies when time, energy, skill, mobility, property
maintenance, or emotional bandwidth is scarce. The service is not always the
visible product. A meal can be a routine or a small affordable treat. A
franchise network can be a location and property system that makes repeated
visits possible. A home-improvement retailer can be the logistics layer that
keeps an aging house usable.

The first-principles question is:

> What does a customer pay someone else to do because doing it alone is too
> slow, difficult, uncertain, or inconvenient?

This is an article-grade sector theme, not proof that service demand is strong
or that any company is attractively valued.

## What the annual reports reveal

### 1. Service demand is often a time-and-friction purchase

Chipotle and McDonald's both sell food, but the economic object is different
from a commodity meal. Customers buy predictability, speed, habit, and a
socially legible occasion. Chipotle makes ingredients visible and keeps the
menu narrow. McDonald's uses locations, loyalty, pricing, and campaigns to
reduce the friction of deciding and returning.

Home Depot reveals the same mechanism outside restaurants. A homeowner or
contractor pays for product availability, delivery, and project support because
the cost of waiting or making a mistake is high. The retailer is increasingly a
service and logistics system, not just a store.

### 2. Low-price messaging is a cash-allocation decision

The $5 Meal Deal and McValue are not merely marketing. They test whether the
system can restore price trust while preserving restaurant economics. The
discount burden may sit with McDonald's, franchisees, suppliers, workers, or
customers through higher prices elsewhere. A successful campaign must produce
repeat visits or profitable basket expansion after discounts expire.

Chipotle faces the inverse test: a visible-quality promise can support a higher
check, but only while service speed, food quality, and household affordability
remain credible. Q2 2026 transactions and average check both improved, yet
restaurant margin declined. That is an immediate reminder that demand and
owner economics can move in different directions.

### 3. Property upkeep is a less discretionary service layer

Home Depot connects household affordability with physical maintenance. When
high rates and low mobility defer a move or remodel, leaks, HVAC failures,
storm damage, and safety repairs still occur. The company can therefore benefit
from an upkeep cycle even while discretionary housing activity is weak.

The risk is that acquisitions and distribution expansion add complexity faster
than they add durable cash. Sales growth, branch count, and cross-sell claims
must be reconciled to inventory, receivables, delivery labor, integration cost,
capital spending, and cash conversion.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

1. Split traffic or transactions from price, mix, and average check; sales growth alone can hide fewer visits.
2. Separate loyalty enrollment from a causal increase in visits; compare behavior before and after joining when data permits.
3. Attribute discounts among parent, franchisees, suppliers, workers, and customers rather than treating promotion as free demand.
4. For company-owned restaurants, test food, labor, packaging, occupancy, digital-order, and maintenance costs against store-level margin and new-store payback.
5. For franchisors, separate parent rent and fee economics from franchisee store economics, remodel funding, technology cost, and closure risk.
6. For Home Depot, separate organic demand from SRS/GMS acquisition contribution; test inventory, receivables, supplier terms, delivery assets, and integration charges.
7. Treat buybacks as a financing allocation, not proof that reported operating cash is surplus after maintenance and growth capital.
8. Keep adjusted operating metrics, restructuring, stock compensation, leases, taxes, legal claims, and dilution visible before naming owner cash.

## Owner-cash and valuation handoff

The correct denominator differs by mechanism:

- **Chipotle:** store-level cash after food, labor, occupancy, maintenance, new-store capital, taxes, and diluted shares; expansion only earns value if new-store payback persists.
- **McDonald's:** parent fee/rent cash plus required property, technology, brand, and capital support, less franchisee-health and discount-related burdens; loyalty and price campaigns enter valuation only through measured repeat behavior and margin.
- **Home Depot:** normalized cash after inventory and receivable funding, maintenance and distribution capital, SRS/GMS integration, leases, taxes, debt, and dilution; Pro throughput and upkeep demand are inputs, not the numerator.

Damodaran-style valuation should make the price pay for explicit assumptions:
same-store demand, traffic recovery, store or branch additions, margin
durability, reinvestment, acquisition returns, and terminal service economics.
Lyn Alden-style stress should test household affordability, wages, food and
material inflation, labor availability, rates, housing turnover, credit terms,
and liquidity-driven inventory funding.

## Filing-based thesis breakers

| Company | Breaker observation |
| --- | --- |
| Chipotle | Transactions remain weak or reverse while check growth and discounts mask declining store margin; new stores fail to cover construction, labor, food, and maintenance burden. |
| McDonald's | Guest counts remain negative after value campaigns, franchisee economics deteriorate, or technology and promotional costs rise without repeat visits, order accuracy, or labor productivity. |
| Home Depot | Pro cross-sell and upkeep demand fail to offset housing weakness, while SRS/GMS integration increases working-capital use, delivery burden, claims, or recurring capital faster than cash generation. |

## Decision and boundary

Status: services-cultural-consumption; article-grade-theme; company-evidence-visible; owner-cash-and-causal-demand-open; no-ranking.

The sector adds a missing social explanation: service spending is often a
purchase of time, predictability, relief, or property continuity. The filings
also show why the theme cannot be reduced to consumer strength. Visits can
fall while sales rise, quality promises can coexist with margin pressure, and
property-upkeep demand can coexist with acquisition and inventory burdens.
