## Short Answer

Yes, there are many other themes.

But the important distinction is:

`theme-ready does not mean proof-complete`

The annual-report work now supports a broad article about economic bottlenecks. It also supports a ranked research queue for deeper digs. The cash-proof lane remains the hardest discipline underneath the article, but it is not the only useful theme.

## The Big Picture

The article-level finding is:

`Annual reports show where the economy is under pressure and which companies are being paid to absorb that pressure.`

The deeper research question is:

`Which pressure points convert into durable cash and returns?`

Those are different jobs.

For writing, we can explain bottlenecks in power, healthcare, industrial uptime, built-environment upkeep, digital control, value retail, and capital platforms.

For proof, we still need receipts, billing support, legal-entity schedules, borrower-use evidence, collateral certificates, debt waterfalls, working-capital conversion, project margin, and return models.

## What This Means

There are two valid ways to continue.

The first is the writing route:

`turn the seven reader-ready themes into a clear article with source notes and overclaim boundaries`

That route is reasonably close. The package already has chapter spines, a final theme synthesis, a reader-facing essay, exhibits, publication review, overclaim review, and a first public source list.

The second is the deeper proof route:

`choose one theme and try to follow money all the way from source to use to cash-back`

That route is not close by design. It requires controlled documents or hard public substitutes.

## The Most Valuable Next Digs

### 1. Power/Grid Customer Cash

This is the cleanest public evidence route.

The target chain is:

`load request -> customer contract or tariff -> utility capital spend -> approved recovery -> billing determinant -> collected cash -> earned return`

This would make the power chapter much stronger because it would show who pays, not only who builds.

### 2. Insurance/Statutory Named Asset Income

This is the best route back to the big-money players.

The target chain is:

`insurance liability or retirement money -> legal entity -> named security or loan -> income/proceeds -> liability cost -> spread/return`

This is where Apollo/Athene and KKR/Global Atlantic matter most. It is also the right comparison path for Blackstone, Brookfield, BlackRock/HPS, Ares, and Carlyle.

### 3. Asset-Backed Collateral

This tests whether reported borrowing capacity is supported by real assets.

The target chain is:

`receivable, inventory, fleet, or lease asset -> eligibility rule -> borrowing base -> debt capacity -> collection/remittance -> debt service`

This would make the working-capital and asset-backed themes more concrete.

### 4. Private-Credit Borrower Facility

This answers the most direct money-flow question:

`who lent to whom, through what facility, for what use, and how is it repaid?`

The current Ares/Frontline evidence is a good starting point, but it needs facility size, lender allocation, funds-flow, borrower cash, and repayment/waterfall proof.

### 5. Healthcare Reimbursement And Cash Conversion

This is the best human-centered theme, but it needs proof discipline.

The target chain is:

`patient/service activity -> payer/reimbursement route -> claims/payment timing -> receivable collection -> service-line cash margin -> quality/trust boundary`

Without that, healthcare stays a strong narrative but not a cash-proof chapter.

## Simple Decision

If the goal is to start writing now:

`Use the seven chapter package and keep the proof boundaries visible.`

If the goal is to deepen the evidence before writing:

`Do Power/Grid Customer Cash first, then Insurance/Statutory Named Asset Income, then Asset-Backed Collateral.`

If the goal is to answer the original big-money-player question:

`Do Insurance/Statutory Named Asset Income first, because it is the shortest path from Apollo, KKR, Blackstone, BlackRock/HPS, Brookfield, Ares, and Carlyle into legal-entity assets and cash evidence.`
