## First-Principles Questions

The deep dive should not start from sector labels.

It should start from questions a normal reader can understand.

| Theme Family | First-Principles Question |
|---|---|
| Services and cultural consumption | What do people pay for when they are buying time, relief, status, convenience, travel, attention, or escape? |
| Consumer goods and household identity | What do households keep buying because it protects a routine, feeling, identity, habit, or sense of control? |
| Basic materials and input scarcity | Which raw inputs become hard to get when many parts of the economy try to build at the same time? |
| Real estate and scarce locations | Who controls a place, site, lease, tower, store, medical location, or logistics node that someone else must use? |
| Broad technology | Which digital, hardware, network, or software control point must work before modern activity can continue? |
| Healthcare devices and medical infrastructure | Which tools, tests, sites, distributors, and care systems make medical access physically possible? |
| Energy affordability | Who controls the route from production to usable energy, and who absorbs the cost when that route becomes expensive? |
| Ordinary finance | How do deposits, card balances, insurance policies, brokerage accounts, payment rails, and loans change household and business behavior? |

## Extraction Method

For each theme family:

1. Pick `5` to `8` representative company packets.
2. Pull only the annual-report fields needed for the theme.
3. Write one plain-English mechanism.
4. Identify the human or business pressure.
5. Identify who pays.
6. Identify what operating bottleneck or financial bottleneck the company handles.
7. State what is not proven.

The target is not full named-cash proof.

The target is article-grade sector breadth.
