## What This Lane Is Really Testing

Acquisition finance is not just:

`Company bought something.`

The useful question is:

`What money bought what control, capacity, routes, reserves, customers, stores, branches, mills, systems, or capabilities?`

The first-pass rows show five subthemes.

## Theme And Subtheme Read

### 1. Roll-Up Infrastructure

Rows:

- `AFL-001`
- `AFL-003`
- `AFL-009`
- `AFL-010`

This subtheme captures companies that consolidate fragmented physical channels.

Examples:

- PCA bought Greif's containerboard business for `1.8B USD` in cash, adding two mills, about `800,000` tons of capacity, and eight sheet feeder/corrugated plants.
- Lowe's shows FBM and ADG acquisition expenses as part of the Pro/building-products expansion.
- Silgan's Weener integration supports higher-value dispensing and specialty closures.
- Core & Main uses acquisitions to deepen waterworks/geography/category density.

The simple claim:

`Some acquisitions are not about financial engineering first. They are about controlling more physical throughput, branch density, customer access, and capacity inside fragmented operating systems.`

The boundary:

`Only PCA currently has a visible transaction value in the packet layer. The others need purchase price, funding source, acquired revenue, and purchase-accounting extraction.`

### 2. Strategic Acquisition Finance

Rows:

- `AFL-002`
- `AFL-004`
- `AFL-007`
- `AFL-008`

This subtheme captures large public companies using acquisitions to reshape strategic position.

Examples:

- Verizon's Frontier integration adds fiber/broadband scale and coincides with a large unsecured-debt step-up.
- Sherwin-Williams' Suvinil acquisition appears in mix and integration commentary.
- Phillips 66 acquired the remaining `50%` of WRB, closed Lindsey, and described 2025 acquisitions and dispositions of about `3.5B USD` each.

The simple claim:

`Strategic M&A turns capital into control over access networks, coatings/geography, refining assets, midstream integration, and customer channels.`

The boundary:

`Debt rising near a deal is not the same as deal financing unless the debt footnote or transaction document ties proceeds to the acquisition.`

### 3. Resource And Materials Consolidation

Rows:

- `AFL-005`
- `AFL-006`
- `AFL-011`

This subtheme tracks energy, gas, and mining assets where acquisitions or portfolio changes alter resource position.

Examples:

- Expand became the largest U.S. natural-gas producer after the Southwestern merger.
- Expand's announced Twin Eagle acquisition points toward commercial integration and gas-market optimization.
- Rio Tinto's packet is more project/portfolio buildout than clean deal-financing evidence, but it flags strategic materials and lithium growth as a capital destination.

The simple claim:

`Resource M&A matters when it changes reserves, basin scale, market access, commercial optimization, or strategic-materials exposure.`

The boundary:

`This pass does not yet extract consideration mix, assumed debt, target financials, or financing commitments.`

### 4. Software And Platform M&A

Rows:

The packet is rich for platform economics, creator payouts, trust and safety, AI tools, and operating cash flow. It is not rich for acquisition finance.
