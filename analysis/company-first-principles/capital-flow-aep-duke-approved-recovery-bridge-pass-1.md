# Capital Flow AEP/Duke Approved-Recovery Bridge Pass 1

## Purpose

This page answers the next power/grid operating-return question:

`Does large-load demand become approved, recoverable, in-service utility investment?`

The structured companion table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-aep-duke-approved-recovery-bridge-pass-1.csv`

## Short Answer

AEP and Duke can be upgraded from `official-source load and capital-plan evidence` to `approved-recovery pathway visible`.

The evidence is not yet full return proof. It shows that demand is moving into tariffs, service-agreement ladders, turbine procurement, project tables, state dockets, customer protections, and at least one named Duke generation approval. It does not prove that every disclosed GW is energized, every project is approved, every dollar is rate-base recoverable, or every investment earns its allowed return.

## Mechanism

The working mechanism is:

`large-load demand -> customer application or ESA/LOA -> tariff/study/financial obligation -> utility capital plan -> turbine/project procurement -> state docket or resource plan -> cost recovery/rate-base treatment -> construction -> in-service load -> earned return`

This is the core distinction in the power/grid lane. A management load number is weak. A load number tied to customer obligations, project procurement, docket status, recovery treatment, and in-service evidence is much stronger.

## Quality Read

The positive read is:

- AEP shows a load bridge from `56 GW` to `63 GW` to `69 GW`, with condition language preserved.
- AEP's capital plan increased from `$72B` to `$78B`, with transmission and generation named in the bridge.
- AEP has `13 GW` secured turbine capacity through `2031`.
- AEP Ohio has an effective data-center tariff with application, study, LOA/ESA, collateral, minimum-demand, exit-fee, and cancellation/delay reimbursement mechanics.
- Duke discloses `7.6 GW` secured data-center ESAs, about `5 GW` under construction, and `15.4 GW` late-stage pipeline.
- Duke has a `$103B` capital plan, `20` secured gas turbines, and a `7,501 MW` new gas generation project table.
- Duke Anderson County is a named `1,365 MW` project with docket and approval evidence.

The caution read is:

- signed load, expected load, secured ESAs, late-stage pipeline, under-construction load, and approved plants are different evidence grades
- secured turbines are not the same as approved projects
- capital plans are not the same as recoverable rate base
- tariff mechanics do not prove actual customer execution or energization
- Anderson County approval is one strong project row, not proof for every Duke project
- exact project costs, ROE, rider treatment, in-service dates, and customer cost allocation remain incomplete

## What This Upgrades

This bridge upgrades the AEP/Duke lane from:

`management load and capital-plan evidence`

to:

`approved-recovery pathway visible`

The reason is that the evidence now spans the legal conversion chain:

- demand status: AEP load bridge and Duke ESA ladder
- customer obligation: AEP Ohio tariff and Duke ESA/customer-provision evidence
- physical procurement: secured turbines and generation project table
- capital absorption: AEP `$78B` plan and Duke `$103B` plan
- regulatory pathway: AEP Ohio tariff, I&M EGR/Big Sandy path, Duke Anderson County docket/approval
- risk boundary: definition drift, recovery uncertainty, cost allocation, and in-service status

## Decision

`upgrade-with-boundary - AEP/Duke are now approved-recovery-pathway visible, not full utility-return-grade.`

## Safe Claim

The claim must stay bounded because not every disclosed GW is signed, approved, energized, recoverable, or earning a regulated return.`

## Claims Not To Make Yet

Do not say:

- all AEP `69 GW` is energized or approved
- AEP's full `$78B` capital plan is caused by data centers
- secured turbines equal approved plants
- Duke's `15.4 GW` late-stage pipeline is additive to `7.6 GW` secured ESAs
- Duke's `$103B` capital plan is fully approved or load-driven
- Anderson County proves all Duke generation projects are approved
- tariff mechanics prove customer-funded infrastructure without executed agreements and recovery orders
- capital plan dollars are returns before cost recovery and in-service evidence
