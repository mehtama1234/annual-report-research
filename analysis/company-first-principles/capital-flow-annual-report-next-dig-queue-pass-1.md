## Big Picture

The broad annual-report project is not one theme.

It is a set of money-movement themes that all ask the same underlying question:

`where did the money come from -> who routed it -> what legal wrapper carried it -> what asset, borrower, project, inventory, receivable, or contract received it -> what cash came back -> who had legal claim to that cash -> was the return enough`

Different annual-report themes expose different parts of that chain.

Power/grid gives the clearest public customer-pay route.

Insurance statutory filings give the best legal-entity asset-income route.

Collateral and securitization give the best borrowing-base route.

Private-credit borrower facilities give the most direct who-funded-whom route.

Infrastructure and energy give the best physical-output route.

Acquisitions and backlog show capital becoming operating scale.

Refinancing shows clean source/use/payoff mechanics.

Working capital shows whether reported growth converts into cash.

## What This Means In Simple Words

The research has moved past asking:

`What is the interesting theme?`

The better question is:

`Which theme gives us documents that can prove money moving from source to use to cash-back?`

On that standard:

1. Insurance/statutory asset-income is the best next route back to the big-money players like KKR, Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Ares, Brookfield, and Carlyle.
2. Asset-backed collateral is the best route for proving whether lending is actually supported by receivables, inventory, fleet, or other eligible assets.
3. Private-credit borrower facilities are the cleanest route for proving who funded a named borrower and what the borrower did with the money.
4. Infrastructure and energy are the best route for showing capital becoming physical assets and output.
5. Regulated recovery remains the closest public customer-receipt route, with FPL as the lead case.
