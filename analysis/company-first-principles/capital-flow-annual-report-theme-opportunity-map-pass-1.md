# Capital Flow Annual Report Theme Opportunity Map Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass answers the user's broader question:

`What other big annual-report themes could we dig into besides the current capital-flow writing package?`

The structured companion table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-annual-report-theme-opportunity-map-pass-1.csv`

This is not a new primary-source extraction. It is a navigation layer for the work already in the local corpus.

## Short Answer

There are at least `12` strong annual-report theme families worth digging into.

The best way to choose among them is not by which theme sounds interesting. The best way is to ask which one can answer the money-path question:

`who supplied the money -> what wrapper carried it -> who received it -> what it funded -> what cash came back -> who had legal claim to that cash -> what return was earned`

On that standard, the next best themes are:

| Rank | Theme | Why It Is Meaty |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Power, grid, and data-center load | It connects AI/data-center demand, utility capex, regulated recovery, contractors, equipment, and customer bills. |
| 2 | Insurance, retirement, and private-credit engines | It exposes how policyholder/retirement liabilities become named credit assets and statutory investment income. |
| 3 | Asset-backed collateral and securitization | It asks whether lending capacity is backed by real eligible collateral and cash conversion. |
| 4 | Private-credit borrower facilities | It follows money into named borrowers and asks whether the loan funded growth, acquisitions, refinancing, or risk migration. |
| 5 | Capital-intensive infrastructure and energy | It follows capex into LNG trains, pipelines, terminals, fleets, backlog, and project cash. |

The simple big-picture answer is:

`The annual reports are showing many versions of the same mechanism: money is being routed through legal wrappers into assets, projects, borrowers, utility recovery mechanisms, collateral pools, and operating platforms. The hard part is proving cash all the way back, not spotting the theme.`

## How To Use This Map

Each row should be read as a potential research program:

1. `theme_family` names the big theme.
2. `plain_english_question` states what the theme is trying to answer.
3. `best_current_evidence_pages` shows where the local corpus already has support.
4. `current_proof_level` says how far the evidence currently goes.
5. `missing_documents` names the documents that would upgrade the theme.
6. `next_extraction` gives the next concrete pass to run.

The priority rank is practical, not permanent. It favors themes that are big, documentable, writing-useful, and close enough to real cash proof to justify deeper extraction.

## What The Map Tells Us In Simple Words

The annual reports are not just about earnings.

They are showing how money moves through the economy.

Different themes show different pieces of that movement:

| Theme Type | What It Shows |
|---|---|
| Power/grid | Money becomes utility assets, customer recovery, contractor backlog, equipment orders, and regulated returns. |
| Insurance/private credit | Policyholder and retirement money becomes bonds, ABS, mortgage loans, private credit, and investment income. |
| Collateral/ABL | Borrowing capacity depends on receivables, fleet, inventory, reserves, and eligible collateral. |
| Private-credit borrowers | Asset managers and BDCs appear in the route to named operating companies. |
| Infrastructure/energy | Capex becomes physical output such as LNG cargoes, pipelines, terminals, throughput, and fleet. |
| Regulated recovery | Utility spending can become customer bills, but receipts still need proof. |
| Streaming finance | Upfront financing can become future commodity delivery and settlement cash. |
| Acquisitions/backlog | Money buys customers, contracts, backlog, and capacity, but conversion must be tested. |
| Refinancing | New lenders and bondholders fund old-debt payoffs, maturity extension, coupon changes, and liquidity resets. |
| Wealth/secondaries | Private-market capital sources widen, but named transaction cash is still hard to prove. |
| Working capital | Receivables and inventory show whether growth turns into cash or consumes cash. |
| Operating return | The final question is whether the money earned enough cash after risk, time, tax, capex, and debt service. |

## Best Next Theme If We Want A New Dig

The best next deep dig outside the current KKR/Global Atlantic / Accordia proof package is:

`Power, grid, and data-center load`

Reason:

1. It is big enough to matter.
2. It connects several annual-report clusters: utilities, contractors, equipment, financing, and asset managers.
3. It has public proof routes through dockets, tariffs, rate factors, resource plans, and recovery filings.
4. It can answer a concrete money question: who pays for power/grid expansion, and who earns from it?
5. It has a clear receipts frontier: billing determinants, customer obligations, collected cash, and earned-return workpapers.

| Customer/load source | Separates hyperscaler, industrial, residential, commercial, and general ratepayer demand. |
| Project/category | Names the grid, generation, distribution, data-center, or contractor activity. |
| Capital source | Separates debt, equity, customer contribution, rate recovery, project finance, and operating cash. |
| Recovery route | Shows tariff, rider, rate-base, customer contract, PPA, or other payback mechanism. |
| Contractor/supplier beneficiary | Shows who gets paid to build or equip the asset. |
| Cash/proxy evidence | Shows receipts, billing, backlog, revenue, EBITDA, OCF, or recovery. |
| Missing proof | Shows what still blocks full source-use-receipt-return proof. |

## Secondary Next Theme

The second-best new dig is:

`Asset-backed collateral and securitization`

Reason:

This lane can answer a different but equally important question:

`Is reported lending capacity actually backed by eligible, collectable assets?`

The best lead case is URI because the existing corpus already has fleet, ABL, AR securitization, liquidity, capex, rental revenue, and cash conversion evidence. The decisive missing proof is a populated borrowing-base certificate and fleet-class return bridge.

## Boundary

This map should not be used to claim that all these themes are already proved.

Safe use:

`The annual-report corpus has identified 12 theme families with different proof routes. Several have strong bridge evidence, but the decisive named-cash layer still depends on receipts, billing, debt waterfalls, legal-entity schedules, collateral certificates, and return models.`

Unsafe use:

`The annual-report corpus proves every capital flow from source to final return.`

That is not true yet.
