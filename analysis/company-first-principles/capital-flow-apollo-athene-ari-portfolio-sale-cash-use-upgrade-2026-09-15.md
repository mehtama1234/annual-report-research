# Apollo–Athene ARI portfolio-sale cash-use upgrade

Research date: `2026-09-15`

Public ARI transaction materials now provide a stronger seller-side boundary
for the `$8.7B`/approximately `$9B` related-party commercial-mortgage transfer.
They confirm a completed cash sale and identify how the seller used the
proceeds. They do not yet show Athene borrower receipts, loan-level collateral
performance, or Apollo parent cash.

## Evidence bridge

| Step | Evidence | Safe interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portfolio perimeter | ARI announced completion of the sale of its approximately `$9B` commercial-real-estate loan portfolio to Athene on April 24, 2026; Athene's 10-Q describes the purchased portfolio, including accrued interest, as `$8.7B` | The transaction is completed and named; the different rounded/perimeter figures must not be silently combined |
| Purchase formula | ARI proxy says consideration was based on `99.7%` of each loan's total commitment, subject to adjustments, and paid entirely in cash | Contractual cash consideration formula is visible; final settlement amount and loan-level allocations are not |
| Seller cash use | ARI completion release says that after repayment of financing facilities, other indebtedness, and transaction expenses, total assets would be approximately `$2.2B`, primarily cash | Sale proceeds visibly funded seller deleveraging and post-sale liquidity |
| Expected residual | ARI proxy describes approximately `$1.4B` net cash and approximately `$1.7B` common equity, or about `$12.05` per share, after debt and expenses | Seller-side residual expectation is visible; it is not Athene's asset return or Apollo HoldCo cash |
| Post-close accounting check | ARI's Q2 2026 10-Q says the portfolio sale closed in April for approximately `$8.6B`, met ASC 860 sale-accounting criteria, left no outstanding loans at June 30, and produced a `$2.6M` net loss from the discount to cost basis; the Chicago Hotel Loan repaid after closing | The portfolio exit and seller-side accounting are confirmed in a subsequent filing; this still does not reveal Athene's borrower-level cash collections |

## What remains open

The evidence does not identify the individual borrowers, property collateral,
loan-level repayment history, CECL adjustments by loan, Athene's cash payment
ledger, subsequent interest collections, losses, or the allocation of any
benefit to Apollo. The `$8.7B` transfer should therefore be graded as
`completed-related-party-sale-and-seller-cash-use-visible`, not as
`borrower-return-proven`.

The ARI proxy's purchase-agreement representations add a collateral-control
boundary: the seller represented that no collateral securing the loans had
been released except as scheduled, the loans were not cross-defaulted or
cross-collateralized, ARI or its subsidiaries owned the loan interests free of
liens other than permitted liens, and schedules existed for outstanding
principal, unfunded advances, and reserve deposits. These are transaction
integrity representations, not independent borrower-performance or cash-
collection evidence.

```text
ARI loan portfolio -> cash sale to Athene -> ARI debt repayment/residual cash
                                           -> Athene loan-level return       hold
                                           -> Apollo parent/common cash       hold
```
