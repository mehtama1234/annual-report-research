# Apollo–Athene ARI transaction cash-consideration boundary

Research date: `2026-09-15`

## Purpose

ARI's official closing announcement adds a transaction-level source between
the definitive agreement and the later seller cash-flow statement. It names
Athene as the buyer, identifies the portfolio sale as a cash transaction, and
states the expected post-debt, post-expense asset perimeter. This is stronger
than a generic related-party route, but it is not a payment-account or
Athene-to-Apollo receipt proof.

## Source-backed observations

ARI announced on April 24, 2026 that it had completed the sale of its
approximately `$9B` commercial real-estate loan portfolio to Athene Holding
Ltd. The same announcement states that, after repayment of financing
facilities, other indebtedness, and transaction expenses, ARI's total assets,
consisting primarily of cash, were expected to total approximately `$2.2B`.

The transaction materials define the consideration more precisely as `99.7%`
of total loan commitments, net of asset-specific CECL reserves, with two loans
having a combined principal balance of `$146M` expected to be repaid before
closing. The transaction summary also states that there were no financing
contingencies. These terms constrain the purchase-price object, but they do
not identify the final settlement amount, the Athene funding account, or the
allocation between purchase consideration and borrower repayments.

The later Q2 filing reports a broader `$9.497267B` line of commercial-loan
repayment and sale proceeds and `$1.239480B` of ending cash at June 30. Those
figures remain a separate seller cash-flow perimeter; they cannot be silently
substituted for the approximately `$9B` transaction consideration or for
Athene's post-close borrower collections.

## Proof-grade result

`buyer identity and transaction-level cash-consideration boundary confirmed;
payment account, borrower collections, buyer return, and Apollo common-owner
receipt remain unresolved`

## Remaining decisive evidence

- Athene payment ledger or closing settlement statement;
- allocation between purchase consideration and pre-/post-close borrower
  repayments;
- loan-level principal, interest, collateral, and impairment collections;
- Athene realized return and liability-cost allocation; and
- any Athene-to-AGM receipt and common-owner waterfall.
