# Apollo/Athene AP Aristotle historical wrapper-source refresh

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## New source

Apollo's March 31, 2022 Form 10-Q contains a historical AP Aristotle
concentration disclosure. It identifies `AP Aristotle Holdings LLC` in the
concentration table at approximately `$1.167B` and describes the amount as a
consolidated-variable-interest-entity investment in which an underlying
investment includes a single issuer exceeding Apollo's concentration
threshold. The same filing's investment discussion places AP Aristotle among
the named Athene-related or Apollo-managed investment exposures.

Primary source: [Apollo March 31, 2022 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1858681/000185868122000029/apo-20220331.htm).

## What it adds

The historical source improves the route from:

`AP Aristotle name -> Apollo/Athene-related consolidated VIE / concentration context`

to a more specific public legal-entity and wrapper observation. It is useful
context for the current Athene statutory CUSIP `00264#-AB-3`, whose 2025 rows
identify AP Aristotle Holdings LLC and show `$776.032348M` of cash-like
consideration candidates after excluding the `$6.588486M` tax-free exchange.

## What it does not add

The 2022 filing is not the 2025 Athene instrument and does not identify:

- CUSIP `00264#-AB-3` or a successor/old-security mapping;
- the 2025 `Paydown` or `Various` transactions;
- a paying agent, custodian, settlement account, or receipt date;
- borrower use of proceeds or liability release;
- Athene legal-entity allocation or liability cost; or
- Apollo parent or common-owner cash.

The `$1.167B` historical concentration must therefore not be added to the
2025 `$776.032348M` statutory candidate pool. The periods, instruments, and
perimeters are not reconciled.

## Updated route status

`historical-wrapper-context-strengthened; current-settlement-and-return-hold`

This is a useful source for the control-point map and source-acquisition
request, but the promotion test remains the same: current CUSIP/lot identity,
dated settlement evidence, borrower or issuer use, Athene cash allocation,
liability-cost treatment, and reproducible return.
