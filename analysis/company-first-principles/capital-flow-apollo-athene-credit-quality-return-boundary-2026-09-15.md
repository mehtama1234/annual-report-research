# Apollo–Athene credit-quality and return boundary

Date: `2026-09-15`

Athene's Q2 2026 Form 10-Q adds credit-quality evidence to the related-party
return bridge. The figures below are insurer- or portfolio-level disclosures;
they are not silently allocated to Apollo funds, ARI, Atlas, or the `$8.7B`
ARI portfolio purchase.

## Source-backed risk signals

| Signal | H1 / June 30 amount | Safe interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Related-party AFS securities | `$33.207B` amortized cost; `$33.078B` fair value; `$1M` allowance | Related-party AFS exposure and a small disclosed allowance are visible; asset-level return remains unjoined |
| Commercial mortgage loans 90+ days past due or non-accrual | `$1.027B` unpaid principal; `$695M` fair value | Portfolio credit stress is visible; no related-party allocation is proven |
| Mortgage instrument-specific credit-risk loss | `$44M` six-month loss | Aggregate mortgage credit-risk pressure is visible; it is not an ARI or Apollo-only loss |
| Intent-to-sell impairments | `$61M` six-month impairments | Realized/intent-to-sell impairment channel is visible; the filing attributes the increase primarily to a mortgage foreclosure, not to a named Apollo asset |
| AFS allowance movement | `$54M` decrease, including `$47M` income-statement impact | Allowance release and credit accounting affect reported income; cash and related-party attribution remain open |

## Return implication

```text
related-party exposure -> asset-level income / loss -> funding cost
  -> insurance capital and policyholder obligations -> upstream distribution
  -> Apollo common-owner cash
```

The filing now supplies portfolio-level stress tests for the middle of this
chain. It does not prove that Apollo-linked assets generated a particular net
return, that the ARI portfolio was performing, or that any related-party return
reached Apollo common owners.
