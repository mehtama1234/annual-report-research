# Apollo–Athene FY2025 Period-Matched Spread Boundary

Research date: `2026-09-15`

## Purpose

Close the period mismatch in the earlier named-route liability-cost
sensitivity without pretending that Apollo's segment reporting allocates cost
to an AMAPS or Concord holding.

## FY2025 segment screen

Apollo's FY2025 filing reports the following Athene Retirement Services
components, in millions of dollars:

| Component | FY2025 |
| --- | ---: |
| Fixed-income and other net investment income | `$13,021M` |
| Alternative investment income, net | `$1,299M` |
| Strategic capital-management income | `$131M` |
| Cost of funds | `$(10,083M)` |
| Mechanical pre-tax spread contribution | `$4,368M` |

The calculation is:

```text
13,021 + 1,299 + 131 - 10,083 = 4,368 ($M)
```

The filing also reports a FY2025 net investment spread of `1.61%`, down from
`1.78%` in 2024. This is a period-matched segment screen, not a statutory
asset-level return.

## What this improves

The named-route liability-cost work can now distinguish two questions:

1. Is the funding-cost burden period matched to the operating spread screen?
   Yes, at the FY2025 Athene Retirement Services segment level.
2. Can that burden be assigned to AMAPS, Concord, or a final Apollo common-
   owner residual? No. The segment screen does not provide asset-level
   duration, liability-block assignment, credit loss, capital charge, tax,
   custodian settlement, or parent-receipt attribution.

The FY2025 statutory statement remains a legal-entity perimeter. Its
`$12.733B` statutory net investment income and Schedule D holdings cannot be
subtracted mechanically from the segment cost-of-funds line to produce a
statutory return, even though both are FY2025 observations.

## Safe claim

`FY2025 Athene segment investment income, strategic-capital income, and cost
of funds form a period-matched $4.368B pre-tax spread screen; public evidence
still does not allocate that burden or spread to named statutory routes or
Apollo common-owner cash.`
