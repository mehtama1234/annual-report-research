# Apollo–Athene legal-dividend capacity boundary upgrade — 2026-09-15

## Question

Does Athene disclose a legally constrained, scheduled dividend path that can
serve as an intermediate link between Athene cash generation and Apollo parent
liquidity?

## Evidence

Athene's first-quarter 2026 Form 10-Q states that it intends to pay regular
common-stock dividends to its parent company of `$750M` per year, generally at
the end of each quarter. The filing also states that declaration and payment
remain solely at the board's discretion. It separately records a `$188M`
common-stock cash dividend declared on January 28, 2026, with a March 13 record
date and March 16 payment date.

The same filing states that insurer dividends are limited by applicable law and
regulation: ordinary dividends are subject to statutory calculations and prior
notification, while distributions above the statutory amount in a twelve-month
period are extraordinary dividends requiring regulator approval. It further
states that AHL did not then plan to have its US subsidiaries pay dividends to
their parents, while Bermuda subsidiary distributions remained subject to
capital-and-surplus and solvency conditions.

Source: [Athene Q1 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1527469/000152746926000028/ahl-20260331.htm).

## Interpretation boundary

This is a legal-distributability and scheduled-payment gate, not a receipt
gate. The `$188M` item is a dividend payable to AHL's common-stock holder; the
public filing set reviewed here does not join that payment to an Apollo HoldCo
bank receipt, unrestricted HoldCo cash, intercompany eliminations, or a
common-owner residual calculation. The `$750M` annual intent is a policy
reference, not a guaranteed cash yield.

## Next upgrade

Join AHL's dividend declaration/payment record to Apollo's parent cash-flow
statement, bank or intercompany ledger, and legal-entity ownership/receipt
mapping; then test the payment against unrestricted HoldCo cash, preferred and
NCI claims, debt service, taxes, and common-owner distributions.
