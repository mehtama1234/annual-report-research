# Apollo–Athene Q2 2026 Primary-Source Upgrade

Date: 2026-09-15

This memo records the Q2 2026 Form 10-Q upgrade to [Integrated Pilot 03](combined-investment-research-pilot-03-apollo-athene.md).

## Exact claim stack now visible

Apollo's June 30, 2026 Form 10-Q reports, in millions of USD:

- total assets of `494,048` and total liabilities of `452,566`;
- Apollo Global Management, Inc. stockholders' equity of `21,006`;
- noncontrolling interests of `20,476`;
- mandatory convertible preferred stock of `1,398`;
- asset-management cash of `3,415` plus `19` restricted;
- retirement-services cash of `21,957` plus `1,583` restricted;
- asset-management debt of `5,895` and retirement-services debt of `7,832`;
- six-month net cash provided by operating activities of `4,501`;
- six-month equity-based compensation of `478`, with `1.1B` of estimated
  unrecognized compensation expense related to unvested RSUs; and
- `2.0B` of net funded commitments by Apollo Capital Markets Partnership,
  funded through revolving and asset-based financing, while no capital had
  been funded by Apollo itself under that commitment as of June 30.

Athene's facilities were reported as having no amounts outstanding at June 30,
2026, and Athene was in compliance with its financial covenants. That is a
liquidity fact, not proof that policyholder, reinsurance, collateral, capital,
or credit risks are absent.

Athene's own six-month financing cash flow also discloses the entity boundary:

- deposits on investment-type policies and contracts of `41.617B USD`;
- withdrawals on those policies/contracts of `14.741B USD`;
- capital contributions from Apollo's parent of `42M USD`;
- capital contributions from noncontrolling interests of `271M USD`;
- capital contributions from NCI of consolidated VIEs of `759M USD`; and
- capital distributions to noncontrolling interests of `301M USD`.
- common stock dividends of `375M USD` and preferred stock dividends of
  `71M USD`;
- net financing cash flow of `27.801B USD`, driven primarily by policy deposits
  and withdrawals rather than parent-owner distributions; and
- ending cash and cash equivalents of `23.711B USD`, a figure that includes
  restricted cash and cash in consolidated VIEs.

These are genuine cash-transfer gates, but they do not create a parent-owner
cash number. They show that Athene required parent capital and distributed
capital to NCI while also moving very large policyholder balances. Apollo's
insurance subsidiaries are also subject to restrictions on dividends, so
consolidated Athene cash cannot be assumed distributable to the parent.

Athene also reports `70.616B USD` of related-party investments, or `15.0%` of
total assets, including `17.931B USD` in Apollo funds, `13.070B USD` in asset
origination platforms, and `4.206B USD` in retirement-services platforms. Its
investment in Athora was `3.191B USD` and Athene had `134M USD` of additional
Athora investment commitments. These holdings connect the origination engine
to the insurance balance sheet, but they also create concentration, valuation,
related-party, and capital-allocation tests.

The filing also supplies return-quality evidence:

- Athene incurred `785M USD` of management fees, including base,
  sub-allocation, and performance fees, to Apollo in the first half;
- net investment spread declined to `1.41%` from `1.62%` year over year;
- net investment earnings were `7.8B USD` and cost of funds was `5.7B USD` in
  the first half;
- Athene's net income available to its common stockholder was approximately
  negative `1.0B USD` for the first half; and
- income from origination platforms was affected by a valuation decrease on
  Atlas caused by an underlying asset impairment in 2026.

These facts improve the return test but do not prove fee cash collected by
Apollo or a clean return on the related-party assets. The `785M USD` is an
Athene expense/related-party fee disclosure, not automatically a parent cash
receipt.

## Owner-cash interpretation

The new filing evidence changes the SOTP haircut from an abstract reminder to a
documented attribution problem:

```text
consolidated assets and operating cash
  - policyholder and insurance liabilities
  - regulated / restricted cash
  - debt and financing
  - noncontrolling interests
  - mandatory convertible preferred stock
  - equity compensation and dilution
  - tax and required capital
= Apollo common-owner residual
```

The `4.501B USD` consolidated six-month operating cash figure must not be
substituted directly into the fee-platform valuation. It includes retirement
services, investment activity, policyholder-liability movements, and other
consolidated entities. The filing therefore strengthens the bridge while
confirming why the common-owner denominator remains unresolved.
