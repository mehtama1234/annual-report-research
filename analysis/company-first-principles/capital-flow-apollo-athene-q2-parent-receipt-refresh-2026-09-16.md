# Apollo–Athene Q2 parent-receipt source refresh

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This refresh rechecks the current Q2 2026 Apollo and Athene filings for the
missing Q-07 join: an Athene-originated distribution or intercompany transfer
that can be followed into an AGM parent-only receiving account and then through
the common-owner waterfall.

## Result

The result remains `searched-negative` for an AGM-only receipt ledger within
the checked public filing perimeter. Apollo's Q2 Form 10-Q states that AGM is
a holding company whose primary source of funds is distributions and other
intercompany transfers from AAM and AHL. It also reports `$25.4B` of
consolidated unrestricted cash and other available facilities. Those are
funding-source and consolidated-liquidity disclosures, not a dated Athene-to-
AGM cash receipt or unrestricted parent-only balance.

Athene's Q2 Form 10-Q continues to provide the source-side observation of
`$32M` of distributions to parent in Q2 and `$110M` in H1. The filings do not
join those amounts to an AGM bank account, an AGM parent-only cash-flow line,
an intercompany elimination, or a residual after preferred claims, debt,
taxes, operating costs, dilution, and common dividends.

The same current-period cash-flow statement separately reports `$42M` of
capital contributions from parent for the six months ended June 30, 2026.
That is a parent-to-Athene financing line, not evidence of an Athene-to-AGM
receipt. The statement of equity also presents contributions from and
distributions to parent as a combined equity movement, but neither display
provides the AGM receiving account, transfer date, source entity, or
intercompany elimination needed to join the `$110M` source-side observation to
AGM cash.

The same Athene filing adds a legal-availability boundary below AHL: AHL
describes subsidiary dividends, capital-market issuances, and intercompany
borrowings as its primary cash sources, but states that it does not currently
plan for its U.S. insurance subsidiaries to pay dividends to their parents.
Bermuda insurance-subsidiary distributions are subject to statutory, board,
regulatory, surplus, and financial-strength-rating constraints. This narrows
the plausible upstream route into AHL, but it does not identify which entity
generated the `$110M`, establish an AGM receipt, or make consolidated cash
available to common owners.

The Apollo Q2 Form 10-Q is now routed through the official SEC filing as well
as the investor-relations mirror. The SEC route confirms the same source
perimeter and does not add an AGM-only receiving account or parent-only cash
flow line; the result therefore remains `searched-negative` rather than
promotion-ready.

## Current liquidity and facility control

The same Q2 filing reports `$25.4B` of consolidated unrestricted cash and
cash equivalents and `$5.6B` of available funds across the AGM credit
facility, Athene credit facility, and Athene liquidity facility. It also
reports no amounts outstanding under the current or previous Athene credit
facilities at June 30, 2026. These figures improve the current liquidity and
senior-claim perimeter, but they do not identify the legal owner of the cash,
trace the `$110M` Athene distribution into an AGM account, or prove that
facility availability was available to common owners.

Keep the objects separate:

`Athene distribution -> AGM receipt (unproven) -> consolidated liquidity (observed) -> senior claims/common residual (unproven)`

## Boundary preserved

The refresh therefore does not add or promote any amount. The following remain
non-additive measurement layers:

- Athene's `$110M` H1 distribution-to-parent observation;
- Apollo's consolidated `$25.4B` unrestricted-cash balance;
- Apollo's statement that distributions and intercompany transfers are primary
  HoldCo funding sources; and
- AHL's stated subsidiary-dividend source and the absence of a current plan for
  U.S. insurance subsidiaries to pay dividends to their parents, with Bermuda
  distribution limits preserved as legal-availability constraints; and
- the existing `0–100%` attribution frontier.

The next promotion-ready object remains a dated Athene-to-AGM transfer or
parent-only cash-flow/bank ledger that reconciles to AGM cash, legal-entity
eliminations, restrictions, and the common-owner claim.

## August 11 corporate-structure filing boundary

Athene's August 11, 2026 Form 8-K says that Athene made an “Overview of
Athene's Corporate Structure” presentation available on its investor-relations
site. The homepage now exposes an official summary of the linked presentation:
more than `$470B` of total assets, `$37B` of regulatory capital, `$79B` of
available liquidity, and `$6.1B` of deployable capital as of June 30, 2026.
These are Athene-level context figures; they do not disclose a distribution
schedule, receiving account, or Athene-to-AGM cash receipt. The detailed
boundary is preserved in the [corporate-structure presentation memo](capital-flow-apollo-athene-corporate-structure-presentation-boundary-2026-09-16.md)
and remains `evidence-insufficient` for Q-07 promotion.

## Live SEC-source recheck — 2026-09-17

A fresh search of the official Apollo Q2 Form 10-Q, Athene Q2 Form 10-Q, and
the related parent-only filing route found no newer public AGM receiving-account
or elimination record. The SEC filings continue to expose the source-side
Athene distribution observation and Apollo's general statement that
distributions and intercompany transfers fund the HoldCo, but not a dated
Athene-to-AGM bank receipt or parent-only cash-flow join.

The Q-07 result therefore remains `searched-negative` for the checked public
perimeter. The next promotion object is still a dated receipt, intercompany
elimination, or parent-only Schedule I/cash ledger that reconciles source,
destination, restrictions, senior claims, and common-owner residual. No
consolidated cash balance or source-side distribution is added to the
common-owner denominator by inference.
