## Question

`Can we prove the actual cash movement from Ares-managed or co-lender capital into Frontline, through Bain's acquisition/growth use, and back through borrower debt service?`

## Short Answer

`Not yet. Ares/Frontline is still the strongest private-credit borrower marker, not facility cash waterfall proof. Public sources show Ares as lead arranger/bookrunner for a senior secured credit facility supporting Bain Capital's acquisition of Frontline in Q1 2025, and later supporting Bain's continued growth plans for Frontline in Q2 2026. Local SEC holder schedules show a controlled 198.675M USD Q2 2026 fair-value lower bound, a broader 622.824M USD same-period public holder view plus 130.796M USD of reported unfunded commitments, 76.6927M USD of Q1 2026 Ares-managed holder context, and 54.300M USD of the prior mixed-period visible commitment trail. The missing proof remains total facility size, Ares-funded allocation, lender schedule, borrower legal structure, collateral package, proceeds flow, prior debt payoff, borrower EBITDA/free cash flow, interest/principal cash receipts, and repayment waterfall.`

## Public Route Checked

This pass checked the public route around the private transaction:

- Ares Q1 `2025` U.S. direct-lending origination activity: Ares served as lead arranger and bookrunner for a senior secured credit facility supporting Bain Capital's acquisition of Frontline Road Safety Holdings.
- Ares Q2 `2026` U.S. direct-lending origination activity: Ares served as lead arranger and bookrunner for a senior secured credit facility supporting Bain's continued growth plans for Frontline.
- Bain Capital January `2025` announcement: Bain agreed to acquire Frontline from The Sterling Group; Frontline had completed `19` acquisitions.
- Sterling March `2025` sale completion release: sale to Bain completed; financial terms were not disclosed.
- Kirkland transaction release: Bain legal-adviser context; financial terms not disclosed.
- Frontline public site and public company profiles: operating borrower context, not facility economics.

## What Would Upgrade Ares/Frontline

The case upgrades from borrower marker to named cash proof only if a source joins:

1. borrower and guarantor legal entities
2. total facility size by tranche
3. administrative agent and full lender allocation schedule
4. Ares-managed funded allocation by vehicle
5. proceeds-use schedule for acquisition consideration, refinancing, fees, working capital, add-on M&A, dividend, or capex
6. closing funds-flow memorandum or settlement statement
7. collateral/security package and UCC records
8. borrower EBITDA, free cash flow, leverage, and debt-service coverage
9. interest and principal receipts by lender or vehicle
10. repayment, payoff, refinancing, or valuation recovery evidence

## Decision

`ares-frontline-facility-cash-waterfall-bridge-hold-with-acquisition-facility-role-visible`

Ares/Frontline improves from broad continued-growth context to a stronger acquisition-facility role because the Q1 `2025` public Ares release ties the senior secured credit facility to Bain's acquisition of Frontline. It still does not cross into facility cash waterfall proof.

## Safe Claim

`Ares/Frontline has strong private-credit borrower-marker evidence. Public Ares releases identify Ares as lead arranger and bookrunner for a senior secured credit facility supporting Bain Capital's acquisition of Frontline in Q1 2025 and Bain's continued growth plans for Frontline in Q2 2026. Local SEC holder schedules show a controlled 198.675M USD Q2 2026 fair-value lower bound, a 622.824M USD same-period public breadth view plus 130.796M USD of reported unfunded commitments, 76.6927M USD of Q1 2026 Ares-managed holder context, first-lien senior secured loan markers, SOFR plus 4.75%-5.00% pricing context, and a March 2032 maturity cluster. Current evidence still does not prove total facility size, Ares-funded allocation within the named facility, full lender group, use-of-proceeds funds-flow, borrower cash generation, debt-service coverage, repayment, payoff, collateral package, covenant terms, or bank replacement.`
