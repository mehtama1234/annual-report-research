The selected borrower list is not a random software-only story.

It touches:

| Destination Category | Visible Examples | Why It Matters |

| Industrial and equipment | AeriTek, Precinmac | Private credit reaches physical manufacturing and specialty equipment. |
| Infrastructure-adjacent services | Atwell, Frontline Road Safety, Valcourt | Capital is funding service layers around power, digital infrastructure, roads, and buildings. |

## Claim From Evidence

The safe evidence-led claim is:

`Ares' Q2 2026 selected direct-lending examples show borrower-facing private credit flowing into acquisition financing, M&A strategies, and growth plans across industrial equipment, infrastructure services, automotive services, wealth management, insurance brokerage, aerospace MRO, building maintenance, and music IP.`

This strengthens the real-economy destination claim.

It does not yet strengthen the broad bank-displacement claim.

## What It Does Not Prove

| Overclaim | Why Not |
|---|---|
| Ares replaced banks in each selected transaction. | The release identifies Ares' role and borrower purpose, but not prior facilities, use of proceeds, bank repayment, or post-close bank roles. |
| The full `8.2B USD` went into the ten selected borrowers. | The selected examples are illustrative; the release says the full Q2 amount covered `69` transactions. |
| These are all productive capex investments. | Some are acquisition financings or M&A strategies; use-of-proceeds documents are needed to separate capex, acquisition finance, refinancing, and working capital. |
| This is insurance-liability-funded credit. | Ares is treated here as a direct-lending platform case, not an owned-insurance-liability case. |

## Simple Version

Ares shows where private credit is going.

It is not just software.

The named Q2 borrowers include refrigeration equipment, power and digital-infrastructure engineering, road safety, automotive service, wealth management, precision manufacturing, insurance brokerage, aerospace MRO, and building maintenance.

So the clean claim is:

`Ares is routing private credit into sponsor-backed operating companies across real-economy service and industrial lanes.`

The careful caveat is:

`We still need borrower documents before saying those loans replaced banks or funded specific capex projects.`
