# Capital Flow Auctane Post-Close Bank Role Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass resolves queue item `CFARQ-002` as far as current public/local evidence allows.

The question is:

`Did private credit only terminate Stamps.com's prior bank-led credit facility, or can we prove banks had no post-close role after the Auctane / Stamps.com acquisition financing?`

Supporting sources:

## Resolution Outcome

`hold-after-pass-1 - confirmed bank-facility termination, post-close ancillary bank role unresolved`

This is a stronger outcome than Atwell because the prior bank facility termination is primary-source proven. It is still not a full bank-exclusion outcome.

## What Passed

| Evidence | Status | What It Proves |
|---|---|---|
| SEC proxy debt-use language | passed | The merger financing was expected to fund the amounts needed to terminate Stamps.com's existing credit facility. |
| SEC closing 8-K | passed | On October `5`, `2021`, Stamps.com terminated the credit agreement with Wells Fargo, JPMorgan Chase Bank, Bank of America, other lenders, and Wells Fargo as administrative agent, including outstanding obligations and security interests. |
| Private-credit financing source | passed | Paul Hastings identifies Blackstone Credit, Ares-managed credit funds, PSP Investments, and Thoma Bravo Credit in the Stamps.com acquisition debt financing. |
| Unitranche/direct-lending characterization | passed | Public market coverage describes the loan as a unitranche/direct-lending financing made directly through credit arms rather than bank-arranged. |
| Transaction completion | passed | Stamps.com was acquired by Thoma Bravo and became Auctane after closing. |

## What Did Not Pass

| Missing Evidence | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Final credit agreement | Would show all lenders, agents, revolver providers, letter-of-credit providers, collateral agents, and any bank roles. |
| Funds-flow or payoff materials | Would show exact repayment amounts and whether any old facility component rolled into the new capital structure. |
| Post-close lender list | Would show whether any banks participated in the new unitranche, revolver, or ancillary services. |
| Treasury, hedging, custody, or cash-management map | Would show whether banks remained commercially important even after the credit facility was terminated. |
| Later Auctane financing documents | Would separate the 2021 Stamps.com take-private from any later Auctane/WWEX refinancing, merger, or revolver package. |

## Current Safe Answer

`Auctane / Stamps.com is a confirmed bank-facility takeout case, but not a confirmed full bank-exclusion case.`

The defensible sentence is:

`Private credit financed Thoma Bravo's acquisition of Stamps.com through a large direct-lending/unitranche package, and the SEC closing 8-K confirms that Stamps.com's prior Wells Fargo-administered credit agreement with bank lenders was terminated at closing. Current evidence does not prove whether banks retained any post-close revolver, agency, treasury, hedging, custody, or other ancillary role.`

## Why This Matters

Auctane is the best current example for the narrow bank-takeout sentence.

It should anchor this claim:

`Private credit can take out named bank-led credit facilities in specific borrower transactions.`

It should not anchor this stronger claim:

`Private credit fully replaced banks across the borrower relationship.`

The difference matters because banks can lose the term-loan or old credit-agreement role while still retaining cash-management, treasury, hedging, collateral, revolving-credit, or other ancillary relationships.
