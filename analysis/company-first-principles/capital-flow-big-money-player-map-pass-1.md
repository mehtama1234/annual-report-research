## What The Map Tells Us

The big players are not all doing the same thing.

BlackRock is the broadest routing system. It controls public-market implementation, portfolio workflow, ETF flows, and expanding private-market access. Its core proof problem is not whether it is large. The proof problem is finding named BlackRock/HPS or infrastructure-linked cases where a vehicle can be tied to borrower/project cash.

Blackstone is the strongest private-market product platform. The evidence shows large Credit & Insurance scale, inflows, deployment, insurance-client SMAs, direct lending, and infrastructure/ABF lanes. Its proof problem is moving from channel-level deployment to vehicle-to-borrower or project-level cash.

Apollo/Athene is the cleanest retirement/insurance-liability-to-credit engine. The evidence shows retirement-services inflows, Athene-related invested assets, direct-origination and ABF scale, and spread-related earnings. Its proof problem is statutory: legal-entity holdings, investment income, credit quality, and liability-cost spread.

KKR/Global Atlantic is a multi-engine version of the same insurance/private-market story. It combines insurance, private credit, ABF, infrastructure, private equity, and strategic capital. Its proof problem is similar to Apollo but also includes borrower allocation and private-placement detail.

Brookfield is the real-asset/infrastructure operating-control platform. It is most relevant where capital needs hard assets: power, renewables, real estate, operating infrastructure, and AI-linked infrastructure. Its proof problem is asset/project cash: source/use, customer receipts, debt waterfall, and return.

Ares is the most borrower-visible private-credit platform in the current local evidence because the Ares lane already contains named borrower-holder schedules and Frontline arranger evidence. Its proof problem is moving from holder fair value and arranger role to actual facility cash, borrower use, repayment, and bank role.

Carlyle is important but currently less proven in the local named-cash stack. Its strongest likely lanes are private equity, global credit, secondaries, AlpInvest, and continuation/liquidity structures. Its proof problem is selecting one named deal or fund cash-flow case strong enough to chase.

## What We Can Safely Say

`The large capital platforms are best understood as routing systems. BlackRock, Blackstone, Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic, Brookfield, Ares, and Carlyle draw capital from different source pools and route it through different wrappers into public markets, private credit, insurance assets, infrastructure, real estate, power, data centers, private companies, secondaries, and wealth alternatives. The current evidence supports a big-money-player map and selected bridge claims, but not full named cash-loop proof for each platform.`

## What We Should Not Say

Do not say:

1. every platform dollar can be traced to a named borrower or asset
2. BlackRock's platform scale proves HPS/private-credit cash realization
3. Blackstone Credit & Insurance AUM proves borrower repayments
4. Apollo/Athene spread earnings prove statutory asset-level cash returns
5. KKR/Global Atlantic insurance assets prove borrower-level use and payback
6. Brookfield infrastructure exposure proves project cash return
7. Ares holder fair value proves full facility size or borrower cash
8. Carlyle platform flows prove secondaries or credit cash outcomes
