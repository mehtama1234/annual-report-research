# Capital Flow Big-Picture Remaining Work Map

## Purpose

This page answers the big-picture question:

`What is still left to prove across the whole capital-flow thesis?`

The operating table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-big-picture-remaining-work-map.csv`

## The Full Chain

The whole research program is trying to prove this chain:

`capital source -> funding vehicle -> company -> asset/project/backlog -> operating output -> cash/return -> durability -> safe claim`

We have evidence for parts of the chain.

We do not yet have the whole chain mapped across companies and sectors.

## What We Know So Far

The current evidence says capital is not moving through one simple channel.

It is moving through several different systems:

| System | Current Read |
|---|---|
| Private credit | Alternative managers and BDCs route capital into named borrowers and sponsor-backed industries, but broad bank replacement is not proven. |
| Insurance and retirement capital | Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic, Brookfield, and Blackstone show real insurance/retirement-linked credit supply, but statutory quality and borrower-level destination proof remain incomplete. |
| Power and grid | Some load, generation, tariff, and regulated-capital evidence has moved into filings and dockets, but project status and recovery mechanisms still need source-by-source tracking. |
| Energy infrastructure | ET and Cheniere show real midstream/LNG funding, throughput, cargo, and debt evidence, but project source-and-use and customer economics are not fully mapped. |
| Industrial buildout | URI and Sterling show two different operating channels: URI now has time-series fleet-access capital absorption plus first collateralized funding support, while Sterling has customer-project backlog execution plus guarantee-capacity support. |
| Funding wrappers | Funding type appears to follow asset type, but that remains a working model until more companies are classified with proof. |

## What Remains

### 1. Capital Source

Question:

`Whose money is entering the system?`

Remaining work:

- separate banks, private credit, insurers, public bonds, equity, customer prepayments, ratepayers, government funds, and project sponsors
- map each source to the vehicle that carries it
- avoid treating all credit as the same source

Why it matters:

`A private-credit dollar, a customer prepayment, a bond-market dollar, and a ratepayer-recovered utility dollar mean different things.`

### 2. Funding Vehicle

Question:

`What wrapper carries the money?`

Remaining work:

- classify corporate revolvers, commercial paper, senior notes, secured notes, entity debt, ABL, AR securitization, private-credit loans, L/Cs, surety support, regulated recovery, and customer advances
- tie each wrapper to collateral, covenant, cash-flow, legal-entity, or recovery logic

Why it matters:

`The wrapper tells us what lenders, investors, customers, or regulators believe can be underwritten.`

### 3. Destination Company

Question:

`Which companies actually receive or route capital?`

Remaining work:

- assign each company a role: source, router, vehicle, borrower, owner, operator, supplier, contractor, or regulated recovery platform
- apply this to the full `519` company triage universe

Why it matters:

`Blackstone, Ares, Cheniere, United Rentals, Sterling, and Duke are not doing the same job in the chain.`

### 4. Asset Or Project

Question:

`What real thing is funded?`

Remaining work:

- identify actual LNG trains, cargoes, pipelines, fleets, data centers, utility load, rate-base assets, public works, backlog, and customer projects
- avoid vague sector labels when source documents give real assets or project metrics

Why it matters:

`The thesis gets stronger when capital can be tied to physical capacity, contract obligations, or operating systems.`

### 5. Use Of Proceeds

Question:

`What did the money do?`

Remaining work:

- separate acquisition finance, refinancing, capex, working capital, dividend recap, rescue capital, project construction, debt repayment, L/C support, and surety support

Why it matters:

`Refinancing old debt is not the same as building new capacity.`

### 6. Operating Conversion

Question:

`Did the funding become real output?`

Remaining work:

- track MW, cargoes, volumes, fleet OEC, utilization, backlog burn, RPO conversion, revenue, margin, OCF, FCF, and rate-base growth
- build repeatable conversion scorecards by lane

Why it matters:

`Money matters only if it becomes capacity, cash flow, control, or durable earnings power.`

### 7. Cash And Return

Question:

`Who earns cash, and what risk is visible?`

Remaining work:

- measure margins, returns, spread earnings, interest cost, covenant burden, impairment risk, rating quality, and project economics

Why it matters:

`A capital-flow beneficiary can still be a poor investment if returns, risk, or cash conversion are weak.`

### 8. Durability

Question:

`Is this a trend or a snapshot?`

Remaining work:

- build eight-quarter or multi-year time series for every major claim
- start with Sterling contract capital/backlog conversion
- then repeat for URI fleet conversion, Cheniere cargo/debt/capex conversion, utility load/rate-base conversion, and private-credit borrower flow

Why it matters:

`A strong quarter is not a thesis.`

### 9. Outside Denominator

Question:

`Is the company evidence large relative to the market?`

Remaining work:

- match company data to FRED, FDIC, EIA, DOE, FERC, ISO, Census, BLS, state utility commissions, and public procurement datasets

Why it matters:

`Outside denominators stop us from turning isolated company anecdotes into macro claims.`

### 10. Disproof Tests

Question:

`What would make the thesis wrong?`

Remaining work:

- define disproof triggers for each lane
- examples: bank credit still growing in the same lane, backlog not converting, projects cancelled, capex mostly maintenance, poor cash conversion, low utilization, low-margin acquired backlog, or weak statutory credit quality

Why it matters:

`A real research system should be able to prove itself wrong.`

### 11. Claim Governance

Question:

`What can we safely say today?`

Remaining work:

- maintain promoted claims and forbidden overclaims
- keep each claim tied to status: `queued`, `filed`, `approved`, `financed`, `cash-converting`, `trend-proven`, or `thesis-grade`

Why it matters:

`The writing should follow the evidence level, not the other way around.`

### 12. Scale-Up System

Question:

`How do we extend this across the full company universe?`

Remaining work:

- convert the method into repeatable extraction templates
- run it across the `519` company triage universe by sector, theme, and proof priority

Why it matters:

`The end state is not a Sterling case study. It is a capital-flow operating system.`

## The Cleanest Current Thesis

The clean version is:

`Capital is flowing through different wrappers because the underlying economic claims are different. Some capital funds corporate platforms, some funds legally separable assets, some funds collateral pools, some funds regulated rate-base assets, some funds customer projects, and some funds sponsor-backed acquisitions or refinancings. The research job is to prove each link before treating the broad thesis as fact.`

## What Comes Next

The next concrete work should be:

1. Sterling acquired-backlog conversion, actual post-reset L/C usage, bonded backlog, and project-margin evidence after the completed eight-quarter, acquired-versus-organic, and L/C/surety boundary passes.
2. Cross-company funding-wrapper classification for ET, Cheniere, URI, Sterling, utilities, and private-credit borrowers.
3. Outside-denominator master map.
4. Company-role taxonomy for the `519` company universe.
5. Repeat the durability time-series method for the next high-priority lane.

## Simple Version

We are not just asking:

`Where is money going?`

We are asking:

`Whose money is it, what wrapper carries it, what asset or company receives it, what output it creates, who earns the cash, whether it repeats, and what claim we are allowed to make.`
