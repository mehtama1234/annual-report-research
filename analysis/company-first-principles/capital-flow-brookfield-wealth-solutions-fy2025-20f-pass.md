## Purpose

The FY2025 20-F adds the audited annual balance-sheet scale, liability base, commitments, reinsurance context, and acquisition buildout that sit behind that channel.

## Claim Boundary

This pass does not prove that FY2025 policyholder liabilities directly funded a named private-credit loan.

It proves something narrower:

`Brookfield Wealth Solutions has a large annual insurance balance sheet, a large policyholder liability base, a large investment book, and a disclosed forward investment commitment stack. The Q2 2026 supplement then tells us a meaningful share of the investment portfolio is private credit.`

The next layer needs statutory schedules, rating-agency reports, portfolio-holding detail, or borrower-level disclosures.

## Simple Version

Brookfield is not just saying "we manage assets."

The filings show a real insurance balance sheet:

`157.181B USD` of assets, `114.833B USD` of investments, `92.992B USD` of policyholder account balances, and `12.3B USD` of unfunded investment commitments.

Then the Q2 2026 supplement shows the portfolio mix:

`33%` private credit on `167.297B USD` of invested insurance assets, implying about `55.208B USD` of private-credit exposure.

So the simple claim is:

`Brookfield has a large insurance-funded investment machine, and a meaningful part of that machine is allocated to private credit.`

The careful caveat is:

`We still need holdings, statutory schedules, and credit-quality data before saying exactly which borrowers got the money or how risky the book is.`
