# Capital Flow Capital-Intensive Buildout Ledger Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass moves the capital-intensive buildout lane from queue logic into company-packet evidence.

The operating table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-capital-intensive-buildout-ledger-pass-1.csv`

The main question is:

`Where is the real economy consuming capital, and what kind of physical or operating capacity is that capital becoming?`

## What Changed

The capital-intensive buildout row in the cross-theme dashboard can now move from `queued` to `filed`.

The lane now has `15` packet-derived rows across six subthemes:

| Subtheme | Companies | What The Evidence Is Trying To Prove |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering and project delivery | Jacobs, AECOM, KBR | Funded public, industrial, defense, water, transport, data-center, and advanced-manufacturing programs require design, advisory, program-management, and execution capacity. |
| Specialty construction | Sterling Infrastructure | Digital and industrial capital is becoming site work, utility work, electrical integration, and mission-critical backlog. |
| Materials and fabrication | Steel Dynamics, Nucor, Knife River, Reliance Steel & Aluminum | Infrastructure and reindustrialization capital becomes steel, fabrication, aggregates, processing capacity, inventory, throughput, and local material delivery. |
| Electrical and power equipment | Eaton, Teledyne | Electrification, AI/data centers, aerospace, defense, sensing, and resilience demand require physical equipment, instrumentation, and capacity investment. |
| Energy infrastructure | Energy Transfer, Cheniere, ONE Gas | Energy security and reliability demand becomes pipelines, export capacity, fractionation, LNG trains, gas-network replacement, and regulated capital. |
| Access and equipment utilization | United Rentals | Customers can consume project capacity by renting fleet access rather than owning equipment. |

## Simple Read

In simple words:

`Money is not only going into financial products. It is being absorbed by companies that turn capital into roads, gas networks, LNG trains, electrical gear, steel, aggregates, construction sites, rental fleet, design work, and project execution.`

The stronger conclusion is:

`The real bottleneck is often not the availability of a financial label. It is whether the operating system has enough equipment, labor, engineering, materials, permits, energy infrastructure, and working capital to turn committed money into usable assets.`

## What This Lets Us Say

The capital-intensive lane is not one thing. It has at least six different economic shapes:

- `Backlog businesses`: Jacobs, AECOM, KBR, and Sterling show demand as awards, backlog, future-phase work, and book-to-burn.
- `Throughput businesses`: Steel Dynamics, Nucor, Reliance, Knife River, Energy Transfer, and Cheniere show demand as tons, cargoes, bpd, mtpa, reserves, and project volume.
- `Capacity-investment businesses`: Eaton, Nucor, Energy Transfer, Cheniere, United Rentals, and ONE Gas show direct capital deployment into capacity, network, fleet, or regulated asset systems.
- `Working-capital businesses`: Reliance, Knife River, AECOM, KBR, and United Rentals show that cash timing, inventory, receivables, DSO, project claims, and fleet positioning matter as much as headline demand.
- `Regulated or contracted infrastructure businesses`: ONE Gas, Cheniere, Energy Transfer, Verizon, and parts of AECOM/KBR require contract, regulatory, or tariff evidence before the cash-flow claim is strong.
- `Operating bottleneck businesses`: Sterling, Jacobs, AECOM, United Rentals, Eaton, and Knife River show that capital can be available but still bottlenecked by labor, equipment, engineering capacity, materials, permitting, and execution.

## What This Does Not Yet Prove

This pass does not prove:

- every capex dollar is growth capex
- every backlog dollar is funded or profitable
- every capacity addition earns the expected return
- every project is externally financed
- every demand driver is structural rather than cyclical
- all AI, electrification, industrial-policy, or energy-security language has converted into in-service assets

## Next Source Work

The next status is `project-and-cash-grade`.

To reach it, the next pass should extract:

- growth versus maintenance capex
- project schedules and in-service dates
- funded backlog versus total backlog
- contract type and cancellability
- customer funding source
- utilization, OEC, fleet productivity, tons, bpd, cargoes, mtpa, and throughput
- operating cash flow, free cash flow, working-capital bridge, DSO, inventory, and project claims
- debt, revolvers, maturities, credit agreements, project debt, and rate recovery
- acquisition funding and purchase accounting where acquisitions changed capacity

## Bottom Line

The evidence now supports a bounded claim:

`Capital is visibly being absorbed by companies that convert funded demand into physical capacity, project execution, throughput, fleet access, materials, engineering, electrical equipment, energy infrastructure, telecom networks, and regulated replacement assets.`

The evidence does not yet support the stronger claim:

`All of that capital is new growth investment, fully funded, high-return, non-cyclical, and already in service.`
