## What Changed

The prior energy pass answered:

`What is visible in Q2 2026?`

This pass answers:

`What was already true at the 2025 annual close, and how does that change the claim?`

The annual baseline makes the energy subthemes more concrete:

- ET is not just a growth-capex story; it is a capital-reallocation story.
- Cheniere is not just an LNG-train story; it is a contracted-production and capital-allocation story.
- Both companies have large debt and liquidity structures that make project funding visible at company level but not yet proven at project level.

## Energy Transfer Subthemes

### 1. Cash Generation Baseline

Energy Transfer entered 2026 with Q4 `2025` adjusted EBITDA of `4.18B USD` and Q4 DCF attributable to partners, as adjusted, of `2.04B USD`.

The FY `2025` 10-K also supports `10.15B USD` of cash provided by operating activities.

Simple claim:

`ET had enough current cash generation to keep funding a large capital program, but the source still does not prove individual project returns.`

### 2. Growth Versus Maintenance Baseline

The annual close gives a cleaner comparison to Q2 `2026`.

ET source rows now support:

- Q4 `2025` growth capex of `1.40B USD`
- Q4 `2025` maintenance capex of `355M USD`
- FY `2025` growth capex of `5.250B USD`, excluding Sunoco LP and USAC
- FY `2025` maintenance capex of `1.150B USD`, excluding Sunoco LP and USAC
- initial FY `2026` growth-capex guide of `5.0B-5.5B USD`
- Q2 `2026` growth-capex guide later raised to `5.6B-5.9B USD`

Simple claim:

`ET's 2026 buildout did not start from nowhere. It followed a 2025 run-rate where growth capex was already several times maintenance capex.`

Boundary:

`This still needs project-level capex allocation; annual growth capex is not automatically Nederland, Lone Star Express, or natural gas pipeline expansion.`

### 3. Capital Reallocation

The most important ET annual finding is not just a number.

ET suspended development of the Lake Charles LNG export project in December `2025` to focus capital on a backlog of natural gas pipeline infrastructure projects that management described as having superior risk/return profiles.

The FY `2025` 10-K also records a `256M USD` fixed-asset impairment tied to that suspension.

Simple claim:

`ET is showing capital discipline inside energy infrastructure: one LNG export project was stopped, and capital attention shifted toward pipeline projects.`

Boundary:

`The source proves the decision and accounting impact. It does not prove the substitute projects have superior realized returns.`

### 4. Funding Baseline

At year-end `2025`, ET had:

- cash and equivalents of `1.27B USD`
- revolver availability of `2.12B USD`
- long-term debt less current maturities of `68.308B USD`

Simple claim:

`The funding base is large and debt-heavy. ET can fund buildout through operating cash, debt markets, and revolver capacity, but project-specific source-of-funds is still unresolved.`

## Cheniere Subthemes

### 1. LNG Cash Baseline

Cheniere's FY `2025` annual-results exhibit supports:

- revenue of `19.98B USD`
- consolidated adjusted EBITDA of `6.94B USD`
- DCF of `5.29B USD`
- `670` cargoes exported
- `2424 TBtu` of LNG exported volumes

Simple claim:

`Cheniere's Q2 2026 cash numbers sit on a large annual LNG cash-generation base, not a one-quarter anomaly.`

Boundary:

`Revenue, EBITDA, DCF, and cargoes are still company-level or platform-level metrics unless contract mix and train-level costs are mapped.`

### 2. Contracted Production

The FY `2025` 10-K says Cheniere had about `90%` of anticipated SPL and CCL production contracted under SPAs and IPM agreements through the mid-`2030s`, with about `15` years of weighted-average remaining life at December 31 `2025`.

Simple claim:

`This is a stronger demand signal than generic LNG enthusiasm: a large share of anticipated production was already contracted for long duration.`

Boundary:

`The 90% figure excludes some shorter-term and conditional volumes, and it does not disclose all customer price terms or credit support.`

### 3. Project Execution

Cheniere's baseline source rows support:

- expected total liquefaction capacity of more than `60 mtpa`
- more than `9 mtpa` under construction at year-end `2025`
- CCL Stage 3 Trains 1-4 reaching substantial completion during `2025`
- CCL Stage 3 at `94.1%` completion at December 31 `2025`
- CCL Midscale Trains 8 and 9 at `31.8%` completion at December 31 `2025`
- CCL Midscale Trains 8 and 9 expected capacity of about `5 mtpa`

Simple claim:

`Cheniere is a capacity-conversion case: contracted demand and project finance are being turned into train completions and cargo throughput.`

Boundary:

`Completion percentages need remaining cost, debt draw, EPC terms, and in-service confirmation before they prove project economics.`

### 4. Customer Contract Evidence

Cheniere disclosed a new long-term SPA with CPC Corporation, Taiwan, for up to `1.2 mtpa` of LNG from `2026` through `2050`.

Simple claim:

`The LNG buildout is backed by named long-duration customer demand, not only spot-market expectations.`

Boundary:

`The public release does not provide full pricing, take-or-pay, cancellation, destination, or credit-support terms.`

### 5. Capital Deployment And Funding Baseline

Cheniere's FY `2025` annual-results and 10-K source rows support:

- `6.1B USD` deployed in `2025` across growth, balance-sheet management, and shareholder returns
- `2.6B USD` of growth capital invested in `2025`
- `2.3B USD` of growth capital funded with equity
- `7.2B USD` of available credit-facility commitments at year-end `2025`
- `23.0B USD` of total debt outstanding before unamortized discount and debt issuance costs

Simple claim:

`Cheniere's funding model is not one clean bucket. Growth capex, equity funding, debt, debt repayment, dividends, and buybacks all coexist.`

Boundary:

`Capital deployment should not be counted as pure infrastructure investment; it includes shareholder returns and balance-sheet actions.`

## What We Are Saying Now

The energy buildout claim is now stronger and more specific:

`Capital is flowing into energy infrastructure where bottlenecks are visible and measurable: natural gas pipelines, NGL export capacity, fractionation, Permian takeaway, LNG liquefaction trains, marine loading, and long-duration customer commitments. The best current evidence ties annual cash generation, growth capex, contracts, physical capacity, construction milestones, debt/liquidity, and operating throughput into one company-level picture.`

But the claim is still bounded:

`We have not yet proven project-level return, exact source-of-funds, or sector-wide denominator scale.`
