## Why This Is The Next Goal

The research can now prove several important things:

- private credit reaches named real-economy borrowers
- BDC and private-credit vehicle stacks can be classified
- URI converts fleet capital into repeated rental revenue and cash-flow proxies
- Sterling converts backlog and acquisitions through a revolver, L/C, surety, and working-capital wrapper
- Cheniere ties contracted production and entity debt to physical cargo output
- FPL Distribution Inspection ties physical work, category cost, plant additions, final recovery, equity/debt/depreciation components, aggregate clause revenue, factor authority, and Form 8A capital-structure support

The harder question is whether those mechanisms become cash at the asset, category, borrower, project, or customer level.

## Current Answer

`The research is now cash-realization-aware, but not fully cash-realization-proven. The strongest cases show destination, output, legal entitlement, collateral support, or aggregate revenue. The next upgrade requires same-period documents that identify who paid, what was billed or collected, what asset/use the cash relates to, and what funding source or repayment source supported the flow.`

## Proof Ladder

| Level | What Is Proven | Example Evidence | Safe Claim |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Destination visible | Holder row, project mention, backlog, capex plan, tariff proposal | Capital has an identifiable destination. |
| 2 | Use or output visible | Use-of-proceeds language, plant additions, project count, cargo count, fleet OEC, backlog burn | The destination has observable use or output. |
| 3 | Legal entitlement visible | Approved tariff/factor, signed contract, credit agreement, borrowing facility, SPA, ESA, customer obligation | There is a legal mechanism for payment or funding. |
| 4 | Aggregate cash visible | Company revenue, clause revenue, operating cash flow, aggregate true-up, rental revenue, cargo revenue proxy | Cash is visible at an aggregate level. |
| 5 | Same-period cash allocation visible | Category receipt schedule, customer-class billing determinant, project revenue, asset-level rental cash, borrower funds-flow, waterfall | Cash can be tied to the named asset/use/category. |
| 6 | Return and source bridge visible | Source/use schedule plus cash receipts, margin, debt service, ROE/IRR, covenant or collateral support | The mechanism is source-to-use-to-cash-return grade. |

Most current evidence sits between levels `2` and `4`. FPL Distribution Inspection is high quality because it reaches legal entitlement and aggregate-revenue evidence, but it still fails level `5`.

## FPL Stress Test

FPL is the best immediate test because the recovery chain is unusually explicit.

What we can show now:

- `193199` Distribution Inspection projects
- `61.5M USD` AP-1 actual cost
- `38.320627M USD` final expenditures
- `22.263219M USD` final additions to plant
- `15.942971M USD` final Distribution Inspection recoverable expense
- `10.481240M USD` equity component grossed up for taxes
- `2.278910M USD` debt component
- `3.182821M USD` depreciation
- `804.620369M USD` aggregate final SPPCRC clause revenue
- `65.318726M USD` aggregate true-up collected/refunded
- `70.622413018B USD` Form 8A adjusted retail capital structure
- `7.0330%` total weighted cost
- `8.8822%` pre-tax total

What this says:

`Distribution Inspection is a strong regulated recovery bridge. It connects work, cost, plant addition, final revenue requirement, component recovery math, aggregate clause revenue, factor authority, and capital-structure support.`

What it does not say:

`It does not prove Distribution Inspection-specific customer cash receipts, category-level billing determinants, source-of-funds allocation, shareholder profit, or project IRR.`

## Decision Rule

Do not upgrade any lane to cash-realization proof unless the evidence identifies:

- named asset, category, borrower, facility, or project
- same period
- source of cash or source of funding
- use or output
- billing, collection, repayment, revenue, or debt-service mechanism
- boundary that prevents aggregate evidence from being misused

## Safe Current Summary
