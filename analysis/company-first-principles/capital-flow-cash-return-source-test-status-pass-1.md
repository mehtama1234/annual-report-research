## Short Answer

`The first six source tests do not produce full asset/project cash-return proof. They do produce a ranked proof ladder: Wheaton is closest to named asset cash-return proof, FPL is strongest regulated recovery, URI is cleanest operating conversion, Cheniere is strongest physical-output/customer-demand bridge, Sterling is strongest backlog-to-company-cash proxy, and Energy Transfer is strongest company-level midstream project proxy.`

The system can now answer the money-movement question in bounded form:

`who controls money -> how it moves -> what it funds -> what output or cash proxy appears -> what proof is still missing`

The key finding is that cash-return proof fails at different places by lane. Wheaton fails at delivered-ounce cash receipts and full PMPA return allocation. FPL fails at category customer receipts and source-of-funds allocation. Cheniere and Energy Transfer fail at train/project waterfall and project contribution. URI fails at fleet-class OEC, utilization, capex split, and ROIC. Sterling fails at named owner funding, retainage collection, actual L/C usage, and project margin.

## What This Proves

The first six tests prove that the research system can separate evidence classes:

- `named asset proxy`: Wheaton Antamina
- `regulated recovery proxy`: FPL Distribution Inspection
- `operating fleet proxy`: URI
- `physical output and customer-demand proxy`: Cheniere
- `backlog and working-capital timing proxy`: Sterling
- `company-level project proxy`: Energy Transfer

They also prove that the missing evidence is not generic. Each lane has a different proof unlock.

## What It Does Not Prove

This pass does not prove full cash return for any of the first six cases. It does not treat revenue, DCF, OCF, backlog, rate recovery, cargoes, capex, collateral, or capacity additions as asset-level return unless the source set joins:

`specific source of funds -> specific use -> specific asset/project/customer output -> specific cash receipt or cash contribution -> debt service/distribution/return`
