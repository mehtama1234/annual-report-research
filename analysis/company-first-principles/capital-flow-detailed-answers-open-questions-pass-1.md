## End-To-End Goal

The goal is to prove, step by step, how large pools of money become real-economy exposure.

The mechanism is:

`original source of money -> capital platform -> vehicle or liability wrapper -> instrument -> borrower/company/project -> use of proceeds -> operating output -> cash-flow durability/risk -> safe claim`

The research is not trying to prove one broad story such as `private credit replaced banks` or `AI infrastructure is the whole cycle`. It is trying to classify each capital flow by mechanism, evidence level, and claim boundary.

## Current Core Answer

The research now supports a serious intermediate answer:

`Capital is moving through distinct wrappers into identifiable borrowers, infrastructure assets, energy systems, fleet/collateral pools, backlog execution, acquisition finance, and sponsor-backed operating platforms. The full source-to-use-to-output-to-bank-displacement chain is proven only in pieces.`

The most important refinement from the latest pass is that private-credit borrower flows are not one thing. The evidence separates acquisition finance, M&A/consolidation support, continued-growth facilities, incremental commitments, transaction-only leads, and selected bank-credit repayment or termination cases.

## Answers We Have

The research has meaningful answers on these points:

- Capital is not only going to abstract platforms. It reaches named borrowers, filed credit vehicles, public debt stacks, grid/power assets, fleet/collateral pools, LNG/midstream systems, backlog-heavy contractors, and acquisition platforms.
- The Ares borrower lane now has holder/source-taxonomized borrower rows and same-period controls, so borrower destination proof is no longer only anecdotal.
- Private-credit borrower use is mixed. Acquisition finance and consolidation are visible; organic productive capex is not the default conclusion.
- Debt/refinancing follow-through is now mechanism-specific across all five pilot rows, but remains below asset-level return proof.
- Bank replacement is real in selected cases, but not broad enough to use as a general conclusion.
- Platform-scale AUM/deployment evidence and borrower-specific exposure evidence are different claim levels and should not be merged without a bridge.
- False aggregation can be controlled by separating audit rows, same-period summaries, and promoted totals.

## Most Important Open Questions

The next research risk is not finding more examples. It is upgrading the weakest mechanisms.

1. Source-of-capital: which investor or liability base ultimately funds each visible vehicle?
2. Use-of-proceeds: which borrower dollars fund acquisition, refinancing, growth, working capital, capex, rescue, or dividends?
3. Facility size: are visible holder rows partial exposures or close to the full borrower debt package?
4. Bank role: did private credit replace banks, or did banks remain through revolvers, agency, hedging, treasury, collateral facilities, or securitization support?
5. Debt/refinancing return: can the five bridge rows be upgraded from mechanism visibility to dollar-level source/use/cash proof?
6. Output conversion: did financed companies generate durable output, revenue, backlog conversion, capacity, cash flow, or returns?
7. Scale: do the case-study patterns hold after lane-specific primary-source extraction across the 519-company universe?

## Hypotheses To Test

The current hypotheses are:

- The next cycle is a capital-routing cycle, not a single-theme AI or private-credit cycle.
- Private credit is materially financing operating companies, but much of the visible borrower evidence is acquisition/consolidation finance rather than clean organic capex.
- Banks are being displaced in some term-loan roles but may remain important through revolvers, administration, collateral facilities, hedging, and treasury.
- The best public-company beneficiaries are not always the capital platforms; they may be the operating bottleneck companies that convert financed demand into physical capacity or recurring maintenance.
- The strongest investable signals will come where capital-flow evidence meets operating conversion evidence: backlog burn, rate-base growth, fleet productivity, utilization, cargo volumes, facility expansion, and cash conversion.

## Answer-Finding Mechanism

Use this proof sequence for each company or borrower:

1. Identify the capital-flow role: borrower, lender/platform, asset owner, contractor, supplier, utility, collateral originator, or maintenance operator.
2. Identify the wrapper: BDC, private fund, insurer, bank facility, public note, securitization, ABL, revolver, project finance, or infrastructure equity.
3. Extract the source row: filing, schedule, agreement, proxy, release, docket, rating report, or government record.
4. Record amount, period, instrument, actor, and confidence.
5. Classify use of proceeds: acquisition, refinancing, growth, capex, working capital, dividend, rescue, liquidity, collateral origination, or project construction.
6. Find output conversion: revenue, EBITDA, cash conversion, utilization, rate base, MW, cargoes, backlog burn, OEC, orders, or project milestones.
7. Check bank role and contrary evidence.
8. Assign proof level and safe claim.
9. State what the evidence does not prove.
10. Add the next exact document needed to upgrade or falsify the claim.
