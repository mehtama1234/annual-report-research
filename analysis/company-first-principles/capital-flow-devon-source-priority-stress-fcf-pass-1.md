## Short Answer

`Devon has same-period source/use/cash-coverage and Coterra contribution proxy evidence; source priority and stress-tested asset contribution remain unproven.`

Devon's local evidence answers the money-movement question at statement level. In H1 `2026`, visible cash sources of `6.451B USD` matched visible cash uses of `6.451B USD`. Sources included `5.329B USD` of operating cash flow, `581M USD` of cash acquired in the Coterra merger, `90M USD` of divestiture proceeds, `22M USD` of investment distributions, a `425M USD` cash/restricted-cash drawdown, and `4M USD` of FX benefit.

The money moved into `2.157B USD` of capex, `2.919B USD` of property/equipment acquisitions, `500M USD` of debt repayment, `521M USD` of dividends, `266M USD` of buybacks, and smaller investment, lease, and equity-compensation cash uses. Devon also had `4.0B USD` of liquidity, no quarter-end revolver or commercial-paper borrowings, and an `18.1%` funded-debt-to-capitalization ratio versus a `65%` covenant limit.

The upgrade does not reach source-priority or stress-FCF proof. The local evidence still does not show which cash source paid which use, how the `2.6B USD` Permian lease acquisition will convert to cash, whether Coterra synergy is realized, or whether payouts/debt service remain covered under commodity stress.

## What This Proves

Devon is a company-level allocation and cash-coverage case. The answer to "who is investing where and how is money moving" is:

`operating cash flow + acquired merger cash + cash balances + minor sources -> Devon treasury envelope -> capex + acquisitions + debt repayment + dividends + buybacks -> production and Coterra contribution proxy -> same-period cash coverage`

That is not the same as:

`specific source -> specific asset -> asset cash return`
