## End-To-End Goal

The goal is to build a capital-flow evidence system that shows how large pools of money become real-economy exposure.

The research is trying to prove this chain:

`original source of money -> capital platform -> vehicle or wrapper -> instrument -> borrower, company, asset, project, backlog, fleet, or collateral pool -> use of proceeds -> operating output -> cash-flow durability and risk -> safe claim`

The important point is that the answer should not collapse into one broad label like `private credit`, `AI infrastructure`, `bank replacement`, or `capital spending`.

The goal is to identify the mechanism:

- whose capital entered the system
- who routed it
- what legal or financial wrapper carried it
- where it landed
- what it funded
- whether it produced operating output
- whether it replaced, supplemented, or sat beside banks
- what sentence the evidence safely permits

## Core Meta Question

`When large pools of money move through private credit, insurance, infrastructure, public debt, asset-backed finance, bank facilities, and wealth channels, where do they actually land in the real economy, what do they fund, and what can we prove versus merely infer?`

## Current Answer

The research now supports a serious intermediate answer:

`Large pools of capital are moving through distinct financial wrappers into identifiable real-economy borrowers, assets, projects, collateral pools, and operating systems. The strongest evidence today is destination, holder, vehicle, selected use-of-proceeds, and selected bank-role evidence. The full source-to-use-to-output-to-bank-role chain is proven only in pieces.`

The answer is meaningful because it moves the work from theme-level investing to mechanism-level investing. It separates:

- platform scale from borrower exposure
- BDC fair value from total facility size
- transaction value from debt size
- acquisition finance from organic growth capex
- bank-facility termination from total bank displacement
- row-level audit evidence from same-period exposure and promoted totals

## What We Have Answered

Yesterday's research answered these points well enough to use internally and, with caveats, externally:

- Capital is not only sitting at abstract platforms. It reaches named borrowers, filed credit vehicles, public debt stacks, grid/power assets, fleet/collateral pools, LNG/midstream systems, backlog-heavy contractors, and acquisition platforms.
- The Ares borrower lane is now holder/source-taxonomized and same-period controlled, so the borrower evidence is no longer just anecdotal.
- Private-credit use of proceeds is mixed. Acquisition finance, consolidation support, continued-growth facilities, incremental commitments, transaction-only leads, and selected bank-credit repayment or termination all appear.
- Bank replacement exists in selected narrow cases, but broad bank displacement is not proven.
- Auctane/Stamps.com is the strongest narrow bank-facility takeout case because the prior bank credit agreement termination is documented, while post-close ancillary bank roles remain unresolved.
- Atwell remains a useful bank-role test case, but current evidence does not prove whether the Ares/KKR-related private-credit package repaid, amended, or coexisted with the prior Bank of America-led facility.
- Platform-scale evidence and borrower-specific evidence are different claim levels and should not be merged without a bridge.
- False aggregation can be controlled by separating row-level audit tables, same-period summaries, and master-rollup promoted totals.

## Open Questions

The remaining work is concentrated in six hard gaps:

1. Source of capital: which investor or liability base ultimately funds each visible vehicle?
2. Use of proceeds: which dollars fund acquisition, refinancing, growth, capex, working capital, rescue, liquidity, or dividends?
3. Facility size: are visible holder rows partial exposures, full facility views, or only one slice of a larger package?
4. Bank role: did private credit replace banks, or did banks remain through revolvers, agency, hedging, treasury, collateral facilities, or securitization support?
5. Output conversion: did the financed company or asset generate durable output, revenue, backlog burn, cash flow, capacity, or returns?
6. Scale: do the patterns hold across the full 519-company universe, or are they only case-study evidence?

## Working Hypotheses

The hypotheses worth testing next are:

- This is a capital-routing cycle, not a single-theme `AI`, `private credit`, or `infrastructure` cycle.
- Private credit is materially financing real operating companies, but many visible borrower flows are acquisition, consolidation, refinancing, or liquidity mechanisms rather than pure growth capex.
- Banks are being displaced in some term-loan or acquisition-finance roles, but may remain important through revolvers, treasury, hedging, collateral facilities, administration, and securitization support.
- The most important public-company beneficiaries may be operating bottlenecks, not only capital platforms: utilities, power owners, industrial distributors, fleet operators, MRO networks, contractors, infrastructure suppliers, and maintenance systems.
- The strongest investment signals should appear where capital-flow evidence meets operating-conversion evidence: backlog burn, rate-base growth, fleet productivity, utilization, cargo volumes, facility expansion, recurring service revenue, and cash conversion.
- The research system can scale because every company now has a standard row: role, wrapper, source availability, metric need, proof level, missing document, and safe claim.

## Answer-Finding Mechanism

Every company, borrower, or project should be pushed through the same proof sequence:

1. Assign the capital-flow role: borrower, lender/platform, asset owner, contractor, supplier, utility, collateral originator, maintenance operator, or indirect beneficiary.
2. Identify the wrapper: BDC, private fund, insurer, public note, bank facility, revolver, ABL, securitization, project-finance vehicle, infrastructure fund, wealth product, or operating-company balance sheet.
3. Capture the source document: filing schedule, annual report, 10-K, 10-Q, N-PORT, merger proxy, credit agreement, release, docket, tariff, rating report, government record, or transaction document.
4. Extract amount, period, actor, instrument, borrower, and confidence.
5. Classify use of proceeds: acquisition, refinancing, growth, capex, working capital, dividend, rescue, liquidity, collateral origination, or project construction.
6. Tie the financing to operating evidence: revenue, EBITDA, cash conversion, utilization, fleet OEC, MW in service, cargoes, backlog burn, orders, rate-base additions, project milestones, or customer contracts.
7. Check contrary evidence, especially bank role and duplicate denominator risk.
8. Assign proof level and safe sentence.
9. State what the evidence does not prove.
10. Name the next exact document that would upgrade, hold, or weaken the claim.

## Upgrade Rules

Use these rules before promoting any claim:

| Claim Type | Can Upgrade When | Must Stay Bounded When |
|---|---|---|
| Destination | Named borrower, asset, project, or company is tied to a source row. | Only platform AUM or broad deployment is visible. |
| Source of capital | Vehicle capital stack, liability source, investor base, or funding note is tied to the exposure period. | Only the holder vehicle is known. |
| Use of proceeds | Agreement, proxy, release, funds-flow language, rating report, or capex/project document states the use. | The borrower and instrument are visible but proceeds are not. |
| Facility size | Credit agreement, amendment, rating report, lender allocation, or debt note shows the facility package. | Only BDC fair value or one holder row is visible. |
| Bank replacement | Prior bank facility and new private-credit facility are both identified, and repayment, termination, amendment, or coexistence is documented. | Bank facility termination is visible but post-close ancillary role is unknown. |
| Operating conversion | Financing can be linked to repeated-period output, revenue, capacity, backlog conversion, utilization, cash flow, or project completion. | Only financing or backlog is visible. |
| Scale | The same role/wrapper/proof template is applied across the 519-company universe and then upgraded with lane-specific primary-source extraction. | Evidence remains template-grade or case-study-only. |
