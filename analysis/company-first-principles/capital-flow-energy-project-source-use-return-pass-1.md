# Capital Flow Energy Project Source-Use-Return Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass resolves `CFARQ-013`:

`Can Energy Transfer and Cheniere be upgraded from company-level funding/output evidence to project-level source-of-funds evidence?`

The operating table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-energy-project-source-use-return-pass-1.csv`

## Short Answer

Not yet.

The current evidence is strong enough to say:

`ET and Cheniere have source-backed company funding, credit-stack, physical-output, and external-denominator evidence around energy infrastructure buildout.`

It is not strong enough to say:

`Named projects and trains have fully mapped source-of-funds, use-of-proceeds, customer economics, cash return, and project-finance waterfalls.`

So `CFARQ-013` stays:

`hold-after-pass-1`

## Energy Transfer Answer

ET is a large midstream capital-allocation and refinancing case.

Visible evidence includes:

- Q2 `2026` adjusted EBITDA of `5.07B USD`
- Q2 `2026` DCF of `2.59B USD`
- Q2 `2026` growth capex of `1.10B USD`
- FY `2026` growth-capex guide of `5.6B-5.9B USD`
- `5.00B USD` Five-Year Credit Facility
- `1.21B USD` outstanding facility borrowings
- `3.00B USD` January `2026` senior notes with refinancing use language
- Nederland ethane export capacity addition of `240000 bpd`
- Nederland LPG capacity addition of `55000 bpd`
- Lone Star Express incremental Permian NGL takeaway capacity of more than `90000 bpd`
- long-term y-grade transportation/fractionation agreements for about `300000 bpd`

The safe claim is:

`Energy Transfer has company-level cash generation, refinancing, liquidity, and named capacity-expansion evidence around an NGL/export logistics buildout.`

The boundary is:

`The current evidence does not map individual ET projects to specific notes, revolver draws, commercial-paper use, retained cash, customer contracts, tariff economics, utilization, EBITDA, or return.`

## Cheniere Answer

Cheniere is the stronger partial-upgrade case because the evidence reaches further down the entity/output ladder.

Visible evidence includes:

- FY `2025` revenue of `19.98B USD`
- FY `2025` DCF of `5.29B USD`
- FY `2025` company-reported LNG cargoes of `670`
- DOE vessel rows of `674` cargoes from Sabine Pass plus Corpus Christi in FY `2025`
- Q2 `2026` company-reported cargoes of `184`
- DOE vessel rows of `184` cargoes from Sabine Pass plus Corpus Christi in Q2 `2026`
- about `90%` of anticipated SPL/CCL production contracted under SPAs and IPM agreements
- about `15` years weighted-average remaining SPA/IPM life at December 31 `2025`
- FY `2025` growth capital of `2.6B USD`, including `2.3B USD` equity-funded growth capital
- Q2 `2026` growth capital of about `1.1B USD`, including `219M USD` equity-funded growth capital
- Q2 `2026` long-term debt of `22.632B USD`
- Q2 `2026` SPL total debt of `5.027B USD`
- Q2 `2026` CQP total debt of `9.550B USD`
- CPC Corporation Taiwan SPA for up to `1.2 mtpa` from `2026` through `2050`

The safe claim is:

`Cheniere has the strongest current energy buildout bridge: contracted LNG production, entity-level debt buckets, equity-funded growth-capex disclosure, train milestones, and DOE cargo rows all point to real physical conversion.`

The boundary is:

`This still does not identify which debt facility, restricted account, equity contribution, or customer contract funded each train or terminal expansion, nor does it prove train-level return.`

## Why The Gate Holds

The `CFARQ-013` acceptance rule was:

`Pass if project cost, financing source, use of proceeds, and output/revenue denominator align.`

The current evidence aligns some pieces, but not all pieces in one named-project row.

| Required Field | ET Status | Cheniere Status |
|---|---|---|
| Named project or train | visible for selected assets | visible for selected trains/assets |
| Project cost | not mapped | partly implied but not fully mapped by train |
| Financing source | company/refinancing evidence visible, not project-specific | entity/equity funding visible, not train-specific |
| Use of proceeds | refinancing language visible, not project allocation | growth-capex split visible, not train allocation |
| Customer economics | y-grade capacity agreements visible, terms missing | SPA/IPM duration visible, pricing/credit terms missing |
| Physical output | throughput/capacity support, limited utilization proof | strong DOE cargo support |
| Cash/return | company EBITDA/DCF visible | company EBITDA/DCF visible |
| Waterfall/debt service | not mapped | debt-service denominator visible, waterfall missing |
