## Short Answer

`No full upgrade yet. Energy Transfer has strong partnership-level cash generation, capex, refinancing, liquidity, debt-stack, and named-capacity evidence, but current local evidence still does not prove named-project cash realization for Nederland, Lone Star, or y-grade assets.`

The local evidence answers the money-movement question at company/project proxy level. Partnership cash generation, revolver availability, long-term debt, and senior-note refinancing support a large growth-capex program. That capital is visibly pointed at midstream/export assets including Nederland ethane and LPG export capacity, Lone Star Express incremental Permian NGL takeaway, and long-term y-grade transportation/fractionation agreements. The output side is visible through named capacity additions and volume-growth metrics.

The strongest visible data points are Q2 `2026` adjusted EBITDA of `5.07B USD`, Q2 `2026` DCF of `2.59B USD`, Q2 `2026` growth capex of `1.10B USD`, Q2 `2026` maintenance capex of `307M USD`, FY `2026` growth-capex guidance of `5.6B-5.9B USD`, `3.76B USD` unused Five-Year Credit Facility availability, `68.393B USD` long-term debt context, `3.00B USD` January `2026` senior notes, `1.60B USD` note redemption/refinancing evidence, `3.01x` partnership leverage ratio, Nederland `240000 bpd` ethane capacity addition, Nederland `55000 bpd` LPG capacity addition, Lone Star Express more than `90000 bpd` incremental NGL takeaway, about `300000 bpd` long-term y-grade agreements into the `2030s`, `13%` NGL transportation growth, and `25%` NGL export growth.

That is still not named-project cash-realization proof. The current local source set lacks named project cost, exact source-to-project allocation, shipper/customer identity, minimum-volume or take-or-pay obligations, tariff/fee economics, billing volume, utilization, project revenue, project EBITDA, DCF contribution, debt-service allocation, and project return.
