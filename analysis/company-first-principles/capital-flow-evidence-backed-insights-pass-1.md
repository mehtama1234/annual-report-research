## Purpose

This is the detailed insight memo for what the research found.

It is different from the synthesis page. The synthesis explains the goal and question system. This page states the actual evidence-backed insights, why they matter, what proves them, and where the claim must stay bounded.

## Core Read

The research now supports one central finding:

`Capital is not moving through one generic channel. It is being routed through different wrappers because the underlying economic claims are different: middle-market loans, BDC balance sheets, public/private credit funds, bank facilities, ABL and receivables pools, LNG entities, utility rate-base mechanisms, project backlogs, and acquisition platforms each require different proof.`

The strongest insight is not simply that more private capital exists. The stronger insight is that each operating lane has a different conversion test:

- private-credit borrowers need holder, facility-size, proceeds, and bank-role proof
- URI needs fleet, collateral, utilization, and cash-conversion proof
- Sterling needs backlog, contract timing, guarantee, and cash-conversion proof
- power/grid needs tariff, docket, approval, recovery, MW, and in-service proof
- LNG/midstream needs entity debt, project source/use, customer contracts, output, and return proof
- the full company universe needs role/wrapper/proof routing before any representative thesis claim

## Insight Narrative

### 1. The Main Finding Is Mechanism, Not Theme

The work is now past the point of asking whether there is a broad private-credit or infrastructure theme. The useful finding is that capital travels through different wrappers because each real-economy asset has a different financing logic.

Middle-market borrowers show up through BDC schedules, private-credit vehicles, commitment rows, and transaction context. URI shows reusable fleet assets, receivables, ABL mechanics, notes, and operating cash generation. Sterling shows backlog, contract timing, revolver capacity, L/Cs, surety mechanics, and acquisition-enabled execution. Cheniere shows entity debt, contracted production, train milestones, and DOE cargo reconciliation. AEP and Duke show that grid demand becomes more serious when it enters tariffs, dockets, resource plans, approvals, and recovery mechanisms.

The implication is that the research should not ask one generic question like `where is capital flowing?` It should ask:

`Which wrapper fits which economic asset, and where does the evidence prove source, use, output, cash, risk, and boundary?`

### 2. Private Credit Is Destination-Proven, But Not Fully Use-Proven

The private-credit borrower evidence is real. The Ares borrower expansion has `89` normalized holder/source-taxonomized rows across nine borrower cases, plus a `9` row same-period borrower control. It covers borrowers in roadway safety, aerospace MRO, wealth-management consolidation, insurance brokerage, refrigeration/foodservice equipment, precision manufacturing, building maintenance, engineering services, and automotive services.

That is enough to say private credit reaches named real-economy borrowers.

It is not enough to say visible fair value equals total borrower debt, or that every loan funded growth capex. The use-of-proceeds pass shows a more mixed pattern: acquisition finance, consolidation, continued-growth language, incremental commitments, transaction-only leads, and selected bank-credit repayment or termination.

The better insight is:

`Private credit is materially present in operating-company finance, but its visible borrower uses often look like acquisition, consolidation, refinancing, or liquidity mechanisms before they look like clean organic capex.`

### 3. Bank Replacement Is A Specific-Case Claim

The bank-role answer is valuable because it is narrower than the market narrative.

Auctane/Stamps.com is the cleanest confirmed takeout case: private credit financed the acquisition and the prior bank-led credit agreement was terminated at closing. Medallia has strong acquisition-debt and repayment evidence, but still needs final lender allocation and post-close bank-role proof. Guidehouse is a strong bank-competition lead, but currently rests on secondary evidence. Atwell is the cleanest unresolved test because the prior `200M USD` Bank of America-led facility and later private-credit evidence are both visible, while repayment or coexistence is still unproven.

The safe insight is:

`Private credit can replace banks in selected borrower financings, but the broader pattern is not bank disappearance. It may be a shift in which private credit takes term-loan or acquisition-finance risk while banks remain in revolvers, agency, treasury, hedging, collateral facilities, and securitization support.`

### 4. The Best Operating Proof Is Where Capital Meets Conversion

URI and Sterling are currently the strongest operating-conversion cases.

URI has repeated-period evidence that a capital-intensive rental fleet can absorb capital and generate output. Rental revenue increased from `2.929B USD` in Q1 2024 to `3.849B USD` in Q2 2026. Fleet productivity stayed positive in every extracted period. Average OEC growth stayed positive in every extracted period. H1 2026 operating cash flow covered `112.8%` of gross rental capex and `162.0%` of net fleet cash investment after sale proceeds.

Sterling has a different mechanism. It is not primarily a fleet-collateral case. It is a backlog and project-execution case with a `1.500B USD` revolver, a `600M USD` L/C sublimit, large contract-liability timing, growing RPO, positive operating cash flow after capex in the observed quarters, and acquisition-enabled backlog.

The insight is:

`The operating winners may not be the companies with the most financing headlines. They may be the companies where the financing wrapper matches a repeatable conversion engine: fleet productivity, backlog burn, rate-base additions, cargo volumes, project milestones, or recurring service revenue.`

### 5. Power/Grid And Energy Need Legal And Physical Proof, Not Just Capex Plans

The power/grid lane is promising, but the evidence standard has to be strict. A load forecast is not the same as recoverable infrastructure. The AEP/Duke docket pass shows the correct path: customer application, study fee, plan of service, LOA/ESA, collateral or minimum demand obligations, forecast inclusion, regulatory approval, and energization. FPL is now the strongest regulated example because the SPPCRC chain reaches category-output-recovery-with-component-and-capital-structure-attribution status for Distribution Inspection, while still stopping short of category customer receipts, source-of-funds allocation, and project-level return proof.

Cheniere shows a stronger energy output bridge because company-reported cargoes reconcile to DOE terminal rows, and entity-level debt plus contracted production are visible. ET is strong at company-level capital allocation, refinancing, and capacity-expansion evidence, but named-project source/use/return remains open.

The insight is:

`Energy and grid capital becomes claim-grade only when financing capacity can be tied to named projects, customer obligations, legal approval, physical output, cost recovery, and cash return.`

### 6. The Research System Is Now Scalable, But The Evidence Is Not Finished

The `519` company template is a major process upgrade. Every company now has a lane, role, likely wrapper, source availability, proof level, denominator need, next document, safe claim, and do-not-claim boundary. That means future work can be executed in batches rather than reinvented company by company.

But the template is not proof that the thesis is representative. It is a routing system. The empirical upgrade happens only when lane-specific primary-source extraction moves rows from radar or queue status into source-grade evidence.

## What This Lets Us Say

The strongest evidence-backed statement is:

`The research shows capital moving through multiple wrappers into identifiable real-economy destinations, with the best current proof in named private-credit borrower rows, BDC and non-listed BDC vehicle stacks, URI fleet conversion, Sterling backlog conversion, Cheniere cargo/entity-output evidence, and AEP/Duke regulatory-grid evidence. The evidence supports a capital-routing thesis with bounded claims, not an unqualified claim that private credit broadly replaced banks or that every dollar is proven source-to-use-to-return.`
