## Short Answer

`The system now has 16 executed money-movement cases. They show capital moving through four main routes: insurance/private-credit platforms into credit assets and borrowers; regulated, contracted, tariffed, or backlog-backed infrastructure into projects and output; corporate treasury/debt channels into capex, acquisitions, refinancing, and collateral structures; and streaming finance into production rights. The evidence is strong enough to describe who routes the money, where it appears to go, and what cash/proxy mechanism exists. It is not yet strong enough to claim full asset-level cash-return proof across the set.`

## What We Are Trying To Build

The target product is not a list of companies. It is an evidence-backed capital-flow graph:

`money source -> routing entity or vehicle -> instrument/channel -> asset/project/borrower/use -> output/revenue/cash mechanism -> missing proof boundary`

That is why each row below keeps two things together: the best current money-movement answer and the exact next source needed to upgrade or disprove it.

## Decision

`executed-money-movement-synthesis-ready`

The first completed graph-upgrade queue now has a reader-facing synthesis. This is the current big-picture answer layer: it shows who routes money, where it moves, what cash/proxy channel is visible, and exactly what remains missing before stronger claims are safe.

## Safe Claim

`Across the 16 executed capital-flow cases, the research system can now describe money movement at source, routing, destination, and cash/proxy level. Wheaton, FPL, Cheniere, URI, Sterling, and Energy Transfer are the strongest asset/project cash-proxy routes; Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic, Ares, and Blackstone are the main insurance/private-credit routing routes; Matador, Devon, PBF, and Liberty are treasury/refinancing allocation routes; and Plains is a tariff route/rate case. None of the 16 should be represented as full asset-level cash-return proof without the missing source layer listed in the synthesis table.`
