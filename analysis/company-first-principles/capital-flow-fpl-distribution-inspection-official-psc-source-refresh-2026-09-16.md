# FPL Distribution Inspection: Official PSC Source Refresh

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This refresh tests whether the official Florida Public Service Commission
record closes the remaining Q-11 gap:

`Distribution Inspection recovery -> rate-class determinant -> billed or collected category cash -> source-of-funds allocation -> return`

The checked sources are the public FPL 2026 Storm Protection Plan filing, the
FPL 2026 Storm Protection Plan Cost Recovery Clause filing, and the later
2026 actual/estimated and 2027 projection filing in Docket `20260010-EI`. The
later filing improves the category cost and activity bridge, but the checked
public record still does not supply a category-level customer receipt ledger
or project funding allocation.

## Evidence gained

| Field | Public observation | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Program identity | The 2026–2035 FPL plan continues the Distribution Inspection Program as one of eight storm-protection programs. | Program inclusion is not a receipt or return. |
| Cost/recovery jurisdiction | FPL states that prudence and true-up of actual 2026 SPP costs occur in the separate annual SPPCRC proceeding. | The SPP plan does not itself prove actual recovery or cash collection. |
| Customer allocation | The 2026 SPPCRC filing says updated revenue requirements, including true-ups, are allocated to rate classes using the approved cost-allocation methodologies and Form 6P/Exhibit RLH-7. | The public filing does not allocate those dollars to Distribution Inspection within the category. |
| Billing period | The requested 2026 SPPCRC factors apply to bills issued January 1 through December 31, 2026. | Bill-factor authority is not billed or collected cash by program. |
| True-up timing | The filing states that prudence and true-up of actual 2026 costs will be addressed in the final 2026 SPPCRC true-up filing in 2027. | The actual category cash test remains a future filing/document test. |
| Project detail | The SPP petition says annual inspection programs are cycle-based and do not lend themselves to specific project-level detail in Appendix D. | This explains why project-level inspection receipts are not visible in the plan filing. |
| Actual/estimated activity | Form 6P reports `180,000` poles and 2026 actual/estimated costs of `$45.4M` capital, `$42.6M` removal, and `$4.1M` O&M, or `$92.1M` total, based on information available at the end of February 2026. | These are program cost and activity observations, not customer cash or shareholder return. |
| Forward burden | The same Form 6P projects `180,000` poles and `$94.1M` total cost for 2027; its ten-year 2026–2035 estimated program cost is `$917.1M`. | Projection and cost visibility do not establish recovery timing, source of funds, or earned return. |
| Capital allocation | The public record provides program revenue requirements and rate-class methodology, but no Distribution Inspection debt/equity/operating-cash source ledger. | Source-of-funds allocation remains open. |

## Decision

`evidence-insufficient — regulatory-recovery route strengthened; category-receipt and source-of-funds gates remain open`

This is not a searched-negative for the existence of customer payments. It is
a searched-negative for the requested category-specific cash and funding
fields within the checked public PSC filings. The correct next source is the
non-confidential rate-class and collection support behind the SPPCRC workpaper
package, followed by the 2027 final true-up.

## Why this matters to the larger goal

The refresh strengthens the first half of the end-to-end chain:

`regulated control point -> approved program -> rate-class allocation method -> billing-period recovery authority`

It does not justify the second half:

`category cost -> billed/collected cash -> funding source -> earned return`

Therefore FPL remains a strong next-cycle admission candidate and not a
promoted investment conclusion. The QoE rule also holds: regulatory revenue
authority, a rate factor, and aggregate utility cash cannot be promoted to
Distribution Inspection owner cash.

The companion [historical recovery-boundary pass](capital-flow-fpl-sppcrc-historical-recovery-boundary-2026-09-16.md)
adds dated total-SPPCRC true-up observations and a separate Form 6P cost
presentation cross-check. It strengthens the recovery-process and denominator
control, but deliberately does not divide total jurisdictional recovery into
Distribution Inspection customer cash.

## Move-on decision — 2026-09-17

A narrowed local-source check of the available FPL/PSC packet—including the
2025 final-true-up petition and Epperson/Pankratz project-detail exhibits—and
the `20260010-EI` route did not surface a non-confidential Distribution
Inspection billing determinant, invoice/collection schedule, category
receivable ledger, or source-of-funds certificate beyond the schedules already
described above. This is a searched-negative for those specific public packet
objects, not evidence that customers did not pay or that no private workpaper
exists.
The lane is therefore parked at `hold-with-strong-route-visible` rather than
expanded through another aggregate factor or cost filing. Reopen only when a
non-confidential discovery response, supporting workpaper, or final 2026
SPPCRC true-up supplies a same-period category allocation that can be joined to
billed/collected cash.

## September 18, 2026 plan-versus-actual source cross-check

The original PSC 2026–2035 SPP program-cost filing provides an independent
planning baseline for the same Distribution Inspection category: 2026 planned
operating expense of `$4.1M`, capital expenditure of `$88.0M`, total cost of
`$92.1M`, and `180,000` pole inspections. The later actual/estimated Form 6P
shows the same rounded 2026 total and activity count. This is useful as a
plan-versus-current-estimate control, but it does not convert the program
budget into billed or collected category cash, nor does it identify debt,
equity, operating-cash, or shareholder funding.

The cross-check therefore improves denominator provenance only. It does not
change Q-11's status:

`planned and actual/estimated program cost visible; category receipts and
source-of-funds allocation unproven.`
