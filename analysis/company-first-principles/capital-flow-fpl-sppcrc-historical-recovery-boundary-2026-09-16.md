# FPL SPPCRC historical recovery boundary

Research date: `2026-09-16`

## Purpose

This pass tests whether the Florida PSC record can move Q-11 from a program
cost and recovery-authority route to customer-cash evidence. The structured
[historical recovery-boundary table](data/capital-flow-fpl-sppcrc-historical-recovery-boundary-2026-09-16.csv)
keeps the public record's total jurisdictional recovery and true-up figures
separate from Distribution Inspection program costs.

## Evidence

The public record shows a dated recovery process. The 2022 final-true-up
record describes FPL's `$1,292,952,697` final SPP cost and a `$5,171,245`
jurisdictional under-recovery including interest. The 2023 final-true-up
filing carries a `$14,860,970` actual/estimated jurisdictional under-recovery
including interest. These are total SPPCRC recovery-process amounts, not
Distribution Inspection collections.

The September 2025 second-amended factor petition makes the next bridge more
specific: FPL proposed alternative updated 2026 SPP revenue requirements of
`$998.817M`, an alternative total net jurisdictional revenue requirement of
`$984.084M`, and allocation to rate classes under the approved methodology for
bills issued during 2026. This is stronger rate-class allocation evidence than
a generic statement that customers pay a surcharge, but it remains a
regulatory factor and revenue-requirement schedule, not a Distribution
Inspection invoice or collection ledger.

The subsequent Commission order confirms the approved total rather than only
the requested alternative: projected 2026 SPP cost of `$1.244B`, including
`$139.871M` of O&M and `$1.104B` of capital expenditure, with a `$998.817M`
jurisdictional revenue requirement. This is a stronger approval-status
observation, but the order still does not allocate the total to Distribution
Inspection or prove customer cash collection.

The companion [rate-class factor boundary](capital-flow-fpl-2026-sppcrc-rate-class-factor-boundary-2026-09-16.md)
captures the order's conditional 2026 class factors. This advances the
regulatory chain to bill-application mechanics, while preserving the missing
usage determinants, category allocation, collections, and source-of-funds
records.

The new [rate-class determinant boundary](capital-flow-fpl-sppcrc-rate-class-determinant-boundary-2026-09-16.md)
recovers projected 2026 kWh sales and demand billing-kW denominators from the
public Form 5P schedule. That is a real denominator upgrade, but it belongs to
the alternative non-settlement projection scenario and remains aggregate
SPPCRC evidence. It is not an actual meter-read, invoice, receipt, or
Distribution Inspection-specific collection schedule.

The FPL amended Form 6P separately reports 2026 Distribution Inspection
activity of `180,000` poles and `$92.1M` total cost, decomposed into `$45.4M`
capital, `$42.6M` removal, and `$4.1M` O&M. Its companion 2026 work schedule
also presents `$88.0M` of capital expenditure and `$4.1M` of O&M for the
program, which is a presentation cross-check of the `$92.1M` total. The
2026/2027 filing projects `$94.1M` for 2027.

## Decision

`historical-recovery-process-visible; category-cash-unproven`

The new evidence strengthens the regulatory chain:

```text
program cost -> total jurisdictional revenue requirement -> true-up process
```

It does not complete:

```text
Distribution Inspection category -> rate-class determinant -> customer bill
-> collected cash -> source-of-funds allocation -> return
```

The total true-up amounts cannot be divided by the Distribution Inspection
cost without a program/rate-class schedule. An approved or requested revenue
requirement is not the same thing as cash collected from customers, and the
public record does not justify treating it as one.

## Next promotion test

Acquire the non-confidential actual/estimated or final Form 5P/Form 7A and
supporting workpapers for the relevant SPPCRC year, then join (1) Distribution
Inspection category cost, (2) rate-class billing determinants and approved
factors, (3) billed and collected amounts or an auditable cash settlement, and
(4) the source-of-funds and capital-recovery treatment. Until that join exists, Q-11 remains
`hold-with-strong-route-visible`.
