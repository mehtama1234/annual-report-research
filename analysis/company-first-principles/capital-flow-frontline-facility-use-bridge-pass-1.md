# Capital Flow Frontline Facility Use Bridge Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass resolves queue item `CFARQ-005` as far as current local and public evidence allows.

The question is:

`Can Frontline Road Safety's holder rows be tied to a named facility and a specific use-of-proceeds category?`

## Resolution Outcome

`hold-after-pass-1 - holder-visible continued-growth facility evidence, exact facility use still unproven`

Frontline is a strong borrower-destination case. It has multiple SEC-filed holder rows, an Ares transaction-role source, sponsor transaction context, and operating context for a roadway safety services platform.

It does not yet pass the facility-use bridge because the current evidence does not include a credit agreement, rating report, lender allocation schedule, acquisition-use schedule, payoff evidence, or financing source summary that maps the holder rows to specific proceeds.

## Current Safe Answer

`Frontline Road Safety is a holder-visible private-credit borrower in roadway safety services with Ares transaction-role evidence for a senior secured facility supporting Bain's continued growth plans. The exact facility size, lender allocation, use of proceeds, Ares funded allocation, and bank role remain unproven.`

Safe sentence:

`Frontline Road Safety can be used as evidence that private credit reaches an infrastructure-adjacent roadway safety services platform: Ares identified a senior secured facility supporting Bain Capital's continued growth plans, and multiple SEC-filed credit vehicles reported Frontline first-lien debt or commitments in 2026. The evidence does not yet show whether proceeds funded organic growth, acquisitions, refinancing, working capital, liquidity, or bank takeout.`

## Why This Matters

Frontline helps answer the real-economy destination question better than it answers the bank-replacement question.

It shows private credit touching a physical-services platform tied to roads, airports, pavement marking, and ancillary roadway safety work. That is useful for the capital-flow thesis because the borrower sits close to infrastructure execution and maintenance, not just software or financial engineering.

But the claim must stay bounded. `Continued growth plans` is not the same thing as organic capex, project construction, acquisition funding, refinancing, or bank takeout. It is a useful use-of-proceeds clue, not a finished answer.
