# Capital Flow Funding-Type Hypothesis Map Pass 1

## Purpose

This page answers the question:

`Why do different companies use different funding containers for capital-intensive activity?`

The operating table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-funding-type-hypothesis-map-pass-1.csv`

This is not a final conclusion page. It is a hypothesis map that turns the financing pattern into testable claims.

## Core Hypothesis

The simple hypothesis is:

`Funding type follows asset type.`

More specifically:

`The cleaner, more reusable, more contracted, more legally separable, or more regulator-recoverable the asset is, the more specialized and underwritable the funding container can be.`

That means the financing structure is itself evidence. It tells us what the market thinks can be pledged, contracted, recovered, refinanced, or flexibly supported.

## What We Think Is Happening

The capital-intensive lane is not one funding story.

It is several different funding systems:

1. **Network funding:** ET can use corporate debt, revolvers, commercial paper, and notes because the platform is diversified and cash-generating.
2. **Entity/project funding:** Cheniere can separate SPL, CQP, CCH, and parent debt because LNG assets and contracts sit in identifiable legal layers.
3. **Collateral funding:** URI can use AR securitization and ABL because receivables and rental fleet are measurable collateral.
4. **Platform flexibility funding:** Sterling can use a large secured revolver because backlog, acquisitions, letters of credit, and working capital need flexible committed capacity.
5. **Customer or regulator funding:** Contractors and utilities may not be the ultimate source of the capital. Customers, governments, hyperscalers, utilities, or ratepayers may be upstream.

## What Would Make The Hypothesis Stronger

The hypothesis becomes stronger if we find that:

- contracted infrastructure gets entity-level debt or project-like debt
- reusable assets get ABL, securitization, or collateral-backed credit
- backlog businesses rely more on revolvers, letters of credit, and working-capital facilities
- regulated assets tie debt/equity funding to rate-base recovery
- acquisition-heavy platforms have larger incremental debt baskets and looser permitted acquisition language
- funding costs and covenant packages differ in ways that match asset quality and cash-flow predictability

## What Would Break The Hypothesis

The hypothesis weakens if:

- funding type does not line up with collateral quality, cash-flow predictability, legal separability, or regulatory recovery
- companies use the same generic unsecured debt regardless of asset type
- customer-funded backlog removes the need for corporate funding
- debt proceeds are mainly refinancing old liabilities rather than funding new buildout
- covenants or borrowing-base limits sharply reduce apparent headline capacity
- capex is mostly maintenance, compliance, or inflation rather than growth

## Simple Bottom Line

In simple words:

`We are trying to learn what the funding wrapper tells us about the asset.`

If the asset is contracted and separable, it may get entity-level debt.

If the asset is reusable collateral, it may get ABL or securitization.

If the business is a project/backlog platform, it may need a revolver and letters of credit.

If the asset is regulated, customers may repay it through rates.

If the project belongs to the customer, the operating company may only fund the execution timing.
