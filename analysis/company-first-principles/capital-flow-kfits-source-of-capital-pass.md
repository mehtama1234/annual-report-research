## Simple Answer

K-FITS is the clearer KKR/FS wealth-channel case.

The Q2 2026 10-Q describes K-FITS as a Delaware statutory trust, specialty finance company, externally managed closed-end investment company, BDC, and RIC. Unlike public FSK, K-FITS reports no exchange listing on the cover page and had Class S shares outstanding.

The filing also describes a continuous private offering, principal placement agents, participating brokers, financial advisors, and a Class S shareholder servicing/distribution fee.

That upgrades K-FITS-linked borrower rows from “medium-confidence non-traded label” to filed non-traded BDC/private-credit vehicle evidence.

It still does not identify the final shareholders or prove insurance funding.

## Current Bottom Line

For K-FITS-linked borrower rows, the immediate source-of-capital answer is now much sharper:

K-FITS is a KKR/FS-managed non-traded BDC/private-credit vehicle funded primarily by Class S shareholder equity and secondarily by revolving credit facilities. The filing shows `1.006058B USD` of shareholder equity against `572.938M USD` of debt and gives direct evidence of a continuous private offering, placement agents, participating brokers, financial advisors, and `0.85%` Class S distribution/servicing fees. That supports a wealth/intermediated private-credit channel claim, but not a final end-investor, insurer-funded, or bank-displacement claim.
