## Short Answer

`MasTec has backlog-to-revenue/cash-proxy evidence, but project-owner funding, retainage, margin, and cash collection remain unproven.`

MasTec answers the money-movement question at proxy level. Customer awards and backlog flow into infrastructure project execution, which shows up as revenue, adjusted EBITDA, contract-liability timing, DSO, operating cash flow, capex, liquidity, and debt support.

The current local evidence shows `21.4B USD` of Q2 `2026` backlog, including `7.8B USD` of Clean Energy and Infrastructure backlog and `6.3B USD` of Power Delivery backlog. Q2 revenue was `4.374B USD`, adjusted EBITDA was `384.2M USD`, H1 operating cash flow was `120.322M USD`, H1 capex was `188.312M USD`, contract liabilities were `760.912M USD`, long-term debt including finance leases was `2.573839B USD`, and the Q2 presentation gave `72` DSO, `1.8x` leverage, and `1.7B USD` liquidity.

That is useful backlog-to-cash-proxy evidence. It is not yet project-cash proof because the local evidence does not identify funded owner programs, signed/non-cancellable status, retainage, project margins, receivable aging, or cash collection by project/backlog cohort.

## Queue Result

`executed-local-boundary-pass-1`

MasTec can be upgraded from partial-local to executed-local boundary status because the local evidence supports backlog/revenue/cash-proxy visibility and a clear next proof agenda. It does not pass the full upgrade target because project-owner funding, retainage, project margin, and cash collection remain missing.

## What This Proves

The current MasTec answer is:

`customer awards/backlog -> infrastructure project execution -> revenue + adjusted EBITDA + contract-liability timing + DSO + operating cash/capex + credit support`

That is not the same as:

`funded owner project -> signed non-cancellable backlog -> project margin -> billed/collected cash -> cash return`

## Missing Proof

| Missing proof | Why it matters | Next source |
|---|---|---|
| Project-owner funding | Needed to prove backlog is backed by funded customer programs. | Customer award contracts, public owner budgets, utility capex authorizations, and government project records. |
| Signed/non-cancellable backlog | Needed to distinguish contract-grade backlog from softer awards or timing visibility. | Backlog definition, contract terms, cancellation clauses, and backlog aging. |
| Retainage and receivable aging | Needed to test cash collection quality. | Receivable aging, retainage disclosure, billing schedules, and collection history. |
| Project margin | Needed to convert revenue/backlog into return evidence. | Segment and project gross margin, cost-to-complete, change orders, and loss-project reserves. |
| Backlog cohort conversion | Needed to trace specific backlog into revenue and cash. | Backlog roll-forward, burn schedule, and cash collection by cohort. |
| Credit-to-project allocation | Needed to show how debt, liquidity, L/Cs, or surety support specific work. | Credit agreement, borrowing base, L/C schedule, surety capacity, and covenant certificate. |
