# Capital Flow Medallia Lender Allocation Repayment Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass resolves queue item `CFARQ-003` as far as current local evidence allows.

The question is:

`Do Medallia's acquisition debt, bank-credit repayment, private-credit lender identity, and later lender-led ownership transition form one clean capital-flow chain?`

## Resolution Outcome

`hold-after-pass-1 - strong repayment and lender-control evidence, final lender allocation map incomplete`

Medallia is stronger than a generic private-credit borrower lead. It has primary-source acquisition-financing amount evidence, primary-source repayment evidence for an existing Wells Fargo-administered credit agreement, transaction-announcement lender identity, and later lender-led ownership-transition evidence.

It still does not pass the clean-chain test because the documents currently available split the decisive facts across different sources.

## What Passed

| Evidence Point | Status | Local Source |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction value | passed | Thoma Bravo announced the Medallia take-private at `6.4B USD`. |
| Acquisition debt amount | passed | Medallia's July 2021 SEC acquisition 8-K states that lenders party to the July 25, 2021 debt commitment letter committed approximately `1.8B USD` of debt financing. |
| Equity commitment amount | passed | The same SEC 8-K states a Thoma Bravo-affiliated fund committed up to approximately `5B USD` of equity contribution. |
| Private-credit lender identity | passed, but separate source | Thoma Bravo's transaction announcement identifies Blackstone Credit, Apollo-managed funds, KKR Credit, Thoma Bravo Credit, and Antares Capital as debt financing sources. |
| Existing bank-administered credit agreement | passed | Medallia's preliminary proxy identifies an existing credit agreement with Wells Fargo Bank, National Association as administrative agent. |
| Repayment of existing credit agreement | passed | The preliminary proxy says merger funding would repay obligations under the existing credit agreement and related fees and expenses. |
| Lender-led ownership transition | passed | Medallia's 2026 announcement says ownership would transition from Thoma Bravo to an investor group led by Blackstone, Apollo, and FS KKR Capital Corp. |
| New capital in transition | passed | The 2026 announcement states `150M USD` of new capital and a debt-reduction transaction. |

## What Did Not Pass

| Missing Link | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Final debt commitment letter parties | Needed to prove the exact lender allocation behind the `1.8B USD` debt commitment. |
| Definitive post-close credit agreement | Needed to show tranches, pricing, maturity, collateral, administrative agent, revolver structure, and bank roles. |
| Lender allocation schedule | Needed before attributing the full debt stack to named private-credit firms. |
| Funds-flow or payoff letter | Needed to confirm exact payoff amount and treatment of the Wells Fargo-administered facility. |
| Post-close bank-role map | Needed to show whether banks retained revolver, agency, treasury, hedging, custody, or other ancillary roles. |
| Recapitalization agreement | Needed to connect the 2026 lender-led ownership transition to the exact 2021 debt holder base. |

## Current Safe Answer

`Medallia is a strong acquisition-finance, bank-credit-repayment, and lender-control case, but it is not yet a clean lender-allocation or full bank-displacement case.`

Safe sentence:

`Medallia's 2021 take-private had primary-source evidence of approximately 1.8B USD of committed acquisition debt and proxy evidence that merger funding would repay obligations under an existing Wells Fargo-administered credit agreement. Transaction and company sources separately identify private-credit lenders and a later lender-led ownership transition, but the final credit agreement, lender allocation schedule, and post-close bank-role map are still needed before treating Medallia as a clean full-replacement case.`

## Why This Matters

Medallia sharpens the bank-role answer in a different way than Auctane.

Auctane is the cleanest narrow bank-facility termination case.

Medallia is a fuller credit-cycle case:

`sponsor acquisition -> committed acquisition debt -> repayment of an existing bank-administered credit agreement -> later debt stress / recapitalization -> lender-led ownership transition`

That supports a stronger claim about private credit as a control path when sponsor-backed debt stacks become stressed. It does not by itself prove that banks disappeared from the borrower relationship.
