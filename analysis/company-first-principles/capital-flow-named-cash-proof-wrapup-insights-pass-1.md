## Core Conclusion

`The research has moved from a broad capital-flow thesis to a proof-controlled money-movement system. It can now identify who appears to be investing, through which wrapper, into which real-economy destination, what cash/proxy evidence is visible, and exactly which document layer still blocks full proof. It cannot yet claim that any named case proves cash all the way through source, use, receipt or settlement, payback/legal availability, and return.`

The most important result is not a single winning company. The important result is the proof architecture:

`source of capital -> wrapper/instrument -> named destination -> cash use -> operating output -> receipt/settlement/cash conversion -> creditor/legal-entity/payback route -> return model`

That architecture gives the work discipline. It prevents the easy but weak move of treating AUM, backlog, capex, aggregate revenue, borrowing capacity, or EBITDA as proof that money moved all the way through and came back.

## Big Insight 1: This Is A Routing Thesis, Not A One-Channel Thesis

The evidence does not support a clean story like `private credit is replacing banks` or `AI demand is causing all capital formation`.

The better conclusion is:

`Capital is being routed through different wrappers because each economic claim has a different proof requirement.`

Examples:

1. Private credit borrower proof needs holder schedules, facility documents, use-of-proceeds, borrower cash flow, repayment, collateral, and post-close bank-role evidence.
2. Utility/grid proof needs dockets, tariffs, rate factors, project/category costs, regulatory recovery, billing determinants, collected receipts, and earned-return support.
3. LNG and midstream proof needs entity debt, customer contracts, project output, cargo or throughput attribution, trustee/restricted-account mechanics, DSCR, and project cash contribution.
4. Fleet/ABL proof needs borrowing-base certificates, eligible collateral, NOLV/reserve deductions, utilization, rental yield, sale proceeds, and lifecycle return.
5. Insurance capital proof needs statutory legal-entity schedules, Schedule D/BA holdings, asset income, credit quality, liability cost, spread, and borrower allocation.
6. Refinancing proof needs proceeds, redemption settlement, accrued interest, fees, tax, liquidity impact, debt-service math, and operating cash contribution.

The wrapper determines the proof. That is the core operating insight.

## Big Insight 2: We Have Many Strong Bridges, But No Full Named-Cash Proof Yet

The system now has several high-quality partial bridges:

| Case | What Is Strong | Why It Still Fails Full Proof |

| FPL Distribution Inspection | Public PSC route, category cost/recovery proxy, factor support, aggregate SPPCRC revenue, discovery/audit route. | Missing billed/collected customer cash, Distribution Inspection receipt allocation, source-of-funds allocation, legal-entity waterfall, earned-return workpapers. |

| United Rentals | ABL mechanics, collateral route, fleet asset base, rental revenue, operating cash flow, capex coverage, ROIC proxy. | Missing populated borrowing-base certificate, eligible collateral schedule, NOLV/reserves, fleet-class lifecycle return. |
| Ares/Frontline | Operating borrower, Bain sponsor, Ares arranger role, first-lien markers, holder-dollar exposure, commitment clues. | Missing full credit agreement, lender allocation, closing funds-flow, collateral package, borrower financials, repayment route. |

The finding is substantial, but bounded: we can map the money path; we cannot yet certify the full cash loop for any one named case.

## Big Insight 3: FPL Is The Best Public Receipts Route, But Public Does Not Equal Decisive

FPL is the best regulated customer-cash candidate because the public PSC route exposes:

1. a current docket route
2. petition and testimony filings
3. factor and billing-base support
4. amended factor support
5. aggregate SPPCRC revenue
6. Distribution Inspection category cost/recovery proxies
7. discovery request routes
8. response/objection notices
9. audit route
10. public API/document inventory route

That is unusually good public evidence. It shows a real regulated recovery mechanism, not just management commentary.

But it still does not show named cash receipt proof. The missing FPL document family is precise:

1. actual billing determinants by rate class and month
2. billed SPPCRC revenue by rate class/month
3. collected customer cash
4. allocation from aggregate SPPCRC receipts to Distribution Inspection
5. Distribution Inspection subaccount or regulatory ledger
6. source-of-funds/legal-entity funding schedule
7. earned-return workpapers

This is the clearest example of the project’s discipline: even a strong public regulatory route remains a hold if it does not show the actual receipt ledger.

## Big Insight 4: Antamina Is The Best Two-Sided Named Transaction, But Return Is Still A Controlled-Document Problem

Antamina is the closest named asset case because both sides of the initial transfer are visible:

`Wheaton paid 4.300B USD -> BHP received 4.300B USD -> BHP booked streaming arrangement liability -> Wheaton has delivery/cash-back mechanics`

That is better than most cases. It gives named payer, named recipient, named asset/channel, legal obligation, delivery mechanics, and partial cash-back evidence.

But it still breaks at the exact places where investment proof becomes real:

1. Did BHP allocate the proceeds to debt repayment, capex, dividends, buybacks, or general treasury?
2. Which Antamina/BHP PMPA-only deliveries happened after close?
3. What invoices, metal credits, realized prices, and settlement cash correspond to the BHP stream?
4. Which lender dollars funded the PMPA and how are those lenders repaid?
5. What is the after-tax, after-interest, reserve-backed IRR/NPV/payback?

So Antamina is the lead named-asset candidate, but full return proof likely requires controlled transaction, treasury, lender, or valuation documents.

## Big Insight 5: PBF Shows How Far Public SEC Filings Can Get In A Refinancing, And Where They Stop

PBF is useful because the cash movement is cleaner than many operating cases:

1. new notes issued
2. net proceeds disclosed
3. old notes redeemed
4. available cash bridge visible
5. accrued-interest proxy derivable from indenture mechanics
6. simple coupon-relief proxy visible

That gets closer to settlement economics than ordinary debt-balance comparison.

The stop point is also clear:

1. trustee settlement cash is not public
2. paying-agent transfer is not public
3. exact accrued-interest cash is not filed as a settlement statement
4. fee/tax amortization and after-tax NPV are not complete
5. ABL availability and refinery-level cash contribution are still indirect

PBF is the cleanest transaction source/use proxy, not a full refinancing-value proof.

## Big Insight 6: The Hardest Missing Evidence Repeats Across Lanes

The recurring blockers are now obvious:

| Missing Evidence | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| receipts and billing | Proves the payer/customer cash actually came in. |
| settlement ledgers | Proves contractual delivery, invoice, price, and cash settlement. |
| debt waterfalls | Proves capital providers were paid and in what order. |
| legal-entity schedules | Proves where assets, liabilities, income, and obligations actually sit. |
| collateral certificates | Proves legal borrowing availability, not just asset size or liquidity. |
| project/asset cash contribution | Proves the funded thing generated cash, not just output or capacity. |
| return models | Proves whether the cash is enough after time, risk, tax, financing, and capex. |
| source-to-use ledgers | Proves which dollars funded which use instead of relying on same-period coincidence. |

These are not cosmetic gaps. They are the difference between route evidence and proof.

## Big Insight 7: The 519-Company System Is Useful As A Funnel, Not As A Proof Claim

The `519` company universe is now an operating funnel. It helps assign lanes, likely wrappers, proof families, source availability, and next extraction routes.

It does not prove the thesis across all `519` companies.

The right use is:

1. classify companies by capital-flow role
2. select likely proof-rich lanes
3. run primary-source extraction
4. promote only cases with enough evidence
5. preserve queue/hold status for everything else

The wrong use is:

1. count queued companies as evidence
2. infer capital movement from sector membership
3. rank incomparable units together
4. turn pilot-source visibility into universe-wide proof

The funnel matters because it prevents one-off case studies from becoming anecdotal. But the funnel remains a research-control system until primary evidence catches up.

## What We Can Say Now

The strongest safe claim is:

`The research shows capital moving through identifiable wrappers into real-economy destinations: private-credit borrower facilities, BDC and insurance balance sheets, fleet ABL systems, utility recovery mechanisms, LNG/project entities, midstream capacity expansions, and refinancing transactions. Several cases now have named source/use/cash-proxy bridges. The best current candidates are Wheaton/BHP Antamina, FPL Distribution Inspection, PBF refinancing, United Rentals fleet, Ares/Frontline, Cheniere, Matador, and insurance statutory entities. No case yet proves cash all the way through source, use, receipt or settlement, payback/legal availability, and return.`

## What We Should Not Say

Do not say:

1. private credit broadly replaced banks
2. all routed capital is productive growth capex
3. aggregate revenue proves project/customer receipts
4. capex plus EBITDA proves project return
5. borrowing capacity proves collateral availability
6. production or throughput proves cash realization
7. company-level ROIC proves asset-level return
8. public docket route proves actual customer cash
9. combined stream cash flow proves BHP PMPA-only settlement
10. note redemption principal proves full refinancing economics

Those claims would outrun the documents.

## Final Takeaway

The research is now in the right shape.

It started as:

`Where is capital going?`

It has become:

`For each named money path, can we prove who supplied the capital, what instrument carried it, who received it, what it funded, what cash came back, who got paid, and what return was earned?`

That is the correct end-to-end question.

The answer today is:

`We can answer the map and the gaps. We cannot yet certify a full named cash loop. The next move is targeted acquisition of decisive source packages, starting with FPL billing/receipt workpapers, Wheaton/BHP PMPA settlement and lender waterfall materials, PBF trustee settlement documents, URI borrowing-base certificates, and statutory/legal-entity schedules for insurance platforms.`
