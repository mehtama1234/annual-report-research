## Question

`For each major capital router, what named cash-proof chase should we run next, what would it prove, and what document would actually promote it?`

## Short Answer

`The best next move is not to keep expanding abstract platform maps. The best next move is to attach each platform or lane to one proof chase that can be won or held with documents: borrower credit files for private credit, statutory schedules for insurance engines, project waterfalls for infrastructure, billing receipts for regulated recovery, trustee settlements for refinancing, and borrowing-base certificates for collateral systems.`

## What This Adds

The player map answers who routes the money.

The destination-lane map answers where the money likely lands.

This matrix answers what to chase next.

The biggest practical insight is that each platform needs a different proof route:

1. BlackRock/HPS, Blackstone, Ares, and parts of KKR/Carlyle need borrower credit files.
2. Apollo/Athene and KKR/Global Atlantic need insurance legal-entity schedules and investment-income support.
3. Brookfield and the energy lanes need project source/use, customer receipts, debt waterfalls, and return models.
4. FPL needs billed and collected customer cash by category.
5. PBF needs trustee and paying-agent settlement proof.
6. URI needs populated collateral certificates and fleet-return support.
7. Wheaton/BHP needs settlement, waterfall, use-allocation, and return-model proof.

## Safe Claim

`The platform proof chase matrix converts the big-money maps into an execution queue. It identifies the best named proof route for BlackRock/HPS, Blackstone, Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic, Brookfield, Ares, Carlyle, and the strongest comparison channels. The current evidence supports route selection and proof prioritization. It does not yet prove full source-use-receipt-payback-return loops for the platforms.`

`platform-proof-chase-matrix-ready-no-full-proof-yet`

The big-money phase now has a concrete next execution queue: run the named proof chases that can produce receipts, settlement ledgers, waterfalls, legal-entity schedules, collateral certificates, and return models.
