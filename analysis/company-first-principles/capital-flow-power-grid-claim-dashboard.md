## Simple Read

The power/grid work is saying this in plain language:

Money is moving into capacity and constraints.

The capacity is generation, transmission, distribution, gas-fired turbines, midstream logistics, NGL/export systems, fiber, field crews, and civil/site work.

The constraint is that the real economy cannot just want more AI, cloud, electrification, LNG, industrial activity, or data centers. It needs power, wires, fuel logistics, permits, contractors, skilled labor, interconnection, and financing.

So the big claim is not:

`AI demand explains everything.`

The better claim is:

`Power and grid are a capital-absorption lane. Capital is being pulled into the physical bottlenecks that make growth possible.`

That claim is now supported by company-source evidence, but it is not finished. We still need project-level economics, regulatory approvals, outside denominators, and conversion tests.

## Theme And Subtheme Detail

### 1. Regulated Rate-Base Growth And Utility Buildout

This is the cleanest subtheme so far.

The company-source pattern is:

`load growth -> service agreements/load queue -> capital plan -> rate base/capital employed -> recovery mechanism -> customer affordability test`

The companies supporting it are NextEra, Duke, AEP, and Exelon.

What the evidence proves:

- NextEra supports the hybrid model: regulated FPL capital-employed growth plus Energy Resources origination and backlog.
- Duke supports the load-to-build model: data-center electric service agreements, a large capital plan, turbine procurement, and capacity additions.
- AEP supports the grid-gateway model: signed and expected load additions, expanded five-year capital plan, gas turbines, and customer-offset language.
- Exelon supports the regulated wires model: customer base, capital plan, rate-base growth, transmission opportunity, and recovery mechanisms.

What it does not prove yet:

- It does not prove that every load request is binding.
- It does not prove every project has regulatory approval.
- It does not prove realized returns.
- It does not prove affordability pressure will be harmless.
- It does not prove AI is the only driver.

The next real-number pass should extract exact tables for capex by utility, rate base by utility, load queue definitions, riders/recovery mechanisms, customer contribution language, and approvals.

### 2. Merchant And Contracted Power

This subtheme is about owners of scarce generation and contracted power.

The company-source pattern is:

`scarce capacity -> customer contract/PPA/BYOP -> fleet availability -> EBITDA/cash flow -> acquisition or reinvestment`

The companies supporting it are Constellation, Vistra, and NRG.

What the evidence proves:

- Constellation supports the clean-power contracting lane through a large generation fleet and specific long-term PPA metrics.
- Vistra supports the dispatchable scarcity lane through EBITDA, commercial availability, a major generation acquisition, and PPA framing.
- NRG supports the customer-backed model through retail customer scale, generation scale, EBITDA/free-cash-flow metrics, and a BYOP power project tied to a hyperscaler.

What it does not prove yet:

- It does not prove PPA prices.
- It does not prove contract margins.
- It does not prove who carries fuel or construction risk.
- It does not prove the full financing stack.
- It does not prove ratepayer protection.

The next real-number pass should chase PPA/BYOP contract disclosures, 8-K exhibits, project approvals, Texas Energy Fund status, counterparty language, and risk-allocation language.

### 3. Midstream Infrastructure And Fuel Logistics

This subtheme keeps the analysis from being too electric-only.

The company-source pattern is:

`energy demand -> gas/NGL/crude/refined-products logistics -> capex -> volumes -> EBITDA -> debt/liquidity capacity`

The companies supporting it are ONEOK and Targa.

What the evidence proves:

- ONEOK supports the integrated logistics model through pipeline network scale, capex guidance, EBITDA guidance, volume growth, and gathering/processing/fractionation/transport/storage/export language.
- Targa supports the NGL/export buildout model through net growth capital, EBITDA, debt, liquidity, project completions, and record operating volumes.

What it does not prove yet:

- It does not cleanly split maintenance capex from growth capex.
- It does not prove project-level returns.
- It does not prove volume commitments.
- It does not isolate fee-based earnings from commodity or marketing exposure.
- It does not yet compare midstream growth to outside basin, LNG, petrochemical, or power-demand denominators.

The next real-number pass should extract project schedules, project capex, volume tables, fee-based percentage, debt maturities, leverage, and acquisition-integration bridges.

### 4. Buildout Service Layer

This is the most important practical subtheme because capital plans do not build themselves.

The company-source pattern is:

`capital plan/award -> backlog/CAP/book-to-bill -> crews and materials -> physical output -> revenue -> margin -> cash`

The companies supporting it are MasTec, Dycom, Primoris, and Granite.

What the evidence proves:

- MasTec supports the field-output lane through backlog and disclosed miles of telecom, power T&D, pipeline, and renewable work.
- Dycom supports the fiber and data-center building-systems lane through backlog, book-to-bill, operating footprint, and acquisition expansion.
- Primoris supports both demand and execution risk through large backlog and natural-gas power-generation awards, but weak adjusted EBITDA and adjusted net loss.
- Granite supports the civil/site-work lane through CAP, revenue growth, operating cash flow, and mission-critical infrastructure positioning.

What it does not prove yet:

- It does not prove backlog quality.
- It does not prove margin durability.
- It does not prove cash conversion.
- It does not prove contractor capacity is unconstrained.
- It does not prove each contractor is equally well positioned.

Primoris matters because it prevents an easy, sloppy conclusion. Strong demand can still meet project redesign, sequencing, weather, labor productivity, and cost-pressure problems.

The next real-number pass should extract segment backlog, backlog aging, customer concentration, gross margin, working capital, project reserves, claims, and operating-cash-flow conversion.

### 5. Digital Infrastructure Adjacency

This subtheme is not yet upgraded enough.

The hypothesis is:

`AI/data-center growth -> fiber, cabling, building systems, facilities services, security, secure infrastructure, and operations`

Dycom and MasTec already show some of this. ABM and CACI are still queued as adjacency tests.

What the evidence proves:

- The current power/grid work already shows that data-center demand touches utilities, power projects, fiber, and building systems.

What it does not prove yet:

- It does not prove a source-backed chain from hyperscaler capex to ABM/CACI revenue.
- It does not prove secure-facility or mission-critical operations exposure.
- It does not prove labor demand or service intensity.

The next pass should source-upgrade ABM, CACI, and any other high-score digital-infrastructure adjacency companies before this becomes a core claim.

## What We Are Really Saying

The simple version:

Capital is chasing bottlenecks.

For this lane, the bottlenecks are not abstract. They are:

- enough electricity
- enough grid capacity
- enough gas and NGL logistics
- enough transmission and distribution
- enough field crews
- enough fiber and building systems
- enough civil/site execution
- enough financing and regulatory approval

That is why this topic is worth tracking across the broader company universe. The best next structure is not to analyze all `511` or `519` companies equally. The better structure is a two-tier system:

1. Use all-company triage across the full universe to keep the map honest.
2. Run deep extraction only on the highest-signal lanes and companies.

For power/grid, the current deep set is the right kind of starting sample because it covers the full chain:

`generation -> regulated wires -> customer-backed power -> midstream logistics -> contractors -> digital infrastructure adjacency`

## Current Claim Grade

The power/grid lane is at:

`Level 3: official company-source proof`

It is not yet at:

`Level 5: outside-denominator and project-level proof`

To reach Level 5, we need the company evidence plus outside datasets:

- EIA capacity and generation data
- FERC transmission filings
- ISO interconnection queues
- state utility commission orders and rate cases
- project-finance and bank-credit data
- utility capex/rate-base time series
- contractor backlog conversion and cash-flow time series
