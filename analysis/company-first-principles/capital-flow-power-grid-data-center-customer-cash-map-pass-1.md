# Capital Flow Power/Grid Data-Center Customer-Cash Map Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass executes the top-ranked next theme from the annual-report opportunity map:

`Power, grid, and data-center load`

The structured companion table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-power-grid-data-center-customer-cash-map-pass-1.csv`

The question is:

`Who pays for the power/grid/data-center buildout, through what mechanism, who receives the money, and what proof still blocks a full cash-return claim?`

## Short Answer

The local corpus supports a strong but bounded answer:

`Power and data-center load are becoming a capital-formation channel. The evidence shows customer-obligation mechanisms, utility capital plans, turbine procurement, state approvals, regulated recovery routes, contractor backlog, and aggregate customer-revenue proxies. It does not yet prove named customer cash, project-level billing, collected receipts, or earned return by asset.`

In simple words:

`We can see the pipeline from demand to utility investment. We can see some mechanisms that make customers or ratepayers pay. We can see contractors and equipment suppliers that may benefit. We cannot yet tie a named data-center customer dollar all the way through a utility bill, project ledger, contractor cash receipt, debt waterfall, and return model.`

## The Money Path

The power/grid/data-center money path should be tested like this:

`data-center or large-load demand -> customer tariff/ESA/LOA/PPA -> utility capital plan -> project/procurement -> contractor or supplier payment -> rate recovery or customer billing -> collected cash -> earned return`

That path has several proof layers:

| Layer | What We Have | What Is Still Missing |
|---|---|---|
| Demand | AEP load bridge and Duke secured ESA ladder. | Named customer identity, timing, load certainty, and in-service status. |
| Customer obligation | AEP Ohio tariff mechanics and Duke secured ESA evidence. | Executed agreements, deposits, collateral, minimum billing, and customer-specific payments. |
| Capital formation | AEP and Duke capital plans; turbine procurement; Duke Anderson County approval. | Project-level source/use, final approved cost, CWIP/rate-base treatment, and financing allocation. |
| Operating beneficiary | Sterling and MasTec backlog/revenue/cash proxies; turbine procurement route. | Project-owner funding, contractor cash collection, retainage, margin, OEM payment and delivery terms. |
| Customer cash | FPL aggregate SPPCRC revenue and rate-factor authority. | Billing determinants, billed revenue, collected cash, category allocation ledger, and earned-return workpapers. |
| Return | Regulated recovery and company cash proxies. | Asset-level ROE, project EBITDA/cash contribution, debt service, tax, timing, and disproof tests. |

## What This Tells Us

The theme is not just:

`AI needs electricity`

That is too vague.

The document-backed theme is:

`Large-load demand is being translated into enforceable utility and infrastructure money paths. Some paths run through customer agreements and tariffs. Some run through regulated recovery. Some run through contractors, equipment suppliers, public debt, equity, and infrastructure platforms. The proof frontier is actual customer cash and asset-level return.`

That is a much stronger writing theme because it gives us a mechanism.

## The Best Case Today

The best receipt route is still FPL:

| FPL Layer | Status |
|---|---|
| Regulated recovery mechanism | Visible |
| Approved rate-factor authority | Visible |
| Aggregate clause revenue | Visible |
| Monthly aggregate revenue timing | Visible |
| Distribution Inspection category recovery | Visible |
| Billing determinants | Missing |
| Category billed revenue | Missing |
| Category collected cash | Missing |
| Earned return by category | Missing |

FPL is valuable because public utility filings can get closer to receipts than most corporate annual reports.

But the boundary remains:

`Aggregate clause revenue is not the same as Distribution Inspection-specific collected customer cash.`

## The Best Data-Center Load Route

The best data-center/load route is AEP plus Duke:

| Company | Why It Matters | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| AEP | It shows a large load bridge, a larger capital plan, secured turbine capacity, and customer-obligation tariff mechanics. | Not every GW is energized, approved, recoverable, or earning return. |
| Duke | It shows secured data-center ESAs, under-construction load, a large capital plan, turbine procurement, a generation project table, and one named approved project. | Secured ESAs and project approval still need billing, in-service, recovery, and earned-return proof. |

This is enough for a strong bridge claim:

`AEP and Duke show that data-center and large-load demand is moving into utility capital-formation channels.`

It is not enough for:

`Data-center customers have already paid for all the disclosed grid investment.`

## The Best Operating Beneficiary Route

The contractor and supplier beneficiary route is currently:

`utility/customer demand -> project backlog/procurement -> contractor or equipment supplier revenue/cash`

The best visible operating beneficiaries in the local power/grid stack are:

1. Sterling
2. MasTec
3. turbine and equipment suppliers still requiring named supplier extraction

Sterling and MasTec matter because they can show whether the buildout becomes backlog, revenue, EBITDA, operating cash, contract liabilities, and credit support.

The gap is project-owner funding and cash collection.
