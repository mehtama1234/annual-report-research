## What We Found

The power/grid lane is not one story. It has at least three different capital-flow mechanisms.

| Mechanism | Companies | What Capital Is Doing |
|---|---|---|
| Regulated utility buildout | NextEra, Duke | Capital becomes rate base, generation, transmission, distribution, storage, and utility service capacity. |
| Merchant and customer-backed power | Constellation, Vistra, NRG | Capital and contracts reprice scarce generation, nuclear availability, customer-backed gas projects, PPAs, and dispatchable fleet value. |
| Physical execution and logistics | ONEOK, Targa, MasTec, Dycom, Primoris, Granite | Capital becomes pipelines, fractionators, export assets, fiber routes, grid work, data-center site work, construction backlog, and field labor demand. |

The simple conclusion:

`AI and electrification are not only technology themes. They are becoming power contracts, rate-base plans, gas turbines, NGL systems, construction backlog, fiber miles, and civil site work.`

## Company Findings

### Constellation

Extracted signals:

- `55 GW` total generation fleet.
- `920 MW` of additional long-term clean-power PPAs in Q2 `2026`.
- `380 MW` CyrusOne Phase 1 agreement in Q1 `2026`, with an exclusive Phase 2 path for another `380 MW` noted in the source analysis.

Claim this supports:

Constellation is a scarce clean-reliability platform. Its assets are becoming more valuable because large customers need reliable power that can also satisfy clean-energy requirements.

Boundary:

This does not yet prove attractive PPA economics or regulatory certainty. The next source pass needs the underlying agreement language, duration, pricing or margin clues, and approval path.

### NextEra

Extracted signals:

- FPL regulatory capital employed grew from `$72.9B` to `$79.7B` in Q2 `2026`, about `9.3%`.
- Energy Resources added `3.6 GW` of renewables and storage origination in Q2 `2026`.
- Energy Resources backlog was about `29.8 GW` at year-end `2025`.

Claim this supports:

NextEra is an electricity-growth development platform. It has both a regulated Florida base and a development arm that can turn power demand into backlog and capital deployment.

Boundary:

Backlog is not operating capacity. The next proof layer must track backlog conversion, interconnection status, project economics, capital cost, and FPL regulatory recovery.

### Duke

Extracted signals:

- `7.6 GW` of secured data-center electric service agreements in Q1 `2026`.
- `$103B` capital plan for `2026` through `2030`.
- Roughly `14 GW` of capacity targeted by `2031`.

Claim this supports:

Duke is a regulated load-conversion machine. Data-center and regional economic-development demand is becoming utility planning, capital spend, generation, and rate-case work.

Boundary:

The claim needs regulatory proof. The next pass should split Duke's capital plan into generation, grid, transmission, distribution, and other categories, then match the plan to approved recovery and service-agreement milestones.

### Vistra

Extracted signals:

- Q2 `2026` Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA of `$1.767B`.
- At least `97%` commercial availability across the fleet during extreme heat.
- Pending `5,500 MW` Cogentrix acquisition in Q1 `2026`.

Claim this supports:

Vistra shows the merchant route through the power bottleneck. Scarce dispatchable assets can become earnings and contract value without the same regulated rate-base mechanism used by traditional utilities.

Boundary:

The next pass must separate durable scarcity economics from commodity, hedge, acquisition, and one-period effects.

### NRG

Extracted signals:

- Roughly `8M` customers.
- About `25 GW` of generation.
- Q2 `2026` Bring Your Own Power project tied to a `1.2 GW` Texas combined-cycle gas project for a cloud and AI hyperscaler.

Claim this supports:

NRG shows a customer-backed power model. The important idea is not only building power, but assigning the cost and responsibility for new capacity to the large-load customer creating demand.

Boundary:

The next pass needs the contract structure, financing path, project approval status, and whether existing retail customers are insulated from cost shifts.

### AEP

Extracted signals:

- Q4 `2025` signed incremental load by `2030`: `56 GW`.
- Q1 `2026` signed incremental load by `2030`: `63 GW`.
- Q2 `2026` expected new load additions by `2030`: `69 GW`.
- Five-year capital plan rose from `$72B` at year-end `2025` to `$78B` by Q1 `2026`.
- Q2 `2026` materials said AEP had secured about `13 GW` of gas-fired turbine capacity, with another `10 GW` under evaluation.
- Q2 `2026` materials highlighted up to `$16B` of expected customer cost offsets and about `$1.4B` of projected customer savings from DOE loans and grants.

Claim this supports:

AEP is the cleanest grid-gateway case in the current power set. It shows load growth becoming transmission, distribution, generation procurement, capital-plan expansion, rate-base opportunity, and affordability engineering.

Boundary:

The next pass must reconcile signed load versus expected load, identify service-territory and customer concentration, separate approved investment from opportunity, and test whether affordability offsets are enough to support regulatory recovery.

### Exelon

Extracted signals:

- Almost `11M` customers through six fully regulated transmission and distribution utilities.
- Q1 `2026` four-year capital plan of `$41.7B`.
- Q1 `2026` expected rate-base growth of `7.9%`.
- Q1 `2026` additional transmission opportunity of `$12B` to `$17B` beyond the base plan.
- Year-end `2025` materials said close to `90%` of rate base was covered by established recovery mechanisms through `2026` to `2027`.

Claim this supports:

Exelon is the regulated wires control case. It shows that power/grid capital flow is not only new generation or hyperscaler load. Transmission, distribution, reliability, and established recovery mechanisms can be their own capital-absorption lane.

Boundary:

The next pass must separate approved capital from opportunity, identify the utility-by-utility capital split, and test whether recovery mechanisms remain durable under affordability pressure.

### ONEOK

Extracted signals:

- Approximately `60,000` miles of pipeline network.
- Q2 `2026` total capital-expenditure guidance of roughly `$2.7B` to `$3.2B`.
- Q2 `2026` adjusted EBITDA guidance raised to `$8.2B` to `$8.5B`.

Claim this supports:

ONEOK shows that infrastructure capital is also flowing into hydrocarbon logistics: natural gas, NGLs, refined products, crude, storage, transportation, and optimization.

Boundary:

The next pass needs to split maintenance capex from growth capex and tie spend to volume, project completion, or fee-based earnings.

### Targa

Extracted signals:

- Q2 `2026` net growth capital of roughly `$4.5B`.
- Q2 `2026` total consolidated debt of `$19.578B`.
- Q2 `2026` adjusted EBITDA expected near the top end of `$5.7B` to `$5.9B`.

Claim this supports:

Targa is a growth-heavy NGL and export infrastructure buildout platform. It is a capital-flow story because the company is still building plants, fractionators, pipelines, and export-related assets while managing a large debt stack.

Boundary:

The next pass needs project-by-project proof and a leverage/liquidity view. Growth capital only supports the thesis if it becomes throughput and cash flow.

### MasTec

Extracted signals:

- Q2 `2026` backlog of `$21.4B`.
- Fiscal `2025` physical output included over `15,000` miles of telecommunications networks, more than `1,000` miles of oil and gas pipelines, more than `14,000` miles of power transmission and distribution lines, and more than `3.6 GW` of renewable energy installed.

Claim this supports:

MasTec is one of the clearest physical-layer companies in the archive. AI, telecom, power delivery, pipeline infrastructure, and clean energy become work only when a contractor can mobilize crews, rights-of-way, equipment, and project management.

Boundary:

Backlog and miles built are not margin proof. The next pass needs segment margins, customer concentration, working-capital usage, and conversion of backlog into cash.

### Dycom

Extracted signals:

- Fiscal Q1 `2027` backlog of `$11.906B`.
- Fiscal Q1 `2027` book-to-bill of `2.2x`.
- Annual materials described `40` operating companies across all `50` states.

Claim this supports:

Dycom shows that AI and data-center demand are becoming fiber, long-haul routes, inside-plant cabling, building systems, and skilled-field-labor demand.

Boundary:

Customer concentration and subsidy timing matter. The next pass should test how much of the backlog is organic versus acquisition-driven and how much depends on BEAD or large telecom customer capex.

### Primoris

Extracted signals:

- Q2 `2026` total backlog of `$13.9B`.
- Q2 `2026` master service agreement backlog of `$8.2B`.
- Roughly `$1.4B` of natural-gas power-generation awards during Q2 `2026`.

Claim this supports:

Primoris is useful because it shows both sides of the buildout. Demand and bookings can be extremely strong, but execution trouble can still produce weak margins or losses.

Boundary:

This is a quality filter for the whole lane. Strong capital inflow does not automatically mean strong shareholder economics if projects are mispriced, redesigned, delayed, or labor-constrained.

### Granite

Extracted signals:

- Q2 `2026` CAP of `$7.4B`.
- Q2 `2026` revenue increased `29%` year over year to about `$1.46B`.
- Full-year `2025` operating cash flow reached `$469M`, or `10.6%` of revenue.

Claim this supports:

Granite shows the civil-infrastructure side of capital absorption: roads, bridges, rail, airport, border, water, and data-center site development.

Boundary:

The next pass needs to verify CAP definitions and funding status. Public and mission-critical demand only becomes a strong capital-flow claim if awards convert into profitable cash flow.

## What This Changes In The Big Picture

The private-credit work tracks the source and routing of money.

This pass tracks the destination and absorption of money.

The big picture becomes:

`capital seeks yield and growth -> power/load demand creates a physical bottleneck -> utilities and generators fund capacity -> contractors and midstream systems turn money into physical infrastructure -> the proof is in capex, GW, PPAs, rate base, debt, backlog, miles, volumes, and cash conversion`
