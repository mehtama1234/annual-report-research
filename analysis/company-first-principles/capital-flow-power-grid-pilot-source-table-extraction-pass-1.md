## Evidence-Backed Answers

### 1. ONEOK

ONEOK is a capital-intensive integrated midstream system, not just a static pipeline owner.

The evidence shows:

- 2026 capital-expenditure guidance of roughly `$2.7B` to `$3.2B`
- Q2 2026 capital expenditures of about `$613M`, including about `$101M` maintenance capital
- higher Q2 2026 operating volumes across NGL raw feed, refined-products shipping, and natural-gas processing
- an approximately `60,000-mile` pipeline network
- Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of `$2.121B`

The safe answer is that ONEOK has a visible capital-use, output, and cash-generation bridge at company level.

The open question is whether specific projects or acquired systems have return-grade evidence. The next documents are project-level capex tables, segment volume schedules, and contribution bridges.

### 2. MasTec

MasTec is the pilot's cleanest contractor backlog case.

The evidence shows:

- Q2 2026 backlog of `$21.4B`
- Q2 2026 revenue of `$4.374B`
- Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of `$384.2M`
- FY 2025 physical work across telecom networks, oil and gas pipelines, power transmission and distribution, and renewable energy
- FY 2025 18-month backlog of `$19.0B`

The safe answer is that MasTec has visible demand, output, revenue conversion, and earnings proxy evidence.

The open question is backlog quality. Backlog is not return proof until the source table adds segment margin, funded status, cancellation terms, working-capital drag, customer concentration, retainage, and change-order recovery.

### 3. Plains All American

Plains is the clearest capital-stack transformation case in this mini-batch.

The evidence shows:

- 2026 organic growth capital guidance increased to `$400M` to `$450M`
- a named `75 Mbbl/d` Cactus III pipeline expansion
- approximately `$2.9B` of debt reduction funded by Canadian NGL sale proceeds
- Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA attributable to PAA of `$738M`
- Q2 2026 pro forma leverage of `3.3x`

The safe answer is that Plains has visible capital-use, named project-output, debt-reduction, and leverage-durability evidence.

The open question is whether the crude-focused portfolio earns better return on capital after simplification. The next documents are shipper commitments, tariff terms, project contribution tables, credit documents, and segment cash-flow bridges.

### 4. Targa

Targa is the highest-growth physical-output case in the mini-batch.

The evidence shows:

- roughly `$4.5B` of 2026 net growth capital
- named project completions: `Train 11`, the `Delaware Express` NGL Pipeline expansion, and the `East Driver` processing plant
- Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of `$1.603B`
- consolidated liquidity of approximately `$3.2B`
- record operating volumes across Permian inlet, NGL transportation, fractionation, and LPG exports

The safe answer is that Targa has a visible source/use/output/cash proxy bridge.

The open question is whether the growth queue is earning project-level returns rather than simply expanding the asset base. The next documents are project cost schedules, utilization data, customer contracts, financing source, and contribution by named project.

### 5. NextEra

NextEra is the hybrid regulated-recovery plus contracted-development case.

The evidence shows:

- FPL Q2 2026 regulatory capital employed of `$79.7B`, up from `$72.9B`
- Q2 2026 Energy Resources renewables and storage origination additions of `3.6 GW`
- Energy Resources year-end 2025 backlog around `29.8 GW`
- FPL Q2 2026 EPS of `$0.67`
- management's reiterated `8%+` adjusted EPS CAGR target through 2032

The safe answer is that NextEra has regulated-capital and contracted-output proxy evidence.

The open question is proof quality by lane. FPL needs docket/rider/order evidence for recovery; Energy Resources needs PPA, interconnection, financing, in-service, and project-return evidence.

## Cross-Batch Findings

| Finding | Evidence | Meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capex is visible before return is visible. | ONEOK, Plains, Targa, and NextEra all disclose large capital or capital-employed numbers. | The pilot can map source/use scale quickly. | Capex does not equal return. |
| Physical output is easier to prove than project economics. | ONEOK volumes, MasTec built units, Plains Cactus III expansion, Targa project starts, and NextEra GW backlog are visible. | The next layer should use output units as denominators. | Output units do not prove margins, utilization, or cost of capital. |
| Backlog needs harsher controls than capex. | MasTec has backlog plus revenue and EBITDA, but not project-level margin quality. | Backlog can be a strong demand signal. | Backlog is not cash recovery until conversion and margins are visible. |
| Midstream cases need project-level contracts. | ONEOK, Plains, and Targa all show throughput or project additions. | The lane is strong for operating-system evidence. | It remains below return-grade without contract/tariff/utilization proof. |
| NextEra must be split into two evidence lanes. | FPL capital employed and Energy Resources backlog answer different mechanisms. | Regulated recovery and contracted development should not be blended. | Renewable backlog is not the same thing as approved utility recovery. |

## Hypotheses To Test Next

1. Midstream companies with named capacity additions and rising EBITDA will become return-grade only when project-level utilization, contract terms, and contribution are extracted.
2. Infrastructure contractors with large backlog are better operating-return candidates when backlog growth is paired with segment margin, cash conversion, and working-capital evidence.
3. Regulated utilities have the cleanest recovery mechanism, but only at the jurisdiction/project level, not at consolidated-company level.
4. Developer-platform utilities may look stronger than pure regulated utilities on growth, but weaker on proof unless PPAs, in-service dates, and financing are tied to backlog.
5. The `519` company system will scale if each mini-batch keeps the same discipline: source, use, output, recovery, durability, disproof, and next document.
