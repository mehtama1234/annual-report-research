## Claim-Level Upgrade

The first-pass rows were mostly Level 2:

`quantified company exposure`

This source upgrade moves selected rows toward Level 3:

`instrument, document, or official-source proof`

That does not mean the system claim is finished. It means the number has an official source package behind it and is ready for page/table extraction.

## Source-Backed Utility Evidence

### Constellation

Source packages:

- `CEG-T18` to `CEG-T22`: Q2 `2026` release page, release PDF, presentation, 10-Q, and 8-K.
- `CEG-T13` to `CEG-T17`: Q1 `2026` release page, release PDF, presentation, 10-Q, and 8-K.

## What This Adds To The Theme/Subtheme Work

The theme/subtheme map said the power lane had five subthemes:

1. regulated rate-base growth
2. merchant and contracted power
3. midstream infrastructure
4. buildout service layer
5. digital infrastructure adjacency

This source upgrade strengthens the first two immediately:

| Subtheme | Stronger Evidence Now |
|---|---|
| Regulated rate-base growth | NextEra, Duke, AEP, and Exelon have official-source packages for capital-employed growth, capital plans, ESAs/load queues, turbines, capacity additions, transmission opportunity, recovery mechanisms, and affordability offsets. |
| Merchant and contracted power | Constellation, Vistra, and NRG have official-source packages for PPAs, availability, acquisitions, BYOP, generation, and customer-backed power. |
| Midstream infrastructure | ONEOK and Targa have official-source packages for capex guidance, NGL/fractionation/export volumes, EBITDA guidance, debt, liquidity, and project execution. |
| Buildout service layer | MasTec, Dycom, Primoris, and Granite have official-source packages for backlog, book-to-bill, CAP, miles built, renewable GW installed, natural-gas generation awards, and margin/cash-conversion risk. |

The next source-upgrade round should do the same for:

- ABM and CACI on digital-infrastructure adjacency.
- Plains and additional midstream peers if we want a broader hydrocarbon logistics denominator.

## Current Bottom Line

The power/grid lane is now more than a thematic claim.

For the first wave of companies, there is an official document trail behind the numbers:

`source ledger -> official release/deck/filing -> metric -> bounded claim -> missing proof`

The most defensible current statement is:

`Power and infrastructure demand are becoming visible in official company disclosures as contracted MW, service agreements, capital plans, rate-base-like capital, generation acquisitions, fleet availability, customer-backed power projects, midstream capex, NGL/export volumes, contractor backlog, book-to-bill, CAP, miles built, awards, and cash-flow/EBITDA bridges.`

The claim is not yet:

`AI demand alone explains all utility value creation.`

That would still require customer-specific contract terms, outside load denominators, regulatory approvals, project conversion, and affordability evidence.
