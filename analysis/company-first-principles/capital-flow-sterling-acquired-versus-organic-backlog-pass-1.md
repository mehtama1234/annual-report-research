# Capital Flow Sterling Acquired Versus Organic Backlog Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass answers the next Sterling proof question:

`How much of the backlog story is organic demand, and how much came from acquired platforms?`

The companion data file is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-sterling-acquired-versus-organic-backlog-pass-1.csv`

## Simple Answer

Sterling's demand signal is real, but the clean claim is not pure organic backlog growth.

The better claim is:

`Sterling is both an organic customer-project execution platform and an acquisition-enabled backlog platform.`

The evidence splits into two periods:

- Q4 `2025`: CEC was the acquired contributor.
- Q2 `2026`: CEC plus Stone Ridge were the acquired contributors.

## What Changed The Claim

At Q2 `2026`:

| Bucket | Total | Acquired Contribution | Derived Non-Acquired Proxy | Acquired Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Signed backlog | `4.3336B USD` | `1.32B USD` | `3.0136B USD` | `30.5%` |
| Unsigned awards | `1.28B USD` | `1.24B USD` | `40.0M USD` | `96.9%` |
| Combined backlog | `5.62B USD` | `2.56B USD` | `3.06B USD` | `45.6%` |

That is the main finding.

Sterling still had meaningful organic backlog growth. The Q2 `2026` release says backlog increased `116%` year over year and `50%` organically, while combined backlog increased `150%` year over year and `36%` organically.

But nearly all of the Q2 `2026` unsigned-award bucket came from CEC and Stone Ridge. That makes unsigned awards the most important caution bucket.

## Acquisition Evidence

CEC was a large E-Infrastructure platform acquisition:

- CEC total fair value of consideration transferred: `561.589M USD`
- CEC acquired contract assets: `40.632M USD`
- CEC assumed contract liabilities: `53.555M USD`

Stone Ridge added a smaller site-development platform:

- Stone Ridge total fair value of consideration transferred: `178.007M USD`
- Stone Ridge acquired contract assets: `5.214M USD`
- Stone Ridge assumed contract liabilities: `5.183M USD`

These acquisition entries matter because they show that some contract-capital balances and backlog visibility arrived through acquisition accounting, not just same-store project awards.

## Q4 2025 Bridge

At Q4 `2025`, Sterling reported:

- signed backlog of about `3.01B USD`
- CEC contribution to signed backlog of `488.9M USD`
- combined backlog of `3.31B USD`
- CEC contribution to combined backlog of `715.2M USD`
- unsigned awards of `300.7M USD`, including `226.4M USD` from CEC

That means the acquisition impact was already material at year-end `2025`, before the Stone Ridge acquisition.

## Q2 2026 Bridge

At Q2 `2026`, the acquisition contribution became much larger:

- CEC and Stone Ridge contributed `1.32B USD` to signed backlog.
- CEC and Stone Ridge contributed `1.24B USD` to unsigned awards.
- The combined acquired signed-plus-unsigned contribution was `2.56B USD`.
- Acquisitions contributed `250.8M USD` of Q2 `2026` revenue.

This does not weaken Sterling's thesis. It changes the mechanism.

Sterling is not only receiving organic demand from mission-critical customer projects. It is also buying capability, geography, customer access, and backlog exposure.

## Claim Update

Promote:

`Sterling's backlog growth is both organic and acquisition-enabled. The time-series evidence supports customer-project execution, while the acquired-versus-organic split shows that CEC and Stone Ridge materially expanded the signed and unsigned backlog funnel.`

Do not promote:

`Sterling's backlog growth is purely organic, or all of its combined backlog is signed, funded, non-cancellable demand.`

## What This Means For Funding Type

This strengthens the reason Sterling uses a flexible corporate revolver.

The revolver is not just a project-timing tool. It is also an acquisition-platform tool:

- CEC and Stone Ridge added backlog visibility.
- Acquisitions added revenue.
- Acquired platforms brought contract assets and contract liabilities.
- Unsigned awards became a larger, more acquisition-linked part of the funnel.

That explains why Sterling needs liquidity for working capital, L/C and bonding support, acquisition consideration, integration, and future awards rather than only one named project-finance vehicle.

## Still Missing

The next proof layer is:

- acquired backlog conversion into signed work, revenue, margin, and cash
- organic same-store awards, burn, cancellations, and RPO growth
- CEC versus Stone Ridge margin and customer concentration
- project-owner source-of-funds
- actual L/C and surety usage tied to backlog
- unsigned-award conversion history

## Simple Version

Sterling is not just riding a demand wave.

It is also buying into the demand wave.

That is useful, but it changes the sentence we are allowed to write.
