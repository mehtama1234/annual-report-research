# Capital Flow Sterling Backlog Credit-Platform Conversion Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass resolves `CFARQ-012`:

`Does Sterling's backlog plus July 2026 credit-platform reset convert into revenue and cash, or is it only backlog plus financing capacity?`

The operating table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-sterling-backlog-credit-platform-conversion-pass-1.csv`

## Short Answer

Yes, with boundaries.

Sterling can be upgraded from:

`credit-platform reset plus backlog context`

to:

`conversion-supported backlog/acquisition execution platform`

The evidence supports a company-level mechanism:

`customer/project commitments -> contract timing and retainage -> backlog/RPO -> revenue -> operating cash after capex -> flexible revolver/L/C/surety wrapper`

It does not prove:

`named project-owner source-of-funds -> specific Sterling credit draw or L/C -> project margin -> project cash collection`

## Why This Is An Upgrade

The acceptance rule was:

`Pass if backlog and credit-platform reset can be tied to repeated revenue/cash conversion or acquisition/buildout execution.`

That test is met at company-level proxy grade.

The strongest evidence is:

| Layer | Evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Credit wrapper | `1.500B USD` revolving commitments | Agreement-backed platform liquidity, not presentation-only capacity. |
| Guarantee capacity | `600M USD` L/C sublimit | L/C capacity is `40.0%` of the revolver and fits bid/performance/payment support. |
| Timing support | `646.306M USD` Q2 2026 net contract-liability timing | Contract timing is large enough to matter to the funding model. |
| Backlog/RPO conversion | RPO rose from `2.055081B USD` in Q3 2024 to `4.233618B USD` in Q2 2026 | The project commitment base expanded materially over eight quarters. |
| Cash conversion | Quarterly operating cash flow less capex was positive in every observed quarter | Company-level conversion is visible, not only backlog accumulation. |
| Debt intensity | Debt/RPO fell from `15.8%` to `6.7%` | Sterling is not carrying debt equal to the whole project value. |
| Acquisition bridge | CEC and Stone Ridge contributed `2.56B USD` of Q2 2026 combined signed and unsigned backlog visibility | Backlog growth is both organic and acquisition-enabled. |

## Mechanism We Can Now Describe

Sterling looks less like a company financing one separable asset and more like a contractor/platform executing customer-funded or customer-sponsored projects.

Its funding need appears to be the execution layer:

- working-capital timing
- contract assets and liabilities
- retainage
- bid, performance, payment, and maintenance guarantees
- letters of credit
- acquisitions
- capex and integration
- refinancing of existing debt

That explains why the July 2026 agreement is a large secured revolver with L/C and acquisition flexibility rather than a clean project-finance vehicle.

## What We Can Say Now

Promote:

`Sterling is a conversion-supported backlog/acquisition execution platform. Public filings and the July 2026 credit agreement show a large backlog/RPO base, favorable contract-liability timing, repeated positive operating cash flow after capex, material acquired backlog contribution, and a 1.500B USD secured revolver with a 600M USD L/C sublimit that fits working-capital, guarantee, acquisition, capex, and refinancing needs.`

## What We Still Cannot Say

Do not promote:

`Sterling's entire backlog is signed, funded, non-cancellable, organic, high-margin, or tied to named customer/project financing.`

Also do not promote:

`Sterling has disclosed actual post-reset L/C outstanding, bonded backlog, formal covenant cushion, project-level margin, or source-to-project credit allocation.`

## Open Questions

| Question | Why It Matters | Best Evidence Needed |
|---|---|---|
| Which projects are owner-funded, lender-funded, publicly funded, or internally bridged? | This is the missing project-source-of-funds bridge. | Customer/project announcements, owner capex budgets, municipal/utility/hyperscaler filings, project financing records. |
| How much L/C and surety capacity is actually used after the reset? | Capacity explains the wrapper; usage proves the operating need. | Post-reset 10-Q debt note, L/C outstanding table, surety program disclosure, bond records. |
| How much acquired backlog converts into revenue, margin, and cash? | Acquisitions are a large part of the backlog funnel. | CEC and Stone Ridge backlog burn, segment revenue, margin, integration and customer-concentration disclosures. |
| What is organic backlog conversion excluding CEC and Stone Ridge? | This separates market demand from acquired exposure. | Same-store backlog additions, awards, burn, cancellations, and RPO by segment. |
| Are there cancellation, cost-overrun, reserve, or collection risks inside backlog? | This decides whether backlog quality is strong or only large. | Project reserve notes, claims, unapproved change orders, retainage collection, receivable aging. |
| Is Sterling formally covenant-cushion positive after July 2026? | Directional leverage looks low, but covenant definitions matter. | Compliance certificate, agreement-defined EBITDA, post-close borrowing, cash-netting details. |
