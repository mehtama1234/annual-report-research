# Capital Flow Sterling Backlog Customer-Funding Pass 1

## Purpose

This page answers the current "why" question for Sterling:

`Why does Sterling use a flexible corporate credit wrapper instead of obvious project debt, ABL, or entity-level finance?`

The operating table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-sterling-backlog-customer-funding-pass-1.csv`

## Simple Answer

The simple answer is:

`Sterling appears to be a customer-project execution platform, not primarily an owned-asset funding vehicle.`

Its customers or project sponsors likely fund the upstream data-center, manufacturing, transportation, infrastructure, and site-development assets. Sterling funds the execution layer: labor, equipment, timing gaps, retainage, bonding, letters of credit, acquisitions, and working capital.

That explains why the July `2026` credit agreement looks like a large flexible corporate revolver:

- `1.500B USD` revolving commitments
- `600M USD` L/C sublimit
- `50M USD` swing-line sublimit
- permitted uses for refinancing, capex, working capital, acquisitions, and general corporate purposes
- acquisition holiday leverage up to `4.00x`
- Project Specific JV boundaries

The funding container fits the job: it is built for timing, guarantees, and platform flexibility rather than a single named project.

Sterling's Q2 `2026` 10-Q makes the customer/project timing evidence stronger:

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Contract liabilities | `802.601M USD` | Customer billing or related liability timing ahead of revenue recognition is large. |
| Contract assets | `156.295M USD` | Sterling also has revenue-before-billing exposure. |
| Contract liabilities less contract assets | `646.306M USD` | Net contract timing was liability-positive at quarter end. |

This supports a specific funding hypothesis:

`Sterling does not need to finance the whole asset being built. It needs liquidity to bridge execution timing around customer contracts.`

That means the good claim is not "Sterling has 5.62B USD of signed funded backlog."

`Sterling had 4.3336B USD of backlog plus 1.28B USD of unsigned-award visibility and another 1.4B USD of future-phase opportunities.`

## Acquisition Subtheme

The backlog growth is not purely organic.

CEC and Stone Ridge contributed:

- `1.32B USD` to signed backlog
- `1.24B USD` to unsigned awards
- `2.56B USD` combined
- about `45.6%` of total combined backlog

That changes the "why" answer. Sterling is not only converting organic project demand. It is also buying capability, customer access, geography, and backlog exposure.

That supports a second hypothesis:

`Sterling's revolver is partly an acquisition-platform tool.`

The credit agreement's permitted-acquisition language, incremental capacity, and acquisition leverage holiday now fit the operating evidence.

## Why Different Funding Types Exist

The current cross-company answer is:

`Different funding exists because different assets create different underwritable cash-flow and collateral patterns.`

| Company Type | Funding Type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Energy Transfer | Corporate revolver, commercial paper, notes | Diversified midstream network cash flow can be underwritten at the platform level. |
| Cheniere | Entity/project-like debt | LNG trains, terminals, SPAs, and issuer entities can be legally separated. |
| United Rentals | ABL, AR securitization, fleet debt, notes | Receivables and rental fleet are measurable collateral. |
| Sterling | Secured revolver, L/C capacity, acquisition flexibility | Backlog execution needs timing liquidity, bonding/L/C support, acquisitions, and working capital. |
| Utilities | Debt/equity with rate recovery | Regulators may allow customers to repay approved assets through rates. |
| Contractors | Customer-funded backlog plus working-capital finance | The customer owns/funds the asset; the contractor funds execution timing and guarantees. |

## Hypothesis Strength

### Stronger Now

The Sterling hypothesis is stronger because we have:

- actual credit agreement terms
- contract liabilities and assets
- retainage balances
- backlog/RPO/MSA buckets
- unsigned-award split
- acquisition contribution
- H1 cash conversion
- L/C and bonding language

### Still Not Proven

It is still not project-source-of-funds-grade because we do not yet have:

- named project owner funding sources
- project-level source-and-use schedules
- customer capex budgets tied to Sterling backlog
- backlog margin by project or segment
- L/C outstanding amount after the July `2026` facility reset
- bonding exposure by contract
- cancellation or conversion history for unsigned awards
- organic versus acquired backlog roll-forward after CEC and Stone Ridge

## Claim We Can Use

Promote:

`Sterling appears to be a customer-project execution and acquisition platform. Its funding need is less about owning the whole asset and more about bridging contract timing, retainage, bonding/L/C support, working capital, capex, acquisitions, and backlog conversion.`

Do not promote:

`Sterling's entire backlog is fully customer-funded and risk-free.`
