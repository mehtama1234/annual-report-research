# Capital Flow Sterling Backlog Return/Risk Bridge Pass 1

## Purpose

This page answers the second operating-return scorecard question:

`Does Sterling backlog convert into cash-returning work or only expand reported RPO?`

The structured companion table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-sterling-backlog-return-risk-pass-1.csv`

## Short Answer

Sterling can be upgraded from `strong-company-conversion-proxy` to `backlog return-and-risk proxy`.

The evidence is stronger than a simple backlog story because signed backlog, RPO, contract-liability timing, operating cash flow after capex, debt/RPO, acquisition split, and the July 2026 revolver/L/C/surety wrapper can be read together.

The evidence is still not project-return-grade. It does not yet prove project-owner funding, project margin, actual post-reset L/C usage, bonded backlog, cancellation history, retainage collection, or source-to-project allocation.

## Mechanism

The working mechanism is:

`customer/project commitments -> signed and unsigned backlog/RPO -> contract assets, contract liabilities, and retainage -> revenue and cash conversion -> revolver/L/C/surety wrapper -> acquisition-enabled backlog expansion -> project-level risk boundary`

This is a different capital-flow pattern from URI. URI is fleet-collateral and rental-output evidence. Sterling is backlog, contract timing, guarantee capacity, and project-execution evidence.

## Quality Read

The positive read is:

- signed backlog and RPO are large
- most RPO is expected within 12 months
- operating cash flow after capex is positive in the latest half-year evidence
- debt intensity against RPO is low in the observed Q2 2026 snapshot
- the July 2026 revolver, L/C, and surety mechanics fit Sterling's execution model

The caution read is:

- `22.9%` of combined backlog visibility is unsigned awards
- `45.6%` of combined backlog visibility is acquisition-related
- `96.9%` of unsigned awards are tied to acquired businesses
- actual post-reset L/C and surety usage is not disclosed in the extracted evidence
- project margins, owner funding, cancellation terms, cost overruns, and retainage collection are still missing

## What This Upgrades

This bridge upgrades Sterling from:

`large backlog plus credit agreement`

to:

`company-level backlog return-and-risk proxy`

The key reason is that the evidence now spans both sides of the operating conversion question:

- demand visibility: RPO, signed backlog, unsigned awards, future phases
- execution funding: revolver, L/C sublimit, surety mechanics, contract timing, retainage
- cash evidence: operating cash flow less capex and OCF/capex
- risk separation: organic versus acquired backlog, signed versus unsigned awards, project-level unknowns

## Decision

`upgrade-with-boundary - Sterling is now a backlog return-and-risk proxy, not project-return-grade.`

## Safe Claim

`Sterling shows company-level evidence that backlog can convert into operating cash, supported by large RPO, signed backlog, positive cash flow after capex, low observed debt/RPO, and a July 2026 revolver/L/C/surety wrapper suited to project execution. The claim must stay bounded because project-level margin, owner funding, cancellation risk, actual L/C/surety usage, retainage collection, and acquired-backlog conversion remain unproven.`

## Claims Not To Make Yet

Do not say:

- Sterling backlog is fully funded
- unsigned awards are equivalent to signed backlog
- acquired backlog will convert at the same quality as organic backlog
- the new credit agreement proves project-level financing
- the L/C sublimit proves actual L/C usage
- positive company cash flow proves project-level returns
- Sterling is low-risk without margin, cancellation, retainage, L/C, surety, and covenant-cushion evidence

## Next Proof

The next documents that would upgrade the answer are:

1. quarterly backlog burn and revenue conversion by segment
2. project margin or gross-profit evidence by backlog class
3. actual L/C outstanding, surety usage, and bonded backlog
4. retainage aging and collection history
5. organic/acquired backlog roll-forward after CEC and Stone Ridge
6. covenant cushion and revolver availability after the July 2026 reset
7. owner/customer funding evidence for the largest projects

After this bridge, the next operating-return target should be the AEP/Duke approved-recovery bridge, because it tests whether load demand becomes approved, recoverable, in-service utility investment.
