# Capital Flow Sterling Funded Backlog And Covenant Cushion Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass answers the next Sterling question:

`Does the funding wrapper look roomy enough, and do bonding/L/C mechanics explain why Sterling needs this kind of facility?`

The operating table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-sterling-funded-backlog-covenant-cushion-pass-1.csv`

## Simple Answer

Yes, directionally.

Sterling's July `2026` credit wrapper looks built for:

- letters of credit
- short-term working-capital timing
- surety/bonding support
- retainage and contract-capital swings
- acquisitions
- capex
- term-loan refinancing

But we still do **not** have actual L/C usage, formal compliance certificates, bonded backlog, or named project-owner source-of-funds.

So the safe claim is:

`Sterling's funding type is increasingly explainable, but not yet fully proven at project-source-of-funds grade.`

## L/C And Bonding Evidence

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Old L/C sublimit | `75.0M USD` | The pre-reset facility had limited L/C capacity. |
| New L/C sublimit | `600.0M USD` | The new facility materially expands guarantee capacity. |
| L/C sublimit increase | `8.0x` | L/C capacity scaled with the larger backlog/acquisition platform. |
| New revolver increase | `10.0x` | Revolver capacity rose from `150.0M USD` to `1.500B USD`. |
| L/C sublimit / revolver | `40.0%` | L/C capacity is not incidental; it is a major component. |
| Swing-line sublimit | `50.0M USD` | Supports short timing needs. |

The operating filings explain why this matters. Sterling's construction contracts can require bid bonds, performance bonds, payment bonds, or letters of credit.

The FY `2025` 10-K describes a bidder posting a bid bond generally equal to `5%` to `10%` of the bid amount, and after award sometimes posting performance and payment bonds for up to `100%` of the construction cost.

That makes the L/C/bonding subtheme real:

`Backlog does not only need labor and equipment. It also needs guarantees.`

## Missing Usage Boundary

The source files disclose:

- old L/C sublimit
- new L/C sublimit
- L/C fee mechanics
- bonding/surety language
- fees on outstanding instruments

They do not disclose:

- actual post-reset L/C outstanding amount
- actual swing-line usage
- bonded backlog amount
- surety capacity
- L/C beneficiaries
- project-by-project guarantee requirements

That means we can explain the funding container, but not quantify actual guarantee usage yet.

## Covenant Cushion

The covenant evidence is directional, not formal.

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Q2 `2026` total debt | `285.305M USD` | Low visible debt before the July reset. |
| Q2 `2026` cash | `464.451M USD` | Cash exceeded total debt before the reset. |
| Cash less total debt | `179.146M USD` | Net cash position on a simple cash-minus-debt basis. |
| FY `2026` adjusted EBITDA guide midpoint | `903.5M USD` | Useful directional denominator, not covenant EBITDA. |
| Total debt / guide adjusted EBITDA midpoint | `0.32x` | Directionally far below the `3.50x` covenant ceiling. |
| Standard leverage ceiling using guide midpoint | `3.162B USD` | `3.50x` times `903.5M USD`. |
| Acquisition holiday ceiling using guide midpoint | `3.614B USD` | `4.00x` times `903.5M USD`. |
| Cash netting cap using guide midpoint | `451.75M USD` | `50%` of `903.5M USD`. |

The useful interpretation is:

`Sterling does not look covenant-constrained from the available public numbers.`

The important limit:

`This is not the formal Total Net Leverage Ratio.`

The formal calculation needs agreement-defined EBITDA, funded debt, permitted cash netting, post-closing revolver borrowings, and compliance-certificate adjustments.

## Backlog Funding Quality

The backlog funding-quality story is mixed:

| Evidence | Value | What It Means |
|---|---:|---|
| RPOs expected within 12 months | `74.0%` | Most RPOs should convert to revenue within a year. |
| Unsigned awards share of combined backlog | `22.9%` | A meaningful piece of combined backlog is not signed backlog. |
| Combined backlog / new revolver | `3.75x` | The revolver is not funding the whole project value. |
| Contract liabilities / new revolver | `53.5%` | Contract timing is large relative to liquidity capacity. |
| Net contract-liability timing / new revolver | `43.1%` | Billing/revenue timing remains central to the funding model. |

This is the clearest version of the Sterling thesis:

`The upstream capital probably belongs mostly to customers/project owners, but Sterling still needs a large facility because execution timing, guarantees, acquisitions, and working capital are large relative to its old credit wrapper.`

## Why This Funding Type Makes Sense

Sterling does not look like Cheniere:

`No single LNG train, terminal issuer, or SPA waterfall is the main funding story.`

Sterling does not look like United Rentals:

`The key asset is not a reusable equipment fleet with a clean borrowing base.`

Sterling does not look like a regulated utility:

`There is no rate-base recovery mechanism that directly repays company-funded assets.`

Sterling looks like a backlog/acquisition execution platform:

`Its balance sheet has to bridge the messy middle between customer project demand and delivered work.`

That messy middle includes:

- contract liabilities and assets
- retainage
- bid/performance/payment bonds
- L/C support
- project working capital
- equipment and prefabrication capex
- acquisitions
- covenant-gated expansion capacity

## What We Can Say Now

Promote:

`Sterling's July 2026 funding wrapper fits a backlog/acquisition execution platform: L/C capacity rose 8.0x, the revolver rose 10.0x, L/C capacity equals 40.0% of the revolver, bonding requirements can reach up to 100% of construction cost on some contracts, and visible Q2 debt was directionally low versus 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance.`

Do not promote:

`Sterling has proven funded backlog with no cancellation, margin, working-capital, L/C, surety, or covenant risk.`
