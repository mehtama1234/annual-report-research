## Purpose

## Simple Answer

The L/C and surety mechanics strongly explain the funding wrapper.

Actual usage is still not disclosed.

So the safe claim is:

`Sterling's expanded revolver is built to support guarantees, letters of credit, bonding, and execution timing, but public sources do not yet show actual post-reset L/C outstanding or bonded backlog.`

That is the key boundary.

## Surety And Bonding Mechanics

Sterling's FY `2025` 10-K gives the operating reason:

- bid bonds are generally `5%` to `10%` of bid amount
- performance and payment bonds can be up to `100%` of construction cost
- maintenance bonds are generally `1%` of contract amount for one to two years
- bonding capacity depends on capitalization, working capital, contract size, past performance, management expertise, and surety-market capacity

The Q2 `2026` 10-Q adds the counterparty/collateral evidence:

- Sterling obtains bonding primarily through Travelers Casualty and Surety Company of America
- Sterling indemnifies Travelers for losses on issued bonds
- Travelers has a security interest in accounts receivable and contract rights
- some projects include performance guarantees tied to cost to complete and remaining billings

## Credit Agreement Mechanics

The July `2026` agreement makes bonding and L/C support part of the formal credit wrapper:

- `L/C Obligations` include undrawn face amounts of outstanding letters of credit plus unpaid reimbursement obligations.
- `Bonding Agreement` covers surety, bid, appeal, indemnity, performance, payment, and similar obligations.
- The definition of indebtedness includes letters of credit, demand guarantees, surety bonds, performance bonds, and similar instruments.

This matters because the facility is not just a borrowing line. It is also a contingent-obligation and guarantee-support framework.

## What We Still Do Not Know

Public sources reviewed so far do not disclose:

- actual post-reset L/C outstanding
- actual L/C beneficiaries
- actual swing-line usage
- bonded backlog
- surety capacity
- bond face amount by project
- performance-guarantee exposure by contract
- claims history or reserves tied to bonded jobs
