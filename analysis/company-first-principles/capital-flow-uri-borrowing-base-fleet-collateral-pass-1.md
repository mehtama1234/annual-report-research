## Simple Read

URI is not only spending money on equipment.

It is operating a repeatable fleet-capital machine:

- it owns or controls a large rental fleet measured at `23.8B USD` OEC
- it keeps adding fleet capital through gross rental capex
- it recycles cash through used-equipment sales
- it generates rental revenue and fleet productivity from that asset base
- it produced operating cash flow above gross rental capex in H1 `2026`
- it funds the model with a stack that includes AR securitization, ABL, term debt, notes, and leases
- the AR securitization already has a visible collateral-cushion ratio, while the ABL has a first draw-intensity ratio

That is the economic difference from Sterling.

Sterling's wrapper is about project execution, backlog timing, surety, letters of credit, and working-capital conversion.

URI's wrapper is about reusable fleet assets, receivables, secured facilities, equipment recycling, liquidity, and debt maturities.

## What The Borrowing-Base Clue Really Says

The Q2 `2026` 10-Q gives a useful but limited AR securitization clue:

- AR securitization borrowings were `1.414B USD`
- receivables in the collateral pool, net of reserves and deductions, were `1.779B USD`
- the implied collateral-pool coverage was `125.8%`
- the implied excess collateral pool was `365M USD`

That is real collateral evidence.

But it is not a full borrowing-base proof.

The missing items are:

- advance rates
- eligible receivable definitions
- reserve formulas
- concentration limits
- dilution/loss data
- purchaser limits
- legal availability
- whether availability changed after letters of credit or other constraints

## What The ABL Clue Really Says

The Q2 `2026` debt table gives a first ABL draw-intensity check:

- ABL balance: `1.666B USD`
- stated ABL facility size: `4.5B USD`
- simple draw intensity: `37.0%`

That tells us the facility was materially used but not close to the stated size on a simple ratio.

It does not prove legal headroom.

The next proof requires:

- ABL agreement terms
- borrowing-base certificates
- eligible equipment and receivable rules
- letters of credit
- reserves
- covenant calculations
- collateral schedules

## Safe Claim

`United Rentals is a fleet-access capital absorber with source-backed evidence for fleet OEC, rental capex, operating cash flow, used-equipment recycling, liquidity, AR securitization collateral cushion, and ABL draw intensity.`

## Claims Not To Make Yet

Do not say:

- all fleet capex is high-return growth capex
- all OEC growth is organic physical capacity growth
- the borrowing base is fully proven
- the ABL has `2.834B USD` of legal availability just because `4.5B - 1.666B = 2.834B`
- specific fleet purchases were funded by specific debt instruments
- fleet returns, ROIC, utilization, or rate/mix economics are proven at thesis grade
