# Capital Flow URI Fleet Return Bridge Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass executes the top-ranked operating-return scorecard item:

`Is URI's fleet investment earning durable returns, or just increasing revenue through more assets?`

The structured companion table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-uri-fleet-return-bridge-pass-1.csv`

## Short Answer

URI can be upgraded from:

`fleet operating-conversion proxy`

to:

`fleet return-quality proxy`

The evidence is still not full fleet ROIC, but it is stronger than simple revenue growth. URI has a source-backed chain from fleet capital to revenue, gross profit, cash conversion, segment asset productivity, used-equipment recovery, liquidity, and collateral support.

The current safe answer is:

`URI shows strong return-quality proxy evidence: Q2 2026 rental revenue equaled 16.2% of OEC, annualized Q2 rental revenue equaled 64.7% of OEC, Q2 adjusted EBITDA equaled 8.6% of OEC, H1 2026 operating cash flow covered 112.8% of gross rental capex and 162.0% of net fleet cash investment, Specialty produced higher H1 equipment-rentals gross margin than General Rentals, and Specialty annualized equipment-rentals gross profit / average segment assets was 26.4% versus 14.7% for General Rentals. This is still proxy-grade, not asset-class ROIC.`

## Bridge Logic

The return bridge is:

`fleet capital base -> rental revenue -> owned-rental mix -> segment gross profit -> cash conversion -> used-equipment recovery -> collateral/liquidity support -> missing ROIC gates`

This matters because a fleet company can grow revenue simply by adding more assets. The return-quality question asks whether the asset base appears to produce revenue, gross profit, cash, recycling value, and funding support without relying only on increasing leverage.

## Evidence Bridge

| Layer | Evidence | Read | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fleet denominator | Q2 2026 OEC was `23.8B USD`. | URI has a public fleet-cost denominator for output proxies. | OEC is not net invested capital, eligible collateral, or asset-class capital. |
| Revenue productivity | Q2 2026 rental revenue / OEC was `16.2%`; annualized Q2 rental revenue / OEC was `64.7%`. | The fleet base is producing large rental revenue relative to OEC. | Annualization ignores seasonality, acquisitions, mix, and cycle effects. |
| EBITDA productivity | Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA / OEC was `8.6%`; annualized Q2 adjusted EBITDA / OEC was `34.6%`. | Company-level EBITDA is high relative to the OEC denominator. | Adjusted EBITDA is company-level, not fleet operating profit or ROIC. |
| Owned-rental purity | Owned equipment rentals were `2.991B USD` in Q2 2026 and `5.676B USD` in H1 2026, or `77.7%` and `78.1%` of equipment-rentals revenue. | Most equipment-rental revenue still comes from owned assets rather than re-rent or ancillary items. | Owned-rental revenue still does not split rate, utilization, and mix. |
| Segment gross margin | H1 2026 Specialty equipment-rentals gross margin was `43.1%` versus `34.8%` for General Rentals. | Specialty has a stronger filed gross-margin profile. | Segment gross margin is not operating margin, EBITDA, ROIC, or category margin. |
| Segment capex intensity | H1 2026 Specialty capex / H1 equipment-rentals revenue was `31.2%` versus `49.0%` for General Rentals. | Specialty absorbed less H1 capex per dollar of segment equipment-rentals revenue in the extracted period. | This is a period ratio, not life-cycle maintenance/growth capex. |
| Segment asset proxy | H1 2026 Specialty annualized equipment-rentals gross profit / average segment assets was `26.4%` versus `14.7%` for General Rentals. | Specialty gross profit productivity remains materially higher on the filed segment-asset denominator. | Segment assets are not rental fleet OEC, and gross profit is not operating income. |
| Trend check | Specialty annual gross profit / segment assets declined from `30.8%` in FY 2023 to `25.0%` in FY 2025. | The higher-return proxy is not one-way; it needs monitoring after acquisitions and mix shifts. | Decline may reflect acquisitions, mix, asset growth, goodwill, inflation, or timing. |
| Cash coverage | H1 2026 OCF covered `112.8%` of gross rental capex and `162.0%` of net fleet cash investment. | Fleet investment is paired with operating cash generation, not only external funding. | OCF is company-level and does not prove each fleet purchase was self-funded. |
| Recycling value | Q2 2026 OEC recovery rate on fleet sold was `52.9%`, and implied used-equipment GAAP gross profit was about `154.1M USD`. | Used-equipment sales provide a visible asset-recycling support leg. | Disposal recovery is not full life-cycle return or asset-class residual value. |
| Collateral support | AR collateral-pool coverage stayed above `100%` in all `10` extracted periods; Q2 2026 was `125.8%`. | Funding support appears durable at proxy level. | Collateral coverage is not legal borrowing-base availability. |
| Missing gates | Growth/replacement capex, utilization, rate/time/mix split, segment OEC, fleet margin, legal borrowing-base availability, and source-to-purchase allocation remain missing. | These are the items needed for true return-grade proof. | Without them, the claim must stay proxy-grade. |

## What This Upgrades

Before this bridge, the URI answer was:

`URI has repeated-period operating-conversion evidence.`

After this bridge, the URI answer is:

`URI has return-quality proxy evidence because fleet capital can be tied to revenue productivity, EBITDA productivity, owned-rental mix, segment gross margin, capex intensity, segment asset productivity, cash coverage, used-equipment recovery, and collateral support.`

That is an upgrade, but not a final return conclusion.

## What It Says About Specialty

Specialty is the most interesting part of the bridge.

The evidence points in two directions at once:

- Specialty has better current gross-margin and segment-asset productivity than General Rentals.
- Specialty's annual gross-profit/assets proxy declined from FY 2023 to FY 2025, and Yak/Matting introduced a goodwill-heavy acquisition layer.

That means the right hypothesis is not simply:

`Specialty is better.`

The better hypothesis is:

`Specialty may be structurally higher gross-margin and more asset-productive, but acquisition mix, Matting timing, goodwill, ancillary revenue, and project timing can pressure the return profile.`

## What Would Make This Return-Grade

The evidence would move from proxy-grade to return-grade if the next source pass finds:

| Missing Evidence | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Growth versus replacement rental capex | Separates expansion from maintenance spend. |
| Segment or fleet-class OEC | Creates the right denominator for General Rentals, Specialty, and product categories. |
| Time utilization and rental rate split | Shows whether growth comes from price, utilization, or mix. |
| Segment operating profit or EBITDA | Upgrades gross-profit/assets into operating-return evidence. |
| Used-equipment recovery by asset class | Tests residual-value support and life-cycle economics. |
| Legal borrowing-base certificate or availability table | Converts collateral proxy into actual funding availability. |
| Acquisition-adjusted Specialty bridge | Separates organic Specialty economics from Yak/Matting and other acquired assets. |

## Disproof Tests

The URI return thesis weakens if:

- fleet capex is mostly replacement rather than growth
- utilization falls while OEC rises
- fleet productivity depends mostly on price or mix rather than better asset use
- Specialty gross-profit/assets keeps declining after acquisition normalization
- used-equipment recovery weakens materially
- legal borrowing-base availability is tighter than public collateral proxies imply
- cash coverage deteriorates when capex remains high
