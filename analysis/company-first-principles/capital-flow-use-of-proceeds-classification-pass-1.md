# Capital Flow Use-Of-Proceeds Classification Pass 1

## Purpose

This pass answers the next operating question:

`When capital reaches a named borrower, what does it actually fund?`

The operating table is:

`analysis/company-first-principles/data/capital-flow-use-of-proceeds-classification-pass-1.csv`

## Current Answer

The borrower evidence does not support one generic answer.

The current evidence separates into:

- acquisition finance and control transfer
- acquisition finance plus bank-credit repayment or termination
- sponsor buyout bank-competition leads
- continued growth plan financing
- continued M&A strategy or consolidation finance
- incremental commitment for continued growth
- transaction context where debt size and holder rows are still missing

The safe current answer is:

`Private credit is visibly funding sponsor-backed borrowers through acquisition finance, M&A strategy support, incremental commitments, continued-growth plans, and in selected cases repayment or termination of existing bank credit. The evidence does not yet prove that every borrower loan funded new productive capacity, organic capex, or bank displacement.`

## What This Says

The research can now say:

- private credit is funding specific sponsor-backed borrowers
- several visible financings are acquisition finance or control-transfer related
- some cases include bank-credit repayment or termination evidence
- some cases use continued-growth or M&A-strategy language but still lack precise use-of-proceeds detail
- holder rows prove exposure, not purpose

## What This Does Not Say

Do not say:

- every private-credit dollar funds organic growth
- every continued-growth facility funds productive capex
- every acquisition loan creates new capacity
- transaction value equals debt size
- holder fair value equals facility size
- unfunded commitments are drawn proceeds
- bank repayment happened unless a document says so
