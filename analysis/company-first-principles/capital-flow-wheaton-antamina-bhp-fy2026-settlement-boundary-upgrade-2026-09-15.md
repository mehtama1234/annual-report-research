# Wheaton–Antamina BHP FY2026 settlement-boundary upgrade

Research date: `2026-09-17`

The BHP FY2026 annual report materially sharpens the Antamina evidence, but it
does not close the delivery-to-owner-cash loop.

## Newly explicit primary evidence

- BHP states that the stream settles through purchase and delivery of metal
  credits to Wheaton; there is no physical delivery of silver.
- BHP states that there are no minimum or fixed delivery requirements under the
  agreement.
- BHP records the streaming arrangement as an IFRS 9 financial liability and
  reports a `$4.273B` liability at June 30, 2026, against the `$4.300B` upfront
  payment received on April 2, 2026.
- BHP’s consolidated cash-flow statement records `$4.300B` of proceeds from
  the streaming arrangement liability and `$41M` of settlements of that
  liability. The filing does not identify the `$41M` by ounce, invoice,
  settlement date, or BHP-only delivery schedule.
- BHP says the implicit-rate estimate uses Antamina production volumes and
  silver prices, including risked reserves and resources that do not currently
  meet the criteria to be treated as proved.

## Consequence for the proof grade

This upgrades the contract and accounting boundary from “delivery records not
located” to a more precise result: the public filing establishes a metal-credit
settlement mechanism without physical shipment, an absence of minimum delivery
guarantees, and a small reported liability settlement. It still does not prove
the quantity of metal credits transferred, the invoice/settlement ledger, the
allocation of the `$41M`, or a source-backed return.

The appropriate grade remains:

`upfront cash loop confirmed; settlement mechanism confirmed; quantity-level and financed-return loop incomplete`

## Source-precision control — 2026-09-17

The FY2026 cash-flow statement provides a reproducible financing bridge:
reported net financing cash outflow of `$3.280B` equals the non-stream
financing lines of approximately `$(7.539B)` plus `$4.300B` of streaming
proceeds less `$41M` of streaming-liability settlements. This is a
classification and arithmetic control, not a funds-flow allocation. The note
also states that BHP received the `$4.300B` upfront payment on April 2, 2026,
will deliver the equivalent of `33.75%` of Antamina silver subject to a fixed
`90%` payable rate until `100M` ounces are delivered, and then `22.5%` over the
remaining mine life. No minimum or fixed delivery requirement exists, and the
settlement object is metal credits rather than physical silver.

These details improve the legal-counterparty, date, tranche, and arithmetic
controls. They still do not identify the BHP-only metal-credit quantity,
invoice, quotation-period price, settlement date for the `$41M`, or Wheaton
receipt account.
