# Wheaton–Antamina BHP legal-counterparty boundary upgrade

Research date: `2026-09-15`

The BHP FY2026 annual report adds a legal-entity boundary to the Antamina
streaming analysis. BHP states that the silver streaming agreement was
completed through a wholly owned BHP subsidiary, while Antamina is not a party
to the agreement. BHP also states that the agreement does not change BHP's
shareholder rights, obligations under the Antamina joint-venture arrangements,
existing customer agreements, or BHP's exposure to copper, zinc, and lead from
its Antamina share.

## What this proves

- The `$4.3B` upfront consideration and the metal-credit obligation belong to
  the BHP-side legal counterparty, not to Antamina as the operating joint
  venture.
- The stream is a monetization of BHP's share of silver output, not a transfer
  of Antamina's copper, zinc, lead, customer contracts, or JV governance.
- The operating production denominator and the stream settlement obligation
  must therefore be kept as separate ledgers: BHP-share payable silver is an
  input to the stream, while Antamina-level production and commercial rights
  remain broader than the streamed metal.

## What this does not prove

This boundary does not identify the BHP-only metal-credit quantity, invoice,
settlement date, realized price, receipt account, or the portion of BHP cash
that can be assigned to Wheaton's first post-close delivery. It also does not
allocate Wheaton's corporate debt, interest, tax, or common-owner cash to the
PMPA.

## Proof-grade result

`legal-counterparty-and-asset-boundary-confirmed`: the BHP annual report
separates the BHP subsidiary's streaming obligation from Antamina's operating
joint-venture perimeter. Quantity-level settlement and after-tax financed
return remain open.
