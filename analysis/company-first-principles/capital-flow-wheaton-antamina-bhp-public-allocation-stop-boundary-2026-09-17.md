# Wheaton–Antamina BHP public allocation stop boundary

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Finding

BHP's public FY2026 materials confirm the `$4.3B` Antamina streaming receipt,
but do not provide a transaction-level cash-use ledger that assigns the
proceeds to debt repayment, growth capital, dividends, taxes, or a particular
legal entity's residual cash.

The April operational review reports approximately `$4.8B` realized from a
combination of the Antamina stream, the Carajás divestment, and cash from prior
Blackwater and Daunia divestments. That combined figure cannot be used as an
Antamina-only source/use bridge. BHP's capital-allocation language says the
proceeds may be reallocated to growth and shareholder returns, but a stated
capital-allocation intention is not evidence of a completed payment or use.

## Evidence and boundary

| Public evidence | Safe conclusion | Unsafe conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| FY2026 20-F: `$4.3B` received for the Wheaton stream | BHP-side upfront receipt is proven | The receipt is recurring Antamina operating cash |
| April operational review: approximately `$4.8B` combined realizations | Several asset-realization inflows improved balance-sheet flexibility | The full `$4.8B` is Antamina proceeds |
| FY2026 results: net debt below `$9B` after capital allocation and returns | Period-end group balance-sheet outcome is visible | Antamina proceeds caused a quantified portion of the net-debt movement |
| February transaction announcement: proceeds may support growth and shareholder returns | Management's intended allocation framework is visible | Debt, capex, tax, or dividends can be assigned to the stream without a ledger |

## QoE controls

The combined-realization and net-debt figures are group-level outcomes. They
must remain separate from:

- the BHP legal seller's `$4.3B` receipt;
- the Wheaton buyer's `$4.3B` payment and financing mix;
- recurring BHP metal-credit settlements;
- BHP taxes and transaction costs;
- debt repayment or growth-capital use; and
- common-owner residual cash.

This is a perimeter and attribution control, not a finding of accounting
impropriety. The financial-shenanigans question is whether a broad group cash
movement is being incorrectly presented as an asset-level return.

## Decision

`public-upfront-receipt-proven; public-use-allocation-searched-negative`

The public route is now bounded. The next useful source is a legal-entity cash
ledger, debt-paydown notice, tax/payment schedule, or other named allocation
record—not another aggregate balance-sheet or capital-allocation statement.
