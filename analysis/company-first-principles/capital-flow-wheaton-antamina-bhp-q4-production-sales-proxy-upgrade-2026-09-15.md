# Wheaton–Antamina BHP Q4 production-and-sales proxy upgrade

Research date: `2026-09-15`

## Denominator correction — 2026-09-16

An earlier draft multiplied the BHP-interest production and sales rows by an
additional `33.75%`, producing conditional figures of `0.32076M` and
`0.28765M` ounces. Those figures are withdrawn. BHP's table already reports
the Antamina payable-metal rows at BHP's `33.75%` interest, so applying that
factor again would double-count ownership. The corrected analysis keeps the
reported BHP-interest rows and applies only the separate mechanical `90.0%`
screen, while continuing to withhold the actual BHP-PMPA metal-credit quantity.

BHP's operational review for the year ended June 30, 2026 provides a useful
counterparty-side denominator for the first quarter after the BHP Antamina
PMPA became effective. In the Antamina table, BHP reports its `33.75%`
interest and Q4 FY2026 (April–June 2026) payable silver of `1,056` thousand
troy ounces produced and `947` thousand troy ounces sold. The same table
reports FY2026 totals of `5,588` thousand produced and `5,406` thousand sold.

The PMPA became effective on April 1, 2026. Applying the contract's fixed
`90.0%` payable factor mechanically to the Q4 BHP-interest rows gives:

| Q4 FY2026 BHP-interest row | Reported | Mechanical 90% factor | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Payable silver produced | `1.056M oz` | `0.9504M oz` | Counterparty-side production proxy for the stream denominator |
| Payable silver sold | `0.947M oz` | `0.8523M oz` | Counterparty-side sales proxy for timing/settlement analysis |

The BHP table is explicitly presented at BHP's `33.75%` interest. Therefore no
additional `33.75%` multiplication is applied to these BHP-interest rows:
doing so would double-count the ownership share. The `90.0%` figures above
remain a mechanical screen only, because the public filings do not establish
whether BHP's operating "payable silver" presentation exactly matches every
contract-level payability convention used in Wheaton's metal-credit settlement.

These are not asserted to be the BHP-PMPA metal-credit ledger. The BHP table
is an operating production-and-sales disclosure, while the PMPA is settled by
metal credits and the Wheaton filing reports only combined Antamina production
and first BHP-PMPA deliveries. The mechanical figures therefore improve the
volume boundary without proving the exact metal-credit quantity, invoice,
realized price, or cash receipt attributable to Wheaton.

## Cross-source Q2 perimeter screen

The same-period figures can be placed beside one another as a bounded
perimeter screen:

| Q2 2026 measure | Wheaton combined Antamina row | BHP-interest row after mechanical 90% factor | Arithmetic difference | Safe reading |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Silver ounces produced | `2.319M` | `0.9504M` | `1.3686M` | Difference between two reported perimeters; not an allocated Glencore balance |
| Silver ounces sold | `2.063M` | `0.8523M` | `1.2107M` | Difference between two reported perimeters; not a delivery, invoice, or cash balance |

This screen makes the remaining allocation problem arithmetic rather than
verbal. It does not prove that the difference belongs to the legacy Glencore
stream, does not reconcile the BHP payable convention to metal credits, and
does not convert production or sales into a settlement receipt. The two rows
must not be added to the BHP-only cash case.

## What this changes

- It ties the post-effective-date quarter to a BHP-side source rather than
  relying only on Wheaton's combined Antamina row.
- It provides a bounded production-versus-sales timing screen for the first
  BHP-PMPA quarter.
- It preserves the BHP-interest denominator without double-counting the
  ownership share and exposes the remaining payability-convention ambiguity.
- It does not allocate taxes, interest, debt service, or the Q2 Wheaton cash
  collection to the BHP stream.
