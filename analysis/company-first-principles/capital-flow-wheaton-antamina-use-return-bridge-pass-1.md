## Current Answer

`Partly. Wheaton now has a source-use-output-cash bridge for Antamina at contract-term and company-cash level. The evidence names the use of cash, the financing context, the delivery payment formula, the production entitlement, the life-of-mine term, and same-quarter company output/cash metrics. It still does not prove Antamina stream-level return because delivered ounces, stream-level revenue, depletion, tax allocation, interest allocation, debt-service waterfall, and lender allocation are not joined.`

## Decision

`source-use-output-cash-bridge-visible-company-level`

This is an upgrade from the prior Wheaton state:

`denominator-visible-stream-return-open`

The upgrade is narrow. It shows that the Antamina funding event has named cash use, visible financing context, visible contract-entitlement terms, and same-quarter company output/cash support.

It does not reach:

`stream-level-return-proven`

## Why This Matters

Wheaton is now the strongest debt/refinancing pilot row for testing whether a financing event becomes an operating-return bridge. PBF, Devon, and Liberty Broadband still need instrument-use allocation before the operating-return question can even be asked cleanly. Matador has a plausible production/acquisition route, but Antamina now has the clearest named use and contract terms.

## Hypothesis

`Wheaton's Antamina PMPA is likely a source-use-output-cash bridge, but not yet a full return bridge. The financing and use are visible, the contract economics are visible, and same-period company cash/output are visible. The decisive test is whether future or supplemental sources disclose Antamina-specific deliveries and cash receipts sufficient to cover the upfront payment and financing cost.`
