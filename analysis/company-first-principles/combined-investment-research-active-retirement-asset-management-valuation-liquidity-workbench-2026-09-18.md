# Active and retirement asset-management valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves T. Rowe Price from annual-report evidence into a company-specific fee, flow, product-transition, talent, and common-owner residual test. It keeps active public-market and retirement-plan relationships separate from Invesco's ETF/QQQ concentration, Franklin's product-migration/alternatives case, and Prudential's liability-backed PGIM model.

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 ending AUM was `$1.776T`, average AUM `$1.677T`, net client outflows `$56.9B`, net revenue `$7.315B`, adjusted net income `$2.195B`, and adjusted EPS `$9.72`.
- Market appreciation of `$216.7B` more than offset the `$56.9B` of net outflows in FY2025. Ending AUM therefore cannot stand in for client-health or owner cash.
- The effective fee rate excluding performance fees fell from `41.0` basis points to `39.4` in FY2025 and `38.1` in Q2 FY2026; fee compression across approximately `$1.7T` of average AUM is economically material.
- Q2 FY2026 ending AUM reached `$1.893T`, but net client outflows remained `$6.5B`. ETFs, SMAs, fixed income, retirement, and alternatives may improve mix while carrying lower fees or different delivery costs.
- T. Rowe returned `$1.768B` to stockholders in 2025, including `$624.6M` of repurchases. Capital returns cannot repair persistent fee-paying relationship loss.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile AUM to market appreciation, gross/net flows, fee-paying AUM, fee rate, product mix, performance revenue, and expenses.
2. Separate active equity, retirement, ETF, SMA, fixed-income, alternatives, and partnerships by flow, fee, performance, retention, compensation, and contribution margin.
3. Charge investment talent, distribution, technology, client service, compliance, and product-development spending as recurring operating capacity.
4. Reconcile adjusted earnings to GAAP, impairment, amortization, carried-interest effects, acquisitions, SBC, cash flow, share count, and capital returns.
5. Test buybacks and dividends after the cost of preserving investment performance and client distribution through a market decline.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style test asks what fee-paying AUM, net flows, fee rates, product mix, investment performance, compensation, distribution cost, margin, and cost of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test focuses on market levels, retirement savings, rates, liquidity, passive substitution, and whether the firm can retain talent while revenue falls.

## Promotion boundary

`active-retirement-asset-management-qualified; fee-flow-talent-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period joins from product-level flows and average fee-paying AUM to fee rates, revenue, recurring talent/distribution/technology cost, investment performance, client retention, capital returns, and diluted common residual. AUM, adjusted EPS, market appreciation, dividends, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
