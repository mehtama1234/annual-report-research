# Adtalem/Covista healthcare education valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Adtalem/Covista from current-period evidence into a
company-specific valuation, enrollment, program-outcome, regulatory, tuition,
campus, and common-owner cash test. It does not pool Walden, Chamberlain, Medical
and Veterinary, and other professional-education economics into a generic education
multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Adtalem/Covista | Collected career-education cash after enrollment, tuition, aid, retention, clinical sites, faculty, student support, regulatory compliance, campus/digital delivery, debt, and dilution | Program and campus expansion, clinical/lab sites, faculty, technology, student services, marketing, employer partnerships, and accreditation | Enrollment decline, contract or regulatory change, outcomes scrutiny, aid policy, site utilization, wage inflation, debt, or dilution | Enrollment and adjusted EBITDA rise while revenue per student, retention, outcomes, compliance, collections, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Q4 FY2026 revenue was about `$501.4M`, GAAP net income `$71.7M`, adjusted EBITDA `$126.9M`, and adjusted EPS `$2.09`.
- Full-year FY2026 revenue was about `$1.95B`, GAAP net income `$251.6M`, adjusted EBITDA `$521.7M`, and adjusted EPS `$8.25`.
- Q4 total enrollment was `99,472`, up `8.4%`; Walden enrollment rose `14.0%`; Medical and Veterinary enrollment rose `7.3%`; Chamberlain grew `1.6%`.
- Q3 FY2026 enrollment exceeded `100,000`; Walden had its eleventh consecutive quarter of growth; the company raised guidance and refinanced debt to extend maturities to `2033`.
- The business is moving from Adtalem to Covista and frames itself as a healthcare-education and labor-shortage platform, making regulatory and outcome credibility central to the cash model.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile enrollment to starts, retention, completions, tuition, aid, refunds,
   revenue per student, collections, and program mix.
2. Separate Walden, Chamberlain, Medical and Veterinary, and other programs by
   regulatory exposure, outcomes, delivery format, faculty, and margin.
3. Keep campuses, clinical and laboratory sites, faculty, technology, student
   support, accreditation, marketing, and employer partnerships in the reinvestment
   denominator.
4. Test whether enrollment growth reflects durable labor-market demand or discounts,
   contract changes, timing, and mix that reduce revenue per student or outcomes.
5. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA, litigation, regulatory or aid claims, debt,
   refinancing, buybacks, SBC, and diluted shares before treating capital returns
   as common-owner surplus.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what enrollment, retention, tuition,
completion, outcomes, site utilization, margin, reinvestment rate, leverage, and
cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether
healthcare labor shortages, student financing, regulation, accreditation, faculty,
and household affordability can preserve liquidity through an education cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`adtalem-covista-healthcare-education-qualified; enrollment-outcomes-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from program enrollment and
completion to tuition collections, aid/refunds, outcomes, required site/faculty
spend, regulatory claims, debt, and diluted common residual. Enrollment, adjusted
EBITDA, guidance, program growth, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
