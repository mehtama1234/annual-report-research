# Large-load regulated construction and affordability valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves American Electric Power from annual-report evidence into a
company-specific regulated transmission/distribution, generation, interconnection,
large-load, rate-recovery, financing, and common-owner residual test. It keeps
AEP separate from merchant generators, other regulated utilities, and signed-load
narratives that have not yet become energized and collected customer demand.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AEP | Regulated common cash after energized large-load investment, approved rate recovery, customer contributions/offsets, construction cost, operating reliability, debt, equity, and dilution | Transmission/distribution, substations, generation, interconnection, gas turbines, storm/reliability work, construction WIP, and environmental obligations | Load cancellation or delay, regulatory disallowance, construction inflation, financing cost, customer affordability, storm costs, or debt/equity availability | Signed load and rate-base growth rise while energized/collected load, recovery, project return, financing burden, affordability, or diluted per-share value deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- AEP serves approximately `5.6M` customers across `11` states. Signed incremental load was approximately `69 GW` by 2030, while its five-year capital plan reached about `$78B`.
- Operating cash flow was `$5.012B` in 2023, `$6.804B` in 2024, and `$6.944B` in 2025, versus construction expenditures of `$7.378B`, `$7.631B`, and `$8.453B`; mechanical cash after construction remained negative in each year.
- AEP reported potential customer cost offsets of up to `$16B` and approximately `$1.4B` of projected customer savings from federal loans and grants. These are funding/recovery claims, not unrestricted owner cash.
- AEP secured approximately `13 GW` of gas-turbine capacity and evaluated another `10 GW`. Capacity availability, construction, fuel, emissions, and customer-contract economics require separate proof.
- The economic chain is `load agreement -> interconnection/construction -> energized asset -> approved recovery -> customer collection -> common cash`; rate base and signed load alone do not close it.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile signed load to executed contracts, credit support, interconnection,
   energized service, customer payment, and regulatory recovery.
2. Separate construction WIP, placed-in-service rate base, customer contributions,
   federal grants/loans, securitization, debt, equity, and actual common cash.
3. Test large-load projects through cost-to-complete, schedule, cancellation,
   minimum-payment, tariff, affordability, and stranded-asset exposure.
4. Track transmission/distribution, generation, gas turbines, storm, reliability,
   environmental, and maintenance capital separately from growth capital.
5. Treat rate base, planned capital, signed load, customer offsets, dividends,
   and buybacks as diagnostic inputs until recovery and diluted residual close.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what energized load, allowed return,
rate recovery, customer contribution, construction margin, capital plan, debt
cost, and cost of equity the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test
asks whether rates, inflation, power affordability, customer credit, financing
markets, fuel, weather, and policy preserve utility liquidity through a buildout.

## Promotion boundary

`aep-large-load-regulated-construction-qualified; energized-recovery-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from load contracts to
interconnection, energized assets, cost-to-complete, approved recovery,
customer collection, grants/offsets, debt/equity funding, taxes, and diluted
common residual. Signed GW, rate base, planned capex, OCF, dividends, and
buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
