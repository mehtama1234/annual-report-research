# AEP Q2 2026 large-load-to-owner-cash refresh

## Purpose

This refresh advances the power-grid customer-cash lane from a public-route
admission to a current filing-backed AEP test. It separates load agreements,
tariff approval, planned capital, construction cash, regulatory recovery,
customer billing, collections, debt, and equity funding. It does not treat
69 GW of contracted load or a `$78B` capital plan as present owner cash.

## The current-period cash surface

AEP generated `$3.421B` of H1 operating cash and spent `$5.606B` on
construction expenditures, `$1.315B` on generation-facility acquisitions, and
`$51M` on nuclear fuel. The mechanical bridges are:

`$3.421B OCF - $5.606B construction = $(2.185B)`

and, before construction advances and other investing items:

`$3.421B - $5.606B - $1.315B - $0.051B = $(3.551B)`

These are not normalized utility owner-cash conclusions. They show the funding
surface underneath a regulated growth thesis. AEP reported `$425M` of
construction advances and uses debt, securitizations, DOE financing, common
equity, and operating cash to fund the system. The common-owner question is
whether approved investments enter rate base or contracted recovery on time and
earn an adequate return after financing, taxes, regulatory lag, customer
affordability, and dilution.

## Load growth is a control point, not a receipt

AEP added approximately 6 GW of signed load agreements in Q2, bringing total
contracted load growth through 2030 to approximately 69 GW. The counterparties
include hyperscalers, data centers, and industrial customers. AEP also reported
up to `$16B` of expected residential cost offsets supported by fully executed
take-or-pay electric-service agreements.

The chain still has open proof steps:

`signed load -> tariff / contract terms -> interconnection and generation spend -> in-service date -> billed determinant -> customer payment -> regulated recovery -> common-owner cash`

Signed load is not equal to realized consumption. A take-or-pay agreement can
improve funding visibility, but the model must identify the customer entity,
security or collateral, minimum payment, termination rights, construction
allocation, tariff treatment, and whether cash is collected before or after
the utility funds the asset.

## Capital plan and funding burden

AEP forecasts approximately `$12.8B` of 2026 capital expenditures and
`$77.937B` for 2026–2030. At June 30, total debt was `$52.836B`, representing
61.4% of total capitalization. Net available liquidity was approximately
`$7.25B`.

The company also has a `$3.5B` ATM capacity and forward equity agreements. The
filing describes common-stock issuance as part of the financing cash-flow
increase. Equity can be accretive if load-backed projects earn above the cost
of capital, but the valuation must test incremental rate-base earnings against
the shares and financing used to build them.

DOE loans and grants were described as creating approximately `$1.4B` of
estimated customer benefits. That is a customer-affordability and funding
benefit, not automatically AEP common-owner cash. The workbench must keep the
benefit recipient, interest savings, grant proceeds, project cost, and utility
recovery path separate.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans screen

1. **Load-contract test:** obtain a named executed large-load agreement or
   permitted redacted contract and map minimum payments, collateral, milestones,
   exit rights, and customer-specific capital allocation.
2. **Billing-determinant test:** connect contracted MW and expected usage to a
   tariff, meter, bill, collection, and regulatory recovery.
3. **Capital-to-rate-base test:** reconcile construction expenditures, CWIP,
   AFUDC, regulatory assets/liabilities, in-service dates, and allowed return.
4. **Affordability-offset test:** separate residential cost offsets, DOE loans,
   grants, securitizations, and customer contributions from AEP cash generation.
5. **Adjusted-earnings test:** reconcile operating EPS to GAAP earnings,
   excluding only clearly nonrecurring items; track partial disallowances,
   tax timing, commodity hedges, and regulatory orders.
6. **Financing-and-dilution test:** track debt, short-term borrowings, ATM and
   forward equity, securitization, interest cost, and common shares.
7. **Customer-credit test:** test the credit quality and concentration of large
   loads, deposits, guarantees, and any take-or-pay counterparties.
8. **Stress test:** combine higher rates, construction inflation, delayed load,
   regulatory lag, customer affordability pressure, and reduced capital-market
   access.

These are forensic controls, not accusations of misconduct. The central risk
is presentation drift: forecast load or non-GAAP operating earnings are treated
as collected utility cash before the asset, bill, recovery, and financing chain
is complete.

## Promotion gate

AEP remains `qualified; current-period owner-cash updated; no-ranking`. The
next joinable evidence object is one named large-load obligation or project
that provides customer identity, billing determinant, construction allocation,
in-service date, billed/collected cash, regulatory recovery, and common-owner
return. Until that object exists, the 69 GW and `$78B` figures remain powerful
expectation inputs, not an owner-cash denominator.
