# Agricultural inputs, retail, and potash valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Nutrien from crop-input and food-security evidence into a
company-specific valuation and liquidity object. It separates potash, nitrogen,
phosphate, farmer retail, agronomy, supplier financing, mine/plant capital, and
portfolio exits from crop-protection chemistry, diversified mining, and
agribusiness processing.

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 sales were `$26.885B`, OCF `$4.007B`, management FCF `$1.979B`, sustaining/mine-development/pre-stripping capital `$1.609B`, and lease principal `$419M`.
- FY2025 cash was about `$701M` against total debt `$10.736B`, receivables `$5.675B`, inventory `$6.977B`, and asset-retirement/environmental obligations `$1.468B`.
- Nutrien operates six potash mines, twelve nitrogen facilities, six phosphate facilities, more than 1,800 retail locations, and more than 4,200 crop consultants.
- FY2025 cash improved alongside higher nutrient prices and supplier financing; that funding must be tested for recurrence and reversal risk.
- Portfolio alternatives involving phosphate, Trinidad nitrogen, and Brazilian retail require proceeds, taxes, stranded cost, lost cash generation, and remaining-asset-return analysis.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate Retail, Potash, Nitrogen, and Phosphate revenue, margin, volume, price, cost, working capital, and capital spending.
2. Reconcile supplier-financing, payables, inventory, and receivables movements before treating OCF as recurring cash.
3. Reconcile management FCF to sustaining, mine-development, pre-stripping, lease principal, environmental, retail, growth, acquisition, and exit cash.
4. Test potash tonnes, realized price, unit cost, automation, reserve life, mine development, and capacity additions through a mid-cycle price.
5. Test retail gross margin, proprietary products, customer retention, agronomy, financing, bad debt, inventory, stores, and technology.
6. Attribute portfolio exits to proceeds, tax, stranded overhead, lost EBITDA/cash, debt reduction, and remaining asset return.
7. Treat FCF, EBITDA, potash volume, supplier cash, dividends, and buybacks as diagnostic until mid-cycle common cash per share is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what mid-cycle nutrient prices,
tonnes, unit costs, utilization, mine life, retail margin, supplier financing,
sustaining capital, lease claims, debt, and cost of capital the valuation
requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether food security, energy,
trade, geopolitics, fertilizer affordability, farmer balance sheets, and rates
can support both upstream assets and downstream retail through a cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`agricultural-inputs-retail-potash-qualified; supplier-capital-and-common-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from nutrient price/volume and
retail activity to unit cost, inventory, receivables, supplier financing,
mine/plant renewal, environmental obligations, portfolio exits, debt, dividends,
and diluted common residual. FCF, EBITDA, production, retail growth, and
capital returns remain diagnostic inputs.
