# Travel marketplace, trust, and payments valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Airbnb from current-period travel and technology evidence into a company-specific valuation, reinvestment, liquidity, and owner-cash test. It treats Airbnb as a two-sided marketplace with trust, payments, host supply, guest demand, and expanding trip services—not as a hotel owner or ordinary application-software subscription.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Airbnb | Retained marketplace cash after host/guest settlements, payment processing, refunds, trust and safety, customer support, regulation, product investment, tax, and dilution | Host acquisition and quality, trust/safety, customer support, app/product engineering, AI infrastructure, marketing, payment operations, and working capital | Travel slowdown, host supply/regulation, fraud and chargebacks, service/experience execution, payment flexibility, platform outages, litigation, and cross-border tax | GBV, nights, revenue, and adjusted EBITDA rise while host retention, take rate after processing, refunds/claims, support cost, trust investment, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was about `$12.2B`, net income about `$2.5B`, adjusted EBITDA about `$4.3B`, free cash flow about `$4.6B`, nights and seats booked `533.0M`, and gross booking value about `$91.3B`.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$3.6B`, gross booking value `$27.2B`, nights and seats booked `148.3M`, net income `$816M`, and adjusted EBITDA `$1.3B`; revenue grew `17%`, GBV `16%`, and nights/seats `10%` year over year.
- Q2 expanded services into grocery delivery, car rentals, airport pickups, luggage storage, and resort passes; experience supply grew nearly `80%` year over year, while about `45%` of support issues beginning with the AI assistant were resolved without a human agent.
- Q1 2026 app-booked nights grew `22%`, represented `63%` of total nights booked, and about `20%` of global GBV came from Reserve Now, Pay Later bookings. Airbnb also reported that approximately `55%` of guests who book a hotel on Airbnb later return to book a home.
- FY2025 had more than `5M` hosts, over `2.5B` guest arrivals, and reach across more than `220` countries and regions, making host supply, local regulation, trust, and payment rails central to the model.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile GBV to Airbnb revenue after host payouts, payment processing, refunds, cancellations, taxes, incentives, and other transaction costs; GBV is not retained owner cash.
2. Test host supply and guest demand separately by geography, stay type, experience/service category, app channel, and regulation rather than treating nights booked as homogeneous volume.
3. Keep Reserve Now, Pay Later and Pay Less Upfront as conversion and collection questions; inspect credit exposure, settlement timing, refunds, chargebacks, and any third-party funding perimeter.
4. Treat hotels, experiences, and services as acquisition, cross-sell, and operating-cost cohorts until repeat booking, contribution, support, insurance, and cancellation economics are evidenced.
5. Reconcile trust and safety, customer support, insurance, host protection, litigation, local taxes, and regulatory compliance as recurring costs of maintaining the marketplace.
6. Separate AI support savings from ongoing product, model, infrastructure, moderation, and human-escalation costs; adjusted EBITDA, buybacks, and per-share value remain residual claims requiring diluted-share validation.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what nights, GBV, take rate, host retention, repeat bookings, services/experiences contribution, payment cost, trust investment, growth reinvestment, and cost of capital the marketplace valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether discretionary travel, host financing, local regulation, fraud, platform dependence, payment interruptions, and geopolitical or currency shocks can impair both sides of the network while fixed trust, support, and technology obligations remain.

## Promotion boundary

`airbnb-travel-marketplace-qualified; host-guest-collection-and-common-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from host supply and guest bookings to retained revenue, settlement/collection, refunds, trust and safety, support, regulation, required product investment, tax, and diluted common residual. GBV, nights/seats, adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, app share, hotel conversion, and AI support resolution remain diagnostic inputs.
