# Allegion physical access and security valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Allegion from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It separates installed physical-access systems, electronics and software adoption, non-residential project flow, regional mix, productivity, acquisition effects, debt, and diluted common residual from reported growth and adjusted earnings.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Allegion | Installed access-control and building-security cash after hardware, electronics, software, installation workflow, retrofit, productivity, taxes, debt, and dilution | Product development, electronics/software investment, inventory, warranty and quality, acquisitions, working capital, debt, and shares | Non-residential project slowdown, residential weakness, European weakness, electronics adoption shortfall, acquisition integration, inflation, or leverage | Revenue and adjusted EPS rise while organic growth, Americas non-residential demand, conversion, recurring installed-base economics, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- 2025 revenue was `$4,067.3M`, organic growth was `4.1%`, adjusted EPS was `$8.14`, adjusted operating margin was `23.2%`, and available cash flow was `$685.7M`.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$1,151.5M`, up `12.7%` reported and `6.9%` organically; adjusted EPS was `$2.40` and adjusted operating margin was `24.2%`.
- Americas non-residential and electronics were the strongest current demand signals, while some European markets and residential activity were weaker.
- The model spans locks, doors, exit devices, access control, workforce productivity systems, electronics, and adjacent software; reported growth therefore requires organic, mix, and acquisition separation.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile reported growth to organic growth, regional mix, price, productivity, inflation, and acquisition or divestiture effects.
2. Test whether electronics and software adoption creates recurring installed-base economics or merely shifts one-time hardware timing.
3. Separate non-residential retrofit and project demand from residential volume and European weakness.
4. Reconcile available cash flow to inventory, receivables, warranty, product development, acquisitions, debt, and share-based dilution.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what access-control adoption, retrofit cadence, price/productivity, margin, reinvestment, and cost of capital are embedded in the valuation. The stress test asks whether institutional security spending, construction, electronics supply, labor, rates, and regional demand support collection and recurring cash after replacement and funding needs.

## Promotion boundary

`allegion-qualified; installed-base-and-electronics-recurring-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from access products and systems to collection, warranty and replacement, development investment, acquisition return, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, adjusted EPS, adjusted margin, OCF, FCF, and guidance remain diagnostic inputs.
