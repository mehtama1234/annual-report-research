# American Eagle and Aerie apparel valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves American Eagle Outfitters from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests the American Eagle and Aerie brand split, comparable sales, store and digital demand, inventory and markdowns, youth and comfort culture, working capital, leases, capital returns, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not pool the company with Gap, Levi, lululemon, Kohl’s, or off-price retailers.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| American Eagle Outfitters | Multi-brand apparel cash after Aerie growth, American Eagle relevance, comparable sales, inventory, markdowns, stores, digital, marketing, leases, taxes, debt, and dilution | Product and brand investment, stores, digital, inventory, markdowns, fulfillment, leases, working capital, debt, and shares | Core-banner weakness, trend failure, Aerie concentration, promotional intensity, inventory write-downs, mall traffic, or lease burden | Total sales and EPS rise while Aerie carries the portfolio, American Eagle comps weaken, inventory quality deteriorates, cash conversion falls, or diluted common residual shrinks |

## Current evidence anchors

- Fiscal 2025 net revenue was about `$5.5B`, up `3%`, with comparable sales up `3%`; Aerie comps rose `9%` while American Eagle was flat.
- Q1 2026 revenue was about `$1.2B`, up `10%`; total comps rose `8%`; Aerie comps rose `25%`, American Eagle comps declined `2%`, digital revenue rose `15%`, and store revenue rose `8%`.
- Q4 2025 revenue rose `10%` to `$1.8B`; Aerie comps rose `23%`; American Eagle comps rose `2%`; and shareholder returns were `$341M`.
- The central quality issue is portfolio concentration: Aerie is increasingly carrying cultural relevance and growth while the core banner remains more fragile.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate Aerie and American Eagle traffic, transactions, average ticket, product, promotions, digital, store, and inventory economics.
2. Reconcile comparable sales and margin to markdowns, inventory write-downs, freight, leases, marketing, and working capital.
3. Test whether Aerie growth is durable and transferable or merely masking core-banner deterioration.
4. Reconcile EPS, operating cash flow, dividends, buybacks, leases, and debt to diluted common residual.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what Aerie growth, American Eagle recovery, comparable sales, inventory turns, brand investment, store productivity, leverage, and cost of capital are embedded in the valuation. The stress test asks whether youth spending, comfort and identity trends, mall traffic, promotions, leases, and fashion cycles support cash after replacement and funding needs.

## Promotion boundary

`american-eagle-qualified; aerie-concentration-and-core-banner-recovery-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from each brand’s demand to collection, inventory and markdowns, store and digital productivity, brand reinvestment, leases, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, comps, EPS, OCF, FCF, guidance, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
