# Closed-loop membership, premium payments, and credit valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves American Express from current-period card and membership evidence into a company-specific valuation, billed-business, fee, merchant, credit, travel, data, agentic-commerce, and owner-cash test. It treats AmEx as a closed-loop premium-spend and membership network, not as a generic bank or payment processor.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| American Express | Collected card-member, merchant, fee, lending, and network cash after rewards, partner economics, funding, credit losses, fraud, servicing, technology, capital, dividends, and dilution | Card acquisition and rewards, merchant acceptance, travel/dining partnerships, digital and AI personalization, fraud systems, servicing, credit infrastructure, marketing, and working capital | Consumer and commercial credit, billed-business slowdown, premium-card retention, merchant economics, funding, fraud, regulatory change, travel/dining cycles, capital ratios, and dilution | Revenue, billed business, card acquisitions, fee growth, EPS, membership, or buybacks rise while spend quality, card retention, rewards cost, merchant yield, credit losses, funding, CET1, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$72.2B`, net income `$10.8B`, EPS `$15.38`, and billed business `$1.67T`.
- AmEx acquired `12.5M` new proprietary cards in 2025, with more than `70%` of new accounts on fee-paying products; Millennial and Gen Z consumers were about `65%` of new global consumer account acquisitions.
- Q2 `2026` revenue was `$19.6B`, net income `$3.1B`, and EPS `$4.53`; management raised full-year 2026 revenue-growth guidance to `10%` while reaffirming EPS guidance of `$17.30–$17.90`.
- Q1 `2026` revenue was `$18.9B`, net income `$3.0B`, and EPS `$4.28`; U.S. consumer billed business grew `10%` and commercial solutions continued expanding.
- Q4 `2025` billed business increased `9%`, net card-fee revenue had its 30th straight quarter of double-digit growth, and credit performance remained strong; CET1 remained within a `10%–11%` target range.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile billed business, merchant discount revenue, card fees, interest income, rewards, partner payments, charge-offs, reserves, fraud, and cash; billed volume is not AmEx cash.
2. Separate consumer, commercial, international, premium, fee-paying, travel/dining, lending, merchant, and agentic-commerce cohorts; retention and credit economics differ.
3. Test new-card acquisition, Platinum refresh, rewards, merchant acceptance, AI and data tools, and partner ecosystems through incremental spend, retention, cost, credit, and return on invested capital.
4. Keep EPS, fee growth, billed business, membership, dividends, and buybacks separate from recurring collected common-owner cash.
5. Reconcile funding, interest expense, rewards, credit provisions, fraud, regulatory capital, taxes, SBC, and diluted shares.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what billed-business growth, premium-card retention, fee income, merchant yield, rewards economics, credit losses, data and AI monetization, reinvestment, and cost of equity the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether consumer spending, credit losses, rates, funding, merchant bargaining power, fraud, regulation, and travel/dining cycles preserve cash conversion.

## Promotion boundary

`amex-closed-loop-membership-qualified; spend-fee-credit-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from card-member, merchant, and lending cohorts to billed-business collection, fee and interest revenue, rewards and partner cost, credit loss, funding, regulatory capital, taxes, dividends, and diluted common residual. Revenue, billed business, card acquisitions, fee growth, EPS, membership, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
