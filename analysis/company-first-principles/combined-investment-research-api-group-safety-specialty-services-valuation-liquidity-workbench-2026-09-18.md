# APi Group safety and specialty-services valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves APi Group from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests recurring inspection, monitoring, safety services, specialty infrastructure projects, backlog, field labor, acquisition integration, cash conversion, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not pool APi with general contractors, security hardware, monitoring, or waste-service operators.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| APi Group | Safety and specialty-services cash after recurring inspection, monitoring, project execution, field labor, acquisitions, backlog conversion, taxes, debt, and dilution | Technical labor, fleet and equipment, branch systems, inspection technology, acquisitions, working capital, debt, and shares | Labor shortage, project delay, acquisition integration, customer concentration, backlog cancellation, leverage, or service-quality claims | Adjusted EBITDA and backlog rise while recurring collection, labor productivity, cash conversion, acquisition return, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- 2025 net revenues were `$7.911B`, adjusted EBITDA margin was `13.2%`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.48`, and adjusted free cash flow was `$836M`.
- Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA was `$311M`, and management raised 2026 guidance to `$8.875B-$9.025B` revenue, `$1.205B-$1.245B` adjusted EBITDA, and `115%` adjusted FCF conversion.
- Backlog exceeded `$5B`; inspection, service, monitoring, and project activity remained strong across Safety Services and Specialty Services.
- APi’s `13/60/80` targets have transitioned to `10/16/60+`, while CertaSite, Wtech, and Onyx highlight continued acquisition dependence.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate recurring inspection and monitoring from project revenue, backlog timing, change orders, and pass-through costs.
2. Reconcile adjusted free-cash-flow conversion to labor, fleet, equipment, working capital, claims, acquisitions, debt, and dilution.
3. Test backlog quality, cancellation risk, customer concentration, service-level obligations, and field-labor availability.
4. Separate acquired EBITDA and synergies from organic productivity and same-entity collected cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what recurring revenue, backlog conversion, labor productivity, acquisition return, margin, cash conversion, leverage, and cost of capital are embedded in the valuation. The stress test asks whether compliance, healthcare, data-center, and critical-infrastructure demand survives labor inflation, project delays, rates, customer budgets, and service claims.

## Promotion boundary

`api-group-qualified; recurring-service-and-acquisition-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from inspections, monitoring, and projects to collection, labor and service cost, backlog delivery, acquisition return, debt, claims, funding, and diluted common residual. Revenue, adjusted EBITDA, backlog, adjusted FCF, guidance, and capital returns remain diagnostic inputs.
