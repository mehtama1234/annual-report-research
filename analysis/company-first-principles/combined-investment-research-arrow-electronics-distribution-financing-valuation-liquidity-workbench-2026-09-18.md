# Electronics distribution, working-capital financing, and channel-control valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Arrow Electronics from annual-report evidence into a
company-specific component and enterprise-computing distribution, inventory,
receivables-financing, supplier-protection, customer-credit, and common-owner
residual test. It keeps Arrow separate from semiconductor manufacturers,
custom-silicon designers, and industrial distributors because financing the
channel is part of Arrow's product and risk.

## Decision matrix

| Arrow Electronics | Through-cycle gross profit and owner cash after inventory turns, receivable collection, supplier protections, factoring/securitization, credit losses, distribution facilities, technology support, debt, and dilution | Distribution centers, inventory, design/technical support, logistics, customer-credit systems, receivables funding, working capital, and acquisitions | Customer or supplier shock, inventory obsolescence, receivable aging, securitization/factoring availability, rate/refinancing, component cycle, or thin-margin compression | Sales and operating income rise while inventory/receivable funding, supplier protection, cash conversion, financing cost, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 sales were `$30.853B`, operating income `$822M`, operating margin `2.7%`, OCF `$64M`, and PP&E spending `$101M`; the simple post-PP&E screen was approximately negative `$37M` before financing, dividends, and buybacks.
- FY2025 accounts receivable were `$19.739B`, inventory `$5.082B`, and cash `$306M`. A `$6.342B` receivable increase used cash while a `$6.108B` accounts-payable increase supplied cash, making working-capital timing central to the analysis.
- Arrow sold `$1.670B` of receivables through an EMEA securitization program and `$1.131B` through factoring programs, with `$970M` borrowed under a North American receivables securitization program.
- Global Components generated approximately `$21.501B` of 2025 sales. Growth can be economically weak if inventory and receivables grow faster than gross profit or if supplier price protection does not cover obsolescence.
- Arrow operates more than `140` sales facilities and `39` distribution/value-added centers across more than `85` countries; scale supports availability but also creates fixed infrastructure and regional-credit exposure.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile sales to gross profit, inventory turns, supplier price protection,
   customer collection, return rights, rebates, and product obsolescence.
2. Rebuild OCF after receivable sales, factoring, payables timing, inventory
   growth, customer-credit losses, and the financing cost of the channel.
3. Separate Global Components from Enterprise Computing Solutions by margin,
   backlog/acceptance, systems integration, service, inventory, and capital.
4. Test securitized and factored receivables for continuing exposure, recourse,
   eligibility, renewal, pricing, concentration, and cash availability.
5. Treat sales growth, adjusted earnings, OCF, dividends, and repurchases as
   diagnostic until full-cycle cash per diluted share is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what gross margin, turns, receivable
collection, supplier funding, credit loss, facility cost, financing spread, and
cost of capital the equity value requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks
whether rates, component shortages, customer liquidity, supplier allocation,
trade cycles, and securitization haircuts preserve cash through a technology
downcycle.

## Promotion boundary

`arrow-electronics-distribution-qualified; working-capital-financing-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from component/system demand to
gross profit, inventory settlement, customer collection, supplier protection,
receivable-financing terms, credit loss, debt, taxes, and diluted common
residual. Revenue, sales growth, operating income, OCF, receivable proceeds,
factoring, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
