# Arthur J. Gallagher brokerage and risk-advisory valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Arthur J. Gallagher from broker-sector evidence into a
company-specific valuation object. It separates brokerage, risk management,
employee benefits, acquisition cohorts, organic fee growth, producer talent,
client retention, integration costs, working capital, debt, and diluted common
residual. It does not treat reported revenue, organic fee growth, adjusted EPS,
adjusted EBITDA, operating cash flow, or acquisitions as normalized owner cash.

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net earnings were about `$2.052B`, with brokerage organic fee growth of
  `6%` for the year; 4Q25 brokerage organic fee growth was `7%`.
- 1Q26 revenue was about `$4.293B` and net earnings about `$913M`; 2Q26 total
  revenue was about `$3.955B` and adjusted diluted EPS about `$2.84`.
- Brokerage remains the economic core, while risk management adds a durable fee
  stream with less balance-sheet intensity than a lender or property owner.
- Gallagher is explicitly acquisition-driven, so purchase price, acquired-book
  retention, producer migration, earn-outs, integration expense, and debt must
  remain visible in the cash bridge.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile organic fee growth to renewal retention, exposure and rate,
   producer productivity, new business, client collection, and carrier
   settlement; reported growth alone does not prove durable cash.
2. Track each acquisition cohort from purchase price and funding through
   acquired revenue, retention, compensation, integration cost, earn-outs,
   goodwill, and per-share return.
3. Separate brokerage from risk-management economics and keep adjusted add-backs
   tied to recurring integration, compensation, restructuring, and technology
   cash requirements.
4. Test whether talent retention and producer compensation consume the apparent
   margin advantage as the platform scales.
5. Treat buybacks and dividends as residual claims only after acquisition,
   integration, working-capital, and debt obligations are funded.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what organic fee growth, renewal,
producer productivity, acquisition return, integration period, reinvestment
rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test
asks whether client budgets, insurance pricing, talent retention, acquisition
financing, and rates remain liquid through a soft market without confusing fee
scale with unlevered owner cash.

## Promotion boundary

`gallagher-qualified; broker-rollup-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from client fees to collection and carrier
settlement, renewal and producer retention, acquired-book returns, integration
cash, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, organic fee growth,
adjusted earnings, OCF, and acquisitions remain diagnostic inputs.
