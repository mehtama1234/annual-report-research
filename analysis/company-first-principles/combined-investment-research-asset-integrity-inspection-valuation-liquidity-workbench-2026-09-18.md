# Asset-integrity inspection valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves MISTRAS Group from annual-report evidence into a company-specific valuation, reinvestment, liquidity, and diluted common-owner residual test. It keeps certified inspection, condition monitoring, engineering, data, and recurring technical-services economics separate from environmental services, specialty construction, industrial distribution, and electronic test.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MISTRAS Group | Asset-integrity cash after recurring-program renewal, technician utilization, collection, contract assets, software/data, accreditation, capex, claims, debt, and dilution | Certified technical labor, training, laboratories, digital inspection, software, accreditation, equipment, safety, and working capital | Turnaround or customer-capital delay, receivable dispute, technician shortage, utilization decline, debt service, recurring capability repair, or claims | Revenue and adjusted EBITDA rise while recurring renewal, collection, technical capability, leverage, or diluted residual deteriorates |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$724.0M`, adjusted EBITDA `$91.1M`, OCF `$33.0M`, and free cash flow only `$3.8M`; FY2024 FCF was `$27.1M`.
- FY2025 PP&E and intangible capex was `$29.2M`; gross debt was `$178.0M` against `$28.0M` of cash.
- The control point is certified technical judgment connected to inspection history, asset data, regulatory support, and failure prevention. Customers still control maintenance schedules, capital budgets, vendor choice, and payment.
- Recurring run-and-maintain programs coexist with projects, turnarounds, products, software, analytics, and aerospace/defense work; these require separate renewal, utilization, margin, and cash tests.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Split recurring programs from projects, turnarounds, products, and one-time work; test renewal, pricing, utilization, acceptance, and cash per technician.
2. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA to OCF and FCF through receivables, contract assets, unbilled work, allowances, capex, capitalized software, and customer concentration.
3. Track certified headcount, billable hours, wage inflation, turnover, training, subcontractors, safety, and rework; technical talent is both moat and cost.
4. Keep restructuring, laboratory consolidation, workflow automation, acquisition integration, and capability investment in the denominator when the strategy depends on recurring transformation.
5. Test debt and interest against normalized FCF rather than adjusted EBITDA; margin improvement alone does not prove liquidity.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what recurring-program renewal, technician utilization, pricing, end-market mix, software/data attachment, reinvestment, and cost of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether oil and gas turnarounds, aerospace/defense budgets, power, industrial production, infrastructure spending, wages, rates, and customer liquidity preserve demand and collections.

## Promotion boundary

`asset-integrity-inspection-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from recurring inspection and technical work to renewal, utilization, billing and collection, certified-labor cost, capability investment, debt service, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, adjusted EBITDA, gross margin, aerospace/defense growth, OCF, FCF, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
