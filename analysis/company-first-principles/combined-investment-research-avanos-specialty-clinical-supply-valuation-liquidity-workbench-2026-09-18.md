# Avanos specialty clinical-supply valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench tests Avanos as a direct-plus-distributor provider of recurring specialty clinical products. It keeps enteral nutrition, neonatal care, pain management, procedure utilization, hospital/distributor routing, transformation savings, the pending take-private, working capital, debt, and diluted common residual separate from reported revenue and adjusted EPS.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Avanos | Specialty clinical-supply cash after nutrition systems, pain/recovery products, provider and distributor settlement, quality/regulatory spending, transformation, inventory, transaction claims, debt, and diluted common residual | Product development, quality systems, manufacturing, provider support, inventory, distributor terms, acquisitions, transformation, debt, and shares | Hospital utilization pressure, nutrition/pain category volatility, supplier or quality event, cost-reset failure, weak cash conversion, take-private execution, or refinancing | Specialty Nutrition Systems growth and adjusted EPS rise while consumables attachment, margins, free cash flow, transformation cash, or diluted per-share residual weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 sales were `$701.2M`; Q1 2026 sales were `$182.2M`, with Specialty Nutrition Systems `$124.0M` and Pain Management and Recovery `$56.3M`.
- Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was `$21.8M`, but operating cash flow was negative `$12.3M` and free cash flow negative `$16.6M`.
- FY2025 free cash flow was `$43.1M`; year-end net debt was `$10.7M`.
- Management expanded transformation initiatives expected to deliver `$15M-$20M` of incremental annualized savings by the end of 2026.
- Affiliates of American Industrial Partners agreed to acquire Avanos for stated enterprise value of `$1.272B`, making transaction terms and public-evidence cutoffs part of the liquidity perimeter.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile nutrition-system and pain/recovery volume to provider and distributor settlement, mix, inventory, and cash conversion.
2. Separate organic growth from Nexus Medical acquisition effects, transformation savings, impairment, and take-private transaction claims.
3. Test cost savings against quality, regulatory, manufacturing, provider-support, and required reinvestment costs.
4. Keep debt, transaction consideration, working capital, stock compensation, and diluted shares in the residual schedule.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what specialty-product growth, clinical-use repetition, margin, transformation savings, reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether hospitals, alternate sites, and distributors continue funding nutrition and pain-management products through utilization, labor, supply, regulatory, and ownership-transition shocks.

## Promotion boundary

`avanos-qualified; specialty-clinical-collection-and-transaction-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from clinical products to provider/distributor collections, inventory, quality and regulatory spend, transformation cash, transaction funding, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, adjusted EPS, adjusted EBITDA, OCF, guidance, and sale consideration remain diagnostic inputs.
