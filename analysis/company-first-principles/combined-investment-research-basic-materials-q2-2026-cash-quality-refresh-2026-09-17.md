# Basic materials Q2 2026 cash-quality refresh

Research date: 2026-09-17

## Finding

The latest official filings strengthen the basic-materials input-scarcity lane,
but they also show why commodity and infrastructure cash must be normalized
before valuation. CF Industries combines improved nitrogen economics with
Yazoo disruption proceeds, tax deposits, and Blue Point partner funding.
Sherwin-Williams shows a more diversified maintenance and distribution model,
but its cash is exposed to working capital, leases, acquisitions, and heavy
shareholder returns. West Fraser is recovering operationally while remaining
cycle-, tariff-, and modernization-sensitive.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

CF's H1 OCF is not a clean nitrogen-cycle numerator. The filing identifies
litigation settlement and Yazoo business-interruption insurance proceeds, and
also notes substantial Canadian tax deposits. Blue Point One includes project
capital for a low-carbon ammonia facility, with partner capital contributions
funding 60% of the joint venture's spending. These are visible facts, not
fraud findings, but a normalized CF bridge must separate recurring operations,
incident recovery, tax timing, sustaining capital, transition capital, and
partner-funded project spending.

Sherwin-Williams' H1 cash statement exposes a different burden pattern. Net
operating cash rose to $1.487B, but working-capital accounts used $397.6M and
operating lease liabilities used $239.5M. Capital expenditures and acquisitions
used $246.7M and $64.9M, while treasury-stock purchases used $1.837B and cash
dividends used $394.6M. The company also reports $57.3M of SBC. Buybacks and
noncash lease expense cannot be used to claim that accounting earnings equal
common-owner cash.

West Fraser's current release reports $192M of Q2 operating cash and $159M of
H1 capex, but Q2 adjusted EBITDA was only $59M and net earnings were negative
$61M. The current result is a recovery diagnostic, not a through-cycle return.
Resin, wax, transportation, lumber pricing, duties, mill ramp-up, closures,
and modernization spending remain the decisive reconciliations.

## Owner-cash and valuation implication

- **CF Industries:** value requires a gas-to-nitrogen spread, utilization,
  outage recovery, sustaining capital, Blue Point funding and return, taxes,
  environmental obligations, and cycle-normalized price assumptions.
- **Sherwin-Williams:** value requires repaint and contractor durability,
  price realization, raw-material costs, working capital, lease burden,
  acquisition integration, debt, SBC, and diluted-share treatment.
- **West Fraser:** value requires normalized lumber/OSB pricing, mill
  utilization, duties and freight, sustaining versus modernization capital,
  restructuring, and liquidity across the housing cycle.

Damodaran-style valuation should make price pay explicitly for utilization,
price/mix, margin duration, reinvestment, and terminal economics. Lyn Alden-
style stress should test gas and power prices, rates, tariffs, freight, food
and housing affordability, geopolitical supply disruption, and transition-
financing access. Adjusted EBITDA, OCF, and current cash recovery are inputs to
that work, not its conclusion.

## Promotion consequence

This is a material current-period upgrade to the basic-materials lane. It
connects scarcity and maintenance control points to named exceptional cash,
partner funding, working-capital absorption, lease burden, project capital,
and shareholder distributions. The lane remains
`cycle-qualified; owner-cash-open; no-ranking`.
