# Specialty retail, loyalty, and brand-repair valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Bath & Body Works from current-period retail evidence into a company-specific valuation, store, direct, loyalty, product, inventory, seasonal, transformation, and owner-cash test. It treats the company as a large but repair-oriented fragrance and specialty-retail platform, not as a consumer-staples or pure digital marketplace business.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bath & Body Works | Collected fragrance and specialty-retail cash after product sourcing, store labor and occupancy, direct fulfillment, promotions, loyalty, inventory, Consumer First Formula investment, taxes, debt, and dilution | Product innovation and testing, stores and remodels, e-commerce and marketplace, marketing, loyalty, inventory, distribution, international expansion, and transformation spending | Product and brand relevance, seasonal demand, promotions, inventory and markdowns, store productivity, channel mix, sourcing/tariffs, lease burden, debt, and dilution | Sales, loyalty members, adjusted EPS, store count, direct growth, or buybacks rise while full-price sell-through, repeat purchase, inventory, markdowns, store productivity, collection, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net sales were `$7.291B`, down `0.2%`; operating income was `$1.126B`, down `11%`; reported net income was `$649M`; and diluted EPS was `$3.11`.
- Bath & Body Works operated `1,927` company stores in the U.S. and Canada, `573` international stores, `34` international e-commerce sites, an Amazon presence, and about `40M` active loyalty members.
- Q1 `2026` sales were `$1.378B`, down `3%`; operating income `$231M`; net income `$183M`; reported EPS `$0.90`; and adjusted EPS `$0.32`.
- Q1 stores and direct sales were `$1.062B` and `$246M`, respectively, while international and other sales were `$70M`; an `$88M` pre-tax interchange-fee settlement benefit affected the quarter.
- Q3 `2025` stores were `$1.222B` versus `$1.220B` prior year, while direct fell to `$299M` from `$321M`; management launched the Consumer First Formula transformation in Q3.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile units, full-price sell-through, average order value, repeat purchase, loyalty activity, store traffic, direct collection, inventory, and markdowns; loyalty membership is not cash.
2. Separate stores, direct, international, Amazon/marketplace, gift, fragrance, body care, and seasonal cohorts; demand, margin, and fulfillment differ.
3. Test the Consumer First Formula through product launches, brand investment, customer response, inventory turns, promotion, store productivity, and return on invested capital.
4. Keep settlement benefits, adjusted EPS, buybacks, loyalty members, store count, and transformation claims separate from recurring collected owner cash.
5. Reconcile product costs, freight and sourcing, occupancy, labor, marketing, leases, debt, taxes, SBC, and diluted shares.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what same-store sales, full-price mix, repeat purchase, store productivity, direct conversion, product innovation, inventory investment, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether discretionary spending, promotions, sourcing costs, tariffs, leases, rates, and brand fatigue preserve cash conversion.

## Promotion boundary

`bath-body-works-specialty-retail-qualified; brand-repair-inventory-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from product and channel cohorts to customer collection, full-price sell-through, inventory and markdown, store and direct cost, transformation spending, debt service, taxes, and diluted common residual. Sales, loyalty members, store count, direct growth, adjusted EPS, settlement benefits, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
