# Becton Dickinson institutional workflow valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench tests BD as a scaled provider, laboratory, and pharmaceutical workflow platform. It keeps medication delivery, specimen management, connected care, prefillable drug delivery, interventional products, the Waters perimeter change, debt, repurchases, quality/regulatory spending, and diluted common residual separate from reported revenue and adjusted EPS.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Becton Dickinson / New BD | Institutional workflow cash after medication delivery, specimen management, connected care, biopharma systems, interventional products, quality, manufacturing, portfolio transition, debt, and diluted common residual | Product development, hospital integrations, manufacturing, prefillable capacity, quality systems, acquisitions, debt retirement, working capital, and shares | Hospital capital pressure, pharmaceutical demand shift, procedure slowdown, Waters transition, quality/regulatory event, currency, leverage, or refinancing | Segment growth and adjusted EPS rise while recurring workflow attachment, margins, debt reduction, quality spending, or diluted per-share cash weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 Medical segment revenue was `$10.074B`; the pre-Waters annual perimeter included Medical, Life Sciences, and Interventional.
- Q3 FY2026 revenue was `$5.0B`; Medical Essentials `$1.675B`, Connected Care `$1.224B`, BioPharma Systems `$670M`, and Interventional `$1.414B`.
- Q2 FY2026 included a `$2.0B` accelerated share repurchase and `$2.1B` debt retirement, which must remain in the residual and funding bridge.
- BD completed the Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions spin/Waters combination in February 2026; continuing-operations comparisons require strict period and perimeter control.
- The platform includes medication management, infusion interoperability, patient monitoring, hazardous-drug testing, prefillable syringes, and interventional systems.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile continuing-operations segment growth to medication, specimen, connected-care, biopharma, and interventional collections after the Waters perimeter change.
2. Separate recurring consumables and workflow attachment from capital projects, contract timing, acquisitions, and portfolio transition effects.
3. Test connected-care and AI claims against implementation, quality, regulatory, service, and required manufacturing investment.
4. Keep debt retirement, accelerated repurchases, stock compensation, litigation, quality claims, and diluted shares in the common-owner residual schedule.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what institutional workflow growth, medication and specimen attachment, biopharma capacity, interventional demand, margin, reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether hospitals, labs, and pharmaceutical manufacturers continue funding embedded workflow infrastructure through budget, labor, regulatory, supply, and rate shocks.

## Promotion boundary

`bd-qualified; institutional-workflow-and-perimeter-transition-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from products and workflow systems to hospital/lab/pharma collections, quality and regulatory costs, manufacturing reinvestment, Waters transition, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, adjusted EPS, segment growth, OCF, guidance, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
