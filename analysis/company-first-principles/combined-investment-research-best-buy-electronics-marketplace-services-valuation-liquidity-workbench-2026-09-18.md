# Electronics retail, marketplace, and services valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Best Buy from current-period retail evidence into a company-specific valuation, category-cycle, attached-services, marketplace, and owner-cash test. It treats the company as a mature electronics retailer trying to add services, advertising, marketplace, repair, trade-in, and recycling economics around a volatile device-upgrade base.

## Decision matrix

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2026 enterprise revenue was `$41.691B`, full-year comparable sales increased `0.5%`, diluted EPS was `$5.04`, and adjusted diluted EPS was `$6.43`.
- Q1 FY2027 enterprise revenue was `$8.936B`, enterprise comparable sales rose `2.0%`, domestic comparable sales rose `1.8%`, diluted EPS was `$1.31`, adjusted diluted EPS `$1.28`, and adjusted operating margin was `4.1%`.
- Q4 FY2026 enterprise revenue was `$13.814B`, enterprise comparable sales declined `0.8%`, while full-year comparable sales turned positive; Best Buy launched and scaled its U.S. digital Marketplace and nearly doubled ad partners year over year.
- Q1 strength was concentrated in gaming, computing, mobile phones, and services, while appliances remained weaker; Q3 showed the same uneven category pattern with home theater and appliances weaker.
- Management is layering Marketplace, Best Buy Ads, Geek Squad, delivery, repair, trade-in, recycling, and omnichannel improvements onto a lower-growth store footprint, while Best Buy Health remains an execution risk.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile comparable sales to units, average selling price, category mix, vendor funding, promotions, returns, shrink, inventory aging, and customer sell-through; comps are not retained owner cash.
2. Separate product margin from Geek Squad/service, Marketplace commissions, Best Buy Ads, delivery, trade-in, recycling, and health initiatives; attached profit streams have different costs and durability.
3. Test vendor-funded promotions and launch timing for pull-forward, channel inventory, markdown, and future-period demand effects.
4. Reconcile inventory, payables, leases, distribution and delivery, service labor, store closures, digital platform investment, and working-capital timing before promoting free cash flow.
5. Test Marketplace and advertising through seller retention, take rate, fulfillment/returns, customer experience, compliance, and incremental contribution rather than partner count.
6. Treat adjusted EPS, dividends, repurchases, and non-GAAP margin as residual claims requiring service investment, leases, debt, and diluted-share validation.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what device replacement, category mix, vendor economics, service attachment, marketplace/ads take rate, inventory turns, store productivity, digital investment, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether households can fund upgrades through affordability, rate, employment, tariff, and supply shocks while stores, service labor, fulfillment, leases, and platform obligations remain.

## Promotion boundary

`best-buy-electronics-qualified; sellthrough-services-marketplace-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from product and service demand to unit sell-through, collection, inventory, vendor funding, returns/shrink, service contribution, Marketplace/Ads economics, leases, debt, taxes, and diluted common residual. Revenue, comps, adjusted EPS, Marketplace/ad growth, service claims, free cash flow, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
