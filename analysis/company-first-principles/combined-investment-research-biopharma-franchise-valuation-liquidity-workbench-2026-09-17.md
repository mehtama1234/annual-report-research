# Biopharma franchise valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Regeneron from product and pipeline evidence into a
company-specific valuation object. It tests whether Dupixent and EYLEA-funded
cash can replace mature franchises through R&D, clinical development,
manufacturing, collaboration economics, pricing, litigation, and dilution.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regeneron | Biologic-franchise cash after net product/collaboration settlement, R&D and clinical replacement, manufacturing, regulatory work, price concessions, litigation, debt, SBC, and dilution | R&D, clinical trials, IPR&D and licenses, biologics manufacturing, capacity, commercialization partners, patent defense, SBC, and share count | EYLEA or Dupixent erosion, failed or delayed pipeline, partner economics, pricing pressure, product liability, manufacturing disruption, tax, or regulatory shock | Revenue and OCF remain strong while franchise concentration, pipeline probability, R&D replacement, collaboration share, litigation, or diluted per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$14.343B`, operating cash flow `$4.979B`, capital
  expenditures `$898M`, and intangible-asset payments `$315M`.
- The screening residual after capex, intangible-asset payments, and
  acquisitions was about `$3.763B`, before buybacks, dividends, and the full
  economic cost of replacing mature products.
- FY2025 R&D expense was `$5.850B` and stock-based compensation `$994M`.
  Dupixent and the EYLEA franchise remain the principal cash engines, making
  patent, indication, pricing, and pipeline replacement central valuation
  denominators.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate Regeneron net product sales from collaboration revenue and partner-
   recorded sales; partner economics can change the reported revenue path and
   cash access.
2. Charge R&D, clinical manufacturing, regulatory work, IPR&D, licenses, and
   patent defense as franchise-replacement requirements rather than treating
   OCF-less-capex as durable owner cash.
3. Track Dupixent and EYLEA by indication, pricing, competition, royalties or
   profit share, patent/exclusivity calendar, and collection.
4. Reconcile manufacturing capacity, inventory, returns, rebates, product
   liability, tax, litigation, and collaboration settlements.
5. Keep `$994M` of SBC and repurchases visible when testing cash per diluted
   share; reported buybacks do not prove surplus common cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what franchise duration, probability-
weighted pipeline output, pricing, collaboration split, R&D intensity,
manufacturing return, and cost of capital the equity value requires. The Lyn
Alden-style stress test asks whether patent expiry, pricing policy, clinical
failure, supply disruption, litigation, and partner or regulatory changes can
reduce cash before replacement products mature.

## Promotion boundary

`biopharma-franchise-qualified; patent-and-pipeline-replacement-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from product demand through
collection, rebates/partner settlement, required R&D and manufacturing
reinvestment, claims, debt, legal-entity availability, and diluted common
residual. Product sales, collaboration revenue, pipeline milestones, reported
OCF, adjusted earnings, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
