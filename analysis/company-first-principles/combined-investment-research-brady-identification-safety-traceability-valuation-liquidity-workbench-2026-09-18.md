# Identification, safety, and traceability valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Brady from current-period industrial evidence into a company-specific valuation, compliance, product, acquisition, and owner-cash test. It treats Brady as embedded workplace-identification and safety infrastructure, not as a guard-force, monitored-security, or generic industrial-products company.

## Decision matrix

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brady | Collected identification, safety, traceability, and workflow cash after product sell-through, specialty materials, printers/software, customer implementation, industrial demand, acquisitions, inventory, R&D, debt, and dilution | Label/signage materials, printers, software, lockout-tagout and safety devices, R&D, acquisitions, channel support, inventory, and working capital | Industrial cadence, regional divergence, input cost, acquisition integration, regulatory/compliance shifts, product-cycle failure, FX, inventory, and dilution | Organic sales and EPS rise while product sell-through, customer workflow embedment, acquisition return, safety/compliance demand, inventory, R&D, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 sales were `$1.51B`, up `12.8%`, with organic growth `2.6%`, record adjusted diluted EPS `$4.60`, and shareholder returns of `$96.4M`.
- Q3 FY2026 sales were `$435.2M`, up `13.8%`, with organic growth `8.2%`, net income `$57.8M`, adjusted EPS `$1.50`, and operating cash flow `$78.2M`.
- Q3 regional organic growth was `10.1%` in Americas & Asia and `4.5%` in Europe & Australia; Q2 organic growth was `3.1%` and negative `1.1%`, respectively.
- Brady's product/workflow surface includes labels, signage, printers, software, lockout-tagout systems, safety devices, and identification tools that help identify and protect premises, products, and people.
- Reported growth includes acquisition and currency effects, making organic growth, new-product contribution, inventory, integration, and working-capital control necessary to the owner-cash test.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile sales to unit/product sell-through, recurring consumables, printer/software attachment, customer collection, inventory, and specialty-material cost; reported growth is not workflow cash.
2. Separate safety, identification, traceability, healthcare/industrial, and regional cohorts; Americas & Asia strength cannot automatically support Europe & Australia.
3. Test acquisitions through purchase price, integration, organic growth, cross-sell, product overlap, working capital, and return on invested capital.
4. Keep FX, materials, freight, channel timing, and distributor inventory separate from genuine product innovation and compliance-driven demand.
5. Reconcile R&D, product launches, safety claims, regulatory obligations, customer implementation, debt, and cash returns before promoting adjusted EPS.
6. Treat shareholder returns, adjusted EPS, acquisition contribution, and organic growth as residual diagnostics requiring inventory, reinvestment, debt, and diluted-share validation.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what compliance demand, product sell-through, consumables/software attachment, regional growth, acquisition return, R&D, working capital, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether industrial activity, safety regulation, supply-chain traceability, labor, geopolitical manufacturing, and regional shocks preserve spending on error reduction and operating continuity while product and channel costs remain.

## Promotion boundary

`brady-identification-qualified; safety-traceability-product-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from customer workflows to collection, product/consumable sell-through, printer/software attachment, acquisition integration, inventory, R&D, safety/compliance claims, debt, taxes, and diluted common residual. Sales, organic growth, adjusted EPS, regional growth, product launches, acquisitions, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
