# Brady workplace identification and safety valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Brady from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests labels, signage, printers, software, lockout-tagout, safety devices, traceability, regional mix, product innovation, acquisitions, working capital, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not pool Brady with access-control, monitoring, communications, or route-service companies.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brady | Workplace identification and compliance cash after labels, signage, printers, software, safety devices, product innovation, regional mix, acquisitions, taxes, debt, and dilution | R&D and new products, printing and software platforms, inventory, customer workflows, acquisitions, working capital, debt, and shares | Industrial slowdown, Europe weakness, acquisition dependence, FX, product-cycle failure, compliance-budget pressure, or leverage | Reported sales and adjusted EPS rise while organic growth, new-product adoption, regional demand, cash conversion, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 sales were `$1.51B`, organic sales growth was `2.6%`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$4.60`, and shareholder returns were `$96.4M`.
- Q3 FY2026 sales were `$435.2M`, up `13.8%`, with `8.2%` organic growth and operating cash flow of `$78.2M`.
- Q3 organic growth was `10.1%` in Americas & Asia and `4.5%` in Europe & Australia; Q2 still showed a `1.1%` organic decline in Europe & Australia.
- The model combines labels, signs, printers, software, lockout-tagout, and safety products; reported growth therefore requires organic, acquisition, FX, and product-mix separation.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile reported growth to organic growth, acquisition contribution, FX, price, volume, and new-product adoption.
2. Test whether compliance and traceability products create recurring workflow dependence or remain episodic industrial supplies.
3. Reconcile adjusted EPS and operating cash flow to inventory, R&D, acquisitions, working capital, warranty, debt, and dilution.
4. Keep stronger Americas & Asia performance separate from Europe’s weaker periods and from a broad industrial recovery claim.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what organic growth, innovation return, regional mix, margin, acquisition contribution, reinvestment, and cost of capital are embedded in the price. The stress test asks whether industrial activity, safety regulation, compliance spending, FX, rates, and customer budgets support durable collection after inventory and product-development needs.

## Promotion boundary

`brady-qualified; compliance-workflow-and-organic-growth-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from identification and safety products to collection, recurring workflow usage, product replacement, R&D return, acquisition integration, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Sales, organic growth, adjusted EPS, OCF, FCF, guidance, and capital returns remain diagnostic inputs.
