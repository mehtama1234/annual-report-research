# Branded consumer staples valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This workbench routes Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Brown-Forman, and Colgate-Palmolive
as branded consumer-staples models distinct from retailer cash. Beverage
concentrate/bottler economics, packaged food and beverage manufacturing,
premium spirits aging, and habitual/pet-care products each retain separate
volume, price/mix, channel, inventory, and reinvestment denominators.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coca-Cola | Trademark/concentrate/bottler-system cash after marketing, bottler support, working capital, acquisitions, debt, and dilution | Brand/marketing, bottler investments, concentrate/finished beverage capacity, package innovation, acquisitions, and SBC | Unit volume, affordability, bottler health, health regulation, currency, fairlife/brand impairment, and debt | Organic revenue and price/mix grow while unit cases, bottler support, brand return, or diluted cash weaken |
| PepsiCo | Food/beverage brand and physical distribution cash after ingredients, packaging, plants, retailer support, acquisitions, debt, and dilution | Plants, distribution, inventory, marketing, reformulation, portfolio acquisitions, working capital, and debt | Volume/private label, input inflation, retailer power, portfolio acquisitions, property-sale dependence, and debt | Revenue/price grows while volume, gross margin, capital needs, acquisition return, or diluted residual deteriorates |
| Brown-Forman | Premium spirits brand cash after aging inventory, cooperage, marketing, distributors, route changes, restructuring, debt, and dilution | Whiskey inventory/aging, distillation, barrels, marketing, owned distribution, working capital, acquisitions, and debt | Discretionary occasions, spirits volume, distributor changes, inventory aging, regulation, and rates | Organic sales/brand strength grows while aging cash, route-to-market, volume, or diluted residual weakens |
| Colgate-Palmolive | Habitual oral/personal/home/pet brand cash after R&D, advertising, packaging, retailer support, manufacturing, and dilution | Product science, advertising, manufacturing, packaging, Hill's pet nutrition, working capital, acquisitions, and debt | Private label, retailer concentration, input cost, FX, impairment, pet/health demand, and rates | Organic sales and base EPS rise while volume, retailer concentration, brand investment, impairment, or diluted cash deteriorates |

## Current evidence anchors

- Coca-Cola FY2025 net revenue was `$47.941B`, OCF `$7.408B`, PP&E purchases
  `$2.112B`, and reported FCF `$5.296B`; OCF included a `$6.069B` fairlife
  contingent-consideration payment, while unit case volume was even.
- PepsiCo FY2025 revenue was `$93.9B`, OCF `$12.087B`, capex `$4.415B`, property
  sale proceeds `$528M`, and company-defined FCF `$8.2B`; it paid `$7.6B` of
  dividends and acquired poppi and Siete for about `$3.15B` combined.
- Brown-Forman FY2025 sales were about `$4.0B`, operating income `$1.1B`, OCF
  `$598M`, capex `$167M`, and the simple FCF screen `$431M`; aging inventory and
  route-to-market costs remain outside that screen.
- Colgate-Palmolive FY2025 sales were `$20.382B`, OCF `$4.198B`, and oral care
  represented about `44%` of sales; Walmart was about `11%` of sales.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate unit volume, price/mix, currency, package/channel mix, gross
   bookings, bottler or distributor sell-in, and consumer sell-through.
2. Charge marketing, retailer support, bottler investment, plants, packaging,
   inventory, aging spirits, research, and reformulation before owner cash.
3. Keep fairlife consideration, property-sale proceeds, asset impairments,
   acquisitions, restructuring, and route changes visible.
4. Test brand strength against private label, retailer bargaining power,
   affordability, regulation, category volume, and repeat purchase.
5. Do not apply retailer cash definitions to branded manufacturers or bottler/
   distributor systems.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what unit volume, price/mix, brand
retention, channel economics, reinvestment, margin, and cost of capital the
valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether household
affordability, input costs, rates, FX, retailer power, regulation, and consumer
trade-down preserve cash.

## Promotion boundary

`branded-consumer-staples-qualified; volume-price-channel-and-reinvestment-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period sell-through/collection, bottler/distributor
settlement, inventory and working-capital normalization, brand/plant/aging
reinvestment, acquisition return, debt, SBC, and diluted common residual.
Revenue, organic sales, unit cases, price/mix, FCF, dividends, and buybacks
remain diagnostic inputs.
