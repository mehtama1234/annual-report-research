# Cash logistics and managed-services valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves The Brink's Company from its annual-report packet into a
company-specific valuation, reinvestment, and liquidity test. It separates
cash-and-valuables routes, ATM managed services, digital retail solutions,
contract density, labor and fleet burden, acquisitions, debt, and dilution. It
does not pool Brink's with ordinary security, correctional, parcel, or software
companies.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brink's | Route-and-workflow cash after cash logistics, ATM/DRS service delivery, labor, fleet, insurance, technology, customer implementation, acquisitions, debt, and dilution | Vehicles, facilities, security technology, ATM deployment, software, route density, labor productivity, integration spending, working capital, and maintenance capex | Cash-volume decline, contract loss, security incident, labor inflation, acquisition integration, NCR Atleos funding, FX, leverage, or claims | AMS/DRS growth and recurring contracts rise while cash conversion, margin, route productivity, capex, integration cost, or diluted residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was approximately `$5.261B`, operating cash flow about `$640M`,
  free cash flow about `$436M`, and net debt leverage about `2.7x` adjusted
  EBITDA.
- Q1 2026 revenue was approximately `$1.375B`, adjusted EBITDA `$237.5M`, and
  operating cash flow increased by about `$89M` year over year.
- Q2 2026 revenue was approximately `$1.392B`, adjusted EBITDA `$257.2M`, and
  trailing-twelve-month free cash flow was approximately `$468M`.
- Management reported the `14th` consecutive quarter of mid-teens-or-better
  AMS/DRS organic growth and expected the NCR Atleos acquisition in early Q1
  2027, subject to closing conditions.
- The cash-logistics, ATM managed-services, and digital-retail solutions
  businesses have different collection, labor, capex, security, and integration
  burdens; consolidated adjusted EBITDA cannot stand in for each route's owner
  cash.

## Q2 2026 filed cash-quality bridge

The latest 10-Q makes the cash denominator materially more constrained than the
headline TTM FCF figure. For the six months ended June 30, 2026, GAAP operating
cash flow was `$65.2M` versus `$143.8M` in the prior-year period, while cash paid
for capital expenditures was `$74.9M`. Investing cash use was `$64.2M`; the
capital program was primarily cash devices, information technology, and armored
vehicles. Financing leases added `$42.9M` of new lease financing during the
first half, so cash capex alone understates the physical reinvestment burden.

Management's filed reconciliation produced only `$32.0M` of H1 free cash flow
before dividends. That measure excludes the effects of restricted customer cash
and cash received and processed in certain secure cash-management operations,
then includes property-and-equipment cash flows and excludes NCR Atleos
transaction costs. The filing attributes the year-over-year GAAP operating-cash
decline primarily to a `$66.3M` unfavorable change in restricted cash held for
customers and lower operating profit, partly offset by a `$40.5M` increase in
certain customer obligations. This is a useful cash-perimeter control, not
proof that the H1 `$32.0M` measure is normalized common-owner cash.

The quarter-end liquidity bridge is also bounded: total debt was `$4.2423B`,
cash and equivalents were `$1.6582B`, `$145.8M` was held by Cash Management
Services operations and excluded from general corporate cash, and reported net
debt was `$2.7299B`. Brink's reported `$500M` of revolver availability. H1
shareholder distributions included `$21.0M` of dividends and `$30.2M` of share
repurchases. These are residual uses, not evidence that the operating system
generated an equivalent amount of distributable cash after all claims.

The NCR Atleos branch is a separate funding and dilution test. Brink's 10-Q
estimates GAAP purchase-price consideration at approximately `$4B`, expects
closing in Q1 2027 subject to regulatory and customary conditions, and had
incurred `$29.2M` of transaction costs by June 30. The same filing reports
`$75.3M` of H1 NCR acquisition and transformation costs in the adjusted-cost
bridge, while management targets approximately `$200M` of run-rate synergies by
year three. The purchase-price estimate, expected synergies, and transaction
cost add-backs remain forecast or non-GAAP objects until the merger closes and
the source/use, financing, acquired cash, integration cost, and diluted-share
bridge is filed.

The current promotion result is therefore:

`Q2 filing fresh; route economics and recurring-service growth qualified;
H1 owner-cash denominator partial; NCR funding/closing/dilution open; no-ranking.`

The next evidence should be a segment/service-level collection and cost bridge,
the merger funding and closing statement, or a subsequent filing that shows
customer-cash settlement, maintenance versus growth capex, financing leases,
security claims, and post-close debt/share-count effects. Do not use TTM FCF of
`$468M` as H1 normalized owner cash merely because management reports 46%
conversion.

## Q2 service-mix and burden bridge

The 10-Q groups the business into CVM and DRS/AMS, but it does not disclose a
separate profit-and-cash statement for DRS/AMS. H1 DRS/AMS revenue was `$812.6M`
of `$2.7674B` total segment revenue, or approximately `29%`; Q2 DRS/AMS revenue
was `$420.1M` of `$1.3923B`, or approximately `30%`. H1 CVM revenue was
`$1.9548B`. DRS/AMS growth is therefore strategically important but still sits
inside a consolidated cost and working-capital perimeter.

The service definitions also show why the mix cannot be treated as software
margin. DRS/AMS includes cash forecasting, cash optimization, remote monitoring,
dispatch, transaction processing, installation, first- and second-line
maintenance, parts provisioning, fund settlement, and—in some contracts—Brink's
ownership of ATM devices. CVM includes armored transport, vaulting, cash
processing, global high-value logistics, and other security services. Both
models consume labor, physical assets, security controls, and customer-service
execution.

The H1 segment table makes the burden visible: labor and fringe costs were
`$1.0410B` across the segments, other cost-of-revenue items were `$976.8M`, and
segment SG&A was `$302.8M`, against `$446.8M` of segment operating profit. In Q2,
segment labor and fringe costs were `$522.9M` against `$233.6M` of segment
operating profit. This is not a claim that labor is avoidable; it is the
reinvestment and productivity denominator for testing whether DRS/AMS growth
actually improves route economics.

The current operating thesis is therefore narrower than “higher recurring
mix”: promote only if DRS/AMS growth is accompanied by stable or improving
revenue per vehicle/employee, service quality, collection, labor productivity,
maintenance-capital intensity, and security-loss experience. The filing lists
labor shortages/work stoppages, IT and cyber risk, service quality, insurance
coverage/claims, and safety/security loss experience as live breakers. No
service-level collection or route-productivity ledger is public in the reviewed
Q2 filing, so those gates remain open.

## Q2 collection and contract-account bridge

The reported revenue increase has not yet been joined to a clean collection
result. Customer-contract receivables increased from `$766.0M` at December 31,
2025 to `$865.7M` at June 30, 2026, a `$99.7M` increase, while H1 revenue rose
`$220.2M` or `9%`. The H1 cash-flow statement separately reports a `$106.3M`
use for the increase in accounts receivable and income-tax receivable, plus a
`$19.9M` use from lower accounts payable, income-tax payable, and accrued
liabilities. The allowance for doubtful accounts was `$20.3M` at June 30, after
`$1.8M` of provision and `$1.9M` of write-offs/recoveries during the first half.

The contract-account movements do not provide a large advance-payment offset:
contract assets declined from `$27.6M` to `$26.5M`, contract liabilities
declined from `$15.0M` to `$14.0M`, and only `$11.6M` of H1 revenue came from the
opening contract-liability balance. Brink's capitalized `$15.1M` of net costs to
obtain contracts at June 30, up from `$14.3M`. Most contracts are invoiced for
units of service delivered; the company elects not to disclose a backlog-style
remaining-performance-obligation schedule because the future service volume is
not known at contract inception.

This creates a concrete promotion gate: DRS/AMS growth must ultimately be
matched to billed units, cash collection, allowance behavior, contract-cost
amortization, and customer-obligation settlement. The current filing supports
revenue and receivables evidence, but not a route-level aging/collection ledger
or service-level cash bridge. Therefore the collection leg remains partial and
cannot be used to promote the recurring mix into normalized owner cash.

## Valuation expectation screen — September 18, 2026

The September 18 market snapshot reports Brink's equity market capitalization
of approximately `$4.404B` at a share price of `$106.12`. Using the Q2 filed
net debt of `$2.7299B` gives an indicative enterprise value of approximately
`$7.134B`. Against management's reported trailing-twelve-month FCF of `$468M`,
that is approximately `15.2x` EV/TTM reported FCF; equity value divided by the
same reported FCF is approximately `9.4x`.

This is an expectation screen, not a valuation conclusion. The denominator is
management's non-GAAP TTM FCF with a `46%` conversion statistic, while the
latest filed H1 FCF before dividends was only `$32.0M` and the pending NCR
Atleos transaction introduces approximately `$4B` of estimated consideration,
financing, integration, and dilution uncertainty. The price therefore appears
to require sustained route productivity, DRS/AMS mix improvement, successful
NCR integration, and stable debt service; the valuation cannot be promoted
until the recurring cash denominator and acquisition funding bridge are
reconciled.

The screen should be refreshed with a post-close share count, financing terms,
acquired cash, realized synergies, and a normalized FCF series. It is not a
cross-sector ranking input.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile route revenue, managed-service contracts, customer retention,
   service levels, labor, fleet, insurance, and actual collection; route density
   is not a cash receipt.
2. Separate cash-and-valuables transport from AMS/DRS recurring service margin
   and test whether growth requires proportional people, vehicles, technology,
   or customer implementation spending.
3. Keep NCR Atleos consideration, financing, purchase accounting, synergies,
   integration costs, and acquired cash separate from organic conversion.
4. Test working capital, restricted cash, claims, security incidents, FX, and
   pension or other obligations before using adjusted free cash flow.
5. Treat adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, debt paydown, dividends, and buybacks
   as residual claims only after route maintenance, technology, integration, and
   dilution are funded.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what recurring AMS/DRS growth,
contract retention, route productivity, margin, reinvestment rate, acquisition
return, and cost of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test
asks whether cash-use decline, labor inflation, security claims, customer
concentration, FX, rates, and acquisition leverage can impair liquidity before
the service mix compounds.

## Promotion boundary

`cash-logistics-qualified; acquisition-and-route-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from named service contracts
and route activity to customer collection, labor and fleet costs, maintenance
capital, technology, acquisition funding, debt service, claims, and diluted
common residual. Revenue, AMS/DRS growth, adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, and
acquisition synergies remain diagnostic inputs.
