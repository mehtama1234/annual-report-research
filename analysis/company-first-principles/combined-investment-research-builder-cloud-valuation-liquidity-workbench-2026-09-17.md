# Builder cloud and inference valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This workbench moves DigitalOcean from a builder-cloud thesis into an explicit
valuation, reinvestment, liquidity, and thesis-breaker object. It keeps simple
cloud access, AI inference, committed capacity, RPO, customer concentration,
hardware, and software economics separate from the lighter software and edge
platforms already routed elsewhere.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DigitalOcean builder cloud | Builder and AI-inference cash after compute capacity, hosting, bandwidth, support, RPO conversion, customer concentration, debt, SBC, and dilution | Servers/GPU commitments, data-center capacity, bandwidth, storage, R&D, support, AI infrastructure, acquisitions, SBC replacement, and diluted shares | AI demand reversal, committed-capacity margin pressure, hyperscaler competition, larger-customer concentration, rates, and liquidity | AI ARR/RPO and revenue grow while gross margin, capacity returns, customer concentration, cash conversion, or per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$901.4M`, operating income `$157.0M`, and operating cash
  flow `$309.6M`.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$281.2M`, up `29%`, and ARR reached `$1.125B`; AI
  customer ARR reached `$234M`, with `85%` from inference and core cloud rather
  than bare metal.
- Q2 2026 RPO was `$894M`, including `$366M` expected in the next 12 months.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile ARR, RPO, bookings, revenue recognition, renewal, usage, and
   actual collection; contracted demand is not current owner cash.
2. Separate AI inference capacity and hardware commitments from the legacy
   builder-cloud cost base; test gross margin after capacity and bandwidth.
3. Test whether larger customers improve economics or create concentration and
   committed-capacity risk that changes the original simple-cloud model.
4. Carry servers, data-center commitments, support, R&D, SBC, debt, and diluted
   shares into maintenance/growth reinvestment.
5. Keep AI customer ARR and RPO conversion separate from management's growth
   narrative until cohort-level collections and margins are visible.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what AI ARR, RPO conversion, renewal,
gross margin, capacity reinvestment, growth, and cost of capital the valuation
requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether rates, AI capex demand,
hardware supply, power/capacity cost, customer liquidity, and hyperscaler price
competition preserve cash through a funding reversal.

## Promotion boundary

`builder-cloud-qualified; AI-capacity-and-RPO-normalization-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period collection, RPO conversion, capacity cost,
customer cohort economics, maintenance/growth capital, debt, SBC replacement,
and diluted common-owner residual. ARR, RPO, revenue growth, AI customer ARR,
and reported OCF remain diagnostic inputs.
