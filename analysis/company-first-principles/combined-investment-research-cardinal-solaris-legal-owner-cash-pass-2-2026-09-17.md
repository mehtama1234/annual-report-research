# Cardinal Health Solaris and legal owner-cash bridge — pass 2 — 2026-09-17

## Decision

Cardinal's `$5.174B` FY2026 operating cash flow is materially reduced by the
combined acquisition, legal, debt, distribution, concentration, and dilution
perimeter. The `$32M` post-listed-uses screen is not final owner cash, but it is
a useful falsifier of the idea that improved OCF was broadly surplus.

Status: `Solaris-and-legal-bridge-pass-2; owner-cash-open; no-ranking`.

## Waterfall and acquisition perimeter

```text
operating cash flow                         $5,174M
  - acquisitions                            $1,991M
  - property and equipment capex               $649M
  = residual after visible reinvestment      $2,534M
  - treasury-share purchases                 $1,358M
  - dividends                                  $491M
  - long-term-debt reduction                   $653M
  = residual after listed uses                  $32M
```

The screen excludes taxes, interest, leases, maintenance/growth allocation,
noncontrolling interests, retained physician units, and other senior claims.
The `$417M` opioid payment is already inside OCF and must not be deducted a
second time; it must instead be tested against remaining settlement cash.

Solaris consumed approximately `$1.9B` of acquisition cash. The Specialty
Alliance issued approximately `$500M` of common units to physicians and
management. Those units are an economic and dilution claim even when not a
current cash outflow. The acquisition-return bridge must join Solaris revenue,
segment profit, integration cost, unit compensation, call rights, working
capital, capex, and distributions.

Other FY2026 transaction/integration costs were `$23M` for ADS, `$11M` for
GIA/Urology America, and `$5M` for ION. A portfolio-level acquisition strategy
means the “one-time” label requires a persistence test.

## Legal, temporary, and concentration claims

Cardinal recorded an approximately `$200M` receivable for expected IEEPA tariff
refunds and approximately `$100M` of net operating earnings benefit after an
expected customer payback expense. This is a transfer/timing item, not durable
margin or unrestricted owner cash; the receipt and remittance must be matched.

CVS represented 28% of revenue and 22% of gross trade receivables; the five
largest customers represented 43% of revenue. That concentration makes
customer contract terms, payment timing, inventory commitments, and renewal a
direct owner-cash risk. FY2025's OptumRx working-capital unwind also means
year-over-year OCF needs a normalized vendor/customer-cycle bridge.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

1. Do not treat the `$2.534B` residual as distributable cash before debt,
   legal, tax, lease, dilution, and maintenance-capital claims.
2. Do not double-count the `$417M` opioid cash payment; require the remaining
   settlement and insurance-recovery schedule instead.
3. Separate tariff-refund receivable, customer payback, actual collection, and
   remittance from recurring segment margin.
4. Test Solaris and specialty acquisitions through post-close gross profit,
   integration cash, physician units, call rights, working capital, and cash
   distributions.
5. Reconcile repurchases and dividends to cash after acquisitions, capex, legal
   payments, debt service, and liquidity; carry `$8.886B` debt and finance leases.

## Promotion gate and thesis breaker

Promotion requires a Solaris/acquisition cohort return schedule, normalized
customer/vendor working-capital bridge, opioid cash-paid and remaining-obligation
schedule, tariff refund/remittance evidence, CVS concentration stress, and
retained-unit/diluted-share treatment.

The thesis breaker is activated if OCF and adjusted EPS improve while listed
uses consume the residual, customer concentration worsens, legal/tariff items
support earnings without durable cash, or retained specialty units absorb the
economic return. This is a filing-based falsifier, not a fraud conclusion.
