# Carnival cruise, destination, and wallet valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Carnival from current-period evidence into a company-specific
valuation, booking, deposit, onboard-spend, fleet-capital, fuel, debt, and common-
owner cash test. It does not pool cruise, airline, hotel, booking, and gaming
economics into an asset-light travel multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carnival | Collected cruise cash after ticket revenue, customer deposits, occupancy, onboard and pre-cruise spend, fuel, crew, food, port costs, fleet maintenance, new ships, debt, and dilution | Fleet maintenance and newbuilds, dry docks, destinations, technology, crew, itinerary capacity, environmental compliance, and working capital | Fuel spike, geopolitical itinerary disruption, booking cancellation, deposit reversal, occupancy/yield decline, ship outage, port cost, refinancing, or dilution | Bookings and deposits rise while net yield, onboard spend, fuel-adjusted margin, fleet returns, debt service, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Full-year 2025 revenue reached `$26.6B`, operating income `$4.5B`, net income `$2.8B`, adjusted EBITDA `$7.2B`, passenger ticket revenue `$17.419B`, and onboard/other revenue `$9.202B`.
- 2025 occupancy reached `105%`, passengers carried `13.6M`, and customer deposits ended the year at about `$7.2B`.
- Q2 2026 revenue was a record `$6.7B`, adjusted EBITDA a record `$1.6B`, and customer deposits reached an all-time high of `$9.0B`.
- Management said Q2 overcame nearly `30%` higher fuel costs through pricing, bookings, onboard sales, and cost efficiency; bookings for the remainder of 2026 were ahead at historically high prices.
- Carnival unified its dual-listed structure under Carnival Corporation Ltd. on May 7, 2026; legal-entity changes and debt/distribution claims must be kept in the same-entity residual analysis.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile bookings and deposits to sailing dates, cancellations, refunds,
   ticket revenue, occupancy, net yield, and cash release during voyages.
2. Separate ticket, onboard, pre-cruise, excursions, beverage, casino, and other
   wallet spend; higher revenue is not enough if acquisition or delivery costs rise.
3. Keep ship maintenance, newbuild commitments, dry docks, crew, fuel, ports,
   environmental obligations, debt, and required liquidity in the owner-cash base.
4. Test whether adjusted EBITDA excludes recurring ship disruption, restructuring,
   fuel, legal, foreign-exchange, or itinerary costs.
5. Reconcile customer-deposit float, debt, leases, interest, dividends, buybacks,
   corporate-structure changes, claims, and diluted shares before calling FCF
   surplus cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what occupancy, ticket yield, onboard
attachment, deposit growth, fuel burden, fleet capex, leverage, and cost of capital
the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether household
travel budgets, geopolitics, fuel, ports, labor, shipbuilding, and refinancing can
preserve liquidity through a leisure and commodity cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`carnival-cruise-destination-qualified; deposit-yield-fleet-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from bookings, deposits, sailings,
occupancy, and onboard spend to cash collections, cancellation/refund behavior,
fuel and fleet costs, required capex, debt, claims, and diluted common residual.
Revenue, bookings, deposits, occupancy, adjusted EBITDA, guidance, and buybacks
remain diagnostic inputs.
