# Real-estate services and critical-infrastructure intermediation valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves CBRE from current-period real-estate-services evidence into a company-specific valuation, brokerage, facilities, project-management, mortgage, investment-management, infrastructure, working-capital, and owner-cash test. It treats CBRE as an intermediation and services platform around property and infrastructure, not as a landlord or pure developer.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CBRE | Collected brokerage, facilities, project, mortgage, investment-management, and infrastructure-services cash after commissions, subcontractors, project delivery, client collection, working capital, acquisitions, debt, and dilution | People and technology, facilities operations, project and critical-infrastructure delivery, mortgage platform, investment-management capital, acquisitions, data and systems, and working capital | Transaction volume, interest rates, commercial property cycle, project timing, client concentration, subcontractor and labor cost, mortgage credit, infrastructure concentration, debt, and dilution | Revenue, Core EPS, EBITDA, FCF, critical-infrastructure growth, assets under management, transaction volume, or acquisitions rise while client collection, project margin, facilities delivery, mortgage credit, working capital, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue reached `$40.6B`, Core EPS `$6.38`, and FCF about `$1.7B`.
- Q1 `2026` revenue was `$10.5B`, GAAP EPS `$1.07`, and Core EPS `$1.61`; Q2 revenue was `$11.2B`, GAAP EPS `$0.69`, and Core EPS `$1.56`.
- 2026 Core EPS guidance moved from `$7.30–$7.60` in February to `$7.80–$7.90` by July 29, 2026.
- Critical infrastructure represented about `14%` of 2025 core EBITDA, up from roughly `3%` in 2021, including data-center, power, telecom, and related services.
- CBRE spans brokerage, facilities, project management, mortgage origination, investment management, and infrastructure services; revenue and EBITDA therefore combine distinct collection and capital burdens.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile transaction volume, brokerage commissions, property and facility contracts, project milestones, mortgage originations, AUM fees, client billing, subcontractors, and cash collection; revenue is not one homogeneous cash stream.
2. Separate advisory, leasing and sales brokerage, facilities, project management, mortgage, investment management, critical infrastructure, and owned or affiliated interests.
3. Test acquisitions and infrastructure expansion through purchase price, people and technology retention, project margin, client renewal, working capital, debt, and return on invested capital.
4. Keep Core EPS, FCF, AUM, transaction volume, critical-infrastructure growth, and acquisitions separate from recurring collected common-owner cash.
5. Reconcile commissions, subcontractor costs, labor, project guarantees, mortgage credit, client advances, debt, taxes, SBC, and diluted shares.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what property transactions, facilities renewal, project margin, mortgage origination, AUM growth, critical-infrastructure demand, reinvestment, and cost of equity the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether rates, commercial property weakness, transaction drought, client payment, labor and subcontractor inflation, mortgage credit, project delays, and leverage preserve cash conversion.

## Promotion boundary

`cbre-real-estate-services-qualified; project-collection-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from service and client cohorts to commission or fee collection, project delivery, facilities cost, mortgage credit, AUM economics, working capital, debt service, taxes, and diluted common residual. Revenue, Core EPS, FCF, critical-infrastructure growth, AUM, transaction volume, and acquisitions remain diagnostic inputs.
