# Cement, carbon, and urbanization valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves CEMEX from current-period infrastructure evidence into a company-specific valuation, reinvestment, carbon, portfolio, and owner-cash test. It treats cement and aggregates as local heavy-material networks with kiln energy, logistics, public-program, housing, and industrial-cycle exposure.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CEMEX | Collected cement, ready-mix, aggregates, and urbanization cash after quarry/kiln renewal, fuel and freight, carbon allowances, working capital, portfolio changes, debt, leases, pensions, and dilution | Kilns, quarries, aggregates reserves, terminals, trucks, maintenance and growth capex, decarbonization, working capital, and local-network projects | Housing affordability, public-program timing, infrastructure/AI industrial demand, energy/freight, carbon policy, weather, geopolitics, leverage, impairments, and FX | EBITDA and pricing rise while volumes, cost pass-through, kiln/quarry renewal, carbon obligations, U.S. goodwill, debt/lease burden, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$16.132B`, operating cash flow after interest and income taxes `$1.975B`, controlling-interest net income `$960M`, and contractual debt and lease obligations about `$7.163B`.
- FY2025 included `$538M` of impairment losses; goodwill was `$7.170B`, including `$5.894B` tied to the U.S. cash-generating-unit group, and a `$307M` U.S. goodwill impairment.
- Q2 2026 sales were `$4.593B`, operating EBITDA `$1.018B`, operating EBITDA margin `22.2%`, and controlling-interest net income `$347M`.
- Mexico demand was supported by self-construction, Rural Roads, social housing, and government-backed programs; U.S. demand was stronger in infrastructure and AI-related industrial construction but weaker in residential; European carbon policy supported pricing and pass-through.
- Project Cutting Edge, pricing discipline, energy/freight conditions, the Couch Aggregates investment, and portfolio reshaping are central to the current margin and return story.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate cement, ready-mix, aggregates, urbanization, and other businesses by geography, price, volume, mix, freight, and local-market scarcity; consolidated EBITDA is not one cycle.
2. Reconcile operating cash after interest and taxes to sustaining kiln/quarry/aggregate capital, decarbonization, carbon allowances, environmental obligations, working capital, and actual common-owner cash.
3. Test Project Cutting Edge savings for recurring operating improvement versus temporary volume, fuel, overhead, weather, or accounting effects.
4. Keep public-program and AI-related industrial demand separate from housing affordability, residential inventory, infrastructure timing, and customer collection.
5. Reconcile acquisitions, disposals, Couch Aggregates, goodwill, impairments, pensions, leases, debt, FX, and minority claims before promoting EBITDA or net income.
6. Treat pricing and carbon pass-through as a collection and volume question; higher stated prices do not prove higher real returns if demand, energy, logistics, or carbon obligations absorb them.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what mid-cycle cement/aggregate prices, volumes, local scarcity, energy cost, carbon pass-through, maintenance capital, decarbonization, working capital, taxes, debt, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether housing, public infrastructure, industrial policy, AI construction, energy, freight, carbon regulation, and sovereign/FX shocks can weaken cash while heavy plants and quarries remain capital-intensive and locally fixed.

## Promotion boundary

`cemex-cement-qualified; price-volume-carbon-capex-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from price, volume, and mix to customer collection, kiln/quarry renewal, carbon and environmental obligations, working capital, portfolio cash, debt/leases, impairments, and diluted common residual. Sales, operating EBITDA, margin, operating cash flow, pricing, public-program demand, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
