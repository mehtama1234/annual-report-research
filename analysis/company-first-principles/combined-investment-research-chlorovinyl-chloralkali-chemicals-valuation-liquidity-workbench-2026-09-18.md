# Chlorovinyl and chlor-alkali chemicals valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Westlake from chemical-spread and building-products
evidence into a company-specific valuation and liquidity object. It separates
HIP downstream pipe/fittings/siding economics from PEM chlorovinyl,
polyethylene, epoxy, and chlor-alkali cycles, plant closures, environmental
claims, and debt.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Westlake | HIP downstream cash plus mid-cycle PEM cash after feedstock/energy, utilization, closures, sustaining capex, working capital, environmental claims, debt, acquisitions, and dilution | Chlor-alkali/vinyl plants, feedstock, energy, maintenance and growth capex, closure/remediation, inventory, receivables, ACI acquisition, environmental and legal claims, debt, and common capital | PVC/caustic/ethylene spread weakness, plant under-utilization, housing decline, closure cost, environmental/legal cash, inventory build, debt, or refinancing | PEM recovery, HIP volume, or EBITDA rise while mid-cycle spread, utilization, sustaining capital, closure savings, debt, or diluted common cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$11.170B`, OCF `$465M`, PP&E spending `$995M`, and OCF less PP&E was negative `$530M`.
- FY2025 HIP operating income was about `$603M` while PEM lost about `$724M`; consolidated sales conceal unlike downstream and commodity exposures.
- Cash and equivalents were about `$2.724B` against debt of roughly `$5.584B`; inventory was `$1.653B` and receivables `$1.229B`.
- Q4 2025 included about `$393M` of charges for North American chlorovinyl/styrene shutdowns and `$102M` connected with the Pernis shutdown.
- Management’s `$600M` PEM improvement plan and the ACI acquisition require price, utilization, fixed-cost, integration, working-capital, and cash-return attribution.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate HIP from PEM price, volume, mix, utilization, feedstock, energy, co-products, freight, and fixed costs; consolidated EBITDA is not a common denominator.
2. Reconcile OCF to sustaining versus growth capex, inventory, receivables, closure/remediation cash, litigation, environmental claims, debt, and dividends.
3. Test whether the PEM improvement plan comes from durable unit cost/utilization or temporary spreads, disruption, gas prices, price timing, and accounting normalization.
4. Track shutdown savings against closure cash, environmental and asset-retirement obligations, stranded overhead, freight, customer qualification, and lost volume.
5. Test ACI acquisition return after purchase price, integration, inventory, capex, and incremental cash.
6. Treat refinancing and debt reduction as liquidity evidence, not proof of improved economic return.
7. Treat EBITDA, favorable spreads, reported OCF, and dividends as diagnostic until mid-cycle owner cash after plant renewal and claims is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what HIP volume/margin, PEM mid-cycle
spread, utilization, feedstock, energy, sustaining capex, closure savings,
working capital, debt, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn
Alden-style stress test asks whether housing, water infrastructure, energy,
trade, rates, and industrial demand can support the integrated asset base while
environmental and financing claims remain liquid.

## Promotion boundary

`chlorovinyl-chloralkali-qualified; spread-capital-and-common-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from HIP/PEM price and volume
to utilization, feedstock, energy, inventory, receivables, closure/remediation,
sustaining capital, ACI return, debt, legal claims, and diluted common
residual. EBITDA, spreads, OCF, improvement plans, and dividends remain
diagnostic inputs.
