# Choice Hotels franchise and conversion valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Choice Hotels from current-period evidence into a company-
specific valuation, franchise-fee, conversion, extended-stay, owner-economics,
pipeline, lending, debt, and common-owner cash test. It does not pool Choice's
practical lodging with owned resorts, premium hotel networks, or casino assets.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Choice Hotels | Collected franchise and management-fee cash after franchisee health, RevPAR, conversions, room openings, loyalty, technology, owner support, lending, debt, and dilution | Brand systems, reservations, loyalty, technology, quality assurance, conversion support, international growth, franchisee financing, and working capital | Franchisee distress, RevPAR decline, conversion slowdown, hotel closures, owner financing, inbound demand, interest rates, debt, or dilution | Rooms and agreements rise while franchisee collections, RevPAR, fee quality, pipeline conversion, lending exposure, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 adjusted EBITDA was `$625.6M`, operating cash flow `$270.4M`, year-end liquidity `$571.4M`, and the global pipeline exceeded `77,800` rooms.
- FY2025 international net rooms grew `12.5%`, U.S. extended-stay net rooms `11.7%`, and `97%` of the pipeline was concentrated in extended-stay, midscale, and upscale brands.
- Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA was `$175M`; franchise and management fees rose `6%` to `$188M`; global net rooms grew `2.6%`; U.S. RevPAR rose `1.3%`.
- Q2 U.S. room openings rose `27%`, global room openings `16%`, and U.S. franchise agreements awarded `30%`, representing about `9,400` new U.S. rooms.
- Management emphasized lower capital outlays for hotel development and lending, making franchisee health and fee collection more important than room-count growth alone.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile rooms and franchise agreements to openings, closures, RevPAR, fees,
   royalties, incentive payments, owner collections, and franchisee health.
2. Separate extended stay, midscale, upscale, international, conversions, and
   legacy brands by fee rate, retention, pipeline quality, and capital burden.
3. Keep reservations, loyalty, technology, quality support, conversion assistance,
   franchisee lending, guarantees, debt, and working capital in the denominator.
4. Test whether agreements awarded and pipeline rooms become open, paying, healthy
   properties rather than remaining a forward-sales metric.
5. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA, franchisee financing, asset sales, restructuring,
   debt, interest, buybacks, dividends, SBC, and diluted shares before treating
   fee growth or capital returns as owner cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what rooms, RevPAR, fee rate, conversion
rate, franchisee retention, pipeline opening, capital intensity, leverage, and cost
of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether
travel budgets, owner financing, labor, interest rates, construction costs, and
international demand can preserve liquidity across a lodging cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`choice-hotels-franchise-conversion-qualified; franchisee-pipeline-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from rooms and agreements to
openings, RevPAR, franchisee collections, owner health, required brand investment,
lending exposure, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Rooms, pipeline,
agreements awarded, RevPAR, adjusted EBITDA, guidance, and buybacks remain
diagnostic inputs.
