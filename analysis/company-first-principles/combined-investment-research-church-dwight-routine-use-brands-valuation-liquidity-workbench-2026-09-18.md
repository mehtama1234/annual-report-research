# Church & Dwight routine-use brands and portfolio valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Church & Dwight from a cleaning-products packet into a company-specific valuation object. It separates household, personal-care, wellness, eCommerce, retailer sell-through, portfolio pruning, tuck-in acquisitions, input costs, inventory, working capital, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not treat organic sales growth, eCommerce growth, adjusted EPS, cash from operations, guidance, or buybacks as normalized owner cash.

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net sales were about `$6.203B`, organic sales growth `0.7%`, adjusted EPS `$3.53`, and operating cash flow about `$1.215B`.
- Q2 2026 net sales increased `1.6%` to about `$1.530B`, organic sales growth was `5.8%`, adjusted gross margin `45.4%`, and first-half operating cash flow about `$461.6M`.
- Q2 eCommerce grew `22.7%` and online sales reached `25.5%` of total consumer sales; digital mix is a channel and acquisition-cost question, not automatically a margin improvement.
- The company exited VMS, FLAWLESS, SPINBRUSH, and WATERPIK showerheads during 2025 while emphasizing THERABREATH, HERO, TOUCHLAND, ARM & HAMMER, and other growth brands.
- Management raised 2026 guidance to reported sales flat to `+1%`, organic growth `4%`–`5%`, adjusted gross-margin expansion `100`–`120` bps, adjusted EPS growth `6%`–`8%`, and operating cash flow about `$1.175B`.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile shipped sales, retailer sell-through, eCommerce orders, returns, promotions, trade spend, inventory, and collections; reported growth is not consumer cash until channel inventory clears.
2. Separate core brands from newer acquisitions and test advertising, innovation, distribution, repeat purchase, customer-acquisition cost, and integration spend.
3. Keep portfolio exits, impairment, stranded manufacturing or marketing costs, taxes, and transaction proceeds separate from ongoing brand economics.
4. Test gross-margin guidance against soda ash, resin, packaging, freight, tariffs, mix, promotional intensity, and retailer bargaining power.
5. Reconcile operating cash flow, inventory and payables, acquisitions, debt, dividends, SBC, share count, and repurchases before accepting adjusted EPS or capital returns as owner cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what organic growth, price/mix, repeat purchase, eCommerce conversion, brand-support rate, acquisition return, reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether households trade down, retailers destock, input and tariff costs compress margins, and digital customer-acquisition or acquisition funding consumes liquidity before routine-use brand strength becomes durable common-owner cash.

## Promotion boundary

`church-dwight-routine-use-qualified; sellthrough-acquisition-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from brand demand to retailer/eCommerce collection, sell-through, inventory, trade spend, input-cost settlement, required innovation and plant spend, acquired-cohort return, portfolio-exit effects, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Organic growth, online mix, adjusted EPS, OCF, guidance, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
