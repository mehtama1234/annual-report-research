# Route-based workplace services and UniFirst valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Cintas from current-period service-density evidence into a company-specific valuation, reinvestment, acquisition, liquidity, and owner-cash test. It treats Cintas as an outsourced workplace-services network spanning uniforms, facility services, first aid and safety, and fire protection—not as an apparel retailer.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cintas | Recurring route-service cash after garment ownership and replacement, plant/route labor, customer retention, facility and safety compliance, UniFirst acquisition funding/integration, debt, taxes, and dilution | Uniform inventory and replacement, processing plants, route fleet, field labor, first-aid/fire equipment, technology, customer acquisition, and acquisition integration | Commercial occupancy/employment, labor and textile costs, customer churn, route density, fire/safety claims, UniFirst closing/integration, debt refinancing, and dilution | Revenue, route growth, margin, free cash flow, and acquisition EPS accretion rise while retention, garment replacement, labor/fleet burden, UniFirst integration, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$10.340B`, operating income `$2.360B`, net income `$1.812B`, operating cash flow `$2.166B`, and free cash flow `$1.757B`.
- Roughly `95%` of revenue comes from route servicing at customer locations, making route density, retention, plant throughput, and field execution central to the model.
- FY2026 Q4 revenue was `$2.91B`, operating income `$673.0M`, net income `$511.0M`, and diluted EPS `$1.26`; management introduced FY2027 revenue guidance of `$12.10B`–`$12.25B` and adjusted diluted EPS guidance of `$5.36`–`$5.50`.
- FY2026 Q3 included the announced UniFirst acquisition question, while management highlighted record revenues, all-time-high gross margins in route-based businesses, and continued value creation from the combination.
- The filing packet covers uniforms, facility services, first aid and safety, and fire protection, with technology investments, route productivity, cross-sell, recurring workplace relationships, and continuing capital returns.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile reported route revenue to customer retention, service frequency, garment ownership and replacement, plant processing, route labor, fleet cost, and actual collection; recurring revenue is not automatically recurring owner cash.
2. Separate uniform rental/service density from one-time product sales, facility-service labor, first-aid replenishment, and fire-protection installation or inspection work.
3. Test the UniFirst transaction through purchase price, closing conditions, financing, acquired route retention, plant overlap, integration cost, synergies, debt, and diluted-share effects; EPS accretion is not acquisition return.
4. Keep free cash flow separate from working-capital timing, garment inventory investment, route-fleet replacement, facility capex, restructuring, and acquisition cash.
5. Test labor availability, wage recovery, textile costs, customer-site activity, safety claims, insurance, and regulatory compliance as recurring operating burdens rather than exceptional items.
6. Treat dividends, repurchases, and adjusted EPS as residual claims requiring maintenance-capital, acquisition-funding, debt, and diluted-common validation.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what route growth, retention, price, cross-sell, plant utilization, labor productivity, maintenance reinvestment, UniFirst integration return, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether commercial activity, employment density, labor shortages, textile inflation, customer consolidation, safety obligations, and acquisition financing can weaken cash conversion while the route and plant network still requires service.

## Promotion boundary

`cintas-workplace-services-qualified; route-retention-unifirst-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from route revenue and customer retention to collection, garment/fleet/plant reinvestment, labor and claims, UniFirst acquisition cash and integration, debt, tax, and diluted common residual. Revenue, adjusted EPS, margin, operating cash flow, free cash flow, and acquisition synergies remain diagnostic inputs.
