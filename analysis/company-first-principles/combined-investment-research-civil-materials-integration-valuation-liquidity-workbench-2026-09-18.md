# Civil construction and materials-integration valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Granite Construction from annual-report evidence into a company-specific valuation, project-conversion, reinvestment, liquidity, and diluted common-owner residual test. It keeps Granite's civil execution, aggregates/asphalt/materials, CAP conversion, joint ventures, and convertible-debt perimeter separate from specialty contractors and design/program managers.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Granite Construction | Collected civil-project and materials cash after estimate risk, contract assets, retainage, equipment/plant capital, acquisitions, JV/NCI claims, convertible debt, and dilution | Equipment, quarries/plants, asphalt/aggregates, field labor, contract-asset funding, joint ventures, maintenance capex, acquisitions, and working capital | Project delay or loss, contract-asset reversal, receivable/retainage lag, weather/labor inflation, public funding pause, acquisition leverage, conversion dilution, or partner claims | CAP and adjusted EBITDA grow while starts, final margins, collection, acquisition return, or cash per diluted share deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was about `$4.4B`, CAP `$7.0B`, OCF `$468.9M`, PP&E purchases `$138.3M`, recurring-capital residual about `$330.6M`, acquisitions `$777.5M`, and stock compensation `$39.2M`.
- Q2 FY2026 CAP reached `$7.4B`; YTD OCF was about `$142M`, adjusted EBITDA about `$186M`, and reported GAAP loss was distorted by convertible-debt transactions.
- FY2025 OCF included a `$122.0M` source from contract assets, while receivables used `$38.7M`, payables used `$20.4M`, and JV contributions used `$9.2M`. Project progression and billing timing are therefore part of cash quality.
- Granite controls civil capability, materials access, project selection, field labor, equipment, and agency relationships; customers control funding, scope, change orders, payment, and timing.
- FY2025 acquisition cash exceeded twice the recurring-capital residual. Acquired CAP and adjusted EBITDA are not organic owner cash until integration, partner claims, debt, and dilution are charged.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Split assigned and awarded CAP from starts, executed work, revenue, billing, collection, and final margin; CAP is visibility, not cash.
2. Reconcile percentage-of-completion estimates, contract assets, retainage, receivables, claims, change orders, loss provisions, and unfavorable estimate changes by project cohort.
3. Test materials integration through plant/quarry utilization, pricing, project pull-through, environmental obligations, and maintenance capital.
4. Attribute acquisitions to revenue, margin, working capital, integration, goodwill, debt, partner/NCI distributions, and diluted shares.
5. Reconcile convertible debt, capped calls, warrants, cash interest, principal, settlement, and dilution; adjusted earnings cannot erase the equity claim.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what CAP conversion, project margin, materials contribution, acquisition return, maintenance capital, working capital, and cost of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether public budgets, federal funding, rates, materials prices, weather, labor, and customer financing preserve starts and collections.

## Promotion boundary

`civil-materials-integration-qualified; CAP-contract-collection-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from CAP to starts, measured progress, billing, collection, final project margin, materials return, acquisition cohort cash, JV/NCI claims, convertible settlement, debt, and diluted common residual. CAP, adjusted EBITDA, OCF, recurring-capital residual, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
