# Paperboard packaging and mill-cycle valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Clearwater Paper from current-period materials evidence into a company-specific valuation, paperboard price, volume, mill-outage, customer, restructuring, debt, and owner-cash test. It treats Clearwater as a focused SBS paperboard packaging operator after the tissue divestiture, not as a generic paper company.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clearwater Paper | Collected paperboard and folding-carton cash after fiber, energy, maintenance outages, mill conversion, customer settlement, restructuring, working capital, debt, and dilution | Mill maintenance and outage spending, fiber and energy, reliability, product launches, conversion and packaging support, restructuring, environmental compliance, inventory, and capex | SBS pricing and operating rates, oversupply and imports, new capacity, outage execution, weather, customer demand, energy/fiber costs, leverage, refinancing, and dilution | Volumes, price, adjusted EBITDA, cost reductions, buybacks, or guidance rise while mill reliability, realized price, maintenance burden, customer collection, liquidity, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 continuing-operations sales were `$1.6B`, adjusted EBITDA `$107M`, and net loss `$53M`, including a `$48M` non-cash goodwill impairment.
- FY2025 volumes were `1,236,114` tons; average net selling price was `$1,167` per ton versus `$1,210` in the prior year.
- Q2 `2026` sales were `$375M`, volumes `328,722` tons, average net selling price `$1,077` per ton, adjusted EBITDA `($8M)`, liquidity `$445M`, and net leverage `3.5x`.
- Q1 `2026` sales were `$360M`, volumes `302,918` tons, average net selling price `$1,101` per ton, adjusted EBITDA `$2M`, liquidity `$407M`, and net leverage `2.9x`.
- Q2 included the Lewiston outage completed on time and on target at `$22M` direct cost; management cited an oversupplied SBS market, `83%` Q4 2025 operating rates, and `500K` tons of new North American capacity added in 2025.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile tons shipped, realized price, customer mix, paperboard grade, inventory, receivables, mill downtime, and cash collection; volume growth is not margin recovery.
2. Separate continuing operations, tissue divestiture, SBS, folding carton, Augusta, Lewiston, Cypress Bend, and product-launch cohorts; asset and restructuring effects can distort comparisons.
3. Test maintenance outages, fixed-cost reductions, restructuring, and capex through reliability, cost recurrence, service levels, working capital, and return on invested capital.
4. Keep adjusted EBITDA, cost savings, guidance, buybacks, goodwill impairment, and tax or insurance recovery separate from recurring collected owner cash.
5. Reconcile fiber, energy, labor, outage cost, imports, customer terms, debt, leverage, liquidity, taxes, SBC, and diluted shares.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what realized price, operating rate, volume, cost reductions, maintenance burden, capex, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether packaging demand, oversupply, imports, new capacity, weather, energy and fiber costs, rates, and leverage preserve Clearwater's cash conversion.

## Promotion boundary

`clearwater-paperboard-qualified; price-volume-mill-cycle-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from mill, grade, customer, and product cohorts to shipment, realized price, collection, outage and maintenance cash, restructuring, debt service, taxes, and diluted common residual. Volumes, pricing, adjusted EBITDA, cost savings, guidance, buybacks, and impairment remain diagnostic inputs.
