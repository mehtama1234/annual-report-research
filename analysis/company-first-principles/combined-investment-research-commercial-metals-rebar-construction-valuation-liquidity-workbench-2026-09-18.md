# Commercial Metals rebar and construction valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Commercial Metals from current-period evidence into a
company-specific valuation, scrap-spread, construction-backlog, precast-
acquisition, leverage, and common-owner cash test. It does not pool recycling,
rebar, fabrication, precast, and European steel economics into a generic steel
multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial Metals | Collected recycled-steel and construction-solutions cash after scrap, steel spreads, rebar/fabrication, precast project execution, acquisitions, maintenance/growth capex, debt, and dilution | Recycling yards, EAF mills, fabrication, precast plants, project backlog, acquisitions, equipment, environmental compliance, and working capital | Scrap/steel spread compression, import pressure, weather, project delay, precast integration, CBAM/trade policy, leverage, or European losses | Steel margins and backlog rise while volume, scrap spread, precast returns, project conversion, leverage, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Fiscal 2025 revenue was `$7.798B`, net earnings `$84.7M`, and total debt plus finance-lease obligations `$1.354B`.
- Q3 2026 net sales were `$2.483B`, net earnings `$173.0M`, adjusted earnings `$193.0M`, and core EBITDA `$353.6M`.
- Q3 North America Steel Group metal margins improved `$111` per ton year over year; average selling prices rose `$130` per ton while scrap rose `$19` per ton.
- Construction Solutions Group precast acquisitions contributed `$52.9M` in Q3; management said more than `$2.5B` had been deployed into the precast growth platform.
- By May 31, 2026, cash and short-term investments were `$559.8M`, while total debt plus finance leases were `$3.400B`; management was targeting net leverage below `2x` ahead of mid-2027.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile tons, rebar, fabrication, precast, and scrap purchases to selling
   prices, metal margins, project acceptance, and customer collections.
2. Separate North America Steel, Construction Solutions, and Europe Steel; the
   segments have different spreads, policy exposure, backlog, and capital burdens.
3. Keep recycling yards, EAF mills, precast plants, equipment, environmental
   compliance, project working capital, acquisitions, and debt service in the
   owner-cash denominator.
4. Test whether precast acquisitions create incremental collected cash after
   purchase accounting, integration, backlog conversion, and maintenance capex.
5. Reconcile adjusted earnings, purchase-accounting charges, Europe/CBAM effects,
   claims, interest, leverage, buybacks, SBC, and diluted shares before promoting
   core EBITDA or FCF.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what scrap spread, steel price, rebar
volume, precast growth, backlog conversion, capex, acquisition return, leverage,
and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks
whether construction demand, public infrastructure, imports, trade policy, scrap
availability, energy, weather, and refinancing can preserve liquidity through a
steel cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`commercial-metals-rebar-construction-qualified; scrap-spread-acquisition-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from scrap, steel, rebar,
fabrication, and precast activity to project acceptance, collections, required
capex, acquisition return, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue,
steel margins, adjusted EBITDA, guidance, backlog, and buybacks remain diagnostic.
