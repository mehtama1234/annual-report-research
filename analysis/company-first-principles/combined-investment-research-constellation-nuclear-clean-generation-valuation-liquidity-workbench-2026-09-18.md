# Nuclear and clean-generation platform valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Constellation Energy from current-period generation evidence into a company-specific valuation, fleet, PPA, merchant, nuclear-license, restart, acquisition, data-center, and owner-cash test. It treats Constellation as a scarce reliable-generation platform, not as a conventional regulated wires utility or simple merchant-power proxy.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Constellation Energy | Collected clean-generation and capacity cash after nuclear operations, fuel, maintenance, licensing, PPAs, merchant exposure, Calpine integration, restarts, capex, debt, dividends, and dilution | Nuclear maintenance and life extension, Crane restart, generation projects, fuel, safety and regulatory compliance, PPAs, data-center interconnection, acquisitions, and working capital | Power prices and hedges, nuclear outage or license, restart execution, PPA counterparty, Calpine integration, regulatory remedies, data-center load, debt, rates, and dilution | Adjusted operating earnings, generation, PPAs, capacity, data-center agreements, dividends, or buybacks rise while fleet availability, maintenance, restart capex, PPA collection, regulatory obligations, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Constellation describes a roughly `55 GW` fleet spanning nuclear, gas, oil, geothermal, hydro, wind, and solar, capable of powering the equivalent of `27M` homes.
- Q2 `2026` GAAP net income was `$1.42` per share and adjusted operating earnings `$2.55` per share; full-year guidance rose to `$11.50–$12.50` adjusted operating earnings per share.
- Q2 highlighted Crane Clean Energy Center progress, `920 MW` of additional long-term clean PPAs, the planned divestiture of the `606 MW` Brazos Valley Energy Center, and license-renewal filings for two New York nuclear units.
- Q1 `2026` adjusted operating earnings were `$2.74` per share; projects included the `105 MW` Pastoria Solar Project, `460 MW` Pin Oak Creek Energy Center, and a signed `380 MW` Freestone data-center agreement with another `380 MW` Phase 2 path.
- The Calpine acquisition, DOE loan guarantee of up to `$1B` for the Crane restart, Clinton and Dresden license extensions, and agreements with Microsoft, Meta, and CyrusOne are strategic inputs—not proof of collected common-owner cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile generation, capacity, PPAs, customer billing, merchant sales, hedge settlements, fuel, outage, maintenance, and cash collection; megawatts and contracted capacity are not cash.
2. Separate nuclear, gas, renewable, merchant, PPA, Calpine, Crane, Freestone, and other project cohorts; ownership, regulation, cost, and counterparty risk differ.
3. Test Calpine, Crane, licenses, data-center co-location, and generation projects through purchase price, integration, construction, commissioning, availability, PPA collection, debt, and return on invested capital.
4. Keep adjusted earnings, PPAs, capacity, data-center agreements, dividends, and buybacks separate from recurring collected common-owner cash.
5. Reconcile fuel and maintenance, nuclear safety, decommissioning, environmental and regulatory obligations, debt, interest, taxes, NCI, SBC, and diluted shares.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what fleet availability, PPA pricing, capacity value, restart probability, license life, data-center load, maintenance capex, leverage, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether power prices, outage risk, regulation, rates, counterparty credit, construction, nuclear policy, and debt preserve Constellation's cash conversion.

## Promotion boundary

`constellation-nuclear-generation-qualified; fleet-ppa-restart-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from named fleet and customer cohorts to generation, billing, PPA or hedge settlement, maintenance and restart cash, license and safety obligations, debt service, taxes, dividends, and diluted common residual. Adjusted earnings, generation, PPAs, capacity, data-center agreements, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
