# Consumer goods and household upkeep Q2 2026 cash-quality refresh

Research date: 2026-09-17

## Finding

The latest interim filings turn the household value-and-upkeep theme into a
current cash-quality comparison. Burlington's off-price growth is accompanied
by tariff-refund support and heavy supply-chain/store investment. Ollie's
generates positive operating cash, but inventory timing and new-store capital
are central to the bargain model. Lowe's has much larger operating cash, yet
its capital-return and debt obligations sit alongside store, service, and
acquisition economics.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

Burlington's H1 operating cash improved to $334.6M, but management attributes
part of the increase to a $55.5M tariff-refund benefit. Investing cash used
$532.4M for property and equipment and $5.1M for lease acquisition costs,
while 91 stores opened. The mechanical OCF-less-property-and-lease screen is
therefore negative, and store growth cannot be treated as free cash without
testing productivity, merchandise availability, markdowns, shrink, leases,
and future supply-chain spending.

Ollie's generated $153.6M of H1 operating cash and spent $68.8M on capex. The
filing explicitly says operating cash was affected by inventory increases from
new-store growth and the timing of merchandise payments. It also reports $137.3M
of repurchases and $4.9M of employee-tax payments, while only $187.5M of cash
and short-term investments and $88.4M of revolver capacity were available at
the period end. The closeout model must therefore pass an inventory-aging,
markdown, store-payback, lease, and liquidity test before owner-cash promotion.

Lowe's H1 OCF of $7.009B exceeded $1.063B of capex, but the company also paid
$1.346B of dividends, $367M of repurchases, and approximately $2.4B of bond
maturities. Its capex supports customer-service capability, existing stores,
and expansion, so the $5.946B mechanical OCF-less-capex figure is a reported
liquidity diagnostic rather than normalized common-owner cash. The FY2026
capex plan is approximately $2.5B, and Q2 adjusted EPS included both acquisition
expenses and a tariff-refund benefit; those items require separate treatment.

## Owner-cash and valuation implication

- **Burlington:** value requires sustainable comp sales, traffic/ticket/mix,
  merchandise turns, markdowns, store payback, maintenance versus growth
  capital, leases, debt, and dilution after tariff effects normalize.
- **Ollie's:** value requires closeout supply, inventory quality, store-level
  payback, distribution capacity, working capital, leases, debt, and share
  count after repurchases.
- **Lowe's:** value requires Pro and home-services conversion, inventory and
  delivery burden, acquisition integration, maintenance versus strategic
  capex, debt maturities, dividends, repurchases, and dilution.

Damodaran-style valuation should make the price pay explicitly for traffic,
ticket, margin, store growth, reinvestment, and competitive duration. Lyn
Alden-style stress should test real wages, housing turnover, rates, household
credit, freight and materials inflation, tariffs, inventory liquidity, and
refinancing conditions. Loyalty, comps, adjusted EPS, and OCF-less-capex are
inputs—not proof of owner cash.

## Promotion consequence

This is a material current-period upgrade to the consumer-goods household-
identity lane. It identifies an important distinction: value retail can show
strong operating cash while inventory and new-store investment absorb cash;
home upkeep can show large cash generation while debt maturities and service
capital compete for it. The cohort remains
`household-value-qualified; owner-cash-open; no-ranking`.
