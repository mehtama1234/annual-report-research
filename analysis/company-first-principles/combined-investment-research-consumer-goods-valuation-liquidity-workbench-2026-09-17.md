# Consumer goods valuation and liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This workbench carries the Q2 2026 consumer-goods refresh through
store-level valuation, reinvestment, inventory/liquidity stress, and filing-
based thesis breakers. Burlington, Ollie's, and Lowe's are not interchangeable
retail denominators and are not ranked here.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Burlington | Mature-store sales, traffic/ticket/mix, merchandise margin, store payback, and common cash after supply-chain investment | Inventory, markdowns, 91 new stores, distribution, maintenance/growth capex, lease acquisition, debt, tax, and dilution | Tariff normalization, inventory aging, freight/input inflation, store ramp failure, revolver use, or refinancing | OCF remains supported by tariff/refund effects while property spending, inventory, markdowns, or new-store payback deteriorate |
| Ollie's | Closeout supply and mature-store contribution after merchandise timing, distribution, new-store payback, and common claims | Inventory funding, 686-store base, 68.8M capex, distribution, leases, repurchases, employee-tax payments, and revolver capacity | Inventory liquidation/markdown risk, supplier timing reversal, consumer trade-down, store-build cash, or limited liquidity | OCF and repurchases rise while inventory grows, merchandise payments reverse, or cash/revolver capacity cannot fund store rollout |
| Lowe's | Mature-store and Pro/home-services cash after service capital, inventory, acquisition integration, debt, and shareholder claims | Stores, delivery/technology/service capital, inventory, acquisitions, dividends, repurchases, bond maturities, leases, and dilution | Housing/credit slowdown, rates, refinancing wall, service demand weakness, inventory drag, or tariff/material inflation | OCF remains high while debt maturities, dividends, repurchases, and strategic capex consume the residual or comps/service economics weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- Burlington reported H1 OCF of `$334.6M`, `$532.4M` of property spending,
  `$5.1M` of lease acquisition costs, 91 stores opened, and a `$55.5M`
  tariff-refund benefit included in OCF. The mechanical OCF-less-property
  screen is negative before assigning maintenance versus growth.
- Ollie's reported H1 OCF of `$153.6M`, `$68.8M` capex, `$137.3M` repurchases,
  `$4.9M` employee-tax payments, `$187.5M` cash and short-term investments,
  and `$88.4M` revolver capacity. Inventory and merchandise-payment timing were
  explicitly affected by new-store growth.
- Lowe's reported H1 OCF of `$7.009B`, `$1.063B` capex, `$1.346B` dividends,
  `$367M` repurchases, and approximately `$2.4B` of bond maturities paid. The
  `$5.946B` OCF-less-capex figure is therefore a liquidity diagnostic, not
  normalized common-owner cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Remove Burlington's `$55.5M` tariff-refund support from recurring cash until
   recurrence, tax treatment, and underlying merchandise economics are tested.
2. Separate Burlington's new-store property spending from maintenance and test
   store payback, inventory turns, markdowns, shrink, and lease burden.
3. Reconcile Ollie's inventory growth and merchandise-payment timing before
   interpreting OCF or repurchases as durable surplus.
4. Treat Lowe's OCF-less-capex as a screen only while bond maturities,
   dividends, repurchases, acquisitions, service capital, and dilution remain
   separate claims.
5. Do not infer household resilience from sales or comps without traffic,
   ticket, mix, markdown, inventory, service, and debt evidence.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what mature-store return, traffic,
margin, inventory turns, store growth, service productivity, and reinvestment
rate the share price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether
inventory, leases, suppliers, household credit, housing turnover, rates,
freight, tariffs, and debt maturities can be funded through a weaker consumer
regime.

The key distinction is that high OCF can coexist with growth-store spending,
inventory funding, tariff support, debt service, and capital returns. The
relevant numerator is common cash after the claims required to preserve the
store and service system—not OCF or management-defined FCF alone.

## Promotion boundary

`consumer-goods-qualified; store-and-inventory-normalization-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period mature-store economics, inventory/markdown
roll-forward, maintenance-versus-growth capital, leases/taxes, debt and
shareholder claims, and diluted common residual. Tariff refunds, sales growth,
store counts, OCF-less-capex, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
