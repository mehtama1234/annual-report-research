# Learning marketplace and human-capital platform valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Coursera from current-period education-platform evidence into a company-specific valuation, learner, enterprise, partner, acquisition, AI-product, and owner-cash test. It treats Coursera as a two-sided learning marketplace and workforce platform, not as a campus, publisher, or generic subscription software company.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coursera | Collected learner and institutional learning cash after content and university economics, platform delivery, enterprise implementation, Udemy integration, AI product investment, sales, working capital, debt, and dilution | Course and credential partner economics, platform and recommendation technology, learner acquisition, enterprise implementation, content/catalog integration, AI infrastructure, acquisitions, R&D, and working capital | Consumer churn, enterprise retention, university/partner economics, Udemy integration, AI delivery cost, refunds, customer concentration, sales efficiency, merger cash, debt, and dilution | Revenue, registered learners, paid subscribers, enterprise customers, NRR, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, synergies, or repurchases rise while learner/enterprise collection, course economics, retention, integration cost, AI delivery, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$757.5M`, gross profit `$413.4M`, gross margin `54.6%`, operating cash flow `$108.7M`, adjusted EBITDA `$63.5M`, and FCF `$78.5M`.
- Q2 `2026` revenue was `$298.6M`, gross profit `$173.4M`, gross margin `58.1%`, adjusted EBITDA `$42.7M`, and FCF `($32.6M)`; Enterprise revenue was `$140.0M`, Consumer revenue `$158.6M`, enterprise customers `12,107`, and paid subscribers `1.655M`.
- The Udemy transaction closed on `2026-05-11`; management expected at least `$85M` of annual run-rate net synergies by year-end 2026 and announced a `$100M` strategic investment in LearnVector.
- Q1 `2026` revenue was `$195.7M`, enterprise net retention `90%`, and total registered learners `205M`; Q4 `2025` revenue was `$196.9M` and full-year Consumer/Enterprise revenue was `$502.2M`/`$255.3M`.
- Q2 `2026` share repurchases were `$90.3M`, with more than `$140M` repurchased through `2026-07-28`; capital returns remain a residual claim, not proof of surplus owner cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile learners, paid subscribers, enterprise customers, NRR, course consumption, credential completion, contract billing, collection, refunds, and partner payouts; registered learners are not cash.
2. Keep Consumer, Enterprise, degree, government, university, Udemy, and AI-product cohorts separate; retention and gross economics differ by channel.
3. Test the Udemy transaction and LearnVector investment through purchase price, integration, catalog overlap, product migration, synergy cash, customer retention, working capital, and return on invested capital.
4. Separate adjusted EBITDA, FCF, non-GAAP income, synergies, deferred or contract revenue, and share repurchases from recurring collected common-owner cash.
5. Reconcile content and partner economics, cloud/AI delivery cost, sales and marketing, R&D, capitalized costs, debt, taxes, SBC, and diluted shares.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what learner and enterprise retention, catalog breadth, credential demand, AI monetization, partner margin, synergy realization, reinvestment, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether household budgets, employer training cycles, AI substitution, partner bargaining power, cloud costs, merger integration, rates, and dilution preserve Coursera's cash conversion.

## Promotion boundary

`coursera-learning-marketplace-qualified; retention-integration-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from learner and institutional cohorts to billing, collection, partner payout, course delivery, retention, Udemy integration, AI cost, debt, taxes, and diluted common residual. Registered learners, subscribers, customers, NRR, revenue, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, synergies, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
