# Crown Castle tower infrastructure valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Crown Castle from current-period tower evidence into a
company-specific valuation, portfolio-reset, financing, and common-owner cash
test. It does not pool tower rent, fiber proceeds, small-cell economics, and
other REIT models into a generic AFFO ranking.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crown Castle | Collected tower site-rental cash after carrier churn, Sprint cancellations, DISH terminations, leasing costs, maintenance capital, portfolio-reset costs, interest, dividends, debt, and dilution | Tower amendments and upgrades, tenant additions, maintenance capex, systems modernization, restructuring, fiber/small-cell separation, debt repayment, and required REIT distributions | Carrier consolidation or credit stress, DISH/Sprint churn, lower tenancy or pricing, refinancing costs, sale execution, interest rates, capex inflation, or dividend coverage | Tower-only simplification is achieved while organic billings, tenant demand, AFFO after maintenance and financing, or diluted common residual deteriorates |

## Current evidence anchors

- Full-year 2025 continuing-operations site-rental revenue was `$4.049B`, income from continuing operations was `$1.103B`, Adjusted EBITDA was `$2.863B`, and AFFO was `$1.904B`.
- Q2 2026 continuing-operations site-rental revenue was `$967M`, total net revenue `$1.008B`, Adjusted EBITDA `$675M`, and AFFO `$488M` or `$1.13` per share.
- Q2 site-rental billings were `$945M`; adjusted organic contribution was `$38M` or `3.9%`, while raw organic contribution remained negative at `($16M)` because of DISH drag.
- Management's July 2026 outlook was site-rental revenue of `$3.833B-$3.878B`, Adjusted EBITDA of `$2.665B-$2.715B`, and AFFO of `$1.950B-$2.000B`.
- The Strategic Fiber Agreement contemplated aggregate consideration of `$8.5B` for the fiber solutions and small-cell businesses, making the 2025 annual report a transition document rather than a steady-state tower baseline.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile site-rental billings to contractual escalators, tenant additions,
   amendments, cancellations, straight-line rent, collections, and carrier
   concentration. Adjusted organic contribution must not hide DISH or Sprint churn.
2. Separate the continuing tower perimeter from fiber and small-cell disposal,
   sale proceeds, closing adjustments, restructuring, stranded costs, and
   headcount savings.
3. Keep maintenance and tenant-driven capital, systems modernization, leasing
   costs, interest, debt repayment, and required REIT distributions in the cash
   denominator; AFFO is not automatically common-owner cash.
4. Test whether the `$65M` targeted annualized operating-cost savings and tower
   simplification are recurring, collected, and durable rather than transition
   presentation.
5. Reconcile non-GAAP AFFO, discontinued operations, sale accounting, debt,
   preferred or noncontrolling claims, dividends, buybacks, and diluted shares.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what tenancy, carrier capex, escalators,
organic billings, maintenance capital, interest burden, payout, and cost of capital
the tower valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether mobile
connectivity demand survives carrier consolidation, refinancing, rate pressure,
tenant distress, and portfolio-sale execution without impairing liquidity or the
common residual.

## Promotion boundary

`crown-castle-tower-infrastructure-qualified; tower-reset-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from tower sites and billings to
carrier collections, tenancy, required capital, portfolio-reset proceeds and costs,
debt service, distributions, claims, and diluted common residual. AFFO, Adjusted
EBITDA, site counts, organic contribution, sale proceeds, and dividend capacity
remain diagnostic inputs.
