# Custom electrical systems and project-conversion valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Powell Industries from custom electrical-system and
backlog evidence into a company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It
keeps engineering, switchgear, substations, milestone billing, contract assets
and liabilities, receivables, fixed-price execution, warranty, backlog mix,
facility expansion, letters of credit, and diluted common-owner cash separate
from grid components, electrical distribution, and large generation-equipment
models.

## Decision matrix

| Powell custom electrical systems | Collected project and service cash after engineering, materials, labor, contract execution, milestone settlement, warranty, facility expansion, working capital, acquisitions, and dilution | Fabrication capacity, engineering, inventory, contract assets, Jacintoport expansion, R&D, service, letters of credit, acquisitions, and common capital | Project delay or cancellation, fixed-price cost overrun, contract-asset aging, receivable stretch, milestone-liability reversal, facility underutilization, warranty claim, or end-market slowdown | Bookings, backlog, revenue, or OCF rise while project margin, contract conversion, collection, warranty, capacity return, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Nine-month FY2026 revenue was `$859.5M`, bookings `$1.9B` (up `112%`), and
  backlog `$2.4B` (up `73%`). Backlog visibility remains an execution claim,
  not completed revenue or cash.
- Nine-month operating cash flow was `$195.0M` against `$10.4M` of capex, but
  contract assets and liabilities supplied `$188.8M` of cash while receivables
  used `$146.4M`. The cash bridge is therefore milestone- and project-stage
  sensitive.
- Net contract liabilities reached `$350.4M` at June 30, 2026, versus `$161.3M`
  at the prior fiscal year-end. This supports liquidity while creating future
  delivery obligations.
- Backlog was diversified across commercial/industrial (`40%`), oil and gas
  excluding petrochemical (`24%`), and electric utility (`24%`). Petrochemical
  revenue declined `39%` after completion of a large prior order.
- Cash and short-term investments were `$633.6M`, with no U.S. revolver
  borrowings; letters of credit outstanding were `$103.9M`. Liquidity is strong,
  but letters of credit and international cash affect practical availability.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile bookings and backlog to cancellations, scope reductions, revenue,
   contract assets, billed receivables, collections, acceptance, and final
   project margin. Backlog is not cash.
2. Treat early milestone billings and contract liabilities as customer-funded
   future delivery obligations. Test whether the associated projects convert at
   the stated margin without claims or loss provisions.
3. Track fixed-price estimates, engineering hours, procurement cost, labor,
   warranty, rework, customer disputes, and project-level gross-margin changes.
4. Separate commercial/industrial, utility, oil and gas, and petrochemical
   demand; do not label every data-center or electrification booking as durable.
5. Include facility expansion, ready-workforce cost, letters of credit, Remsdaq
   acquisition return, SBC, and diluted shares in the owner-cash bridge.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what bookings conversion, project
margin, milestone collection, capacity utilization, end-market mix, working
capital, facility return, terminal growth, and cost of capital the valuation
requires. It must value completed-project cash rather than capitalize backlog.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether utility, industrial, oil and gas,
and infrastructure customers continue funding projects through rates, commodity
cycles, tariffs, and delayed site readiness. Liquidity passes only when contract
advances, receivables, warranty, letters of credit, facility commitments, and
common residual remain visible.

## Promotion boundary

`custom-electrical-systems-qualified; project-conversion-and-contract-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from bookings and backlog to
project revenue, estimated margin, contract assets/liabilities, billed and
collected receivables, acceptance, warranty, capacity investment, letters of
credit, acquisition return, and diluted common residual. Bookings, backlog,
revenue, OCF, adjusted metrics, and dividends remain diagnostic inputs.
