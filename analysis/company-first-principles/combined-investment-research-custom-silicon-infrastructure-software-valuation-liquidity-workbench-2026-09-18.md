# Custom silicon and infrastructure-software valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Broadcom from custom-AI and infrastructure-software
evidence into a company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It keeps
custom accelerators, networking silicon, connectivity, VMware and other
infrastructure software, customer concentration, design wins, renewals,
acquisition amortization, debt, SBC, and diluted common-owner cash separate
from NVIDIA's platform, Lam/KLA/ASML equipment, and Arista/Ciena networking
models.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Broadcom custom silicon and infrastructure software | Semiconductor design-in cash plus software renewal cash after R&D, foundry/supplier commitments, customer concentration, VMware integration, acquisition amortization, debt, interest, SBC, and dilution | Custom ASIC/networking R&D, outsourced supply and packaging, inventory, software product investment, support, acquisition integration, debt repayment, SBC replacement, and common capital | Hyperscaler capex pause, architecture change, customer bargaining, foundry constraint, VMware migration, software churn, debt/rates, export rule, or dilution | AI revenue, software revenue, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, dividends, or repurchases rise while design-win durability, renewals, cash conversion, acquisition return, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$63.887B`, operating cash flow `$27.537B`, and
  company-defined FCF `$26.914B`; PP&E purchases were only `$623M`, so the
  acquisition and dilution denominator matters more than physical capex alone.
- Q3 FY2026 revenue was `$29.591B`: semiconductor solutions `$20.839B` and
  infrastructure software `$8.752B`. AI semiconductor revenue was `$16.7B`, up
  `221%` year over year; this is a powerful but high comparison point.
- FY2024 included approximately `$25.978B` of net cash paid for acquisitions,
  principally VMware. FY2025's stronger cash year is therefore post-acquisition,
  not proof that the purchase already earned an adequate return.
- Infrastructure software was `30%` of Q3 revenue. VMware pricing, renewal,
  migration, support, deferred revenue, RPO, and product investment remain the
  core recurrence tests.
- Broadcom's non-GAAP exclusions include acquisition amortization, SBC,
  restructuring, integration, and other costs. These may be useful analytical
  adjustments but remain claims on common-owner value when recurring.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate AI, networking, wireless, broadband, storage, and other silicon;
   measure design-win duration, customer concentration, shipped units, margin,
   receivables, inventory, and supplier commitments.
2. Test software retention through renewals, cancellations, migrations, pricing,
   contract duration, support cost, deferred revenue, RPO, and customer product
   investment. A recurring invoice is not automatically durable retention.
3. Keep VMware purchase price, integration, amortization, debt, interest,
   software capex, and dilution visible. Do not treat acquired growth as free.
4. Reconcile revenue growth to receivables, inventory, payables, contract
   liabilities, cash, and customer advances over several quarters.
5. Show SBC, diluted shares, debt, dividends, repurchases, and acquisition cash
   together. Adjusted EBITDA and FCF remain diagnostic until common cash is
   evidenced after recurring platform claims.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what AI design-in, semiconductor mix,
gross margin, software renewal, VMware retention, acquisition return, R&D,
interest, terminal growth, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The
semiconductor and software engines should be modeled separately before a
conglomerate multiple is applied.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether hyperscaler budgets, customer
architecture, foundry capacity, export rules, rates, and enterprise software
budgets preserve cash through a technology cycle. Liquidity passes only when
debt, integration, supplier commitments, software obligations, SBC, and common
residual remain visible.

## Promotion boundary

`custom-silicon-software-qualified; design-win-renewal-and-acquisition-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from semiconductor design wins
and software renewals to shipped revenue, gross margin, collections, inventory,
supplier commitments, R&D, VMware retention, acquisition integration and return,
debt, SBC replacement, and diluted common residual. AI revenue, software
revenue, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, dividends, and repurchases remain diagnostic
inputs.
