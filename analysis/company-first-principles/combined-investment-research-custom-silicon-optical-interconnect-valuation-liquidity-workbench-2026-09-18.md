# Custom silicon and optical-interconnect valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Marvell from AI-demand and design-win evidence into a
company-specific qualification, shipment, margin, customer-concentration,
roadmap, acquisition, and dilution test. It is separate from Broadcom's wider
custom-silicon/infrastructure-software model and from semiconductor equipment
lanes. Design wins, bookings, revenue, non-GAAP earnings, operating cash, and
repurchases are not normalized common-owner cash by themselves.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marvell | Collected common cash after qualified custom-compute and optical/interconnect shipments, gross margin, R&D, customer engineering, inventory, support, acquisitions, debt, SBC, and dilution | Design and qualification engineering, R&D, manufacturing partners, yield, inventory, product transitions, support, Celestial AI integration, and working capital | Hyperscaler capex pause, architecture change, customer concentration, delayed acceptance, optical yield/standards shift, inventory build, acquisition debt, or dilution | Bookings rise while volume, acceptance, margin, collections, customer breadth, roadmap qualification, or per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$5.767B`, GAAP net loss `$(885.0M)`, non-GAAP net income
  `$1.377B`, and operating cash flow `$1.68B`.
- FY2026 revenue was `$8.195B`; Q1 FY2027 revenue `$2.418B`, GAAP net income
  `$34.5M`, non-GAAP net income `$718.0M`, and operating cash `$638.8M`.
- Management described 800G/1.6T optics, 51.2T Ethernet switching, scale-up
  optical products, and custom XPU solutions; these remain claims until shipped,
  accepted, collected, and margin-bearing.
- Celestial AI adds acquisition, integration, acquired-intangible, customer-
  qualification, and dilution tests to the optical roadmap.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile GAAP to non-GAAP earnings and classify amortization, SBC,
   acquisition integration, restructuring, and other exclusions as noncash,
   unusual cash, or recurring architecture-maintenance cost.
2. Separate design wins, bookings, remaining obligations, customer acceptance,
   shipments, revenue, receivables, and cash collection.
3. Track custom compute, optical interconnect, Ethernet/switching, and other
   products by customer, program, volume, margin, qualification, and roadmap.
4. Test customer concentration, hyperscaler budgets, customer-funded development,
   pricing power, program duration, and in-house or competitor substitution.
5. Charge R&D, engineering, yield, manufacturing-partner, inventory, support,
   and multi-generation product-transition costs before owner-cash promotion.
6. Test Celestial AI's incremental design wins, revenue, margin, retention,
   integration spending, acquired intangibles, and cash return.
7. Reconcile debt, purchase commitments, SBC, dilution, working capital, and
   repurchases to mid-cycle—not peak AI-cycle—cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what design-win conversion, shipment
volume, product mix, gross margin, R&D burden, customer breadth, acquisition
return, and cost of capital the equity price requires. The Lyn Alden-style
stress test asks whether hyperscaler financing, AI capex, architecture cycles,
rates, supply-chain yield, and customer concentration allow the company to fund
the next roadmap through a program pause.

## Promotion boundary

`custom-silicon-optical-qualified; design-win-to-collected-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period joins from design wins and bookings to qualified
volume, shipment/acceptance, margin, receivables, collections, customer breadth,
R&D and inventory burden, acquisition return, debt, SBC, and diluted common
residual. Bookings, revenue, non-GAAP earnings, OCF, and repurchases remain
diagnostic inputs.
