# Data-center power and thermal systems valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Vertiv from AI-infrastructure demand evidence into a
physical-system, backlog-conversion, capacity, warranty, and common-cash test.
It keeps Vertiv distinct from nVent's electrical-protection/cooling platform,
Cisco/Cloudflare networking, and data-center property owners.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vertiv | Power, thermal, deployment, monitoring, and service cash after backlog shipment, project margin, working capital, warranty, capacity, acquisitions, debt, SBC, and dilution | Factories, tooling, inventory, regional capacity, R&D, commissioning, service labor, warranty, and acquisition integration | Data-center capex delay, backlog cancellation/rescheduling, tariff or component inflation, customer concentration, working-capital reversal, or capacity overbuild | Backlog, AI demand, and adjusted margin rise while shipment conversion, project cash, warranty, service economics, or diluted residual weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 sales were `$10.230B`, operating cash flow `$2.114B`, capex `$220M`,
  and acquisitions `$1.185B`; the approximate cash after those uses was
  `$703M` before other claims.
- Backlog rose from `$7.2B` to `$15.0B` at year-end 2025, mostly expected to
  ship within `12–18` months. Backlog remains unearned until shipment,
  acceptance, collection, warranty, and service obligations are tested.
- Q1 2026 operating cash flow was `$767M`; Q2 sales were `$3.274B`, organic
  growth `18%`, adjusted operating margin `22.6%`, and adjusted FCF `$925M`.
- PurgeRite cost `$1.138B` net of cash, with up to `$250M` of contingent
  consideration. Its customer relationships, goodwill, integration, and cash
  return require a separate cohort test.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Bridge backlog through shipment, contract price/cost, gross margin,
   receivables, customer advances, collection, warranty, and service tail.
2. Separate product from service growth and test field labor, commissioning,
   warranty, and installed-base economics.
3. Normalize the `$339M` 2025 working-capital contribution and test whether
   customer advances reverse as backlog ships.
4. Split maintenance capex from capacity expansion, regional localization,
   tooling, software, and acquisition spending.
5. Test PurgeRite cash return, customer retention, contingent consideration,
   goodwill, integration cost, and acquired margin.
6. Include SBC, debt, swaps, tariffs, customer concentration, and dilution
   before promoting adjusted FCF or backlog.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test converts backlog, shipment timing,
project margin, service attachment, capacity utilization, working capital,
warranty, acquisition return, capex, and diluted shares into owner cash. The
Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether compute-density growth and physical
power/thermal bottlenecks offset rates, tariffs, supply chains, customer
concentration, and data-center capex normalization. Physical scarcity can
create pricing power without eliminating execution risk.

## Promotion boundary

`data-center-power-thermal-qualified; backlog-conversion-and-capacity-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from backlog and orders to
shipment, acceptance, project margin, collection, inventory, warranty, service
labor, capacity, acquisition return, debt, SBC, and diluted common residual.
Backlog, orders, adjusted margin, adjusted FCF, and buybacks remain diagnostic.
