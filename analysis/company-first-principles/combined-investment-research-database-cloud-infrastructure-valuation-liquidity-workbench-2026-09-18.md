# Database and cloud-infrastructure valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Oracle from enterprise-software and AI-demand evidence
into a company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It keeps the
installed database/application franchise separate from the capital-intensive
cloud-infrastructure buildout, RPO, customer prepayments, customer-supplied
hardware, debt, leases, preferred financing, depreciation, SBC, and diluted
common-owner cash.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oracle database and cloud infrastructure | Database/support/application cash plus cloud cash after data-center capex, power and equipment, customer prepayment obligations, customer-supplied hardware, depreciation, interest, leases, preferred claims, SBC, and dilution | Data-center facilities, servers/networking, capitalized software, migration support, power, depreciation/replacement, RPO delivery, lease commitments, debt issuance, preferred financing, and SBC replacement | AI-demand reversal, cloud-utilization shortfall, power/construction delays, debt-market closure, customer-concentration or prepayment reversal, falling compute prices, rising depreciation/interest, or dilution | RPO and cloud revenue grow while cash after capex, utilization, margin, debt service, prepayment obligations, or per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2026 revenue was `$67.357B`, operating cash flow `$31.977B`, and capital
  expenditures `$55.663B`, producing negative OCF-less-capex of approximately
  `$(23.686B)`.
- Oracle raised `$43B` of debt and `$5B` of equity financing in FY2026. This is
  a financing-dependent infrastructure expansion, not merely a software
  growth story.
- FY2026 cloud infrastructure revenue grew `77%` to `$18.1B`; Q1 FY2027 cloud
  infrastructure revenue was `$7.388B`, up `121%` year over year.
- RPO reached `$638B` at FY2026 year-end, up `363%`; Oracle stated that `$75B`
  of prepaid and customer-supplied hardware reduced the capital it needed to
  raise for AI data centers. RPO is visibility and funding evidence, not
  normalized owner cash.
- Q1 FY2027 operating cash flow was `$23.103B` against `$28.499B` of capex;
  deferred revenue from customer prepayments with a significant financing
  component increased `$11.363B`.
- FY2026 SBC was `$4.811B`, depreciation `$7.623B`, and at August 31, 2026
  non-current plus current borrowings were approximately `$125.337B`, with
  operating-lease liabilities of `$30.594B`.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Split database/support/application economics from cloud infrastructure. A
   durable installed base can fund a risky capacity buildout without proving
   that the buildout earns an adequate return.
2. Reconcile RPO into fixed, usage-based, contingent, prepaid, and
   customer-supplied-hardware components. Track delivery, utilization, margin,
   cash collection, and future service obligations.
3. Keep customer prepayments visible as customer-funded financing with a future
   delivery burden. Do not treat deferred-revenue inflow as freely distributable
   profit.
4. Reconcile capex, depreciation, useful lives, replacement capital, power,
   lease commitments, debt interest, preferred dividends, and equity issuance.
   Repeated external financing is part of the common-owner denominator.
5. Show GAAP income, non-GAAP exclusions, SBC, diluted shares, repurchases,
   debt, and preferred claims together. Recurring SBC is not a one-time cost.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test values database retention, support pricing,
application migration, cloud utilization, RPO conversion, infrastructure margin,
reinvestment rate, financing burden, and cost of capital separately. The
valuation must not capitalize RPO as if it were high-margin software revenue.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether enterprise customers and AI-cloud
partners can fund deployment through higher rates, power scarcity, construction
delays, falling compute prices, weaker usage, and tighter debt markets. Liquidity
passes only when customer prepayments, equipment, leases, debt, preferred
claims, service obligations, and diluted common residual remain visible.

## Promotion boundary

`database-cloud-qualified; infrastructure-return-and-financing-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from database/application
retention and cloud RPO to delivered revenue, utilization, margin, capex,
depreciation, customer-prepayment settlement, customer-supplied hardware risk,
debt and lease service, preferred claims, SBC replacement, and diluted common
residual. RPO, cloud revenue, deferred revenue, OCF, capex, adjusted EPS, and
repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
