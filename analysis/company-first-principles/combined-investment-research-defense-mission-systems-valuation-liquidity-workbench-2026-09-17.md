# Defense and mission-systems valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This workbench moves CACI, Leidos, and Northrop Grumman from defense-demand and
backlog evidence into company-specific valuation objects, contract-execution
burdens, funding stresses, and thesis breakers. It keeps government services,
mission integration, and large fixed-price defense programs distinct.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CACI | Cleared mission-systems cash after funded-backlog conversion, labor, program performance, acquisitions, debt, SBC, and dilution | Cleared labor, mission-system integration, classified facilities, AR/unbilled work, ARKA and other acquisitions, debt, SBC, and share count | Federal budget or procurement change, recompete loss, fixed-price cost overrun, acquisition integration, receivable stretch, or refinancing | Backlog/awards rise while funded work, margins, collections, acquired capability return, or diluted per-share cash weaken |
| Leidos | Diversified government-execution cash after contract-cost estimates, working capital, capital spending, Entrust integration, interest, debt, SBC, and dilution | Specialized labor, software and systems, contract assets/unbilled receivables, maintenance capital, acquisitions, debt, and diluted claims | Appropriations delay, fixed-price/incentive estimate deterioration, health/civil payment pressure, debt refinancing, or acquisition underperformance | Backlog remains near `$49B` while unbilled receivables, cost-to-complete losses, working-capital reversal, leverage, or per-share residual deteriorate |
| Northrop Grumman | Program-level defense-platform cash after cost estimates, production ramp, unbilled receivables, pension, B-21 and other loss provisions, debt, and dilution | Engineering, production tooling, supplier commitments, inventory, pension contributions, program claims, capex, and shareholder claims | Program loss, appropriations or schedule delay, production ramp, supplier failure, pension funding, customer modification, or refinancing | Backlog rises while B-21 or other program estimates, unbilled receivables, production cash, pension burden, or diluted residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- CACI FY2026 revenue was `$9.6B`, operating cash flow `$886.7M`, capital
  expenditures `$106.7M`, backlog `$32.0B`, and funded backlog `$5.4B`.
  Acquisitions used `$2.636B`; federal government contracts were `95.6%` of
  revenue, including `53.6%` from the Department of War and `24.6%` from the
  Intelligence Community.
- Leidos FY2025 operating cash flow was `$1.750B` against `$125M` of property,
  equipment, and software payments, with `$293M` of acquisition cash. Backlog
  was approximately `$49B`; Q2 FY2026 debt was approximately `$6.0B`.
- Northrop FY2025 operating cash flow was `$4.757B`, capital expenditures
  `$1.450B`, free cash flow `$3.307B`, backlog `$95.7B`, and required pension
  contributions `$72M`. The filing recorded a `$477M` B-21 loss provision and
  significant unbilled-receivable and production-commitment exposure.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Split total backlog from funded backlog and then follow funded work through
   staffing, performance, acceptance, billing, collection, and renewal.
2. Reconcile contract assets, unbilled receivables, advance payments, cost-to-
   complete estimates, loss provisions, and working-capital benefits before
   treating reported operating cash as durable.
3. Keep CACI's `$2.636B` acquisition cash and Leidos's Entrust/debt-funded
   capability expansion separate from recurring contract cash.
4. Keep Northrop's B-21 loss provisions, production ramp, pension contributions,
   and program-specific claims visible even when backlog and strategic relevance
   are strong.
5. Treat buybacks, dividends, adjusted EBITDA, and mechanical OCF-less-capex as
   residual claims or diagnostic screens, not proof of common-owner cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what funded backlog conversion,
contract margin, recompete rate, acquisition return, reinvestment, and cost of
capital the equity value requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether
appropriations, labor scarcity, program estimates, working capital, pension,
debt, and supplier commitments can absorb a budget or execution shock before
backlog becomes collectible cash.

## Promotion boundary

`defense-mission-qualified; program-margin-and-collection-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from funded program through
delivery, acceptance, billing, collection, required reinvestment, contract
claims, debt, legal-entity availability, pension/funding obligations, and
diluted common residual. Backlog, awards, adjusted EBITDA, reported OCF, and
buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
