# Deposit franchise and consumer-credit valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Bank of America from scale and earnings evidence into a
deposit-cost, credit-cycle, capital, liquidity, and common-return test. It
keeps the bank separate from private-credit funds, card-specialist economics,
and industrial owner-cash models.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bank of America | Risk-adjusted common earnings after deposit funding, loan losses, markets normalization, technology, compensation, legal, liquidity, regulatory capital, debt, and dilution | Deposit/payment infrastructure, branches/digital systems, technology, cybersecurity, fraud, compliance, personnel, reserves, and capital retention | Deposit beta/outflow, consumer or commercial credit stress, securities duration, market shock, operational event, regulatory capital, or funding spread | Deposits, loans, markets fees, or EPS rise while funding cost, charge-offs, reserves, CET1, technology/control cost, or per-share value deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue net of interest expense was `$113.1B`, net income `$30.5B`,
  average deposits `$1.98T`, year-end deposits `$2.02T`, and year-end loans
  and leases `$1.19T`.
- Standardized CET1 was `11.4%`, net charge-offs/average loans `0.50%`,
  allowance/loans `1.12%`, and provision for credit losses `$5.7B` in FY2025.
- The consumer and small-business network served approximately `69M` clients,
  with about `49M` active digital users and `41M` mobile users.
- The bank's earnings include net interest income, markets and investment-
  banking cycles, card/consumer credit, wealth, payments, technology, and
  capital requirements; no single revenue line is a through-cycle denominator.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate deposit balances, deposit beta, funding mix, loan yields, net
   interest income, and rate/mix effects.
2. Reconcile credit provisions, reserve releases/builds, delinquencies,
   charge-offs, payment rates, utilization, severity, and macro assumptions.
3. Normalize markets and investment-banking fees above a full-cycle floor;
   remove one-time gains, tax effects, and securities events.
4. Test technology, cyber, fraud, compliance, legal, and employee costs as
   required operating controls rather than discretionary adjustments.
5. Measure CET1, stress capital, liquidity, risk-weighted assets, securities
   duration, preferred claims, and common distributions together.
6. Treat deposits, loans, digital users, EPS, dividends, and buybacks as
   diagnostic until risk-adjusted common return is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test converts deposits, funding cost, loan
growth, loss rates, markets normalization, fees, operating efficiency,
technology, required capital, and dilution into risk-adjusted common earnings.
The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether rates, recession, deposit
competition, securities duration, liquidity, and regulatory capital preserve
the franchise through a credit cycle. Scale spreads fixed control costs but
also magnifies small loss-rate changes across a large balance sheet.

## Promotion boundary

`deposit-franchise-qualified; credit-cycle-and-capital-owner-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from deposits and beta,
funding, loans, yields, credit migration, reserves, charge-offs, markets
normalization, technology/control cost, liquidity, CET1, stress capital,
dividends, and diluted common residual. EPS, deposits, loans, digital users,
ROTE, dividends, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
