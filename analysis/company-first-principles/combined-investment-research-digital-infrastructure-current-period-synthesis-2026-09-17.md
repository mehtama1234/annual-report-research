# Digital infrastructure current-period synthesis — Equinix and Digital Realty

## Purpose and comparability boundary

This synthesis joins the current first-half 2026 evidence for Equinix and
Digital Realty. It compares cash surfaces, capital intensity, quality of
earnings, and financing claims—not intrinsic value and not a ranking. Equinix
emphasizes interconnection density, colocation, and powered capacity; Digital
Realty has a larger hyperscale-development and lease-conversion surface.

## Shared insight

Both companies show strong demand evidence and rising non-GAAP performance, but
both require a two-surface model: stabilized recurring operations, and a
growth-capital surface for power, land, construction, customer fit-out,
lease-up, acquisitions, JV funding, and financing.

Equinix H1 OCF less PP&E capex less real-estate acquisitions was approximately
`$(1.274B)`. Digital Realty H1 OCF less cash-basis capex was approximately
`$(32M)` before acquisition and other investment activity. These are not
normalized owner-cash conclusions; they are capital-allocation diagnostics.

## Different growth machines

Equinix reported annualized gross bookings, record interconnection additions,
and 52 projects underway. Its filing also shows receivables rising by `$255M`,
`$6.1B` of unaccrued capex commitments, and `$2.1B` of other purchase
commitments including power. The main question is whether interconnection and
powered-capacity growth converts to cash fast enough to cover capital and
financing claims.

Digital Realty reported backlog of approximately `$1.4B` at its share, H1
development capex of `$1.477B`, and roughly `$2.5B` of ATM equity proceeds
year-to-date. The main question is whether development yields and
commencement timing compensate common owners after equity issuance, project
debt, preferred claims, partner economics, and leasing costs.

The [Digital Realty signed-rent and development-contribution bridge](combined-investment-research-digital-realty-signed-rent-development-contribution-bridge-2026-09-17.md)
now makes that question concrete. Q2 reports approximately `$205M` of
Digital Realty-share annualized July hyperscale base rent, H1 development
cash-basis spending of `$1.477B`, and two development projects contributed to
a fund for approximately `$447M` against `$439M` carrying value. These are
matched research objects, not a project yield or common-owner return: project
identity, commencement, collections, debt, partner allocation, and remittance
remain open.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans comparison

| Control | Equinix | Digital Realty |
|---|---|---|
| Add-back risk | SBC, non-real-estate depreciation, amortization, impairments, transaction costs, straight-line items | Non-real-estate depreciation, financing amortization, SBC, rent adjustments, leasing compensation, internal commissions |
| Growth-capital opacity | PP&E combines maintenance and growth; recurring capex is management-defined | Development, enhancement, recurring, and indirect costs are separately disclosed |
| Working-capital test | `$258M` H1 OCF use from receivables; AR up `$255M` | Lease-up, rent commencement, and acquisition accounting require conversion testing |
| JV/private-capital test | xScale VIEs, `$392M` loan commitment, `$118M` future equity contribution commitments, `$48M` guarantee exposure | Fund contributions, unconsolidated debt, partner capital, promote economics, and retained interests |
| Dilution test | SBC, employee-equity effects, and forward equity activity | `$2.5B` ATM proceeds, OP units, Teraco put rights, preferred units |
| Macro test | Power costs, construction, rates, lease-up, hyperscaler timing | Power, construction, rates, hyperscaler timing, cap-rate expansion, capital-market access |

These are forensic controls, not allegations of misconduct. They are controls
against denominator drift and presentation risk.

## Decision status

The digital-infrastructure lane is current-period qualified at both companies
and remains diagnostic. Neither company should be promoted to a normalized
owner-cash ranking until the project-return workbench captures total project
capital, partner contributions, project debt, power and leasing costs,
commencement and stabilized yield, recurring capital, preferred/debt/NCI/OP-unit
claims, and per-share return after dilution.
