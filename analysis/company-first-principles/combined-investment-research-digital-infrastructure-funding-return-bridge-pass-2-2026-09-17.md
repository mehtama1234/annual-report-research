# Digital infrastructure funding-to-return bridge — pass 2 — 2026-09-17

## Decision

Equinix and Digital Realty both show strong demand and physical-capacity
control-point evidence, but their growth programs are funded through distinct
combinations of parent cash, debt, equity, partner capital, and project-level
claims. This pass makes the funding-to-return boundary explicit. It does not
rank the companies or convert backlog, AFFO, or FFO into common-owner cash.

Status: `funding-return-bridge-pass-2; project-return-open; no-ranking`.

The negative full-growth screens are not value conclusions. They are funding
facts: future rent and stabilized NOI must arrive in time and at a sufficient
yield to repay or compensate the capital providers and common-owner dilution.

## Project-return chain

`land/power/capacity -> total project capital -> partner/debt/equity funding -> energized and accepted capacity -> lease commencement -> stabilized NOI -> recurring capex and leasing cost -> debt/preferred/JV claims -> diluted common-owner cash`

Equinix's interconnection bookings, projects underway, and `$15.0B` remaining
performance obligations are demand and visibility evidence. Digital Realty's
`$1.4B` backlog, `$208M` Q2 bookings, and July hyperscale leases adding `$205M`
of annualized base rent are similar visibility evidence. None closes the
commencement, collection, stabilized-yield, or return bridge.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

1. Keep management-defined recurring capex and AFFO/FFO adjustments separate
   from the total physical capital required to energize and lease capacity.
2. Reconcile partner contributions, unconsolidated debt, guarantees, fund
   proceeds, retained ownership, promotes, and future capital calls; do not
   count partner funding as free parent capital.
3. Join signed rent or backlog to customer acceptance, power availability,
   commencement, incentives, receivables, and stabilized NOI.
4. Treat ATM issuance, OP units, preferred claims, SBC, and dilution as a
   senior claim on per-share value even when total FFO grows.
5. Include power costs, construction inflation, leasing commissions, recurring
   equipment refresh, and refinancing in the return denominator.

## Damodaran and Lyn Alden handoff

Damodaran-style valuation requires total invested capital, project-level
return on capital, reinvestment rate, competitive-advantage period, and cost of
capital—not just a multiple on AFFO or FFO. The Lyn-Alden-style stress case
combines rates, power scarcity, construction inflation, hyperscaler timing,
cap-rate expansion, partner funding, equity dilution, and refinancing.

Promotion requires one named project or cohort to join total capital, funding
source, commencement, stabilized yield/NOI, recurring capital, senior claims,
and per-share cash. The thesis breaker is activated if bookings/backlog and
non-GAAP cash measures rise while commencement slips, project capital expands,
equity/debt claims increase, and diluted common-owner cash fails to cover the
funding burden.
