# Digital infrastructure project-return workbench — Equinix and Digital Realty

## Purpose

This workbench is the next gate after the current-period synthesis. It makes
the project-return question explicit without inventing missing project data.
The observed bridges are mechanical screens; the normalized return fields are
deliberately marked open until project-level source evidence is available.

## Formula

For each company and project cohort:

`common-owner project cash = stabilized NOI or operating cash - power and operating claims - recurring capex - leasing commissions - growth/development capital - project debt/preferred claims - JV/NCI/partner economics - dilution`

`project return = stabilized common-owner project cash / total project capital funded by all parties`

The model must keep parent cash, proportionate JV cash, and consolidated cash
separate so that partner funding is not counted as free capital and future rent
is not counted before commencement.

## Required project-level fields

### Equinix

- cabinet, megawatt, site, or project identifier;
- total land, power, construction, equipment, and indirect capital;
- consolidated versus xScale/JV ownership and partner contributions;
- power price, pass-through, utility usage, and lease commitments;
- signed bookings/backlog, customer, commencement date, incentives, and
  stabilized utilization;
- recurring capex and equipment-refresh requirement;
- debt, guarantees, lease claims, and parent funding obligations;
- SBC, equity issuance, and diluted common-share allocation; and
- stabilized NOI and common-owner cash after all claims.

### Digital Realty

- development project, market, MW, land, and powered-shell status;
- total project cost including capitalized indirect costs and interest;
- consolidated, fund, and unconsolidated ownership percentages;
- partner contribution, project debt, guarantees, promote, and retained share;
- backlog, signed rent, commencement timing, occupancy, and customer incentives;
- recurring capex, external/internal leasing commissions, and power cost;
- acquisition cohort, purchase price, post-close NOI, and integration cost;
- ATM equity, OP units, Teraco put rights, preferred claims, and diluted share
  count; and
- stabilized NOI and common-owner cash after all claims.

## Decision

The [funding-to-return bridge pass 2](combined-investment-research-digital-infrastructure-funding-return-bridge-pass-2-2026-09-17.md)
now connects the current-period growth screens to debt, equity, partner
capital, senior claims, commencement, and per-share promotion gates.

Equinix and Digital Realty remain `qualified; diagnostic; no-ranking`. The
current evidence is strong enough to reject an AFFO/FFO-only valuation screen,
but not strong enough to claim that either company earns an attractive
common-owner return on incremental capacity. The next evidence chase should
target project-level supplemental tables, development-yield schedules,
commencement roll-forwards, JV/fund agreements, and capitalized-cost detail.

## September 18, 2026 valuation expectation refresh

The current market snapshot reports Equinix equity value of approximately
`$101.252B` at `$1,021.34` per share and Digital Realty equity value of
approximately `$65.844B` at `$182.12` per share. These values create a simple
reported-cash expectation screen, not an AFFO or project-return valuation.

| Company | Screen denominator | Mechanical market-cap / screen | Boundary |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Equinix | Annualized H1 OCF `$3.568B` less full-year recurring-capex guide midpoint `$0.300B` = `$3.268B` | `31.0x` | H1 PP&E was `$2.834B`; the guide is not a project-level maintenance schedule |
| Digital Realty | Annualized H1 OCF less twice H1 recurring capex: `($1.595B - $0.136B) × 2 = $2.918B` | `22.6x` | H1 development capex was `$1.477B`; recurring capex is not total physical replacement burden |

The denominator distinction matters: Equinix's `$300M` recurring-capex input is
a full-year guide, while Digital Realty's `$136M` is a six-month reported
amount. Neither screen deducts project debt, preferred/OP-unit claims, JV or
fund economics, leasing commissions, power, partner capital, equity issuance,
SBC, or dilution. Neither is a normalized common-owner cash multiple, and the
spread is not a ranking.

The valuation question remains whether energized capacity produces sufficient
stabilized NOI and common-owner cash to repay all funding claims. Backlog,
signed rent, AFFO/FFO, and recurring-capex labels remain expectation inputs
until a named project or cohort joins total capital, commencement, stabilized
yield, recurring burden, funding, and diluted residual.
