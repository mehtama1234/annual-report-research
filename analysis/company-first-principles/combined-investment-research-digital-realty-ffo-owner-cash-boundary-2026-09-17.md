# Digital Realty Core FFO-to-owner-cash boundary — FY2025

## Purpose

This pass completes the first digital-infrastructure comparison by translating
Digital Realty's hyperscale-capacity thesis into a cash and quality-of-earnings
bridge. It keeps Core FFO, AFFO, development capex, private capital, debt,
preferred claims, and diluted per-share economics separate. It is not a ranking
or an intrinsic-value conclusion.

## Operating control point

Digital Realty's control point is powered, connected capacity that can be turned
into long-term leases and network relationships. The sequence is:

`cloud/enterprise demand -> powered land and data-center construction -> lease commencement -> rent/NOI -> Core FFO -> recurring capital and leasing costs -> debt, preferred, JV, and dilution claims -> common-owner cash`

FY2025 Core FFO per diluted share was `$7.39`, approximately `$2.56B` in total
Core FFO, against approximately 346.1M diluted shares/units. Year-end backlog
was `$817M` of annualized base rent at Digital Realty's share, but the reported
weighted-average commencement gap was eight months. Backlog is therefore
visibility evidence, not current cash.

## The cash surfaces

The FY2025 filing reports `$2.412B` of operating cash flow, `$309M` of net
business-acquisition payments, `$1.728B` of dividends, and `$3.452B` of year-end
cash. A simple OCF-less-acquisition screen would be approximately `$2.103B`,
but that is not owner cash because it excludes recurring capex, leasing
commissions, development capital, debt service, preferred claims, and equity
issuance.

The company guided to `$3.25B–$3.75B` of 2026 development capex, net of partner
contributions, and `$400M–$425M` of recurring capex plus capitalized leasing
costs. The development spend alone is larger than FY2025 operating cash flow.
That does not prove the projects are uneconomic; it shows that the investment
case depends on lease-up timing, stabilized yield, partner capital, and access
to external financing.

The filing also reports approximately `$4.977B` of development-in-process
assets. This is a useful balance-sheet warning: substantial capital can sit in
construction before it contributes rent or FFO.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans tests

### Core FFO is a comparison measure, not an owner-cash endpoint

FFO excludes real-estate depreciation, impairments, and property gains/losses;
Core FFO and AFFO make further adjustments. The company itself warns that FFO
does not capture the capital expenditures and capitalized leasing commissions
needed to maintain operating performance. The correct bridge is:

`GAAP income -> FFO -> Core FFO/AFFO -> recurring capex and leasing commissions -> development capex -> debt/preferred/JV claims -> diluted common cash`

### Backlog and bookings have a timing and execution burden

Fourth-quarter bookings were approximately `$400M` of annualized GAAP rent at
100% share, while the year-end signed-but-not-commenced backlog was `$817M` at
Digital Realty's share. The commencement gap means that the model must test
construction completion, energization, customer acceptance, lease incentives,
and the date at which rent actually begins.

### Private capital changes the perimeter, not necessarily the economics

Digital Realty contributes projects and assets to funds and joint ventures,
retains interests and operating roles, and receives partner capital. For each
vehicle, the model must capture ownership, partner contributions, debt,
guarantees, management fees, promote economics, distributions, and the share
of future capex and rent. A lower parent-level capital requirement can be
genuine capital efficiency or a shifted claim on future cash.

### Per-share value must survive financing and dilution

The company carries approximately `$18.4B` of debt and reported a 4.9x net-
debt-to-adjusted-EBITDA ratio at year-end. The per-share bridge must include
operating-partnership units, preferred instruments, ATM issuance, SBC,
interest, dividends, and diluted shares. Total Core FFO growth is insufficient
if each new dollar requires disproportionately more debt or equity.

## Lyn Alden-style stress map

The main liquidity transmission is a simultaneous slowdown in hyperscaler
deployment, power scarcity, construction inflation, and higher refinancing
costs. In that regime:

- backlog can remain signed while commencement is delayed;
- development yields can compress as construction and power costs rise;
- recurring capital and leasing costs can absorb a larger share of AFFO;
- private-capital partners can demand better economics or reduce funding; and
- debt and equity issuance can preserve growth while reducing common-owner
  value per share.

The balance sheet can support the transition, but it does not eliminate the
duration and refinancing risk of a capital-intensive real-estate platform.

## Valuation implication

The September 2026 expectation screen used approximately `$68.2B` of equity
value against approximately `$2.56B` of FY2025 Core FFO, or about 26.7x. The
market must underwrite the stated 10%+ stabilized development yields, backlog
conversion, power availability, financing access, and FFO/share resilience.

The next promotion object is a project or portfolio bridge that joins total
capital, partner funding, power and construction cost, lease commencement,
stabilized yield, recurring capex, debt/preferred claims, and diluted shares.
Until that exists, Core FFO is an expectation input rather than common-owner
cash.
