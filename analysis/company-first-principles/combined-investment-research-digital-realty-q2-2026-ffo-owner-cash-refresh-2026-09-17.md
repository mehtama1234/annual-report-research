# Digital Realty Q2 2026 FFO-to-owner-cash refresh

## Purpose

This refresh advances Digital Realty from the FY2025 diagnostic boundary to a
current first-half 2026 filing-backed test. It keeps Core FFO, AFFO, bookings,
backlog, development capex, acquisitions, private capital, debt, preferred
claims, and equity issuance separate. It does not rank Digital Realty against
Equinix or promote backlog to current common-owner cash.

## Current-period cash bridge

Digital Realty generated `$1.595B` of operating cash in the first half of 2026.
Cash-basis capital expenditures were `$1.627B`, including `$1.477B` of
development projects and `$136M` of recurring capital expenditures. The simple
OCF-less-total-capex screen is approximately:

`$1.595B OCF - $1.627B cash-basis capex = $(32M)`

This is before acquisition/asset-acquisition activity, cash invested in
unconsolidated entities, financing costs, debt and preferred claims, and
common distributions. The filing’s investing comparison indicates roughly
`$2.170B` of 2026 acquisition/asset-acquisition spend versus `$218M` in the
prior-year period. The resulting broad capital-allocation surface is therefore
far below Core FFO or AFFO, even though the underlying projects may earn
attractive stabilized returns.

The capital-basis distinction matters. Recurring capex was only `$136M` in the
period, while development capex was `$1.477B`; indirect costs were another
`$157M`, including `$73M` of capitalized interest. A recurring-capex AFFO
screen is useful for dividend capacity, but it does not answer whether the
development portfolio earns its cost of capital after lease-up and financing.

## Growth, backlog, and commencement

Digital Realty reported six-month diluted AFFO per share of `$4.39`, up from
`$3.45`, and six-month diluted FFO per share of `$4.73`. It also reported Q2
bookings of `$208M` at its share and a record backlog of approximately `$1.4B`
at its share. These are meaningful demand and visibility indicators.

They are not current cash. The model must connect signed rent to power
availability, construction completion, customer acceptance, lease
commencement, tenant incentives, operating costs, and stabilized NOI. July
hyperscale leases added another `$205M` of annualized base rent at Digital
Realty’s share, but signed rent still carries execution and timing risk.

The current results release raised 2026 Core FFO per share excluding net
promote to `$8.15–8.20`. It also raised the surrounding growth expectations,
while the 10-Q states that development capital is generally funded with
available cash, equity, and debt. That combination makes the financing
perimeter central to per-share value.

## Equity issuance is part of the cash story

Digital Realty issued approximately `$2.5B` of net ATM equity proceeds through
13.5M shares year-to-date, including approximately `$1.2B` in Q2. This is not
automatically destructive: if new equity funds projects whose incremental
returns exceed the cost of equity, common-owner value can rise. But it means
total FFO growth and even diluted FFO growth must be tested against the capital
issued to produce it.

The current panel therefore tracks:

- diluted common shares and operating-partnership units;
- Teraco-related minority and put-right effects;
- ATM issuance and equity proceeds per share;
- preferred dividends and preferred claims;
- stock compensation and internal leasing costs; and
- project-level return after partner funding and dilution.

## Debt, preferred, and private-capital perimeter

At June 30, consolidated debt was `$18.768B`, with 93.6% fixed or swapped and
6.4% unhedged variable-rate debt. Pro-rata secured debt of unconsolidated
entities was approximately `$2.0B`. Net debt-to-adjusted-EBITDA was `4.7x`,
debt-plus-preferred-to-enterprise-value was `22.3%`, and fixed-charge coverage
was `5.2x`.

These metrics show a substantial but currently managed financing burden. They
do not remove duration risk: the platform still depends on refinancing access,
stable spreads, construction funding, and partner capital while development
projects are not yet fully contributing rent.

The fund/JV model adds another perimeter. In May, Digital Realty contributed
two development projects to a fund for gross proceeds of approximately `$447M`
against carrying value of approximately `$439M`. The right question is not
whether the transaction produced a reported gain. It is whether the resulting
partner capital, retained ownership, operating role, distributions, promote,
and future funding obligations improve common-owner returns after all claims.

## Quality-of-earnings and financial-shenanigans screen

1. **AFFO definition test:** reconcile non-real-estate depreciation, financing
   amortization, SBC, straight-line rent, above/below-market rent, leasing
   compensation, internal leasing commissions, and recurring capex across
   periods.
2. **Development-capital test:** include development capex, indirect costs,
   capitalized interest, and the costs required to energize and lease capacity.
3. **Backlog conversion test:** track signed backlog by commencement year,
   customer, market, power status, incentives, and stabilized yield.
4. **Promote/transaction test:** isolate net promote, insurance recoveries,
   asset contributions, gains, and other items that lift FFO without being
   recurring common-owner operating cash.
5. **Private-capital test:** capture partner contributions, fund-level debt,
   retained ownership, guarantees, distributions, and future capital calls.
6. **Dilution test:** compare FFO/AFFO growth with ATM issuance, operating-
   partnership units, preferred claims, Teraco put rights, and SBC.
7. **Acquisition-return test:** separate land, operating assets, development
   assets, minority stakes, and strategic private-capital acquisitions; then
   measure post-close NOI and cash return.
8. **Macro-liquidity test:** stress hyperscaler timing, power scarcity,
   construction inflation, interest rates, refinancing, and cap-rate expansion
   together.

These tests are forensic controls, not accusations of misconduct. The primary
risk is denominator drift: treating FFO/AFFO or signed rent as if the platform
had already funded all the capital and dilution required to produce it.

## Valuation consequence

Digital Realty remains qualified and diagnostic. The current data supports a
strong operating-demand thesis and a visible backlog, but it does not yet prove
common-owner value creation. The next workbench must calculate:

`stabilized common-owner cash = project NOI after power and operating claims - recurring capex - leasing costs - development capital - debt/preferred claims - dilution`

It must also show the return on the `$1.477B` development program and the
incremental return on the `$2.5B` of ATM equity raised year-to-date. Until that
bridge is built, Digital Realty should not be promoted or ranked against
Equinix.
