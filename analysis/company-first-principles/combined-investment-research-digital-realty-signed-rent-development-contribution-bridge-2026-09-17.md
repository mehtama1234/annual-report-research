# Digital Realty signed-rent and development-contribution bridge — Q2 2026

Research date: 2026-09-17

## Question

Does Digital Realty current-period signed hyperscale rent and development
contribution activity close the path from development capital to common-owner
cash?

## Observed bridge

| Object | Reported observation | Safe use | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Development capital | H1 cash-basis development projects of $1,477.072M | Denominator for the current growth-capital surface | Does not identify project-level total cost, indirect capitalized cost, debt, partner funding, or maintenance burden |
| Signed rent | July hyperscale leases added approximately $410M annualized GAAP base rent at 100% share, or $205M at Digital Realty share | Commencement and customer-commitment input | Signed rent is not collected cash, stabilized NOI, or proof that the related development capital has entered service |
| Development contribution | In May, two development projects were contributed to Digital Realty DC Partners NA Fund for approximately $447M gross proceeds against approximately $439M aggregate carrying value; the filing says an approximately $8M gain on disposition was recognized | Named capital event and visible proceeds-versus-carrying-value comparison | Project identities, ownership percentage, debt assumption, taxes, partner economics, and cash remittance are not disclosed in this object |
| Recurring capital | H1 recurring capex of $136M | Separate recurring-capital burden | Management definitions and project-level maintenance allocation remain incomplete |

The mechanical signed-rent-to-H1-development-capital ratio is approximately
13.9% ($205M / $1,477.072M). This is not a yield, return on capital, or
valuation input by itself: the numerator is annualized signed GAAP base rent at
Digital Realty share, while the denominator is a half-year development cash
outflow that may cover multiple projects and periods. It is useful only as a
scale diagnostic showing why lease commencement must be matched to the exact
capital cohort.

The contribution event has a separate diagnostic. The filing identifies the
recipient as Digital Realty DC Partners NA Fund and explicitly recognizes an
approximately $8M gain on disposition. That is stronger than an inferred
proceeds-minus-carrying-value calculation, but it is still not a realized
common-owner return: project identities, ownership, proceeds settlement, debt
assumed or repaid, taxes, partner allocation, and parent remittance remain
unresolved.

The same filing reports approximately $2.5B of net ATM proceeds from about
13.5M common shares issued at an average $184.94 per share, after about
$12.6M of agent commissions, with approximately $6.3B remaining available.
This makes the common-equity funding and dilution burden explicit. It is not
evidence that the ATM financed these two contributed projects or that the
signed-rent economics cover the new share count.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

1. Do not treat annualized GAAP base rent as collected rent or stabilized NOI.
2. Do not compare a half-year development outflow with a lease numerator as if both describe the same project cohort.
3. Do not treat fund-contribution proceeds as recurring operating cash or as evidence that development economics are attractive.
4. Reconcile straight-line rent, lease incentives, rent commencement, receivables, and cash collections.
5. Reconcile development costs, capitalized interest, land, power, leasing commissions, partner contributions, and project debt.
6. Keep consolidated, fund, and unconsolidated ownership separate before allocating proceeds or return to common owners.

## Decision

This pass improves Digital Realty from a broad development-capital screen to a
bounded signed-rent and capital-contribution bridge. It remains
qualified; project-return promotion open; no-ranking. The next decisive
source is a project-level schedule or supplemental disclosure that joins the
July leases and the two contributed projects to project cost, commencement,
ownership, funding, collections, and common-owner residual cash.
