# Distributed resilience power valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Generac Holdings into a company-specific distributed
resilience-power, backup-generation, controls, storage, and monitoring
valuation/liquidity object. It keeps Generac separate from merchant power,
utilities, grid infrastructure, industrial uptime, and equipment rental. The
object tests residential, C&I, telecom, and data-center demand after backlog
conversion, inventory, warranty, dealer/service economics, factory capacity,
tariffs, legal claims, capex, debt, and diluted common residual.

## Decision matrix

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$4.209B`, OCF `$438.0M`, capex `$169.9M`, and the simple
  cash-after-capex screen `$268.1M`, down from `$604.6M` in 2024. Adjusted EBITDA
  was `$715.5M` and included `$158.0M` of legal/regulatory/other costs.
- FY2025 residential product sales were `$2.267B`, C&I `$1.457B`, and other
  sales `$484.9M`; residential fell `6.8%` while C&I rose `4.9%`.
- Q2 2026 C&I external sales rose `29%`, with reported data-center backlog of
  about `$1.6B`. Backlog requires shipment, customer acceptance, collection,
  gross-margin, inventory, and warranty joins.
- Q2 2026 margin benefited from approximately `$71M` of tariff refunds in the
  local evidence packet. Margin after refund roll-off is the relevant test.
- Year-end warranty liabilities were `$131.9M`; deferred extended-warranty
  revenue `$219.4M`; borrowings and finance leases `$1.282B`. Deferred warranty
  revenue is a future service obligation, not pure owner cash.
- FY2025 legal provisions, settlements, patent-related costs, and other legal
  matters were about `$142.3M`; recurring legal, warranty, and capacity costs
  should not be automatically added back.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Bridge backlog to inventory, shipment, acceptance, receivable collection,
   warranty/service cost, and cash after capex.
2. Separate residential, C&I, data-center, telecom, rental, services,
   monitoring, storage, and extended-warranty economics.
3. Rebuild margin excluding tariff refunds and test price realization against
   input/freight, customer concessions, and channel inventory.
4. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA legal/regulatory and optimization adjustments to
   actual cash, recurrence, warranty, and field claims.
5. Treat extended-warranty/deferred revenue, buybacks, backlog, and service
   attachment as claims or diagnostics until renewal and cost evidence exists.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what C&I/backlog conversion,
residential normalization, margin after tariff roll-off, service attachment,
warranty burden, factory return, capex, debt cost, and cost of capital the
valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether grid fragility,
consumer liquidity, hyperscale budgets, rates, input costs, weather, and
customer concentration preserve cash across a synchronized demand shock.

## Promotion boundary

`distributed-resilience-power-qualified; backlog-warranty-and-cash-conversion-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from backlog and installed
equipment to shipment, acceptance, collection, inventory, warranty/service
cost, factory/capacity return, capex, legal claims, debt, diluted shares, and
common-owner residual. Backlog, adjusted EBITDA, C&I growth, tariff refunds,
extended warranties, OCF, FCF, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic
inputs.
