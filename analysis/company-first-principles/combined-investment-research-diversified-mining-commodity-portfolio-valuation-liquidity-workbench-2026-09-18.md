# Diversified mining and commodity portfolio valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves BHP from broad mining evidence into a segment, project,
ownership, and common-cash test. It keeps BHP's diversified operating assets
separate from Lundin Gold's single-asset mine and Franco-Nevada's royalty and
streaming claims.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BHP | Iron ore, copper, coal, and potash cash by asset after realized prices, grades, costs, ownership, sustaining capital, taxes, partner claims, closure, debt, and dilution | Mine replacement, sustaining and growth capital, exploration, project development, JV funding, closure, water, environmental, and safety obligations | Commodity-price normalization, project overrun, Samarco/legacy claims, net-debt expansion, partner funding, or dividends above sustainable residual | Copper mix and production rise while cash per BHP share, project return, ownership-adjusted residual, or balance-sheet flexibility deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$51.262B`, operating cash flow `$18.692B`, capital and
  exploration expenditure `$9.794B`, and management free cash flow `$5.342B`.
- BHP paid `$6.403B` of dividends to shareholders and `$1.873B` to
  non-controlling interests; net debt rose to `$12.924B` from `$9.120B`.
- FY2025 capital allocation included roughly `$4.5B` in copper, `$1.6B` in
  potash, and `$3.2B` in steelmaking materials. Samarco settlement payments
  were about `$1.8B`.
- FY2026 half-year operating cash flow was `$9.372B`, free cash flow `$2.906B`
  after `$5.263B` of capital and exploration spending, and copper supplied
  `51%` of Underlying EBITDA.
- Asset-sale and portfolio-recycling proceeds, including the Antamina silver
  stream and Carajas transaction, improve liquidity but are not recurring
  owner cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile operating cash and free cash flow to PP&E, exploration,
   equity-accounted funding, acquisitions, asset sales, Samarco, leases, and
   non-controlling distributions.
2. Build asset-level bridges for production, grade, recovery, realized price,
   unit cost, sustaining capital, growth capital, ownership, and cash received.
3. Separate copper-led growth from iron-ore and coal price support; test each
   project against mid-cycle prices, revised capital, timing, taxes, and closure.
4. Track potash, copper, and mine-life options as probability-weighted projects,
   not reserves or recurring production before engineering and approval.
5. Reconcile JV funding, partner distributions, non-controlling interests,
   Samarco, water, environmental, labor, and host-country obligations.
6. Treat underlying EBITDA, production, asset sales, dividends, and buybacks as
   diagnostic until ownership-adjusted renewal cash and diluted residual are
   evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test values iron ore, copper, coal, potash, and
project options separately using mid-cycle prices, sustaining capital, reserve
renewal, ownership, taxes, debt, and closure. The Lyn Alden-style stress test
asks whether electrification, energy security, food security, China-linked
demand, trade policy, and resource nationalism offset commodity cycles and the
capital burden of being a producer. Diversification can reduce single-mine risk
without making consolidated cash equivalent to common-owner cash.

## Promotion boundary

`diversified-mining-qualified; asset-level-return-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from asset production,
realized price, unit cost, ownership, sustaining and growth capital, JV and
non-controlling cash, taxes, claims, closure, debt, dividends, and diluted
common residual. Production, EBITDA, management FCF, asset-sale proceeds,
dividends, and copper exposure remain diagnostic inputs.
