# Consumables-heavy neighborhood convenience valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Dollar General from current-period retail evidence into a company-specific valuation, traffic, consumables, store-condition, shrink, and owner-cash test. It distinguishes Dollar General's rural and neighborhood replenishment model from Dollar Tree's multi-price basket and from broad omnichannel retail.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dollar General | Collected everyday-essentials retail cash after traffic, consumables mix, inventory, shrink, labor, store maintenance, remodels, new stores, distribution, leases, debt, and dilution | Store fleet, Project Renovate/Elevate, distribution centers, inventory, private label, store labor, DG Media, technology, and working capital | Lower-income household purchasing power, food/fuel and consumables inflation, traffic, shrink, labor, rural store productivity, supply chain, leases, and dilution | Traffic, comps, gross margin, EPS, and cash flow rise while units, inventory per store, shrink, store condition, labor, maintenance capital, lease burden, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net sales increased `5.2%` to `$42.7B`, same-store sales increased `3.0%`, operating profit increased `28.6%` to `$2.2B`, and diluted EPS increased `34.1%` to `$6.85`.
- Fiscal 2025 ended with `20,893` stores; Dollar General opened `589` stores, remodeled `2,000` through Project Renovate and `2,254` through Project Elevate, and closed `290` stores.
- Consumables were `81.0%` of FY2025 sales, making traffic, replenishment frequency, mix, shrink, and inventory productivity central to the model.
- Q1 2026 sales increased `3.4%` to `$10.8B`, same-store sales increased `2.0%`, traffic increased `1.4%`, gross margin improved `65` basis points to `31.6%`, and operating profit increased `10.8%` to `$638.5M`.
- Q4 2025 operating cash flow increased `21.3%` to `$3.6B` and inventory per store declined `7.0%`; Q3 growth was traffic-led with traffic up `2.5%` and average transaction flat.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile comps to traffic, units, average transaction, consumables mix, price, promotions, shrink, returns, and customer sell-through; traffic-led and ticket-led growth have different quality.
2. Separate consumables, seasonal, discretionary, private-label, tobacco, and perishables; 81% consumables does not mean all sales have equal margin or working-capital behavior.
3. Test Project Renovate and Project Elevate through sales lift, traffic, labor, shrink, inventory, maintenance capex, and store closure/cannibalization rather than remodel counts.
4. Reconcile inventory per store, vendor terms, distribution-center capacity, labor, shrink, leases, new stores, closures, and working-capital timing before promoting operating cash.
5. Keep DG Media Network, technology, and other attached monetization separate from store economics, advertising cost, customer data, compliance, and incremental contribution.
6. Treat EPS, dividends, repurchases, and cash flow as residual claims requiring maintenance capital, leases, debt, and diluted-share validation.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what traffic, replenishment frequency, consumables mix, average ticket, shrink, labor productivity, store productivity, remodel return, inventory turns, maintenance capital, lease burden, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether lower-income households preserve frequent small-basket purchasing through inflation, employment, food/fuel, credit, and regional shocks, and whether the store network can maintain value perception and availability.

## Promotion boundary

`dollar-general-consumables-qualified; traffic-replenishment-store-condition-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from traffic and consumables units to collection, mix, inventory/shrink, store condition, labor, remodel/new-store productivity, supply chain, leases, debt, taxes, and diluted common residual. Revenue, comps, traffic, EPS, remodel counts, inventory per store, DG Media, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
