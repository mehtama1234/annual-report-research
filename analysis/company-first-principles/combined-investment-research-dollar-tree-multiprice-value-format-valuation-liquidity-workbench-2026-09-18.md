# Discount-variety multi-price value-format valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Dollar Tree from current-period retail evidence into a company-specific valuation, value-architecture, store-format, inventory, and owner-cash test. It treats Dollar Tree as a low-ticket, multi-price convenience and treasure-hunt network after the Family Dollar separation—not as a membership warehouse or broad omnichannel ecosystem.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dollar Tree | Collected continuing-operations retail cash after unit sell-through, traffic/ticket mix, multi-price assortment, shrink, inventory, store refresh, supply chain, leases, labor, separation effects, debt, and dilution | Stores, 3.0 conversions, distribution centers, inventory, private-label/value sourcing, technology, store labor, delivery, and working capital | Trade-down and traffic, price elasticity, tariffs, vendor terms, shrink, lease/store burden, supply-chain execution, store conversion, and dilution | Average ticket and EPS rise while traffic, units, gross margin after promotions, inventory, 3.0 store productivity, lease burden, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 continuing-operations net sales increased `10.4%` to `$19.4B`, diluted EPS was `$5.94`, and operating income was `$1.7B`; the company operated about `9,000` U.S. stores and `275` Canadian stores.
- Q1 2026 net sales increased `7.2%` to `$5.0B`, comparable sales increased `3.5%`, operating income increased `23%` to `$473.3M`, and diluted EPS was `$1.76`.
- Q1 growth was driven by a `4.5%` average-ticket increase while traffic declined `1.0%`, and gross margin improved `120` basis points; management raised full-year adjusted EPS outlook to `$6.70`–`$7.10`.
- Q4 2025 comparable sales increased `5.0%`, gross margin increased `150` basis points to `39.1%`, and approximately `5,300` stores had converted to the Dollar Tree 3.0 multi-price format.
- Q3 converted about `646` stores, with average ticket up `4.5%` but traffic down `0.3%`; the Family Dollar sale completed July 5, 2025, creating a continuing-operations reset.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile comparable sales to traffic, units, average ticket, price, mix, promotions, shrink, returns, and customer sell-through; ticket growth can conceal traffic erosion.
2. Separate the `$1.25` opening price point, multi-price assortment, private label, consumables, seasonal, and discretionary categories; price architecture changes the basket and margin denominator.
3. Test Dollar Tree 3.0 conversions through sales lift, traffic, labor, shrink, inventory turns, markdowns, capital cost, and cannibalization—not store-count rollout alone.
4. Keep Family Dollar separation proceeds and corporate costs separate from continuing-operations store economics, supply-chain investment, leases, and working capital.
5. Reconcile vendor terms, tariff exposure, inventory, distribution-center capacity, store refresh, labor, lease obligations, and cash generation before promoting adjusted EPS.
6. Treat dividends, repurchases, adjusted EPS, and average-ticket growth as residual claims requiring maintenance capex, leases, debt, and diluted-share validation.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what traffic, ticket, price/mix, multi-price penetration, store productivity, supply-chain savings, inventory turns, maintenance capital, lease burden, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether households continue to trade down into low-ticket value formats through inflation, tariffs, employment, and affordability shocks, and whether value perception survives price increases and reduced traffic.

## Promotion boundary

`dollar-tree-multiprice-qualified; traffic-ticket-store-format-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from traffic and ticket to unit sell-through, collection, margin after promotions, inventory, shrink, store conversion productivity, supply-chain investment, leases, debt, taxes, and diluted common residual. Revenue, comps, average ticket, EPS, store counts, 3.0 conversions, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
