# Duke Energy Q2 2026 project-recovery-to-owner-cash refresh

## Purpose

This refresh advances Duke from an approval-boundary case to a current H1 2026
cash, financing, and shared-ownership test. It keeps regulatory approval,
construction spend, asset-sale proceeds, NCI funding, debt, customer recovery,
and common-owner cash separate.

## Current-period cash surface

Duke generated `$4.272B` of H1 operating cash and spent `$8.240B` on capital
expenditures. The mechanical bridge is:

`$4.272B OCF - $8.240B capex = $(3.968B)`

The period also included `$2.501B` of Piedmont Tennessee sale proceeds,
`$2.827B` of noncontrolling-interest contributions, `$6.464B` of long-term debt
issuance, `$4.831B` of long-term debt redemption, and `$1.693B` of dividends.
These are important financing and portfolio events, but they should not be
presented as recurring regulated operating cash.

Accrued capital expenditures were `$2.574B`, making cost-to-complete and cash
payment timing a required part of the owner-cash bridge. The segment table
places `$8.024B` of capex in Electric Utilities and Infrastructure, showing that
the investment burden is tied to the regulated platform rather than only a
corporate item.

## Anderson County boundary

The Anderson County project remains a useful named-project control point. The
current filing describes planned co-ownership in which Duke Energy Carolinas
would own approximately 1,170 MW, NCEMC 100 MW, and CEPC 95 MW, subject to final
contractual terms.

The evidence chain must remain:

`approval -> final ownership and cost allocation -> paid construction -> CWIP/rate base -> in-service date -> tariff/customer billing -> collection -> project and common-owner return`

The project’s capacity and approval improve the regulatory map, but neither
proves that Duke funds the full project, receives the full return, or collects
customer cash before financing and shared-owner claims are paid.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans tests

1. **Capex payment test:** reconcile accrued capex, paid capex, CWIP, AFUDC,
   construction milestones, and cost-to-complete.
2. **Recovery test:** connect regulatory approval and rate-base treatment to a
   tariff, billing determinant, customer contribution, and collection.
3. **Shared-ownership test:** identify final MW ownership, capital calls, debt,
   NCI, distributions, and the portion of project cash belonging to Duke.
4. **Portfolio-event test:** exclude Piedmont sale proceeds and NCI funding from
   recurring utility owner cash; model proceeds and uses separately.
5. **Financing test:** track debt issuance, redemptions, maturities,
   convertible notes, preferred dividends, and interest burden.
6. **Customer-credit test:** identify customer commitments, deposits, collateral,
   take-or-pay terms, and payment timing.
7. **Adjusted-earnings test:** reconcile GAAP income, regulatory items,
   mark-to-market effects, asset sales, tax timing, and other adjustments.
8. **Stress test:** combine rates, construction inflation, fuel availability,
   permitting delay, load delay, affordability pressure, and capital-market
   access.

These are forensic controls, not allegations of misconduct. The key risk is
confusing approval and planned capacity with paid, recoverable, collected,
common-owner cash.

## Promotion gate

Duke remains `qualified; current-period cash updated; approval-and-recovery
open; no-ranking`. The next joinable object is a named Anderson County project
record or customer-recovery filing that provides final ownership, cost
allocation, paid capex, in-service timing, rate-base/tariff treatment,
customer billing or contributions, and Duke common-owner return.
