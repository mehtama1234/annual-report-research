# Eastman Kodak print and materials valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Kodak from current-period evidence into a company-specific
valuation, legacy-franchise, print-consumables, advanced-materials, commodity-input,
restructuring, debt, and common-owner cash test. It does not pool print, film,
chemicals, workflow, and consumer-device economics into a generic imaging multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eastman Kodak | Collected Print and Advanced Materials & Chemicals cash after consumables, film, manufacturing, silver/aluminum, inventory, R&D, restructuring, pensions or other claims, term-loan service, and dilution | Print systems and consumables, AM&C capacity, film, chemicals, workflow, plant maintenance, R&D, inventory, and process modernization | Silver/aluminum and manufacturing inflation, print decline, customer concentration, inventory, KRIP/legacy claims, restructuring, term debt, or dilution | Revenue and operational EBITDA improve while recurring print demand, AM&C margin, commodity pass-through, cash collection, claims, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Full-year 2025 revenue was `$1.069B`, gross profit `$232M`, and operational EBITDA `$62M`; GAAP loss was materially distorted by the non-recurring KRIP termination and excise-tax impact.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$311M`, up `18%`; gross profit was `$82M`, gross margin `26%`, GAAP net income `$17M`, and operational EBITDA `$36M`.
- Q2 AM&C revenue was `$105M`, up `40%`, and Print revenue `$195M`, up `10%`; cash was `$290M` versus `$337M` at year-end after term-loan repayment and higher inventory.
- Q1 2026 explicitly identified silver and aluminum inflation as offsets to operational improvement.
- The modern model spans Print, AM&C, motion-picture film, workflow systems, and industrial manufacturing rather than a dominant consumer-camera franchise.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile Print, AM&C, film, workflow, and other revenue to consumables,
   installed systems, customer concentration, recurring usage, and collections.
2. Separate pricing, volume, mix, manufacturing efficiency, silver/aluminum,
   inventory reserve, FX, and plant utilization effects.
3. Keep process modernization, R&D, plant maintenance, inventory, restructuring,
   pensions or legacy claims, and term-loan service in the owner-cash denominator.
4. Test whether operational EBITDA excludes recurring transition, KRIP, excise,
   restructuring, legal, environmental, or customer-transfer burdens.
5. Reconcile cash, term debt, inventory, working capital, claims, SBC, preferred or
   other equity interests, and diluted shares before treating turnaround cash as
   durable common-owner surplus.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what Print usage, AM&C growth, film demand,
pricing, commodity pass-through, plant utilization, reinvestment rate, debt service,
and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks
whether visual-production demand, manufacturing inputs, silver, aluminum, FX,
legacy obligations, and refinancing can preserve liquidity through a transition.

## Promotion boundary

`eastman-kodak-print-materials-qualified; legacy-transition-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from Print and AM&C activity to
consumable collections, installed-system usage, input costs, inventory, required
plant/R&D spend, legacy claims, debt, and diluted common residual. Revenue, gross
profit, operational EBITDA, guidance, turnaround claims, and buybacks remain
diagnostic inputs.
