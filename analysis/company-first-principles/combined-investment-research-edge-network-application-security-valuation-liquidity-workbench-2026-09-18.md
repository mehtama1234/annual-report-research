# Edge network and application-security valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Cloudflare from edge-platform evidence into a network
capacity, customer-retention, margin, dilution, and common-cash test. It keeps
Cloudflare distinct from data-cloud consumption, endpoint security, and
enterprise workflow software.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cloudflare | Retained platform contribution after bandwidth, colocation, servers, support, security, R&D, sales, restructuring, leases, SBC, debt, and dilution | Global network capacity, software, Workers/edge execution, security systems, technical labor, customer support, and product expansion | Traffic or customer-budget slowdown, gross-margin compression, security incident, RPO non-conversion, debt/lease burden, or recurring restructuring | Revenue/RPO and non-GAAP margin rise while gross margin, GAAP operating leverage, diluted cash per share, or network returns deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$2.168B`, operating cash flow `$603M`, and reported FCF
  `$261M` after PP&E and capitalized internal-use software.
- FY2025 GAAP operating loss was `$207M`; SBC was `$451M`, about `21%` of
  revenue and `75%` of operating cash flow. FY2025 new notes proceeds were
  `$2B`.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$696.1M`, gross margin `71.8%`, FCF `$56.4M`, and RPO
  approximately `$2.732B`; current contract liabilities were about `$812M`.
- The agentic-AI-first restructuring reduced the workforce by approximately
  `1,100` employees. Its benefit must be tested against severance, technical
  capacity, customer support, reliability, and future margin.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile gross margin after bandwidth, colocation, servers, compute, leases,
   and support labor by product and traffic/workload cohort.
2. Treat expensed and capitalized SBC as a recurring labor claim; reconcile
   diluted shares and cash per share rather than accepting FCF alone.
3. Test RPO and contract liabilities for conversion, renewal, implementation,
   collection, support cost, and customer concentration.
4. Separate organic platform attachment from bundling across security, Workers,
   payments, and machine/AI traffic.
5. Track restructuring cash, lost technical capacity, service quality, and
   whether “one-time” charges recur as the operating model changes.
6. Include debt, leases, network commitments, acquisitions, and infrastructure
   replacement before promoting non-GAAP operating income or FCF.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test converts customer retention, RPO
conversion, traffic/workload, platform attachment, gross margin, technical
labor, network reinvestment, SBC, and diluted shares into owner cash. The Lyn
Alden-style stress test asks whether internet security and availability remain
essential while enterprise budgets, bandwidth/compute costs, rates, and global
infrastructure obligations pressure liquidity. Network control can be durable
without being asset-light in economic terms.

## Promotion boundary

`edge-network-qualified; network-return-and-diluted-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from customer retention, RPO
conversion, traffic/workload, product attachment, gross margin, bandwidth and
network cost, support, R&D, restructuring, SBC, leases, debt, and diluted
common residual. Revenue, RPO, non-GAAP income, FCF, adjusted metrics, and
repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
