# Internet edge and security valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Akamai, Zscaler, and Fastly from first-principles
technology observations into company-specific valuation, reinvestment,
liquidity, and thesis-breaker objects. It does not treat delivery traffic,
ARR, RPO, security revenue, or reported free cash flow as interchangeable
owner cash.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Akamai | Security and distributed-cloud cash after legacy-delivery decline, edge infrastructure, CIS commitments, R&D, and customer concentration | Edge capacity, servers, bandwidth, security engineering, CIS commitments, acquisitions, SBC, debt, and delivery-network replacement | Delivery erosion, CIS contract conversion, frontier-model concentration, bandwidth/compute cost, debt, and pricing pressure | Security/CIS growth continues while delivery decline, infrastructure cost, contract conversion, or diluted per-share cash deteriorates |
| Zscaler | Recurring cloud-policy cash after data-center capacity, traffic processing, R&D, sales efficiency, SBC, acquisitions, and dilution | Cloud points of presence, bandwidth, security engineering, R&D, deferred-contract obligations, SBC replacement, and diluted shares | Enterprise IT-budget pressure, platform bundling, AI-security execution, traffic-cost inflation, acquisition integration, and rate stress | ARR/RPO and large-customer growth continue while retention, traffic economics, SBC replacement, or per-share cash weakens |
| Fastly | Programmable-edge cash after security/compute adoption, network capacity, customer concentration, R&D, SBC, and dilution | Edge servers, bandwidth, software development, security/observability investment, customer concentration, SBC, and liquidity runway | Top-customer loss, usage/pricing pressure, thin cash generation, edge-capacity cost, and security/compute transition | Revenue and RPO growth continue while top-ten concentration, net retention, security/compute mix, or cash conversion deteriorates |

## Current evidence anchors

- Akamai FY2025 revenue was `$4.208B`, including `$2.243B` security,
  `$1.257B` delivery, and `$708M` compute revenue; operating cash flow was
  `$1.519B`. Its Q1 2026 CIS commitment from a frontier-model provider was
  `$1.8B`, and year-to-date multi-year CIS contracts exceeded `$2.8B` by Q2.
- Zscaler FY2025 revenue was `$2.6731B`, operating cash flow `$972.5M`, and
  approximate capex-only free cash flow `$808.2M`. FY2026 Q3 ARR was `$3.525B`,
  RPO about `$6.459B`, with `748` customers above `$1M` ARR and `4,003` above
  `$100K` ARR.
- Fastly FY2025 revenue was `$624.0M`, up `15%`, with free cash flow `$45.8M`.
  Security revenue was `$125.1M`; Q2 2026 revenue was `$183.3M`, RPO `$341M`,
  and the top ten customers were `37%` of revenue.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate billings, deferred revenue, RPO, ARR, usage, and cash collection;
   future contract visibility is not current owner cash.
2. Reconcile infrastructure capacity, bandwidth, data-center commitments,
   capitalized contract costs, and technical labor before accepting software-
   like margins.
3. Replace SBC economically and carry diluted shares into per-share value;
   repurchases do not prove that recurring residual cash exists.
4. For Akamai, test whether CIS bookings convert into profitable collected
   revenue while legacy delivery declines.
5. For Zscaler, test retention, large-customer cohorts, traffic cost, and
   module expansion rather than treating ARR growth as a complete moat.
6. For Fastly, stress the top-ten customer concentration and the cash runway
   required to fund security, compute, and observability expansion.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what renewal, usage, security mix,
edge/cloud growth, infrastructure reinvestment, margin, cost of capital, and
terminal economics the price requires for each company. The Lyn Alden-style
stress test asks whether enterprise budgets, traffic volumes, financing costs,
hardware commitments, customer concentration, and AI infrastructure demand can
support the claims through a liquidity reversal.

## Promotion boundary

`internet-edge-security-qualified; contract-visibility-and-capacity-normalization-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period collection, infrastructure-capacity cost,
contract-cost treatment, retention or usage cohorts, SBC replacement, debt,
and diluted common-owner cash. Revenue, ARR, RPO, bookings, security growth,
and reported free cash flow remain diagnostic inputs.
