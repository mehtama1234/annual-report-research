# Electronic Arts live-services and franchise valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Electronic Arts from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests full-game launches, live services, net bookings, player engagement, sports loops, Apex and Battlefield franchises, deferred revenue, licensing, development costs, transaction disruption, cash, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not pool EA with Take-Two, Roblox, DraftKings, or general streaming platforms.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Electronic Arts | Franchise and repeat-participation cash after live services, full-game releases, content, player retention, licensing, development, deferred revenue, merger costs, debt, and dilution | Game development, live content, servers, licensing, marketing, player acquisition, deferred-cost accounting, debt, and shares | Franchise failure, launch delay, live-service churn, licensing costs, platform fees, merger disruption, development overspend, or leverage | Net bookings and live-service revenue rise while player engagement, retention, deferred-revenue quality, content return, cash conversion, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2026 net bookings reached a record `$8.026B`, GAAP net revenue was `$7.531B`, and operating cash flow was `$2.553B`.
- Q1 FY2027 net bookings were `$1.349B`, net revenue `$1.986B`, and net income `$397M`; live services and other revenue was `$1.472B` versus `$514M` full-game revenue.
- FY2026 live services and other revenue was `$5.383B` versus `$2.148B` full-game revenue; live services represented `71%` of Q4 revenue.
- Battlefield 6, EA SPORTS FC, Apex Legends, and the pending merger make franchise durability, release execution, and transaction disruption central valuation variables.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate net bookings, GAAP revenue, deferred revenue, full-game launches, live services, extra content, licensing, and platform fees.
2. Test player retention, engagement, seasonality, content cadence, sports-license economics, and development return.
3. Reconcile operating cash flow to development capitalization, servers, marketing, royalties, deferred revenue, merger costs, debt, and dilution.
4. Keep blockbuster launch contribution separate from durable repeat-participation cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what player retention, live-service growth, net bookings, franchise cadence, sports-license return, development investment, leverage, and cost of capital are embedded in the valuation. The stress test asks whether competition, platform fees, release delays, licensing, player fatigue, regulation, and transaction disruption preserve durable collected cash.

## Promotion boundary

`electronic-arts-qualified; live-service-retention-and-franchise-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from players and franchises to collection, retention, live content, development return, deferred revenue, licensing, merger funding, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Net bookings, revenue, live-service growth, adjusted EBITDA, guidance, and capital returns remain diagnostic inputs.
