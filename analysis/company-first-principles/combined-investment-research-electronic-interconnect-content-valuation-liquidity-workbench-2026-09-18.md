# Electronic interconnect content valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench adds Amphenol as a distinct qualified connector, sensor, cable,
antenna, and power-interconnect content lane. It separates design-in and
manufacturing economics from electronics distribution, semiconductor
manufacturing, networking control, and test equipment.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Amphenol | Specification-heavy interconnect cash after organic product growth, qualification, manufacturing, inventory, working capital, tariffs, acquisitions, integration, debt, SBC, and diluted common residual | Connector and cable plants, tooling, sensors, antennas, R&D, quality, metals/materials, inventory, capacity, acquisitions, purchase accounting, debt, and common capital | Datacom/AI cycle reversal, customer concentration, tariff recovery roll-off, inventory commitments, acquisition pricing, integration failure, debt, or dilution | Sales, orders, book-to-bill, adjusted margin, and EPS accretion rise while organic growth, collections, inventory turns, acquired return, or per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 sales were about `$23.1B`; OCF was `$5.375B`; capex was `$996.6M`; and acquisitions consumed `$3.819B`, leaving a diagnostic post-capex/acquisition residual of about `$559M`.
- Acquisition cash was `$2.156B` in 2024 and `$970M` in 2023, making cohort returns and integration a recurring denominator rather than a side issue.
- Q2 2026 sales were `$8.758B`, organic growth was `30%`, orders were `$10.7B`, book-to-bill was `1.23:1`, and OCF was approximately `$1.6B`.
- Q2 included an `$80M` net IEEPA-tariff recovery benefit; that policy recovery must not be capitalized as recurring pricing power.
- CommScope CCS contributed approximately `$871.5M` of sales from acquisition through March 31, 2026; management expected about `$4.6B` of 2026 sales contribution and `$0.30` of adjusted diluted EPS accretion.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Split organic growth, acquisition revenue, price, mix, volume, and tariff
   recovery before valuing content density.
2. Reconcile orders and book-to-bill to shipment, acceptance, receivables,
   inventory, customer deposits, and cash.
3. Track each acquisition through purchase price, acquired margin, customer
   retention, integration, working capital, capex, debt, SBC, and return on
   invested capital.
4. Separate specification-heavy qualified content from generic connector
   volume; design-in does not eliminate customer concentration or price risk.
5. Treat manufacturing capacity, tooling, quality, metals, inventory, and R&D
   as required reinvestment even when company-defined FCF is high.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what organic growth, content per
system, datacom and electrification demand, acquired-cohort return, capex,
working capital, dilution, and cost of capital the equity value requires. The
Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether AI/datacom, aerospace, defense,
automotive, tariffs, customer concentration, inventory funding, and acquisition
financing can preserve liquidity when the strongest end-market cycle slows.

## Promotion boundary

`electronic-interconnect-qualified; acquisition-and-tariff-normalization-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from qualified design-in and
orders to shipments, collection, inventory turns, capacity return, acquisition
cash return, tariff normalization, debt, SBC replacement, and diluted common
residual. Sales, orders, book-to-bill, adjusted margin, EPS accretion, OCF, and
buybacks remain diagnostic inputs rather than normalized owner cash.
