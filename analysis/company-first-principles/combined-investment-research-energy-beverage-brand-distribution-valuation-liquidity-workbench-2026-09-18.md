# Energy-beverage brand and distribution valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Monster Beverage from energy-drink demand evidence into a
company-specific valuation and liquidity object. It separates case volume,
brand/concentrate economics, bottler dependence, promotional allowances,
international mix, and the weak alcohol extension from diversified branded
staples.

## Decision matrix

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net sales were `$8.29B`, operating income `$2.42B`, and net income `$1.91B`.
- Energy-drink case sales rose `13.3%` to `959.0M` 192-ounce case equivalents, while average net sales per case fell `1.6%` to `$8.48`.
- Gross margin improved to `55.8%`, but promotional allowances, commissions, and other expenses rose `22.6%` to `$1.57B`, or `16.0%` of gross billings.
- FY2025 OCF was `$2.098B`; PP&E purchases were `$132M`, intangible additions `$25M`, and SBC add-back `$125.7M`. Share issuance under compensation plans was `$164.6M` versus `$103.6M` of repurchases.
- Alcohol Brands sales were `$134.7M` and declined `21.8%`; the segment recorded a `$127.0M` operating loss including `$53.7M` of impairment charges.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Join case growth to consumer sell-through, net revenue per case, package/mix, geography, and promotional support; case volume alone can reflect channel loading.
2. Track bottler and distributor commissions, receivables, inventory, accrued promotional allowances, supplier terms, and Coca-Cola concentration.
3. Reconcile OCF to working capital, brand investment, intangible additions, SBC, repurchases, and actual diluted shares; buybacks do not automatically offset issuance.
4. Keep Energy Drinks, Strategic Brands, and Alcohol Brands separate; repeated impairment and alcohol losses are capital-allocation evidence.
5. Test gross-margin improvement after ingredients, cans, freight, marketing, promotions, currency, regulation, and new-product launches.
6. Treat adjusted earnings, cases, market share, and buybacks as diagnostic until continuing common cash per share improves.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what case growth, net revenue per
case, gross margin, promotional intensity, marketing, international mix, SBC,
alcohol losses, and reinvestment rate the valuation requires. The Lyn
Alden-style stress test asks whether repeat consumption survives purchasing-
power pressure, input inflation, FX, regulation, bottler disruption, and a
consumer shift toward lower-priced packages or competing beverages.

## Promotion boundary

`energy-beverage-brand-distribution-qualified; sell-through-and-common-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from cases and consumer
sell-through to net revenue per case, bottler settlement, promotions,
receivables, inventory, brand investment, alcohol claims, SBC, and diluted
common residual. Cases, sales, adjusted earnings, OCF, and buybacks remain
diagnostic inputs.
