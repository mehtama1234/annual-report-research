# Engine and power-systems valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Cummins from annual-report evidence into a company-specific
valuation and liquidity perimeter. It keeps Engine, Components, Distribution,
Power Systems, and Accelera on separate lifecycle denominators and distinguishes
installed-engine reliability cash from data-center backup-power demand, emissions
transition spending, warranty liabilities, and diluted common-owner cash.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cummins engine and power systems | Lifecycle cash after truck-cycle normalization, parts/service, generator and controls delivery, manufacturing capacity, emissions compliance, warranty, inventory, transition investment, debt, and dilution | Manufacturing PP&E, tooling, product/R&D spend, inventory, receivables, service network, warranty payments, emissions compliance, Accelera cash burn, joint ventures, and SBC | Truck or freight downturn, data-center order concentration, customer bargaining power, warranty/recall or emissions cash, inventory buildup, recurring Accelera charges, rates, or refinancing | Power Systems sales and EBITDA grow while service attachment, cash collection, warranty accruals, inventory turns, emissions claims, or diluted per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$33.670B`, operating cash flow `$3.621B`, PP&E capex
  `$1.235B`, acquisitions `$12M`, and the mechanical OCF-less-capex residual
  `$2.374B` before dividends, debt, joint-venture funding, and normalization.
- Cash and short-term investments were `$3.609B`, long-term debt and capital
  leases `$6.792B`, accounts receivable `$5.818B`, and inventory `$5.822B`.
- Product-warranty accrual was `$2.778B`; 2025 product-warranty payments were
  `$730M`. Warranty is a core installed-base claim, not a minor accounting
  adjustment.
- Q2 2026 revenue reached `$9.5B`; Power Systems sales rose `19%`, Distribution
  sales `9%`, and Q2 segment sales included Engine `$3.1B`, Components `$2.9B`,
  Distribution `$3.3B`, Power Systems `$2.3B`, and Accelera `$145M`.
- Q2 first-half inventory was about `$6.4B` and receivables above `$6.5B`.
  Management raised 2026 revenue guidance to `10%–13%` growth and EBITDA
  guidance to `18.0%–18.5%`, but the cash and working-capital bridge remains
  open.
- FY2025 Accelera actions were `$458M`, including electrolyzer goodwill and
  inventory charges; the company also has a history of major emissions-related
  charges and settlement payments. Repeated “strategic” exclusions remain an
  economic denominator.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate the five segments by cycle, margin, service attachment, and capital
   burden. Do not treat Power Systems growth as recurring software-like revenue
   until equipment acceptance, maintenance, controls, and replacement cash are
   evidenced.
2. Reconcile data-center and OEM orders through price, unit volume, customer
   concentration, deposits, cancellation terms, shipment, acceptance, receivable
   aging, and cash collected. Hyperscaler demand can be real while still moving
   bargaining power and financing risk to Cummins.
3. Track warranty accrual additions, claims paid, campaign and recall charges,
   warranty duration, and failure experience by engine/generator generation.
   Historical accrual ratios may not fit emissions-complex or high-output new
   products.
4. Keep Accelera restructuring, inventory, goodwill, contract-termination, and
   severance cash in the transition denominator until the investment stops
   requiring repeated cleanup and shows a return on capital.
5. Test inventory turns, receivable days, service-network capital, emissions
   compliance, joint-venture funding, debt service, and diluted shares before
   treating OCF-less-capex or dividends as owner cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test separates mature Engine/Components cycle
cash from Power Systems growth and values Accelera as an option with explicit
future cash burn. The required return must cover manufacturing, warranty,
service labor, inventory, emissions compliance, and replacement capital—not
only reported EBITDA growth.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether physical power demand, truck
replacement, grid constraints, fuel flexibility, and installed-base service
remain funded through higher rates, freight weakness, commodity inflation,
regulatory changes, customer concentration, and a data-center capex pause.
Liquidity passes only when warranty, transition, debt, and working-capital
claims remain visible at the legal entity.

## Promotion boundary

`engine-power-systems-qualified; lifecycle-return-and-warranty-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from segment shipments and
service attachment to customer collection, inventory and supplier settlement,
warranty claims, emissions/compliance cash, required PP&E and service-network
reinvestment, transition return, debt, and diluted common residual. Revenue,
EBITDA, backlog, OCF, guidance, and dividends remain diagnostic inputs.
