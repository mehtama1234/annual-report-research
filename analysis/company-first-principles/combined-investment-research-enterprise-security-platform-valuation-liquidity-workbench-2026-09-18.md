# Enterprise security platform valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Palo Alto Networks from security-platform evidence into a
company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It keeps network/firewall,
cloud, identity, data, and security-operations products separate from Akamai,
Zscaler, and Fastly edge delivery, while testing platformization, RPO/ARR
quality, hosting and support cost, CyberArk/Chronosphere integration, SBC, and
diluted common-owner cash.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Palo Alto enterprise security platform | Subscription and support cash after firewall/cloud/identity/SOC delivery, hosting, support, threat research, customer credits, acquisitions, integration, SBC, debt, and dilution | Security R&D, cloud/data processing, technical support, sales commissions, platform integration, CyberArk/Chronosphere cohorts, working capital, SBC replacement, and repurchases | Security incident, customer churn, platform overlap, acquisition underperformance, cloud-cost compression, enterprise budget cuts, regulation, or dilution | ARR, RPO, subscription mix, and adjusted FCF rise while organic retention, cross-sell, gross margin, integration return, remediation cash, or diluted per-share cash weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$9.2215B`, subscription/support `80.5%`, OCF `$3.716B`,
  and non-GAAP FCF `$3.4698B`.
- FY2025 property/equipment and other assets used `$246M` and cash acquisitions
  `$1.054B`; the diagnostic residual was approximately `$2.415B` before SBC,
  debt, integration, and common-share claims.
- FY2026 Q3 Next-Generation Security ARR reached `$8.1B`, RPO `$18.4B`, and
  adjusted FCF `$910M`; CyberArk and Chronosphere affect comparability.
- Platform value depends on cross-sell from firewall/network security into
  cloud, identity, SOC, data, and observability—not only total ARR.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate organic ARR/revenue from CyberArk and Chronosphere contributions;
   measure acquired cohorts through retention, cross-sell, margin, integration,
   and incremental cash return.
2. Reconcile RPO and ARR to recognized revenue, renewal, customer credits,
   implementation, hosting, support, and collected cash.
3. Track gross retention, net retention, product overlap, platform adoption,
   cloud/data-processing cost, support labor, and threat-research investment.
4. Keep non-GAAP FCF separate from SBC, acquisition cash, integration costs,
   debt, buybacks, and diluted shares.
5. Include security incidents, remediation, customer concessions, legal claims,
   and trust costs in the durability denominator.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what organic ARR, retention,
cross-sell, subscription margin, platform integration, acquisition return,
reinvestment, and cost of capital the valuation requires. ARR and RPO must
convert into collected cash after support and security obligations.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether enterprises continue funding
security consolidation through recession, regulation, breach risk, cloud-cost
pressure, and tighter budgets. Liquidity passes only when customer credits,
hosting, support, acquisition, debt, SBC, and common residual remain visible.

## Promotion boundary

`enterprise-security-qualified; organic-platform-and-acquisition-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from subscription revenue,
ARR, and RPO to organic renewal, cross-sell, collection, hosting/support cost,
incident/remediation cash, acquisition return, SBC replacement, debt, and
diluted common residual. ARR, RPO, subscription mix, adjusted FCF, OCF, and
repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
