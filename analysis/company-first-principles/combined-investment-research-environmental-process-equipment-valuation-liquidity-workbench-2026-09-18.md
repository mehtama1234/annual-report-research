# Environmental and process equipment valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves CECO Environmental from environmental-infrastructure
demand evidence into a company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It
keeps engineered air, water, thermal, emissions, and process systems separate
from waste-route operators, facility services, and utility rate base, while
testing project backlog, contract assets, working capital, Thermon integration,
divestiture gains, debt, and diluted common-owner cash.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CECO environmental/process equipment | Collected project and service cash after engineering, materials, installation, customer acceptance, contract assets, inventory, acquisition integration, taxes, debt, and dilution | Manufacturing and PP&E, engineering, project inventory, contract assets, receivables, field labor, warranty, Thermon integration, acquisitions, and technology | Fixed-price project loss, customer delay, backlog cancellation, receivable/contract-asset growth, Thermon underperformance, divestiture dependence, leverage, or compliance-cycle slowdown | Orders, backlog, revenue, and adjusted EBITDA grow while GAAP cash conversion, project margin, collection, integration return, or diluted per-share cash weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$774.381M`, net income `$50.051M`, operating cash flow
  only `$5.861M`, and PP&E spending `$11.343M`, producing a negative
  `$(5.482M)` OCF-less-PP&E screen.
- FY2025 included a `$63.701M` gain on the Global Pump Solutions sale. That
  divestiture gain cannot be capitalized as recurring operating earnings.
- FY2025 receivables rose to `$172.9M`, inventory to `$54.0M`, and SBC was
  `$13.1M`; working-capital and dilution burdens are material relative to the
  reported net income.
- The Thermon combination increases environmental/process relevance and
  backlog but introduces purchase accounting, integration, comparability,
  goodwill, and synergy-realization risk.
- The filing-based cash bridge contradicts a simple adjusted-FCF narrative:
  revenue and net income improved while GAAP OCF collapsed. Backlog therefore
  remains a visibility signal, not owner cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile orders and backlog to funded, cancellable, fixed-price, and
   customer-acceptance exposures; then bridge to shipment, project margin,
   contract assets, receivables, and cash collection.
2. Keep the Global Pump Solutions divestiture gain separate from continuing
   operating profit and test whether remaining assets earn an adequate return.
3. Track inventory, contract assets/liabilities, retainage, advances, project
   claims, and customer concentration. Revenue can rise while cash is trapped in
   unfinished or disputed work.
4. Separate Thermon revenue, margin, goodwill, integration cost, synergies,
   debt, and cash return from legacy CECO economics until a post-close cohort
   return is visible.
5. Reconcile adjusted FCF to GAAP OCF, PP&E, acquisition cash, taxes, debt,
   SBC, and diluted shares. Repeated integration or transaction costs remain an
   economic burden if the portfolio requires them to operate.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what project volume, backlog
conversion, gross margin, working-capital rate, Thermon return, maintenance
capital, and cost of capital are required. Environmental demand is valuable
only when engineered systems produce collected cash after execution risk.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether industrial customers and utilities
can fund pollution, water, thermal, and energy-transition projects through
higher rates, weaker manufacturing activity, permitting delays, and tighter
credit. Liquidity passes only when contract assets, receivables, inventory,
claims, debt, and common residual remain visible.

## Promotion boundary

`environmental-process-equipment-qualified; backlog-to-cash-and-integration-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from orders/backlog to funded
conversion, shipment, acceptance, project margin, contract-asset settlement,
collection, inventory, Thermon return, divestiture separation, debt, SBC, and
diluted common residual. Backlog, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted FCF, revenue, OCF,
and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
