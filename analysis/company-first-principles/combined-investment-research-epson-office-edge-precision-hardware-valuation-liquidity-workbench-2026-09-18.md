# Epson office-edge and precision-hardware valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench tests Seiko Epson as an installed-base office-edge and precision-hardware platform. It keeps printers, ink and consumables, office fleets, projectors, industrial printing, robotics, wearables, tariffs, energy efficiency, impairment, capex, and diluted common residual separate from reported revenue and business profit.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Epson | Office-edge and precision-device cash after hardware, consumables, installed-base service, industrial/robotics development, display exposure, tariffs, impairment, capex, debt, and diluted common residual | Product R&D, manufacturing, printheads, consumables supply, fleet support, industrial equipment, robotics, energy-efficiency development, working capital, debt, and shares | Hardware price pressure, consumables mix deterioration, tariffs, China/education weakness, projector decline, impairment, supply disruption, or refinancing | Revenue and installed-base activity rise while consumables attachment, office fleet economics, segment margin, inventory conversion, or diluted per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2026 revenue was `¥1,413,344M`, business profit `¥83,822M`, operating profit `¥49,603M`, and profit attributable to owners `¥18,201M`.
- Three months ended June 2026 revenue was `¥367,241M`, business profit `¥22,122M`, operating profit `¥20,601M`, and owner profit `¥12,984M`.
- Office & Home Printing revenue rose `12.3%` in the latest quarter, helped by high-capacity ink-tank printers and office shared inkjet hardware plus consumables, while its profit fell `21.7%`.
- Manufacturing-related and wearables grew `14.7%` in the nine months ended December 2025; Visual Communications fell `13.6%`, showing material mix and cyclicality.
- U.S. tariffs and a partial Fiery goodwill impairment affected FY2026 profit, while Heat-Free Technology is a commercial replacement and procurement claim requiring evidence.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile device shipments and installed base to ink/consumables attachment, fleet retention, pricing, inventory, and collections.
2. Separate recurring print economics from projector, display, one-time hardware, impairment, tariff, and foreign-exchange effects.
3. Test Heat-Free and efficiency claims against customer replacement, procurement savings, energy cost, and required R&D/manufacturing spend.
4. Keep goodwill impairments, stock compensation, debt, capex, buybacks, and diluted shares in the common-owner residual schedule.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what installed-base growth, consumables attachment, office-fleet share, industrial-printing demand, segment margin, reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether homes, schools, offices, retailers, and manufacturers continue funding physical output tools through tariffs, education weakness, China softness, energy costs, and technological substitution.

## Promotion boundary

`epson-qualified; installed-base-consumables-and-precision-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from devices to consumables, fleet/service collections, industrial and robotics return, required reinvestment, inventory, tariffs, impairment, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, business profit, shipments, adjusted metrics, guidance, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
