# Equinix AFFO-to-owner-cash boundary — FY2025

## Purpose

This pass converts the Equinix digital-infrastructure thesis into a
denominator-controlled owner-cash bridge. It does not reject AFFO as a useful
REIT metric. It shows exactly which economic claims remain outside the company-
defined recurring-capital screen and therefore must be tested before valuation
or ranking.

## The two cash surfaces

Equinix reported FY2025 operating cash flow of `$3.911B`, recurring capital
expenditures of `$284M`, and AFFO attributable to common stockholders of
`$3.761B`. The management-style recurring-capital screen is therefore:

`$3.911B OCF - $284M recurring capex = $3.627B`

That screen is useful for comparing stabilized REIT operations, but it is not
the same as cash after all capital required by a growing interconnection and
powered-capacity platform.

The visible full-investment bridge is:

`$3.911B OCF - $4.311B property/equipment capex - $994M real-estate acquisitions - $251M business acquisitions = $(1.645)B`

The negative result is not proof that the platform destroys value. It is the
capital-allocation fact that the owner must underwrite: expansion, acquisitions,
and capacity deployment require debt, equity, joint ventures, asset recycling,
or future cash from newly stabilized sites.

## What AFFO adds back

The FY2025 AFFO bridge includes `$498M` of stock-based compensation, `$568M` of
non-real-estate depreciation, `$200M` of amortization, `$68M` of impairments,
`$33M` of restructuring, and `$18M` of transaction costs, while subtracting
`$284M` of recurring capital expenditures. These adjustments can be legitimate
for REIT comparability, but they are not interchangeable from an owner-cash
perspective:

- SBC is non-cash at grant but can dilute common owners.
- Non-real-estate depreciation and amortization may represent technology,
  systems, or other capability that must be refreshed.
- Impairments and transaction costs may be nonrecurring, but repeated charges
  are evidence about the economics of expansion.
- Recurring capex is management-defined; total property capex reveals the
  scale of growth capacity being built.

The analytical sequence is therefore:

`GAAP net income -> FFO -> AFFO -> recurring capex -> total property growth -> acquisitions -> JV/VIE and lease burden -> debt/equity claims -> per-share owner cash`

## Physical and financing burden

Equinix had approximately `$21.4B` of total indebtedness, `$1.5B` of operating
lease liabilities, and `$3.2B` of cash and short-term investments at FY2025
year-end. The filing also reports 52 major development projects expected to
deliver more than 55,000 retail cabinets and more than 100 MW of xScale
capacity through 2028, while land acquisitions support approximately 1 GW of
future capacity.

This is a substantial growth option, but it creates a timing gap between cash
spent, power secured, capacity energized, lease-up, and stabilized return. The
owner-cash model must not count a booked cabinet or future megawatt as current
cash.

## JV/VIE and attached-claim control

Equinix uses xScale and other joint ventures, including variable-interest
entities. The economic questions are not answered by consolidated OCF alone:

1. What share of project capex, debt, guarantees, and operating commitments is
   outside the parent statements?
2. What partner contributions, management fees, distributions, promote
   economics, and non-controlling interests alter the common-owner residual?
3. Does private capital lower the platform's cost of growth, or merely move the
   same burden into a different legal entity?

Likewise, utility costs, rent, facilities labor, cooling, security, and outage
obligations are recurring economic claims even when they are not separately
deducted in a recurring-capex definition. FY2025 Americas cost of revenue
included higher depreciation and utility costs, while EMEA costs included
higher rent and compensation.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans tests

The Equinix version of the project's quality-of-earnings screen is:

- **Non-GAAP add-back test:** identify which AFFO adjustments recur over a
  three- to five-year period.
- **Maintenance-capital test:** compare recurring capex with total property
  capex, uptime requirements, power density, and customer-quality obligations.
- **Lease-up test:** compare bookings and signed backlog with commencement lag,
  occupancy, customer incentives, and stabilized NOI.
- **JV perimeter test:** capture debt, guarantees, partner funding, distributions,
  and NCI outside the parent denominator.
- **Per-share test:** reconcile AFFO growth against SBC, equity issuance,
  operating-partnership units, preferred claims, and diluted shares.
- **Liquidity test:** model higher rates, power scarcity, construction inflation,
  refinancing, and slower hyperscaler deployment together.

These are forensic prompts, not accusations of accounting misconduct.

## Valuation implication

The September 2026 expectation screen used approximately `$102.0B` of equity
value, roughly 27x FY2025 AFFO and 28x the recurring-capital screen. The market
therefore needs to believe that network density, recurring customer revenue,
capacity lease-up, and xScale returns will more than offset the full capital
burden and financing claims.

The next promotion requires an incremental-return bridge by cabinet, megawatt,
site, or project, with total capital, power, lease-up, JV ownership, debt,
SBC/dilution, and stabilized cash included. Until then, valuation is an
expectation screen rather than intrinsic value.
