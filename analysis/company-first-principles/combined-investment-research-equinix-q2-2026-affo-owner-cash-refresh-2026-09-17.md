# Equinix Q2 2026 AFFO-to-owner-cash refresh

## Purpose

This refresh moves Equinix from a FY2025 diagnostic boundary to a current
first-half 2026 filing-backed cash and quality-of-earnings test. It does not
rank Equinix against Digital Realty or convert AFFO growth into common-owner
cash. The objective is to identify the exact bridge required before valuation
promotion.

## The current-period bridge

Equinix generated `$1.784B` of operating cash in the six months ended June 30,
2026. It spent `$2.834B` on PP&E and `$224M` on real-estate acquisitions. The
visible parent cash bridge is therefore:

`$1.784B OCF - $2.834B PP&E capex - $224M real-estate acquisitions = $(1.274)B`

That is not a claim that expansion destroys value. It is the funding fact that
must be matched to incremental rent, power, customer commitments, lease-up,
stabilized yield, partner capital, and financing. The company also paid
`$1.029B` of dividends during the period, so the post-growth-capital cash
surface remains dependent on debt, equity, asset sales, joint ventures, or
future stabilization.

The management-defined AFFO surface is different. Six-month AFFO attributable
to common stockholders was `$2.233B`, up from `$1.919B`, and diluted AFFO per
share was `$22.57`, up from `$19.59`. The company’s definition includes
recurring capex but also adds back SBC, non-real-estate depreciation,
amortization, impairments, transaction costs, straight-line items, and other
adjustments. That makes AFFO useful for REIT comparability and dividend
capacity, but insufficient as the sole denominator for a high-growth build.

## What changed versus the prior annual boundary

The key update is not simply that AFFO grew. The first-half filing shows:

- OCF increased only `$31M` year over year.
- PP&E purchases increased from `$1.739B` to `$2.834B`.
- Real-estate acquisitions increased from `$99M` to `$224M`.
- Receivables rose by `$255M` from the start of the year, with a `$258M` OCF use.
- Utilities increased by `$26M` in the Americas and `$13M` in Asia-Pacific,
  primarily from higher power costs and usage/power prices.
- Interest expense increased to `$299M` from `$257M`; capitalized interest rose
  to `$70M` from `$25M`.
- The company raised 2026 total-capex guidance to `$5.0–6.0B`, with recurring
  capex guidance of `$290–310M` and non-recurring capex excluding xScale and
  real-estate acquisitions of `$4.710–5.690B`.

The resulting insight is a widening gap between the recurring-capital screen
and the total-capital program. The business may be winning an attractive
capacity cycle, but the valuation needs a project-level return bridge rather
than only an AFFO multiple.

## Contract and physical build

Remaining performance obligations were approximately `$15.0B`, and the Q2
release reported `$424M` of annualized gross bookings, record additions of
`9,700` net interconnections, and 52 projects underway across 33 markets. These
are useful demand indicators. They are not current cash because the model still
has to pass through installation timing, power availability, construction
spend, customer commencement, incentives, lease-up, and stabilized utilization.

The filing also reports approximately `$6.1B` of unaccrued capital-expenditure
commitments, primarily for IBX equipment and construction, with most payable
within 12 months, plus approximately `$2.1B` of other purchase commitments,
including power. This is a material claim on future liquidity that an AFFO-only
screen can hide.

## JV, VIE, and attached claims

Equinix reported `$751M` of equity-method investments, including `$735M` in
xScale joint ventures. Several xScale ventures are VIEs and most listed
ventures have 20% Equinix ownership. The disclosed commitment table includes a
`$392M` loan commitment, `$118M` of future equity-contribution commitments, and
`$48M` of maximum future payments under debt guarantees.

The analysis must therefore distinguish consolidated OCF from the economics of
the whole project. A JV can reduce the parent’s immediate capex while leaving
the parent with funding commitments, guarantees, governance constraints,
partner economics, non-controlling interests, and only a partial share of
future cash. The next workbench must model both parent cash and proportionate
project economics without double counting.

## Financial-shenanigans and quality-of-earnings screen

The current Equinix tests are:

1. **AFFO add-back recurrence:** track SBC, non-real-estate depreciation,
   amortization, impairment, transaction costs, straight-line items, and
   capitalized interest over three to five years.
2. **Maintenance-capital perimeter:** reconcile management-defined recurring capex
   to uptime, power density, equipment refresh, customer installation duties,
   and the actual PP&E program.
3. **Bookings-to-cash conversion:** connect gross bookings and backlog to
   commencement, billing, receivable collection, incentives, and stabilized NOI.
4. **Working-capital quality:** investigate the `$255M` receivable increase,
   DSO, contract assets, deferred revenue, and any customer concentration or
   collection deterioration.
5. **JV/VIE perimeter:** capture partner contributions, guarantees, loan
   commitments, future equity funding, distributions, NCI, and project debt.
6. **Dilution control:** reconcile SBC and employee-equity issuance to diluted
   AFFO per share, forward equity sales, operating-partnership units, and any
   other common-owner claims.
7. **Power and lease burden:** keep utility inflation, lease payments, renewal
   options, and construction commitments visible; do not subtract lease expense
   twice, but do not omit contractual claims.
8. **Liquidity stress:** test higher rates, refinancing, power scarcity,
   construction inflation, slower hyperscaler deployment, and weaker lease-up
   together.

These are forensic controls, not allegations of misconduct. The main risk is
denominator drift: a recurring AFFO measure is treated as if it already
contains all capital required to grow the platform.

## Valuation consequence

Equinix’s Q2 release raised 2026 AFFO guidance to `$4.240–4.300B` and diluted
AFFO per share to `$42.69–43.29`, while also guiding to `$5.0–6.0B` of total
capital expenditures and approximately `$2.039B` of cash dividends. This means
the market is underwriting a substantial conversion from current growth spend
to future stabilized cash. The valuation workbench should therefore use:

`stabilized common-owner cash = operating cash after power/lease claims - recurring capex - sustaining growth capital - financing claims - dilution`

and separately show the return on incremental cabinets, megawatts, or projects.
Until that workbench exists, Equinix remains qualified and diagnostic, with no
promotion or cross-company ranking.
