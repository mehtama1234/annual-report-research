# ETF and index asset-management valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Invesco from annual-report evidence into a company-specific fee, flow, product-concentration, liquidity, and common-owner residual test. It keeps ETF/index scale, QQQ dependence, active/fixed-income runoff, China JV economics, private markets, and capital returns separate from Franklin's product-migration case and Prudential's liability-backed PGIM model.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Invesco | Fee cash after average fee-paying AUM, product flows, fee rates, distribution, compensation, technology, China JV, private-market marks, impairment, debt, and dilution | ETF/index product support, active/fixed-income research, distribution, technology, compliance, private-market capability, China JV, and product migration | Market drawdown, QQQ outflow/underperformance, fee compression, active runoff, China restrictions, private-market marks, acquisition impairment, or fixed costs | AUM and adjusted earnings rise while broad flows, fee rates, QQQ economics, distributable JV cash, or per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 ending AUM was `$2.170T`, average AUM `$2.000T`, total net flows `$104.4B`, operating revenue `$6.377B`, adjusted operating margin `33.4%`, and adjusted diluted EPS `$2.03`.
- ETFs and Index AUM was `$630.2B`; QQQ AUM `$407.2B`; China JV AUM `$132.5B`; private-markets AUM `$130.7B`. These pools have different fee, flow, liquidity, and ownership economics.
- Q2 2026 revenue was approximately `$1.826B`, adjusted operating margin `37.5%`, and net long-term inflows about `$45.1B`; strong current flows still require product-level attribution.
- Q4 2025 included an approximately `$1.8B` non-cash intangible impairment. It is not current-period cash, but it is evidence about acquisition value and capital allocation.
- Capital returns must be tested against technology, distribution, product investment, debt, stock compensation, share count, and the durability of fee-paying cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile average and ending AUM to market appreciation, gross/net flows, fee-paying AUM, fee rate, product mix, performance revenue, and expenses.
2. Separate ETF/index, active equity, fixed income, multi-asset, China JV, and private-market flows, fee rates, performance, compensation, and distribution cost.
3. Test QQQ concentration through benchmark underperformance, redemptions, fee rate, distribution, tracking quality, and revenue sensitivity.
4. Separate China JV and private-market fee cash from AUM, marks, partner economics, lockups, realizations, currency, and repatriation constraints.
5. Reconcile adjusted EPS to GAAP, impairment, amortization, restructuring, integration, SBC, cash flow, capital returns, and diluted shares.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style test asks what fee-paying AUM, product flows, fee rates, operating margin, QQQ concentration, technology/distribution costs, tax, and capital returns the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test focuses on market declines, savings and retirement flows, rates, credit, liquidity, China regulation, and private-market marks.

## Promotion boundary

`etf-index-asset-management-qualified; fee-flow-concentration-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period joins from product-level flows and average fee-paying AUM to fee rates, revenue, recurring talent/distribution/technology cost, China JV cash, private-market economics, impairment, debt, and diluted common residual. AUM, adjusted earnings, market appreciation, dividends, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
