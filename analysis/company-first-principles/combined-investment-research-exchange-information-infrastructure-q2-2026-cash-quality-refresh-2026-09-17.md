# Exchange and information infrastructure Q2 2026 cash-quality refresh

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## First-principles question

What part of financial activity is being monetized: trading and clearing
liquidity, or trusted information, ratings, indexes, and data workflows?

## Current-period observations

| Company / model | H1 2026 observation | What it tests | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| CME Group, exchange/clearing | Revenue `$3.586B`; clearing and transaction fees `$2.895B`; market data `$462.2M`; operating cash `$2.207B`; Federal Reserve cash account `$138.5B`; performance-bond/guaranty-fund contributions decreased `$1.545B` | Volume/rate mix, collateral perimeter, clearing-liquidity timing, data monetization | Exchange-owner cash independent of collateral flows, default risk, or capital requirements |
| S&P Global, information/rating/index platform | Revenue increased `10%`; operating cash `$2.476B`; capex `$65M`; acquisitions `$26M`; disposition proceeds `$361M`; free cash flow `$2.249B`; amortization of intangibles `$551M`; SBC `$95M` | Recurring subscriptions versus transactions, acquisition/disposition timing, intangible and SBC burden | Durable organic owner cash or value creation from disposed/acquired assets |

## Interpretation

CME monetizes a network whose usefulness depends on trusted price discovery,
clearing, collateral, and liquidity. Its huge collateral balances are part of
the market's risk-control machinery, not automatically corporate cash. Higher
volume and reinvestment income can lift operating cash while collateral
movements make financing cash volatile.

S&P Global monetizes information and institutional decisions: ratings,
benchmarks, indexes, data, analytics, and workflow products. Its cash profile
must separate subscriptions, transaction-linked activity, acquisitions,
dispositions, amortization, and working-capital movements. The `$361M`
disposition inflow and `$26M` acquisition outflow are not interchangeable
evidence of organic recurring cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

- Do not treat CME collateral or guaranty-fund balances as unrestricted cash.
- Do not infer durable exchange economics from volume alone; test rate per
  contract, default-fund movements, capital requirements, and technology spend.
- Do not treat S&P free cash flow as comparable to CME operating cash or as
  organic owner cash while disposition proceeds, acquisition effects, and
  working-capital changes are material.
- Do not treat amortization of purchased intangibles as a universal add-back;
  test acquisition replacement and retention economics.
- Treat SBC, repurchases, debt, and noncontrolling-interest distributions as
  separate per-share and owner-cash claims.

These are reconciliation controls, not allegations of misconduct.

## Decision

`exchange-information-control-point-visible; collateral-acquisition-and-organic-cash-reconciliation-open`

The lane adds a distinct control-point theme—market liquidity and trusted
information—but the two models remain incomparable for ranking. No collateral
balance, adjusted free cash flow, or market-data growth is promoted to
normalized owner cash.
