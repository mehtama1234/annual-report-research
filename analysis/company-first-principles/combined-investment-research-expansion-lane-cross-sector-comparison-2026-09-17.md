# Combined Investment Research: Expansion-Lane Cross-Sector Comparison

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This comparison extends the original three-pilot comparison into the next
sector lanes. It is a comparison of economic structure and proof readiness,
not a recommendation or a cross-sector ranking. The companies are not
mechanically comparable: the correct numerator changes from regulated
customer recovery to project cash, statutory investment income, installed-base
utilization, or fleet lifecycle economics.

## What carries across sectors

The same five questions apply in every lane:

1. What scarce control point does the company actually own?
2. Who pays, and which party performs or funds the difficult work?
3. Which denominator is economically valid for this business model?
4. Which QoE or financial-shenanigans diagnostic could make the reported result look stronger than the owner result?
5. What filing, settlement, certificate, project schedule, or legal-entity record would promote or break the thesis?

The answer must remain sector-specific. OCF less capex is only a diagnostic
for a utility, REIT, contractor, distributor, insurer, or fleet business until
the capital definition, legal claims, and cash destination are reconciled.

## Sector-specific forensic controls

- **Power grid:** do not convert approved recovery, signed load, or project capacity into billed and collected customer cash. Reconcile paid capex, CWIP, AFUDC, regulatory balances, tariffs, in-service dates, customer deposits, and shared ownership.
- **Digital infrastructure:** do not pool AFFO/FFO or treat bookings and backlog as stabilized cash. Separate development, recurring, power, leasing, JV/VIE, partner, preferred, OP-unit, and ATM-equity claims.
- **Industrial uptime:** separate organic backlog from acquisitions and unsigned awards; test percentage-of-completion estimates, retainage, contract assets, inventory, receivables, supplier finance, fleet replacement, resale gains, and borrowing-base availability.
- **Medical devices:** separate placement from utilization, procedures from collections, acquired revenue from acquired return, and “one-time” recall/integration/structural charges from recurring claims. Carry SBC, leases, inventory, and dilution into the common-owner bridge.
- **Healthcare distribution:** normalize working capital and supplier financing; separate legal settlements, acquisitions, specialty-channel economics, securitization, and attached services from recurring distributor cash.
- **Insurance/private credit:** statutory interest is not borrower cash; fair value is not funded principal; a wrapper or arranger role is not lender allocation; and consideration is not receipt without custodian, trustee, borrower, or remittance evidence. Liability cost and capital treatment must precede common-owner return.
- **Physical capacity:** facility availability is not legal borrowing-base availability, gross fleet capex is not recurring maintenance capex, and project yield is not common-owner return until collateral, funding, partner, and replacement claims are visible.

## Decision surface

The expansion lanes are useful as differentiated research instruments, not as
a pooled ranking:

- Power grid is the strongest route for testing regulated recovery and large-load payment mechanisms.
- Digital infrastructure is the clearest test of whether demand and backlog become project-level cash after power and funding claims.
- Industrial uptime offers the richest cross-business QoE contrast because contractor, distributor, and fleet denominators differ materially.
- Medical devices offers the cleanest installed-base/utilization test but still needs downstream collection and acquisition-return joins.
- Healthcare distribution tests whether scale and working-capital velocity survive legal, financing, and acquisition burdens.
- Insurance/private credit provides the deepest legal-entity and liability-allocation problem; it should not be ranked on statutory income or fair value.
- Physical capacity is the shared stress lens for replacement, collateral, funding access, and delayed conversion.
- Integrated oil and gas is the clearest test of price-versus-volume earnings quality, reserve replacement, and whether integrated scale actually protects common-owner cash through a commodity cycle.

Current conclusion: all nine lanes are `qualified` or `qualified-expectation`
research cases. None should be promoted to a normalized cross-sector owner-cash
ranking until its lane-specific promotion object is closed. This preserves the
larger goal while moving the research into additional sectors without
substituting theme breadth for cash proof.
