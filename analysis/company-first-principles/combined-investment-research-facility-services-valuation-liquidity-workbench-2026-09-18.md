# Facility services and building operations valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench adds ABM Industries as a distinct outsourced-facility-operations
lane. It keeps labor-intensive site execution, technical services, aviation,
parking, customer collection, and acquisition obligations separate from
industrial distribution, construction, environmental services, and recurring
route businesses.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ABM Industries | Contracted facility-operations cash after staffing, wage recovery, claims, receivables, equipment, contract transitions, acquisitions, contingent consideration, debt, and dilution | Site labor, recruiting/training, vehicles, equipment, technology, safety, insurance, working capital, technical capability, acquisitions, leases, and common capital | Wage inflation, labor shortages, customer rebid, unbilled work, airport/manufacturing volume, claims, acquisition funding, debt, or contract-transition loss | Revenue, bookings, and adjusted EBITDA rise while cash per employee/site, billing, service quality, acquired-cohort return, claims, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was about `$8.7B`; OCF was `$234.4M`; capex was `$79.3M`; cash acquisitions were `$36.7M`; and the simple post-capex/acquisition screen was `$118.4M`.
- ABM also paid `$59.0M` to settle contingent consideration, so the economic acquisition burden exceeds the investing-cash line.
- FY2025 OCF included a `$109.8M` use from trade receivables and costs incurred in excess of amounts billed; collection and contract-asset timing remain central.
- FY2026 Q1 revenue was `$2.2B` with `5.5%` organic growth; Q2 revenue was `$2.3B` with `6.1%` organic growth plus `2.3%` acquisition growth; Q2 adjusted EBITDA was `$131.7M`; first-half bookings were `$1.2B`.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Treat labor, recruiting, overtime, turnover, workers' compensation, safety,
   and service quality as recurring economic costs even when adjusted EBITDA
   excludes selected charges.
2. Reconcile trade receivables, unbilled costs, retainage, customer disputes,
   aging, and cash collection before treating bookings or revenue growth as
   durable cash.
3. Include contingent consideration, integration, acquired labor, retention,
   and working capital in every acquisition-return calculation.
4. Separate recurring site service, project work, transportation, aviation,
   and technical operations; mix improvement must be shown in cash per site
   and cash per employee.
5. Charge recruiting, training, vehicles, technology, insurance, safety, and
   site equipment as reinvestment even when PP&E capex appears modest.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what contract renewal, bookings
conversion, labor productivity, wage recovery, service mix, collection,
acquisition return, and cost of capital the equity value requires. The Lyn
Alden-style stress test asks whether customer facility budgets, airport and
industrial activity, wage inflation, insurance claims, working-capital timing,
leases, and debt can absorb a downturn without weakening service delivery.

## Promotion boundary

`facility-services-qualified; labor-collection-acquisition-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from awarded contracts and
staffing to customer billing, collection, claims, required operating
reinvestment, acquisition/contingent-consideration cash, debt, and diluted
common residual. Revenue, bookings, adjusted EBITDA, OCF, and buybacks remain
diagnostic inputs rather than normalized owner cash.
