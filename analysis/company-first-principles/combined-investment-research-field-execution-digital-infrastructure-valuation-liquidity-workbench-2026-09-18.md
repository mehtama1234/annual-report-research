# Field-execution digital-infrastructure valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Dycom from fiber, wireless, and critical-facility demand
into a company-specific execution, backlog, working-capital, equipment,
acquisition, and liquidity test. It is separate from upstream engineering and
program management. It does not treat MSA backlog, book-to-bill, adjusted
EBITDA, free cash flow, or buybacks as normalized common-owner cash.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dycom | Field-execution cash after organic communications/building-systems work, crews, equipment, receivables, contract assets, insurance, customer concentration, Power Solutions integration, debt, and dilution | Fleet and equipment replacement, capacity expansion, labor, facilities, permitting, working capital, surety, acquisitions, and safety/insurance | Carrier or hyperscaler capex slowdown, MSA cancellation, DSO/contract-asset expansion, labor shortage, acquisition leverage, customer concentration, surety, or claims | Backlog rises through estimated MSA volume or acquisition while starts, margin, collections, equipment utilization, cash per share, or leverage deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2026 contract revenue was about `$5.546B`, backlog `$9.542B`, operating
  cash `$642.5M`, capex `$240.8M`, and Power Solutions acquisition cash
  `$1.629B`; the recurring-capital residual was only a screening denominator.
- Q1 FY2027 contract revenue was `$1.965B`, backlog `$11.906B`, book-to-bill
  about `2.2x`, and `53.7%` of backlog was expected within twelve months.
- Q1 FY2027 operating cash used `$24.6M` while receivables used `$283.9M` and
  contract assets `$80.5M`; DSO was `96` days. Growth therefore required cash
  before customer collection.
- At May 2, 2026, cash was `$538.8M`, debt about `$2.816B`, accrued insurance
  claims about `$116.4M`, surety bonds about `$653M`, and standby letters of
  credit about `$53.6M`. Power Solutions was funded with cash, stock, and
  assumed debt, so integration and dilution remain live claims.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate master-service-agreement backlog estimates from firm fixed-price or
   modified fixed-price performance obligations.
2. Reconcile backlog and book-to-bill to customer-funded starts, organic revenue,
   segment margin, billing, collection, and cancellations.
3. Track customer concentration by revenue, backlog, utilization, pricing,
   receivables, and payment behavior; concentration is both moat and risk.
4. Bridge receivables, contract assets, retainage, contract liabilities, DSO,
   payables, and operating cash across growth periods.
5. Separate fleet/equipment replacement from capacity expansion and test
   utilization, project margin, replacement cycle, and resale value.
6. Test Power Solutions and NTI acquired revenue, retention, cross-selling,
   data-center awards, margin, integration cost, debt, and cash return.
7. Charge insurance, safety, surety, leases, SBC, interest, buybacks, and
   diluted shares before promoting residual cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what organic revenue, backlog quality,
crew and equipment utilization, segment margin, collection, acquisition return,
and cost of capital the equity price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test
asks whether carrier capex, hyperscaler projects, BEAD timing, rates, labor,
permitting, and customer financing can support starts and collections while
acquisition debt remains serviceable.

## Promotion boundary

`field-execution-digital-infrastructure-qualified; backlog-conversion-and-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period joins from backlog and awards to starts, organic
revenue, project margin, customer funding, billing, collection, equipment
utilization, acquisition return, debt, insurance, and diluted common residual.
MSA backlog, book-to-bill, adjusted metrics, FCF, dividends, and repurchases
remain diagnostic inputs.
