# First Quantum copper restart valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves First Quantum from current-period evidence into a company-
specific valuation, mine-plan, restart, jurisdiction, hedge, capex, leverage, and
common-owner cash test. It does not pool Cobre Panama, Zambia, nickel, and copper
assets into a generic miner multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First Quantum | Collected copper and nickel cash after grade, throughput, smelting, fuel, FX, hedges, royalties, stockpile processing, sustaining capital, development, debt, claims, and dilution | Kansanshi S3, Sentinel and Enterprise mine plans, Cobre Panama restart, Taca Taca/La Granja, smelters, sustaining capex, inventory, and environmental/social obligations | Panama approval or restart delay, grade decline, fuel/power, Zambian FX, hedge loss, capex overrun, social license, refinancing, or dilution | Production and copper price rise while restart cash, grade/throughput, cost, hedge, debt service, jurisdiction, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- 2025 production was `396kt` of copper, `152koz` of gold, and `23.2kt` of nickel; Kansanshi benefited from S3 while Sentinel faced lower grades and maintenance.
- Q2 2026 sales revenue was `$1.522B`, gross profit `$297M`, EBITDA `$400M`, and net earnings attributable to shareholders `$136M`.
- Q2 copper production was `100,487` tonnes; copper C3 cost was `$4.12/lb`, AISC `$4.27/lb`, and realized copper price `$5.34/lb`.
- Cobre Panama produced first concentrate from one circuit using stockpiled ore; the stockpile was described as about `38M` tonnes containing roughly `70kt` of recoverable copper.
- Q4 2025 net debt rose to `$5.192B`; the company refinanced into a new `$2.2B` term loan and revolving credit facility while Panama remained unresolved.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile mine production to grade, recovery, throughput, concentrate,
   smelting, realized price, treatment charges, royalties, hedges, and cash sales.
2. Separate Kansanshi, Sentinel, Enterprise, Cobre Panama, nickel, and development
   options by operating cash, restart status, jurisdiction, and required capital.
3. Keep sustaining and growth capex, stockpile processing, commissioning, inventory,
   reclamation, environmental/social obligations, interest, and debt repayment in
   the owner-cash denominator.
4. Test whether hedge losses, restart labels, preservation costs, and non-recurring
   charges conceal recurring economic burdens or merely shift timing.
5. Reconcile net debt, FX, intercompany claims, royalties, legal exposure, SBC,
   buybacks, and diluted shares before treating EBITDA or copper-price upside as
   common-owner surplus.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what copper price, grade, recovery,
throughput, mine life, restart timing, capex, cost curve, leverage, and cost of
capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether copper
scarcity, fuel, power, FX, geopolitics, social license, permitting, and refinancing
can preserve liquidity across a mine and restart cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`first-quantum-copper-restart-qualified; jurisdiction-restart-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from mine production and stockpile
activity to concentrate sales, collections, grade/throughput, required capex,
restart spend, debt, claims, jurisdictional obligations, and diluted common residual.
Production, copper price, EBITDA, guidance, reserves, and buybacks remain diagnostic.
