# Focused copper mine and smelter valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Freeport-McMoRan from copper-scarcity evidence into a
mine-disruption, processing, operating-rights, and common-cash test. It keeps
Freeport distinct from BHP's diversified commodity portfolio and Lundin Gold's
single-asset gold operation.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Freeport-McMoRan | Copper-producing mines, gold/molybdenum byproducts, smelters, and project options after grade/recovery, disruption, sustaining/growth capital, environmental, operating-rights, debt, minority, and dilution claims | Grasberg restart, underground development, Kucing Liar, Arizona leach, El Abra, downstream processing, reclamation, and reserve replacement | Mine disruption, copper-price decline, byproduct-price weakness, Indonesia export/rights restrictions, project overrun, environmental claims, or cash-location constraints | Copper production or unit cash cost rises while full capital, byproduct dependence, Grasberg recovery, operating rights, or common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$25.915B`, operating cash flow `$5.610B`, productive-
  asset spending `$4.494B`, and the simple post-spending residual `$1.116B`.
- Dividends were `$865M` and repurchases `$107M`; long-term debt was `$9.379B`,
  cash `$4.173B`, raw-materials and supplies inventory `$2.738B`, and
  asset-retirement obligations `$3.836B`.
- FY2025 capital included about `$2.3B` for major mining projects and `$0.6B`
  for downstream processing. Gold and molybdenum byproducts reduce reported
  copper unit costs but expose cash to three commodity prices.
- The Grasberg mud-rush incident and phased restart demonstrate that resources
  and reserves are not the same as reliable production. Indonesia export-
  proceeds rules also make cash location and legal access relevant.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Recalculate copper cost using conservative gold and molybdenum credits,
   sustaining capital, environmental, taxes, royalties, and minority claims.
2. Track Grasberg tonnes, grade, recovery, availability, restart capital,
   insurance, inventory, shipments, and cash—not production alone.
3. Separate sustaining capital from Kucing Liar, Arizona leach, El Abra,
   downstream, and other growth projects; probability-weight project returns.
4. Test Indonesian operating rights, export rules, government participation,
   permits, community obligations, cash restrictions, and debt access.
5. Reconcile environmental and asset-retirement obligations to dividends,
   debt reduction, and common residual.
6. Treat copper demand, reserves, unit net cash cost, adjusted earnings,
   dividends, and future projects as diagnostic until full-cycle cash is joined.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test values producing mines, byproducts,
smelters, reserve replacement, projects, and operating-rights options separately
using mid-cycle prices, recovery, grade, sustaining capital, taxes, and closure.
The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether copper scarcity, electrification,
and replacement-cost inflation offset mine disruption, permitting, energy,
geopolitical, environmental, and capital-cycle risk. Physical scarcity raises
long-run option value without guaranteeing near-term owner cash.

## Promotion boundary

`focused-copper-qualified; mine-restart-and-full-cost-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from production, grade,
recovery, realized copper/byproduct prices, full costs, restart and growth
capital, operating rights, environmental claims, cash location, debt,
minorities, and diluted common residual. Reserves, unit cash cost, FCF,
dividends, and project options remain diagnostic inputs.
