# Franchise media and experiences valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Walt Disney into a company-specific franchise-media,
streaming, sports, and experiences valuation/liquidity object. It keeps Disney
separate from travel platforms, advertising measurement, consumer staples,
membership franchises, and transaction marketplaces. The object tests whether
IP and fan relationships convert after content, sports rights, streaming
technology, parks/cruise/property capex, labor, corporate costs, debt, taxes,
dividends, buybacks, and diluted common claims.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Walt Disney | Consolidated franchise cash across Entertainment, Sports, and Experiences after content and rights, streaming delivery, advertising, parks/cruise/property maintenance and growth, corporate costs, debt, taxes, NCI, and diluted common residual | Content production and amortization, sports rights, technology, marketing, parks/resorts/cruise capacity, maintenance/safety, labor, deferred obligations, acquisitions, debt, and buybacks | Lower attendance/per-capita spend, advertising or subscriber weakness, rights inflation, failed content, labor/fuel/weather/FX, safety event, capex intensity, refinancing, and regulatory/distribution pressure | Segment income, subscribers, film releases, park attendance, adjusted EPS, OCF, or buybacks rise while content/rights returns, streaming contribution, experiences renewal, or per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$94.4B`, OCF `$18.1B`, and investments in parks and
  other property `$8.024B`, leaving a mechanical `$10.077B` residual before
  content, acquisitions, financing, and other claims.
- FY2025 segment operating income was Entertainment `$4.674B`, Sports `$2.882B`,
  and Experiences `$9.995B`; Experiences produced more than half of segment
  operating income while carrying the largest property, labor, cruise, and
  maintenance burden.
- FY2025 corporate/unallocated costs were `$1.646B` and restructuring/
  impairment charges `$819M`; segment income cannot be added directly to common
  owner cash.
- Disney spent `$3.5B` on repurchases and `$1.803B` on dividends in FY2025 while
  also funding content, rights, parks, cruise, and debt obligations.
- Q3 2026 revenue was `$25.2B` and total segment operating income `$5.6B`;
  Experiences operating income grew about 20%, but the period included a tariff
  refund benefit and timing effects. Entertainment SVOD revenue grew 11% with
  reported margin of 13%; streaming contribution still requires full content,
  marketing, and technology cost.
- FY2026 capital spending was expected at about `$9B`, primarily for Experiences.
  Maintenance, safety, capacity growth, cruise, and pre-opening spending need
  separate treatment.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate streaming subscription, advertising, sports rights, theatrical,
   licensing, parks, hotels, cruises, and merchandise economics.
2. Reconcile content cash spending, capitalized content, amortization,
   impairments, release timing, rights commitments, and downstream franchise
   returns.
3. Split Experiences maintenance, safety, technology, capacity growth, cruise,
   and pre-opening capital; do not treat all property spending as growth.
4. Remove tariff refunds, restructuring, impairments, acquisition items, and
   other exceptional effects only after testing whether they recur in the
   operating model.
5. Keep dividends and buybacks subordinate to content, rights, parks, cruise,
   debt, NCI, taxes, and common-owner residual requirements.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what streaming contribution after
content, sports-rights economics, advertising yield, park attendance,
per-capita spending, capacity returns, corporate costs, and cost of capital the
price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether discretionary
travel, advertising, sports rights, labor, fuel, weather, FX, rates, and safety
obligations preserve liquidity across a household and capital-market downturn.

## Promotion boundary

`franchise-media-experiences-qualified; content-rights-and-capacity-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from audience, content,
rights, attendance, and guest spending to collection, content/rights cost,
maintenance and growth capital, corporate costs, debt, NCI, taxes, diluted
shares, and common-owner residual. Segment income, subscribers, film releases,
park attendance, OCF, FCF, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
