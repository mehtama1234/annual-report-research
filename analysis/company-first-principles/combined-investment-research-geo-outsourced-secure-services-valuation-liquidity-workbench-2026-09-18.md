# GEO outsourced secure-services valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves The GEO Group from protection-services evidence into a
company-specific valuation object. It separates secure facilities, processing,
reentry, transportation, electronic monitoring, case management, healthcare,
government contracts, idle capacity, labor, occupancy, debt, legal claims, and
diluted common residual. It does not treat beds, participants, revenue,
adjusted EBITDA, operating cash flow, free cash flow, guidance, or repurchases
as normalized owner cash.

## Decision matrix

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GEO Group | Government-outsourced secure-services cash after contract collection, facility occupancy, labor, transportation, electronic monitoring, case management, healthcare, compliance, capex, debt, claims, and dilution | Facility maintenance and activation, secure transport, monitoring hardware and software, staffing, healthcare, reentry programs, compliance, working capital, and debt reduction | Contract loss or cancellation, policy change, idle-capacity persistence, labor inflation, facility or safety event, government payment delay, legal claims, debt refinancing, or dilution | Revenue and adjusted EBITDA grow while contract collection, occupancy, labor productivity, capacity activation, monitoring economics, claims, leverage, or diluted per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was about `$2.63B`, adjusted EBITDA `$464.4M`, year-end cash
  about `$70M`, and total debt about `$1.65B`; repurchases were about `$90.6M`.
- Q2 2026 revenue grew `15%` to about `$732.1M`, adjusted EBITDA grew `20%`
  to `$142.0M`, debt fell to about `$1.54B`, and trailing net leverage fell
  below `3.0x`.
- The business includes secure facilities, processing, reentry, transport,
  electronic monitoring, case management, and correctional healthcare; it is an
  institutional capacity-and-control platform rather than a single custody
  contract.
- Q2 added five-year support contracts for the `1,188`-bed Big Horn Facility
  and `1,320`-bed Rivers Facility, but contracted revenue still depends on
  staffing, government collection, compliance, and facility execution.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile awarded and activated contracts to beds, occupancy, staffing,
   government collection, payment terms, and cash.
2. Separate secure-facility revenue from transportation, electronic monitoring,
   case management, reentry, and healthcare; test participant counts and device
   deployments against service cost and collection.
3. Keep idle-capacity activation, new-contract guidance, policy-driven demand,
   and one-time claims or settlements separate from recurring economics.
4. Connect labor, safety, healthcare, facility maintenance, compliance, and
   transport costs to the capacity required to preserve contracts.
5. Treat repurchases and debt reduction as residual claims only after facilities,
   labor, claims, government receivables, and service obligations are funded.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what contract activation, occupancy,
participant growth, labor productivity, monitoring attachment, renewal, capex,
and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks
whether public budgets, enforcement priorities, government payment, labor,
policy, rates, and legal claims remain liquid through a contract or political
shock without confusing capacity optionality with owner cash.

## Promotion boundary

`geo-qualified; outsourced-secure-services-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from contracts and participants to
collection, occupancy and staffing, transport and monitoring delivery, healthcare
and compliance cost, capacity activation, debt, claims, and diluted common
residual. Beds, participants, revenue, adjusted EBITDA, OCF, FCF, guidance, and
repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
