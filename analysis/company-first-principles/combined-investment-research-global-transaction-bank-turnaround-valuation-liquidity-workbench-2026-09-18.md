# Global transaction-bank turnaround valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Citigroup from turnaround and global-network evidence into
a services, markets, legacy-asset, control-cost, capital, and common-return
test. It keeps Citi distinct from Bank of America's domestic deposit-franchise
case, private-credit funds, card specialists, and industrial owner-cash models.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Citigroup | Services, Markets, Banking, Wealth, and U.S. Consumer Cards earnings after credit, global controls, technology, compensation, transformation, liquidity, CET1, divestitures, and dilution | Transaction/payment systems, custody/settlement, compliance, cyber, technology, control remediation, employee cost, credit reserves, and capital retention | Deposit/funding stress, market shock, card losses, legacy-asset runoff, divestiture residuals, regulatory remediation, capital rules, or currency/liquidity | Revenue, EPS, RoTCE, or buybacks rise while Services economics, control cost, credit quality, CET1, liquidity, or through-cycle common return deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue net of interest expense was `$85.2B`, net income `$14.3B`,
  RoTCE `7.7%` reported and `8.8%` excluding notable items, and CET1 `13.2%`.
- FY2025 provision for credit losses and benefits/claims was `$10.3B`; Q2 2026
  revenue was `$24.8B`, net income `$5.8B`, RoTCE `13.0%`, and CET1 `12.8%`.
- Citi returned more than `$17.6B` in 2025 and repurchased approximately
  `$4.0B` in Q2 2026. Capital declined as payouts, risk-weighted assets, and
  Banamex/Russia transactions offset earnings.
- Q2 2026 average high-quality liquid assets were roughly `$641B`, average net
  outflows `$568B`, LCR `113%`, and available liquidity resources about `$1.1T`.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate Services recurring fees/spreads, Markets cycle, Banking issuance,
   Wealth flows, and USCC card economics before normalizing earnings.
2. Keep transformation, control remediation, technology, legal, separation,
   and stranded costs visible if they recur.
3. Reconcile card and corporate credit migration, reserves, charge-offs,
   delinquencies, payment rates, rewards, and capital by product.
4. Test divestiture proceeds, residual costs, released capital, tax, and
   continuing-business organic return.
5. Join liquidity, HQLA, outflows, collateral, CET1, RWA growth, stress capital,
   subsidiary dividends, distributions, and diluted shares.
6. Treat RoTCE, EPS, revenue, buybacks, and quarterly Markets strength as
   diagnostic until target-state returns persist through a cycle.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test values Services, Markets, Banking, Wealth,
and USCC separately using normalized revenue, credit losses, control cost,
technology, capital, liquidity, and RoTCE. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks
 whether a global institutional network can maintain dollar liquidity, client
settlement, sanctions/control systems, and capital through rates, recession,
currency, and market shocks. A turnaround multiple should pay for proven
returns rather than an unproven target state.

## Promotion boundary

`global-transaction-bank-qualified; turnaround-and-capital-owner-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from Services fees/spreads,
Markets normalization, Banking completion, Wealth flows, card credit, control
costs, transformation, liquidity, CET1, RWA, subsidiary capital, dividends,
and diluted common residual. EPS, RoTCE, revenue, liquidity resources,
divestitures, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
