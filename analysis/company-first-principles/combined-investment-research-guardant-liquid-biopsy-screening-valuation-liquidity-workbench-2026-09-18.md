# Guardant liquid biopsy and screening valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Guardant Health from current-period evidence into a
company-specific valuation, test-volume, reimbursement, screening-adoption,
biopharma-data, clinical-validation, cash-burn, and common-owner cash test. It
does not pool Shield screening, oncology tests, and biopharma data into a generic
diagnostics or biotech multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Guardant | Collected oncology, screening, and biopharma-data cash after lab processing, reimbursement, denials, clinical validation, commercial channels, R&D, regulatory work, sales, working capital, debt, and dilution | Shield development and adoption, oncology assays, companion diagnostics, laboratory capacity, evidence, biopharma partnerships, and commercialization | Screening coverage or adoption, reimbursement, test cost, clinical/regulatory setback, channel dependence, cash burn, debt, or dilution | Test volume and guidance rise while reimbursement, Shield collection, oncology mix, biopharma durability, cash conversion, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Q2 2026 revenue was `$335.0M`; oncology revenue `$219.1M`; biopharma/data `$60.9M`; screening `$52.9M`; Shield tests about `66,000`; FCF about `$(69.5M)`.
- Management raised 2026 revenue guidance to `$1.34B-$1.36B`, implying growth of `36%-38%`.
- Q1 2026 revenue was `$301.7M`; oncology `$205.0M`; biopharma/data `$53.0M`; screening `$41.6M`; Shield tests about `44,000`; FCF about `$(71.2M)`.
- Q4 2025 revenue was `$281.3M`; oncology `$189.9M`; biopharma/data `$54.0M`; screening `$35.1M`; Shield tests about `38,000`.
- Shield's commercial case depends on guideline inclusion, insurer coverage, Quest/channel access, consumer awareness, clinical proof, and the ability to convert tests into collected cash at acceptable cost.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile tests to billed volume, ASP, payer mix, denials, appeals, coverage,
   collections, laboratory cost, and accounts receivable aging.
2. Separate oncology, Shield screening, and biopharma/data revenue by test economics,
   clinical evidence, reimbursement, partnership control, and recurrence.
3. Keep assays, lab capacity, clinical studies, regulatory work, commercial channels,
   patient acquisition, R&D, and cash burn in the reinvestment denominator.
4. Test whether Shield adoption reflects durable guideline and payer conversion or
   temporary campaigns, channel loading, and early-adopter demand.
5. Reconcile adjusted measures, deferred revenue, inventory, claims, debt, SBC,
   equity issuance, and diluted shares before promoting revenue growth or guidance
   into owner cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what oncology utilization, Shield screening
adoption, reimbursement, biopharma data growth, test cost, evidence spending, cash
burn, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test
asks whether household and provider behavior, payer budgets, regulation, lab inputs,
clinical proof, and financing can preserve liquidity through a diagnostics cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`guardant-liquid-biopsy-screening-qualified; reimbursement-screening-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from tests and data services to
billed collections, payer responsibility, test cost, evidence and capacity spend,
claims, debt, and diluted common residual. Revenue, test volume, Shield tests,
guidance, adjusted metrics, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
