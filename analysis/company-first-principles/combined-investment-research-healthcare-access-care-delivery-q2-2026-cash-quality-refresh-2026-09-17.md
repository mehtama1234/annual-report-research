# Healthcare access and care delivery Q2 2026 cash-quality refresh

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This refresh advances the healthcare-access and care-delivery cluster with
current primary filing evidence. It keeps payer economics, recurring treatment,
alternate-site therapy, and home/community care separate; the numbers are not a
cross-company ranking.

## Quality-of-earnings and financial-shenanigans controls

1. **UnitedHealth reserve development:** the `$860M` favorable prior-period
   development is an earnings and cash-quality input, not recurring medical
   margin. Reconcile current-period medical-cost trend, claims paid, IBNR,
   days claims payable, and the reserve roll-forward before capitalizing the
   result. The Q2 10-Q now supplies `$149.320B` of medical payments against
   `$148.847B` of reported medical costs in the six-month roll-forward, but it
   does not provide a complete claims-paid waterfall by business or legal
   entity; the `$66M` held-for-sale adjustment remains separately bounded.
2. **Cigna liability growth:** the increase in net medical costs payable from
   `$4.49B` a year earlier to `$5.09B` at June 30, 2026 must be tested against
   enrollment, medical-cost trend, claims timing, risk adjustment, and cash
   payments. The Q2 10-Q now shows `$15.825B` of Cigna Healthcare incurred
   claims and `$14.834B` paid claims, but the `$268M` favorable development and
   broader pharmacy/service and receivable-factoring perimeter remain open.
   Revenue and adjusted operating income do not settle that bridge.
3. **DaVita free-cash definition:** DaVita defines free cash flow after
   distributions to noncontrolling interests, development and maintenance
   capital, and then adds noncontrolling contributions and proceeds from the
   sale of self-developed properties. The H1 filing reconciles `$811M` OCF to
   `$396M` FCF after `$150M` NCI distributions, `$4M` contributions, `$197M`
   maintenance capital, `$75M` development capital, and `$2M` property-sale
   proceeds. This is a useful disclosed screen, but it is not automatically
   common-owner cash because debt, tax, leases, put claims, and dilution remain.
4. **Treatment volume versus margin:** DaVita's `7.2266M` treatments and
   `92,649` per-day cadence establish recurring activity. They do not prove
   payer-rate, labor, supply, center-maintenance, or patient-outcome economics.
5. **Option Care collection and buybacks:** H1 accounts receivable used
   `$37.941M` of cash while inventory supplied `$71.544M`; H1 repurchases were
   `$170.545M` against `$171.485M` operating cash before PP&E. This is a
   funding and working-capital control, not evidence of durable surplus cash.
   The Q2 filing also reports `18.5%` gross margin, `$154.9M` earned but
   unbilled receivables, and an estimated `$55M` CID therapy-mix headwind;
   therapy-level collection and payer margin remain open.
6. **Addus labor and payer mix:** H1 personal-care revenue was `$577.089M` of
   `$741.028M` total service revenue, personal-care gross margin was `28.3%`,
   and personal-care payer mix was `50.0%` state/local/other government and
   `47.3%` MCO. Addus reported `$92.376M` H1 OCF and `$145.123M` ending AR, but
   says cash was affected by the timing of AR receipts and payroll/AP payments.
   Segment growth must therefore be tested against caregiver wages, state/payer
   rates, receivable collection, and acquisition/branch costs.
7. **BrightSpring adjustment burden:** H1 operating cash of `$166.859M` sat
   beside `$50.576M` PP&E, `$42.203M` acquisitions, `$75.5M` net interest,
   `$23.169M` share-based compensation, `$1.139B` AR, and `$575M` inventory.
   The `$810.908M` Community Living divestiture proceeds are a separate
   liquidity event. Continuing-operations comparability and common-owner cash
   require an explicit bridge across pharmacy drug cost, provider service cost,
   working capital, debt, SBC, and the divestiture perimeter.

## Correct cash paths

Payer route:

`premium or plan payment -> claims and medical-cost liability -> provider/pharmacy payment -> reserve and working-capital settlement -> legal-entity cash -> common-owner residual`

Treatment route:

`dialysis treatments -> reimbursement -> labor/supplies/facility cost -> maintenance and development capital -> NCI/debt/tax claims -> common-owner residual`

The routes cannot be pooled. UnitedHealth and Cigna carry medical-cost and
claims-reserve risk; DaVita carries recurring treatment, labor, facility,
capital, debt, and noncontrolling-interest risk.

## Promotion boundary

`healthcare-access-current-period-cash-quality-updated; reserve-and-reimbursement-open; no-ranking`

The refresh improves the evidence chain, but it does not prove affordability,
care quality, normalized claims cash, reimbursement durability, or common-owner
cash. The next source objects are a payer claims/reserve roll-forward and
segment-level collection/cost bridge, plus DaVita's treatment-cohort,
maintenance-capital, debt, and NCI waterfall. Option Care now has payer-
category revenue, gross-profit, billing-delay, unbilled-receivable, and
therapy-mix controls, but its therapy-level collection/margin schedule remains
open. Addus now has payer, wage-reference, receivable, branch-density, and
acquisition/technology cash controls, but its branch return and normalized
owner-cash schedule remain open. BrightSpring now has segment drug/service,
working-capital, divestiture, debt, and SBC controls, but service-line
collection and continuing-operations common residual remain open.
