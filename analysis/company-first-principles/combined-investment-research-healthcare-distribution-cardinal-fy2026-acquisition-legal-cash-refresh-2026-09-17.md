# Cardinal Health FY2026 acquisition, legal, and owner-cash refresh

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This refresh advances the [healthcare-distribution first pass](combined-investment-research-healthcare-distribution-first-pass-2026-09-17.md) with Cardinal Health's FY2026 10-K. It tests whether improved operating cash survives acquisitions, legal claims, customer concentration, tariff-related temporary benefits, debt repayment, dividends, repurchases, and dilution.

## The cash waterfall

Cardinal's fiscal 2026 reported cash stack is:

```text
operating cash flow                         $5,174M
  - acquisitions                            $1,991M
  - property and equipment capex               $649M
  = residual after visible reinvestment     $2,534M
```

This `$2.534B` residual is a useful first-pass owner-cash screen, not a final denominator. It includes `$417M` of opioid-litigation cash paid because that payment is already inside operating cash flow; it should not be deducted again. It remains before interest, taxes, lease and service claims, maintenance-versus-growth capex allocation, noncontrolling interests, and dilution.

The financing and distribution stack then shows how much of the residual was committed:

```text
residual after acquisitions and capex       $2,534M
  - treasury-share purchases                $1,358M
  - dividends                                 $491M
  - long-term-debt reduction                  $653M
  = residual after listed distributions       $32M
```

The `$32M` result is not “true owner cash.” It is a stress-screen showing that the year's reported cash improvement was largely allocated among growth, shareholder distributions, and debt reduction. Cardinal also received `$990M` of net long-term-obligation proceeds, including financing for Solaris and general purposes. Cash ended at `$4.856B`, so the company retained liquidity, but the annual common-owner cash claim cannot be inferred from OCF alone.

## Acquisition and dilution perimeter

Cardinal deployed approximately `$1.9B` for Solaris Health during FY2026. The purchase accounting also issued common units in The Specialty Alliance to physicians and management, with estimated grant-date fair value of approximately `$500M`; part is recognized as post-combination expense within acquisition-related cash and share-based compensation costs. The filing says the Solaris purchase-price allocation remains subject to adjustment.

This creates a dual return test: cash consideration must earn an after-tax, post-integration return, while retained physician/management units are an economic claim and dilution/perimeter issue even when they are not a current cash outflow. The next cohort schedule must separate Solaris revenue, segment profit, integration cost, unit compensation, call rights over remaining interests, working capital, capex, and cash distributions.

The FY2026 acquisition pool also contains ADS, GIA/Urology America, and ION. Cardinal reports `$23M` of ADS transaction/integration costs in FY2026, `$11M` for GIA/Urology America, and `$5M` for ION. These costs are not necessarily non-recurring at the portfolio level while Cardinal continues to use acquisitions as a growth engine.

## Legal and temporary items

Cardinal paid `$417M` related to opioid litigation in FY2026, versus `$798M` in FY2025. The lower payment helps the year-over-year cash comparison, but it is not proof that the legal burden has ended. The owner-cash model must carry remaining settlement schedules, payments, insurance recoveries, and tax effects.

The filing also gives a post-period legal-cash checkpoint: Cardinal paid a
sixth annual National Opioid Settlement payment of `$374M` in July 2026, and
reported approximately `$2.6B` paid to settling governmental entities through
July 2026. It expects additional payments of up to `$3.7B` through 2038. The
July payment is outside the FY2026 OCF denominator, while the longer-term
amount is a claim schedule rather than a present-period cash deduction; both
must remain separate in the owner-cash bridge.

The IEEPA tariff item is another important QoE boundary. Cardinal recorded a receivable of approximately `$200M` for expected government refunds and recognized a net operating earnings benefit of approximately `$100M` in Q4 after recording the expected customer payback expense. The filing says the ultimate resolution can affect future segment profit and consolidated operating income. This is a temporary settlement/transfer item, not a durable margin expansion or unrestricted owner cash source.

## Concentration and working-capital funding

CVS Health accounted for 28% of fiscal 2026 revenue and 22% of gross trade receivables. The five largest customers represented 43% of revenue. Cardinal's distribution economics therefore depend on large-customer contract terms, payment timing, inventory commitments, and concentration risk. The FY2025 cash comparison was also affected by the unwinding of negative net working capital associated with expired OptumRx contracts; FY2026 OCF includes normal vendor-payment timing. A repeatable owner-cash denominator requires a normalized working-capital cycle rather than accepting one year's payable timing as structural cash generation.

## Quality-of-earnings and financial-shenanigans controls

1. **Working-capital timing:** separate customer collections, inventory purchases, vendor settlement, and the OptumRx contract unwind before comparing OCF across years.
2. **Acquisition accretion:** do not infer return from EPS growth while Solaris and prior specialty acquisitions carry integration expense, physician units, call rights, and unallocated cash claims.
3. **Legal normalization:** the `$417M` opioid payment is already in OCF; test remaining obligations without double-counting it.
4. **Temporary benefit:** remove the tariff-refund/customer-payback timing effect from recurring segment margin and test the eventual cash receipt and remittance.
5. **Concentration:** test CVS contract renewal, receivable collection, margin, and inventory consequences; revenue scale is not equivalent to economic spread.
6. **Capital allocation:** reconcile the `$1.358B` repurchase and `$491M` dividend to cash after capex, acquisitions, legal payments, debt service, and liquidity requirements.
7. **Debt and dilution:** carry `$8.886B` of debt and finance-lease obligations, new debt proceeds, debt repayment, retained specialty units, and common-share count together.

## Decision

Cardinal's FY2026 cash generation is materially better than the FY2025 first-pass surface, but the improvement is not cleanly available to common owners: the company simultaneously funded acquisitions, capex, debt reduction, repurchases, dividends, legal payments, and a temporary tariff-refund/payback cycle. The `OCF less capex less acquisitions` screen is `$2.534B`; after listed repurchases, dividends, and debt reduction it is only `$32M`, before several unresolved owner claims.

Status: `fy2026-acquisition-legal-cash-updated; concentration-and-normalization-open; no-ranking`.

Promotion requires an acquisition-cohort return schedule, normalized vendor/customer working-capital bridge, opioid settlement cash schedule, tariff-refund remittance evidence, CVS concentration stress, and diluted common-owner cash. Cardinal remains a high-value diagnostic rather than a promoted owner-cash candidate.
