## Three different cash-conversion problems

### McKesson: payable-supported quarterly liquidity

McKesson's quarter is a timing and perimeter test. The company has enormous
receivables and inventory, and the payable release was not enough to produce
positive OCF. The Apollo transaction improves consolidated liquidity but creates
a redeemable noncontrolling/preferred claim in Medical-Surgical Solutions. The
decisive question is whether the segment's cash can support both operating needs
and the preferred/redemption economics while McKesson continues to repurchase
shares.

### Cencora: acquisition-funded specialty expansion

Cencora's issue is not merely working-capital volatility; it is the scale of
OneOncology and prior RCA acquisition capital relative to operating cash. The
adjusted metric removes acquisition amortization, deal costs, LIFO, litigation,
and remeasurement items. That can help compare operations, but it also creates a
large bridge that must be converted into incremental after-tax cash, not just
adjusted EPS.

### Cardinal: improved cash with many simultaneous claims

Cardinal has the strongest current OCF surface, but the allocation test is
stringent. Acquisitions, capex, opioid payments, debt reduction, repurchases,
dividends, CVS concentration, and temporary tariff-refund remittance all compete
for the same cash. Its FY2026 residual after acquisitions and capex is about
`$2.534B`, but the stress screen after listed repurchases, dividends, and debt
reduction is approximately `$32M`. Cardinal also reports a `$374M` July 2026
post-period opioid payment and up to `$3.7B` of additional payments through
2038; those legal cash claims are not folded into the FY2026 screen without
period matching.

## Cross-company QoE controls

1. Revenue is a weak denominator across all three distributors; gross profit,
   rebates, inventory risk, receivables, supplier terms, and collected cash are
   the operating denominator.
2. Working-capital releases cannot be capitalized into a permanent cash margin;
   the next period must test settlement and normalization.
3. Acquisition cash, physician/management units, contingent consideration,
   amortization, and integration expense must be kept in the return hurdle.
4. Legal credits and payments must be period-matched; credits are not recurring
   cash and cash paid must not be deducted twice from OCF.
5. Temporary tariff or LIFO items must be separated from durable margin and
   inventory economics.
6. Preferred equity, redeemable NCI, VIEs, held-for-sale entities, and other
   legal-entity claims must be allocated before common-owner cash is named.
7. Repurchases are a financing decision, not proof that the preceding OCF was
   surplus cash.

## Valuation and macro handoff

The Damodaran input is now a company-specific normalized gross-profit and
owner-cash range, not a revenue multiple. The Lyn Alden-style stress paths are:
working-capital funding during liquidity tightening; reimbursement and payer
pressure; drug-price and LIFO volatility; acquisition financing rates; customer
concentration; and legal or regulatory settlement cash. Each company has a
different transmission mechanism, so the cohort remains `no-ranking`.
