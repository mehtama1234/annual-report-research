# Healthcare distribution market-implied expectation screen — 2026-09-17

## Purpose

This screen places the three healthcare distributors on a dated market-cap
versus reported annual cash-after-capex surface. It is a Damodaran-style
expectation input, not normalized owner cash, intrinsic value, or a ranking.

Market caps and prices are the September 17 finance snapshot. Reported cash
denominators come from the FY2025 company packets. The screen deliberately
does not subtract acquisition cash from the comparable denominator; acquisition
stress is shown separately below because acquisitions may be growth,
maintenance, or a recurring feature of the business model.

company's disclosed expansion program.

## What the market may be assuming

- **McKesson:** approximately `20.0x` reported cash-after-capex requires the
  market to believe that specialty mix, working-capital discipline, legal
  claims, and capital returns preserve the cash surface.
- **Cencora:** approximately `18.9x` reported cash-after-capex looks cheaper on
  this screen, but the negative acquisition-stress residual means the market
  must underwrite acquisition returns, financing capacity, and adjusted-EPS
  quality rather than simply capitalize current operating cash.
- **Cardinal Health:** approximately `29.1x` is the highest reported-cash
  burden in this cohort; the price requires a credible post-acquisition and
  post-contract-loss cash path after debt, legal payments, and dilution.

These interpretations are inferences from the screen, not target prices.

## Promotion boundary

The screen remains `qualified-expectation-screen`. Promotion requires
period-matched working-capital settlement, acquisition cohort cash returns,
litigation and contract cash, debt and dilution, and a legal-entity owner-cash
bridge. It must not be compared directly with KLA, Equinix, Digital Realty,
retail, Wheaton, or Apollo without harmonizing accounting and claim perimeters.
