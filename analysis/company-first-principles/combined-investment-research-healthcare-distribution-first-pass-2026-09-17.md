# Healthcare distribution first-pass — cash and QoE comparison

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Question

Do McKesson, Cencora, and Cardinal Health convert essential-distribution scale
into durable common-owner cash after working capital, acquisitions, litigation,
capital spending, financing, and dilution?

This is a sector diagnostic, not a ranking. The three companies have different
fiscal year ends, segment mixes, acquisition programs, and legal claims, so the
figures below are a first-pass surface rather than a pooled quality score.

## Operating control point

The shared chain is:

`manufacturer access -> regulated logistics and inventory -> pharmacy/provider workflow -> specialty and service adjacency -> gross-profit spread -> collected cash`

Revenue is a weak denominator because product value largely passes through the
distributor. The relevant tests are gross-profit conversion, inventory and
receivable funding, supplier terms, claims, acquisition returns, and cash per
diluted share.

The mechanical residual is not normalized owner cash. It is a burden screen:

`operating cash flow - visible capex/software - acquisition or investment cash`

It does not subtract the same working-capital movement twice, and it does not
assume all acquisitions are maintenance or all are growth. It shows why
revenue and adjusted EPS cannot be compared without the cash and claim bridge.

## Quality-of-earnings and financial-shenanigans screen

### McKesson

- **Revenue pass-through:** FY2025 revenue growth must be decomposed into
  product price, volume, specialty mix, and acquisitions rather than treated
  as economic growth.
- **Cash conversion:** test whether receivables, inventory, rebates, returns,
  chargebacks, and payables contributed disproportionate operating cash.
- **Capitalization:** separate PP&E from capitalized software and test whether
  technology spending is recurring operating infrastructure or growth capital.
- **Claims:** opioid, controlled-substance, compliance, and litigation cash
  obligations are senior to common-owner cash.
- **Breaker:** revenue and adjusted EPS rise while gross profit, normalized
  working capital, or post-claim cash conversion deteriorates.

### Cencora

- **Adjusted-to-GAAP gap:** FY2025 diluted EPS was `$7.96` GAAP versus `$16.00`
  adjusted; the bridge includes PharmaLex goodwill impairment and deal or
  integration costs. The question is recurrence, not whether the adjustment is
  legally permitted.
- **Margin sensitivity:** Q3 FY2026 gross margin was approximately `4.26%`;
  small changes in product mix, LIFO, GLP-1 exposure, rebates, and inventory
  accounting can move earnings materially.
- **Acquisition burden:** RCA and OneOncology expansion must be tested against
  post-close gross profit, integration cost, working capital, and debt capacity.
- **Breaker:** adjusted EPS growth does not convert to collected cash after
  acquisitions and recurring integration costs.

### Cardinal Health

- **Contract and mix:** the expired OptumRx contract and segment mix make
  reported revenue and operating cash period-sensitive.
- **Working capital:** inventory and a reported `$2.732B` accounts-payable
  contribution require settlement-date analysis rather than a second subtraction
  from period-end balances.
- **Claims:** the filing chain includes approximately `$403M` of opioid-related
  payments in the quarter sequence; legal cash is an owner claim, not a remote
  footnote.
- **Acquisition financing:** approximately `$5.3B` of acquisitions alongside
  `$2.9B` of new long-term debt proceeds and an `$0.8B` term loan means the
  acquisition return must be measured after financing and dilution.
- **Breaker:** acquisition-supported earnings and non-GAAP EPS rise while debt,
  legal payments, and post-close cash conversion consume the residual.

These are Sloan-, Schilit-, and Beneish-style reconciliation prompts. They are
not manipulation findings, and no pooled score is calculated because the fiscal
periods, taxonomies, and claim perimeters are not yet aligned.

## Comparative interpretation

1. **McKesson currently presents the strongest reported cash surface**, but its
   scale makes revenue especially easy to misread; gross profit and claims are
   the control variables.
2. **Cencora has the clearest adjusted-earnings warning**, because the adjusted
   EPS bridge is large and acquisition cash exceeds the visible post-capex
   operating residual.
3. **Cardinal has the clearest capital-allocation stress**, because acquisitions
   exceed operating cash after capex and the program uses debt while litigation
   remains a cash claim.

This is not a conclusion that McKesson is superior. It is a prioritization of
the next evidence work: McKesson needs working-capital and claims normalization;
Cencora needs an adjusted-EPS-to-cash and acquisition-return bridge; Cardinal
needs a debt-funded acquisition and legal-cash reconciliation.
