# McKesson Q1 FY2027 cash-perimeter and working-capital refresh

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This refresh advances the [healthcare-distribution first pass](combined-investment-research-healthcare-distribution-first-pass-2026-09-17.md) with McKesson's quarter ended June 30, 2026. It tests the earlier strongest-reported-cash case against a negative quarterly cash conversion, payable timing, software investment, shareholder distributions, and the Apollo minority-capital transaction.

## The quarter reverses the annual cash headline

Q1 revenue increased 8% to `$105.4B` and adjusted diluted EPS rose 20% to `$9.93`, but operating activities used `$220M`. The mechanical post-reinvestment screen is:

```text
operating cash flow                         $(220)M
  - PP&E cash spend                           $112M
  - capitalized software                       $40M
  - acquisitions, net of cash                  $23M
  = residual after visible uses               $(395)M
```

This is not a normalized annual owner-cash estimate; it is a quarterly conversion warning. The filing says operating cash was affected by the timing of customer receipts, inventory receipts, and vendor payments. That makes the correct test a longitudinal cash-conversion panel, not a conclusion from one quarter.

## Working capital explains the warning—but does not erase it

Receivables used `$2.374B` and inventories used `$2.074B` of cash, while drafts and accounts payable supplied `$3.773B`. The net movement from these three lines was approximately a `$675M` cash use before other operating items, consistent with the negative OCF. At June 30, receivables were `$30.316B` and inventory `$26.259B`, up from `$27.985B` and `$24.207B` at March 31.

McKesson explicitly says working capital is driven by sales and purchase volumes, inventory requirements, vendor payment terms, seasonality, and receipt timing. The financial-shenanigans control is therefore to ask how much of the prior annual cash surface came from favorable vendor timing and how much of Q1's reversal is seasonal or structural. Neither the annual OCF number nor adjusted EPS resolves that question.

## Apollo transaction changes the owner-cash perimeter

On June 1, 2026, Apollo funds invested approximately `$1.25B` in convertible preferred equity of Medical-Surgical Solutions for an approximately 13% minority ownership interest. McKesson retains operating control and majority ownership and continues to consolidate the segment, while recognizing a redeemable noncontrolling interest.

This is not ordinary operating cash and not automatically common-owner cash. It is a legal-entity capital contribution that improves consolidated liquidity while creating a preferred/redeemable claim and a minority economic allocation. The model must determine the preferred conversion/redemption terms, dividends or accretion, segment-level cash generation, separation costs, and how much of Medical-Surgical Solutions cash remains available to McKesson common owners.

## Shareholder returns versus operating cash

McKesson returned approximately `$2.6B` to shareholders in Q1, including approximately `$2.5B` of common-stock repurchases and `$102M` of dividends. The quarter's operating cash and visible reinvestment screen was approximately `$(395)M`, while the financing statement reported net financing cash provided of `$1.632B`, including the Apollo minority-capital transaction.

That juxtaposition does not prove improper funding: McKesson began the quarter with cash, has debt and other financing sources, and may use a deliberate capital-allocation policy. It does prove that per-share EPS improvement and buyback volume cannot be interpreted as surplus operating cash without a period-matched funding bridge.

## Quality-of-earnings and financial-shenanigans controls

1. **Working-capital timing:** separate recurring gross-profit conversion from customer-receipt, inventory-receipt, and vendor-payment timing.
2. **Pass-through revenue:** the `$105.4B` top line is not the economic denominator; gross profit, rebates, chargebacks, inventory risk, and collected cash are.
3. **Capitalization:** keep `$40M` capitalized software separate from `$112M` PP&E and test recurring technology maintenance versus growth.
4. **Funding perimeter:** trace the `$1.25B` Apollo preferred contribution, redemption/conversion claims, segment cash, and separation costs before assigning cash to common owners.
5. **Repurchase optics:** reconcile `$2.6B` shareholder returns to beginning cash, financing proceeds, and the negative operating cash screen.
6. **Segment margin:** Medical-Surgical Solutions operating profit fell to `$122M` from `$221M`, including `$45M` of separation charges; the separation-adjusted result must still be tested against recurring segment cash.
7. **Claims:** McKesson recorded a `$34M` credit related to estimated opioid claims in Q1. A credit is not recurring operating performance or cash received.

## Decision

McKesson remains a powerful distribution-scale business, but the newest quarter weakens the first-pass assumption that scale automatically produces durable common-owner cash. Q1 has negative OCF, a `$395M` visible post-reinvestment deficit, substantial receivable/inventory funding, large shareholder returns, and a new Apollo preferred/noncontrolling-interest perimeter. The appropriate classification is `q1-fy2027-cash-perimeter-updated; working-capital-and-preferred-claim-open; no-ranking`.

Promotion requires at least two comparable periods of normalized working-capital conversion, a Medical-Surgical Solutions legal-entity cash and preferred-claim bridge, specialty gross-profit reconciliation, software/PP&E maintenance allocation, and a repurchase-funding waterfall.

now places FY2025 and Q1 FY2027 side by side without annualizing the quarter or
calling the contrast permanent deterioration.
