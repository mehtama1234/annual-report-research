# Healthcare distribution owner-cash denominator — pass 2 — 2026-09-17

## Decision

McKesson, Cencora, and Cardinal Health now have a comparable burden taxonomy,
but not a pooled owner-cash ranking. Their periods differ, and the economic
denominator is gross-profit conversion plus working-capital funding, legal
claims, acquisition returns, financing, and dilution—not revenue or adjusted
EPS.

Status: `denominator-pass-2; company-specific; normalized-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`.

These screens are not directly comparable as a ratio league table. McKesson is
one quarter, Cencora nine months, and Cardinal a full fiscal year. The useful
comparison is how each business converts scale into cash while funding its
operating and legal perimeter.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

1. **Working capital:** vendor terms and customer/inventory timing can reverse;
   test multi-period receivable, inventory, payable, rebate, and LIFO behavior.
2. **Acquisition accounting:** cash consideration, contingent consideration,
   retained physician/management equity, amortization, integration cost, and
   post-close gross profit must be joined before adjusted EPS is treated as
   value creation.
3. **Legal and temporary items:** opioid payments, litigation credits, tariff
   refunds, and customer payback obligations are period-matched; credits are
   not recurring cash and cash already inside OCF is not deducted twice.
4. **Legal-entity perimeter:** Apollo preferred/redeemable NCI, VIEs,
   securitization, held-for-sale entities, and retained specialty interests
   must be allocated before naming common-owner cash.
5. **Capital allocation:** repurchases and dividends are financing decisions;
   compare them with post-reinvestment cash and debt funding rather than
   assuming they were funded from current OCF.

## Promotion gates

- McKesson: two comparable quarters of normalized working-capital conversion,
  Medical-Surgical Solutions segment cash, Apollo preferred terms, and
  repurchase funding.
- Cencora: OneOncology incremental gross profit, integration cash, physician
  economics, working-capital settlement, debt interest, contingent
  consideration, and diluted common-owner cash.
- Cardinal: Solaris/acquisition cohort return, vendor/customer-cycle
  normalization, opioid settlement schedule, tariff-refund remittance, CVS
  concentration, and retained-unit dilution.

The sector thesis breaker is activated if revenue and adjusted EPS grow while
working-capital funding, acquisition cash, legal claims, financing, or dilution
consume the apparent owner-cash residual. This is a filing-based falsifier,
not an accusation of misconduct.
