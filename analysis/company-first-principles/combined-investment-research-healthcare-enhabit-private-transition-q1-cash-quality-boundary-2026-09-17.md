# Enhabit private-company transition and Q1 2026 cash-quality boundary

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Why this matters

Enhabit is a useful healthcare-access and care-delivery control because its
economics are driven by payer reimbursement, clinical labor, patient service,
and debt rather than by a payer's claims reserve. The public evidence has a
hard perimeter: Enhabit completed its Kinderhook acquisition on May 15, 2026
and became private. Its last public quarterly filing is therefore the March
31, 2026 Form 10-Q; no public Q2 filing should be assumed.

This is both an evidence upgrade and a stop rule. The Q1 filing supplies a
period-matched service, labor, cash, financing, and reimbursement baseline, but
the post-close private period cannot be filled with estimates or carried
forward as if it were a public-company quarter.

## Q1 public baseline

| Object | Q1 2026 observation | What it proves | What remains open |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Operating cash | `$35.2M` | Reported Q1 OCF | Sustainable cash after payer timing, labor, and settlement effects |
| Investing cash | `$(2.6M)` | Q1 investing outflow, primarily property/equipment purchases | Maintenance versus growth and clinical-capacity return |
| Financing cash | `$(26.6M)` | Debt prepayment/refinancing and financing cash are period-visible | Post-close debt, lender allocation, and owner distribution |
| Cash and revolver | `$50.0M` cash and `$37.5M` revolver availability at March 31 | Public liquidity perimeter | Restricted cash, covenant capacity, and private-company cash access |
| Attorney-fee/mitigation settlement | `$17.7M` gain reduced Q1 G&A | Non-recurring QoE item is identified | Gross cash, legal cost, tax, and recurrence |
| Home Health | `$201.8M` revenue, `$96.3M` labor, `$35.1M` segment Adjusted EBITDA | Service and labor denominator | Payer collection, quality, episode mix, and capital return |
| Hospice | `$63.0M` revenue, `$19.5M` labor, `$14.6M` segment Adjusted EBITDA | Distinct service and labor denominator | Per-day reimbursement, cap limits, patient mix, and NCI |
| Reimbursement | Pricing is generally based on payer reimbursement; Medicare changes affect results | Payer is the economic price setter | Rate realization, denial/appeal, receivable aging, and payer mix |
| CMS FY2027 hospice rule | Medicare hospice payment update is `2.3%`; aggregate cap is `$36,174.75`; failure to submit required quality data produces a `1.7%` payment reduction; CMS also publishes a nine-measure hospice service/spending variation index | A current reimbursement, cap, and quality/compliance boundary is visible | Enhabit-specific rate realization, SSVI exposure, patient mix, labor response, and cash collection |

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

The `$17.7M` settlement gain must not be retained as recurring operating
profit or subtracted from cash a second time. Adjusted EBITDA also must not be
treated as owner cash while debt prepayment, refinancing fees, lease costs,
tax, capex, and noncontrolling interests remain separate.

The public Q1 filing states that the company would no longer file periodic
reports if the merger closed. After the May 15 closing, the next evidence must
come from private-company reporting, lender materials, a transaction filing, or
another named primary source. Public Q2 revenue, labor, reimbursement, or cash
figures do not exist in the checked issuer route and must not be invented from
the Q1 baseline.

The CMS rule is a macro reimbursement input, not Enhabit cash. It supplies a
bounded stress range for Hospice revenue and a quality/compliance breaker, but
does not prove Enhabit-specific rates, admissions, labor productivity, or
collections.

## Promotion decision

`q1-care-delivery-baseline-proven; post-close-public-series-closed`

The route is qualified for healthcare access and care-delivery analysis, but it
is not a public-company longitudinal cash series after the acquisition. The
correct next source is a private post-close financial or lender package that
joins payer collections, labor, debt service, capex, and owner/ sponsor claims.
