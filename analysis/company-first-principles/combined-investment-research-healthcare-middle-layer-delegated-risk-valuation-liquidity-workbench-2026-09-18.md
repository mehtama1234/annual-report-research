# Healthcare middle-layer and delegated-risk valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Astrana Health from provider-network scale and value-based
care growth into a company-specific medical-cost, contract, subsidiary-capital,
acquisition, and dilution test. It is separate from payers, devices,
distribution, infusion, and home-care operators. Patient counts, revenue,
adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, AI claims, and acquisitions are not normalized
common-owner cash by themselves.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Astrana | Retained shareholder cash after payer receipts, medical/risk obligations, provider payments, clinical and technology labor, working capital, subsidiary capital, acquisitions, debt, and dilution | Provider-network development, clinical infrastructure, care enablement, technology, working capital, risk reserves/capital, and acquisitions | Medical-cost trend, claims/reserve development, reimbursement/regulatory change, provider liquidity, receivable growth, subsidiary cash restriction, or acquisition financing | Patient/provider scale and adjusted EBITDA rise while retained margin per patient, claims, reserves, collections, parent cash, or per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$3.1818B`, adjusted EBITDA `$205.4M`, and free cash flow
  `$104.5M`; EBITDA was about twice reported free cash flow.
- Q2 FY2026 revenue was `$972.5M`, Care Partners revenue `$932.8M`, adjusted
  EBITDA `$68.9M`, six-month operating cash `$100.8M`, and FCF `$92.9M`.
- The packet reports more than `20,000` providers and `1.6M` patients in
  value-based arrangements; those are scale indicators, not patient-level
  profitability or retained cash proof.
- Prospect integration and delegated-risk expansion make purchase accounting,
  claims/reserve development, provider retention, subsidiary capital, and
  dilution live valuation controls. The local packet did not yet include the
  Q2 2026 10-Q, so latest-quarter balance-sheet evidence remains open.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate organic membership and revenue from acquisitions, contract mix,
   pricing, and patient count; calculate retained margin per patient/contract.
2. Bridge payer receipts through medical claims/risk obligations, provider and
   partner payments, clinical/technology labor, working capital, and parent cash.
3. Test incurred-but-not-reported claims, risk adjustment, quality bonuses,
   contract assets, receivables, settlements, and prior-period development.
4. Separate Care Partners, Care Delivery, and Care Enablement economics and
   distinguish fee revenue from delegated-risk exposure.
5. Test Prospect and future acquisitions through purchase price, goodwill,
   amortization, integration cash, provider retention, organic growth, and return.
6. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA and FCF to GAAP cash, capex, acquisition payments,
   SBC, debt, restricted/subsidiary cash, and diluted shares.
7. Determine whether AI/workflow investment reduces cost per patient or merely
   supports a larger footprint and higher required capital.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what organic membership, retained
margin per patient, medical-cost trend, provider retention, contract economics,
acquisition return, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-
style stress test asks whether payer budgets, Medicare Advantage/value-based
policy, provider liquidity, clinical labor, utilization, risk adjustment, and
receivable timing can be absorbed without capital calls or parent-level cash
shortage.

## Promotion boundary

`healthcare-middle-layer-qualified; retained-margin-and-delegated-risk-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period joins from members/providers and contracts to
medical-cost and claims obligations, provider payments, retained margin,
collections, subsidiary capital, acquisition return, debt, and diluted common
residual. Patient count, revenue, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, AI claims, and
acquisitions remain diagnostic inputs.
