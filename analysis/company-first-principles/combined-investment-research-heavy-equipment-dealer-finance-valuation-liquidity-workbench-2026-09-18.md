# Heavy equipment, dealer channel, and finance valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Caterpillar from backlog and installed-base evidence into
a machine-cycle, dealer-channel, finance-receivable, and common-cash test. It
keeps heavy equipment distinct from field contractors, industrial components,
data-center systems, and commodity producers.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Caterpillar | Equipment, services, Power & Energy, Resource Industries, and Cat Financial cash after dealer inventory, finance receivables, manufacturing, warranty, capex, tariffs, debt, SBC, and dilution | Plants, tooling, service parts, dealer support, machine replacement, Power & Energy capacity, R&D, warranty, and finance funding | Industrial/commodity downturn, dealer inventory reversal, credit losses, used-equipment values, tariff reversal, capex, or funding spread | Backlog, services, tariff recovery, or adjusted margin rise while end-user demand, dealer health, finance portfolio, warranty, or diluted residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 sales were `$67.589B`, operating cash `$11.739B`, PP&E capex
  `$2.821B`, and the mechanical post-capex/acquisition screen `$8.871B`.
- Dividends were `$2.788B` and repurchases `$5.190B`, nearly consuming that
  screen; inventory was `$18.135B` and net finance receivables about `$25.191B`.
- Backlog reached approximately `$51.2B` at FY2025 year-end and about `$63B`
  in Q1 2026 commentary. Backlog still requires dealer inventory, acceptance,
  collection, service, and end-user utilization validation.
- Cat Financial additions to finance receivables were `$15.329B`, collections
  `$13.515B`, allowance for credit losses `$277M`, and write-offs `$148M` in
  2025. Q2 2026 also included expected `$392M` of IEEPA tariff recovery.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile backlog through dealer inventory, customer acceptance, end-user
   utilization, receivables, cash collection, and warranty.
2. Separate machine, service/parts, Power & Energy, Resource Industries, and
   Cat Financial economics by cycle, margin, capital, and credit burden.
3. Test finance-receivable aging, delinquencies, charge-offs, used-equipment
   values, allowance coverage, funding spread, and residual-value risk.
4. Normalize tariff recoveries, dealer support, inventory turns, capitalized
   manufacturing capacity, warranty, and service labor.
5. Compare services revenue and margin with parts inventory, field labor,
   warranty, and dealer density before treating it as recurring software-like
   cash.
6. Treat backlog, adjusted profit, services, tariff recovery, dividends, and
   buybacks as diagnostic until cycle-adjusted owner cash is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test values mature equipment, services, Power &
Energy, Resource Industries, and Cat Financial separately, using unit demand,
dealer inventory, service attach, finance losses, capex, tariffs, and the
required return through the cycle. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether
real-economy capital formation, infrastructure, energy demand, and installed-
base replacement offset rates, commodities, industrial policy, dealer credit,
and global liquidity. The ecosystem moat does not remove cyclicality.

## Promotion boundary

`heavy-equipment-qualified; dealer-finance-and-cycle-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from orders/backlog through
dealer inventory, shipments, acceptance, service, finance receivables, credit
losses, warranty, capex, tariffs, debt, SBC, and diluted common residual.
Backlog, services, adjusted profit, tariff recovery, dividends, and repurchases
remain diagnostic inputs.
