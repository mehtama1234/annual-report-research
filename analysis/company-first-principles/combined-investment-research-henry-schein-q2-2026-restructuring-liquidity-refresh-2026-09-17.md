# Henry Schein Q2 2026 restructuring and liquidity refresh

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This refresh joins Henry Schein's Q2/H1 2026 filing to the [FY2025 restructuring and liquidity waterfall](combined-investment-research-henry-schein-restructuring-liquidity-waterfall-pass-1-2026-09-17.md). It updates the operating, working-capital, restructuring, repurchase, securitization, and debt evidence. It does not promote adjusted EPS or reported operating cash to normalized owner cash.

## What changed in the period

Q2 net sales were `$3.458B`, up from `$3.240B`; H1 sales were `$6.826B`, up from `$6.408B`. The issuer's Q2 bridge attributes growth across internal growth, acquisitions, and foreign exchange: total dental sales grew 5.3%, medical 2.5%, value-added services 5.1%, specialty products 8.7%, and technology 8.2%. This is a healthier demand and mix signal than a purely acquisition-led top line, but it does not resolve the cash conversion of the distribution model.

Non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.27` in Q2 and `$2.59` H1, versus `$1.10` and `$2.25`. The same reconciliation removes `$29M` of Q2 restructuring costs and `$41M` H1, plus other items including `$6M` of Q2 and `$19M` H1 shareholder-advisory/implementation consulting costs. The adjusted result is therefore useful for operating comparison, but it is not a clean owner-cash denominator while the 2024 Plan remains active through the end of 2027 and the excluded cash, legal, and capital-allocation claims are unresolved.

## Cash conversion and working capital

The H1 cash-flow statement reports:

```text
net cash provided by operations                   $145M
  - property and equipment                         $55M
  - capitalized software                           $30M
  - equity investments/business acquisitions      $30M
= visible pre-debt residual                       $30M
```

This `$30M` is a diagnostic residual, not a final owner-cash estimate. It is before debt service, lease claims, noncontrolling distributions, contingent consideration, taxes, remaining restructuring cash, and the required liquidity buffer. It also must not deduct the `$41M` restructuring charge a second time from OCF without a cash-paid schedule.

The working-capital pressure is visible in the statement itself: accounts receivable used `$116M`, inventories used `$49M`, and accounts payable/accrued expenses used `$79M` in H1. At June 27, 2026, accounts receivable were `$1.763B` and inventory `$2.059B`; DSO increased to 45.7 days from 44.7, while inventory turns declined to 4.6 from 4.7. This is a direct quality-of-earnings control: sales growth is not yet translating into a stable cash conversion cycle, and the receivable securitization means liquidity must be analyzed together with pledged/secured receivables rather than cash alone.

## Restructuring remains a recurring cash question

Q2 restructuring and related charges were `$29M` versus `$23M` prior year; H1 charges were `$41M` versus `$48M`. The company says the 2024 Plan primarily covers severance, facility exits, and other exit costs and is expected to generate charges through the end of 2027. The lower H1 charge is encouraging but does not prove the plan is economically finished. The required next artifact is a charge-to-cash-paid rollforward by segment, including accrued liabilities and any asset-disposal proceeds.

## Repurchase and capital structure waterfall

Henry Schein repurchased approximately `$200M` in Q2 and `$325M` H1, leaving `$455M` authorized and available. The cash-flow statement also shows `$261M` of net bank-credit-line borrowing, `$144M` of long-term-debt issuance, `$50M` of long-term-debt principal repayment, and net financing cash use of `$48M`. The period therefore cannot be described as a simple “surplus cash funded buyback” period.

At June 27, 2026, cash was `$157M` against total debt of `$3.462B`, including `$1.024B` of bank credit lines, `$138M` of current maturities, and `$2.300B` of long-term debt. The U.S. trade-receivable securitization had a `$450M` purchase limit and `$430M` outstanding, backed by `$526M` of certain receivables. That facility is a liquidity source, but it is also a senior claim and collateral boundary. The owner-cash model must show the amount of receivables unavailable to common owners and the refinancing/availability risk if collections or customer credit weaken.

The KKR strategic investment remains a separate legal-capital-flow item. KKR's `$250M` investment issued 3,285,152 shares; the H1 equity statement shows issuance and the contemporaneous repurchase/retirement activity. The share-count effect, accelerated-repurchase accounting, KKR governance rights, and future authorization must be modeled together rather than treating gross repurchases as an automatic per-share gain.

## Financial-shenanigans and quality-of-earnings tests

1. Adjusted EPS rises while the H1 pre-debt residual is only about `$30M`; test whether the adjustment set is masking recurring restructuring, advisory, litigation, or cash-funded technology costs.
2. Receivables and inventory consume `$165M` of H1 cash; test whether the cash conversion shortfall is seasonal, growth-related, credit-related, or a persistent funding requirement.
3. Receivable securitization borrowings rise to `$430M`; test whether reported liquidity depends on monetizing collateral that would otherwise support common-owner cash.
4. Repurchases of `$325M` exceed the visible pre-debt residual; reconcile the difference to credit-line borrowing, long-term debt, beginning cash, and other financing sources.
5. Restructuring charges are lower year over year but the plan runs through 2027; require cash-paid and remaining-obligation evidence before normalizing them away.
6. KKR issuance and share retirement must be joined to diluted shares, not evaluated from headline buyback dollars alone.

## Current conclusion

Henry Schein shows improving sales, internal growth across the operating segments, and higher adjusted EPS, but the period also shows thin post-reinvestment cash, working-capital absorption, a nearly fully utilized receivable-securitization purchase limit, higher debt, and buybacks larger than the visible pre-debt residual. The correct classification remains `q2-restructuring-liquidity-updated; no-ranking`.

Promotion requires: a cash-paid restructuring schedule; a debt/repurchase funding bridge; a seasonal-normalized working-capital and securitization analysis; software and property maintenance/growth allocation; acquisition/equity-investment returns; and KKR/ASR diluted-share treatment. Until those are connected, Henry Schein is an informative diagnostic for the medical-devices lane, not a promoted owner-cash candidate.
