# Henry Schein restructuring, debt, and repurchase waterfall — pass 1

Research date: `2026-09-17`

The [structured waterfall](data/combined-investment-research-henry-schein-restructuring-liquidity-waterfall-pass-1-2026-09-17.csv)
separates Henry Schein's FY2025 operating cash, visible reinvestment,
restructuring burden, shareholder distributions, liquidity, and debt. It is a
control artifact, not a final owner-cash estimate.

## Waterfall

```text
operating cash flow                         $712M
  - property capex                          $139M
  - capitalized software                     $52M
  - acquisition/equity-investment cash      $199M
  = first-pass residual                     $322M
```

The `$322M` residual is before the unresolved cash portion of restructuring,
debt service, required liquidity, lease and legal claims, stock compensation,
and diluted-share treatment. It should not be reduced by the `$105M`
restructuring charge a second time unless the filing establishes that the
corresponding cash was not already reflected in operating cash flow and is
being modeled as a separate future cash obligation.

## Restructuring is not safely one-time

The FY2025 10-K reports `$105M` of restructuring and related charges under the
2024 Plan. The plan is expected to continue through the end of 2027. The
charges include severance and employee-related costs, accelerated amortization
of right-of-use and fixed assets, and other exit costs. This means an adjusted
EBITDA bridge that removes restructuring must carry a remaining-cost schedule
and cash-paid reconciliation; it cannot assume the burden disappears after
one annual period.

## Repurchases are a financing and liquidity test

Henry Schein repurchased approximately `$850M` of common stock in FY2025,
well above the `$322M` first-pass residual. That does not by itself prove that
the repurchases were harmful or debt-funded, because the residual is not a
complete common-owner cash measure and the company may use beginning cash,
financing, or other sources. It does prove that the capital-allocation
waterfall must be joined before a per-share thesis is promoted.

The year-end balance sheet adds pressure to that question: cash was
approximately `$156M` and reported debt approximately `$3.1B`. The next proof
must identify debt draws, maturities, interest, facility amendments, cash
minimums, KKR-related investment scope, and diluted shares. A buyback can
improve per-share optics while leaving the common owner with less balance-sheet
optionality.

## Quality-of-earnings implications

The waterfall creates four explicit financial-shenanigans checks:

1. do not remove restructuring from earnings without tracing cash paid and
   remaining cash obligations;
2. do not treat acquisition/equity cash as maintenance or growth until its
   post-close return is visible;
3. do not call `$850M` of repurchases “surplus cash” while cash, debt,
   working capital, and technology funding remain unresolved; and
4. reconcile GAAP EPS of `$3.27` to non-GAAP EPS of `$4.97` alongside the
   cash, restructuring, acquisition, debt, and share-count waterfall.
