# Home medical equipment and recurring-resupply valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves AdaptHealth from home-continuity and patient-service
evidence into a company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It keeps
equipment placement, recurring resupply, payer authorization, referral access,
capitated contracts, delivery and field service, inventory, receivables,
goodwill, portfolio sales, debt, and diluted common-owner cash separate from
hospital, device-manufacturing, specialty-pharmacy, and healthcare-payment
workflow models.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AdaptHealth home medical equipment and resupply | Collected patient and payer contribution after equipment placement, recurring supplies, product cost, delivery, field service, authorization, capitated utilization, working capital, replacement capital, debt, and dilution | Equipment fleet and replacement, inventory, delivery/branch network, digital support, documentation, payer integration, acquisitions, and contract onboarding | Payer-rate or coding change, denial/collection delay, supplier price increase, capitated utilization loss, patient attrition, equipment shortage, goodwill impairment, debt, or contract margin compression | Patient count, organic revenue, adjusted EBITDA, or FCF rises while active resupply, paid claims, contract contribution, working capital, replacement capital, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$3.2449B`, operating cash flow `$601.8M`, management free
  cash flow `$219.4M`, and adjusted EBITDA `$616.7M`; net loss included a
  `$128.0M` goodwill impairment.
- The network served approximately `4.3M` annual patients through about `640`
  locations in `48` states and operations across all `50` states. Patient count
  is an activity measure until resupply, authorization, collection, and service
  cost are joined.
- Q2 2026 revenue grew `12.7%` and organic revenue `15.9%`, but adjusted EBITDA
  fell `3.2%`; a `$144.2M` pre-tax goodwill write-down and negative year-to-date
  free cash flow of `$48.4M` kept the growth-to-cash bridge open.
- A major West Coast capitated partnership reached full scale, while a
  manufacturer price increase created margin pressure. Capitated patients must
  be valued after equipment, supplies, utilization, quality, and settlement
  costs rather than against fee-for-service revenue.
- Diabetes Health was agreed to be sold for `$235.0M` cash. Proceeds, lost
  contribution, stranded overhead, taxes, and continuing-network effects need a
  separate divestiture bridge.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate referred, authorized, delivered, active, resupplying, and paid
   patients. A patient census or referral does not establish collected cash.
2. Bridge placements to recurring resupply, product cost, delivery, field
   service, documentation, denials, payer mix, and replacement capital.
3. Keep fee-for-service and capitated contracts separate. Test covered lives,
   utilization, vendor pricing, quality adjustments, contract reserves, and cash
   settlement.
4. Reconcile organic growth with price, mix, new contracts, acquisitions, and
   divestitures. Track receivables, inventory, supplier terms, equipment, and
   rollout cash before annualizing growth.
5. Treat goodwill impairments as evidence that acquisition return assumptions
   need rebuilding. Adjusted EBITDA, FCF, debt reduction, and digital users
   remain diagnostic until common cash after dilution is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what active-patient retention,
resupply frequency, reimbursement, payer collection, capitated contribution,
product/service cost, equipment replacement, working capital, debt, terminal
growth, and cost of capital the valuation requires. Patient growth must become
cash per diluted share after the full service chain.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether chronic-care demand remains funded
through payer pressure, supplier inflation, labor and fuel cost, authorization
friction, household strain, and tighter credit. Liquidity passes only when
receivables, inventory, equipment replacement, contract obligations, debt, and
common residual remain visible.

## Promotion boundary

`home-medical-equipment-qualified; resupply-capitated-return-and-collection-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from placements, active
patients, resupply, and capitated lives to authorized and collected claims,
product and service cost, inventory, equipment replacement, contract settlement,
goodwill/acquisition return, debt, SBC replacement, and diluted common residual.
Patient count, organic revenue, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, OCF, and repurchases
remain diagnostic inputs.
