# Mid-tier copper-gold and project-partnership valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Hudbay Minerals from its annual-report packet into a
company-specific valuation, reinvestment, and liquidity test. It treats copper,
gold by-product credits, mine grades, concentrate logistics, project partners,
permitting, debt, and dilution as separate economic objects. It does not pool
Hudbay with giant diversified miners, pure copper operators, or single-asset
restart cases.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hudbay Minerals | Americas copper-gold cash after sustaining mine capital, grade and recovery changes, concentrate logistics, project partnerships, permitting, debt, taxes, and dilution | Sustaining capex, mill throughput, stripping, exploration, Copper World and Arizona Sonoran development, JV contributions, reclamation, and working capital | By-product-credit reversal, grade depletion, port/logistics interruption, permitting delay, commodity-price fall, project funding, FX, debt, or dilution | Copper and gold output remain on plan while cash costs, sustaining capital, shipment conversion, project funding, net debt, or per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was about `$2.211B` and adjusted EBITDA about `$1.061B`; copper
  production was `118,188` tonnes and gold production `267,934` ounces.
- Q1 2026 revenue was `$757.3M`, adjusted EBITDA `$421.9M`, cash `$1.004B`,
  total liquidity `$1.429B`, and net debt only `$5.6M` after the Mitsubishi
  Copper World JV contribution.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$631.3M`, adjusted EBITDA `$321.2M`, and free cash flow
  `$101.8M`; a temporary Peru port closure delayed about `10,000` dry metric
  tonnes of concentrate shipments into July.
- Q1 gold revenue was `39%` of gross revenue and consolidated cash cost was
  negative `$1.80` per pound, showing how by-product credits can dominate a
  copper reading.
- Copper World, Arizona Sonoran, Manitoba, Peru, and British Columbia create
  different project, jurisdiction, grade, and funding burdens; production,
  adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, and guidance are not a common owner-cash
  denominator.

## Hudbay Q2 2026 production-to-owner-cash bridge

Hudbay's Q2 filing and release make the by-product and shipment timing issues
quantifiable. Q2 consolidated production was `28,267` tonnes of copper and
`51,234` ounces of gold, while consolidated cash cost net of by-product credits
was negative `$0.40` per pound and sustaining cash cost was `$1.39` per pound.
Gold represented `38%` of Q2 revenue. The negative copper cash-cost result is
therefore a portfolio-price and by-product-credit outcome, not evidence that
copper extraction itself is free of sustaining or project burden.

Q2 cash generated from operations was `$297.0M`, but `$86.9M` came from a
favorable change in non-cash working capital. Cash operating flow before that
working-capital change was `$210.1M`. Cash sustaining capital expenditures were
`$108.3M`, consisting of `$80.1M` total sustaining capital costs, `$15.2M` of
capitalized lease and equipment-financing payments at operating sites, and
`$13.0M` of community-agreement cash payments. The resulting reported free
cash flow was `$101.8M`. This is a useful operating-period bridge, but it is
not yet a normalized through-cycle owner-cash number because concentrate
shipment timing, metal prices, by-product credits, reclamation, growth capital,
and project funding remain open.

The quarter also demonstrates why production is not settlement. Ocean swells
temporarily closed a Peru port and delayed approximately `10,000` dry metric
tonnes of copper concentrate shipments into July. The company reported the
delay and subsequent delivery, but a matched invoice, provisional-pricing,
customer-receipt, and bank-settlement ledger is not established by the release.
Concentrate inventory and working-capital timing therefore remain part of the
cash-quality test.

The Copper World partnership improves liquidity but does not equal unrestricted
Hudbay cash. At June 30, 2026, cash and equivalents were `$890.9M`, total
liquidity was `$1.0446B`, and net debt was negative `$80.5M`. However, `$334.5M`
of cash was held by Copper World LLC and contractually restricted solely for
advancement of that project. Hudbay also repaid `$472.5M` of senior unsecured
notes at maturity using cash on hand and a `$272M` revolver draw, while the
initial Mitsubishi Copper World contribution was approximately `$420M`. The
balance-sheet improvement is therefore a mix of operating cash, partnership
funding, restricted project cash, and refinancing—not a single unrestricted
common-owner cash pool.

The current Hudbay test is:

```text
payable copper/gold production
  -> concentrate inventory, shipment, provisional pricing, and receipt
  -> by-product credits and realized metal prices
  -> sustaining capital, leases, community payments, and reclamation
  -> Copper World/JV funding, project sanctioning, debt, and dilution
  -> unrestricted common-owner residual
```

Copper World remains a funding and sanctioning branch. The Q2 release says a
sanctioning decision is expected later in 2026, but restricted JV cash and
guidance do not prove the final project funding waterfall or return on invested
capital.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile payable metal, concentrate inventory, port timing, shipment,
   provisional pricing, and cash receipt; production is not sale or collection.
2. Keep gold by-product credits separate from copper operating durability and
   test the cash-cost result under normalized metal prices.
3. Separate sustaining capital, growth capital, stripping, exploration,
   reclamation, JV contributions, acquisitions, and project funding.
4. Reconcile Mitsubishi consideration, Copper World ownership, Arizona Sonoran
   acquisition, debt reduction, and diluted shares; partnership proceeds are not
   recurring mine cash.
5. Treat adjusted EBITDA, negative cash cost, free cash flow, dividends, and
   buybacks as diagnostic until shipment, capital, legal-entity, and common-
   residual joins close.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what copper and gold prices, grades,
recoveries, throughput, sustaining capital, project sanctioning, reinvestment,
and cost of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks
whether commodity volatility, port and power logistics, permitting, FX, debt,
community obligations, and project funding can consume liquidity before the
Americas-growth thesis matures.

## Promotion boundary

`copper-gold-qualified; by-product-and-project-funding-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from payable production and
shipments to customer settlement, sustaining and growth capital, project
funding, debt service, reclamation, and diluted common residual. Production,
cash costs, adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, JV proceeds, and guidance remain
diagnostic inputs.
