# ICU Medical infusion and consumables valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench tests ICU Medical as a provider-facing clinical workflow and replenishment platform. It keeps infusion-system placement, recurring consumables, Vital Care, hospital and distributor channels, inventory safety stock, tariffs, quality spending, acquisitions, debt, and diluted common residual separate from reported revenue and adjusted EBITDA.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ICU Medical | Clinical-workflow cash after infusion systems, consumables, Vital Care, quality and regulatory spending, inventory resilience, channel terms, acquisitions, debt, and diluted common residual | Product development, quality systems, manufacturing, safety stock, raw materials, distributor support, working capital, acquisitions, debt, and shares | Hospital and alternate-site spending pressure, supplier concentration, tariff/input inflation, quality event, inventory buildup, divestiture noise, or refinancing | Consumables and systems revenue grow while attachment, gross margin, inventory conversion, quality costs, or diluted per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$2.2313B`; Consumables were `$1.1092B`, Infusion Systems `$684.2M`, and Vital Care `$437.9M`.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$551.7M`, GAAP gross margin `43%`, adjusted diluted EPS `$2.37`, and adjusted EBITDA `$110.0M`.
- Q2 mix included Consumables `$289.3M`, Infusion Systems `$189.0M`, and Vital Care `$73.4M`.
- Cash and short-term investment securities were `$308.0M` at 2025 year-end after debt paydown and the IV Solutions transaction.
- The filing identifies direct and distributor sales, single- or limited-source inputs, safety-stock inventory, tariff capitalization, quality-system spending, and care across hospital, alternate-site, and home settings.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile system placements to recurring consumables pull-through, renewal/retention, distributor inventory, shipment timing, and collections.
2. Separate organic clinical-workflow economics from the IV Solutions divestiture, acquisitions, tariff refunds, and foreign-exchange effects.
3. Test safety-stock inventory and supplier concentration against gross margin, obsolescence, service levels, and working-capital conversion.
4. Keep quality, regulatory, debt, integration, stock compensation, and diluted shares in the residual schedule.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what installed-base growth, consumables attachment, product mix, gross margin, quality reinvestment, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether hospitals, alternate sites, and home-care channels can maintain clinical replenishment through budget, labor, tariff, supply, and regulatory shocks.

## Promotion boundary

`icu-medical-qualified; infusion-attachment-and-clinical-collection-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from systems to consumables and Vital Care collections, distributor inventory, quality and regulatory spending, required reinvestment, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, adjusted EBITDA, EPS, OCF, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
