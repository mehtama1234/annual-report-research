# iHeartMedia audio and podcast attention valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves iHeartMedia from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests broadcast radio, podcasting, digital audio, events, audience scale, programmatic selling, data and attribution, non-cash trade, political distortions, FCC-license impairments, liquidity, leverage, and diluted common residual. It does not pool iHeart with CTV platforms, agencies, publishers, or out-of-home operators.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| iHeartMedia | Cross-platform audio cash after broadcast reach, podcast and digital growth, events, data and attribution, talent, content, non-cash trade, debt service, and dilution | Content and talent, podcast and digital technology, station and network operations, events, data/measurement, working capital, debt, and shares | Broadcast decline, advertising softness, political comparison, podcast monetization shortfall, impairment, non-cash trade, high leverage, or shrinking liquidity | Podcast and digital revenue rise while ex-political organic demand, cash conversion, debt service, audience monetization, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Q1 2026 revenue was about `$884M`, Digital Audio revenue about `$327M` up `18%`, podcast revenue about `$147M` up `27%`, adjusted EBITDA about `$93M`, and free cash flow was negative about `$114M`.
- FY2025 segment revenue was about `$2.274B` for Multiplatform, `$1.329B` for Digital Audio, and `$272.5M` for Audio & Media Services.
- Q1 2026 available liquidity was about `$495M`; management guided to about `$800M` adjusted EBITDA, `$200M` free cash flow, and mid-five-times year-end net leverage.
- The platform includes more than `860` broadcast stations, podcasting, digital audio, events, SmartAudio, national sales, Katz Media, and RCS software; non-cash trade and political revenue require explicit normalization.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate political revenue, spot broadcast, podcast, digital, events, programmatic, and non-cash trade economics.
2. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow to content and talent, impairment, working capital, cash interest, FCC licenses, debt repayment, and dilution.
3. Test whether podcast and digital growth produces collected incremental cash or merely offsets declining legacy broadcast margins.
4. Track liquidity and leverage directly; audience scale and adjusted EBITDA do not prove common-owner cash when interest and refinancing dominate.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what podcast growth, digital monetization, ex-political broadcast demand, programmatic revenue, margin, cost savings, leverage reduction, and cost of capital are embedded in the valuation. The stress test asks whether advertisers, listeners, talent, platforms, and lenders support the stack through broadcast decline, political volatility, rates, impairments, and liquidity pressure.

## Promotion boundary

`iheart-qualified; podcast-digital-conversion-and-debt-service-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from audience and advertising to collection, ex-political demand, podcast retention, content and talent cost, non-cash trade, debt service, claims, funding, and diluted common residual. Revenue, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, audience scale, guidance, and podcast growth remain diagnostic inputs.
