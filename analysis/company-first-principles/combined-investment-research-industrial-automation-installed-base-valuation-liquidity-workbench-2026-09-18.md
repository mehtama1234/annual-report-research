# Industrial automation installed-base valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Honeywell from automation and installed-base evidence into
a company-specific valuation and liquidity object. It separates controls,
process technology, building systems, aerospace systems, service/software
attachment, portfolio transactions, and separation cash from contractors,
grid components, electronic interconnects, and mission-system integrators.

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 sales were `$37.442B`, reported continuing OCF `$6.075B`, capex `$986M`, adjusted EPS `$9.78`, and backlog about `$37.5B`.
- FY2025 OCF included a `$1.590B` Resideo indemnification receipt and a `$1.428B` asbestos-liability divestiture payment; normalized continuing OCF is closer to `$5.913B` before capex.
- Acquisitions used `$2.211B` in 2025 and `$8.880B` in 2024; the post-acquisition cash denominator is materially different from adjusted EPS.
- Honeywell completed the Solstice spin-off and was preparing the Aerospace separation; future continuing-company results require restated revenue, margin, debt, tax, pension, and stranded-cost joins.
- 2025 capex was `$986M`, versus `$871M` in 2024 and `$741M` in 2023; maintenance, capacity, software, and separation allocation remain open.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Remove indemnity, asbestos, discontinued-operation, and other legal/divestiture cash from recurring owner cash while preserving the liquidity effect.
2. Separate organic growth, acquisitions, backlog, service/software attachment, and customer-supported programs; backlog is not delivered or collected cash.
3. Reconcile installed-base claims to placed systems, renewal, replacement, service margin, software retention, cybersecurity, warranty, and working capital.
4. Test acquisition cohorts after purchase price, integration, goodwill/intangibles, financing, and stranded cost.
5. Split capex and R&D into maintenance, capacity, software, customer programs, and separation; do not capitalize pure portfolio narrative.
6. Allocate pensions, asbestos, tax, environmental, warranty, debt, and stranded overhead to the correct post-spin entity.
7. Treat adjusted EPS, backlog, OCF, and buybacks as diagnostic until post-separation diluted cash per share is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what post-spin organic growth,
service/software attachment, margin, backlog conversion, capex, R&D,
acquisition return, stranded cost, and cost of capital the valuation requires.
The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether industrial capital budgets, energy,
rates, supply chains, aviation, and government demand can fund installed-base
renewal and separation obligations through a weaker cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`industrial-automation-installed-base-qualified; post-separation-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from placed controls and
backlog to delivered revenue, service/software renewal, margin, collection,
maintenance/capacity capital, acquisitions, legal claims, debt, standalone
cost, and diluted common residual. Backlog, adjusted EPS, installed-base
claims, OCF, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
