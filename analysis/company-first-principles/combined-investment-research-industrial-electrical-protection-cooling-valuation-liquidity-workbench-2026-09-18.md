# Industrial electrical protection and cooling valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves nVent Electric from infrastructure-demand evidence into a
company-specific valuation and liquidity object. It keeps qualified electrical
interfaces, enclosures, bus systems, control buildings, and liquid cooling
separate from grid-component manufacturing, electrical distribution, data-
center construction, and electronic interconnects.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nVent Electric | Qualified electrical-protection and cooling cash after organic and acquired growth, integration, working capital, capacity, debt, and dilution | EPG acquisition price, acquired margin, liquid-cooling capacity, plants, tooling, inventory, R&D, warranty/service, tariffs, integration, debt, and common capital | Data-center or utility project delay, backlog cancellation, liquid-cooling failure, tariff reversal, acquisition leverage, working-capital absorption, or refinancing | Sales and adjusted EPS rise while organic growth, backlog conversion, acquired return, cooling utilization, debt, or diluted common cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 continuing-operations sales were `$3.893B`, operating cash flow `$649M`, capex `$93.3M`, and reported free cash flow `$561M`.
- Cash paid for the Electrical Products Group acquisition was `$979.6M`; OCF less capex and that acquisition was approximately negative `$424M`.
- Q2 2026 sales were `$1.471B`, with `47%` reported growth; management attributed more than 30 points to new products. Q2 free cash flow was `$167M` after `$54M` in Q1.
- Backlog was about `$2.6B` in Q1 2026. Backlog still requires production, acceptance, receivable collection, inventory, warranty, and project execution.
- Year-end 2025 cash was about `$237M`, revolver availability about `$600M`, and debt about `$1.6B`; the Thermal Management divestiture improved liquidity but is not recurring operating cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate organic price, volume, mix, acquisition contribution, and new-product growth; do not let EPG or launch growth masquerade as organic durability.
2. Reconcile company-defined FCF to OCF, capex, property proceeds, acquisition cash, integration spending, and discontinued-operation proceeds.
3. Convert backlog into shipments, acceptance, customer deposits, receivables, inventory, gross margin, warranty, and collected cash.
4. Test liquid-cooling qualification, capacity, yield, leak/warranty claims, service cost, customer concentration, and return on expansion capital.
5. Keep tariff and inflation recovery separate from sustainable price, productivity, and volume; test the disclosed 2025 tariff burden after benefits normalize.
6. Include debt, interest, revolver capacity, future acquisitions, SBC, dividends, buybacks, and diluted shares in the common-owner bridge.
7. Do not use divestiture proceeds or adjusted EPS accretion as proof that the continuing portfolio funds its own growth.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what organic growth, EPG cash return,
liquid-cooling margin, backlog conversion, capacity utilization, reinvestment,
and cost of capital the market value requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test
asks whether data-center and utility projects, tariffs, metals, labor, debt,
and customer concentration can be funded through a cyclical slowdown.

## Promotion boundary

`industrial-electrical-protection-cooling-qualified; acquisition-capacity-and-liquidity-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from backlog and new-product
sales to shipment, collection, utilization, warranty, acquired-cohort return,
required capex, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Sales, backlog,
adjusted EPS, reported FCF, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
