# Industrial gases and project-capital valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench adds Air Products as a distinct industrial-gas and physical-
infrastructure lane. It separates mature on-site and merchant gas contracts
from hydrogen and clean-energy projects, joint ventures, separation costs,
non-recourse structures, debt, and the dividend claim. It is not a generic
chemicals or materials screen.

## Decision matrix

| Air Products | Core industrial-gas cash after plant utilization, energy pass-through, maintenance capital, contract collection, project/JV funding, debt, pension, separation obligations, and diluted common residual | On-site and merchant plants, pipelines, logistics, maintenance/replacement, hydrogen projects, JV equity, completion support, asset retirement, debt, and dividends | Project cancellation or impairment, customer volume/credit, energy-cost mismatch, JV support, non-recourse debt, separation liabilities, refinancing, or dividend funding | Adjusted operating profit and contract visibility rise while core return on capital, maintenance-adjusted cash, project funding, parent liquidity, or per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$12.037B`, OCF `$3.257B`, total plant additions `$7.023B`, management capex `$5.064B`, dividends `$1.589B`, cash/restricted cash `$1.856B`, and debt/finance obligations `$17.664B`.
- OCF less management capex and acquisitions was approximately negative `$1.867B`; total additions were driven heavily by clean-energy projects and included NEOM-related spending.
- FY2025 GAAP net income was negative `$395M`, so adjusted operating results require a project-charge, impairment, restructuring, and cash reconciliation.
- Air Products later exited or reset the Louisiana Clean Energy Complex and the Arizona/Casa Grande liquid-hydrogen facility. Those actions are evidence against treating the prior project pipeline as earned future value.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate mature industrial-gas cash from NEOM, Louisiana, Alberta, hydrogen,
   and other project/JV economics; consolidated OCF is not a sufficient owner-
   cash denominator.
2. Reconcile total plant additions with management capex, partner funding,
   non-recourse debt, guarantees, completion support, and parent cash exposure.
3. Read on-site and pipeline contract duration, minimum volumes, take-or-pay,
   energy-indexation, termination, customer credit, and utilization together.
4. Keep impairments, cancellations, restructuring, separation costs, pension,
   asset-retirement obligations, and recurring project resets in the lifecycle
   return test even when labeled non-recurring.
5. Test dividends against parent-level maintenance-adjusted cash, debt service,
   project commitments, separation obligations, and actual liquidity.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test values the mature gas base, growth
projects, joint ventures, and separation independently, with explicit capital
intensity and return-on-capital assumptions. The Lyn Alden-style stress test
asks whether energy, materials, labor, interest, customer demand, project
finance, and government or partner support can preserve liquidity when large
plants are underutilized or delayed.

## Promotion boundary

`industrial-gases-qualified; core-return-project-funding-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from named gas contracts and
plant utilization to customer receipts, maintenance capital, project/JV cash,
guarantees, debt, separation liabilities, dividends, and diluted common
residual. Revenue, adjusted operating income, contract backlog, OCF, project
spending funded by partners, and dividends remain diagnostic inputs rather
than normalized owner cash.
