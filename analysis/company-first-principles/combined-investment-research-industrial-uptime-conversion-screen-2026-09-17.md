# Industrial uptime conversion screen — WESCO and Fastenal — 2026-09-17

## Purpose

This is the first evidence pass after the industrial-uptime move-on admission.
It tests whether visible distribution growth is converting into operating cash
after reported property spending, while keeping balance-sheet working-capital
movement separate from the cash-flow statement.

The structured companion is the [WESCO/Fastenal conversion table](data/combined-investment-research-industrial-uptime-conversion-screen-2026-09-17.csv).

The figures below are from each company's locally preserved Q2 2026 Form 10-Q
and are in USD millions unless stated otherwise.

## What the screen supports

1. Both companies show a period-matched positive mechanical bridge from
   reported operating cash flow to operating cash flow less disclosed property
   and equipment spending.
2. The result is not equivalent to normalized owner cash: leases, taxes,
   maintenance versus growth spending, acquisitions, dilution, financing
   flows, and service burden still require separate treatment.
3. WESCO's public filing identifies the working-capital pressure as a real
   conversion issue: its H1 operating cash flow was `$275.1M`, versus `$135.8M`
   in the prior-year period, while trade receivables, inventory, and current
   operating liabilities changed materially.
4. Fastenal's H1 2026 filing reports `$644.1M` of operating cash flow and
   `$123.0M` of property/equipment spending; its balance sheet shows receivables
   of `$1,557.4M`, inventory of `$1,735.2M`, and accounts payable of `$399.8M`
   at June 30, 2026, compared with `$1,245.3M`, `$1,748.0M`, and `$316.8M` at
   December 31, 2025.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans diagnostics

The screen creates the following questions rather than conclusions:

- Is WESCO's receivable and inventory investment a temporary growth bridge,
  acquisition/project timing, or a recurring burden of the project-channel
  model?
- Does Fastenal's onsite/vending growth improve retention and turns enough to
  offset service cost and large-account mix pressure?
- Are supplier-finance, contract-asset, acquisition, or other current-liability
  movements embedded in the reported operating-cash result?
- Which portion of property/equipment spending is maintenance, network support,
  technology, or growth?
- Do taxes, lease payments, buybacks, dividends, and debt claims leave a
  period-matched residual available to common owners?

No Beneish-style composite or fraud conclusion is calculated. The available
vectors are not yet fully comparable across entities and periods, and the
balance-sheet bridge must not be double-counted against operating cash flow.

## Evidence boundary

This pass supports `reported-cash-conversion-screen` for WESCO and Fastenal.
It does not support `normalized-owner-cash`, `maintenance-capital-return`, or
an investment ranking. The next promotion bundle is:

- same-period revenue and gross-profit denominator;
- current and non-current lease cash or a defensible lease treatment;
- taxes paid, dividends, buybacks, debt principal, and dilution;
- maintenance/growth classification of property and equipment spending;
- supplier-finance and acquisition cash-flow treatment; and
- a Damodaran valuation range carried through the Lyn Alden rate, credit, and
  infrastructure-demand stress cases.
