## End-to-end hypothesis

`power / data-center / infrastructure demand`
`-> equipment, parts, electrical supply, and field execution`
`-> uptime or completed project`
`-> invoice and collection`
`-> working-capital and reinvestment burden`
`-> owner-cash and valuation test`

The first-principles claim is narrow:

> Industrial access companies can earn a durable position when they reduce
> the cost of physical downtime, but demand, backlog, fleet scale, and branch
> density do not by themselves prove cash conversion or attractive returns.

## Required evidence packet

Each candidate must receive the same five joins before it can be promoted:

1. **Demand:** named end market, period, and customer/project indicator.
2. **Control point:** fleet, branch, vending, technical service, or project
   position that explains why the company captures value.
3. **Conversion:** revenue-to-collection bridge with inventory, receivables,
   payables, contract assets/liabilities, and supplier-finance treatment.
4. **Reinvestment:** maintenance versus growth capex, fleet replacement,
   acquisitions, leases, and dilution.
5. **Return:** Damodaran-style value range with an explicit Lyn Alden-style
   rate, credit, liquidity, and affordability stress case.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans gate

The lane inherits the system's diagnostic overlay. The first checks are:

- backlog or rental demand growing faster than collections;
- inventory or receivables absorbing the apparent growth;
- fleet or project capex capitalized while maintenance burden is understated;
- acquisition contribution presented as organic conversion;
- supplier-finance or contract-asset movements being counted twice;
- used-equipment proceeds, asset sales, or one-time settlements being treated
  as recurring owner cash;
- debt capacity being confused with customer funding or legal availability.

These are prompts for same-entity, same-period reconciliation. They are not a
fraud score and cannot promote owner cash without a cash-or-claim join.
