# Industrial uptime next-cycle decision memo — 2026-09-17

## Decision

The industrial-uptime lane remains an evidence-backed candidate cohort, not an
investment ranking. The next cycle should pursue the first joinable cash object
in this order:

1. Sterling project-level backlog-to-collection and CEC post-close cash;
2. WESCO/Fastenal same-period owner-cash burden allocation; and
3. United Rentals certificate-grade collateral and fleet lifecycle return.

This order follows evidence leverage, not apparent valuation cheapness. Each
lane can be promoted only by a new legal-entity, same-period cash or claim join.

## Why Sterling goes first

Sterling has the clearest currently visible control point: signed backlog,
combined backlog, future phases, acquisition contribution, and an explicit
acquisition cash/stock/earn-out hurdle. The missing object is therefore narrow:
connect project backlog to revenue, contract assets, retainage, cost-to-complete
changes, collections, and post-close CEC cash.

The next source package should seek:

- project or segment backlog roll-forward with signed versus awarded work;
- revenue recognized and remaining performance obligations by comparable
  project cohort;
- contract-asset, contract-liability, retainage, and receivable movements;
- cost-to-complete revisions, claims, change orders, and loss provisions; and
- CEC cash generation, integration costs, earn-out payments, and dilution.

Promotion requires a reconciled project-level backlog-to-cash bridge. Unsigned
awards, future phases, percentage-of-completion revenue, and acquisition
revenue remain expectation inputs until that bridge exists.

The [Sterling Q2 2026 cash and acquisition-return refresh](combined-investment-research-industrial-uptime-sterling-q2-2026-cash-return-refresh-2026-09-17.md)
now makes the H1 burden stack explicit: `$328.021M` OCF less `$69.646M` capex,
`$139.985M` cash acquisitions, and `$7.767M` earn-outs leaves `$110.623M`
before senior claims; placing `$27.406M` stock consideration beside the cash
uses creates an economic screen of approximately `$83.217M`. This improves the
return hurdle but does not close the post-close CEC/Stone Ridge collection or
cohort-cash gate.

The [Sterling post-close cohort request packet](combined-investment-research-industrial-uptime-sterling-post-close-cohort-request-packet-2026-09-18.md)
now specifies the CEC, Stone Ridge, and organic-control joins required to test
collections, integration costs, earn-outs, capex, and dilution. It is a source
request, not evidence that the acquisition cohorts have earned their hurdle.

The Q2 filing subsequently confirms the exact Stone Ridge and CEC acquisition
perimeters, consideration, assumed contract/lease burdens, and the immaterial
one-month Stone Ridge reported contribution. This is a denominator and legal-
entity upgrade only; the post-close collection and return gate remains open.

## WESCO/Fastenal second test

The longitudinal bridge shows two different diagnostics:

- WESCO is a conversion-change test: H1 2026 cash conversion improved sharply
  versus FY2025, so the decisive question is persistence after receivable,
  inventory, supplier-finance, and acquisition timing normalize.
- Fastenal is a continuity test: H1 2026 remains near its FY2025 cash baseline,
  so the decisive question is whether service, lease, tax, maintenance, mix,
  dilution, and customer-support burdens still leave recurring owner cash.

The next package should be period-matched and should not add balance-sheet
working-capital movements twice. It must identify, for each entity:

- collections and receivable aging or equivalent cash evidence;
- inventory and supplier-finance settlement effects;
- maintenance/property spending versus growth or platform investment;
- service, lease, tax, and support costs; and
- diluted-share, SBC, debt, and other owner-claim effects.

Promotion requires a comparable period with a complete owner-cash bundle. OCF
less reported property spending remains only a mechanical screen.

## United Rentals third test

URI has the most visible fleet-scale cash and liquidity proxy, but the decisive
certificate object is not public in the current perimeter. The next request is
therefore narrowly defined:

- populated borrowing-base certificate or collateral availability report;
- eligible receivables, fleet collateral, NOLV, reserves, and L/C deductions;
- utilization and replacement-versus-growth capex; and
- fleet purchase funding, used-equipment proceeds, debt cost, and lifecycle
  return.

If no populated certificate or equivalent lender report appears, retain the
facility-availability proxy as a qualified expectation input and stop repeating
the same public-document search.

## Valuation and macro discipline

The current valuation workbench is a price-implied-expectations surface. Its
low/base/high haircuts are scenario controls, not free cash flow and not a
Damodaran intrinsic-value conclusion. The next cycle may update price inputs,
but must not convert them into a ranking until the denominator promotion gates
are met.

The macro handoff remains a transmission map: rates and credit affect project
awards, inventory financing, fleet utilization, and acquisition hurdles;
infrastructure and power scarcity support demand; inflation tests margins and
cost-to-complete; liquidity reversal tests working capital and covenant headroom.
Each mechanism needs repeated company-specific observations before causal
promotion.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

The next cycle must use Sloan-/Schilit-/Beneish-style diagnostics as prompts,
not findings. The required checks are:

- timing: receivables, contract assets, supplier finance, and collections;
- capitalization: fleet, platform, maintenance, and project costs;
- estimate risk: percentage-of-completion, claims, retainage, and cost-to-complete;
- perimeter: acquisition contribution, legal entity, JV, and related-party flows;
- owner claims: leases, taxes, debt, SBC, dilution, and earn-outs; and
- cash corroboration: same-entity, same-period cash or settlement evidence.

No pooled Beneish score or manipulation conclusion is permitted unless the
entity, period, accounting taxonomy, total-assets/accrual inputs, and cash/claim
perimeter are comparable.

## Stop and move-on rule

After a targeted source request, classify the result as `promoted`,
`evidence-insufficient`, or `searched-negative`. A searched-negative result
preserves the missing object and moves the work to the next candidate; it does
not become a negative investment conclusion. The cohort remains a
`qualified-expectation-screen` until at least one candidate clears its complete
cash/return gate.
