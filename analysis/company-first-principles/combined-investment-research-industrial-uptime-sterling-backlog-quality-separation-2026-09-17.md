# Sterling backlog-quality separation — 2026-09-17

## Purpose

Sterling's industrial-uptime case needs a stricter distinction between work
that is contractually signed, work management expects to sign, and future
phases that remain project optionality. This memo separates those layers before
cash conversion or valuation is discussed.

## Backlog hierarchy

| Layer | Q2 2026 observation | Proof meaning | Valuation treatment |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Signed backlog | `$4.33B` | Unearned revenue expected from executed contracts | Highest visibility, but still requires margin, billing, collection, and cost-to-complete proof |
| Combined backlog | `$5.62B` | Signed backlog plus unsigned awards | Do not treat the `$1.29B` difference as contracted revenue |
| Future-phase opportunities | More than `$1.4B` | High-probability future work associated with projects | Optionality only; not backlog or revenue |
| Management pipeline / opportunity pool | Broader than the above | Commercial possibility | Exclude from base revenue and owner-cash denominator |

The hierarchy is:

`signed backlog > unsigned awards / combined backlog > high-probability future phases > management pipeline`

The figures are drawn from the [Sterling deep company page](../../analysis/deep-company-pages/sterling-infrastructure-inc.md), the Q2 2026 filing chain in the [Sterling source ledger](../../extracted/industrial-goods/heavy-construction/sterling-infrastructure-inc/source-ledger.md), and the [industrial conversion pass](capital-flow-capital-intensive-industrial-conversion-pass-1.md).

## Acquisition and organic-quality separation

The Q2 2026 conversion packet reports:

- acquisition revenue contribution of `21.5%` of Q2 revenue;
- CEC and Stone Ridge contribution of `$2.56B` to signed backlog plus
  unsigned awards; and
- that contribution representing approximately `45.6%` of the `$5.62B`
  combined backlog.

This means the growth narrative cannot be read as purely organic execution.
The acquired backlog may be economically attractive, but it carries a return
hurdle for purchase price, stock consideration, earn-outs, intangible
amortization, integration, working capital, and incremental labor/equipment.

## Cash-conversion control

Sterling's H1 2026 operating cash flow less company capex is reported as
`$258.375M`. That is a useful reported-cash screen, not normalized owner cash.
The conversion chain still requires:

`signed project -> measured progress -> billing -> receivable / contract asset -> collection -> cash after project cost`

The main missing joins are:

- contract assets and retainage to collected cash;
- contract liabilities and customer advances to reported operating cash;
- project-estimate changes, change orders, claims, rework, and warranty costs;
- customer or hyperscaler funding source and payment timing;
- acquisition-specific revenue, margin, cash, earn-out, and integration costs;
- maintenance versus growth equipment and facility spending; and
- noncontrolling interest, stock consideration, dilution, debt, and lease claims.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans diagnostics

The backlog presentation creates specific forensic questions:

1. Is signed backlog converting to revenue at the disclosed margin, or are
   cost-to-cost estimates pulling profit forward?
2. Are unsigned awards being described in a way that makes them appear
   contractually committed?
3. Are future phases being counted in demand, backlog, valuation, and cash
   more than once?
4. Is acquisition contribution being mixed with organic growth or same-store
   productivity?
5. Are favorable contract-estimate revisions, unapproved claims, or change
   orders supporting reported margin?
6. Does operating cash flow remain strong when contract liabilities stop
   increasing and receivables/contract assets normalize?

These are Sloan/Schilit/Beneish-style diagnostic prompts. They are not a fraud
score or a misconduct allegation. A warning can narrow a valuation range or
activate a thesis-breaker only after same-entity, same-period, comparable
taxonomy, and cash/claim reconciliation.

## Damodaran and Lyn Alden handoff

The valuation model should use at least three revenue-visibility cases:

- **Contracted case:** signed backlog conversion with explicit margin and
  collection assumptions.
- **Conversion case:** signed backlog plus only a bounded share of unsigned
  awards, with acquisition contribution separated.
- **Stress case:** delayed unsigned awards and future phases, lower project
  margin, slower collections, and higher funding cost.

The macro/liquidity stress should test customer capex budgets, project finance,
rates, labor/material inflation, power availability, and Sterling's own working
capital and revolver burden. The `$1.5B` revolver expansion is funding capacity,
not proof that customer projects are funded or that the capacity earns a return.

## Decision

Sterling earns the narrower status:

`conversion-supported execution platform; backlog-quality-separated; owner-cash and return proof open`

It does not earn:

`fully funded backlog`, `organic growth`, `normalized owner cash`, or `high-return project capital`.

## Next evidence object

The next promotion document is a project-level or segment-level roll-forward
that joins signed backlog, revenue recognized, gross-margin estimate changes,
contract assets/liabilities, retainage, billing, and cash collection. If that
object is not publicly available, the lane remains qualified and the research
moves to the next candidate without promoting the broader backlog layers.
