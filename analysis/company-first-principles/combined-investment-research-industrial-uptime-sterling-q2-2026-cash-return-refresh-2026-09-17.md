# Sterling Infrastructure Q2 2026 cash and acquisition-return refresh

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This refresh advances the [Sterling project-to-cash bridge](combined-investment-research-industrial-uptime-sterling-project-cash-bridge-pass-1-2026-09-17.md) and the [CEC acquisition return hurdle](combined-investment-research-industrial-uptime-sterling-acquisition-return-hurdle-2026-09-17.md). The purpose is to make the H1 2026 cash burden explicit, not to treat backlog, contract liabilities, or adjusted earnings as owner cash.

## H1 cash stack

The Q2 2026 10-Q reports H1 operating cash flow of `$328.021M` and capital expenditures of `$69.646M`. It also reports `$139.985M` of acquisition cash and `$7.767M` of earn-out payments.

```text
operating cash flow                         $328.021M
  - company capital expenditures             $69.646M
  - cash acquisitions                         $139.985M
  - earn-out payments                           $7.767M
  = cash residual after visible growth uses  $110.623M
```

The `$110.623M` result is a disciplined cash screen, not normalized owner cash. It remains before interest, taxes, leases, noncontrolling interests, maintenance-versus-growth allocation, and any unlisted acquisition integration or project claims. It also does not subtract the `$27.406M` of non-cash stock consideration from cash flow. That stock consideration is instead an economic owner claim and dilution input.

If the stock consideration is placed beside the cash acquisition outflow as a total acquisition-capital screen, the H1 residual becomes approximately `$83.217M`. That is a scenario boundary, not a GAAP cash-flow line: stock consideration is not cash paid in the period and must not be mixed into the reported OCF bridge.

## Project-accounting conversion pressure

At June 30, 2026, accounts receivable were `$770.671M`, contract assets `$156.295M`, contract liabilities `$802.601M`, and accounts payable `$316.019M`. Contract-asset retainage increased to `$72.8M` from `$40.7M`; contract-liability retainage increased to `$197.1M` from `$147.6M`. Revenue recognized from opening contract liabilities was `$488.4M` in H1 2026 versus `$281.0M` in H1 2025.

These observations show billing/work progression and a growing set of future collection or performance obligations, but they are not interchangeable with cash. Contract liabilities can be customer-funded progress, while contract assets and retainage are claims on future billing or settlement. The correct next join is a project or cohort collection roll-forward, not a further subtraction of every balance-sheet movement from OCF. The cash-flow statement already captures the period's operating working-capital movement.

## Backlog and acquisition perimeter

Sterling's June 30 presentation separates `$4.33B` of contractual signed backlog from `$5.6B` of combined backlog that adds unsigned awards and identifies more than `$1.4B` of future-phase opportunities. CEC and Stone Ridge contribute approximately `$1.32B` of signed backlog and `$1.24B` of unsigned awards. The ex-acquisition backlog growth rate is approximately 50%, while reported continuing-operations backlog growth is 116%.

This is a real control improvement: the demand stack can now be split into signed, unsigned, organic, and acquired components. It still does not tell us which backlog dollars will become billed revenue, what gross margin they carry, how much working capital they consume, or when cash is collected.

The purchase-accounting perimeter makes the return hurdle concrete:

| CEC / Stone Ridge item | Amount | Treatment |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| CEC cash consideration | `$442.937M` | cash capital at close |
| CEC stock consideration | `$79.458M` | dilution/economic capital |
| CEC earn-out | `$39.194M` disclosed purchase-price component; up to `$80M` operating-income hurdle in the broader agreement | conditional claim; do not treat as current cash unless paid |
| Stone Ridge cash consideration | `$139.985M` | H1 cash acquisition outflow |
| H1 acquisition/earn-out cash | `$147.752M` | direct H1 cash uses in the filing |
| H1 acquired CEC contribution, 2025 reference | `$19.4M`, net of `$3.0M` intangible amortization | partial operating contribution, not standalone cash |

The H1 cash outflow is not identical to the full CEC consideration because timing and purchase-accounting perimeter differ. That distinction is itself a QoE control: a return model must identify the close date, acquired-period contribution, integration cost, earn-out payment, and diluted shares by cohort.

## Financial-shenanigans and quality-of-earnings tests

1. **Percentage-of-completion and estimates:** H1 revenue included `$138.9M` of increases from changes in prior-period contract estimates, versus `$72.5M` in H1 2025. Reconcile those changes to project margin, contract assets, claims, retainage, and collections.
2. **Backlog quality:** keep signed backlog, unsigned awards, future phases, and acquired backlog separate. Do not apply one terminal-growth or conversion assumption to all four.
3. **Capitalization:** distinguish equipment/property capex needed to deliver projects from growth capacity and acquisition integration spending.
4. **Perimeter:** separate CEC and Stone Ridge revenue, margin, working capital, cash, earn-outs, and stock consideration from organic Sterling results.
5. **Cash corroboration:** require customer billings, collections, contract-asset settlement, or a post-close cohort cash schedule before calling the backlog economically realized.
6. **Owner claims:** carry taxes, interest, leases, noncontrolling interests, earn-outs, stock consideration, SBC, and debt funding after the operating cash screen.

## Decision

The H1 cash screen improves precision but not promotion status. Sterling has strong contracted and acquired demand visibility, yet `$110.623M` after visible H1 growth uses—or approximately `$83.217M` after an economic stock-consideration screen—is materially smaller than the acquisition and backlog narrative suggests. The decisive unknown is not whether backlog exists; it is whether project and acquired-cohort cash survives working capital, claims, capex, integration, earn-outs, funding cost, and dilution.

Status: `q2-cash-return-refresh; acquisition-cohort-return-open; no-ranking`.

Next joinable object: a post-close CEC/Stone Ridge cohort schedule connecting revenue, gross profit, billings, contract assets/liabilities, retainage, receivables, collections, integration costs, earn-outs, capex, and diluted shares.
