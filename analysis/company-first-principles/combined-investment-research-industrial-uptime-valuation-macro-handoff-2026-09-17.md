# Industrial uptime valuation and macro handoff — 2026-09-17

## Purpose

This memo carries the industrial-uptime candidates into the Damodaran and
Lyn-Alden-style layers without promoting reported cash screens to normalized
owner cash or pretending that the business models have identical capital
intensity.

The structured companion is the [industrial uptime valuation workbench](data/combined-investment-research-industrial-uptime-valuation-workbench-2026-09-17.csv).
It uses explicit low/base/high denominator-integrity haircuts rather than
pretending that a mechanical cash screen is intrinsic value.

## Damodaran-style valuation cases

The industrial workbench should use three cases rather than one perpetuity
assumption:

### Contracted/base case

- Sterling: convert signed backlog only, with project margin and collections
  held at a conservative normalized range; unsigned awards and future phases
  remain outside the base numerator.
- WESCO: use project-channel sales and cash conversion only after receivable,
  inventory, supplier, and acquisition effects are separated.
- Fastenal: use recurring onsite/digital replenishment economics with service
  cost and large-account mix carried explicitly.
- URI: separate fleet replacement capex from growth capex and model used-fleet
  proceeds as a volatile recovery stream rather than recurring revenue.

### Conversion case

Add a bounded share of unsigned awards or future phases only when the filing
shows conversion, margin, funding, and collection. Add acquired revenue only
with acquisition-specific cash, integration, earn-out, dilution, and return
assumptions.

### Stress case

- delay unsigned awards and future phases;
- reduce project margin and increase cost-to-complete uncertainty;
- reverse working-capital support and slow receivable collection;
- increase rates and credit spreads;
- lower fleet utilization and used-equipment recovery;
- maintain necessary replacement capex;
- retain dilution, NCI, leases, taxes, and debt claims.

The output remains a `qualified-expectation-screen` until the owner-cash
promotion matrix is satisfied.

## Lyn Alden-style transmission map

| Macro/liquidity condition | Industrial transmission | Filing observation that would confirm or weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Higher rates / tighter credit | Customer projects are deferred; Sterling's revolver and acquisition hurdle rise; URI fleet financing costs rise; WESCO/Fastenal customers reduce inventory or capex | Backlog cancellations, slower awards, borrowing-cost roll-forward, utilization, inventory turns, and cash interest |
| Fiscal and infrastructure spending | Utility, power, data-center, and public works demand supports electrical distribution, site execution, and rental equipment | End-market sales, signed backlog, funded project awards, customer concentration, and collections |
| Inflation in labor/materials | Contractor margin and cost-to-complete risk rise; distributors may face price/mix and inventory effects | Gross-margin revisions, claims, inventory valuation, supplier terms, and project estimate changes |
| Power scarcity and data-center buildout | Physical bottleneck raises value of site preparation, electrical integration, equipment access, and supply availability | Segment mix, project timing, backlog conversion, fleet productivity, and customer funding |
| Liquidity reversal | Working capital becomes a cash drain and financing capacity is consumed before revenue arrives | Receivables, inventory, contract assets/liabilities, supplier finance, revolver draws, and covenant headroom |

## QoE and financial-shenanigans gate

The valuation layer cannot override these diagnostics:

- Sterling percentage-of-completion estimates, claims, change orders,
  contract assets, retainage, acquisition contribution, and JV accounting;
- WESCO receivable/inventory growth, project backlog quality, acquisition
  effects, and current-liability or supplier-finance timing;
- Fastenal margin mix, service burden, inventory turns, onsite economics, and
  digital/physical channel classification;
- URI fleet capitalization, utilization, used-equipment proceeds, borrowing
  base, debt allocation, and replacement-versus-growth capex.

The overlay is a diagnostic register—not a fraud score. An unusual ratio can
bound a scenario or trigger a thesis breaker, but only same-entity,
same-period, comparable-taxonomy, and cash/claim evidence can promote the
denominator.
