# WESCO/Fastenal owner-cash burden bundle — pass 1 — 2026-09-17

## Denominator rule

The H1 2026 filings expose several cash burdens, but the categories are not
additive. Cash interest and cash income taxes are already reflected in
operating cash flow. They must not be subtracted a second time from OCF. Share
settlement taxes, dividends, buybacks, and lease additions are financing or
non-cash claim surfaces outside the mechanical OCF-less-property screen; they
must be shown separately before any owner-cash conclusion.

Amounts are USD millions.

The correct conclusion is not that WESCO has $223.5M or Fastenal has $521.1M
of normalized owner cash. Those figures are mechanical screens after reported
productive/property spending. The filing package now makes the surrounding
cash, claim, and financing surfaces visible without double counting them.

## WESCO interpretation

WESCO's H1 OCF was $275.1M against $51.6M of capital expenditures. The cash
flow statement also shows $641.0M of trade-receivable use, $432.4M of inventory
use, $726.0M of accounts-payable support, and $192.5M of other asset use in the
operating bridge. That working-capital movement is the central persistence
test. Cash interest of $172.3M and cash taxes of $154.6M are already embedded
in OCF; separately reported $48.1M share-settlement taxes and $48.8M common
dividends show post-OCF claim and distribution surfaces.

The H1 screen therefore remains a conversion-change diagnostic. It should not
be annualized until the next comparable period demonstrates that receivable,
inventory, supplier terms, acquisition effects, and lease obligations do not
reverse the improvement.

## Fastenal interpretation

Fastenal's H1 OCF was $644.1M against $123.0M of property and equipment
purchases. The operating bridge shows $320.2M of receivable use, $7.7M of
inventory release, $84.1M of accounts-payable support, $28.6M of accrued-
expense support, and $24.2M of income-tax movement. Cash taxes of $203.0M and
cash interest of $2.0M are already reflected in OCF. The company also paid
$550.9M of common dividends, repurchased $50.3M of common stock, and added
$63.0M of operating-lease assets.

Fastenal's H1 conversion remains closer to its FY2025 baseline, but the owner-
cash question is now explicitly tied to service economics, lease commitments,
large-account mix, dilution, and recurring reinvestment rather than to a
second subtraction of tax or interest.

## Q2 filing-detail refresh — 2026-09-18

The Q2 notes sharpen the reinvestment denominator without creating a
maintenance/growth split. WESCO says its `$51.6M` H1 capital expenditures were
primarily leasehold improvements, equipment for its location network, internal-
use software, and IT hardware. Its working-capital commentary separately
identifies customer-receipt timing and supplier prepayments as drivers of the
cash bridge. Those are useful burden classifications, but they do not identify
maintenance versus growth or prove recurring collection.

Fastenal describes five capital-spending categories—FMI hardware, facilities
and distribution-center expansion, software/IT, fleet vehicles, and
manufacturing equipment. Its H1 net capital expenditures were `$118.2M`,
including facility construction/upgrades, industrial vending, IT, and trucking;
2026 guidance is `$310M–$330M`. The filing supplies a category map and a
forward spending range, not category-level maintenance cash, service cost, or
owner residual.

The correct status remains `burden-bundle-pass-1; non-additive-denominator-
controlled; owner-cash-not-promoted`.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

These observations support Sloan-/Schilit-style follow-up prompts:

- receivable growth and collection timing;
- inventory and supplier-term support;
- capitalization through property, leases, or platform investment;
- tax and interest presentation boundaries;
- share-settlement taxes, repurchases, dividends, and dilution; and
- service costs that are not separately allocated in the filing taxonomy.

They do not support a Beneish score or a manipulation finding. A promotion
requires same-entity, same-period collection, maintenance/service, lease, tax,
and owner-claim joins with a consistent denominator.

## Promotion decision

This pass upgrades WESCO/Fastenal to
`reported-cash-conversion-screen; burden-bundle-pass-1`. It does not promote
either company to normalized owner cash or create a cross-company ranking.
The next decisive object remains a comparable quarter with the unresolved
maintenance/service and collection bundle.
