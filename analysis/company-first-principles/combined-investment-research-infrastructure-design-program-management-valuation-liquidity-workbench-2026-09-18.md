# Infrastructure design and program-management valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves AECOM from backlog and infrastructure-demand evidence into
a company-specific valuation, cash-conversion, claims, funding, and dilution
test. It separates fee-bearing adjusted net service revenue from gross
pass-through revenue and does not treat backlog, book-to-burn, adjusted EBITDA,
free cash flow, or buybacks as normalized owner cash.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AECOM | Adjusted-NSR cash after technical labor, subcontractors, receivable and contract-asset funding, project claims, international collection, capex, acquisitions, debt, and dilution | Technical staff and systems, project execution, capital expenditures, acquisitions, joint ventures, working capital, insurance, and professional-liability support | Public-budget or private-project delay, DSO expansion, contract claims, Middle East/Asia collection, customer approval, refinancing, or working-capital funding | Backlog grows while adjusted NSR, margin, collections, or cash per diluted share weakens; repeated working-capital releases or acquisitions mask poor project returns |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$16.140B`, adjusted NSR `$7.573B`, and backlog about
  `$24.8B`; FY2025 operating cash was `$821.6M`, capex `$136.7M`, and
  acquisitions `$212.5M`.
- Q2 FY2026 revenue was `$3.801B`, adjusted NSR `$1.948B`, backlog `$26.204B`,
  and adjusted operating margin `16.5%`, while free cash flow was negative
  `$27M` because Middle East collection and claim timing delayed cash.
- DSO rose from `74` days at September 30, 2025 to `83` days at March 31, 2026
  in the packet. FY2025 cash benefited from a `$334.4M` source from accounts
  receivable and contract assets after a `$500.8M` use in FY2024.
- FY2025 capital returns included about `$388.4M` of repurchases and `$133.6M`
  of dividends, while stock compensation was `$61.4M`; these remain claims on
  cash rather than evidence of surplus.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile gross revenue to adjusted NSR and test whether growth is fee-bearing
   or pass-through/subcontractor content.
2. Treat backlog and book-to-burn as future-work indicators, not profit or cash;
   review project estimates, change orders, claims, approvals, and cancellations.
3. Track receivables, contract assets, DSO, contract liabilities, payables, and
   accrued expenses across several quarters rather than capitalizing one cash
   release.
4. Separate Americas and International margins, collection, currency, legal,
   and political risk; do not apply the strongest region's margin globally.
5. Reconcile adjusted operating income, EBITDA, and FCF to SBC, interest,
   acquisitions, joint ventures, claims, debt, buybacks, dividends, and taxes.
6. Test acquired NSR, retention, margin, integration cost, goodwill, and cash
   return against acquisition price and joint-venture capital calls/distributions.
7. Charge technical labor, technology, insurance, professional liability,
   working-capital funding, and diluted shares before promoting residual cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what adjusted NSR growth, fee mix,
employee productivity, backlog conversion, regional margin, claims, working
capital, acquisition return, and cost of capital the equity price requires.
The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether public appropriations, private
infrastructure financing, rates, fiscal pressure, geopolitical risk, and
customer liquidity allow awarded work to start, bill, and collect on schedule.

## Promotion boundary

`infrastructure-design-qualified; backlog-conversion-and-collection-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period joins from adjusted NSR and backlog to project
margin, billings, receivables, contract assets, claims, regional cash, required
reinvestment, acquisition/JV return, debt, and diluted common residual. Gross
revenue, backlog, book-to-burn, adjusted metrics, FCF, dividends, and buybacks
remain diagnostic inputs.
