# Infrastructure engineering and program-management valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Jacobs Solutions from infrastructure-demand evidence into a
company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It keeps Infrastructure &
Advanced Facilities and PA Consulting separate from specialty contractors,
KBR's post-spin mission technology, and Huron's managed services, while
testing pass-through revenue, backlog quality, contract assets, PA acquisition
financing, employee consideration, debt, and diluted common-owner cash.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jacobs infrastructure engineering and program management | Fee and collected project cash after technical labor, subcontractors, pass-through cost, contract estimates, receivables, contract assets, PA integration, employee consideration, debt, taxes, and dilution | Engineering labor, project support, software/digital tools, working capital, contract assets, PA purchase price and retention, pensions, leases, debt, and SBC | Public/corporate capex pause, project estimate loss, customer approval delay, receivable/contract-asset buildup, PA underperformance, acquisition leverage, or dilution | Gross backlog and adjusted EBITDA rise while adjusted-net revenue, margin, collection, acquisition return, or diluted per-share cash weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$12.03B`, operating cash flow `$686.7M`, capex `$79.2M`,
  and recurring-capital residual about `$607.5M`; FY2024 cash included
  separation and working-capital effects and is not a clean permanent baseline.
- Q3 FY2026 revenue was `$4.076B`, backlog `$28.9B`, RPO about `$22.2B`, and
  trailing book-to-bill `1.4x` on gross revenue versus `1.2x` on adjusted net
  revenue.
- June 26, 2026 receivables and contract assets were approximately `$3.761B`,
  up from `$2.989B` at FY2025 year-end; nine-month OCF included a `$595M` use
  from those balances.
- Jacobs paid approximately `£997.6M` for the remaining PA shares, issued about
  `2.04M` shares, and owes another `£75M` of consideration in fiscal 2028.
- Nine-month cash uses included about `$917.6M` for PA redeemable NCI and related
  costs, `$614.1M` common repurchases, and `$123.4M` dividends; debt rose to
  approximately `$3.579B` from `$2.236B` at FY2025 year-end.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate gross revenue/backlog from adjusted net revenue and fee-bearing work;
   pass-through scale is not equivalent to economic control or owner cash.
2. Reconcile backlog and RPO to funded conversion, labor, subcontractors,
   contract assets, estimate changes, customer acceptance, receivables, and cash.
3. Keep PA transaction effects, employee consideration, amortization, taxes,
   integration, redeemable NCI, and share issuance visible in the acquisition
   return calculation.
4. Track contract assets, receivables, payables timing, restricted cash,
   pensions, leases, debt service, and the management-identified cash timing
   reversal.
5. Test repurchases and dividends after acquisition settlement, debt funding,
   employee claims, taxes, and diluted shares—not against adjusted EBITDA alone.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what adjusted-net revenue growth,
project margin, utilization, backlog conversion, PA return, working-capital
rate, reinvestment, and cost of capital are required. The valuation should
capitalize fee-bearing cash, not pass-through scale.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether public infrastructure, water,
transport, semiconductor, data-center, and life-science customers can fund
projects through higher rates, budget delays, and tighter credit. Liquidity
passes only when contract assets, receivables, employee claims, debt, and
common residual remain visible.

## Promotion boundary

`infrastructure-engineering-qualified; backlog-and-pa-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from adjusted-net backlog and
RPO to funded conversion, project margin, labor/subcontractor burden, contract
assets, collections, PA cash return, employee consideration, debt, SBC, and
diluted common residual. Gross revenue, backlog, book-to-bill, adjusted EBITDA,
OCF, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
