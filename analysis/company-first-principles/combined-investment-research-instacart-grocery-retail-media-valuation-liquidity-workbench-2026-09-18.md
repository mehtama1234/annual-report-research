# Instacart grocery and retail-media valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench tests Instacart as a grocery-demand aggregation, retailer-enablement, and retail-media platform. It keeps GTV, orders, shopper economics, retailer settlement, advertising, Storefront software, in-store technology, AI products, acquisitions, working capital, and diluted common residual separate from reported revenue and adjusted EBITDA.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Instacart / Maplebear | Grocery marketplace and retailer operating-layer cash after shopper and retailer settlement, incentives, ads, software, in-store technology, acquisitions, working capital, and diluted common residual | Marketplace technology, shopper support, retailer integrations, Storefront and in-store systems, AI products, acquisitions, customer/brand incentives, working capital, claims, and shares | Grocery volume slowdown, retailer disintermediation, price-parity pressure, shopper supply/cost inflation, advertising pullback, payment/settlement issues, or platform competition | GTV, orders, advertising, and adjusted EBITDA rise while retailer/consumer retention, take-rate conversion, settlement cash, technology costs, or diluted per-share cash weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 GTV was `$37.224B`, orders `338.8M`, revenue `$3.742B`, advertising and other revenue `$1.065B`, GAAP net income `$447M`, adjusted EBITDA `$1.087B`, and operating cash flow `$971M`.
- Q2 2026 GTV was `$10.351B`, orders `90.3M`, revenue `$1.043B`, advertising and other revenue `$297M`, GAAP net income `$111M`, adjusted EBITDA `$313M`, operating cash flow `$493M`, and free cash flow `$480M`.
- The platform served more than `2,200` retail banners and nearly `100,000` locations; Storefront technology powered more than `380` grocers' ecommerce sites.
- Instacart reported more than `26M` customers in 2025 and more than `310` Carrot Ads partners, making retailer, brand, consumer, and ad relationships part of one collection system.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile GTV and orders to retailer settlement, consumer charges, shopper payments, refunds, incentives, take rate, and collected cash.
2. Separate advertising and software economics from grocery-order economics; test whether ads and Storefront require ongoing integration and service costs.
3. Track price parity, no-markup programs, retailer concentration, customer retention, shopper supply, and fulfillment quality.
4. Keep acquisitions, stock compensation, working capital, payment obligations, and diluted shares in the common-owner residual schedule.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what GTV growth, order frequency, take rate, advertising yield, retailer software adoption, reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether grocery affordability, labor, delivery, payment, retailer bargaining, and ad-budget shocks reduce platform liquidity before reported growth reaches common owners.

## Promotion boundary

`instacart-qualified; grocery-demand-and-retail-media-collection-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from GTV and orders to retailer settlement, shopper and consumer claims, ad collections, software retention, required reinvestment, acquisitions, debt/claims, and diluted common residual. GTV, orders, active customers, advertising revenue, adjusted EBITDA, OCF, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
