# Installed network and subscription-platform valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Cisco Systems from installed-base and AI-order evidence
into a hardware-cycle, subscription, acquisition-return, and common-cash test.
It keeps Cisco's mature enterprise platform distinct from Arista/Ciena network
control, Cloudflare edge trust, and Broadcom custom silicon/software.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cisco Systems | Enterprise network, security, observability, collaboration, subscription, and support cash after hardware shipment, RPO delivery, inventory, receivables, Splunk return, SBC, debt, and dilution | Network/security R&D, support, software, supply chain, inventory, warranty, product refresh, Splunk integration, and customer migration | Enterprise or hyperscaler capex pause, AI-order concentration, supply-chain or collection stress, Splunk integration failure, debt, or buyback crowding | AI orders, subscription revenue, and RPO rise while gross margin, shipment conversion, Splunk retention, diluted cash per share, or installed-base control deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$56.654B`, subscription revenue `$31.5B`, operating
  cash flow `$14.193B`, and Cisco-defined FCF `$13.288B` after property
  purchases.
- FY2025 SBC was `$3.641B`; total debt was about `$28.093B`. Dividends and
  repurchases returned approximately `$12.4B`, or about `94%` of Cisco-defined
  FCF, before treating dilution as a recurring owner claim.
- Q3 FY2026 revenue was `$15.8B`; Cisco reported `$5.3B` of hyperscaler AI
  infrastructure orders through Q3 and raised the expectation to `$9B` for
  FY2026. Orders require shipment, margin, collection, and customer-
  concentration validation.
- Splunk adds security, observability, amortization, integration, retention,
  debt, and cross-sell-return requirements to the installed-base thesis.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile subscription revenue to hardware, software, support, RPO,
   deferred revenue, renewals, pricing, and gross margin.
2. Bridge AI orders through shipment, acceptance, inventory, receivables,
   deferred revenue, warranty, customer concentration, and cash collection.
3. Test Splunk revenue, net retention, cross-sell, integration, employee
   retention, amortization, debt cost, and acquisition return.
4. Treat SBC as a recurring platform labor claim; compare awards, repurchases,
   diluted shares, and cash per share.
5. Separate maintenance product investment and support from acquisition,
   restructuring, and hardware-cycle spending.
6. Treat orders, RPO, subscription mix, FCF, dividends, and buybacks as
   diagnostic until recurring per-share cash is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test converts installed base, subscription
renewal, RPO conversion, AI-order shipment, hardware/software mix, gross
margin, Splunk return, SBC, debt, and product reinvestment into owner cash. The
Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether enterprise and hyperscaler capital
spending, open networking, cloud-native security, rates, supply chains, and
cyber demand preserve Cisco's control through a hardware-cycle downturn. A
large installed base can support recurring cash while still requiring heavy
replacement and acquisition capital.

## Promotion boundary

`installed-network-qualified; subscription-conversion-and-splunk-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from installed-base renewal,
subscription/RPO delivery, AI orders, shipment, collection, inventory,
Splunk integration and return, support, SBC, debt, and diluted common residual.
Orders, RPO, subscription revenue, FCF, adjusted metrics, dividends, and
repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
