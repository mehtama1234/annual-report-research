# Insurance brokers versus carrier Q2 2026 cash-quality refresh

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Question

Does the latest same-period filing set show the different cash engines of an
insurance broker and an insurer without treating either adjusted earnings or
reported operating cash as normalized owner cash?

## Current-period observations

| Company / model | Q2 2026 observation | What it helps test | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marsh, broker/advisor | H1 operating cash `$835M`; commissions-and-fees receivables `$8.289B`; fiduciary cash `$12.203B`; H1 share compensation `$235M`; H1 acquisition consideration payments `$61M` | Fee collection, fiduciary-perimeter separation, acquisition and SBC burden | Unrestricted common-owner cash or acquired-cohort return |
| Arthur J. Gallagher, broker | H1 operating cash `$967M`; capital expenditures `$87M`; H1 acquisition-related earnout accretion `$22M`; H1 acquired-period revenue `$27M` | Organic-versus-acquired conversion, earnout economics, operating-capital timing | Cash paid for acquisitions, full producer retention, or normalized owner cash |
| Chubb, carrier | Q2 operating cash `$3.73B`; adjusted operating cash `$3.48B`; Q2 P&C net premiums earned rose `5.5%`; H1 cash-flow adjustments include unpaid losses, unearned premiums, future policy benefits, and insurance/reinsurance balances payable | Premium growth, reserve/liability timing, adjusted-cash reconciliation, capital return | Reserve adequacy, statutory-entity availability, catastrophe-normalized return, or common-owner residual |

## First-principles interpretation

Broker cash is a collection-and-acquisition problem: commissions and fees must
be collected, producers and acquired books retained, and fiduciary balances
kept separate from corporate cash. Gallagher's earnout adjustments and Marsh's
receivable growth show why reported operating cash needs a cohort and timing
bridge.

Carrier cash is a premium-and-liability problem: premiums arrive before or
around claims, while unpaid losses, policy benefits, reinsurance, and invested
assets shape the timing and risk. Chubb's adjusted operating cash is a useful
reconciliation input, not a substitute for reserve development, statutory
capital, catastrophe normalization, and policyholder claims.

## New Chubb liability and catastrophe boundary

Chubb's Q2 filing gives the carrier burden a measurable stress surface. During
H1 2026, net unpaid losses and loss expenses ended at `$71.216B`, while gross
unpaid losses ended at `$89.669B` and reinsurance recoverable was `$18.453B`.
The same reconciliation shows `$11.353B` of net losses paid and `$584M` of
favorable prior-year reserve development. These are not deductions to be
subtracted mechanically from operating cash a second time; they identify the
liability and reserve movements that must be normalized before calling adjusted
operating cash owner cash.

Chubb also reports modeled pre-tax catastrophe probable maximum losses of
`$5.650B` for a worldwide 1-in-100 annual aggregate scenario and `$3.766B` for
a U.S. hurricane 1-in-100 annual aggregate scenario, equal to 5.0% of total
shareholders' equity for the latter. The model assumes reinsurance recoverables
are fully collectible and is not a forecast of expected losses. It is therefore
a Lyn Alden-style liquidity and capital-stress input, not a forecast adjustment
or a reserve adequacy conclusion.

The new evidence tightens the carrier test: Chubb's reported and adjusted cash
must survive reserve development, claims paid, reinsurance collectibility,
catastrophe retention, statutory capital, and parent-availability analysis. It
does not close the legal-entity or common-owner residual gate.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

- Do not treat Marsh fiduciary cash as corporate cash.
- Do not treat broker EBITDAC, amortization, earnout remeasurement, or acquired
  revenue as recurring owner cash without acquisition and producer bridges.
- Do not treat Chubb adjusted operating cash as a clean cash proxy until unpaid
  losses, unearned premiums, policy benefits, reinsurance, realized gains, and
  statutory restrictions are reconciled.
- Do not compare broker fee receivables directly with carrier premiums or pool
  their margins, reserves, capital, or valuation multiples.

These are reconciliation controls, not allegations of misconduct.

## Decision

`insurance-broker-carrier-q2-cash-quality-visible; owner-cash-and-statutory-return-open`

The current filings make the contrast between fee intermediation and risk
intermediation clearer, but they do not close common-owner cash, acquired-cohort
return, reserve quality, or legal-entity availability. No cross-model ranking is
promoted.
