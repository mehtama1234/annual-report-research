# Intuitive Surgical installed-base cohort diagnostic — pass 2 — 2026-09-17

## Decision

The Q2 2026 filing now supports a stronger installed-base transmission
diagnostic: system counts, procedures, utilization, instruments/accessories,
services, and usage-based lease revenue move in the same operating chain. The
evidence still does not provide a clean cohort margin or lease cash-collection
ledger, so this pass is `qualified; diagnostic; no-ranking` and does not
promote revenue-per-system to owner cash.

## Same-period operating chain

| Object | Q2/H1 2026 observation | Mechanical screen | What remains unresolved |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| da Vinci installed systems | 11,710 at June 30; 10,488 prior year | +11.6% year over year | age, geography, sale/lease cohort, and system-level margin |
| Ion installed systems | 1,096 at June 30; 905 prior year | +21.1% year over year | utilization, procedure mix, and cohort economics |
| Worldwide procedures | Q2 growth about 16%; da Vinci about 15%; Ion about 36% | demand/use growth exceeds simple placement evidence | absolute procedure count and platform allocation |
| da Vinci utilization | procedures per system per year up 3% year over year | utilization is improving, not merely installed base | denominator construction and cash per procedure |
| Instruments/accessories revenue | H1 `$3.421B`; Q2 `$1.735B` | `$292k` H1 revenue per ending da Vinci system, orientation only | gross margin, instrument count, and platform allocation |
| Service revenue | H1 `$906M` | `$77k` H1 revenue per ending da Vinci system, orientation only | attachment, renewal, field labor, and parts cost |
| Operating-lease revenue | H1 `$512M`, including `$326M` variable | `$43.7k` H1 lease revenue per ending da Vinci system, orientation only | receivables, collections, allowances, and system recovery |

The recurring-revenue surface of instruments/accessories, services, and
operating leases is approximately `$4.839B` for H1. Dividing that amount by
the ending da Vinci system count produces a mechanical `$413k` per-system
screen, but it is not unit economics: Ion revenue, system sales, differing
system ages, leased versus sold equipment, and timing are all mixed into the
numerator or denominator.

## Lease-cohort boundary

Q2 placements included 254 da Vinci operating-lease systems, of which 131
were usage-based. Operating leases were 54% of total da Vinci placements. At
June 30, the da Vinci operating-lease base was 3,425 systems, including 1,903
usage-based systems; the Ion operating-lease base was 397, including 287
usage-based systems.

These disclosures establish a meaningful usage-linked business model. They do
not establish lease profitability or cash collection. The decisive ledger
would join each lease cohort to system cost, procedures, variable revenue,
receivables, deferred revenue, allowances, cancellations/exits, maintenance,
and cash receipts.

## Owner-cash and QoE controls

- H1 capitalized SBC was `$67.8M`; Q2 operating-income SBC was `$213M`.
  Income-statement, cash-flow, capitalized, employee-plan, and diluted-share
  scopes must be reconciled before treating SBC as a common-owner claim.
- Q2 repurchases were approximately `$380M` for 0.9 million shares. This is a
  financing outflow, not proof that all ending cash is distributable.
- The filing states usage-based lease revenue is recognized as procedures occur
  and may not recover system cost if utilization falls or a customer exits.
  This is the central financial-shenanigans/QoE test: revenue recognition can
  be correct while the lease cohort still earns a weak cash return.
- Inventory is already reflected in operating cash flow. It should be tested
  through turns, obsolescence, capacity, and subsequent release—not deducted
  twice from OCF.
- The `$28M` after-tax Q2 tariff-refund benefit is period-specific and must be
  separated from recurring procedure and installed-base economics.

## Promotion test and thesis breaker

Promotion requires a same-period cohort bridge with: systems by platform and
age; procedures per system; instruments/accessories per procedure; service
attachment and cost; usage-based lease receivables and cash collections;
inventory turns; support/R&D; SBC; repurchases; and diluted per-share cash.

The filing-based thesis breaker is activated if installed systems and reported
recurring revenue continue to grow while procedures per system, lease
collections, inventory turns, system-cost recovery, or diluted per-share cash
deteriorate. Until that test is populated, the correct conclusion is a strong
control-point hypothesis with unresolved owner-cash economics.
