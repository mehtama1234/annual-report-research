# Intuitive Surgical installed-base economics — pass 1

Research date: `2026-09-17`

The [structured installed-base register](data/combined-investment-research-intuitive-installed-base-economics-pass-1-2026-09-17.csv)
joins the FY2025 installed-system and revenue disclosures. The calculations
are deliberately labeled mechanical screens: they are not reported per-system
economics, because the filing does not provide a clean platform-, cohort-,
procedure-, margin-, and collection-level allocation in the preserved packet.

## Installed base and revenue layers

At year-end FY2025, the packet reports more than 11,100 da Vinci systems and
995 Ion systems installed, or more than 12,095 systems in total. FY2025 revenue
was approximately:

| Layer | Revenue |
| --- | ---: |
| Instruments and accessories | `$6.019B` |
| Services | `$1.572B` |
| Systems | `$2.474B` |
| Total revenue | `$10.065B` |
| Operating lease revenue | `$0.874B` |
| Variable usage-based lease revenue | `$0.531B` |

Instruments plus services were approximately `$7.591B`, or roughly `$627,600`
per mechanically counted installed system. Total revenue was roughly `$832,200`
per mechanically counted installed system. These are orientation ratios only:
they should not be read as unit economics because Ion and da Vinci have
different revenue profiles, systems may be under different sale or lease
structures, installed systems have different ages and utilization, and the
revenue figures are not assigned to the year-end installed base.

## What the mechanical screen does prove

The revenue mix supports the existence of a recurring-use layer beyond system
placements. Instruments and accessories were the largest revenue component,
services were material, and `$531M` of variable lease revenue was explicitly
tied to usage-based arrangements. This is stronger evidence of workflow
attachment than a system-count thesis alone.

The screen does not prove:

- procedures per system or per platform;
- instrument revenue or gross profit per procedure;
- service attachment, renewal, field labor, or parts cost;
- cash collection on usage-based lease revenue;
- inventory turns and obsolescence by product family; or
- per-share owner cash after SBC, repurchases, manufacturing, R&D, and capex.

## Quality-of-earnings and financial-shenanigans tests

The installed-base model creates a specific set of falsifiable tests:

1. system placements should be separated from actual procedure utilization;
2. instruments should be compared with procedures and installed-system age,
   not merely total revenue;
3. variable lease revenue should be reconciled to receivables, deferred
   revenue, allowances, and collections;
4. inventory growth should be tested against instruments demand, turns,
   excess/obsolete reserves, and manufacturing expansion; and
5. SBC and repurchases should be tested against diluted shares and cash per
   installed system, not excluded because operating cash is positive.

## Valuation consequence

The recorded historical market value of approximately `$131.9B` requires a
cash outcome materially above the conservative first-pass screen or a very
durable high multiple. The installed-base evidence supports duration, but the
valuation cannot be promoted by multiplying system count or revenue per system
alone. Damodaran inputs require procedure growth, reinvestment, margin,
competitive-advantage period, and cash conversion; the liquidity overlay must
stress hospital capital budgets, reimbursement, tariffs, labor, and platform
investment.
