# Intuitive Surgical Q2 2026 installed-base and cash-quality refresh

## Purpose

This pass updates the Intuitive Surgical clinical-workflow lane with Q2 2026
evidence. It tests whether system placement is converting into procedures,
instruments, services, usage-based lease revenue, and cash, while preserving
the risks from lease utilization, inventory, SBC, tariff benefits, tax effects,
and repurchases. It is not a unit-economics proof or valuation conclusion.

## Installed-base transmission is visible

As of June 30, 2026, Intuitive reported 11,710 da Vinci systems, up 12% from
10,488 a year earlier, and 1,096 Ion systems, up 21% from 905. Q2 placements
were 468 da Vinci systems and 55 Ion systems. Worldwide procedures grew about
16%; da Vinci procedures grew about 15% and Ion procedures about 36%.

This is stronger evidence than system placements alone because the filing links
instrument and accessory demand to procedure volume. Q2 instruments and
accessories revenue increased 18% to `$1.735B`; the company attributed the
increase primarily to procedure growth and higher instruments revenue per
procedure. The official Q2 update also reports that da Vinci utilization,
measured as procedures per system per year, increased 3% year over year.

The control-point chain is therefore currently observable:

`installed systems -> procedures per system -> instruments/accessories -> service -> usage-based lease revenue -> operating cash`

The chain is still not a completed owner-cash bridge because manufacturing,
support, inventory, R&D, SBC, lease receivables, and customer collection sit
between revenue and common-owner cash.

## Six-month recurring-revenue surface

For the six months ended June 30, 2026, Intuitive reported approximately:

- `$3.421B` of instruments and accessories revenue;
- `$906M` of service revenue; and
- `$512M` of operating lease revenue, including `$326M` of variable revenue
  primarily from usage-based arrangements.

Together these categories represent approximately `$4.839B` of the recurring-
revenue surface before considering cost, taxes, working capital, manufacturing
capacity, and lease collection. Six-month operating lease revenue rose from
`$409M` to `$512M`, and variable lease revenue from `$242M` to `$326M`.

This is economically attractive because it links the installed base to repeat
use. It also introduces variability: the filing says usage-based revenue is
recognized as procedures occur, can vary materially, and may not recover the
cost of the leased system if utilization falls short or customers exit or
cancel arrangements.

## Leasing is a cash and accounting control

In Q2, 254 da Vinci systems were placed under operating leases, including 131
usage-based arrangements; operating leases represented 54% of total da Vinci
placements. At June 30, the da Vinci operating-lease installed base was 3,425
systems, including 1,903 usage-based systems. The Ion operating-lease base was
397 systems, including 287 usage-based systems.

The next cash test must reconcile usage-based revenue to lease receivables,
deferred revenue, allowances, cash collections, system cost, and customer
exit/buyout behavior. A usage-linked revenue stream is not automatically a
high-return lease portfolio.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

### Non-GAAP and one-time benefit control

Q2 GAAP net income was `$818M` versus non-GAAP net income of `$1.00B`. Both
included a `$28M` after-tax benefit from refunds of prior-period tariffs under
IEEPA. The benefit should be separated from recurring procedure economics and
not capitalized into a steady-state earnings multiple. The company also
expects 2026 non-GAAP gross margin to include an estimated tariff impact of 1%
of revenue.

### SBC scope control

Q2 operating income included `$213M` of SBC. Separately, `$32.9M` of SBC was
capitalized to the balance sheet in Q2; six-month capitalized SBC was `$67.8M`.
The owner-cash model must reconcile income-statement SBC, cash-flow SBC,
capitalized SBC, employee-plan issuance, diluted shares, and repurchases. It
must not subtract or add the same compensation amount twice.

### Inventory and manufacturing control

The annual lane already identified approximately `$1.063B` of FY2025 inventory
cash use. The Q2 installed-base growth raises the economic question rather than
eliminating it: is inventory building ahead of durable procedure demand,
supporting supply resilience, or funding expansion? Inventory is already in
operating cash flow and must not be deducted twice; it must instead be tested
through turns, obsolescence, production capacity, and later cash release.

### Per-share cash control

Intuitive repurchased approximately 0.9 million shares for `$380M` in Q2. Cash,
cash equivalents, and investments ended the quarter at `$8.63B`, up `$650M`
primarily from operating cash and partly offset by repurchases. The balance
sheet is strong, but repurchases must be compared against SBC, capitalized SBC,
R&D, manufacturing capacity, and the diluted share count rather than treated as
proof that all reported cash is distributable.

## Interpretation

The Q2 filing materially strengthens Intuitive's installed-workflow thesis:
system growth is accompanied by procedure growth, higher utilization,
instrument pull-through, service growth, and usage-linked lease revenue. It
also clarifies the main falsifier: installed base can grow while lease
utilization, procedure mix, instruments per system, customer collections,
inventory turns, or per-share cash deteriorate.
