# Ivanhoe African copper buildout valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench tests Ivanhoe Mines as a JV-heavy African copper and development platform rather than as a simple copper-price proxy. It keeps Kamoa-Kakula attributable cash, Kipushi inventory, Platreef construction, smelter ramp, corridor logistics, project finance, tax claims, debt, and dilution in one company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ivanhoe Mines | Attributable copper-and-development cash after JV ownership, smelter integration, logistics, project construction, inventory, taxes, debt, and diluted common residual | Kamoa-Kakula, Kipushi, and Platreef mine/smelter capex, sustaining capital, corridor and power infrastructure, working capital, project finance, exploration, taxes, and shares | Copper/zinc/PGM price decline, JV tax dispute, smelter or rail interruption, inventory buildup, cost inflation, host-country action, debt service, or delayed project ramp | Kamoa output and JV profit rise while attributable cash, inventory conversion, Platreef return, smelter economics, or diluted per-share residual weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- Q2 2026 profit attributable to owners was `$49.7M`; cash was about `$635M`; Platreef H1 capital expenditure was about `$150M`.
- FY2025 Kamoa-Kakula sold `351,674` tonnes of payable copper and generated about `$3.281B` of JV revenue, while Ivanhoe owns `49.5%`; JV-level production is not automatically common-owner cash.
- FY2025 cash and short-term deposits were about `$885M`, total assets about `$7.626B`, and liabilities about `$1.901B`.
- The company issued `$750M` of `7.875%` senior notes due `2030` and continued heavy Platreef development spending.
- The Kamoa smelter ramp, sulphuric-acid credits, copper destocking, Kipushi concentrate inventory, and Lobito Corridor reliability are operating-to-cash controls, not narrative detail.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile JV production and profit to Ivanhoe's attributable cash, distributions, financing, tax, and noncontrolling interests.
2. Track copper and zinc inventory, concentrate/anode shipment timing, corridor charges, smelter credits, and working capital before calling earnings durable.
3. Separate sustaining capital from Platreef/Kamoa growth capital and test each project against completion, throughput, funding, and return.
4. Keep tax claims, project finance, senior notes, host-country obligations, and diluted shares in the residual schedule.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what copper prices, payable production, JV attribution, smelter margin, project capex, ramp timing, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether energy, rail, flood, geopolitical, tax, currency, and financing shocks can interrupt the path from African ore and concentrates to collected common-owner cash.

## Promotion boundary

`ivanhoe-qualified; attributable-copper-and-buildout-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from mine output and JV profit to distributions, inventory liquidation, smelter/logistics costs, project capex, taxes, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Copper production, JV revenue, adjusted EBITDA, reported OCF, and project guidance remain diagnostic inputs.
