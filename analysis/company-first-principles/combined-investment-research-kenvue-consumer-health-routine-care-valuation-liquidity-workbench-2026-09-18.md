# Consumer health, routine care, and transaction-overhang valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Kenvue from the annual-report archive into a
company-specific consumer-health, self-care, skin-health, oral-care, brand,
retailer, e-commerce, seasonal-incidence, transaction, and common-owner residual
test. It keeps Kenvue separate from packaged food, general household staples,
medical devices, and the pending Kimberly-Clark combination.

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net sales were approximately `$15.1B`, down `2.1%`; organic sales declined `2.2%`; OCF was `$2.2B`; FCF was `$1.7B`; diluted EPS was `$0.76`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.08`.
- Q2 2026 net sales grew `3.0%`, organic sales `1.6%`, diluted EPS was `$0.24`, adjusted diluted EPS `$0.31`, H1 OCF `$1.2B`, and H1 FCF `$1.0B`.
- Kenvue serves approximately `1.2B` people in more than `165` countries, with about `50%` of sales outside North America and three reporting segments. Scale does not eliminate category-incidence and retailer-execution risk.
- Skin Health and Beauty grew `8.4%` in Q1 2026 and Essential Health `4.9%`; Self Care remains more incidence-sensitive. Segment and need-state mix matter more than consolidated consumer-health growth.
- Kenvue has withheld forward guidance while pursuing its proposed Kimberly-Clark combination. Transaction timing, costs, financing, integration, and common-owner effects remain open.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile sales through category incidence, units, price/mix, retailer
   inventory, e-commerce sell-through, promotions, returns, and regional mix.
2. Separate Self Care, Skin Health and Beauty, and Essential Health by volume,
   margin, innovation, quality/regulatory burden, and working capital.
3. Test brand and product-quality investment, recalls, regulatory claims,
   manufacturing, distribution, and retailer/e-commerce activation before
   treating margin improvement as durable.
4. Roll Kimberly-Clark transaction costs, integration, debt, financing,
   stranded costs, tax, legal claims, and diluted shares.
5. Treat organic growth, adjusted EPS, FCF, dividends, and buybacks as
   diagnostic until routine-care cash survives seasonality and transaction burden.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what category incidence, volume,
price/mix, brand reinvestment, innovation return, e-commerce growth, margin,
working capital, transaction cost, debt, and cost of capital the equity value
requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether household affordability,
seasonality, retailer power, product-quality events, rates, refinancing, and
transaction liquidity preserve cash through a consumer-health cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`kenvue-consumer-health-qualified; incidence-brand-transaction-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from need-state demand to
sell-through, collection, product quality, innovation and marketing capital,
transaction cash, debt, taxes, and diluted common residual. Revenue, organic
growth, adjusted EPS, FCF, guidance absence, dividends, and buybacks remain
diagnostic inputs.
