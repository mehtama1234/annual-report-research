# KLA cycle-normalized owner-cash workbench — pass 2 — 2026-09-17

## Decision

KLA's FY2026 `$3.767B` reported free-cash-flow screen is strong but not a
cycle-normalized owner-cash denominator. The filing shows that cash conversion
also depends on receivable factoring, customer advances, inventory and
receivable funding, large R&D and purchase commitments, customer concentration,
SBC, debt, and aggressive capital returns. Status:
`cycle-normalization-workbench; reported-cash-qualified; owner-cash-open; no-ranking`.

`$873.9M` before considering other operating items:

`641.8 + 466.0 - 172.4 - 116.1 + 54.6 = 873.9`

That calculation is a diagnostic reconstruction, not a substitute for the
filed cash-flow statement. It shows why a single-year OCF-less-PP&E screen can
look distributable while the operating system is absorbing substantial capital.

## Financial-shenanigans and QoE controls

1. Reconcile the `$515.5M` receivable factoring proceeds to reported OCF and
   model a no-factoring or unwind case; non-recourse legal form does not make
   accelerated customer collection economically recurring.
2. Treat deferred system and service revenue as performance obligations, not
   free cash. Test acceptance, delivery, renewal, cancellations, and margin.
3. Stress the 19% single-customer exposure and approximately 87% international
   revenue against fab-cycle, export-control, currency, and credit shocks.
4. Carry R&D, technical labor, field service, inventory, and purchase
   commitments into maintenance/growth analysis even though only PP&E is in
   the mechanical FCF screen.
5. Reconcile `$2.290B` buybacks and `$1.058B` dividends with `$310.2M` SBC,
   `$168.6M` common-stock issuance, `$205.0M` tax withholding, debt service,
   and liquidity rather than treating gross repurchases as pure value creation.

## Damodaran and Lyn Alden handoff

The valuation denominator must be a range: reported OCF-less-PP&E, no-factoring
cash, cycle-normalized working-capital cash, and cash after technical/R&D and
commitment requirements. Damodaran-style growth requires service durability,
reinvestment, competitive-advantage period, and capital efficiency. The
Lyn-Alden-style stress case applies semiconductor capex contraction, rates,
export controls, customer concentration, inventory build, factoring unwind,
and floating debt together.

## Promotion test and thesis breaker

Promotion requires a multi-year service-per-installed-tool and cash-conversion
panel, factoring unwind, customer/geo concentration, deferred-revenue delivery,
commitment settlement, R&D/support maintenance, SBC/dilution, and per-share
cash. The thesis breaker is activated if service and reported revenue grow while
receivables/inventory require recurring funding, factoring rises, deferred
revenue fails to convert into delivery margin, and capital returns consume
liquidity or debt capacity.
