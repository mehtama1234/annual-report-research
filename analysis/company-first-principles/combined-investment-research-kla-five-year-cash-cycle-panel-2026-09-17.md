# KLA five-year cash-cycle panel — FY2022–FY2026

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This panel upgrades the KLA process-control lane from a single-year cash
surface to a five-year, same-fiscal-year trend using the locally preserved SEC
company-facts history. It is a cycle diagnostic, not a normalized owner-cash
conclusion or manipulation finding.

## Observed panel

| Fiscal year | Revenue | OCF | PP&E capex | OCF less PP&E | OCF margin | AR + inventory - AP |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| FY2022 | `$9.212B` | `$3.313B` | `$307M` | `$3.005B` | `36.0%` | `$3.515B` |
| FY2023 | `$10.496B` | `$3.670B` | `$342M` | `$3.328B` | `35.0%` | `$4.259B` |
| FY2024 | `$9.812B` | `$3.309B` | `$277M` | `$3.031B` | `33.7%` | `$4.508B` |
| FY2025 | `$12.156B` | `$4.082B` | `$335M` | `$3.747B` | `33.6%` | `$5.018B` |
| FY2026 | `$13.579B` | `$4.143B` | `$376M` | `$3.767B` | `30.5%` | `$5.914B` |

Amounts are rounded from the SEC facts in the structured companion. The
working-capital column is a balance-sheet exposure screen, not a cash-flow
statement reconstruction and not a claim that all balances are operating
capital.

## What changed across the cycle

Revenue grew from `$9.212B` in FY2022 to `$13.579B` in FY2026, while reported
OCF-less-PP&E also grew from `$3.005B` to `$3.767B`. That headline is less
clean than it appears: OCF margin declined by about 5.5 percentage points, and
the receivable-plus-inventory-less-payable exposure increased by approximately
`$2.399B`.

The 2026 filing separately identifies `$515.5M` of non-recourse receivables
factoring, compared with `$230.6M` in FY2025, plus deferred system and service
revenue and material purchase commitments. The five-year panel therefore
supports a narrower conclusion: KLA has demonstrated strong reported cash
generation, but the cash conversion surface is becoming more dependent on
working-capital scale, customer funding, and cycle-sensitive operating assets.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans tests

1. Treat the OCF-margin decline as a prompt to reconcile price, mix, customer
   concentration, service growth, receivable days, inventory turns, and
   contract liabilities—not as evidence of accounting misconduct.
2. Test whether the `$2.399B` increase in the working-capital exposure screen
   reverses in a down-cycle or requires continuing customer/supplier funding.
3. Add factoring proceeds to each comparable period when available; FY2026's
   `$515.5M` versus FY2025's `$230.6M` requires a no-factoring and unwind case.
4. Keep R&D, service labor, parts, warranty, deferred-revenue delivery, and
   purchase commitments outside the narrow PP&E screen until maintenance and
   growth components are evidenced.
5. Reconcile capital returns and SBC to the cycle-adjusted diluted share
   count; a stable gross buyback amount is not proof of stable per-share cash.

## Valuation and promotion implication

The panel improves the Damodaran input by replacing a peak-year cash point with
a five-year observed range. It does not yet identify normalized reinvestment,
service-per-installed-tool economics, factoring unwind, or cycle-adjusted
common-owner cash. The Lyn Alden stress case is now more concrete: a capex
slowdown paired with inventory build, receivable delay, factoring withdrawal,
export restrictions, and floating-rate debt can reduce owner cash faster than
reported revenue suggests.

Status:

`five-year-cycle-panel; reported-cash-qualified; cycle-normalized-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion still requires service-installed-base, acceptance, factoring,
working-capital settlement, R&D/support maintenance, commitment, debt, SBC,
and diluted per-share joins. The next source object is a comparable multi-year
service and cash-conversion schedule with factoring and customer concentration
controls. The [installed-base denominator boundary](combined-investment-research-kla-installed-base-denominator-boundary-2026-09-17.md)
records that the FY2026 10-K names installed systems, utilization, and renewal
as drivers but does not quantify them.
