# KLA FY2026 installed-service and owner-cash refresh

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This refresh advances the [KLA FY2026 QoE forensic refresh](combined-investment-research-kla-fy2026-qoe-forensic-refresh-2026-09-17.md) with the annual installed-base, factoring, deferred-revenue, customer-concentration, commitment, and capital-return evidence. It does not treat free cash flow or service revenue as a completed cycle-normalized owner-cash denominator.

## Cash and capital-return bridge

KLA reported FY2026 operating cash flow of `$4.143B`, capital expenditures of `$375.945M`, and free cash flow of `$3.767B`.

```text
operating cash flow                         $4,143.079M
  - capital expenditures                      $375.945M
  = reported free cash flow                  $3,767.134M
  - dividends and dividend equivalents      $1,057.832M
  - common-stock repurchases                 $2,289.769M
  = residual after listed capital returns     $419.533M
```

The `$419.533M` residual is a stress screen, not a normalized owner-cash measure. It excludes debt principal, taxes, lease claims, required liquidity, R&D, service-support reinvestment, working-capital normalization, and the economic cost of stock compensation. KLA's reported capital returns therefore consumed approximately 89% of reported free cash flow in FY2026.

## Installed service and cycle exposure

Services represented approximately 23% of FY2026 revenue, and international revenue approximately 87%. This is a meaningful recurring installed-base signal, but the service denominator remains exposed to customer fab utilization, tool uptime, export restrictions, geography, and semiconductor-capital spending cycles. R&D was `$1.532B` and is expensed as incurred; it is an economic reinvestment claim even though it is not a capital-expenditure deduction.

The correct owner-cash test is therefore not simply OCF less PP&E. It must separate maintenance/support R&D from growth R&D, service labor and parts, customer-financed system and service advances, and the cash required to maintain the technical lead through a down-cycle.

### Management expectation boundary

KLA's March 2026 Investor Day introduced a management 2030 target model of
approximately `$26B ± $2.5B` revenue, a 13–15% long-term Services revenue CAGR,
and a target to allocate more than 90% of free cash flow to capital returns.
These are useful Damodaran-style expectation inputs and a capital-allocation
stress case, not filing-proven installed-base economics. The model still
requires numeric systems in field, utilization, renewal, service margin,
factoring unwind, R&D/support reinvestment, and diluted-share assumptions
before it can support an owner-cash valuation.

## Receivables, factoring, and deferred revenue

Accounts receivable increased to `$2.889B` from `$2.264B`, up 28%; contract assets increased to `$131.7M` from `$105.1M`; and long-term receivables were `$180.7M`. KLA sold `$515.5M` of receivables under non-recourse factoring agreements in FY2026, versus `$230.6M` in FY2025. The filing warns that stopping factoring could adversely affect cash flow through collection delays or failures and creates counterparty exposure to the financial institutions involved.

Deferred system revenue was `$932.9M` and deferred service revenue `$604.1M` at year-end. Deferred revenue is a customer-funded operating liability and can support cash conversion, but it is not free cash: the company still owes systems, service, support, and performance. The bridge must test whether deferred revenue growth is matched by delivery, margin, service cost, and future working-capital requirements.

## Customer concentration and commitment burden

One customer represented approximately 19% of total revenue in each of FY2026 and FY2025. KLA says the majority of receivables come from large multinational semiconductor and electronics manufacturers, primarily in Asia, and generally require little or no collateral. Customer concentration and limited collateral are therefore simultaneous demand and credit risks.

KLA reported approximately `$5.97B` of significant purchase commitments, a majority due within twelve months. Those commitments are not automatically liabilities or maintenance capex, but they are a supplier and liquidity claim that matters if semiconductor capex slows, customers delay tool acceptance, or export controls interrupt delivery.

## Debt, liquidity, and dilution

Long-term debt was `$5.887B`; cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities totaled `$4.902B`, of which `$735.1M` was held by foreign subsidiaries and branches. KLA also had a `$1.5B` revolving credit facility. Stock-based compensation expense was `$310.2M`, and the company had `$9.74B` of repurchase authorization available at June 30, 2026.

The authorization is not a claim that repurchases should occur. It is an optionality and per-share-risk input: if cycle cash weakens while the company continues repurchases, the capital-return policy can consume liquidity or increase debt claims. If repurchases stop, per-share growth assumptions must be recalculated rather than treated as operational deterioration.

## Quality-of-earnings and financial-shenanigans controls

1. **Factoring:** reconcile reported OCF and receivable growth to the `$515M` of receivables sold; test whether cash conversion depends on repeated monetization.
2. **Customer acceptance:** separate system revenue, deferred system revenue, contract assets, customer deposits, acceptance terms, and collection from shipment headlines.
3. **Service durability:** test 23% service revenue against installed tools, utilization, service labor, parts, pricing, and customer capex cycles.
4. **Capitalization:** carry `$1.532B` R&D and support investment as an economic claim even though it is expensed rather than capitalized.
5. **Commitments:** stress the `$5.97B` purchase-commitment book against order cancellations, export controls, inventory, and supplier settlements.
6. **Concentration:** test the 19% customer and Asian receivable concentration against fab spending, credit, currency, and geopolitical disruption.
7. **Capital returns:** do not treat `$3.767B` FCF as fully distributable while R&D, factoring, debt, commitments, and cycle-normalized reinvestment remain open.

## Decision

KLA's FY2026 results strengthen the installed-service and process-control thesis, but they also reveal the exact burden surface that a cycle-normalized valuation must absorb: receivable growth, factoring, deferred-revenue delivery obligations, customer concentration, R&D, purchase commitments, debt, SBC, and aggressive capital returns. The classification is `fy2026-installed-service-cash-updated; cycle-normalization-and-owner-claim-open; no-ranking`.

Promotion requires a multi-year service-per-installed-system and cash-conversion panel, factoring unwind sensitivity, customer concentration stress, delivery/acceptance evidence, R&D/support allocation, commitment settlement, and diluted common-owner cash. Until then, KLA remains a qualified semiconductor diagnostic rather than a promoted owner-cash candidate.
