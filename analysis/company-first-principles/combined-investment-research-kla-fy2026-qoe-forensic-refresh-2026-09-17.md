# KLA FY2026 QoE and financial-integrity forensic refresh

## Purpose

This pass upgrades the semiconductor process-control lane from a thematic
control-point case to a filing-backed quality-of-earnings diagnostic. It uses
KLA's fiscal-year 2026 Form 10-K and tests whether the reported cash surface is
supported by customer concentration, geographic exposure, working-capital
settlement, contract liabilities, receivables factoring, commitments, taxes,
debt, SBC, and repurchases. It is not a manipulation finding, a fraud score, or
a normalized owner-cash conclusion.

## Control point and revenue mix

KLA describes its products as inspection, metrology, process-control software,
and related services used from R&D through high-volume production. Services
were approximately 23% of FY2026 revenue and increased to `$3.126B` from
`$2.683B`, a 16% increase. Total revenue was `$13.579B`, up 12%; wafer
inspection was `$6.631B`, patterning `$2.707B`, and the remaining product and
service categories supplied the balance.

The service result is economically important because KLA says service revenue
is typically a function of installed systems and utilization, but it is not
automatically recurring owner cash: service contracts still require field
labor, technical support, inventory, warranty, and customer collection.

## The most important forensic observations

### 1. Customer and geographic concentration

One customer—identified in the geographic and segment note as Taiwan
Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited—accounted for approximately 19% of
total revenue in both FY2026 and FY2025, versus 13% in FY2024. International
revenue was 87% of FY2026 revenue. China, Taiwan, and Korea together were
approximately 70.1% of FY2026 revenue based on ship-to location.

This is a control-point strength and a thesis risk at the same time. KLA may
occupy an indispensable yield-management position, but a single customer's
capital cycle, export restrictions, payment behavior, or procurement leverage
can move revenue and cash. Revenue growth must therefore be stress-tested by
customer, geography, product category, and service renewal—not treated as
independent demand.

### 2. Cash conversion required roughly `$1.1B` of additional operating assets

Accounts receivable rose from `$2.264B` to `$2.889B`, a 28% increase, while
inventory rose from `$3.212B` to `$3.649B`, a 14% increase. The cash-flow
statement records a `$641.8M` use from receivables and a `$466.0M` use from
inventory. Accounts payable supplied `$172.4M`, while deferred system revenue
supplied `$116.1M`; deferred service revenue was a `$54.6M` use.

Operating cash flow was still strong at `$4.143B`, but the mix matters. A
reported cash-after-PP&E screen should not be read as cash that is freely
available to owners without asking whether the next cycle reverses these
working-capital investments or requires another inventory build.

### 3. Contract liabilities support cash, but are not the same as earned margin

At June 30, 2026, current deferred system revenue was `$932.9M` and current
deferred service revenue was `$604.1M`; non-current deferred service revenue was
`$238.1M`. The cash-flow statement shows the year-over-year movement in deferred
system revenue added `$116.1M` to operating cash, while deferred service revenue
reduced it by `$54.6M`.

These balances are useful evidence of customer deposits or advance payment
mechanics, but they are contract liabilities that require future performance.
They cannot be used to argue that all current cash conversion is repeatable
without testing delivery, acceptance, renewal, and cancellation terms.

### 4. Factoring is a real cash-conversion control, not a footnote

KLA sold `$515.5M` of receivables under factoring agreements in FY2026, up from
`$230.6M` in FY2025, and sold `$86.0M` of customer letters of credit, up from
`$55.5M`. The arrangements were without recourse and fees were not material,
but the proceeds still belong in the cash-conversion reconciliation. Factoring
can accelerate cash without changing the underlying customer collection cycle;
the relevant test is whether this source is ordinary, stable, and already
embedded in the historical comparison.

### 5. Economic maintenance is broader than PP&E

KLA spent `$375.9M` on capital expenditures, but FY2026 R&D was `$1.532B`, or
11% of revenue, and increased 13%. KLA states that continued product
development and engineering investment are essential to its competitive
position. It also reports `$5.97B` of significant purchase commitments, a
majority due within the next twelve months, plus `$301.5M` of operating-lease
obligations.

The current `$3.767B` OCF-less-PP&E screen is therefore a deliberately narrow
reported-cash surface. A conservative owner-cash model must test what portion
of R&D, technical labor, service inventory, warranty, and purchase commitments
is required to preserve the installed-base and process-control position.

### 6. SBC and repurchases must be analyzed together

FY2026 stock-based compensation was `$310.2M`, while common-stock repurchases
were `$2.290B`; dividends and dividend equivalents were `$1.058B`. The
stockholders' equity statement also shows `$168.6M` of common-stock issuance and
`$205.0M` of tax-withholding payments related to vested awards.

The correct question is not whether buybacks reduce the reported share count in
the current year. It is whether repurchases create per-share value after SBC,
employee-plan issuance, withholding, debt service, and the reinvestment needed
to maintain KLA's technical moat.

## Liquidity and rate transmission

KLA held `$4.90B` of cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities and had
`$1.50B` of undrawn revolving capacity at June 30, 2026. It had `$5.95B` of
senior-notes principal and had swapped `$2.00B` of fixed-rate debt into floating
exposure. A 100-basis-point increase would raise annual interest expense on the
swapped amount by approximately `$20M`, according to the filing.

This makes the Lyn Alden-style transmission concrete: a semiconductor capex
slowdown can hit orders and inventory while higher rates affect both the
customer's fab economics and KLA's floating debt. The balance sheet provides
liquidity, but it does not eliminate cycle or concentration risk.

## Current interpretation

KLA remains a compelling control-point case, but the quality of its cash surface
is more conditional than a simple OCF-less-PP&E multiple suggests. The filing
supports four linked tests:

`customer concentration -> orders and geography -> receivable/inventory funding -> factoring and contract-liability support -> R&D/service maintenance -> diluted owner cash`

Current status:

`KLA-fy2026-qoe-upgrade; reported-cash-screen-qualified; normalized-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

The next promotion object is a period-matched multi-year bridge joining
customer concentration, order/backlog or acceptance timing, factoring,
inventory and receivable settlement, service renewal economics, R&D/service
maintenance burden, SBC, and per-share cash. Until that exists, the `$3.767B`
screen is a diagnostic denominator, not intrinsic value.
