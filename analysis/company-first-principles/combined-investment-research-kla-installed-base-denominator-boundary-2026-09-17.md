# KLA installed-base denominator boundary — 2026-09-17

## Finding

KLA's FY2026 Form 10-K states that service revenue is typically a function of
the number of systems installed at customer sites and their utilization, and
is also affected by service-contract renewals, system type, and foreign
currency. The filing quantifies service revenue at `$3.126B` and its 16% growth,
but does not provide a numeric installed-system count, utilization rate,
renewal rate, or service revenue per installed system in the checked filing.

The result is `searched-negative` for a quantitative installed-base
denominator in the FY2026 10-K—not evidence that KLA lacks an installed base or
that service revenue is not durable.

KLA's March 2026 Investor Day supplies a forward management scenario—13–15%
Services revenue CAGR within a `$26B ± $2.5B` 2030 revenue model—but does not
replace the missing installed-system, utilization, renewal, or service-margin
denominator. It is therefore routed to valuation expectations, not promoted
to operating proof.

## Why this matters

The economic chain is:

`installed systems × utilization × renewal/service mix × price -> service revenue -> service labor, parts, warranty, collection -> owner cash`

Only the aggregate service-revenue node is quantified. Treating the 16% growth
as recurring installed-base cash would silently assume utilization, renewal,
pricing, service cost, customer credit, and FX effects.

## Promotion boundary

The next joinable object is a company-reported installed-base, service-contract
renewal, utilization, or service-margin schedule; alternatively, a matched
multi-year service-per-system dataset with a clearly defined denominator. Until
then, service revenue remains a control-point indicator and not a normalized
owner-cash denominator.
