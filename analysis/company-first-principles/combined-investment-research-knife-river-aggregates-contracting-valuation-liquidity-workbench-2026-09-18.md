# Knife River aggregates and contracting valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Knife River from current-period evidence into a company-
specific valuation, reserve, backlog, contracting, acquisition, leverage, and
common-owner cash test. It does not pool quarry scarcity, downstream materials,
and public-project contracting into a generic building-materials multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Knife River | Collected aggregates and contracting cash after reserve depletion, pricing, plant and fleet, energy, asphalt inputs, project execution, acquisitions, maintenance/growth capex, debt, and dilution | Quarries, plants, hauling, ready-mix, asphalt, greenfields, acquisitions, equipment, backlog conversion, and working capital | Weather, project delay, public-award timing, energy/freight, contractor mix, reserve permitting, acquisition integration, leverage, or margin compression | Backlog and pricing rise while aggregate volume, project conversion, reserve economics, acquisition returns, leverage, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Knife River controls approximately `1.3B` tons of aggregate reserves; about `35%` of 2025 aggregate volume was used internally in downstream products and contracting.
- Full-year 2025 revenue was `$3.146B`, net income `$157.1M`, and adjusted EBITDA `$496.5M`; year-end backlog was `$1.0B`, about `89%` tied to publicly funded projects.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$938.6M`, net income `$43.9M`, and adjusted EBITDA `$139.7M`; contracting-services revenue rose `20%` and product-mix-adjusted aggregate pricing improved `8%`.
- First-half 2026 growth investment was `$244.5M`, including `$184.4M` of acquisitions and `$60.1M` of aggregate expansions and greenfields; net leverage reached `3.2x` at June 30.
- Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was negative `$31.8M`, illustrating winter seasonality and the difference between annual backlog quality and quarterly cash conversion.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile aggregate tons sold to reserve depletion, pricing, internal pull-
   through, ready-mix, asphalt, contracting, project acceptance, and collections.
2. Separate public backlog from awarded-but-unfunded, delayed, weather-affected,
   or margin-variable work; backlog is not collected owner cash.
3. Keep quarries, plants, hauling, equipment, greenfields, acquisitions, asphalt
   inputs, maintenance capex, and seasonal working capital in the denominator.
4. Test whether acquisition-led footprint growth creates incremental collected cash
   after integration, reserve replacement, capex, and leverage.
5. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA, project claims, contract assets, debt, interest,
   taxes, noncontrolling claims, and diluted shares before promoting FCF.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what aggregate volume, price, reserve
life, downstream pull-through, backlog conversion, margin, capex, acquisition return,
and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks
whether public infrastructure budgets, weather, energy, freight, population growth,
project timing, and leverage can support liquidity through a construction cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`knife-river-aggregates-contracting-qualified; backlog-reserve-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from tons, plants, and backlog to
project acceptance, customer collections, reserve depletion, required capex,
acquisition return, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, backlog,
pricing, adjusted EBITDA, guidance, and public-funding share remain diagnostic.
