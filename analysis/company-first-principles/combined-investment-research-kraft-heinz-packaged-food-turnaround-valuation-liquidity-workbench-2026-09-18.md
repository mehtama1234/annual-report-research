# Kraft Heinz packaged-food turnaround valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Kraft Heinz from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests whether legacy grocery brands can recover volume and share through commercial reinvestment without impairing margin, cash conversion, dividend capacity, leverage, or diluted common residual. It does not pool Kraft Heinz with Brown-Forman, PepsiCo, Mondelez, or other staples into one ranking.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kraft Heinz | Center-store brand and meal platform cash after price/value architecture, volume recovery, marketing, innovation, supply chain, taxes, dividend, debt, and dilution | Brand and commercial investment, R&D, packaging, inventory, working capital, portfolio actions, dividend, debt, and shares | Volume/share erosion, consumer trade-down, commodity and tariff inflation, reinvestment overspend, leverage, dividend burden, or failed separation strategy | Organic sales or adjusted EPS stabilize while volume, share, brand returns, free cash flow after reinvestment, leverage, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- 2025 net sales were `$24.942B`, organic net sales declined `3.4%`, adjusted operating income was `$4.745B`, adjusted EPS was `$2.60`, and free cash flow was `$3.7B`.
- Q2 2026 organic net sales declined `1.3%`, constant-currency adjusted operating income declined `18.4%`, adjusted EPS was `$0.56`, and free cash flow increased `10%`.
- Management raised planned 2026 incremental investment by `$100M` to approximately `$700M`, after initially announcing roughly `$600M` for marketing, sales, R&D, product superiority, and selective pricing.
- The business is explicitly in repair mode: North America and share trends remain pressured, while affordability, Taste Elevation, Hydration, international momentum, and brand reinvestment are the recovery variables.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate price, mix, volume, distribution, market share, and category effects; reported sales and adjusted EPS are not proof of brand recovery.
2. Reconcile free cash flow to inventory, payables, commodity and tariff effects, marketing and R&D investment, restructuring, taxes, dividends, debt, and dilution.
3. Test whether the incremental commercial spend produces durable volume and share or merely supports short-term pricing and adjusted-margin optics.
4. Keep the paused separation strategy, portfolio actions, and any proceeds or costs outside recurring operating cash until legal-entity and collection joins are clear.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what volume recovery, price realization, market-share stabilization, brand return, reinvestment rate, leverage path, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The stress test asks whether household affordability, commodity inflation, tariffs, retailer power, private label, rates, and dividend obligations leave enough cash to repair the brands without weakening the balance sheet.

## Promotion boundary

`kraft-heinz-qualified; brand-reinvestment-and-volume-recovery-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from brand and category performance to collection, volume/share, reinvestment return, working capital, debt, dividend funding, claims, and diluted common residual. Organic sales, adjusted operating income, adjusted EPS, FCF, guidance, and capital returns remain diagnostic inputs.
