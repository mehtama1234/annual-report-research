# Liberty Global connectivity holding-company valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench tests Liberty Global as a fixed-mobile connectivity holding company, joint-venture platform, and capital-recycling vehicle. It keeps subscriber and ARPU economics, network investment, JV distributions, listed stakes, asset sales, debt, portfolio governance, and diluted common residual separate from reported revenue and operating cash flow.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Liberty Global | Connectivity holding-company cash after fixed/mobile network investment, JV economics, listed stakes, infrastructure monetization, portfolio transactions, debt, and diluted common residual | Network capex, spectrum/mobile investment, JV funding, customer acquisition, technology, acquisitions, portfolio support, debt, and shares | Subscriber/ARPU decline, regulatory or competitive pressure, JV underperformance, asset-sale dependence, refinancing, currency, or portfolio discount | Cash and share recycling rise while operating connectivity, JV distributions, network reinvestment, leverage, or diluted per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$4.8785B`, operating cash flow `$1.2111B`, cash `$2.0814B`, and Liberty Telecom served about `80M` fixed and mobile connections.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$1.1720B`, operating income `$3.0M`, operating cash flow about `$230.9M`, and cash about `$2.4186B`.
- Q2 included about `$604.2M` of proceeds from the remaining EdgeConneX sale and `$110.7M` from the UPC Slovakia sale; Q1 included additional EdgeConneX and ITV stake proceeds.
- Virgin Media O2, VodafoneZiggo, fixed-mobile convergence, listed investments, and infrastructure monetization make equity-accounted and portfolio cash joins essential.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile direct operating cash with JV distributions, equity-accounted earnings, listed-stake proceeds, asset-sale gains, and portfolio funding.
2. Separate recurring subscriber/ARPU and network economics from one-time disposals, spinoffs, refinancing, and capital recycling.
3. Test fixed-mobile convergence claims against churn, pricing, capex, spectrum, service quality, and regulatory obligations.
4. Keep debt, guarantees, JV commitments, transaction costs, share repurchases, and diluted shares in the common-owner residual schedule.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what subscriber retention, ARPU, network returns, JV distributions, asset values, reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether regulation, competition, rates, currency, infrastructure funding, and asset-sale dependence impair liquidity before portfolio value reaches common owners.

## Promotion boundary

`liberty-global-qualified; convergence-jv-and-portfolio-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from connectivity operations and JVs to collections, distributions, network reinvestment, asset-sale proceeds, debt, guarantees, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, connections, ARPU, adjusted metrics, OCF, guidance, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
