# Life insurance and retirement-capital valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves MetLife from premium and adjusted-earnings evidence into a
company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It keeps life and retirement
underwriting, policyholder liabilities, long-duration assets, reinsurance,
statutory capital, subsidiary dividends, holding-company cash, variable
investment income, debt, and diluted common-owner cash separate from P&C
insurance and brokerage economics.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MetLife life insurance and retirement capital | Distributable holding-company cash after premiums/fees, benefits, reserves, asset-liability matching, reinsurance, statutory capital, collateral, debt, preferred claims, SBC, and dilution | Reserve growth, risk-based capital, policyholder benefits, annuity/pension risk transfer, reinsurance collateral, asset duration, technology/distribution, debt, and capital returns | Lower rates, credit losses, spread widening, private-asset marks, higher lapses, mortality/morbidity/longevity stress, reinsurance counterparty failure, collateral calls, or blocked subsidiary dividends | Adjusted earnings and ROE rise while reserve adequacy, statutory capital, asset liquidity, book value, holding-company cash, or diluted per-share residual weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net income was `$3.173B`, adjusted earnings `$5.986B`, adjusted ROE
  `16.0%`, and shareholder returns approximately `$4.4B`.
- Q2 FY2026 net income was `$705M`, adjusted earnings about `$1.6B`, adjusted
  EPS `$2.43`, net investment income `$6.7B`, and holding-company cash/liquid
  assets approximately `$3.4B`.
- FY2025 net investment income was `$22.559B`; variable investment income was
  below the business-plan goal because of real-estate, fund, and private-equity
  returns. Variable marks and realizations should not be capitalized as ordinary
  spread income without a cash and repeatability bridge.
- At June 30, 2026, fixed-maturity securities had fair value `$322.183B` versus
  amortized cost `$347.781B`. Policyholder benefits, future-policy reserves,
  market-risk benefits, separate accounts, and reinsurance recoverables absorb
  much of the asset portfolio before common shareholders have a claim.
- Q2 2026 shareholder returns exceeded `$1.1B`; capital release must be tested
  against reserve growth, statutory capital, collateral, and future benefits.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Bridge adjusted earnings to statutory earnings, risk-based capital,
   subsidiary dividends, holding-company cash, debt service, dividends, and
   repurchases. Net income is not automatically distributable owner cash.
2. Separate contractual spread income from variable investment income,
   unrealized marks, private-asset gains, and realized proceeds.
3. Track mortality, morbidity, longevity, lapse, expense, market-risk-benefit,
   reserve, and reinsurance assumption changes. Reserve releases can lift
   earnings while reducing future protection.
4. Reconcile fixed-maturity fair-value changes, duration, credit losses,
   collateral, reinsurance recoverables, counterparty quality, and liquidity
   under stressed rates and policyholder behavior.
5. Treat adjusted ROE, adjusted book value, capital release, dividends, and
   buybacks as diagnostic until statutory capital and common-share residual are
   evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what recurring premiums and fees,
underwriting margin, contractual spread, variable-investment normalization,
reserve growth, capital release, and cost of equity the valuation requires.
Value the insurer on distributable capital after liabilities, not on the gross
investment portfolio or adjusted EPS alone.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether policyholder benefits can be paid
through lower rates, credit losses, spread widening, higher lapses, longevity
or morbidity shocks, private-asset illiquidity, reinsurance friction, and
reduced capital-market access. Liquidity passes only when subsidiary and holding-
company claims remain visible.

## Promotion boundary

`life-insurance-qualified; statutory-capital-and-distribution-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from premiums, fees, and
underwriting to reserves, benefits paid, asset income, reinsurance settlement,
statutory capital, subsidiary dividends, holding-company cash, debt, preferred
claims, SBC, and diluted common residual. Adjusted EPS, ROE, book value, net
investment income, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
