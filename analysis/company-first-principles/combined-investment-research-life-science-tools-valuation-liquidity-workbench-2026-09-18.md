# Life-science tools and laboratory workflow valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Thermo Fisher Scientific from scientific-workflow evidence
into a company-specific valuation object. It separates instruments,
consumables, services, diagnostics, and biopharma production capacity while
testing acquisition-funded growth, utilization, inventory, debt, and dilution.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thermo Fisher | Installed laboratory workflow cash after instrument adoption, consumables/service attachment, pharma-services utilization, replacement capex, R&D, quality, acquisitions, debt, SBC, and dilution | Instruments, reagents/consumables, service network, laboratory/manufacturing capacity, R&D, inventory, Solventum/Clario integration, debt, and common capital | Research/pharma budget cycle, hospital capital pressure, biopharma production delay, inventory/quality issue, acquisition integration, debt/refinancing, or FX/cash-transfer stress | Revenue and adjusted earnings rise while consumables attachment, utilization, acquired return, working capital, debt, quality, or diluted residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$44.564B`, operating cash flow `$7.818B`, PP&E capex
  `$1.525B`, and reported FCF `$6.337B`.
- Acquisitions were `$4.037B`, including approximately `$4B` for Solventum's
  purification and filtration business. The residual after capex and
  acquisitions was approximately `$2.300B`.
- Thermo issued `$7.759B` of debt, repaid `$2.412B`, paid `$636M` of dividends,
  and repurchased `$3.0B` of stock in 2025. Total debt was approximately
  `$39.384B` at year-end.
- 2026 net PP&E spending was expected at `$1.8–2.0B`; 2025 operating cash also
  absorbed `$430M` from receivables, `$380M` from contract assets/liabilities,
  and `$1.31B` from other assets/liabilities.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate instrument placements from recurring consumables, service,
   software-adjacent workflows, and biopharma-services utilization.
2. Reconcile Solventum and Clario acquisition price, acquired margin, customer
   retention, integration cost, debt funding, amortization, and cash return.
3. Track receivables, contract assets/liabilities, inventory turns, supplier
   terms, customer deposits, capacity utilization, quality, and cash conversion.
4. Split sustaining versus capacity capex and require incremental utilization,
   margin, and cash return before treating new capacity as value-creating.
5. Keep debt issuance, dividends, buybacks, SBC, foreign cash location, and FX
   visible when testing common-owner cash per diluted share.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what installed-base attachment,
consumables growth, pharma-services utilization, acquisition return, capex,
margin, debt cost, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn
Alden-style stress test asks whether research budgets, hospital capital,
biopharma production, inventory, rates, debt, FX, and integration can absorb a
cycle before the workflow produces durable common cash.

## Promotion boundary

`life-science-tools-qualified; utilization-acquisition-and-debt-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from instrument/workflow
adoption through consumable/service collection, utilization, quality claims,
required capacity and replacement capital, acquisition return, debt, legal-
entity availability, and diluted common residual. Revenue, reported FCF,
adjusted earnings, acquisitions, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic or
residual inputs.
