# Lithography-control valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves ASML from technical scarcity and semiconductor-demand
evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It keeps lithography system
sales distinct from installed-base service and tests whether technical control
survives customer concentration, export policy, supplier capacity, backlog
conversion, and the cost of next-generation investment.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ASML | Lithography system and installed-base cash after customer acceptance, down payments, service/field options, R&D, supplier coordination, capacity, export compliance, tax, debt, and dilution | EUV/DUV systems, optics/light-source ecosystem, R&D, manufacturing capacity, supplier commitments, field service, inventory, customer financing, and common capital | Fab-capex cycle, customer concentration, China/export restriction, order cancellation or delay, supplier bottleneck, next-generation execution, or FX | Revenue and backlog rise while system acceptance, service attachment, customer concentration, export permissions, supplier capacity, gross margin, or diluted residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net sales were `€32.667B`, gross margin `52.8%`, net income
  `€9.609B`, and US-GAAP operating cash flow `€12.659B`. The cash-after-capex
  screen was approximately `€11.027B`.
- Year-end backlog was `€38.8B`; system sales were `€24.474B` and installed-
  base-management sales were a separate service/field-options stream.
- The largest customer represented `23.9%` of revenue, the two largest `38.0%`,
  and China `29.1%`. Customer concentration and export policy therefore belong
  inside the valuation and liquidity denominator.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Trace backlog and customer down payments through system completion,
   acceptance, revenue recognition, collection, warranty, and final margin.
2. Separate system sales from recurring service, field options, upgrades,
   installed-base utilization, and customer support cost.
3. Test supplier concentration, precision-manufacturing capacity, inventory,
   purchase commitments, and the cash cost of next-generation EUV/DUV systems.
4. Keep China/export restrictions, customer concentration, order timing,
   cancellations, FX, and customer financing separate from technical scarcity.
5. Treat backlog, gross margin, reported OCF, dividends, and buybacks as
   diagnostic or residual measures until acceptance, reinvestment, and diluted
   common claims are joined.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what system volume, price, gross
margin, service attachment, backlog conversion, R&D burden, supplier capacity,
and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks
whether fab spending, export controls, geopolitical concentration, supplier
constraints, and customer bargaining power can interrupt cash before technical
scarcity produces a durable common residual.

## Promotion boundary

`lithography-control-qualified; acceptance-export-and-supplier-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from customer order through
system acceptance and collection, service settlement, required R&D/capacity
reinvestment, supplier obligations, export/legal claims, debt, and diluted
common residual. Systems revenue, backlog, service revenue, gross margin,
reported OCF, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
