# LNG liquefaction and export infrastructure valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Cheniere Energy from LNG-export evidence into a
train-level, contract, derivative, capital-cycle, and common-cash test. It
keeps liquefaction infrastructure distinct from upstream gas production,
integrated oil, and regulated midstream models.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cheniere Energy | Commissioned and incremental liquefaction-train cash after feedgas, shipping, maintenance, contract, derivative, debt, tax, noncontrolling, and dilution claims | Train turnarounds, maintenance PP&E, Stage 3 and other growth construction, docks, storage, pipelines, safety, and regulatory compliance | LNG-cycle weakness, hedge/derivative cash mismatch, counterparty failure, train outage, construction overrun, refinancing, or buybacks consuming liquidity | Distributable cash flow and contracted visibility rise while total capital, debt, hedge settlement, train returns, or per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 operating cash flow was `$5.539B` against `$3.078B` of PP&E
  spending, leaving a simple post-capex residual of about `$2.461B`.
- FY2025 repurchases were `$2.724B` and dividends `$451M`; cash ended near
  `$1.099B` against roughly `$22.507B` of long-term debt.
- Sabine Pass had six operating trains at approximately `30 mtpa`; Corpus
  Christi had three operating trains at approximately `15 mtpa`, with Stage 3
  adding brownfield capacity.
- Management's `$5.3B` distributable-cash-flow measure must be reconciled to
  consolidated OCF, total PP&E, derivative settlements, noncontrolling claims,
  debt movement, and maintenance versus growth capital.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile realized derivative cash settlements to unrealized marks, feedgas,
   LNG sales, and contracted versus merchant exposure.
2. Separate maintenance PP&E from train construction, Stage 3, docks, storage,
   and other growth capital before assessing payout capacity.
3. Track train capacity, availability, utilization, realized margin, turnaround,
   safety, contract concentration, take-or-pay, and counterparty credit.
4. Reconcile supplier/customer advances, working capital, hedge collateral,
   debt maturities, refinancing, noncontrolling interests, and cash balances.
5. Test whether buybacks reduce diluted shares without starving train returns or
   weakening liquidity during a low-LNG-price cycle.
6. Treat DCF, adjusted EBITDA, contracted capacity, GAAP earnings, dividends,
   and repurchases as diagnostic until train-level owner cash is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test values commissioned and incremental trains
separately, using utilization, feedgas, shipping, maintenance, contract margin,
construction cost, debt, and required return. The Lyn Alden-style stress test
asks whether energy-security demand and export scarcity offset rates, global LNG
supply growth, geopolitical shocks, counterparty risk, and capital intensity.
Long-term contracts reduce volume risk but do not make the system a risk-free
toll road.

## Promotion boundary

`lng-liquefaction-qualified; train-return-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from train availability,
utilization, contracts, realized margin, feedgas, derivative cash, maintenance
and growth capital, debt, counterparties, noncontrolling claims, taxes, and
diluted common residual. DCF, adjusted EBITDA, contracted capacity, GAAP net
income, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
