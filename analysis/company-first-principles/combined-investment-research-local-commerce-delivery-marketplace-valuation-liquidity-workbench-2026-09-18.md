# Local-commerce delivery marketplace valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves DoorDash from local-commerce and delivery evidence into a
company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It keeps consumer,
merchant, Dasher, membership, advertising, Commerce Platform, delivery labor,
insurance, regulation, Deliveroo integration, capitalized software, SBC,
processor funds, and diluted common-owner cash separate from Uber's broader
mobility/delivery model, eBay goods transactions, and Shopify merchant
software.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DoorDash local-commerce delivery marketplace | Retained marketplace and commerce-platform cash after Dasher/merchant incentives, payouts, refunds, support, insurance, regulation, software, acquisitions, SBC, and dilution | Matching/pricing systems, payments, fraud, support, delivery operations, merchant tools, capitalized software, international integration, acquisitions, and common capital | Consumer affordability, merchant disintermediation, Dasher supply, worker rules, insurance, local fee regulation, Deliveroo integration, or dilution | GOV, orders, members, advertising, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, or repurchases rise while contribution per order, merchant/Dasher health, regulatory cost, organic retention, SBC-adjusted cash, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$13.717B`, net income attributable to common
  `$935M`, and marketplace activity was approximately `$112B` GOV. GOV is an
  activity denominator, not retained revenue or owner cash.
- Q2 2026 GOV was `$33.078B`, orders `970M` (up `27%`), revenue `$4.454B`, and
  net revenue margin `13.5%`. Reported growth included Deliveroo; excluding
  Deliveroo, Q2 orders grew `17%`, GOV `23%`, and revenue `24%`.
- Six-month 2026 operating cash flow was `$1.538B`, company-defined FCF
  `$1.162B`, capitalized software/website development `$258M`, and SBC `$580M`.
  A rough cash-after-SBC screen was about `$552M` before acquisitions and other
  owner claims.
- Q2 contribution profit was `$1.641B` and adjusted EBITDA `$914M`, both useful
  operating measures but not common cash because they add back SBC and exclude
  other recurring burdens.
- DoorDash held approximately `$6.2B` in cash, investments, and liquidity plus
  `$513M` at payment processors; processor funds remain connected to merchant
  and Dasher settlement obligations.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile GOV to retained revenue after Dasher payouts, merchant/consumer
   incentives, refunds, credits, promotions, payments, and support. GOV is not
   sales.
2. Track orders, GOV per order, revenue per order, contribution per order,
   delivery availability, city density, merchant retention, and Dasher net
   earnings together.
3. Separate organic growth from Deliveroo and other acquisition consolidation;
   measure integration cost, merchant retention, order density, and cash return.
4. Test DashPass and other membership frequency after waived fees, discounts,
   delivery, and service cost. Test advertising after merchant return and
   retention rather than ad revenue alone.
5. Include worker classification, minimum-pay, insurance, refunds, local fees,
   capitalized software, SBC, processor funds, acquisitions, repurchases, and
   diluted shares in the owner-cash bridge.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what organic orders, GOV per order,
net revenue margin, contribution per order, merchant retention, member frequency,
delivery density, software investment, acquisition return, terminal growth, and
cost of capital the valuation requires. The marketplace base must be valued
separately from uncertain international and category expansion.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether households, merchants, and delivery
workers remain economically active through affordability pressure, fuel and
labor costs, regulation, insurance claims, and tighter liquidity. Liquidity
passes only when settlement funds, platform obligations, software renewal,
acquisition cash, SBC, and common residual remain visible.

## Promotion boundary

`local-commerce-delivery-qualified; contribution-supply-and-dilution-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from organic orders and GOV to
retained revenue, merchant and Dasher settlement, contribution after service
cost, membership and advertising economics, insurance/regulatory cash,
capitalized software, acquisition integration, SBC replacement, and diluted
common residual. GOV, orders, members, advertising, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, OCF,
and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
