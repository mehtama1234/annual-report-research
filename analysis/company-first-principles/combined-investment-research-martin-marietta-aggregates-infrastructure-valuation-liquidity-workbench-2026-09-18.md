# Martin Marietta aggregates and infrastructure valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Martin Marietta Materials from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It treats aggregates as a local-scarcity, freight-limited infrastructure material business and keeps acquisition mix, purchase-accounting noise, residential weakness, capital intensity, debt capacity, and diluted common residual separate. It does not pool Martin Marietta with Vulcan, Knife River, or contractors into a single materials ranking.

## Decision matrix

| Martin Marietta Materials | Aggregates-led local-scarcity cash after quarry reserves, hauling, permitting, price/mix, acquisition integration, portfolio reshaping, taxes, debt, and dilution | Sustaining quarry and plant capital, growth projects, acquisitions, freight and labor, environmental obligations, inventory, debt, and shares | Residential weakness, infrastructure timing, acquisition funding, purchase-accounting charges, freight inflation, permitting, weather, or leverage | Shipments and EBITDA rise while realized price/mix, gross profit per ton, acquired-asset returns, borrowing capacity, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- The 2025 filing describes approximately `400` quarries, mines, and distribution yards across `28 states`, Canada, and The Bahamas; aggregates generated `88%` of 2025 reportable segment gross profit.
- 2025 net earnings from continuing operations attributable to Martin Marietta were `$990M`.
- Q2 2026 revenue rose `21%` to `$1.947B`, aggregates shipments rose `17%` to `61.6M` tons, and adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations rose `13%` to `$638M`.
- Q2 average selling price was `$22.74` per ton, while a `$52M` purchase-accounting charge pressured aggregates gross profit per ton after acquired inventory was marked to fair value.
- The QUIKRETE exchange included `$450M` of cash, and New Frontier added a planned roughly `8M` tons of annual aggregates capacity; unused borrowing capacity declined to `$742M` at `2026-06-30`.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile shipments, realized price per ton, geographic mix, freight, gross profit per ton, and acquisition purchase-accounting effects before treating EBITDA growth as durable.
2. Separate continuing aggregates economics from portfolio disposals, the QUIKRETE exchange, bolt-on acquisitions, and any cash proceeds or markups.
3. Test whether quarry reserves, permits, hauling distance, environmental obligations, sustaining capital, and plant availability support the claimed local pricing power.
4. Keep residential softness, infrastructure awards, data-center and industrial projects, and public-works timing distinct; end-market narrative is not same-entity collected cash.
5. Treat borrowing capacity, acquisition funding, share issuance, dividends, and repurchases as financing and residual claims until the common-owner bridge is complete.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what shipment growth, price per ton, mix, quarry life, acquisition returns, maintenance capital, and cost of capital are embedded in the price. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether physical scarcity and infrastructure demand survive freight, permitting, housing, rates, weather, public-budget timing, acquisition leverage, and working-capital pressure. The central question is whether local scarcity converts into recurring cash after the physical replacement and funding burden.

## Promotion boundary

`martin-marietta-qualified; aggregates-price-mix-and-capacity-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from tons and realized price/mix to collection, quarry and plant maintenance, acquisition return, permits and claims, debt and funding, and diluted common residual. Shipments, revenue, adjusted EBITDA, OCF, FCF, guidance, and portfolio proceeds remain diagnostic inputs.
