# IP-driven play, entertainment, and digital-games valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Mattel from current-period toy evidence into a company-specific valuation, franchise, entertainment, digital-game, licensing, inventory, and owner-cash test. It treats Mattel as an IP-driven play and family-entertainment portfolio, not as a generic seasonal toy manufacturer.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mattel | Collected franchise cash after toy sell-through, entertainment/content, licensing, digital games, DTC, royalties, inventory, product development, partner economics, acquisition integration, debt, and dilution | Brand/product design, molds and tooling, content, mobile games, Mattel163, licensing, marketing, DTC/data, inventory, distribution, and working capital | Seasonal toy demand, film/game execution, partner IP, retailer inventory, product safety/recalls, tariff/sourcing, digital acquisition, acquisition integration, and dilution | Gross billings and entertainment growth rise while toy sell-through, royalty quality, content cost, game retention, inventory, product claims, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net sales were `$5.348B`, down `1%`; Q4 sales were `$1.766B`, up `7%`; and year-end cash exceeded `$1.2B` after `$600M` of share repurchases.
- Q2 2026 net sales were `$1.125B`, up `10%` reported and `9%` constant currency; vehicles gross billings were `$463M`, up `14%`, and action figures/building sets/games/other `$358M`, up `35%`.
- Q1 2026 net sales were `$862M`, up `4%` reported and `1%` constant currency; vehicles gross billings were `$361M`, up `17%`; action figures/building sets/games/other `$233M`, up `21%`.
- Mattel completed the Mattel163 acquisition, announced approximately `$150M` of strategic investments, and authorized a new `$1.5B` repurchase program through 2028.
- The brand-centric strategy explicitly links toys, major IP partnerships, theatrical releases, mobile games, licensing, digital customer acquisition, DTC, first-party data, and adult-fan extensions.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile gross billings and revenue to retailer sell-through, returns, markdowns, inventory, seasonal timing, licensing/royalty terms, and actual collection; gross billings are not owner cash.
2. Separate owned brands, licensed brands, entertainment, digital games, DTC, commercial partnerships, and Mattel163; a successful film or franchise launch does not prove portfolio-level return.
3. Test theatrical and mobile-game economics through production/development spend, marketing, retention, platform fees, royalties, content amortization, and repeat monetization.
4. Reconcile inventory, tooling, product safety/recall, tariff/sourcing, retailer concentration, and working capital before promoting adjusted operating results.
5. Test Mattel163 and other acquisitions through purchase price, integration, user retention, revenue conversion, development cost, and common-share effects.
6. Treat buybacks, EPS, strategic investments, and brand value as residual claims requiring product/content reinvestment, debt, tax, and diluted-share validation.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what franchise sell-through, film/game conversion, licensing royalty, digital retention, DTC contribution, product innovation, inventory turns, content investment, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether household budgets, retailer orders, partner-IP terms, film/game execution, tariffs, platform dependence, and seasonal shocks can weaken cash while brand, content, tooling, and inventory obligations remain.

## Promotion boundary

`mattel-ip-qualified; franchise-sellthrough-content-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from brand/franchise demand to toy sell-through, collection, licensing/royalty, content and game economics, inventory, product claims, acquisition integration, debt, taxes, and diluted common residual. Sales, gross billings, EPS, entertainment releases, game launches, cash, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
