# McKesson longitudinal cash-perimeter bridge — pass 2 — 2026-09-17

## Decision

McKesson's FY2025 annual cash surface and Q1 FY2027 quarter cannot be pooled
into an annualized conclusion, but the contrast is analytically valuable. A
`$5.202B` FY2025 post-visible-investment residual was followed by a Q1 FY2027
`$(395)M` post-visible-reinvestment screen. The change is a warning about
working-capital timing and capital allocation, not proof of permanent
deterioration. The next gate is a matched-quarter panel with the Apollo
preferred/redeemable-NCI bridge.

Status: `longitudinal-perimeter-pass-2; timing-versus-structure-open; no-ranking`.

## Period-controlled cash surface

| Period | OCF | PP&E | Capitalized software | Acquisitions | Mechanical residual | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| FY2025 | `$6.085B` | `$537M` | approximately `$322M` | `$24M` | `$5.202B` | annual reported-cash screen before claims and working-capital normalization |
| Q1 FY2027 | `$(220)M` | `$112M` | `$40M` | `$23M` | `$(395)M` | quarterly conversion warning; not an annualized owner-cash result |

The FY2025 screen sits beside `$3.146B` of annual repurchases, `$5.691B` of
year-end cash, and `$5.654B` of long-term debt. Q1 FY2027 returned about `$2.6B`
to shareholders while operating cash was negative and the visible residual was
`$(395)M`. This proves buyback volume is not a synonym for current-period
surplus operating cash; it does not prove the funding source or improper
capital allocation.

## Working-capital timing test

Q1 FY2027 receivables used `$2.374B` and inventory used `$2.074B`, while drafts
and accounts payable supplied `$3.773B`; receivables rose to `$30.316B` from
`$27.985B` and inventory to `$26.259B` from `$24.207B`. The net of the three
listed movements was approximately a `$675M` use before other operating lines.

The next matched-quarter panel must separate customer collections,
rebates/chargebacks, inventory receipts, vendor payments, seasonality, and
acquisitions/divestitures before judging gross-profit cash conversion. Current
evidence cannot distinguish normal seasonality from a structural cash need.

## Apollo preferred and legal-entity boundary

Apollo funds invested `$1.25B` of convertible preferred equity in
Medical-Surgical Solutions for approximately 13% minority ownership. McKesson
retains control and consolidates the segment, but the contribution is not
common-owner operating cash. Required fields are conversion/redemption terms,
dividends or accretion, segment cash generation, separation costs, and the
amount of segment cash available after the redeemable NCI/preferred claim.

Q1 also included a `$34M` opioid-claim credit. A credit is not recurring
operating performance or cash collected.

## Current liquidity and operational-stress overlay — 2026-09-18

McKesson's August 25, 2026 Form 8-K reports that the company discovered a
cybersecurity incident affecting its information systems. The investigation
was in its early stages, and McKesson had not determined the incident to be
material or reasonably likely to have a material impact as of the filing.
This does not change the Q1 cash numbers or prove a financial loss. It does
add a current operational-liquidity stress gate: a follow-up period should
test customer collections, pharmacy/provider service continuity, remediation
costs, insurance recovery, claims, and working-capital timing before treating
the Q1 negative conversion as only seasonal.

The incident is therefore a risk-control input, not an adjustment to OCF or a
new owner-cash deduction. No materiality or cash impact should be imputed until
McKesson files a quantified update.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

1. Do not annualize the negative quarter or dismiss it as timing without two
   matched subsequent quarters.
2. Do not treat FY2025 favorable working-capital conversion as permanent cash
   without settlement and seasonality evidence.
3. Keep `$40M` software and `$112M` PP&E separate and test maintenance versus
   growth investment.
4. Reconcile `$2.6B` Q1 shareholder returns to beginning cash, Apollo capital,
   debt and other financing, and the negative operating cash screen.
5. Keep the Apollo preferred/redeemable-NCI claim and Medical-Surgical Solutions
   segment cash separate from McKesson common-owner cash.

## Promotion gate and thesis breaker

Promotion requires at least two comparable quarters, a receivable/inventory/
payable settlement bridge, specialty gross-profit conversion, Apollo preferred
terms and segment cash, software/PP&E allocation, claims cash, and repurchase
funding. The thesis breaker is activated if revenue and adjusted EPS grow while
working-capital use recurs, vendor funding weakens, preferred claims absorb
segment cash, and repurchases continue to exceed current-period residual cash.
