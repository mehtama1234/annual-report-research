# Medical devices and clinical workflow — next-sector admission

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Decision

Admit medical devices and clinical workflow infrastructure as the next sector
after the healthcare-distribution lane. The initial cohort is:

- **Stryker:** procedure-platform medtech and acquisition-heavy growth.
- **Intuitive Surgical:** installed systems, instruments/accessories, and
  service-driven procedure economics.
- **Henry Schein:** fragmented-practice procurement and workflow channel.

This is a controlled research lane, not a ranking. The common chain is:

`clinical need -> hospital or practice workflow -> equipment/system adoption -> repeated procedure or procurement use -> service/consumable revenue -> owner cash after reinvestment, claims, and dilution`

## Why this sector is different

Healthcare distribution tests thin spreads and working-capital scale. Medical
devices test clinical adoption, installed workflows, procedure utilization,
regulatory quality, and the capital required to keep a product platform useful.
The central question is whether a durable clinical control point produces
per-share cash after acquisitions, R&D, inventory, service, cyber/regulatory
burden, and stock compensation.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans tests

1. **Clinical utilization versus placement:** systems delivered, backlog, or
   procedure growth must be separated from actual utilization, instrument
   consumption, service collections, and hospital/practice economics.
2. **Acquisition accounting:** impairments, inventory step-ups, amortization,
   integration costs, and adjusted EPS must be reconciled to post-close cash
   returns.
3. **Capitalization:** R&D, software, manufacturing capacity, instruments,
   inventory, and service infrastructure must be classified by maintenance and
   growth role.
4. **Regulatory and cyber claims:** recalls, product quality, compliance,
   cyber incidents, litigation, and remediation are senior owner claims.
5. **SBC and dilution:** buybacks or per-share growth must be tested against
   share issuance, employee plans, and the cash cost of retaining technical and
   clinical capability.

These are reconciliation prompts, not fraud findings. No pooled Beneish score
is assembled because the companies have different revenue mixes, accounting
perimeters, fiscal calendars, and claim structures.

## Valuation and macro handoff

The Damodaran question is whether procedure growth, consumables, service, and
workflow control produce cash returns above the cost of R&D, regulatory
investment, acquisitions, inventory, and capital equipment. The Lyn Alden
question is how hospital/practice budgets, reimbursement, rates, labor
shortages, healthcare inflation, and capital-market liquidity reach utilization
and owner cash.

The first valuation screen should separate systems/equipment from recurring
instruments, consumables, services, procurement, and acquired growth. It must
also stress procedure volume, reimbursement, hospital capital budgets, supply
constraints, cyber/regulatory claims, acquisition financing, and dilution.
