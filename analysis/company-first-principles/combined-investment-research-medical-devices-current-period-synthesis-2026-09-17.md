# Medical devices and clinical workflow — current-period synthesis — 2026-09-17

## Purpose and boundary

This synthesis joins the current Stryker, Intuitive Surgical, and Henry Schein
refreshes on a common evidence surface. It is a comparison of control points,
burdens, and missing proof objects—not an investment ranking. The companies
have different business models and fiscal calendars, so the figures remain
company-specific and are not pooled into a sector multiple.

The common chain is:

`clinical or practice workflow -> installed equipment / procurement control -> repeated use or product flow -> reported revenue -> working capital, claims, reinvestment, financing, dilution -> common-owner cash`

## Cross-company themes

### 1. Adoption is not utilization or collection

Stryker requires acquired-platform revenue and margin, Intuitive provides the
strongest installed-base-to-procedure signal, and Henry Schein provides channel
and practice-volume evidence. In each case, the next cash object is downstream:
settlement, utilization, inventory/receivable release, and customer collection.
Placement, sales growth, procedures, or channel expansion cannot substitute for
that join.

### 2. “One-time” claims need a cash-paid persistence test

Stryker's integration, structural, recall, and regulatory categories are
economically senior claims if they recur. Henry Schein's 2024 Plan runs through
2027, so lower current charges do not prove completion. Intuitive's tariff
refund benefit is a period-specific benefit and must not be capitalized into
steady-state earnings. These are quality-of-earnings controls, not allegations
of misconduct.

### 3. Capitalization and dilution are central owner-cash boundaries

Intuitive has capitalized SBC and meaningful repurchases; Stryker has debt
repayment, dividends, and potential future buybacks after lower liquidity; Henry
Schein has KKR issuance, ASR/repurchase activity, debt funding, and a nearly
utilized receivable securitization. The model must reconcile income-statement
SBC, cash-flow SBC, capitalized SBC, diluted shares, financing, and buybacks
without double counting.

### 4. The businesses carry different recurring-capital burdens

Stryker's burden is acquisition and clinical-quality integration; Intuitive's
is manufacturing, support, R&D, inventory, and leased-system utilization;
Henry Schein's is distribution working capital, software, restructuring,
securitization, and debt. A common reported-OCF margin would hide these
differences rather than clarify them.

## Promotion gates

The lane remains `qualified; current-period-integrated; no-ranking`. Promotion
requires all of the following company-specific joins:

1. Stryker: Inari post-close revenue, margin, amortization, integration cash,
   inventory, quality/recall costs, debt, and incremental cash generation.
2. Intuitive: system cohort, procedures per system, instruments/accessories,
   service attachment, usage-based lease receivables and collections, inventory,
   R&D, SBC, and diluted per-share cash.
3. Henry Schein: restructuring cash-paid roll-forward, working-capital
   seasonality, securitized-receivable collateral and availability, debt and
   repurchase funding, software/property classification, acquisition returns,
   and KKR/ASR diluted-share treatment.

The sector-level thesis breaker is triggered if clinical adoption or practice
volume grows while utilization, collection, inventory turns, cash after
reinvestment, or per-share residual deteriorates. No company is promoted until
the relevant same-entity, same-period evidence closes its own gate.
