# Medical devices and clinical workflow QoE ratio panel — 2026-09-17

The structured [ratio-panel CSV](data/combined-investment-research-medical-devices-qoe-ratio-panel-2026-09-17.csv)
puts Stryker, Intuitive Surgical, and Henry Schein on a same-fiscal-year
diagnostic surface. It is not a pooled Beneish score, fraud probability,
normalized owner-cash ranking, or conclusion that any company misstated its
accounts.

The panel separates three different economic shapes. Stryker reported strong
operating cash, but its acquisition program was nearly one-fifth of revenue
and larger than the cash left after PP&E. Intuitive generated a large reported
cash surface after PP&E, but inventory used approximately `$1.063B` and stock
compensation was approximately `$0.788B`; system placement therefore cannot be
used as a substitute for utilization, recurring instrument economics, or
per-share cash. Henry Schein's residual was positive but small relative to
sales, and its reported/non-GAAP EPS gap and continuing restructuring require
more work before the residual can be treated as durable owner cash.

The `acquisition_or_other_investing` column is deliberately not identical
across the three businesses. Stryker and Henry Schein have visible acquisition
or equity-investment cash that is central to the thesis. Intuitive's FY2025
figure is the dossier's acquisitions, intellectual property, and other
investing line. This is a comparability warning, not permission to treat the
three residuals as a ranking.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans interpretation

### Stryker

The key test is whether acquired clinical platforms produce post-close cash
returns after inventory step-up, amortization, integration, quality and
regulatory work, debt, and dilution. The `$5.23` reported-to-adjusted EPS gap
is a reconciliation queue, not an accusation. Because acquisitions are a
recurring growth mechanism, recurring acquisition costs cannot automatically
be labeled non-economic. Inventory rose as a use of cash, so procedure growth
must be separated from inventory accumulation and acquired sales.

### Intuitive Surgical

The primary control point is the installed surgical workflow. Systems revenue
is only the entry point; instruments and accessories were approximately 60% of
FY2025 revenue and services added a further recurring layer. The next test is
procedure and instrument use per installed system, service attachment and
collection, usage-based lease timing, inventory turns, manufacturing capacity,
R&D, SBC, and dilution. The large reported residual is therefore a promising
cash surface, not a final owner-cash conclusion.

### Henry Schein

The main risk is adjusted operating improvement without equivalent common-owner
cash. Restructuring charges continued, acquisition/equity cash was material
relative to the residual, and receivables plus inventory were large compared
with cash on hand. The work must reconcile internal growth, acquisition growth,
inventory and receivable settlement, software, restructuring, debt, buybacks,
KKR-related ownership, and stock compensation. The `$3.27` GAAP versus `$4.97`
non-GAAP EPS gap raises the required bridge; it does not itself establish
aggressive accounting.

## Promotion boundary

Status: `medical-devices; same-fiscal-year-diagnostic-panel; normalized-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`.

Promotion requires, for each company:

1. a same-entity, same-period bridge from operating cash to maintenance and
   growth reinvestment;
2. a treatment of SBC and diluted shares as an owner claim;
3. an acquisition or installed-base return measure appropriate to the model;
4. claims for quality, regulatory, cyber, reimbursement, debt, and working
   capital; and
5. a valuation input tied to the resulting cash denominator rather than to
   adjusted EPS alone.
