# Medical devices and clinical workflow valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Stryker, Intuitive Surgical, and Henry Schein from
current-period evidence into company-specific valuation objects, reinvestment
burdens, funding stresses, and thesis breakers. It does not pool procedure,
installed-base, distribution, or acquisition economics into a sector ranking.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stryker | Procedure-platform cash after Inari and other acquisition cohorts earn through integration, amortization, inventory, quality claims, debt, and dilution | Acquisition price, acquired margin, integration cash, PP&E, R&D, SBC, recalls, debt, and share issuance | Hospital capital-budget pressure, integration failure, recall/regulatory cash, debt refinancing, or margin compression | Procedure/revenue growth continues while Inari cash, acquired margin, inventory, recall, or diluted per-share return deteriorates |
| Intuitive Surgical | Installed-base cash per system and procedure after instruments/accessories, service, lease collection, manufacturing, R&D, SBC, and dilution | Systems, instruments, service, usage-based leases, inventory, manufacturing, R&D, SBC replacement, and repurchases | Hospital spending slowdown, utilization decline, lease-collection pressure, supply bottleneck, tariff/input inflation, or dilution | Systems/procedures grow while instruments per system, lease collections, inventory turns, or per-share cash weaken |
| Henry Schein | Practice/procurement workflow cash after distribution working capital, software, acquisitions, restructuring, securitized receivables, debt, and dilution | Receivables, inventory, software, PP&E, acquisitions, restructuring, securitization collateral, repurchases, debt, and KKR/ASR claims | Practice-spending slowdown, receivable aging, securitization availability, supplier terms, refinancing, or acquisition integration | Adjusted EPS and buybacks rise while receivable collateral, debt, restructuring cash, or diluted common residual deteriorates |

## Current evidence anchors

- Stryker H1 2026 OCF was `$1.842B`; current H1 investing cash use was about
  `$824M`; `$1.000B` of notes was repaid; acquisition/integration charges were
  `$44M`, structural charges `$213M`, recall charges `$52M`, and regulatory
  charges `$1M`. Inari return remains an acquired-cohort question.
- Intuitive's installed base was `11,710` da Vinci and `1,096` Ion systems;
  procedures rose about `16%`; H1 instruments/accessories revenue was `$3.421B`,
  service revenue `$906M`, operating lease revenue `$512M`, variable lease
  revenue `$326M`, capitalized SBC `$67.8M`, and Q2 repurchases `$380M`.
- Henry Schein H1 OCF was `$145M`, property spending `$55M`, capitalized
  software `$30M`, acquisitions/equity investments `$30M`, repurchases `$325M`,
  and receivable securitization outstanding `$430M`. Its visible pre-debt
  residual was only about `$30M`.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Keep Stryker's acquisition, structural, recall, and regulatory charges in a
   cash-paid persistence test; “one-time” labels do not erase recurring quality
   or integration burden.
2. Connect Intuitive's systems to procedures, instruments per system, service,
   lease receivables and collections, inventory turns, R&D, SBC, and shares;
   installed-base growth alone is not owner cash.
3. Reconcile Henry Schein's adjusted EPS, restructuring plan, working capital,
   securitized receivables, repurchases, debt, KKR/ASR activity, and dilution.
4. Do not subtract inventory twice from OCF, but do not ignore inventory turns
   and collateral quality when testing future cash conversion.
5. Treat repurchases as a residual claim and potential funding use, not proof of
   surplus cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what procedure utilization, instrument
consumption, service attachment, acquired-cohort return, distribution margin,
reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style
stress test asks whether hospitals and practices can fund equipment, whether
reimbursement and labor costs impair use, and whether rates, supply chains,
tariffs, debt, or securitization reduce liquidity.

## Promotion boundary

`medical-devices-qualified; cohort-return-and-liquidity-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from installed equipment,
procedures, practice procurement, or acquired assets to collection, required
reinvestment, claims, funding, legal-entity availability, and diluted common
residual. Procedure growth, systems, adjusted EPS, reported OCF, and buybacks
remain diagnostic inputs.
