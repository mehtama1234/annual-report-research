# Membership-franchise wellness valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Planet Fitness into a company-specific membership and
franchise-network valuation/liquidity object. It keeps affordable fitness
access separate from restaurant franchising, recurring route services,
consumer goods, and software. The object tests whether corporate royalties,
membership, advertising, and club cash become common-owner cash after member
acquisition, retention, franchisee health, support, technology, debt, SBC,
buybacks, and dilution.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Planet Fitness | Corporate royalty, membership, advertising, and corporate-club cash after member retention/collection, franchisee support, marketing, technology, equipment standards, debt, SBC, and diluted common residual | Member acquisition cost, churn, attendance, Black Card mix, club openings/closures, franchisee returns, equipment and support programs, technology, debt, SBC, and buybacks | Same-club slowdown, membership churn, franchisee distress, rent/labor/utilities, equipment replacement, slower openings, debt/refinancing, and parent support obligations | Members, systemwide sales, clubs, adjusted EBITDA, OCF, or buybacks rise while retention, franchisee health, corporate support burden, or per-share cash deteriorates |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 ended with about `20.8M` members and `2,896` clubs, including `2,604`
  franchisee-owned and `292` corporate-owned clubs; revenue grew `12.1%` and
  same-club sales were `6.7%`.
- Q2 2026 showed the control point under stress: revenue was `$365.2M`,
  same-club sales slowed to `1.7%`, clubs reached `2,930`, and 23 new clubs
  opened. Membership and unit growth therefore cannot substitute for franchisee
  return and retention evidence.
- FY2025 operating cash flow was `$418.4M` against `$163.7M` of PP&E purchases;
  the reported residual was about `$254.8M`, or roughly `$241.4M` after `$12.3M`
  SBC. FY2025 repurchases were about `$500.4M`, while long-term debt was about
  `$2.482B`.
- Q2 2026 repurchases were about `$200.0M` for roughly 4.0M shares, while cash
  and marketable securities were `$544.4M`. Buybacks require a debt, SBC,
  franchisee-support, and diluted-share bridge.
- Independent franchisees own approximately 90% of clubs and carry much of the
  labor, rent, utilities, cleaning, insurance, equipment, and local-execution
  burden. Corporate royalty cash is only durable if the franchise network earns
  adequate returns and continues to invest.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate membership, dues, annual fees, advertising, royalties, systemwide
   sales, and corporate-club revenue; systemwide sales are not corporate cash.
2. Match member additions and price changes to churn, attendance, payment
   failure, Black Card mix, marketing cost, and franchisee-level returns.
3. Track club openings, closures, remodels, equipment, franchisee liquidity,
   concessions, and support spending rather than inferring network health from
   club count.
4. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA and OCF to GAAP income, SBC, technology/support,
   debt service, and parent-level capital requirements.
5. Treat buybacks as a residual claim, not proof of surplus, while same-club
   sales slow and franchisee economics remain incompletely disclosed.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what member retention, dues and price
mix, franchisee return, club openings, corporate support, technology spending,
debt cost, and cost of capital the equity value requires. The Lyn Alden-style
stress test asks whether discretionary household spending, rent, labor,
utilities, equipment financing, franchisee credit, and rates preserve the
network during a consumer slowdown.

## Promotion boundary

`membership-franchise-wellness-qualified; franchisee-health-and-retention-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from members and clubs to
retention, attendance, collection, franchisee economics, support/technology
cost, equipment replacement, debt, SBC, diluted shares, and common-owner
residual. Members, systemwide sales, club count, same-club sales, adjusted
EBITDA, OCF, FCF, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
