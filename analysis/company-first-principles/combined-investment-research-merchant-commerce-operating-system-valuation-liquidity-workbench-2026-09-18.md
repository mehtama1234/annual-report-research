# Merchant-commerce operating-system valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench adds Shopify as a distinct merchant-commerce operating-system
lane. It separates subscription software, storefront and checkout workflow,
payments attachment, fulfillment connections, and merchant lending from eBay's
goods marketplace, enterprise workflow software, and ordinary consumer credit.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shopify | Merchant-workflow cash after subscription retention, payment processing, transaction costs, merchant support, credit losses, settlement obligations, product/AI investment, SBC, and diluted common residual | Storefront/checkout software, payments, fraud controls, merchant data, fulfillment connections, lending/receivables, R&D, support, cloud, SBC, and common capital | Merchant failure, GMV/consumer slowdown, payment/fraud loss, credit deterioration, settlement timing, regulatory change, external-discovery dependence, or dilution | GMV, Payments penetration, revenue, adjusted earnings, OCF, and buybacks rise while merchant survival, payment contribution, loan quality, settlement obligations, SBC, or per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$11.556B`: subscription solutions were `$2.752B` and merchant solutions `$8.804B`; GMV was `$378.4B`; Shopify Payments penetration was `65.6%`.
- FY2025 OCF was `$2.033B`, PP&E spending `$26M`, SBC `$449M`, and the screen after PP&E and acquisitions was about `$1.951B`.
- Shopify used `$579M` for purchases and originations of loans, net of repayments and sales, and ended 2025 with `$1.784B` of loans and merchant cash advances.
- Cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities were `$5.778B`; MRR was `$205M` at year-end 2025 and `$221M` in Q2 2026.
- Payments penetration reached `68%` in Q2 2026. Higher attachment expands monetization but also expands processing, fraud, chargeback, and regulatory burdens.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate subscription revenue from merchant-solutions revenue, payment
   processing cost, take rate, chargebacks, fraud, and settlement obligations.
2. Treat GMV as activity rather than Shopify revenue; connect it to retained
   revenue, gross profit per merchant, payment contribution, and collection.
3. Reconcile loan originations, repayments, sales, delinquency, provisions,
   funding, merchant concentration, and returns after credit losses.
4. Carry `$449M` of SBC into diluted per-share cash and test repurchases as a
   residual claim rather than proof of surplus cash.
5. Track deferred revenue, merchant balances, refunds, payment liabilities,
   reserves, investments, and AI/compute costs before declaring cash durable.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what merchant retention, MRR,
GMV-per-merchant, payment attachment, take rate, processing cost, credit loss,
AI cost, SBC replacement, and cost of capital the equity value requires. The
Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether consumer demand, small-business
formation, rates, payment networks, external discovery, credit losses, and
regulation can weaken both transaction volume and merchant solvency at once.

## Promotion boundary

`merchant-commerce-qualified; payment-credit-and-dilution-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from merchant cohorts and
GMV to subscription collection, payment contribution after processing and
fraud, loan performance, settlement obligations, platform reinvestment, SBC
replacement, and diluted common residual. GMV, Payments penetration, revenue,
adjusted earnings, OCF, FCF, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs rather than
normalized owner cash.
