## First-party Damodaran method references

The valuation workbenches use Damodaran-style expectation and cash-flow
discipline. The underlying method routes are kept explicit here so a reviewer
can distinguish the methodology from the project’s company-specific inputs:

- [Approaches to valuation](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/adamodar/New_Home_Page/lectures/approach.html)
  — matching cash flows to the appropriate discount rate.
- [Intrinsic versus relative value](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/littlebook/intrinsicvsrelative.htm)
  — valuing assets from expected cash flows, growth, and risk versus pricing
  by comparables.
- [Damodaran valuation tools and spreadsheets](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/adamodar/New_Home_Page/tools.html)
  — primary reference index for valuation and corporate-finance tools.

These references support the method choice; they do not validate the project’s
cash denominators, market snapshot, or future operating assumptions.

## First-party Lyn Alden framework references

The macro layer uses a Lyn Alden-style transmission lens: monetary and fiscal
conditions, leverage, liquidity, inflation, and rates are traced into a
company’s demand, funding, liability, and valuation mechanisms. The framework
references are:

- [A Century of Fiscal and Monetary Policy](https://www.lynalden.com/fiscal-and-monetary-policy/)
  — long-term debt-cycle, fiscal, and monetary-regime context.
- [What Is Money, Anyway?](https://www.lynalden.com/what-is-money/)
  — monetary-system and liquidity context.
- [Lyn Alden Investment Strategy](https://www.lynalden.com/)
  — first-party index for the broader investment-research framework.

These references support the analytical lens; they do not establish company
causality, through-cycle resilience, or a valuation conclusion. Company claims
remain subordinate to the dated macro data and primary filings in the pilot
artifacts.
