# Mining and commodity-marketing valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Glencore from annual-report evidence into a company-specific valuation, reinvestment, funding, and common-owner residual test. It keeps industrial mines, processing/refining, and physical commodity marketing separate from mine-only producers and royalty/streaming businesses.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/funding denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glencore | Through-cycle industrial-asset cash plus marketing EBIT after inventory funding, leases, working capital, project capital, partner claims, debt, and shareholder distributions | Mine replacement and expansion, processing/refining, copper growth, closure, inventory/prepayments, receivables, logistics, collateral, leases, and trading funding | Commodity-price and mark-to-market shock, counterparty failure, inventory financing, freight disruption, DRC quotas, project overruns, refinancing, or partner obligations | Marketing earnings remain strong while net funding, working capital, project capital, significant items, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 adjusted EBITDA was `$13.511B`, Marketing Adjusted EBIT `$2.900B`, FFO `$8.714B`, net capex about `$6.900B`, and the FFO-less-net-capex screen about `$1.814B`.
- Non-RMI working capital released about `$1.600B` and the Viterra disposal contributed roughly `$940M`; neither should be capitalized as recurring operating cash.
- Net funding was `$39.405B`, including readily marketable inventories, while net debt including marketing lease liabilities was `$11.171B`. The marketing model is funding-intensive even when reported net debt is stable.
- FY2025 shareholder distributions and buybacks were about `$3.466B`; reported net income attributable to equity holders was only `$363M` versus `$2.3B` before significant items, requiring a full reconciliation.
- H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was `$10.1B`, Marketing Adjusted EBIT `$3.3B`, and net debt `$10.2B`; strong conditions are evidence of operating leverage, not proof of through-cycle owner cash.
- Copper growth targets above one million tonnes annualized by 2028 and about `1.6M` tonnes by 2035 require asset-level ownership, grade, cost, capital, jurisdiction, closure, and ramp evidence.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate marketing volume, spread, freight, financing, counterparty, inventory revaluation, derivatives, and realized cash; Marketing Adjusted EBIT is not fee-like income.
2. Roll readily marketable inventory, receivables, payables, prepayments, commodity loans, collateral, and trading leases into a net-funding bridge.
3. Remove working-capital releases, asset-sale proceeds, and prepayments from normalized owner cash unless the underlying operating mechanism is repeatable.
4. Build copper project cohorts with ownership, revised capital, production, grade, recovery, unit cost, schedule, tax, closure, and marketing economics.
5. Reconcile significant items, impairments, legal provisions, restructuring, partner claims, distributions, and buybacks to common-owner cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style test values industrial assets, marketing earnings, inventory funding, and copper projects separately, using through-cycle Marketing EBIT, mid-cycle commodity prices, full working-capital and capital requirements, taxes, debt, and probability-weighted project returns. The Lyn Alden-style stress test focuses on strategic metals and energy security against credit liquidity, inventory prices, trade restrictions, freight, counterparty, and host-country risk.

## Promotion boundary

`mining-commodity-marketing-qualified; inventory-funding-project-return-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from commodity marketing and mine operations to realized cash, inventory funding, working-capital reversal risk, project return, partner claims, debt, and diluted common residual. FFO, Marketing Adjusted EBIT, production, copper targets, working-capital releases, asset sales, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
