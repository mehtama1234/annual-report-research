# Mission-critical public-safety workflow valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench adds Motorola Solutions as a distinct institutional safety and
mission-critical workflow lane. It separates radio networks, broadband,
dispatch, video/evidence, records, cybersecurity, and managed services from
defense mission systems, passive connectivity, and general enterprise
networking.

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was about `$11.682B`; OCF `$2.837B`; PP&E spending `$265M`; and the simple OCF-less-PP&E screen was `$2.572B`.
- FY2025 included approximately `$4.4B` of Silvus acquisition consideration, common repurchases and dividends of about `$1.882B`, goodwill of `$6.8B`, and SBC of `$293M`.
- Q2 2026 sales were `$3.13B`; OCF was `$469M`; backlog was `$15.6B`; Software and Services sales were `$1.23B`; Products and Systems Integration sales were `$1.91B`.
- Q2 included a disclosed `$60M` pre-tax IEEPA tariff-refund benefit; policy recovery must remain separate from recurring margin and cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate organic software/services growth from acquired revenue, earnouts,
   purchase accounting, integration, and platform migration.
2. Treat backlog as an acceptance and collection pipeline, not earned cash;
   reconcile implementation, contract assets, support labor, cybersecurity,
   customer acceptance, and margin.
3. Charge hardware refresh, cloud, support, security, R&D, and customer
   onboarding as required platform reinvestment.
4. Test Silvus and other acquisitions through customer retention, recurring
   mix, incremental margin, integration cash, goodwill, debt, dilution, and
   return on invested capital.
5. Exclude tariff refunds and other policy recoveries from normalized operating
   margin until recurrence and cash timing are demonstrated.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what organic renewal, backlog
conversion, recurring software/service mix, implementation margin, acquisition
return, and cost of capital the equity value requires. The Lyn Alden-style
stress test asks whether public budgets, procurement cycles, rates,
cybersecurity requirements, geopolitical rules, and acquisition financing can
preserve liquidity while mission-critical service quality is maintained.

## Promotion boundary

`mission-critical-workflow-qualified; acquisition-backlog-and-dilution-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from installed workflows and
backlog to acceptance, collection, recurring renewal, support and security
cost, acquired-cohort cash return, debt, SBC replacement, and diluted common
residual. Backlog, software/services revenue, adjusted EPS, OCF, FCF, and
buybacks remain diagnostic inputs rather than normalized owner cash.
