# Mobility and delivery marketplace valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench adds Uber as a distinct mobility, delivery, membership, and
autonomous-supply marketplace lane. It separates ride and order coordination,
driver/courier and merchant economics, insurance, payments, incentives, and
future vehicle partners from travel booking, lodging, e-commerce marketplaces,
and merchant-commerce software.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Uber | Retained mobility/delivery platform cash after driver and merchant incentives, insurance, payment costs, refunds, support, legal/regulatory obligations, membership benefits, technology, AV transition, SBC, and diluted common residual | Matching/pricing systems, mapping, payments, fraud, support, insurance, incentives, membership, autonomous-vehicle partnerships, R&D, capitalized software, and common capital | Consumer demand, driver supply, minimum-pay/worker rules, insurance claims, merchant health, payment/chargeback loss, AV funding, regulation, or dilution | Gross bookings, trips, MAPCs, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, and membership rise while retained contribution, driver/merchant health, insurance, legal cost, AV return, or per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 gross bookings were approximately `$193B`, OCF about `$10.1B`, and company-defined FCF about `$9.8B`; these figures require reconciliation for SBC, working capital, insurance, legal, and investment claims.
- Q2 2026 gross bookings were `$58.0B`, revenue `$14.2B`, trips `3.9B`, MAPCs `208M`, adjusted EBITDA `$2.8B`, and FCF `$2.8B`.
- Q2 year-over-year growth was `18%` for trips, `16%` for MAPCs, `24%` for gross bookings, and `33%` for adjusted EBITDA.
- Uber One had approximately `50M` members in Q1 2026 materials, with members generating roughly half of Mobility and Delivery gross bookings; retention, benefits, contribution, and acquisition cost remain open.
- Autonomous vehicles may add supply, but partner share, insurance, maintenance, charging/fuel, remote support, regulatory compliance, and capital claims remain separate from current platform cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate gross bookings from retained revenue and connect take rate to driver
   earnings, merchant health, incentives, refunds, insurance, and payment cost.
2. Reconcile MAPCs, trips, and membership to contribution after promotions,
   support, safety, chargebacks, and retention—not activity alone.
3. Track driver/courier supply, utilization, minimum-pay rules, classification,
   insurance claims, and service quality before treating take-rate expansion as
   durable margin.
4. Reconcile delivery contribution after courier payments, merchant support,
   promotions, refunds, fraud, and insurance.
5. Model AV trips separately through partner share, vehicle cost, insurance,
   maintenance, charging, remote assistance, regulation, and required capital.
6. Carry SBC, legal/regulatory obligations, leases, payment liabilities, and
   working-capital timing into diluted per-share owner cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what retained take rate, trips per
user, membership retention, driver/merchant supply, insurance cost, delivery
contribution, AV economics, SBC replacement, and cost of capital the valuation
requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether household income,
employment, fuel, rates, restaurant activity, worker rules, insurance, and
regulation can weaken demand and supply economics simultaneously.

## Promotion boundary

`mobility-delivery-qualified; supply-insurance-and-AV-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from rides/orders and users
to retained revenue, driver and merchant settlement, incentives, insurance,
payment losses, membership contribution, AV partner cash, legal obligations,
SBC replacement, and diluted common residual. Gross bookings, trips, MAPCs,
adjusted EBITDA, FCF, and membership remain diagnostic inputs rather than
normalized owner cash.
