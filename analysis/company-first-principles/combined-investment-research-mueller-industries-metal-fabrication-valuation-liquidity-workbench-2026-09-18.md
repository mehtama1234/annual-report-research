# Mueller Industries metal fabrication valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Mueller Industries from current-period fabrication evidence
into a company-specific valuation, commodity-pass-through, acquisition, working-
capital, and common-owner cash test. It does not pool copper tube, brass rod,
valves, cable, HVAC, and industrial component economics into a generic metals
multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mueller Industries | Collected fabricated-component cash after copper, brass, aluminum, energy, freight, hedge effects, plant costs, acquisitions, capex, debt, taxes, and dilution | Downstream capacity, automation, plant and tooling, Bison/Nehring/Elkhart/Chicago Extruded integration, R&D, inventory, and channel expansion | Raw-metal price volatility, pass-through lag, hedge losses, tariffs, residential weakness, customer concentration, acquisition integration, refinancing, or working-capital strain | Price and operating income rise while unit volume, spread, hedge economics, acquisition returns, capex, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Full-year 2025 net sales were `$4.179B`, operating income `$958.5M`, and net income attributable to Mueller `$765.2M`.
- Q2 2026 net sales were `$1.428B`, operating income `$310.0M`, and net income attributable to Mueller `$249.7M`; all three segments reported unit-volume growth.
- Q1 2026 included a `$41.4M` gain on the Sherwood Valve sale; reported operating income must therefore be separated from the underlying base.
- Q4 2025 gross margin included an `$18.2M` unrealized loss on open hedge contracts after a rapid copper-price rise.
- Management's 2030 plan targets `$1.5B` of operating income through automation, downstream expansion, HVAC, electrical, and related growth lanes.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile revenue and margin to unit volume, copper/brass/aluminum prices,
   pass-through timing, mix, hedges, and customer collections; price growth is
   not the same as real demand growth.
2. Separate Piping Systems, Industrial Metals, and Climate economics, including
   repair/remodeling, residential, commercial, electrical, and industrial demand.
3. Keep inventory, metal commitments, plant and tooling capital, automation,
   tariffs, acquisitions, integration costs, debt service, and taxes in the cash
   denominator.
4. Remove asset-sale gains and test whether Bison, Nehring, Elkhart, and Chicago
   Extruded produce incremental collected cash after integration and capex.
5. Reconcile adjusted earnings, hedge marks, restructuring, debt, repurchases,
   SBC, and diluted shares before treating operating cash or capital returns as
   common-owner surplus.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what fabricated volume, spread, pass-
through, utilization, acquisition return, capex rate, leverage, and cost of capital
the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether copper and
aluminum, tariffs, energy, construction, grid investment, repair demand, and
working-capital funding can preserve liquidity through a commodity cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`mueller-metal-fabrication-qualified; pass-through-acquisition-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from fabricated volume and price
to customer collections, input costs, hedge settlement, inventory, required capex,
acquisition returns, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, price,
operating income, adjusted metrics, guidance, and repurchases remain diagnostic.
