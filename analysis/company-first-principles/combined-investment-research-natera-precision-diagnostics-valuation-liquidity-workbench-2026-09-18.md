# Natera precision diagnostics valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Natera from current-period evidence into a company-specific
valuation, reimbursement, reinvestment, and liquidity test. It does not pool
Signatera, Panorama, Horizon, and Prospera economics into a generic laboratory
multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Natera | Collected precision-diagnostics cash after assay production, payer reimbursement, denials, clinical evidence, sales and marketing, laboratory capacity, R&D, trials, working capital, debt, and dilution | Laboratory and sequencing capacity, assay development, clinical studies, evidence generation, reimbursement expansion, commercialization, software, acquisitions, and SBC | Payer coverage or denial pressure, ASP/mix reversal, oncology adoption slowdown, test-cost inflation, capacity bottlenecks, litigation/regulatory exposure, debt, or dilution | Test and oncology growth continue while reimbursement, collection, test cost, clinical spend, or diluted common residual deteriorates |

## Current evidence anchors

- Q2 2026 revenue was `$752.8M`, up `37.7%`; total test volume was about `1.04M`, up `22.4%`; oncology tests were about `296,700`, up `57.2%`.
- After Q2, Natera raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to `$2.85B-$2.91B`.
- Q1 2026 revenue was `$696.6M`, up `38.8%`, with more than `1.0M` tests and about `258,900` oncology tests, up `54.4%`.
- Q4 2025 revenue was `$665.5M`; full-year 2025 revenue was `$2.31B`, up `35.9%`, while full-year oncology tests rose `51.6%`.
- Signatera, Panorama, Horizon, and Prospera are distinct branded franchises with different clinical, payer, volume, and evidence burdens. Revenue guidance and test counts are visibility inputs, not collected common-owner cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile test volume to billed volume, ASP, payer mix, denials, appeals,
   cash collections, contractual adjustments, and accounts receivable aging.
2. Separate Signatera oncology monitoring, Panorama/Horizon women’s health,
   and Prospera organ health by reimbursement, clinical evidence, utilization,
   and test-cost trajectory.
3. Keep clinical trials, evidence generation, regulatory work, commercialization,
   laboratory capacity, and software investment in the reinvestment denominator;
   growth in tests does not prove durable owner cash.
4. Test whether Medicare and commercial coverage expansion is repeatable or
   dependent on one-time policy, approval, guideline, or contract events.
5. Reconcile adjusted measures, stock compensation, acquisitions, legal or
   regulatory claims, deferred revenue, inventory, leases, debt, and diluted
   shares before promoting reported cash flow.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what oncology adoption, test frequency,
ASP, payer coverage, gross margin, laboratory reinvestment, evidence spending,
and cost of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks
whether reimbursement timing, household and provider budgets, clinical proof,
regulatory decisions, labor, sequencing inputs, and working-capital needs can
support the claimed cash conversion through a cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`natera-precision-diagnostics-qualified; reimbursement-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from test activity and assay
mix to billed collections, payer responsibility, required evidence and capacity
spend, claims, debt, and diluted common residual. Revenue growth, oncology test
growth, guidance, ASP, and adjusted metrics remain diagnostic inputs.
