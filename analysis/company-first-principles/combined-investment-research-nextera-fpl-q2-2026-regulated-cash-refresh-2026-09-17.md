# NextEra Energy / FPL Q2 2026 regulated-cash refresh

## Purpose

This refresh completes the current-period power-grid comparison by updating
FPL’s legal-entity cash surface. It separates FPL cash from NextEra corporate
and NEER activity, and it keeps regulatory recovery, billed/collected dollars,
capital investment, debt, nuclear fuel, and parent distributions distinct.

## FPL cash bridge

FPL generated `$5.388B` of H1 operating cash and spent `$5.780B` on capital
expenditures plus `$152M` on nuclear fuel. The visible bridges are:

`$5.388B OCF - $5.780B capex = $(392M)`

and:

`$5.388B - $5.780B - $0.152B = $(544M)`

FPL also issued `$2.502B` of long-term debt, retired `$648M`, reduced
commercial paper by `$1.044B`, and paid `$100M` in dividends to NextEra. Accrued
property additions were `$1.231B`. These facts show a regulated utility that
converts customer and clause cash into a large investment program, but whose
common-owner residual depends on recovery timing, financing, and legal-entity
allocation.

## Why aggregate FPL cash is not category receipt proof

The prior FPL research identified a strong category-recovery route: SPPCRC
revenue requirements, Distribution Inspection activity and costs, true-ups,
and component attribution. The current 10-Q adds period cash, but it does not
automatically allocate aggregate FPL OCF to Distribution Inspection or another
named category.

The required chain remains:

`approved category cost -> billing determinant -> customer bill -> receipt -> clause/true-up allocation -> paid capex and earned return -> common-owner cash`

Customer-contract revenue and operating cash are useful corroboration. They are
not a substitute for the category billing determinant, receipt ledger, and
source-of-funds allocation.

## Capital and recovery burden

FPL’s Q2 release described approximately `$2.8B` of quarterly capex and
`$12–13B` of full-year 2026 capital investment. The 10-Q capital schedule shows
approximately `$54.730B` across the displayed FPL capital categories for the
remainder of 2026 through 2030. The filing explicitly says FPL capex includes
both new facilities to meet demand and capital improvements and maintenance.

That distinction is essential. Rate-base growth can create future earnings,
but paid construction, AFUDC, regulatory lag, storm recovery, fuel clauses,
customer affordability, and financing claims determine when cash reaches the
relevant legal entity and then the parent common owner.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans tests

1. **Legal-entity perimeter:** keep FPL, NextEra parent, NEER, and affiliates
   separate; trace dividends, intercompany funding, guarantees, and debt.
2. **Category receipt test:** obtain the named billing determinant, category
   bills, receipts, true-up, and source-of-funds allocation.
3. **Rate-base/capex test:** reconcile paid capex, accrued additions, CWIP,
   AFUDC, regulatory assets/liabilities, depreciation, and in-service dates.
4. **Clause-timing test:** separate fuel, storm, franchise, SPPCRC, and customer
   credit timing from recurring utility cash.
5. **Debt-and-return test:** model FPL debt issuance, retirements, commercial
   paper, interest, allowed return, and parent distributions.
6. **Adjusted-earnings test:** reconcile any adjusted FPL or NextEra earnings
   to GAAP and isolate market, tax, storm, and nonrecurring effects.
7. **Affordability test:** measure whether lower customer bills or cost offsets
   alter recovery, load, credit quality, or common-owner residual cash.
8. **Stress test:** combine rates, storms, construction inflation, fuel cost,
   regulatory lag, load timing, and capital-market access.

These are forensic controls, not allegations of misconduct. The risk is
allocating aggregate regulated cash to a category or parent owner without a
receipt and legal-entity bridge.

## Promotion gate

NextEra/FPL remains `qualified; current-period cash updated; category-receipt
and allocation open; no-ranking`. The next joinable object is a named category
or project with a billing determinant, customer receipts, cost allocation,
period-matched paid capex, earned return, and remittance to the relevant owner.
