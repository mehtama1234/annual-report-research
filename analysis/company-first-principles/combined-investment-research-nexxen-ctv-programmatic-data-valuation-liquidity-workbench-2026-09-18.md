# Nexxen CTV, programmatic, and data valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Nexxen International from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests DSP and SSP economics, CTV inventory, ACR data, smart-TV home-screen activation, data licensing, programmatic contribution, partner concentration, privacy, cloud dependence, cash, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not pool Nexxen with agencies, publishers, measurement firms, or pure supply-side ad-tech platforms.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nexxen | Programmatic CTV and data-workflow cash after media settlement, ACR rights, DSP/SSP costs, partner concentration, privacy, cloud infrastructure, product investment, debt, and dilution | CTV and data rights, DSP/SSP technology, AI workflow, cloud and data-center spend, sales, working capital, debt, and shares | DSP concentration, privacy and identifier loss, CTV tracking limits, partner termination, non-core erosion, advertiser slowdown, or platform competition | Programmatic revenue and contribution rise while retention, data rights, media settlement, ex-TAC conversion, cash collection, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was about `$364.8M`, programmatic revenue `$340.6M`, contribution ex-TAC `$353.1M`, and adjusted EBITDA `$115.1M`.
- Q1 2026 revenue was about `$86.8M`, programmatic revenue `$81.9M`, contribution ex-TAC `$84.5M`, adjusted EBITDA `$16.3M`, cash `$94.6M`, and long-term debt zero.
- Exclusive `V`/`VIDAA` ACR data, smart-TV home-screen activation, data licensing, and `nexAI` are the core differentiation claims.
- Q4 2025 contribution ex-TAC retention fell to `92%` from `102%`, illustrating partner and customer-concentration risk despite a programmatic-heavy mix.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile revenue and contribution ex-TAC to media pass-through, supply costs, data licensing, partner concentration, and programmatic mix.
2. Test whether ACR and home-screen rights are durable, exclusive, consented, and collectible rather than merely strategic descriptions.
3. Separate organic CTV and data growth from one-off licensing, partner timing, non-core declines, and DSP customer changes.
4. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA and cash to cloud, data, privacy, product, sales, working-capital, debt, and diluted-share requirements.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what programmatic growth, CTV monetization, data-right duration, ex-TAC conversion, retention, reinvestment, and cost of capital are embedded in the valuation. The stress test asks whether privacy rules, identifier loss, DSP concentration, cloud dependence, platform competition, and fragmented advertiser demand leave durable collected cash.

## Promotion boundary

`nexxen-qualified; ctv-data-rights-and-partner-retention-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from CTV inventory and data rights to collection, media settlement, partner retention, privacy and consent, platform investment, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Programmatic revenue, contribution ex-TAC, adjusted EBITDA, guidance, and data agreements remain diagnostic inputs.
