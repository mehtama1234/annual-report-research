# O-I Glass furnace packaging valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves O-I Glass from current-period evidence into a company-specific
valuation, furnace-utilization, energy, regional-pricing, restructuring, capex,
debt, and common-owner cash test. It does not pool glass, metal, fiber, and other
packaging formats into a generic container multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| O-I Glass | Collected glass-container cash after cullet and materials, gas and electricity, furnace uptime, regional pricing, freight, plant restructuring, maintenance/growth capex, debt, claims, and dilution | Furnaces, plants, cullet systems, lightweighting, maintenance, capacity rationalization, energy contracts, environmental compliance, and working capital | European energy reset, volume decline, price pressure, furnace outage, regional competition, impairment, restructuring, debt, or dilution | Fit to Win benefits and Americas strength continue while furnace utilization, energy cost, Europe pricing, FCF, leverage, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- O-I operated `64` manufacturing plants across `18` countries in 2025, with Americas and Europe reportable segments.
- Full-year 2025 net sales were `$6.426B`, segment operating profit `$846M`, cash from operations `$600M`, and free cash flow `$168M`.
- Q2 2026 net sales were `$1.668B`; segment operating profit fell to `$171M`; adjusted EPS fell to `$0.09`; volumes declined `4.5%`.
- Q2 Americas segment operating profit was `$165M` with `17.4%` margins, while Europe segment operating profit fell to `$6M` with `0.9%` margins; O-I recorded an `$873M` goodwill impairment and a `$96M` increase in deferred-tax valuation allowances.
- 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance was cut to `$1.0B-$1.1B`, FCF guidance to `($50M)-$150M`, and cumulative net Fit to Win target to `$650M`; planned 2026 capex was about `$425M`.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile container shipments to furnace utilization, cullet/material inputs,
   regional price, product mix, customer contracts, freight, and collections.
2. Separate Americas and Europe by energy procurement, pricing, plant footprint,
   utilization, impairment, and customer economics.
3. Keep furnace maintenance, rationalization, lightweighting, capex, energy
   contracts, environmental obligations, restructuring, and debt service in the
   owner-cash denominator.
4. Test whether Fit to Win benefits are recurring collected cash or temporary cost
   cuts that impair capacity, service, quality, or future volume.
5. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA, goodwill impairment, tax valuation allowances,
   restructuring, pension/claims, inventory, debt, interest, and diluted shares
   before treating FCF as common-owner surplus.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what glass volume, furnace utilization,
regional pricing, energy cost, Fit to Win savings, capex, leverage, and cost of
capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether energy,
freight, European industrial policy, consumer packaging choice, cullet availability,
and refinancing can preserve liquidity through a manufacturing cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`oi-glass-furnace-packaging-qualified; energy-utilization-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from glass shipments and furnaces
to customer collections, energy and cullet cost, utilization, required capex,
restructuring, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Volume, segment operating
profit, Fit to Win benefits, adjusted EBITDA, guidance, and buybacks remain
diagnostic inputs.
