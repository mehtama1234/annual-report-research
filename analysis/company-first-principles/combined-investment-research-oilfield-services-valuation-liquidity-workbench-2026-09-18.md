# Oilfield-services valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Halliburton from oilfield-activity evidence into a
company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It keeps service execution,
pressure pumping, drilling and evaluation, trained crews, equipment renewal,
customer capex, receivables, international project risk, warranty/claims,
debt, and diluted common-owner cash separate from upstream reserve and
production economics.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Halliburton oilfield services | Service cash after crew and equipment utilization, pricing, customer collection, fleet renewal, working capital, international mobilization, claims, debt, SBC, and dilution | Pressure-pumping fleet, completion tools, drilling equipment, maintenance and technology capex, skilled labor, inventory, receivables, digital/software spend, acquisitions, and joint-venture capital | Producer capex pause, commodity decline, customer credit, North American pricing pressure, idle fleet, international political/currency risk, equipment failure, rates, or refinancing | Revenue and segment margins rise while utilization, price, collection, fleet return, service intensity, or diluted per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$22.184B`, operating cash flow `$2.926B`, productive-asset
  spending `$1.254B`, acquisitions `$185M`, and the mechanical residual
  `$1.487B` before dividends, buybacks, and equity-method investment cash.
- FY2025 dividends and repurchases totaled approximately `$1.586B`, exceeding
  that mechanical residual before debt and other claims. Cash was `$2.206B`
  against long-term debt of `$7.158B`.
- Year-end receivables were `$4.942B` and inventory `$2.976B`; these balances
  remain exposed to customer credit, activity timing, equipment parts, and
  international collection.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$5.714B`, operating income `$778M`, and first-half OCF
  `$1.097B`; first-half capex was `$427M` and acquisitions `$107M`, while
  receivables rose to `$5.325B` and inventory to `$3.056B`.
- Q2 international revenue was approximately `$3.4B` versus `$2.3B` in North
  America. Completions and Production revenue was `$3.2B` and Drilling and
  Evaluation `$2.5B`; mix changes the cycle, mobilization, and collection
  burden.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile customer activity to service-line price, volume, utilization,
   crew deployment, equipment returns, contract terms, and cash collection.
   Production growth does not prove service-company owner cash.
2. Separate fleet renewal, maintenance, technology, capacity, and strategic
   capex. Under-spending can inflate current FCF while weakening future
   utilization, reliability, and pricing power.
3. Track receivable aging, contract assets, allowance for credit losses,
   customer/geographic concentration, disputes, and post-period collections.
4. Keep adjusted operating income and EPS exclusions visible alongside GAAP
   cash. Impairments, restructuring, litigation, integration, FX, and software
   charges can be non-cash now while representing funded economic burdens.
5. Test dividends, buybacks, acquisitions, joint-venture funding, debt service,
   and diluted shares against through-cycle service cash rather than a single
   high-activity quarter.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what customer drilling/completion
activity, service intensity, pricing, utilization, international margin,
maintenance rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. Halliburton
should be valued on service returns through a commodity and producer-capex
cycle, not on reserves, production, or a short-lived pressure-pumping peak.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether producers can finance work through
lower oil and gas prices, higher rates, tighter credit, geopolitical disruption,
currency volatility, and shareholder-return pressure. Liquidity passes only
when crews, fleet, inventory, receivables, claims, debt, and common residual
remain fundable at the legal entity.

## Promotion boundary

`oilfield-services-qualified; utilization-cycle-and-collection-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from service activity and
segment shipments to customer collection, price/volume, utilization, fleet
renewal, inventory settlement, international project cash, claims, debt, and
diluted common residual. Revenue, segment margin, adjusted EPS, OCF, FCF,
production activity, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
