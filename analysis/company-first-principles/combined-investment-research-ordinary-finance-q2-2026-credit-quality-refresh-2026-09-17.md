# Ordinary finance Q2 2026 credit-quality refresh

Research date: 2026-09-17

## Finding

The latest official filings show three different ways a financial platform
turns promises into earnings: JPMorgan combines deposits, lending, payments,
and markets; American Express combines premium spending, fees, rewards, and
credit; Capital One combines consumer lending with the Discover integration.
The common analytical error is treating transaction volume, pre-provision
earnings, or reported net income as loss-adjusted common-owner cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

JPMorgan's headline Q2 earnings and revenue include a $4.55B pretax gain
related to Visa shares. That gain must be separated from recurring spread,
fee, payment, and market economics. The bank's $2.5B credit cost, $2.4B net
charge-offs, and $149M reserve build are more relevant to loss normalization,
while the 14.1% CET1 ratio is a regulatory constraint and allocation surface,
not proof that all earnings are distributable.

American Express reported $455.8B of billed business and 9% spending growth,
but the Q2 credit-loss provision declined partly because of a reserve release
while net write-offs increased. H1 provisions were $2.336B and H1 SBC was
$358M. The membership model therefore requires separate tests for fee-paying
retention, rewards and benefits, merchant economics, funding, payment rates,
delinquencies, write-offs, reserves, marketing, and diluted shares.

Capital One's Q2 net income was $3.0B and pre-provision earnings were $6.8B,
but the quarter also had $3.0B of provision, $3.642B of net charge-offs, and a
$662M allowance release. The addition of Discover expanded loans and network
activity, but the integration and purchase-accounting adjustments remain part
of the economic burden. The correct screen is loss-adjusted spread and
capital generation after reserves, funding cost, integration, rewards,
marketing, regulatory capital, and dilution—not pre-provision earnings.

## Owner-cash and valuation implication

- **JPMorgan:** value requires normalized markets revenue, net interest income
  after deposit cost, credit losses, technology and regulatory expense, CET1
  requirements, preferred claims, and common distributions.
- **American Express:** value requires billed-business conversion into fees,
  merchant economics, rewards, service, funding, credit losses, acquisition,
  SBC, and diluted common earnings.
- **Capital One:** value requires loss-normalized card/auto spread, deposit
  pricing, reserve adequacy, Discover network economics, integration cost,
  capital needs, and the return on the acquired loan and network assets.

Damodaran-style valuation must make price pay for NIM, credit losses, deposit
beta, fee growth, payment volume, capital requirements, and normalized market
income. Lyn Alden-style stress must test rates, unemployment, inflation,
household affordability, deposit competition, liquidity, collateral values,
regulation, and refinancing. Composite accounting scores remain prompts for
reconciliation rather than fraud findings.

## Promotion consequence

This is a material current-period upgrade to the ordinary-finance lane. It
adds a live separation between activity, funding, credit cost, reserve release,
write-offs, exceptional gains, integration cost, capital, and common-owner
residual. The cohort remains
`credit-quality-qualified; loss-normalization-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`.
