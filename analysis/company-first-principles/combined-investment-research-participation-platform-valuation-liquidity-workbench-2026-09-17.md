# Participation platform valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Roblox from a platform thesis into an explicit valuation,
reinvestment, liquidity, and thesis-breaker object. Roblox is not treated as a
generic software subscription business: users, hours, bookings, creator
payouts, virtual-currency economics, trust and safety, age policy, moderation,
and platform infrastructure all sit between engagement and common-owner cash.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Roblox participation platform | Cohort- and age-adjusted bookings and collected platform cash after developer exchange fees, infrastructure, trust/safety, moderation, payments, R&D, SBC, and dilution | Creator payouts, cloud/hosting, moderation and safety systems, age verification, discovery, payment costs, R&D, acquisitions, SBC replacement, and diluted shares | Engagement-to-bookings conversion, algorithm changes, creator retention, safety/regulatory costs, app-store/payment exposure, infrastructure spend, and liquidity | DAUs/hours continue growing while bookings, creator economics, safety cost, cash conversion, or diluted per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue grew `36%` to `$4.9B`, bookings grew `55%` to `$6.8B`, average
  DAUs were `127M`, and hours engaged reached `124B`.
- Developer exchange fees exceeded `$1.5B` in 2025, making creator payouts a
  first-order economic claim rather than a minor operating expense.
- Q1 2026 DAUs grew `35%` to `132M` and hours grew `43%` to `31B`; Q2 2026
  monetization softened after algorithm changes prioritized retention and
  content diversity over near-term revenue.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile bookings, revenue recognition, virtual-currency breakage or
   deferral, payment settlement, and developer exchange fees; bookings are not
   collected common-owner cash.
2. Connect DAUs and hours to payer conversion, bookings per user, creator
   payouts, geography, age cohort, and platform take rate.
3. Treat trust-and-safety, age-checking, moderation, fraud, and content review
   as required operating and maintenance costs, not optional growth spend.
4. Replace SBC economically and carry diluted shares into per-share value;
   repurchases do not prove that platform residual cash is distributable.
5. Test whether algorithm changes improve long-term retention and creator
   health at the cost of near-term monetization, or merely defer revenue.
6. Separate infrastructure capacity, payment/app-store claims, and legal or
   regulatory costs from reported operating cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what user growth, age-cohort mix,
engagement, payer conversion, bookings, take rate, creator payout ratio,
trust/safety reinvestment, margin, and cost of capital the price requires. The
Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether household discretionary budgets,
payment rails, platform rules, regulatory costs, infrastructure prices, and
creator liquidity remain durable through a macro reversal.

## Promotion boundary

`participation-platform-qualified; cohort-monetization-and-governance-cost-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period bookings-to-collection, developer payout,
cohort monetization, infrastructure and governance cost, SBC replacement, debt,
and diluted common-owner cash. DAUs, hours, bookings, revenue growth, and
reported FCF remain diagnostic inputs.
