# Permanent-capital conglomerate valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Berkshire Hathaway from diversified-earnings evidence into
a company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It keeps insurance float,
BNSF railroad renewal, Berkshire Hathaway Energy regulated capital,
manufacturing/service/retail subsidiaries, securities, parent liquidity,
acquisitions, and common-owner cash separate from Loews' holding-company model,
standalone insurers, and asset managers.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Berkshire permanent-capital conglomerate | Look-through common value after insurance claims and capital, BNSF maintenance, BHE rate-base funding, operating-subsidiary reinvestment, taxes, debt, parent liquidity, securities, acquisitions, and dilution | Insurance reserves/capital, railroad track and equipment, regulated utility transmission/generation, manufacturing/retail working capital, parent acquisitions, debt, and capital allocation opportunity cost | Catastrophe or reserve shock, railroad underinvestment, utility/regulatory or wildfire claims, operating-subsidiary weakness, securities drawdown, acquisition overpayment, trapped cash, or successor allocation failure | Operating earnings and cash rise while required capital, claims, subsidiary remittance, maintenance, acquisition return, or per-share value deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net earnings attributable to shareholders were `$67.0B`, operating
  earnings `$44.5B`, net operating cash flow `$46.0B`, and consolidated capex
  `$20.9B`.
- Year-end cash and U.S. Treasury bills were approximately `$373B`, while
  consolidated insurance claim liabilities were approximately `$152B`.
  Gross cash is not unrestricted parent cash.
- BNSF generated approximately `$7.4B` of operating earnings and used about
  `$3.8B` of capital expenditures; railroad maintenance and service quality must
  be funded before look-through owner cash is estimated.
- Berkshire Hathaway Energy used approximately `$10.6B` of 2025 capex and is
  subject to rate recovery, regulatory capital, wildfire/environmental claims,
  and subsidiary debt requirements.
- Manufacturing, service, retailing, McLane, and Pilot generated approximately
  `$13.6B` of operating earnings and used about `$5.3B` of capex, but these
  businesses have unlike working-capital and reinvestment denominators.
- Berkshire acquired OxyChem for approximately `$9.7B` and agreed to acquire
  Taylor Morrison for approximately `$8.5B` in 2026 announcements. Successor
  capital allocation is now a measurable return hurdle.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate operating earnings from GAAP security marks and separate
   consolidated OCF from distributable parent cash.
2. Reconcile insurance float to claims, reserve development, catastrophe risk,
   reinsurance, duration, and required capital; float is not ordinary corporate
   cash.
3. Charge BNSF and BHE for maintenance, renewal, regulated funding, debt, and
   safety/environmental obligations before valuing their earnings.
4. Disaggregate manufacturing, service, retail, McLane, and Pilot for working
   capital, inventory, capex, labor, and margin rather than pooling them.
5. Test acquisitions, securities purchases, retained Treasury bills, taxes,
   parent overhead, debt, and diluted shares against opportunity cost and
   per-share value. Operating earnings alone cannot validate allocation.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test uses look-through segment cash after
required capital and assigns separate growth, margin, reinvestment, and cost of
capital assumptions to insurance, rail, regulated energy, and operating
subsidiaries. The parent allocation decision is part of valuation, not a
footnote.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether claims, regulated capital, railroad
renewal, utility debt, subsidiary cash traps, securities losses, and acquisition
funding remain manageable through recession, catastrophe, inflation, rates, and
capital-market closure. Liquidity passes only when legal-entity cash and senior
claims remain visible.

## Promotion boundary

`permanent-capital-conglomerate-qualified; look-through-capital-and-allocation-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from each segment's earnings
and cash to required insurance/regulated capital, maintenance capex, subsidiary
remittance, taxes, debt, acquisitions, parent liquidity, security realization,
and diluted common residual. Net income, operating earnings, OCF, float,
consolidated cash, book value, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
