# Pharma and MedTech portfolio-renewal valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Johnson & Johnson from diversified healthcare evidence
into a product-lifecycle, litigation, acquisition-return, and common-cash test.
It keeps Innovative Medicine's patent and pipeline economics separate from
MedTech's procedure, quality, and hospital-budget economics.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Johnson & Johnson | Innovative Medicine and MedTech cash after product lifecycle, clinical/R&D, quality, litigation, acquisition, debt, dividend, and dilution claims | Internal R&D, clinical trials, regulatory submissions, launches, manufacturing, device quality, salesforce, acquisitions, IPR&D, and portfolio renewal | Patent loss, failed pipeline, litigation, recall/quality event, hospital-budget pressure, acquisition overpayment, debt, or dividend crowding out renewal | Sales and adjusted EPS grow while patent replacement, acquired return, litigation cash, MedTech procedure economics, or cash per diluted share deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- 2025 Innovative Medicine sales were `$60.401B` and MedTech sales `$33.792B`.
  FY2025 operating cash flow was `$24.530B`, PP&E capex `$4.832B`, and
  acquisitions plus acquired IPR&D about `$17.926B`.
- The conservative residual screen after PP&E, acquisitions, and acquired
  IPR&D was about `$1.772B`, despite strong reported operating cash.
- J&J paid `$12.381B` of dividends and repurchased `$5.953B` of common stock in
  2025. Talc-related reserves were approximately `$3.4B`; litigation cash and
  legal costs remain a recurring owner-cash question.
- 2025 accounts receivable used about `$1.781B` and inventories `$1.450B` of
  cash, while accounts payable and accrued liabilities provided `$2.377B`.
  Working capital requires normalization alongside adjusted earnings.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate product-level sales into volume, price, mix, currency, launches,
   acquired revenue, divestitures, and patent/exclusivity erosion.
2. Track each major acquisition's price, acquired sales, operating income,
   pipeline probability, integration, amortization, debt funding, and return.
3. Reconcile GAAP earnings, adjusted EPS, internal R&D, acquired IPR&D,
   amortization, litigation payments, reserves, insurance, and share count.
4. Test MedTech growth against procedures, hospital budgets, clinical evidence,
   quality, recalls, manufacturing, consumables, and service economics.
5. Normalize receivables, inventory, rebates, returns, litigation, debt,
   dividends, buybacks, and dilution before promoting cash per share.
6. Treat adjusted EPS, operational sales, dividend history, acquisitions, and
   repurchases as diagnostic until product-renewal cash is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test values medicines by remaining exclusivity
and probability-weighted pipeline replacement, while valuing MedTech by
procedure adoption, consumables, quality, and hospital workflow. The Lyn
Alden-style stress test asks whether regulation, reimbursement, rates, trial
costs, manufacturing inflation, litigation, and acquisition funding leave
enough cash to renew the portfolio. Diversification reduces concentration but
does not remove lifecycle or legal claims.

## Promotion boundary

`pharma-medtech-qualified; portfolio-renewal-and-litigation-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from product sales,
exclusivity, pipeline, R&D, MedTech procedures, quality claims, acquisitions,
IPR&D, litigation cash, working capital, debt, dividends, and diluted common
residual. Adjusted EPS, sales excluding patent pressure, OCF, dividends, and
buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
