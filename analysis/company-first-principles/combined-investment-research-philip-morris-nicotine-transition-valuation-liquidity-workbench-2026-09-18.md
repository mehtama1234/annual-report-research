# Nicotine transition and regulated-habit valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Philip Morris International from its annual-report packet
into a company-specific valuation, reinvestment, and liquidity test. It treats
combustibles, smoke-free products, regulation, tax, market access, litigation,
and transition investment as separate but connected economic objects. It does
not pool nicotine habit economics with ordinary staples, beverage, beauty, or
licensed-consumer models.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Philip Morris International | Regulated nicotine cash after combustibles excise taxes, smoke-free manufacturing and market access, device/platform investment, R&D, litigation, working capital, debt, and dilution | IQOS/ZYN and other smoke-free capacity, devices, consumables, product conversion, regulatory submissions, marketing, taxes, working capital, and acquisitions | Excise-tax or regulatory change, product authorization loss, illicit trade, litigation, currency, transition cannibalization, debt, or consumer switching slowdown | Smoke-free mix and users rise while smoke-free margin, repeat purchase, market access, cash conversion, or diluted common residual weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net revenue exceeded `$40B`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$7.54`; smoke-free
  revenue was nearly `$17B`, or `41.5%` of total net revenue.
- Smoke-free shipment volume rose `12.8%` in FY2025 and products were available
  in `106` markets. PMI reported more than `43M` adult users at year-end.
- Q1 2026 net revenue increased `9.1%` to `$10.1B`; smoke-free revenue rose
  `12.4%` and represented `43%` of revenue.
- Q2 2026 net revenue increased `10.4%` to `$11.2B`; smoke-free revenue rose
  `11.7%`, with products available in `109` markets.
- These mix and user metrics do not by themselves prove normalized owner cash:
  the transition requires capacity, devices, consumables, authorizations, tax
  payments, market access, and repeat collection to be joined.

## PMI Q2 2026 transition-to-owner-cash bridge

The Q2 2026 filing confirms strong transition momentum but also supplies the
cash denominator that the headline mix omits. For H1, PMI reported `$21.338B`
of net revenue, including `$9.024B` of smoke-free revenue and `$12.314B` of
combustible revenue. Smoke-free revenue was therefore approximately `42.3%`
of total revenue on the filed period, while H1 smoke-free volume increased
`8.3%` and cigarette volume declined `1.9%`. This is a meaningful mix and
volume transition, not yet proof that smoke-free revenue has the same margin,
tax, reinvestment, or collection profile as the cash it is replacing.

H1 net cash provided by operating activities was `$5.093B`, compared with
`$3.062B` in the prior-year period. Capital expenditures were `$733M`, and PMI
expects full-year 2026 capex of `$1.4B–$1.6B`, predominantly supporting
smoke-free manufacturing capacity. H1 financing cash use included `$4.021B`
of long-term debt repayment and `$4.604B` of dividends. Total debt was
`$49.1B` at June 30, 2026. The reported operating cash is therefore a
consolidated cash object after taxes and interest but before the full-year
transition-capacity burden and shareholder/debt claims.

PMI does not disclose capital expenditures by segment because that information
is not reported to or used by its chief operating decision maker. The statement
that 2026 capex is predominantly smoke-free is therefore a management
allocation description, not a smoke-free cash ledger. The same boundary applies
to operating cash: the filing does not allocate collections, excise taxes,
working capital, debt service, or owner cash between International Smoke-Free,
International Combustibles, and U.S.

Regulatory and tax claims remain live cash risks. PMI reports that Germany's
disputed supplemental heated-tobacco excise tax was provisionally paid in
January 2025, while the appeal remains pending and an oral hearing is expected
no earlier than Q4 2026. Japan's HTP excise structure was harmonized with
cigarettes in stages beginning April 1, 2026, with another step on October 1,
2026. The EU has also proposed expanding the Tobacco Excise Directive to cover
smoke-free products with differentiated treatment. These are not abstract
policy risks: they can change relative consumer pricing, tax collection, and
the return on conversion capacity.

The correct PMI test is therefore:

```text
adult-user conversion and repeat consumable purchase
  -> smoke-free and combustible shipment / collection
  -> excise-tax and regulatory burden
  -> device, consumable, science, and manufacturing reinvestment
  -> working capital, litigation, debt, and currency
  -> dividends and diluted common residual
```

The current PMI status remains qualified and unranked. Smoke-free revenue,
adult-user counts, shipment growth, adjusted EPS, dividends, and consolidated
operating cash cannot substitute for a same-entity smoke-free margin,
collection, tax, capacity-return, and residual join.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate reported revenue and gross profit by combustibles and smoke-free
   category; a rising smoke-free mix can conceal device subsidies, launch costs,
   or margin dilution.
2. Keep excise taxes, regulatory submissions, market-access spending, product
   authorizations, and litigation as economic burdens rather than treating them
   as non-recurring narrative friction.
3. Reconcile adult-user counts, shipment volumes, consumables, device sales,
   repeat purchase, inventory, and cash collection; users are not receipts.
4. Test whether cigarette cash is being harvested, reinvested, or cannibalized
   faster than smoke-free economics replace it.
5. Treat adjusted EPS, dividends, buybacks, and leverage capacity as residual
   claims only after transition capex, taxes, legal exposure, and dilution are
   funded.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what smoke-free user conversion,
repeat consumption, category margin, regulatory duration, reinvestment rate, and
cost of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether
taxes, policy, currency, litigation, illicit trade, consumer affordability, and
debt can impair the cash engine before the transition reaches its claimed
steady state.

## Promotion boundary

`nicotine-transition-qualified; regulation-and-margin-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from adult-user or shipment
metrics to product collection, excise taxes, manufacturing and device
reinvestment, authorization costs, litigation, debt service, and diluted common
residual. Smoke-free mix, users, shipment volume, adjusted EPS, dividends, and
buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
