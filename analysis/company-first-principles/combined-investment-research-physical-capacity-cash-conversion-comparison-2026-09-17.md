# Physical capacity: collateral, project funding, and owner-cash conversion

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Thesis

The same macro force—more spending on physical capacity—can create very
different investment mechanisms. United Rentals monetizes access to equipment;
Equinix monetizes powered, interconnected capacity; Digital Realty funds
hyperscale development and lease commencement. Demand, fleet scale, backlog,
and available financing are not interchangeable evidence of owner cash.

The reusable chain is:

`physical demand -> scarce control point -> payer contract -> capital deployment -> operating conversion -> funding burden -> owner cash -> return`

## Same-period comparison

| Lane | Control point | Current cash/capital surface | Main funding or claim burden | Decisive promotion object |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United Rentals | Fleet access, branch density, equipment availability, and resale channel | H1 OCF `$3.305B`; rental-equipment purchases `$2.720B`; resale proceeds `$680M`; ABL capacity `$2.802B`; AR capacity `$85M` | Replacement versus growth fleet capex, debt, leases, maintenance, resale-cycle risk, and borrowing-base eligibility | Populated borrowing-base certificate plus fleet-age, utilization, replacement, resale, and source-to-purchase return schedule |
| Equinix | Interconnection density and powered data-center capacity | H1 OCF `$1.784B`; PP&E purchases `$2.834B`; real-estate acquisitions `$224M`; AFFO `$2.233B`; unaccrued capex commitments `$6.1B` | Power and construction cost, lease-up, JV/VIE funding, debt, recurring-capex definition, and dilution | Project capital, commencement, stabilized yield, power/lease cost, partner funding, debt, and per-share residual |
| Digital Realty | Hyperscale development pipeline, backlog, and lease commencement | H1 OCF `$1.595B`; cash-basis capex `$1.627B`; development capex `$1.477B`; roughly `$2.5B` ATM equity proceeds; backlog about `$1.4B` at company share | Development timing, private capital, project debt, preferred/OP-unit claims, equity dilution, and lease-up | Project-level yield/commencement, private-capital economics, debt/preferred/OP-unit claims, and diluted common return |

## What can be compared safely

1. All three have visible demand or activity surfaces but require substantial
   capital before the customer economics become owner cash.
2. All three can show a positive operating-cash or non-GAAP metric while the
   full growth-capital burden remains unresolved.
3. Financing capacity is an enabling claim, not cash generated by the control
   point.
4. The missing evidence is not generic “more growth”; it is a legal, project,
   or collateral object that joins capital deployed to cash collected and
   residual return.

## What cannot be pooled

- URI's `$2.887B` ABL/AR capacity is not comparable to Equinix's AFFO or
  Digital Realty's ATM proceeds.
- Equinix's management-defined recurring capex is not the same denominator as
  Digital Realty's development capex or URI's rental-equipment purchases.
- URI resale proceeds are asset-disposal recovery; data-center backlog is a
  contractual demand signal; neither is recurring owner cash by itself.
- Digital Realty equity issuance and Equinix JV/VIE funding affect common-owner
  claims differently from URI debt secured by eligible collateral.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans overlay

| Diagnostic | URI | Equinix | Digital Realty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capitalization risk | Replacement fleet and depreciation may obscure lifecycle maintenance | PP&E combines growth and maintenance; recurring capex is management-defined | Development/enhancement/recurring categories and indirect costs require reconciliation |
| Cash-conversion risk | Receivables, payables, resale proceeds, and supplier terms | Receivables, power commitments, construction, and lease-up timing | Lease commencement, development-in-process, acquisitions, and partner funding |
| Funding risk | Borrowing-base eligibility, NOLV, reserves, and ABL/AR capacity | Debt, JV/VIE commitments, guarantees, and capital-market access | ATM dilution, debt, preferred claims, OP units, and private capital |
| False-owner-cash shortcut | Capacity or resale recovery treated as free cash | AFFO treated as residual after all growth funding | FFO/backlog treated as common-owner cash |

These are reconciliation prompts, not allegations of misconduct. No cross-
company manipulation score is appropriate while the accounting perimeters and
project objects differ.

## Valuation and macro handoff

The Damodaran question is different for each lane:

- **URI:** what replacement, resale, utilization, and funding spread produce a
  durable fleet return?
- **Equinix:** what project yield and commencement pace cover power,
  construction, JV/VIE, debt, and dilution claims?
- **Digital Realty:** what development yield and lease-up timing justify
  private-capital, debt, preferred, and ATM equity burdens?

The Lyn Alden-style stress test is also mechanism-specific: used-equipment
prices and credit availability for URI; power costs, rates, construction
inflation, and hyperscaler timing for Equinix; and rates, cap rates, power,
lease-up, and capital-market access for Digital Realty.

## Decision

The physical-capacity cohort is `qualified; diagnostic; no-ranking`. The
strongest shared insight is not that one company is better. It is that the
scarce control point only becomes investable after its funding and receipt
mechanism is proven. The three next promotion objects are now explicit and
non-interchangeable:

1. URI borrowing-base and fleet lifecycle package;
2. Equinix project-return and JV/VIE funding package; and
3. Digital Realty development-yield, private-capital, and diluted-return
   package.
