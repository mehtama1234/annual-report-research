## The question

Wheaton paid `4.300B USD` for the BHP Antamina precious-metals purchase
agreement (PMPA). The pilot asks:

> Can the research system prove what capital funded, what asset and contractual
> right it bought, what operating output and cash came back, who carried the
> financing burden, and whether the price paid is justified after tax, debt,
> delivery, and mine-life risk?

The current answer is:

> The public evidence now proves both Wheaton's $4.300B closing payment and
> BHP's approximately $4.300B proceeds receipt for the named Antamina
> transaction, as well as a strong named-asset and stream-economics proxy. It
> does not yet prove BHP-only settlement receipts, Antamina-specific tax and
> interest, lender-level repayment, or a source-backed IRR/NPV.

## 1. Force and industry context

The relevant force is capital concentration around scarce, long-life physical
assets and the increasing use of contractual or streaming structures to obtain
exposure to those assets without owning and operating the entire mine.

The industry system contains:

- the mine operator and its reserves, labor, permitting, sustaining capital,
  and operating risk;
- the streaming or royalty company, which supplies upfront capital and receives
  contractual metal deliveries at a defined payment formula;
- lenders and administrative agents that fund part of the upfront purchase;
- the metal market and refiners that determine realized prices and settlement;
- governments and taxing jurisdictions; and
- the ultimate equity owner, who receives the residual after operating,
  financing, tax, depletion, and corporate burdens.

The control point being tested is not mine operation alone. It is the right to
receive a defined share of future production from a qualified, already
operating asset while shifting much of the mine's labor, cost inflation,
permitting, and sustaining-capital burden to the operator.

This is consistent with the existing commodity-chain and capital-intensity
research, but this pilot does not treat an industry force as proof of the
transaction's return.

## 4. Operating model

The basic operating model is:

```text
Antamina mine production
  -> payable production percentage
  -> payable factor
  -> delivered and sold ounces
  -> metal price received
  -> 20% stream payment
  -> Wheaton revenue
  -> corporate costs, depletion, tax, financing, and owner residual
```

The stream is economically different from a mining company. Wheaton's direct
cash cost is governed by the contract payment formula, while the mine operator
must carry labor, energy, equipment, sustaining capital, environmental,
permitting, and operating execution risk. That burden transfer can improve
stream economics, but it does not eliminate mine-life, counterparty,
production, price, or delivery risk.

The critical denominator is not mine revenue alone. It is the cash returned to
the streaming owner after the payment formula, corporate tax, interest,
depletion interpretation, debt service, and the upfront PMPA investment.

## 5. Owner-cash bridge

The strongest current bridge is:

```text
H1 2026 Antamina revenue                    277.563M USD
- cost of sales excluding depletion          55.340M USD
= cash-margin / OCF proxy                   222.223M USD
- Antamina-specific tax                      not allocated
- Antamina-specific interest                 not allocated
- PMPA funding debt service                  not allocated
- sustaining or corporate burden             not allocated
= after-tax common-owner residual             not proven
```

The accounting bridge adds:

```text
cash-margin proxy 222.223M
- depletion 51.322M
= profit proxy 170.901M
```

These are useful operating measures, but neither is a completed owner-cash
calculation. The [financed-return frontier](capital-flow-wheaton-antamina-financed-return-frontier-2026-09-15.md)
shows how the company-level current-SOFR interest range would mechanically
reduce the annualized proxy before tax, without assigning that burden to the
PMPA. The first-half OCF/upfront-payment screen is approximately
`5.168%`, or `10.336%` when mechanically annualized. That annualization is a
screen only; it is not an IRR, NPV, payback period, or reserve-life-adjusted
return.

## 6. Damodaran valuation questions

The valuation layer must answer:

1. What is the correct cash-flow denominator: stream OCF, after-tax cash flow,
   distributable cash, or common-owner residual?
2. How many payable ounces remain under the BHP contract and the combined
   Antamina stream?
3. What production, delivery, price, and payment assumptions support the
   current value?
4. How much reinvestment is required at Wheaton and how much sustaining burden
   remains with the operator?
5. What discount rate reflects metal-price volatility, mine execution,
   counterparty, jurisdiction, contract, financing, and duration risk?
6. Does the `4.300B USD` upfront payment earn an adequate return after tax and
   financing, or does the current price rely on peak metal prices or a long
   delivery life?
7. What is the value of the BHP PMPA separately from the legacy Glencore
   stream?

The current evidence supports a valuation workbench, not a finished valuation.
The missing delivery curve and BHP-only cash separation prevent a defensible
IRR/NPV conclusion.

## 7. Lyn Alden macro and liquidity tests

The macro/liquidity layer should test:

- silver and precious-metals price sensitivity;
- inflation in mine labor, energy, equipment, and sustaining capital;
- interest cost and refinancing conditions on Wheaton's debt;
- availability of bank and capital-market funding for large PMPA purchases;
- counterparty and jurisdiction risk at the mine;
- currency effects on mine costs and Wheaton receipts;
- whether higher metal prices improve cash returns or merely increase the
  upfront price paid for streaming assets; and
- whether an accounting asset and reported profit remain durable if delivery,
  price, or funding conditions weaken.

The current local evidence shows the funding channel and operating cash proxy,
but not a scenario table that separates metal price, volume, delivery timing,
interest, tax, and reserve-life effects. That is the next macro/valuation
workbench.

## 8. Capital-flow proof level

The case is currently classified as:

`strongest-named-asset-return-proxy`

It passes these gates:

1. named capital deployment;
2. named contractual destination;
3. visible output and combined-stream units received/sold;
4. stream revenue and OCF proxy;
5. company funding and lender-channel context; and
6. a usable upfront-payment and operating-cash denominator.

It fails or remains on hold at these gates:

1. BHP PMPA-only delivered ounces;
2. invoice or settlement cash by delivery;
3. Antamina-specific tax allocation;
4. Antamina-specific interest and debt-service allocation;
5. lender repayment or restricted-account waterfall;
6. reserve-backed delivery curve; and
7. source-backed IRR, NPV, or after-tax financed return.

The upfront cash loop itself is no longer on hold: Wheaton's payment and BHP's
reported proceeds now agree at approximately `4.300B USD`. The remaining hold
is the downstream delivery-to-owner-cash loop.

The first illustrative screen is deliberately conservative about attribution:
it models only the incremental BHP stream and does not assign legacy Glencore
cash flow to the BHP purchase. Under the documented bear/base/bull assumptions,
the model produces negative NPV in all three cases; the bull case reaches only
a `4.2%` unlevered IRR screen before a source-backed delivery tail, taxes, and
debt-service allocation. This is an expectation-burden result, not a final
investment conclusion.

## 9. Thesis and falsifier

### Current qualified thesis

Wheaton's Antamina PMPA gives the company a contractual claim on a large,
operating silver stream while leaving much of the mine's direct operating and
sustaining-capital burden with the mine operator. The public filings show a
substantial named investment, post-close production, cumulative combined-stream
ounces received and sold, stream revenue, and a high pre-tax/pre-interest cash
proxy. The economics appear promising enough to justify deeper return work, but
the available evidence does not yet prove the BHP-only cash loop or the
after-tax, debt-adjusted return.

### Filing-based thesis breakers

The thesis should weaken if future evidence shows any of the following:

- BHP PMPA deliveries are materially below the assumed schedule;
- PBND remains elevated or delivered ounces fail to convert into cash;
- the BHP stream cannot be separated from the legacy Glencore stream;
- Antamina production, payable share, or reserve life is reduced;
- realized prices or the payment formula produce materially lower cash than
  the current proxy;
- taxes, interest, fees, or debt service consume a large share of the stream
  cash margin;
- Wheaton must provide unexpected additional capital or support; or
- the PMPA's after-tax, financed IRR/NPV falls below the required return.

## 10. Decisive next evidence

The next source acquisition should be narrow and document-led:

1. BHP PMPA-only delivered and sold-ounce schedule after April `1, 2026`.
2. Reconciliation separating the BHP PMPA from the legacy Glencore Antamina
   stream.
3. Invoice, settlement, or cash-receipt support for delivered silver.
4. PBND rollforward from production to delivery to sale.
5. Realized price and `20%` production-payment reconciliation.
6. PMPA closing funds-flow or settlement statement.
7. Term-loan agreement, borrowing notices, interest schedule, lender
   allocation, and repayment waterfall.
8. Antamina reserve report, mine plan, and delivery curve.
9. Tax and finance-cost allocation sufficient to build an after-tax return
   model.

Until those documents are obtained and reconciled, the safe statement remains:

> Wheaton–Antamina is a strong named-asset cash-return proxy with visible
> investment, contractual entitlement, output, combined received/sold ounces,
> stream economics, and funding context. It is not yet full named-cash proof.
