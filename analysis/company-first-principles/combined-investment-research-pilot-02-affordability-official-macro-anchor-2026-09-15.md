# Pilot 02 affordability official macro anchor

Research date: `2026-09-15`

The affordability/substitution force now has an official current-regime anchor
alongside the social-research signals. The evidence is intentionally mixed:
household cost pressure is visible, while nominal retail demand remains
resilient. That combination is consistent with substitution and value-seeking
as a hypothesis, but it does not prove the behavior or identify a winning
retailer.

## Current observations

| Indicator | Latest official observation | Safe interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPI, August 2026 | All items `+3.4%` year over year; food at home `+2.2%`; energy `+16.3%`; shelter `+3.0%` | Household and operating-cost pressure remains material |
| Real average hourly earnings, August 2026 | `-0.3%` year over year | Purchasing-power pressure is visible in the labor-income denominator |
| Advance retail and food-services sales, July 2026 | `$763.6B`, `+5.0%` year over year and `-0.6%` month over month | Nominal retail spending remained resilient, but the series is not adjusted for price changes |

The combined read is:

```text
cost pressure + weaker real hourly earnings
  -> plausible substitution / trade-down incentive
nominal retail sales resilience
  -> consumption has not simply collapsed
price-unadjusted sales
  -> cannot identify real volume, mix, traffic, or retailer cash quality
```

This strengthens the dated force observation but does not upgrade the social
package to population-level causal proof. The company test remains whether
TJX, Target, or Walmart shows traffic, units, mix, margin, inventory, and cash
conversion consistent with the mechanism after normalization.

## Falsifiable follow-through

The next company filings should test whether the pressure/resilience pattern
appears as:

1. transaction or traffic resilience after price adjustment;
2. lower-ticket or private-label mix changes;
3. gross-margin and markdown behavior;
4. inventory and payable timing; and
5. owner cash after leases, capex, taxes, and temporary support.

If nominal sales rise only through price, while traffic, units, margins, and
normalized owner cash weaken, the affordability-to-retail thesis is weakened.
