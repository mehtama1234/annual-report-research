# Integrated Pilot 02: Affordability, Substitution, and Retail Value

Date: 2026-09-15  
Status: `qualified multi-source pilot — directional social/business signal, filing-backed company comparison`

This is the second execution of the [combined investment research goal](combined-investment-research-meaty-end-to-end-goal.md).

The structured evidence ledger is [pilot-02-affordability-value.csv](data/combined-investment-research-pilot-02-affordability-value.csv).

## The question

The social-research system identifies a possible change in household behavior:

> Households are not simply stopping consumption. They are trading down,
> substituting, searching harder, and preserving useful or identity-linked
> consumption through cheaper equivalents.

The end-to-end investment question is narrower:

> Which retail model captures that behavior, and can it convert value-seeking
> traffic into durable owner cash after inventory, markdowns, leases, labor,
> fulfillment, shrink, and store reinvestment?

The current answer is qualified:

> The social package provides usable draft context, the Inc. 5000 bridge shows
> broad business formation around affordability and substitution, and the
> annual-report evidence supports a real comparison between TJX, Target, and
> Walmart. None of those layers alone proves consumer prevalence, causality,
> or investment attractiveness.

## 1. Social signal

The social-research layer classifies the theme as **Affordability,
Substitution, And Constrained Choice**.

Current local evidence says:

- `33` social signals are assigned to the theme;
- the deep synthesis describes trading down, substitution, access hacks, and
  preservation of dignity or identity through cheaper equivalents;
- budget food, grocery pressure, cheap meals, affordable beauty, and repair-
  instead-of-replace appear as subthemes; and
- the social package grades the theme as usable draft evidence, not as a
  population estimate or causal finding.

The appropriate interpretation is a research lead, not a market-size claim.
The social evidence suggests questions about value perception, substitution,
and shopping effort. It does not tell us which retailer wins, whether the
behavior persists, or whether the retailer earns adequate returns.

The [independent affordability validation](combined-investment-research-pilot-02-independent-affordability-validation-2026-09-15.md)
adds official Fed and BLS corroboration: household price responses include
switching to cheaper products and using less, while expenditure data show that
food-at-home spending and category mix can move in different directions. This
strengthens the force observation without proving retailer attribution,
causality, or owner cash.

The [official current-regime macro anchor](combined-investment-research-pilot-02-affordability-official-macro-anchor-2026-09-15.md)
adds a mixed observation: August 2026 CPI shows food-at-home inflation of
`2.2%`, energy inflation of `16.3%`, and shelter inflation of `3.0%` year over
year, while real average hourly earnings declined `0.3%` year over year and
July nominal retail sales rose `5.0%` year over year. This supports a live
affordability-pressure hypothesis with resilient nominal consumption; it does
not prove real volume, causality, or retailer owner cash.

## 2. Inc. 5000 discovery layer

The cross-repository bridge maps the same theme to Inc. 5000 business data:

- `1,553` Inc. 5000 subpattern assignments;
- `1,777` unique 2026 companies in the mapped roster; and
- `$87.3B` of summed revenue across the mapped subpattern records.

This is business-formation and business-scale context. The counts are not
unique-company revenue totals, consumer prevalence, or proof that affordability
caused the growth.

The discovery implication is that constrained-choice behavior may create
opportunity across discount retail, lower-cost substitutes, repair, value
food, private brands, embedded financing, and logistics. The annual-report
layer must determine which model has actual control, pricing, inventory, and
cash advantages.

## 3. IBIS industry handoff

The [IBIS industry-to-filing handoff](combined-investment-research-pilot-02-ibis-industry-handoff.md)
maps three industry forces into the cohort's filing tests: the `hollow middle`
frames the premium/value split, the `margin vise` frames spread and burden
pressure, and the `channel shift` frames stores, digital, membership, and
attached services. IBIS supplies the industry hypothesis and adjacency
context; it does not prove consumer prevalence, company causality, or cash.

This is the bridge from broad industry framing to the primary-source cohort.

## 3. Public-company cohort

The filing-backed cohort is:

| Company | Control point being tested | Main burden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TJX | Flexible buying, branded closeouts, treasure-hunt traffic, and store density | Inventory selection, markdowns, shrink, stores, distribution, leases, labor |
| Target | Broad planned assortment, owned brands, stores, digital access, and attached services | Merchandise demand, vendor income, tariffs, inventory, fulfillment, stores, labor |
| Walmart | Broad availability, low-price perception, store coverage, pickup/delivery, membership, marketplace, advertising, and financial services | Thin retail spread, fulfillment, automation, inventory, stores, labor, heavy capital needs |

The existing annual-report comparison asks the correct operating question:

> Can the company repeatedly source or offer value, convert that value into
> traffic and gross margin, and fund the physical and digital system required
> to deliver it?

## 4. Control point and burden map

The cohort should not be treated as three interchangeable “retail” stocks.

### TJX

TJX's control point is buying flexibility and the ability to turn changing
brand supply into a repeated value-discovery experience. Its advantage depends
on finding attractive merchandise and selling it quickly enough that the value
gap remains visible.

The burden sits in buying risk, inventory age, markdowns, shrink, stores,
distribution, leases, labor, and the capital required to expand the network.

### Target

Target's control point is the relationship between planned assortment, owned
brands, physical stores, digital fulfillment, and attached services. The value
proposition is more curated and planned than TJX's off-price discovery model.

The burden includes merchandise demand, promotional investment, vendor-income
collection, tariffs, inventory timing, fulfillment, store renewal, leases, and
labor. A temporary vendor or tariff benefit cannot be treated as durable value
creation.

### Walmart

Walmart's control point is broad low-price availability combined with scale,
stores, pickup, delivery, membership, advertising, marketplace, and financial
services. The company can spread fixed systems across a larger customer and
supplier network.

The burden is heavier in absolute terms: thin retail spreads, inventory,
fulfillment, automation, store and supply-chain investment, technology,
leases, labor, and the capital needed to maintain the network.

## 5. Operating model

The relevant revenue and cash chains differ:

```text
TJX:
vendor availability -> opportunistic buying -> value discovery -> traffic
  -> fast sell-through -> gross margin -> store/distribution reinvestment

Target:
planned assortment and owned brands -> traffic and basket -> merchandise
  margin + vendor programs + services -> inventory/fulfillment/store funding

Walmart:
scale purchasing + low prices + availability -> frequency and basket
  -> retail spread + ads + membership + marketplace + financial services
  -> large physical/digital system funding
```

The correct test is not simply sales growth. It is whether incremental sales
produce adequate returns after inventory, markdowns, fulfillment, lease,
maintenance-capital, and labor requirements.

## 6. Owner-cash bridge

The cohort requires a company-specific bridge:

```text
reported earnings or adjusted operating profit
  -> operating cash flow
  -> inventory and payables timing
  -> vendor-income and rebate timing
  -> fulfillment and technology spending
  -> store, distribution, and maintenance capital
  -> lease obligations and other fixed claims
  -> buybacks, dilution, and share-count change
  -> common-owner cash residual
```

The social signal does not answer this bridge. The Inc. 5000 data does not
answer it. The annual reports are the authoritative layer for the reported
figures, and the valuation layer must decide whether the cash is recurring or
temporarily helped by working capital, supplier terms, tariffs, or unusually
low markdowns.

## 7. Damodaran questions

For each company, valuation should ask:

1. Is growth coming from stores, same-store traffic, ticket, digital,
   membership, advertising, marketplace, or acquisitions?
2. What reinvestment is required for that growth?
3. Does incremental store or distribution capital earn more than its cost?
4. Is gross-margin improvement structural or dependent on temporary buying,
   vendor, tariff, freight, or markdown conditions?
5. What future cash flows and margins are already implied by the stock price?
6. Should TJX, Target, and Walmart use the same terminal margin or multiple?
7. How should attached services be valued without hiding the burden of the
   retail system that supports them?

The comparison must preserve different operating models rather than collapse
the cohort into one retail multiple.

The current valuation workbench is [available as a structured CSV](data/combined-investment-research-pilot-02-retail-valuation-workbench.csv).
It deliberately contains two usable scenario-register rows and one explicit
missing row:

| Company | Current valuation screen | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TJX | No live valuation row yet | The dossier correctly withholds a price conclusion until its owner-cash bridge is complete. |
| Target | `$2.0B / $3.0B / $4.0B` normalized cash at `18x / 24x / 30x`, implying `$36B / $72B / $120B` versus approximately `$71.2B` market value | The base case is near the market and remains an expectations audit. |
| Walmart | `$13B / $18B / $24B` normalized cash at `30x / 38x / 45x`, implying `$390B / $684B / $1.080T` versus approximately `$854.8B` market value | The base case is below the market; the price requires substantial attached-service cash growth, a higher multiple, or both. |

These are illustrative scenario screens, not price targets. The denominators
are not interchangeable, and the gaps do not establish mispricing until the
filing bridges, maintenance-capital assumptions, share counts, and current
market inputs are refreshed.

## 8. Lyn Alden macro and liquidity tests

The macro layer should test:

- household income and affordability;
- food, fuel, housing, and wage inflation;
- consumer credit and payment stress;
- interest rates and lease or supply-chain financing costs;
- supplier health and inventory availability;
- tariffs and cross-border sourcing;
- labor availability and wage pressure;
- the resilience of value-seeking traffic in a downturn; and
- whether a consumer trade-down helps the retailer while weakening suppliers,
  employees, or franchise and vendor ecosystems.

The same macro force can help traffic and hurt margins. A low-price promise is
not automatically a liquidity advantage.

The [through-cycle retail lagged diagnostic](combined-investment-research-through-cycle-retail-lagged-diagnostic-2026-09-16.md)
now tests the stronger claim that prior real-wage conditions lead next-period
cash-after-property changes. Only 7 of 15 usable transitions move in the same
direction, versus 8 in the opposite direction. This is a useful falsifier of
the simple affordability-to-cash story, not causal proof: company actions,
capex timing, vendor terms, tariffs, promotions, and attached-service mix remain
uncontrolled.

## 9. Current conclusion and proof boundary

### Safe conclusion

Affordability and substitution are useful directional signals for selecting a
retail cohort. The public-company evidence supports a meaningful distinction:
TJX controls flexible buying and value discovery, Target operates a planned
assortment and attached-service system, and Walmart controls the broadest
low-price availability and fulfillment network. Each must prove that its
customer-value proposition survives inventory, margin, labor, capital, lease,
and cash-conversion burdens.

### Unsafe conclusions

Do not claim that:

- the social signals represent all households;
- Inc. 5000 company counts prove consumer adoption or market size;
- affordability caused the Inc. 5000 growth;
- a low-price retailer automatically gains pricing power;
- current traffic or sales growth proves healthy inventory and store returns;
- vendor income, tariffs, or working-capital benefits are permanent; or
- one retailer is more attractive without a denominator-specific valuation.

## 10. Thesis breakers

The cohort thesis should weaken if future filings show:

- traffic or transactions fall despite value messaging;
- inventory age, markdowns, shrink, or clearance rise materially;
- gross-margin gains depend on nonrecurring vendor, tariff, or freight effects;
- supplier terms deteriorate or vendor concentration becomes restrictive;
- fulfillment, labor, lease, or store capital consumes the apparent cash gain;
- attached services fail to improve customer economics or produce recurring
  cash;
- Walmart's scale requires increasingly heavy capital for declining returns;
- Target cannot repair merchandise productivity without repeated promotions; or
- TJX's buying flexibility fails to preserve the value gap and repeat traffic.
