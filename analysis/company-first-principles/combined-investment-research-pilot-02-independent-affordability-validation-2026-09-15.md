# Pilot 02 independent affordability validation — 2026-09-15

This memo adds official U.S. household and expenditure evidence beside the
social-research package. It strengthens the observation that price pressure
can change the composition and method of consumption; it does not prove that
the behavior caused any named retailer's sales, margin, or cash result.

## Independent observations

| Source | Dated observation | Safe interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Federal Reserve SHED, 2024 survey | `63%` of adults reported switching to cheaper products and `61%` used less of or stopped using products in response to higher prices; `79%` took at least one action | Direct survey corroboration of substitution and constrained-choice behavior |
| Federal Reserve SHED, 2024 survey | `60%` said changes in prices made their financial situation worse; the share was `68%` for family income of `$25,000–$49,999` and `53%` for `$100,000+` | Affordability pressure is uneven across household income; not a retailer demand estimate |
| BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey, 2024 | Food-at-home spending rose `2.8%`, while fruits and vegetables declined `3.5%` and cereals/bakery declined `6.1%` | Aggregate spending can rise while category mix shifts; consistent with substitution but not proof of trade-down to a particular retailer |
| BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey, 2024 | Food-away-from-home spending was nearly unchanged, increasing only `$12` | A bounded signal that nominal spending resilience does not necessarily mean broad volume growth |

## Handoff to the company test

```text
household price response and expenditure composition
  -> value / substitution hypothesis
  -> TJX, Target, Walmart control-point comparison
  -> traffic, mix, margin, inventory, vendor, capex, and cash tests
```

The survey measures reported household actions, and the BLS survey measures
reported expenditures. Neither identifies the winning retailer, proves that
lower prices caused the action, or reconciles retailer revenue into owner cash.
The annual-report cohort remains the authority for those company questions.

## Scope, parties, and alternative explanations

The observation is U.S.-wide and primarily covers calendar-year 2024 adults and
consumer units. It is used as a near-term discovery signal for the FY2025–FY2027
retail filing tests, not as a forecast of any one retailer's next quarter.
Consumers facing the pressure may benefit from lower-cost access or may bear
reduced quantity, quality, convenience, or choice; value retailers may gain
traffic while premium retailers, branded suppliers, labor, and landlords carry
different parts of the adjustment burden. The main alternative explanations
are category-specific price inflation, post-pandemic normalization, income
composition, product-mix changes, promotions, retailer assortment decisions,
and substitution among channels rather than toward TJX, Target, or Walmart.

The testable economic question is narrow: when U.S. affordability pressure is
visible, do the selected retailers show repeated company-specific traffic/mix
and cash-quality improvement after inventory, payables, temporary support,
capex, lease, tax, service, and dilution controls? The current answer is
qualified; the macro signal alone cannot establish that chain.

## Grade

`independent-affordability-corroboration`: CA-01 is better supported than by
the social package alone, but remains qualified because prevalence, causality,
retailer attribution, and durable owner-cash conversion are separate tests.
