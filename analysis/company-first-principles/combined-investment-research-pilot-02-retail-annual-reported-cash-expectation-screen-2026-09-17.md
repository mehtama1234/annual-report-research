# Pilot 02 annual reported-cash expectation screen

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This screen divides the current market capitalization snapshot by the latest
fiscal-year reported cash-after-property denominator. It makes the market
burden visible on one annual basis for TJX, Target, and Walmart. It is not a
normalized owner-cash multiple, intrinsic value, or buy/sell conclusion.

| Company | Fiscal period | Market capitalization ($M) | Reported cash after property ($M) | Market cap / reported cash after property |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| TJX | FY2026 | 137,212 | 4,917 | 27.906x |
| Target | FY2025 | 70,627 | 2,835 | 24.913x |
| Walmart | FY2026 | 857,635 | 14,923 | 57.471x |

## Interpretation boundary

The screen shows the price burden attached to each company's latest annual
reported cash-after-property result. Walmart's higher screen multiple requires
more durable cash conversion from scale, membership, advertising, fulfillment,
and platform investment. TJX and Target still require inventory, vendor-term,
maintenance-capital, lease, tax, attached-service, dilution, and seasonality
normalization before these ratios can be treated as owner-cash multiples.

Lease and tax cash remain context already embedded in operating cash flow;
supplier-finance balances are not subtracted again. The market caps are the
September 17 snapshot and the fiscal periods are not identical calendar years,
so this is an expectation screen rather than a cross-company ranking.
