# Pilot 02 Retail Attached-Services Cash Upgrade

Research date: `2026-09-15`

This upgrade executes queue item `Q-06` against the current Target and Walmart
Q2 filings. It improves the payer, accounting, and denominator map for
advertising, card, membership, and ecosystem revenue. It does not manufacture
a standalone service margin or add attached-service revenue one-for-one to
owner cash.

## Target: revenue and payer boundary

Target's H1 2026 Form 10-Q reports `$525M` of advertising revenue, `$269M` of
credit-card profit sharing, and `$347M` of other revenue against `$50.841B` of
merchandise sales and `$51.982B` of total net sales. The combined attached
revenue screen is `$1.141B / $51.982B = 2.19%`.

The filing gives two important attribution rules:

- Roundel advertising may be classified as Net Sales or as a reduction of
  Cost of Sales or SG&A, depending on the arrangement. Therefore the reported
  advertising number is not necessarily a clean incremental gross-profit line.
- TD Bank Group underwrites, funds, and owns the Target Circle credit-card
  receivables and controls risk management and regulatory compliance. Target
  performs account servicing and primary marketing and receives a percentage
  of profits. The credit-card amount is therefore a profit-sharing receipt
  with a named external payer and burden carrier, not Target-owned card-loan
  revenue.

Target's filing also reports `$4.5B` of six-month operating cash flow and
`$2.4B` of investing cash use, but those are consolidated company denominators.
They cannot be allocated to Roundel, card profit sharing, or other revenue
without cost, working-capital, tax, and capital-intensity data.

## Walmart: segment contribution boundary

Walmart's H1 FY2027 filing reports membership and other income of `$1.676B`
for Walmart U.S., `$851M` for Walmart International, and `$1.328B` for Sam's
Club U.S. The corresponding segment total revenues are `$244.034B`,
`$71.159B`, and `$50.446B`. The mechanical screens are `0.69%`, `1.20%`, and
`2.63%`, respectively, when measured against total segment revenue.

The consolidated filing reports `$3.904B` of membership and other income,
compared with the `$3.855B` sum of the three segment rows. The `$49M` gap is a
presentation/perimeter boundary, not an incremental service-cash amount; see
the [denominator reconciliation](combined-investment-research-pilot-02-walmart-attached-income-denominator-reconciliation-2026-09-16.md).

Walmart states that membership and other income includes membership fees and
other items such as rental and tenant income, recycling income, gift-card
breakage, and corporate-campus income. It also says eCommerce ecosystem net
sales can include certain advertising arrangements, fulfillment services, and
data insights. The filing therefore does not provide a pure advertising or
membership revenue denominator.

The segment table does provide total segment cost and operating income. For
Walmart U.S., H1 2026 total revenue was `$244.034B`, cost of sales `$172.991B`,
operating expenses `$57.026B`, and operating income `$14.017B`. That is a
complete segment operating bridge, but not an attached-services operating
bridge. Walmart explicitly says the individual products and services are
impractical to segregate and that cost mix differs by segment.

Management says growth in higher-margin businesses, including advertising,
benefited gross-profit rate. This is directional evidence of mix quality, not
a disclosed advertising margin, cash collection schedule, or service-level
return on capital.

## Latest operating signal: growth is visible, cash conversion is not

Walmart's Q2 FY2027 earnings release and presentation add a current operating
signal that is useful for the theme map but not for owner-cash conversion:
Walmart U.S. advertising increased `38%` in Q2, including `43%` growth in
Walmart Connect excluding VIZIO, while global membership-fee revenue grew
`17%`. The presentation also reports eCommerce growth and Marketplace growth,
but these are activity and revenue signals rather than service-level cash
receipts. The 10-Q's segment table remains the authoritative denominator for
cost and operating income.

This distinction matters because the growth percentages do not disclose the
advertising dollar amount, incremental service labor, fulfillment and
technology burden, working-capital timing, tax, capex, or the legal-entity
route by which cash would reach common owners. They therefore strengthen the
control-point and cultural-consumption thesis while leaving the Q-06 cash gate
open.

## Proof-grade result

| Gate | Result | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attached revenue visibility | Proven for Target and Walmart segments | Walmart combines membership with other income; Target combines other streams separately but not their costs |
| External payer / burden carrier | Target card route proven | TD owns, funds, underwrites, and controls card risk; Target's service burden and net share are not separately costed |
| Accounting classification | Target Roundel classification boundary proven | Classification can reduce cost of sales or SG&A, so revenue is not a standalone margin proxy |
| Segment operating denominator | Walmart segment bridge proven | Segment costs and operating income include merchandise and attached services together |
| Cash conversion | Still not separately proven | Consolidated cash flow does not allocate cash collection, tax, working capital, capex, or overhead to services |
| Owner-cash addition | Not permitted | The normalized cash screen continues to exclude standalone attached-service additions |

## Updated safe claim

`Target and Walmart filings prove that advertising, card profit sharing,
membership, fulfillment, data, and other ecosystem services sit inside the
retail control point. Target's filing further identifies TD as the owner,
funder, underwriter, and risk controller of Target Circle card receivables,
while Target earns a profit share for servicing and marketing. Walmart reports
segment-level revenue, costs, and operating income, and says advertising helps
gross-profit rate. The filings do not prove standalone service costs, taxes,
working capital, capex, cash collection, or common-owner cash. Attached-service
revenue remains excluded from normalized owner cash.`

## Next gate

The remaining Q-06 upgrade requires company disclosures that allocate service
labor, fulfillment, technology, overhead, tax, capital spending, and collection
cash—or a defensible external payer settlement schedule. The current evidence
has moved the question from “is the service revenue real?” to “what exact
portion survives all burdens and reaches owners?”
