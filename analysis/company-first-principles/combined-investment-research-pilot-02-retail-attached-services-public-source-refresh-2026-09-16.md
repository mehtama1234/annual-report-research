# Retail attached-services public-source refresh

Research date: `2026-09-16`

This refresh rechecks current official disclosures for Q-06. It records
attached-service activity and accounting placement without treating revenue as
standalone owner cash.

| Company | Latest observation | What it proves | What remains missing | Result class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Target | Q2 2026 non-merchandise sales grew more than 20%; Roundel advertising, Target Circle 360, and Target+ are named; Roundel may be classified in net sales or as a reduction of cost of sales/SG&A | Service activity and accounting placement are visible | Allocated service costs, working capital, capex, tax, collection, and owner-cash conversion | `evidence-insufficient` |
| Walmart | H1 FY2027 membership and other income was `$3.904B` globally; advertising is described as a higher-margin business and may be recorded in net sales or as a reduction of cost of sales | Membership and advertising are material ecosystem layers | Standalone costs, capital burden, tax, collection, and common-owner cash | `evidence-insufficient` |
| TJX | H1 FY2027 cash flow includes the `$419M` non-recurring credit-card interchange-fee settlement received during the quarter ended May 2, 2026; it is separate from recurring retail operations | A dated exceptional ancillary cash receipt can be identified | Recurring service-level economics and allocated costs | `evidence-insufficient` |

Walmart's current Q2 10-Q further states that Sam's Club membership fees are
deferred and recognized ratably over the one-year membership term. This is a
revenue-timing control, not a disclosed membership-only contract-liability
balance or service-level cash-collection schedule; the `$3.904B` consolidated
membership-and-other-income figure therefore remains a reported revenue
surface rather than period cash.

TJX's current Q2 10-Q supplies a comparable customer-funding control: deferred
gift-card revenue ended at `$825M` on August 1, 2026, after `$895M` of deferred
revenue additions, `( $4M )` of exchange-rate effects, and `$963M` of H1
recognized revenue. TJX states that gift cards are held in one homogeneous
pool and are not separately identifiable, so this balance cannot be assigned
to a service or converted into recurring owner cash.

Target's June 2026 Roundel fact sheet provides a capability-burden context:
the business is described as an 800-plus-person team integrated within Target,
working with more than 2,000 vendors, and generating more than `$2B` of value.
Because the fact sheet does not define that value as GAAP revenue, profit, or
cash, it is retained as a control-point and labor-burden observation rather
than added to the attached-service denominator.

## September 17 targeted recheck

A targeted SEC-source recheck of Target's Q2 2026 filing and earnings exhibit,
Walmart's Q2 FY2027 filing, and TJX's Q2 FY2027 filing returned the same Q-06
boundary. Target's `$525M` advertising, `$269M` card profit-sharing, and
`$347M` other-revenue observations remain visible, as do Walmart's segment and
consolidated membership-and-other-income amounts; the filings still do not join
service-level collection to labor, fulfillment, technology, capital, tax, or
legal-entity cash. TJX's gift-card and interchange observations remain timing
and support controls rather than attached-service owner cash. Q-06 therefore
remains `evidence-insufficient` for standalone service cash conversion.

The September 17 annual-source recheck adds Target's FY2025 accounting
perimeter: `$915M` advertising revenue, `$522M` credit-card profit sharing, and
`$626M` other revenue. The filing states that Roundel can be recorded in net
sales or as a reduction of cost of sales/SG&A; TD underwrites, funds, and owns
the credit-card receivables while Target performs servicing and primary
marketing; and other revenue includes Target Plus commissions, Shipt,
membership, and related items. This sharpens payer, legal-owner, and
classification controls, but still does not allocate service costs, collection,
tax, or cash to normalized owner cash.

Walmart's FY2026 annual filing adds a comparable segment perimeter: global
membership-and-other income was `$6.750B`, including Walmart U.S. `$2.624B`,
Walmart International `$1.565B`, Sam's Club U.S. `$2.525B`, and corporate/support
`$36M`. Membership fees alone were `$4.4B`; the remainder includes rental and
tenant income, recycling, and gift-card breakage. The filing provides segment
operating expense totals but does not allocate advertising, fulfillment,
membership, or other-service costs to the income lines, so the cash conversion
gate remains open.

## Promotion consequence

The refresh supports a control-point thesis around customer ecosystems, but it
does not justify adding service revenue to merchandise cash or applying a
merchandise margin. The next promotion-ready object remains a period-matched
service profit-and-cash schedule with allocated costs, capex, taxes, working
capital, and collection evidence.

The [Target attached-service public-search boundary](combined-investment-research-pilot-02-target-attached-service-public-search-boundary-2026-09-16.md)
now preserves the exact Q2/H1 revenue and aggregate-cost fields searched in the
latest 10-Q. It classifies the result as a searched-negative service-level
allocation, not as evidence that the underlying private allocation records do
not exist.
