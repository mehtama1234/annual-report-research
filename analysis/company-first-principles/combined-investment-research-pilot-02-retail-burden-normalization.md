# Pilot 02 retail burden normalization

Research date: `2026-09-15`

This is a burden map, not a claimed normalized owner-cash number. It joins the
reported retail cash bridge to lease obligations, dilution, capital-return
signals, and disclosed capex categories. “Maintenance” is only a filing label
where the company supplies one; renovations and remodels can contain both
maintenance and growth.

## What this changes

The cohort cannot be ranked by reported free cash flow alone. Target's Q2
2026 cash-flow statement adds `$154M` of share-based compensation expense and
the financing section shows `$92M` of shares withheld for related taxes; these
are now visible in the Target burden row and conservative cash screen, but the
screen still does not claim a final owner-cash number. TJX has the
largest disclosed lease cash payment relative to its reported cash-after-
property bridge, Target’s lease-adjusted ROIC is contaminated by the tariff
refund unless normalized, and Walmart’s `$14.181B` H1 capital spending is
explicitly tied to omnichannel and platform expansion. Repurchases and diluted
shares also show why EPS is not equivalent to per-share operating cash.

No arithmetic subtraction is promoted as owner cash here. Lease payments are
already embedded in operating cash flow, and capex categories do not cleanly
identify maintenance. The next defensible step is to obtain each company’s
annual capex narrative, stock-compensation note, lease cash schedule, and
maintenance/growth split where disclosed, then construct a sensitivity range.
