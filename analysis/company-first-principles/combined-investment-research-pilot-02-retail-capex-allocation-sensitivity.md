# Pilot 02 retail capex-allocation sensitivity

Research date: `2026-09-15`

This sensitivity converts the disclosed retail operating-cash and property-
spending rows into a transparent maintenance-capital boundary. It does not
claim that any company has disclosed the maintenance share. Instead, it shows
the pre-working-capital, pre-lease, pre-tax, and pre-dilution cash range under
three explicit allocation cases:

```text
cash after an assumed maintenance allocation
  = reported operating cash flow
  - total property spending × assumed maintenance share
```

The `100%` case is the existing OCF-less-property bridge. The `0%` case is an
upper-bound screen that treats all property spending as growth. The `50%` case
is a midpoint sensitivity only, not a company estimate.

| Company / period | Operating cash flow | Total property spending | 0% maintenance | 50% maintenance | 100% maintenance |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| TJX H1 FY2027 | `$3.345B` | `$1.159B` | `$3.345B` | `$2.766B` | `$2.186B` |
| Target H1 2026 | `$4.519B` | `$2.404B` | `$4.519B` | `$3.317B` | `$2.115B` |
| Walmart H1 FY2027 | `$19.710B` | `$14.181B` | `$19.710B` | `$12.620B` | `$5.529B` |

These are capex-allocation screens, not owner-cash conclusions. Inventory and
payables, supplier finance, leases, taxes, share compensation, buybacks,
attached services, and legal obligations remain outside the calculation. The
screen therefore narrows the capex uncertainty without pretending to resolve
the full cash denominator.

## Latest category-boundary overlay

Walmart's H1 FY2027 filing identifies `$1.087B` of new-store and new-club
spending inside the `$14.181B` total. Under the narrow classification that this
is a growth-capital floor and that every remaining dollar is maintenance, the
implied maintenance ceiling is `$13.094B` and operating cash less that implied
maintenance is `$6.616B`. This is a source-bounded classification screen, not
reported owner cash: the category may contain replacement work, and the
remaining categories are not disclosed as maintenance.

TJX's FY2026 annual filing separately identifies `$185M` of new stores,
`$921M` of renovations and improvements, and `$851M` of offices/distribution
centers including IT, but those annual categories do not allocate the current
H1 period's maintenance share. Target's public materials identify remodels,
new stores, supply chain, and technology as investment priorities without a
joinable dollar split. Neither company therefore receives the same growth-floor
overlay in the current-period table.
