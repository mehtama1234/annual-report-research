# Pilot 02 retail capex classification boundary

Research date: `2026-09-17`

This artifact closes a common modeling error: treating all reported property
spending as maintenance or treating every remodel and technology investment as
growth. The latest filings identify uses unevenly, so the cohort table records
what is proven and what remains unallocated.

| Company / period | Total capex | Filing-backed category evidence | Current grade |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| TJX H1 FY2027 | `$1.159B` | Store improvements and renovations; new stores; distribution centers and offices including information technology | H1 categories visible; amounts not split; FY2027 forward categories now quantified separately |
| Target H1 2026 | `$2.404B` | Q2 disclosure says the increase was driven primarily by store remodels and new stores; H1 category amounts are not disclosed | Direction visible; amount split unresolved |
| Walmart H1 FY2027 | `$14.181B` | Q2 filing reports `$7.659B` supply chain/customer-facing/technology/other, `$3.623B` remodels, `$1.087B` new stores/clubs, and `$1.812B` international | H1 categories visible; maintenance share remains unresolved |

## Annual category reference proxy

The annual filings provide a useful category reference without proving that the
same mix applies to the H1 periods above:

| Company / annual period | Reported category amounts | Mechanical share of annual total | Safe use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TJX FY2026 | `$921M` renovations/improvements; `$185M` new stores; `$851M` offices/distribution | `47.1%`; `9.5%`; `43.5%` of `$1.957B` | Reference mix only; renovations may contain maintenance and growth |
| Walmart FY2026 | `$16.468B` supply chain/customer-facing/technology/other; `$5.571B` remodels; `$1.406B` new stores/clubs; `$3.197B` international | `61.8%`; `20.9%`; `5.3%`; `12.0%` of `$26.642B` | Reference mix only; automation, fulfillment, and international spend are not pure growth |
| Target FY2025 | Approximately `$1.3B` existing-store investments; `$1.1B` new stores; `$0.7B` supply chain; `$0.6B` information technology and other | Approximately `35.1%`; `29.7%`; `18.9%`; `16.2%` of `$3.7B` | Reference mix only; existing-store investment is not automatically maintenance |

These percentages are arithmetic descriptions of reported categories, not
maintenance estimates and not inputs to the owner-cash valuation. The Walmart
H1 and FY2026 periods are kept separate so the annual category disclosure is
not silently substituted for the H1 allocation.

## TJX maintenance-expense boundary

The TJX FY2026 Form 10-K states that maintenance and repairs are charged to
expense as incurred, while capital expenditures are reported separately in the
new-store, store-renovation/improvement, and office/distribution-center
categories. This means the retail cash screen must not subtract an additional
generic maintenance allowance for ordinary repairs that are already included
in operating expenses and operating cash flow. It does not establish that
renovations or improvements are all growth capital: those categories can still
contain replacement, productivity, and expansion spending. The H1 FY2027
maintenance-versus-growth allocation therefore remains unresolved.

## Target H1 disclosure boundary checked

The Target Q2 2026 Form 10-Q was re-checked on `2026-09-15` for a quantified
H1 capital-spending schedule. The filing reports cash used in investing of
approximately `$2.4B` for the six months ended August 1, 2026 and attributes
the increase versus the prior year to higher capital expenditures. It also
mentions new-store and remodel-related expense in SG&A and identifies store
remodels and new stores as the primary drivers of the capital-spending
increase, but it does not provide H1 dollar amounts for remodels, new stores,
supply chain, or information technology and other.

Therefore the FY2025 annual category amounts remain a reference proxy only.
They cannot be prorated or carried into H1 2026 without an explicit seasonality
and project-mix assumption. Target's `$2.404B` H1 property-spending amount
must remain in the unresolved maintenance/growth range in the owner-cash
screen.

Target's FY2025 Form 10-K also states that repair and maintenance costs are
expensed as incurred. It reports FY2025 operating cash flow of `$6.562B` and
property-and-equipment spending of `$3.727B`, a mechanical reported
cash-after-property screen of `$2.835B`. That prevents a second generic
maintenance deduction from the operating-cash bridge for ordinary repairs, but
it does not identify the maintenance content of capitalized remodels, store
improvements, supply chain, or technology spending. The H1 2026
maintenance/growth allocation therefore remains unresolved.

The FY2025 Form 10-K was also checked directly for a replacement for the older
annual category reference. Its MD&A discusses the store network and the
strategic importance of remodels and new stores, but the filing's reported
property-spending total and cash-flow tables do not provide a dollar schedule
that separates existing-store investment, new stores, supply chain, and
technology/other. Accordingly, the approximately `$1.3B`/`$1.1B`/`$0.7B`/`$0.6B`
annual category reference remains a historical mix proxy, not a FY2025 filing-
verified maintenance schedule or an input to normalized owner cash.

Primary source: [Target FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27419/000002741926000018/a2025_annualreportxtgt.pdf).

The [Target 2026 capital-plan boundary](combined-investment-research-pilot-02-target-2026-capital-plan-boundary-2026-09-15.md)
adds official forward context: approximately `$5B` of 2026 investment,
more than `30` new stores, more than `130` remodels, supply-chain and
technology projects, and more than `$1B` of food-and-beverage investment.
These disclosures make strategic uses visible but still do not allocate H1
dollars between maintenance and growth.

## Latest forward-spending refinement

The current TJX Q2 FY2027 filing adds forward context without closing the H1
classification gap. TJX says H1 capital spending primarily reflected store
improvements and renovations, new stores, and distribution centers and offices,
including information technology, and expects approximately `$2.2B–$2.3B` of
capital spending for the full fiscal year. The FY2026 10-K further quantifies
that FY2027 guide as approximately `$1.000B` renovations, `$992M` offices and
distribution centers including IT, and `$222M` new stores; the `$2.214B` named
sum reconciles to the guide. This makes the planned burden more visible, but
the filing still does not quantify the maintenance component or assign dollars
to each H1 category.

Walmart's Q2 FY2027 filing remains the strongest current cohort disclosure: it
reports `$7.659B` of supply-chain/customer-facing/technology/other, `$3.623B`
of remodels, `$1.087B` of new stores and clubs, and `$1.812B` of international
spending for the six months. The separately identified new-store category can
support a labeled growth-capital floor, but the other categories still mix
maintenance, productivity, fulfillment, and growth.

The FY2026 Walmart 10-K adds an accounting boundary: normal repairs and
maintenance are expensed as incurred, while major improvements are capitalized.
This means a generic maintenance deduction must not be subtracted again from
operating cash for ordinary repairs. It does not classify the capitalized
remodel, supply-chain, technology, fulfillment, or international categories;
the maintenance content of those capitalized investments remains unobserved.

Primary source: [Walmart FY2026 Form 10-K](https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-26-000055/wmt-20260131.htm).

Target's Q2 FY2026 filing reports approximately `$2.4B` of H1 investing cash
use driven by higher capital expenditures and identifies remodels and new
stores as the primary drivers of the increase, but still supplies no H1 dollar
allocation. The annual approximately `$5B` 2026 capital-spending outlook is
planning context, not a maintenance schedule.

## Q-05 promotion surface

The current evidence can be stated in one comparable control table:

| Company / period | Total period capex | Identified growth-capital floor | Implied mixed/maintenance remainder | Can it enter normalized owner cash? |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| TJX H1 FY2027 | `$1.159B` | None for the H1 period; FY2027 forward guide separately names `$222M` of new stores | H1 allocation not available | No; forward categories cannot be substituted for H1 cash |
| Target H1 2026 | `$2.404B` | None in dollars; more than 30 planned stores and more than 130 remodels are directional only | H1 allocation not available | No; annual plan cannot be prorated without project and seasonality evidence |
| Walmart H1 FY2027 | `$14.181B` | `$1.087B` new stores and clubs under a narrow classification | `$13.094B` remainder treated as an implied maintenance ceiling for a sensitivity only | No; the remainder mixes automation, fulfillment, technology, remodels, and international spend |

For Walmart only, the narrow overlay yields `$6.616B` of OCF less implied
maintenance (`$19.710B - $13.094B`). This is a classification sensitivity,
not reported owner cash. The table makes the promotion rule operational:
identified growth categories may narrow a range, but they do not make the
remaining capex maintenance, and no company has supplied the full H1
maintenance schedule required for normalized owner cash.

The cohort conclusion is therefore upgraded only to `forward-spending-context`
for TJX and Target: the expected burden and direction of use are clearer, while
the maintenance-versus-growth denominator remains unresolved for all three.

## Cohort growth-floor control — 2026-09-17

The comparable growth-floor field is now stated explicitly:

| Company / period | Identifiable growth-capital floor | What it does not mean |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| TJX H1 FY2027 | No H1 dollar floor; the forward FY2027 guide identifies `$222M` of new stores | The forward amount cannot be substituted for H1 cash or treated as the H1 floor |
| Target H1 2026 | No disclosed H1 dollar floor | No disclosed floor is not zero growth spending; the filing names remodels and new stores without dollar allocation |
| Walmart H1 FY2027 | `$1.087B` new stores and clubs | The category may still include non-expansion work, and the `$13.094B` remainder is not maintenance |

This control prevents a silent zero-growth assumption for TJX or Target and a
silent maintenance classification for Walmart. It narrows the range only
where the filing supplies a labeled category; normalized owner cash remains
blocked by maintenance content, working capital, leases, taxes, dilution, and
attached-service allocation.

## Modeling rule

Until a company supplies a quantified maintenance schedule, the owner-cash
screen should show total property spending and a range or unresolved field. It
must not subtract only “maintenance” from OCF while silently treating stores,
automation, fulfillment, or technology as free growth.
