# Retail capex public-source refresh

Research date: `2026-09-16`

This refresh checks the latest official public routes for the Q-05
maintenance-versus-growth-capital test. It adds period and category evidence,
but does not infer maintenance from a remodel, technology, fulfillment, or
international label.

| Company | Latest observation | What it proves | What it does not prove | Result class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TJX | H1 FY2027 capex was `$1.159B`: Marmaxx `$690M`, HomeGoods `$146M`, TJX Canada `$190M`, and TJX International `$133M`; spending primarily reflected store improvements/renovations, new stores, and distribution centers/offices including IT; FY2027 guide is `$2.2B–$2.3B` | Exact H1 segment allocation, category direction, and forward burden are disclosed | Dollar allocation between replacement/maintenance and growth within each segment | `evidence-insufficient` |
| Target | Q2 2026 capex was `$1.4B`, up 27%, driven primarily by store remodels and new stores; H1 10-Q reports `$2.404B` of property-and-equipment expenditures, `$7M` of other investing activity, and `$2.397B` of total investing cash use | The property-spending denominator, quarterly timing, stated drivers, and the mechanical H1 operating-cash-less-property screen (`$4.519B - $2.404B = $2.115B`) are visible | Q2 or H1 dollar split for maintenance, remodels, new stores, supply chain, or technology; total investing cash use is not interchangeable with property spending | `evidence-insufficient` |
| Walmart | H1 FY2027 categories are `$7.659B` supply chain/customer-facing/technology/other, `$3.623B` remodels, `$1.087B` new stores/clubs, and `$1.812B` international; the Q2 presentation also reports approximately `280` remodels YTD and four new stores/clubs in Q2 | Category amounts, project activity, and a narrow identified growth floor are visible | Activity counts do not allocate dollars or reveal the maintenance share inside the mixed categories | `evidence-insufficient` |

The same Walmart filing defines free cash flow as operating cash flow less
payments for property and equipment, then explicitly cautions that the measure
does not deduct debt service, other contractual obligations, or business
acquisition payments. This is a direct denominator boundary: the `$5.529B`
company-defined H1 free-cash-flow figure is not residual common-owner cash.

Target's official 2026 store-investment materials describe remodels and new
stores as part of a long-term growth investment program. This supports a
`strategic-growth-context` label for the category direction, but it still does
not allocate the `$2.404B` H1 property-spending denominator or establish what
portion is maintenance, replacement, or growth capital.

Target's H1 cash-flow presentation also requires a denominator distinction:
the 10-Q reports `$4.519B` of operating cash flow and `$2.404B` of property-and-
equipment expenditures, while total investing cash use is `$2.397B` after
`$7M` of other investing activity. The resulting `$2.115B` is therefore a
mechanical operating-cash-less-property screen, not Target-reported capex and
not normalized owner cash. It remains exposed to the unresolved maintenance-
versus-growth, lease, tax, working-capital, support, and service-cost tests.

For TJX, the same filing reports H1 operating cash flow of `$3.345B`; less the
exact `$1.159B` of capital expenditures produces a mechanical `$2.186B`
cash-after-property screen. This is a denominator control only: it is not
normalized owner cash because the maintenance/growth split, taxes, leases,
working capital, and other claims remain outside the screen.

The FY2025 annual filing was checked as a possible source of a more useful
historical category bridge. Its MD&A confirms the store-network and remodel/new-
store context, but the filing does not provide a dollar table separating
existing-store investment, new stores, supply chain, and technology/other.
The older approximate annual category reference is therefore retained only as
historical context; it is not upgraded to a FY2025 filing-backed maintenance or
growth allocation.

## Promotion consequence

The refresh improves the timing and category evidence for Q-05, but the
owner-cash denominator remains a range. Identified new-store spending may
narrow the growth floor; it cannot make the remainder maintenance. The next
promotion-ready source remains a company-disclosed maintenance/replacement
schedule or project-level capex ledger joined to the same reporting period.

## September 17, 2026 primary-source recheck

The targeted search found one useful historical category control in Walmart's
FY2026 annual report: U.S. supply chain/customer-facing/technology/other was
`$16.468B`, store and club remodels were `$5.571B`, new stores/clubs including
expansions and relocations were `$1.406B`, and Walmart International was
`$3.197B`. This improves category and perimeter control, but does not identify
the maintenance share inside remodels, technology, supply chain, or existing
store investment.

The same recheck found no company-disclosed maintenance/replacement schedule
for Target. Target's official plan instead describes approximately `$5B` of
2026 capital expenditures across store experience/remodels, supply chain and
technology, and new stores, with more than `$1B` of incremental operating
investment. That is useful growth-context evidence, not a period-matched
maintenance allocation.

TJX's FY2026 annual filing adds a historical category control: `$185M` for new
stores, `$921M` for store renovations and improvements, and `$851M` for offices
and distribution centers, including information technology. TJX separately
states that ordinary maintenance and repairs are expensed as incurred. The
capitalized maintenance share within renovations, distribution centers, and IT
therefore remains unobservable; the categories cannot be treated as all-growth
or all-maintenance.

Decision: the public-source capex search is now a move-on boundary. Q-05 stays
`evidence-insufficient` until a project ledger, maintenance schedule, or other
same-period allocation record appears; no residual capex is relabeled as
maintenance by subtraction.
