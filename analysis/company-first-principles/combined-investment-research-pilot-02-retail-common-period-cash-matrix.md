# Retail Common-Period Cash Matrix

Research date: `2026-09-15`

The three retail cases now have a common first-half bridge, although their
fiscal calendars and business definitions differ. The table reports net sales,
operating cash flow less property spending, cash-after-property as a percent of
sales, and a mechanical cash-after-property per diluted share. It also shows
the reported or calculated gross-margin basis so temporary support and
accounting-definition differences remain visible. A mechanical ex-support
margin screen removes the disclosed or source-bounded tariff benefit from gross
profit; it is a sensitivity, not a normalized margin conclusion. The matrix is
a denominator screen, not normalized owner cash.

| Company | Period | Net sales | Operating cash flow | Property spending | Cash after property | Cash after property / sales | Gross margin | Ex-support gross margin | Diluted shares | Mechanical cash/share | Key adjustment boundary |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| TJX | H1 FY2027 | `$29.503B` | `$3.345B` | `$1.159B` | `$2.186B` | `7.409%` | `32.376%` calculated from net sales less cost of sales including buying/occupancy | `31.254%` after a `$331M` tariff-refund candidate | `1,118M` | `$1.955` | Interchange settlement, maintenance/growth capex, leases, inventory, tax, and the tariff-support classification boundary |
| Target | H1 2026 | `$51.982B` | `$4.519B` | `$2.404B` | `$2.115B` | `4.069%` | `31.4%` reported, including `$994M` tariff-refund benefit | `29.479%` after removing the disclosed refund | `456.2M` | `$4.636` | Vendor/payable timing, supplier finance, stock compensation, capex, and seasonality |
| Walmart | H1 FY2027 | `$361.784B` | `$19.710B` | `$14.181B` | `$5.529B` | `1.528%` | `24.9%` reported, with tariff-refund benefit | `24.054%` after removing approximately `$2.9B` of Q2 refunds | `7,989M` | `$0.692` | Inventory/payable timing, fulfillment, leases, SBC, maintenance capex, and refund allocation |

The figures are deliberately not ranked. The periods are not identical, and
cash after property spending still contains timing, service-mix, reinvestment,
lease, tax, and dilution effects. The percentage-of-sales field makes cash
intensity visible but does not solve those burdens. The next upgrade is a
common full-year or trailing-period normalization with disclosed maintenance
versus growth capital and recurring versus temporary working-capital effects.
