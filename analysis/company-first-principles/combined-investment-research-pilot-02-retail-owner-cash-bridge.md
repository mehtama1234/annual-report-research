# Pilot 02 retail owner-cash bridge

Research date: `2026-09-16`

This bridge makes the retail cohort comparable at the first defensible level:
reported operating cash flow less property spending. It is not yet normalized
owner cash. Inventory timing, vendor terms, leases, maintenance versus growth
capital, taxes, dilution, attached-service costs, and one-time benefits remain
separate upgrade items.

## Interpretation

Target’s H1 2026 cash improvement is not clean owner-cash evidence: inventory
was `$13.2B`, accounts payable supplied `$612M`, and `$3.2B` of vendor
obligations were eligible for supplier-finance programs. The quarter also
included a `$994M` tariff refund that increased reported earnings and gross
margin. Walmart’s H1 FY2027 bridge shows `$2.660B` of inventory cash use and
`$1.648B` of accounts-payable support while capital spending rose to `$14.181B`.
Its company-defined free cash flow excludes debt service, contractual
obligations, and acquisitions.

Target's FY2025 annual bridge is now cross-checked to the current 10-K: the
`$2.835B` cash-after-property figure is a reported period screen, and the filing
states that repair and maintenance costs are expensed as incurred. That policy
prevents a second generic maintenance deduction for ordinary repairs, but the
filing does not allocate the `$3.727B` property-spending total between
replacement, remodel, supply-chain, technology, and growth capital. The annual
screen therefore remains qualified rather than normalized owner cash. Source:
[Target FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27419/000002741926000018/a2025_annualreportxtgt.pdf).

TJX now has a filing-backed FY2024-FY2026 bridge and an H1 FY2027 bridge,
including the FY2026 capex split between new stores, renovations, and
offices/distribution centers. The H1 FY2027 filing reports `$603M` of inventory
cash use, `$470M` of accounts-payable support, `$85M` of share-based
compensation, `$1.418B` of repurchases, and `$1.147B` of operating-lease cash
paid. Operating cash flow also benefited from tariff refunds and a credit-card
interchange-fee settlement, so the `$2.186B` figure is not normalized owner
cash. No TJX valuation number is promoted until those burdens and a dated
market input are populated.

## Next proof gates

1. Separate the TJX H1 FY2027 tariff and settlement benefits, then extract
   maintenance-versus-growth capex and a dated market input.
2. Split Target and Walmart capital spending into maintenance and growth where
   filings permit, then subtract normalized working-capital and lease burdens.
3. Reconcile advertising, membership, marketplace, and vendor income after
   fulfillment, seller support, discounts, and technology costs.
4. Convert each bridge to cash per diluted share and compare it with a dated
   market snapshot.
