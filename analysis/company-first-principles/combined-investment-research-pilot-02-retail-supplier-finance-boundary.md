# Pilot 02 retail supplier-finance boundary

Research date: `2026-09-15`

Supplier-finance balances can make accounts payable look like a source of
operating cash, but the period-end obligation is not itself a period cash use
or cash source. The table preserves the balance-sheet burden and the correct
next test.

| Company / period | Eligible supplier-finance obligation | Filing treatment | Safe interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Target / August 1, 2026 | `$3.2B` | Included in accounts payable; vendors may elect early payment from financial institutions; Target's payment date can be up to 120 days from invoice date | Vendor-finance exposure is visible; do not subtract the full balance from H1 cash flow |
| Walmart / July 31, 2026 | `$6.4B` | Outstanding obligations to financial institutions under supplier-finance programs; prior balances were `$6.0B` and `$5.7B` | Large payable-finance balance is visible; the cash-flow effect requires roll-forward and settlement timing |

## Required upgrade

The [dated comparative roll-forward screen](combined-investment-research-pilot-02-retail-supplier-finance-roll-forward-2026-09-16.md)
now records Target's `$2.9B` / `$3.0B` / `$3.2B` and Walmart's `$5.7B` /
`$6.0B` / `$6.4B` period-end observations. The resulting `+$0.2B` Target,
`+$0.3B` Target year-over-year, `+$0.4B` Walmart, and `+$0.7B` Walmart
year-over-year movements are obligation screens only; they are not settlement
cash or additions to operating cash flow.

Obtain the annual or quarterly supplier-finance roll-forward, supplier-payment
settlement dates, and the accounts-payable change bridge. The current owner-cash
screen uses the disclosed `$1.648B` Walmart H1 accounts-payable source as a
sensitivity, not the `$6.4B` period-end balance.
