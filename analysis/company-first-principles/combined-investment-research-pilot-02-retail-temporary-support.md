# Pilot 02 retail temporary-support and pass-through boundary

Research date: `2026-09-15`

This memo records identifiable retail support items that affect reported
earnings or cash but should not be treated as recurring owner economics without
testing the pass-through and replacement cost.

| Company / period | Source-backed item | Amount | Safe treatment |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| TJX H1 FY2027 | Tariff refunds plus received interchange-fee settlement | `$750M` candidate | Existing TJX sensitivity; settlement is nonrecurring and tariff benefit may offset real cost |
| Target H1 2026 | IEEPA tariff refund | `$994M` pretax; `$752M` net earnings | Remove from normalized earnings/cash sensitivity; payable support remains separate |
| Walmart H1 FY2027 | IEEPA tariff refunds received in Q2 | Approximately `$2.9B` | Cash was received and reduced cost of sales; Walmart says remaining refunds are prioritized into lower prices, so the amount is both cash support and a potential pass-through investment |

## Walmart screen consequence

Walmart's reported H1 FY2027 cash-after-property bridge is `$5.529B`. The
existing low screen removed `$1.648B` of accounts-payable support. A more
conservative source-bounded low case also removes the approximately `$2.9B`
tariff-refund cash-support candidate, producing approximately `$0.981B` before
any separate treatment of stock compensation, leases, taxes, or maintenance
capital. Base and high cases remain `$5.529B` because the company may be
reinvesting the refund in lower prices and the filing does not provide a clean
cash-to-owner allocation.

This is a sensitivity boundary, not a claim that Walmart's normal cash flow is
`$0.981B`.
