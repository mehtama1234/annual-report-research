# Pilot 02 Target 2026 capital-plan boundary — 2026-09-15

This memo upgrades the retail reinvestment test with Target's current official
capital-plan disclosures. It identifies strategic and growth-directed uses of
capital, but it does not convert the plan into a maintenance-capital estimate
or normalized owner cash.

## Current plan

Target's official 2026 plan calls for approximately `$5B` of capital
investment, including:

- more than `30` new stores;
- more than `130` full-store remodels;
- supply-chain and technology projects; and
- more than `$1B` directed to food and beverage investment.

## Execution update

Target's September 2, 2026 official store-opening update says the eight fall
openings were the last of the more than `30` stores planned for 2026 and again
describes the `$5B` plan as supporting more than `130` remodels. This confirms
that the plan has moved from guidance into a materially executed store and
remodel program. It still does not disclose the dollars spent by project
class, nor does it establish a maintenance-capital amount.

Target describes the plan as supporting a broader store transformation,
fulfillment capability, assortment, and category growth. Its Q2 2026 results
also report approximately `$2.4B` of six-month capital expenditures and state
that the increase was driven primarily by store remodels and new stores.

## Denominator implication

The plan provides a stronger qualitative classification boundary:

```text
total 2026 capital plan
  -> new stores / remodels / supply chain / technology / category investment
  -> strategic and growth-directed uses are visible
  -> maintenance share and project-level cash return remain unallocated
```

The new evidence supports three safe conclusions:

1. Target's H1 property spending should not be treated as entirely
   maintenance.
2. It should also not be treated as entirely growth capital: remodels,
   supply-chain projects, technology, and store investments can contain both
   maintenance and productivity components.
3. The `$5B` plan is a management spending plan, not a cash-return schedule;
   it does not disclose project-level cash collection, tax, depreciation,
   working-capital, or owner-cash conversion.

The H1 `$2.404B` property-spending denominator therefore remains a range. The
official plan narrows the interpretation of the spending direction, but does
not supply the maintenance/growth dollar allocation needed to promote Target's
cash-after-property figure to normalized owner cash.

## Proof-grade result

| Gate | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Current total plan | Approximately `$5B` disclosed for 2026 |
| New-store and remodel direction | More than 30 new stores and 130 remodels disclosed |
| Strategic category direction | Supply chain, technology, and more than `$1B` food/beverage investment disclosed |
| H1 dollar category split | Not disclosed |
| Maintenance-capital amount | Not proven |
| Owner-cash promotion | Withheld |

Status: `target-forward-capital-plan-boundary-confirmed; maintenance split and owner cash unresolved`.
