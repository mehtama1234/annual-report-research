# Pilot 02 Target Q2 Attached-Service Burden Boundary

Research date: `2026-09-15`

This upgrade extends the Target attached-services screen from revenue and
payer visibility into the current filing's adjacent cost and temporary-support
boundaries. It does not allocate those costs to Roundel, card profit sharing,
or other revenue, and it does not promote attached-service revenue to owner
cash.

## Current revenue screen

Target's Q2 2026 Form 10-Q reports Q2 merchandise sales of `$25.947B`,
advertising revenue of `$279M`, credit-card profit sharing of `$139M`, and
other revenue of `$174M`, for total net sales of `$26.539B`. The attached
revenue screen is therefore `$592M`, or `2.28%` of merchandise sales and
`2.23%` of total net sales.

For the six months ended August 1, 2026, the filing reports merchandise sales
of `$50.841B`, advertising revenue of `$525M`, credit-card profit sharing of
`$269M`, and other revenue of `$347M`, for total net sales of `$51.982B`.
The six-month attached revenue screen is `$1.141B`, or `2.24%` of merchandise
sales and `2.20%` of total net sales.

## Current burden and classification boundary

The same filing reports Q2 merchandising cost of sales of `$15.775B` and
supply-chain and digital-fulfillment costs of `$1.828B`. For the first six
months, the corresponding amounts are `$32.053B` and `$3.611B`. The filing
also reports Q2 SG&A of `$5.725B` and H1 SG&A of `$11.286B`, with D&A of
`$651M` and `$1.337B`, respectively.

These are useful burden denominators, but they are not service-level costs:

- Target discloses supply-chain and digital-fulfillment costs in aggregate;
  it does not allocate them among merchandise, Roundel, card servicing, and
  other revenue.
- Roundel advertising may be presented as Net Sales or as a reduction of Cost
  of Sales or SG&A depending on the arrangement. Reported advertising revenue
  therefore cannot be treated as a clean incremental gross-profit line.
- TD Bank Group underwrites, funds, and owns the Target Circle receivables and
  controls risk management and regulatory compliance. Target services the
  accounts and performs primary marketing in exchange for a percentage of
  profits, but the filing does not disclose Target's allocated servicing or
  marketing cost.

## Temporary support boundary

Target recognized `$994M` of IEEPA tariff refunds in Q2 and H1 2026 as a
reduction of Cost of Sales. Refunds that remained outstanding were not
recognized. This is a material current-period cost-of-sales benefit, but it is
temporary support and cannot be treated as recurring attached-service margin
or recurring owner cash.

## Proof-grade result

| Gate | Result | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attached revenue scale | Proven for Q2 and H1 2026 | Exact advertising, card, other, merchandise, and total-sales fields are visible |
| Aggregate operating burden | Proven at Target-company level | Cost of sales, fulfillment, SG&A, and D&A are not allocated to attached services |
| Accounting classification | Proven | Roundel can reduce cost of sales or SG&A depending on arrangement |
| External payer and risk carrier | Proven for card route | TD owns/funds/underwrites receivables; Target's service cost is not separately disclosed |
| Temporary support | Proven | `$994M` tariff refund benefit is current-period support, not recurring service economics |
| Service-level cash conversion | Not proven | Working capital, taxes, capex, collection cash, and legal-entity attribution remain open |

## Updated safe claim

`Target's current filing puts attached-service revenue beside a disclosed but
aggregate operating-cost denominator and identifies a material temporary tariff
refund that reduced cost of sales. That improves the burden screen, but it does
not prove the costs, taxes, capital intensity, cash collection, or recurring
owner cash attributable to Roundel, card profit sharing, or other revenue.`
