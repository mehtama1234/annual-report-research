# TJX FY2027 forward capex category boundary

Research date: `2026-09-15`

## Question

Does TJX's latest annual filing quantify the forward capital-spending
categories well enough to narrow the owner-cash sensitivity, without pretending
that any category is pure maintenance or pure growth?

## Filing evidence

TJX's FY2026 Form 10-K gives FY2027 capital-spending guidance of approximately
`$2.2B–$2.3B` and identifies the following included categories:

| FY2027 category | Approximate amount | Safe use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Store renovations | `$1.000B` | Forward store-investment burden; renovation can contain both maintenance and growth |
| Offices and distribution centers, including IT systems | `$992M` | Forward infrastructure/technology burden; not a pure growth bucket |
| New stores | `$222M` | Named expansion category; still requires return and timing evidence |
| Implied sum of named categories | `$2.214B` | Falls within the `$2.2B–$2.3B` company guidance range |

The named amounts therefore explain the guide mechanically, but they do not
provide a maintenance-versus-growth split. The current H1 FY2027 filing still
describes actual spending categories without assigning H1 dollars to each.

## Owner-cash consequence

The forward category disclosure improves burden visibility and provides a
source-backed upper-bound context for TJX's reinvestment requirement. It must
not be used to subtract `$1.000B`, `$992M`, or `$222M` from current-period
operating cash as though those were H1 maintenance amounts. The disclosed
categories also do not establish store-level return, distribution-center
utilization, IT payback, tax treatment, lease burden, seasonality, or common-
owner cash.

## Proof-grade result

`TJX's FY2026 10-K quantifies the FY2027 capex guide into approximately $1.000B
of renovations, $992M of offices/distribution/IT, and $222M of new stores. The
forward spending boundary is proven; maintenance-versus-growth classification,
period allocation, and owner-cash conversion remain unresolved.`
