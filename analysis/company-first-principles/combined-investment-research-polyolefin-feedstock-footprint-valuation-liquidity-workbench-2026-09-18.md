# Polyolefin feedstock and footprint-repair valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves LyondellBasell from annual-report evidence into a company-specific valuation, spread-cycle, reinvestment, liquidity, and common-owner residual test. It keeps global polyolefin production, North American feedstock advantage, European footprint repair, joint ventures, and the Cash Improvement Plan separate from other chemical models.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LyondellBasell | Mid-cycle regional/product cash after feedstock, utilization, spread, plant renewal, environmental obligations, working capital, JV claims, debt, dividends, and dilution | Sustaining PP&E, plant availability, energy/logistics, environmental closure, circularity, footprint repair, JV capital, and working capital | Global oversupply, spread compression, outage, low utilization, feedstock/freight mismatch, European exit cost, debt refinancing, or delayed maintenance | EBITDA and cash-improvement savings rise while post-capex cash, utilization, footprint returns, or diluted common value deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$30.153B`, OCF `$2.262B`, PP&E spending `$1.878B`, OCF less PP&E only `$384M`, cash `$3.443B`, inventory `$3.533B`, and repurchases `$201M`.
- The OCF-less-PP&E screen was approximately `$3.411B` in 2023, `$1.980B` in 2024, and `$384M` in 2025; adjusted EBITDA is not a sufficient owner-cash denominator.
- The Cash Improvement Plan delivered `$800M` in 2025 against a `$600M` initial target and a cumulative `$1.3B` target by end-2026. Savings must be split into structural margin, working-capital timing, deferred maintenance, closures, and lost volume.
- Four European asset divestitures completed in Q2 2026 require proceeds, taxes, closure/environmental cash, stranded overhead, lost EBITDA, and remaining-footprint return analysis.
- The model is a spread and utilization business: North American feedstock can help, but global oversupply, outages, co-products, freight, energy, and regional demand can overwhelm the advantage.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Rebuild price, feedstock, energy, co-product, freight, utilization, and fixed-cost spreads by region and product; do not capitalize a temporary spread peak.
2. Bridge adjusted EBITDA to OCF through inventory, receivables, payables, customer advances, JV distributions, tax, interest, and working-capital releases.
3. Attribute every Cash Improvement Plan dollar to recurring procurement/fixed-cost savings, reversible working capital, delayed capex, closure, asset sale, or lost volume.
4. Analyze European exits through proceeds, tax, closure, environmental obligations, stranded overhead, lost cash, and avoided capital.
5. Reconcile JV ownership, debt, distributions, retained cash, co-product economics, environmental obligations, dividends, and buybacks to common residual.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style test values each regional/product business using mid-cycle spreads, utilization, feedstock, maintenance capital, and probability-weighted footprint outcomes. The Lyn Alden-style stress test focuses on energy geography, trade, industrial demand, geopolitics, fixed costs, debt, and global excess capacity.

## Promotion boundary

`polyolefin-feedstock-footprint-qualified; spread-capex-restructuring-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from product/spread and utilization to collected cash, sustaining capital, environmental/closure cost, JV distributions, footprint-exit return, debt, and diluted common residual. EBITDA, cash-plan targets, OCF, asset-sale proceeds, dividends, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
