# Pool specialty distribution valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Pool from current-period distribution evidence into a
company-specific valuation, working-capital, channel, and common-owner cash test.
It does not pool maintenance distribution, new construction, industrial wholesale,
or consumer-retail economics into one generic margin or FCF comparison.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pool | Collected branch-distribution cash after supplier purchases, inventory, receivables, early-buy programs, branch labor, fleet and systems, taxes, debt, and dilution | Inventory positioning, branch expansion, technology, distribution capacity, acquisitions, customer service, and seasonal working capital | Housing and consumer slowdown, pool construction weakness, product mix, supplier availability, inventory build, receivables aging, revolver/receivables-facility reliance, or margin compression | Maintenance and repair demand appear resilient while inventory, receivables financing, margin, branch productivity, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Pool operated `456` sales centers as of December 31, 2025, across five distribution networks serving pool, landscape, irrigation, and related trade customers.
- Approximately `64%` of 2025 sales came from recurring maintenance and minor repair, `22%` from remodeling and upgrades, and `14%` from new pool construction.
- Full-year 2025 net sales were `$5.289B`, operating income `$580.2M`, net income `$406.4M`, and operating cash flow `$365.9M`.
- Q2 2026 net sales increased `2%` to `$1.823B`, gross profit was `$540.8M`, operating income `$267.7M`, and net income `$188.1M`.
- The receivables facility had `$315.5M` outstanding at June 30, 2026; seasonal inventory, early buys, and customer receivables are therefore core funding variables rather than incidental treasury items.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile sales to maintenance, repair, remodeling, equipment, chemicals,
   building materials, and new-construction mix; product mix can change margins
   without changing branch demand.
2. Join branch sales to inventory turns, early-buy timing, markdowns, supplier
   terms, receivable aging, facility borrowings, and cash collections.
3. Keep inventory and receivables financing in the cash denominator; reported OCF
   can be temporarily helped or hurt by seasonal working-capital reversals.
4. Separate base-business performance from acquisitions, CEO-transition costs,
   restructuring, branch additions, software, and other productivity claims.
5. Reconcile adjusted earnings, capital spending, dividends, repurchases, debt,
   receivables-facility capacity, taxes, and diluted shares before calling cash
   surplus owner cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what maintenance share, branch density,
gross margin, inventory turns, receivable collection, reinvestment rate, and cost
of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether housing,
consumer credit, Sun Belt migration, supplier terms, seasonal financing, and
discretionary outdoor-living demand can support liquidity through a cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`pool-specialty-distribution-qualified; seasonal-working-capital-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from branch and product activity
to supplier purchases, inventory turns, receivable collections, financing usage,
required reinvestment, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Sales growth,
maintenance mix, gross profit, operating cash flow, and repurchases remain
diagnostic inputs.
