# Power-grid customer cash current-period synthesis — H1 2026

## Purpose and comparability boundary

This synthesis joins the H1 2026 evidence for NextEra/FPL, AEP, and Duke
Energy. It compares the cash, capital, regulatory, customer, financing, and
owner-cash surfaces without ranking the companies. Their evidence objects are
different: FPL has a category-recovery route, AEP has large-load contracts and
tariff expansion, and Duke has a named generation project with shared
ownership.

## What the synthesis reveals

### FPL: the strongest recovery mechanism, but category allocation remains open

FPL has the strongest existing route from approved category costs to true-ups,
output, and recovery. Its H1 legal-entity OCF exceeded capex less severely than
the AEP and Duke mechanical screens. But aggregate FPL cash cannot be assigned
to Distribution Inspection or another category without a billing determinant,
receipt ledger, true-up allocation, and source-of-funds bridge.

### AEP: the strongest load-growth expectation, but the cash object is forward

AEP’s 69 GW signed-load figure and `$77.937B` capital plan create a powerful
load-to-rate-base thesis. The current filing also shows customer-affordability
offsets and large-load tariffs. The open object is a named customer obligation
with minimum payment, collateral, construction allocation, tariff treatment,
in-service date, billed revenue, and collected cash.

### Duke: the cleanest named-project boundary, but shared ownership matters

Duke’s Anderson County project provides a specific approval and ownership
object. The H1 cash surface shows why the project cannot be valued from
capacity alone: capex exceeded OCF, accrued capex was material, and financing
and NCI contributions were significant. The open object is final ownership,
paid cost, rate-base/tariff treatment, customer payment, and Duke’s share of
the return.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

1. Do not substitute approved recovery, signed load, or project capacity for
   billed and collected revenue.
2. Reconcile cash paid, accrued capex, CWIP, AFUDC, regulatory assets/liabilities,
   and in-service dates.
3. Separate customer deposits, take-or-pay agreements, grants, DOE loans,
   securitizations, NCI, asset-sale proceeds, and parent funding.
4. Trace debt, preferred claims, shared ownership, taxes, fuel, storms, and
   dilution before claiming common-owner cash.
5. Reconcile operating/non-GAAP earnings to GAAP and test the recurrence of
   regulatory credits, true-ups, asset sales, tax effects, and mark-to-market
   adjustments.
6. Stress rates, construction inflation, storms, fuel availability, load
   delays, affordability pressure, and capital-market access together.

## Decision status

The power-grid lane is now current-period qualified across all three companies,
but remains `no-ranking`. The shared promotion gate is a named category or
project that closes the path:

`approved cost / contract -> billing determinant -> paid capex -> billed revenue -> collection -> recovery -> common-owner return`
