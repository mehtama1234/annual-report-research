# Power-grid customer cash — next-sector admission

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Decision

Admit regulated power-grid customer cash as the next sector lane after the
medical-devices cohort. The initial comparison is:

- **NextEra Energy / FPL:** category recovery and regulated-distribution route.
- **AEP:** large-load obligations and regulated grid-investment route.
- **Duke Energy:** generation-project approval, shared ownership, and recovery

This is a controlled admission, not a ranking. The [promotion action register](data/combined-investment-research-power-grid-customer-cash-promotion-action-register-2026-09-17.csv)
preserves the exact next document and thesis breaker for each company.

## Force and control point

The force is the rising value of reliable electricity under data-center/AI
load, industrial reshoring, electrification, aging infrastructure, and
affordability pressure. The control point is not “electricity demand” in the
abstract. It is the regulated utility's ability to convert an approved asset,
tariff, billing determinant, or contracted load into recoverable customer
revenue and then into legal-entity cash after construction, financing, fuel,
tax, and customer-credit claims.

The core chain is:

```text
load or reliability need
  -> project / distribution category / customer obligation
  -> regulatory approval and cost allocation
  -> billing determinant or contract
  -> billed and collected customer dollars
  -> rate-base or project return
  -> cash after capex, debt, taxes and dilution
```

Approval, rate-base inclusion, contracted output, and planned in-service dates
are distinct proof objects. None automatically proves collected customer cash
or common-owner return.

## Company boundaries

### NextEra Energy / FPL

The existing power-grid work supplies category-level recovery, factor, true-up,
output, component, and capital-structure evidence. The route is strong enough
to remain active, but it is still `hold-with-strong-route-visible`: the missing
object is a Distribution Inspection-specific billing determinant or collection
ledger joined to category allocation and earned return.

### AEP

AEP extends the lane toward large-load customer obligations and grid investment.
The research must distinguish a customer commitment or load forecast from a
billed amount, construction recovery, or collected cash. The owner-cash bridge
must separate construction work in progress, regulated recovery, receivables,
fuel/power purchases, debt, and dilution.

### Duke Energy

The Anderson County boundary is now specific: approximately 1,365 MW, with 95
MW of Central Electric Power Cooperative ownership and 100 MW of North Carolina
Electric Membership Corporation ownership. The South Carolina approval and
forward target dates improve the regulatory map, but construction anticipated
for summer 2027 and customer service targeted for early 2031 remain forward
dates. Approval is not rate-base cash, and shared ownership is not Duke net
capacity or Duke common-owner return.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans tests

1. Separate approved recovery from billed and collected revenue.
2. Reconcile rate-base growth, construction work in progress, maintenance and
   growth capex, depreciation, and regulatory assets/liabilities.
3. Test whether true-ups, fuel riders, storm costs, and customer credits shift
   cash between periods.
4. Separate contracted load, deposits, interconnection obligations, and
   forecasts from realized load and payment.
5. Treat debt-funded capex, preferred or shared-owner claims, tax, and dilution
   as senior or competing claims to common-owner cash.

These are reconciliation prompts, not manipulation findings. A utility can
report valid regulated earnings while still exposing common owners to timing,
financing, affordability, and recovery risk.

## Damodaran and Lyn Alden handoff

The valuation question is whether rate-base/project growth, allowed returns,
recovery timing, load realization, and financing costs produce common-owner
cash above the cost of capital. The liquidity question is how rates,
construction inflation, gas/fuel access, storm costs, permitting, regulatory
lag, industrial/AI load timing, and customer affordability transmit into debt
service, equity issuance, and collected revenue.
