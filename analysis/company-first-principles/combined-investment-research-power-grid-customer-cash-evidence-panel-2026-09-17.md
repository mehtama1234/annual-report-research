## NextEra Energy / FPL

FPL is the clearest recovery-mechanism case. The preserved work contains an
SPPCRC 2026 jurisdictional revenue requirement of approximately `$859.244M`,
Distribution Inspection output of `193,199` projects and `$61.500M` actual
cost, and final Distribution Inspection recovery of approximately `$15.943M`.
The broader final SPPCRC clause-revenue figure is approximately `$804.620M`,
and FPL-wide H1 operating cash and capex were approximately `$5.388B` and
`$5.780B`.

This is a strong operating-return bridge, but it is not category receipt proof.
Aggregate clause revenue and true-up collection cannot be allocated to
Distribution Inspection without the billing determinant, rate-class schedule,
and category collection/source-of-funds bridge. The next object remains the
category-specific receipt ledger.

## AEP

AEP is the load-to-rate-base comparison. The company analysis reports a grid
footprint of approximately `5.6M` customers across `11` states, about `40,000`
transmission line miles and `252,000` distribution line miles. Its expected
new-load bridge reached approximately `69 GW` by 2030, and its five-year
capital plan reached approximately `$78B`.

These figures identify a powerful control point: grid access can constrain
industrial, data-center, and electrification growth. They do not prove that
forecast load becomes billed cash or that capital earns an allowed return.
The decisive document is an executed or redacted large-load obligation joined
to project allocation, approval/recovery, in-service timing, and customer
payment or collateral.

## Duke Energy

Duke provides the cleanest named-project boundary. Anderson County is
approximately `1,365 MW`; the current DEC construction-cost estimate is
approximately `$3.218B`, with approximately `$45.86M` of on-site interconnection
facilities and `$294.5M` of network transmission upgrades. The record also
identifies `95 MW` and `100 MW` of co-op ownership, construction anticipated in
summer 2027, and customer service targeted for early 2031.

The PSC certificate and construction monitoring strengthen approval and
execution evidence. They do not establish paid capex, final ownership terms,
CWIP/rate-base treatment, customer contributions, billed revenue, debt service,
or common-owner return. The project therefore remains approval-visible but
cash-open.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans overlay

The utility-specific tests are:

1. approved recovery versus billed and collected revenue;
2. rate-base growth versus construction work in progress and paid capex;
3. true-ups and regulatory assets/liabilities versus period cash;
4. load forecasts, contracts, deposits, and collateral versus realized load
   and payment;
5. shared ownership and customer contributions versus consolidated utility
   claims; and
6. debt-funded capital, taxes, fuel/power purchases, and dilution versus
   common-owner residual cash.

These are quality-of-earnings diagnostics. They do not imply accounting
misconduct; regulated earnings can be valid while cash timing and allocation
remain unresolved.

## Valuation and liquidity handoff

Damodaran-style valuation must model rate-base/project growth, allowed return,
construction-to-service timing, regulatory lag, financing mix, and dilution.
Lyn Alden-style stress must model rates, construction inflation, fuel/gas
availability, storms, permitting, customer affordability, load realization,
and equity/debt-market access.

The market thesis is promoted only when one named category or project reaches
the full path from approved cost and billing determinant to billed/collected
revenue, period-matched cash, capital use, and bounded return.
