## NextEra Energy

The Q2 2026 dossier reports `$7.276B` of first-half operating cash against
`$19.389B` of capital expenditures, independent-power investments, and nuclear
fuel purchases. Funding included `$15.566B` of long-term debt issuance and
`$3.431B` of commercial-paper and other short-term debt increase, alongside
asset sales, equity, and tax-credit proceeds. Net available liquidity was
approximately `$18.1B`.

This is not a shenanigan by itself; utilities and developers fund long-lived
assets externally. It is the correct valuation burden. The recorded market
snapshot of approximately `$171.7B` is supported in the dossier by illustrative
common-earnings cases of `$0B`, `$3.0B`, and `$6.0B` at 20x, 35x, and 45x,
implying `$0B`, `$105B`, and `$270B`. The base case requires FPL recovery,
NEER project returns, tax-credit and financing support, and controlled dilution
to offset the consolidated funding gap.

The liquidity test is whether rate-base growth and contracted projects produce
cash returns above financing and construction burden, not whether adjusted EPS
continues to compound.

## Duke Energy

Duke reported 2025 operating cash of `$12.330B` against `$14.024B` of capital
expenditures. Operating cash also included approximately `$723M` of
transferable tax-credit sale proceeds. Its capital plan is approximately
`$103B` for 2026–2030.

The dossier records a market value of approximately `$93.0B` and illustrative
common-owner cases of `$0B`, `$1B`, and `$3B` at 12x, 16x, and 20x, implying
`$0B`, `$16B`, and `$60B`. These are not price targets; they demonstrate that
the market is valuing future regulated earnings and rate-base conversion,
not current cash after construction.

The Damodaran question is whether capital enters service, receives timely
recovery, earns an acceptable return, and avoids excessive debt/equity dilution.
The Alden question is whether rates, construction inflation, gas/fuel access,
tax-credit dependence, regulatory lag, and customer affordability make that
funding path durable.
