# Precious-metals streaming valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Wheaton Precious Metals into a company-specific
streaming/royalty valuation and liquidity object. It keeps contractual metal
streams separate from direct mine operators, materials manufacturers,
integrated oil and gas, and ordinary finance. The model tests whether operator-
funded production becomes collected common-owner cash after stream purchase
payments, new portfolio investments, metal prices, operator delivery risk,
debt, dividends, taxes, and diluted claims.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wheaton Precious Metals | Portfolio cash from delivered and collected metal after stream purchase payments, new streams/royalties, operator counterparty risk, taxes, debt, dividends, and diluted common residual | Upfront stream acquisition payments, ongoing payable metal costs, development-stage commitments, reserve/delivery curves, portfolio concentration, debt, and transaction costs | Metal-price decline, operator delay/default, permitting, debt service, revolver/term-loan access, dividend burden, and inability to fund or exit new streams | GEOs, revenue, operating cash, or Antamina contribution rise while delivered/collected metal, stream returns, debt reduction, or per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$2.315B`, operating cash flow `$1.905B`, and total
  investing cash outflow `$1.279B`, leaving a conservative cash screen of about
  `$626M` before dividends. Physical PP&E purchases were only about `$2M`, so
  PP&E is not an adequate reinvestment denominator for a streaming business.
- Wheaton sold `689.9K` GEOs in FY2025 across 23 operating-mine agreements and
  25 development/other projects. Approximately 85% of attributable production
  came from assets in the lowest half of their cost curves, but operator output
  and delivery remain external dependencies.
- The Antamina stream required a `$4.300B` payment. H1 2026 Antamina revenue
  was `$277.563M`, operating cash flow `$222.223M`, and cash cost excluding
  depletion `$55.340M`; these are strong named-asset diagnostics, not an
  after-tax financed IRR or NPV.
- Antamina's attributable payable production share rose from 33.75% to 67.5%
  effective April 1, 2026, with a 90% payable factor and an ongoing payment of
  20% of spot price or price received. Attributable ounces are not identical to
  delivered, invoiced, or collected cash.
- Wheaton moved from debt-free at FY2025 year-end to approximately `$2.0B` of
  debt after funding Antamina; Q2 2026 cash was about `$100M`. Debt service,
  maturity, tax allocation, and Antamina-specific repayment remain open joins.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate metal price, GEO volume, operator timing, stream payment cost,
   receivable collection, taxes, and portfolio-investment cash within OCF.
2. Do not treat low PP&E capex as low reinvestment; upfront stream payments and
   development commitments are the relevant capital denominator.
3. Reconcile Antamina and every major stream from attributable production to
   delivery, invoice, collection, payable cost, debt service, and common cash.
4. Test operator reserves, cost-curve claims, expansion rights, delivery terms,
   audit rights, and counterparty concentration against actual receipts.
5. Treat GEO growth, dividends, and record metal-price cash as diagnostic until
   acquisition return, leverage reduction, and diluted per-share residual are
   evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what metal prices, delivered GEOs,
stream margins, acquisition price, reserve life, operator delivery, portfolio
growth, debt cost, and cost of capital the equity value requires. The Lyn
Alden-style stress test asks whether the royalty/stream model's lower exposure
to mine labor and energy inflation survives metal-price volatility, operator
credit/permitting risk, rates, refinancing, and scarce attractive deal supply.

## Promotion boundary

`precious-metals-streaming-qualified; acquisition-return-and-delivery-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from each material stream to
delivered and collected metal, payable stream cost, operator obligations,
reserve/delivery evidence, acquisition price, debt/tax allocation, dividends,
diluted shares, and common-owner residual. GEOs, revenue, OCF, low PP&E capex,
annualized stream screens, and dividends remain diagnostic inputs.
