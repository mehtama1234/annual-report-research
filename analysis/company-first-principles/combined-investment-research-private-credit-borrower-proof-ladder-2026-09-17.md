## What the ladder teaches

These routes are not interchangeable:

- Bear Financing starts with a legal-entity statutory holding and income field,
  but stops before borrower/use proof.
- Concord starts with an issuer/wrapper and public collateral/use context,
  but stops before Athene-specific receipt and liability-adjusted return.
- Frontline starts with an operating borrower and facility purpose, but stops
  before lender allocation and actual debt-service cash.

The strongest end-to-end investment claim needs both sides. A platform or
insurer holding does not prove borrower cash. A borrower facility announcement
does not prove that a particular insurer or manager funded it. A public
refinancing or acquisition purpose does not prove the closing funds flow.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

The ladder keeps the following diagnostic boundaries explicit:

1. statutory interest is an accrual/collection field at the insurer, not
   borrower-level operating cash;
2. fair value and unfunded commitments are not funded principal or cash
   receipts;
3. acquisition-facility role is not proof of Ares-funded allocation;
4. refinancing/use language is not proof of payoff settlement; and
5. lender return requires principal, interest, fees, losses, timing, and funding
   cost—not just coupon, fair value, or reported income.

These are quality-of-earnings and source-integrity controls. They identify
which reconciliation is missing; they do not allege manipulation or fraud.
