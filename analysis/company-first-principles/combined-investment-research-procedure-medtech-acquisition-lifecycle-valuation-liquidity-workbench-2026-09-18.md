# Procedure-led MedTech acquisition and lifecycle valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Boston Scientific from procedure-growth evidence into a
clinical-adoption, acquisition-return, quality, and common-cash test. It keeps
Boston Scientific distinct from Stryker/Intuitive/Henry Schein, whose
workbench emphasizes other device, installed-base, and distribution models.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Boston Scientific | Cardiovascular and MedSurg procedure cash after clinical adoption, reimbursement, R&D, sales training, inventory, quality, acquisitions, integration, debt, SBC, and dilution | Clinical trials, R&D, regulatory submissions, manufacturing, inventory, salesforce, physician training, product quality, and acquired-cohort integration | Hospital-budget or procedure slowdown, reimbursement pressure, product recall, acquisition overpayment, inventory/collection stress, debt, or dilution | Sales, procedure growth, organic growth, or adjusted EPS rise while acquisition return, quality, price/mix, R&D, working capital, or per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 sales were `$20.074B`, operating cash flow `$4.534B`, PP&E and
  internal-use software `$876M`, acquisitions `$1.593B`, and simple post-listed-
  uses residual `$2.065B`; strategic investments reduced that screen further.
- FY2025 Cardiovascular and MedSurg portfolios included electrophysiology,
  structural heart, coronary, peripheral, endoscopy, urology, and
  neuromodulation procedures.
- 2025 organic sales growth was approximately `15.8%` after acquisition and
  currency effects, but acquisition spending was material in 2023–25.
- FY2025 R&D was `$2.052B`, SBC `$299M`, year-end receivables `$2.926B`, and
  inventory `$2.943B`; these are claims on the apparent growth cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile sales into units, price, mix, procedure volume, hospital adoption,
   reimbursement, acquisitions, divestitures, and currency.
2. Track clinical evidence, physician training, hospital stocking, repeat use,
   product reliability, recalls, field actions, and regulatory reserves.
3. Test acquisition price, acquired revenue/margin, integration, amortization,
   goodwill, contingent consideration, and cash return by cohort.
4. Separate mature-product replacement R&D from expansion R&D and salesforce,
   inventory, launch, and clinical-trial costs.
5. Normalize receivables, inventory, concessions, debt, strategic investments,
   SBC, diluted shares, and capital returns.
6. Treat procedure growth, organic growth, adjusted EPS, OCF, dividends, and
   repurchases as diagnostic until lifecycle cash is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test converts procedure share, utilization,
price, gross margin, R&D, clinical adoption, product life, and acquired-cohort
return into owner cash. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether aging and
medical need offset hospital budgets, reimbursement, labor, rates, regulatory,
and product-quality pressure. Clinical indispensability can support demand
without guaranteeing manufacturer-level returns.

## Promotion boundary

`procedure-medtech-qualified; acquisition-return-and-quality-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from procedures, units,
price, reimbursement, adoption, quality, R&D, acquisitions, integration,
inventory, receivables, debt, SBC, and diluted common residual. Sales, organic
growth, procedures, adjusted EPS, OCF, dividends, and buybacks remain
diagnostic inputs.
