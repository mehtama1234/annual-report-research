## Purpose

This panel makes the quality-of-earnings method visible across the current
sector refreshes. A signal is a reconciliation prompt, not evidence of
misconduct. The panel does not calculate a pooled Beneish score because the
companies have different accounting perimeters, fiscal periods, balance-sheet
structures, and economic denominators.

### Retail QoE / CA-06 diagnostic boundary

The retail lane is a denominator-control exercise, not a claim that any of the
three companies used improper accounting. TJX's settlement and tariff-related
receipts must remain separately identified until payment dates, tax treatment,
and recurrence are established; they should not be removed or annualized by
assumption. Target's `$3.2B` supplier-finance balance is an eligible-obligation
disclosure, not evidence that the company made `$3.2B` of accelerated cash
payments. Walmart's `$5.529B` FCF is management-defined and excludes debt
service and acquisitions, so it cannot be treated as common-owner cash without
lease, maintenance-capital, tax, service-cost, and dilution bridges.

The promotion tests are therefore explicit: do not subtract a supplier-finance
balance as if it were cash; do not treat tariff or interchange settlements as
recurring gross profit; do not treat broad property-capex categories as
maintenance capital; and do not compare retailer FCF definitions until the
same-period lease, debt, tax, service, and owner-distribution perimeter is
reconciled. This lane remains `CA-06 partial; no ranking` until those joins are
closed.

## Method controls

### Sloan-style accrual and cash-conversion prompts

Use accrual and cash-conversion signals directionally: compare earnings,
operating cash, working capital, and noncash adjustments for the same entity
and period. Do not infer poor quality from a single divergence; identify the
reversal schedule and whether the business model naturally creates contract,
inventory, reserve, or receivable timing.

### Schilit-style unusual-item prompts

Separate gains, refunds, insurance, litigation, reserve releases, asset sales,
derivative marks, purchase accounting, and restructuring. The test is whether
the item is recurring, cash-paid, operationally embedded, legally restricted,
or merely shifted between periods. “Adjusted” presentation is a flag for the
bridge, not a reason to erase the item.

### Beneish-style composite boundary

No composite Beneish-style score is assembled across these lanes. A valid
composite would require comparable revenue, receivables, gross profit, total
assets, depreciation, SG&A, accruals, cash, accounting policies, and fiscal
periods. Several of those fields are absent or economically non-comparable.
The correct output is a field-level diagnostic and a filing-based thesis
breaker, not a fraud probability.

## Promotion rule

`diagnostic-signal-observed; reconciliation-open; no-fraud-finding; no-ranking`

A QoE flag can alter the valuation or liquidity sensitivity only after the
same-entity, same-period cash or claim reconciliation is found. Until then,
reported earnings, adjusted FCF, deferred revenue, reserves, OCF, and capital
returns remain labeled observations rather than normalized owner cash.
