# Qualified dispensing and packaging valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves AptarGroup into a company-specific qualified dispensing,
dosing, closure, and packaging-interface valuation/liquidity object. It keeps
specialized component manufacturing separate from medical-device makers,
consumer staples, semiconductor hardware, and industrial chemicals. The
object tests Pharma, Beauty, and Closures cash after qualification, quality,
clean-room and tooling costs, raw materials, labor, plant utilization, capex,
working capital, acquisitions, debt, dividends, SBC, and diluted common claims.

## Decision matrix

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AptarGroup | Qualified dispensing and dosing-interface cash by Pharma, Beauty, and Closures after customer launches, regulatory/quality investment, materials, labor, plant utilization, tooling, capex, working capital, acquisitions, debt, and diluted common residual | Pharma mix, customer qualification, product launches, clean-room and tooling, resin/elastomer/raw materials, inventory, plant capacity, R&D, maintenance/growth capex, acquisitions, debt, SBC, and buybacks | Pharma/consumer demand or launch delay, destocking, raw-material/labor inflation, plant underutilization, second-source qualification, quality/recall, customer concentration, refinancing, and capital-return pressure | Revenue, adjusted EBITDA/EPS, Pharma growth, product launches, or buybacks rise while core demand, qualification returns, quality investment, plant utilization, cash conversion, or per-share residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 sales were `$3.777B`; operating income `$501.0M`; adjusted EBITDA
  margin `21.6%`; Pharma was about `46%` of sales but `69%` of adjusted EBITDA
  excluding corporate costs.
- Q2 2026 sales were `$1.03B`, up `6%`, with net income `$88M`, diluted EPS
  `$1.36`, adjusted EPS `$1.42`, and adjusted EBITDA margin `20.7%`. Reported
  growth was Pharma `4%`, Beauty `10%`, and Closures `7%`, while core growth was
  only Pharma `1%`, Beauty `1%`, and Closures `4%`.
- Q1 2026 reported growth was `11%`, but Pharma core growth was negative `1%`;
  reported growth therefore requires price, mix, currency, acquisition, launch,
  and destocking reconciliation.
- Pharma's profit concentration makes segment mix more important than
  consolidated sales. Closures and consumer-facing capacity carry greater raw
  material, labor, freight, and plant-utilization exposure.
- FY2025 shareholder returns were about `$486M`; those returns require a bridge
  to operating cash, maintenance/growth capex, acquisitions, debt, quality and
  regulatory investment, SBC, and diluted shares.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate Pharma, Beauty, and Closures core sales from price, volume, mix,
   currency, acquisitions, launches, and destocking.
2. Test whether qualified drug-delivery and dispensing interfaces earn returns
   after regulatory, clean-room, quality, tooling, and failure costs.
3. Reconcile raw-material, labor, freight, plant utilization, inventory,
   purchase commitments, and price recovery rather than inferring durability
   from one quarter of margin.
4. Track customer concentration, qualification, product launches, second-source
   activity, recalls, quality events, and repeat orders.
5. Keep adjusted EBITDA/EPS, dividends, buybacks, debt, and SBC separate from
   common-owner cash after specialized-capacity renewal.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what Pharma mix, core growth,
qualification duration, launch conversion, material/labor recovery, plant
utilization, capex, acquisition return, debt cost, and cost of capital the
valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether healthcare,
beauty, food, beverage, and home-care demand, rates, raw materials, labor,
customer launches, and quality obligations preserve liquidity through a cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`qualified-dispensing-packaging-qualified; segment-core-growth-and-quality-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from qualified products and
customer launches to repeat orders, collection, quality/regulatory cost,
materials and labor, plant utilization, maintenance/growth capex, acquisition
return, debt, diluted shares, and common-owner residual. Sales, adjusted
EBITDA/EPS, Pharma mix, reported OCF, FCF, dividends, and buybacks remain
diagnostic inputs.
