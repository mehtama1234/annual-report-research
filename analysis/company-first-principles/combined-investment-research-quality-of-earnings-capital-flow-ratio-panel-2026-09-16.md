# Capital-flow quality-of-earnings ratio panel

Research date: `2026-09-16`

## Purpose

This panel extends the financial-shenanigans and quality-of-earnings review
from retail statements into the two named capital-flow pilots. It puts the
largest available cash, collection, settlement, fee, turnover, and liability
ratios beside their exact denominator boundaries.

The structured [ratio-panel CSV](data/combined-investment-research-quality-of-earnings-capital-flow-ratio-panel-2026-09-16.csv)
is the source of truth. The rows are not comparable company scores: Wheaton's
stream proxy, BHP's streaming liability, Athene's statutory entity, and
Apollo's fee roll-forward belong to different legal entities and accounting
perimeters.

## What the ratios show

- Wheaton's H1 Antamina stream proxy has `$222.223M` of OCF proxy against
  `$277.563M` of stream revenue, an approximately `80.09%` screen before
  corporate financing, tax, and BHP-only allocation. The corporate finance-cost
  ratio is shown only as an allocation-sensitive boundary.
- BHP's `$41M` streaming-liability settlement against the `$4.300B` upfront
  receipt is visible in the consolidated filing, but it cannot be converted
  into BHP-only ounces, invoice pricing, or Wheaton receipt cash.
- Athene's FY2025 statutory screen shows `96.46%` cash-flow net-investment-
  income conversion and `97.08%` collected-to-earned investment income. These
  are legal-entity quality signals, not Apollo common-owner cash.
- The H1 2026 fee roll-forward implies `$779M` of settlement against `$785M`
  of expense, or approximately `99.24%`, but the recipient and bank receipt
  remain unproven.
- Athene's `$54.035B` bond sale/maturity/repayment proceeds against a
  `$158.852B` Schedule D bond base are a turnover screen, not asset-level
  realized return or borrower repayment proof.
