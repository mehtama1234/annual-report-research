# Current retail quality-of-earnings diagnostic ratio panel

Research date: `2026-09-16`

## Scope

This panel derives comparable diagnostic ratios from the [current retail composite input panel](combined-investment-research-quality-of-earnings-current-retail-composite-input-panel-2026-09-16.md). It compares the six-month filings for TJX, Target, and Walmart with the prior year-end balance-sheet point used by each filing. The ratios direct forensic follow-up; they are not a Beneish score, a manipulation conclusion, or an investment ranking.

## Reading rules

- `Net inventory less AP change / revenue` is a mechanical timing signal. A positive value means inventory grew more than accounts payable over the balance-sheet interval; it is not an estimate of normalized working capital.
- `Debt + lease claims / OCF` uses the current-period claim perimeter where the filing makes it joinable. Target is held blank because the extracted current statement does not isolate a clean combined debt-and-lease denominator.
- Missing SBC or claims are not zero. They are missing inputs and remain promotion blockers for a composite score.
- High or low ratios are prompts to inspect period matching, temporary support, accounting classification, and legal-entity scope. They are not evidence of financial shenanigans by themselves.

## Decision impact

The panel strengthens the current retail QoE layer in two ways. First, it shows that all three businesses have positive earnings-to-OCF conversion while capital intensity and working-capital timing differ materially. Second, it prevents a single positive OCF ratio from hiding the post-property and claim burdens. The result remains `current-period-diagnostic-partial`: the full multi-period accrual score still requires comparable receivables, SG&A, SBC, lease, tax, transaction, and maintenance-capital histories.
