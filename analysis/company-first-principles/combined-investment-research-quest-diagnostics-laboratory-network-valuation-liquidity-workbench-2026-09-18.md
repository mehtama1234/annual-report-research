# Quest Diagnostics laboratory-network valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench tests Quest Diagnostics as national diagnostic infrastructure. It keeps requisition volume, routine and advanced assays, payer and employer channels, consumer testing, logistics density, lab automation, acquisitions, reimbursement, data connectivity, debt, and diluted common residual separate from reported revenue and adjusted EPS.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quest Diagnostics | Diagnostic-routing and testing cash after specimen logistics, lab capacity, automation, advanced assays, payer collection, acquisitions, transformation, debt, and diluted common residual | Laboratory equipment and automation, logistics, assay development, quality/regulatory systems, acquisitions, digital connectivity, working capital, debt, and shares | Reimbursement pressure, payer mix deterioration, test-volume decline, labor or logistics disruption, assay/regulatory failure, acquisition integration, or capital-market stress | Revenue and test volume rise while revenue per requisition, payer collections, lab productivity, advanced-assay return, or diluted per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$11.04B` and adjusted diluted EPS `$9.85`.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$3.04B`, up `10.2%`, organic growth `10.0%`, and adjusted diluted EPS `$3.12`; full-year revenue guidance rose to `$11.95-$12.05B`.
- The platform spans providers, hospitals, health plans, employers, consumers, questhealth.com, wellness partners, collaboration laboratories, routine testing, and Advanced Diagnostics.
- Growth initiatives include Alzheimer's and cardiometabolic testing, oncology diagnostics, AI Companion, automation, Invigorate cost savings, and Project Nova; each carries separate collection and reinvestment tests.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile requisitions and test volume to revenue per requisition, payer mix, denials, collections, employer/consumer settlement, and cash conversion.
2. Separate routine testing density from advanced-assay growth, acquisitions, pricing, and mix.
3. Test lab automation, logistics, quality, digital connectivity, and transformation spending against productivity claims.
4. Keep reimbursement, litigation, regulatory claims, acquisitions, debt, buybacks, and diluted shares in the common-owner residual schedule.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what requisition growth, revenue per test, advanced-assay mix, lab productivity, reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether payers, employers, consumers, and providers continue funding diagnostic infrastructure through reimbursement, labor, logistics, regulatory, and healthcare-budget shocks.

## Promotion boundary

`quest-qualified; diagnostic-volume-and-collection-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from requisitions and assays to payer/employer/consumer collections, lab capacity, logistics, required reinvestment, acquisition return, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, test volume, adjusted EPS, OCF, guidance, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
