# Rail network valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Union Pacific from freight-demand and network evidence
into a company-specific valuation object. It tests whether a hard-to-replicate
rail network produces common-owner cash after maintenance and capacity capital,
labor, fuel, safety, service, debt, regulatory obligations, and the proposed
Norfolk Southern combination.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Union Pacific | Freight-network cash after track, bridge, locomotive, car, terminal, technology, and safety renewal; labor, fuel, service, debt, and merger costs | Maintenance/capacity capex, equipment, labor agreements, fuel, safety, terminals, technology, environmental obligations, debt, and diluted common claims | Volume/mix downturn, labor settlement, fuel shock, service failure, regulatory/merger delay, integration leverage, or deferred maintenance | Pricing/productivity improve while service, safety, maintenance, network reliability, capital intensity, leverage, or diluted residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 operating revenue was `$24.5B`, net income `$7.138B`, operating cash
  flow `$9.290B`, and capital investments `$3.791B`; the pre-owner-claim cash
  residual was approximately `$5.499B`.
- FY2025 operating ratio was `59.8%`; dividends were `$3.236B` and repurchases
  `$2.679B`. The operating ratio does not include all capital needed to preserve
  network capacity and service.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$6.9B`; the current plan included approximately `$3.3B`
  of capital spending. Q1/Q2 evidence included pricing and productivity gains,
  but also volume, mix, fuel, service, merger-cost, and integration questions.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile carloads, revenue per car, price/mix, fuel surcharge, labor,
   maintenance, equipment utilization, safety, and service reliability.
2. Separate maintenance, capacity, technology, and merger-related capital;
   lower operating ratio is not proof of owner cash if renewal is deferred.
3. Test customer-specific facilities, terminal access, interchange, commodity
   exposure, and regulatory/common-carrier obligations.
4. Keep Norfolk Southern transaction costs, integration investment, leverage,
   regulatory delay, and service risk separate from the existing railroad.
5. Treat dividends, buybacks, operating ratio, and mechanical OCF-less-capex as
   diagnostic or residual measures until network renewal and common claims are
   joined.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what volume, price/mix, productivity,
operating ratio, maintenance capital, capacity return, merger synergy, and cost
of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether
trade, commodity, labor, fuel, safety, regulation, and leverage shocks can
damage service and cash before the network recovers.

## Promotion boundary

`rail-network-qualified; maintenance-service-and-merger-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from shipper demand through
delivered freight, collection, maintenance/capacity capital, labor/fuel/safety
claims, debt, merger obligations, and diluted common residual. Carloads,
pricing, operating ratio, reported OCF, dividends, and buybacks remain
diagnostic inputs.
