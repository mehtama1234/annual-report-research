# Recurring pest-control routes valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Rollins into a company-specific recurring-route service
valuation and liquidity object. It keeps pest-control and termite service
economics separate from environmental waste, healthcare access, industrial
distribution, and software. The object tests whether customer retention and
route density become common-owner cash after technician labor, customer
acquisition, claims, chemicals, vehicles, receivables, acquisitions, debt,
dividends, and dilution.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rollins | Organic and acquired route-service cash after customer collection, technician/service cost, claims, chemicals, vehicles, branch/technology investment, acquisitions, debt, and diluted common residual | Customer acquisition cost, route density, technician capacity, maintenance capital, acquired customer books, financed receivables, insurance letters of credit, unearned service obligations, debt, and SBC | Lead-cost inflation, customer cancellation, wage/technician shortage, weather, claims, receivable losses, acquisition funding, refinancing, and regulatory/chemical exposure | Recurring revenue, renewal, margin, adjusted EBITDA, OCF, or buybacks rise while organic retention, route productivity, acquired-cohort return, collections, claims, or per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$3.761B`, operating cash flow `$678.1M`, and capital
  expenditures `$28.1M`; management-defined FCF was `$650.0M` before the
  `$309.5M` of acquisitions. The simple residual after capex and acquisitions
  was about `$340.5M`, before debt, dividends, and other owner claims.
- More than 80% of revenue is described as recurring and ancillary, but that
  label does not prove renewal, collection, service frequency, technician
  productivity, or customer lifetime value.
- Acquisition spending was `$309.5M` in 2025, versus `$157.5M` in 2024 and
  `$366.9M` in 2023; the 2025 amount was primarily driven by Saela Pest Control.
  Acquired revenue and margin require a cohort-return bridge.
- Trade and financed receivables used cash; the filing included a `$35.9M`
  expected-credit-loss provision and `$21.5M` of cash used by financed
  receivables. Unearned revenue provided `$2.7M` of operating cash in 2025,
  down from `$7.5M` in 2024.
- Stock-based compensation was `$39.7M` in 2025. Rollins also reported `$82.4M`
  of insurance letters of credit, making claims, retained risk, and legal
  availability part of the liquidity bridge.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate organic route growth from acquisitions, franchise buybacks,
   residential/commercial routes, termite work, and one-time ancillary service.
2. Match new-customer marketing cost to first-year gross profit, cancellation,
   renewal, service frequency, collection, and payback period.
3. Track technician productivity, wage/turnover pressure, drive time,
   callbacks, safety, claims, chemical cost, and service completion rather than
   inferring route density from margin alone.
4. Treat unearned revenue as a future service obligation and reconcile financed
   receivables, aging, write-offs, payment terms, and credit-loss provision.
5. Keep acquisitions, debt, insurance letters of credit, dividends, SBC, and
   repurchases in the common-owner bridge; recurring revenue is not recurring
   owner cash by itself.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what organic route growth, renewal,
customer-acquisition payback, technician productivity, acquired-cohort return,
maintenance capital, claims, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The
Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether household and commercial service
demand, labor availability, weather, lead costs, rates, claims, and acquisition
financing preserve cash conversion in a slower economy.

## Promotion boundary

`recurring-pest-control-qualified; organic-retention-and-acquisition-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from route cohorts to
collection, technician/service cost, customer acquisition cost, retention,
claims, maintenance capital, acquired revenue and return, debt, insurance
obligations, SBC, diluted shares, and common-owner residual. Recurring revenue,
renewal language, OCF, FCF, adjusted EBITDA, dividends, and buybacks remain
diagnostic inputs.
