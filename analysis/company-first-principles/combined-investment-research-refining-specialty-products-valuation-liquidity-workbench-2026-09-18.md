# Refining, renewable fuels, and specialty-products valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves HF Sinclair from downstream-energy evidence into a
company-specific valuation and liquidity perimeter. It keeps refinery spreads,
turnarounds, inventory valuation, renewable credits, specialty products,
midstream, marketing, environmental obligations, the planned specialty
separation, debt, and diluted common-owner cash separate from upstream,
midstream, LNG, and oilfield-services models.

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net income attributable to stockholders was `$579M`, operating cash
  flow `$1.315B`, PP&E spending `$449M`, precious-metals purchases `$72M`, and
  total debt `$2.769B` against `$978M` of cash.
- FY2025 turnaround expenditures were `$437M`; the 2023–2025 range was
  `$413M`–`$556M`. Turnarounds are recurring maintenance cash, not removable
  “one-time” noise.
- 2025 inventory and derivative effects require normalization; Q1 2026 included
  a reported `$604M` inventory valuation benefit excluded from adjusted refinery
  EBITDA.
- The portfolio includes refining, renewable diesel, branded and wholesale
  marketing, midstream, and Lubricants & Specialties. Planned specialty
  separation and Mississauga asset retirement require proceeds, stranded-cost,
  tax, debt, and lost-contribution bridges.
- FY2025 capital returns included `$354M` of treasury-stock purchases and
  `$376M` of dividends. These must be tested against the full turnaround and
  environmental lifecycle.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Rebuild refinery margin from crude charge, product yield, crack spread,
   utilization, energy, freight, turnaround, and outage data. Do not annualize a
   favorable spread quarter.
2. Normalize inventory lower-of-cost-or-market adjustments and derivative marks
   against physical volumes, settlement cash, and future exposure.
3. Show renewable margin before and after credits, cash received versus accrued,
   feedstock, utilization, compliance, and policy dependence.
4. Separate Lubricants & Specialties from refining and bridge its separation,
   debt, stranded overhead, working capital, and lost contribution.
5. Include environmental, safety, closure, insurance, pension, letters of
   credit, debt, dividends, repurchases, and related-party incentives in the
   common-owner bridge.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what mid-cycle crack spread,
utilization, turnaround burden, renewable credit, specialty margin, midstream
throughput, working capital, debt, terminal growth, and cost of capital the
valuation requires. Each segment needs a different normalized cash and multiple.

The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether crude supply, refined-product
demand, regional logistics, rates, policy, environmental requirements, and
inventory funding preserve liquidity through a weak refining cycle. Liquidity
passes only when turnaround, environmental, debt, working-capital, and
common-residual claims remain visible.

## Promotion boundary

`refining-specialty-qualified; spread-turnaround-and-separation-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from segment price, volume,
utilization, and credits to settled physical margin, turnaround and maintenance
cash, inventory, derivatives, specialty separation, environmental obligations,
debt, and diluted common residual. EBITDA, OCF, FCF, dividends, repurchases,
inventory benefits, and renewable credits remain diagnostic inputs.
