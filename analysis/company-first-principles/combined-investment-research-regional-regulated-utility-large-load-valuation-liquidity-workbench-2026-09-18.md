# Regional regulated utility and large-load valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Alliant Energy from rate-base and large-load evidence
into a project-recovery, affordability, financing, and common-return test. It
keeps Alliant's regional regulated franchise distinct from AEP's larger
multi-state large-load buildout and from merchant-power generation.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alliant Energy | Regulated electric/gas rate base plus customer-specific large-load projects after approved recovery, customer contributions, construction, financing, fuel, debt, equity, and dilution | Transmission/distribution, generation, renewables, customer interconnection, construction WIP, storm/environmental work, and reliability | Large-load delay/cancellation, regulatory lag/disallowance, construction inflation, affordability pressure, rates, debt/equity funding, or stranded assets | Contracted load and rate base rise while energized/collected load, earned return, customer funding, affordability, or common per-share return deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 operating cash flow was `$1.169B` against `$2.277B` construction and
  `$206M` other productive-asset spending; the mechanical post-investment
  residual was approximately negative `$1.314B`.
- FY2025 revenue was `$4.362B`, net income `$810M`, common dividends `$521M`,
  cash `$556M`, debt `$12.028B`, and asset-retirement obligations `$704M`.
- Company materials cite roughly `3.4 GW` of contracted data-center load,
  projected demand up about `60%` by 2031 versus 2025, and a `$13.4B` 2026–2029
  capital plan. These are demand and spending indicators, not collected cash.
- Customer-specific rates, advance ratemaking, deposits, contributions,
  interconnection milestones, and termination terms determine whether new load
  pays for the system before shareholders fund it.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconstruct each major project from signed load to credit support,
   interconnection, construction, in-service date, approved recovery, billing,
   collection, and earned return.
2. Separate rate base, CWIP, regulatory assets, customer contributions,
   federal support, debt, equity issuance, and common-owner cash.
3. Model energized, delayed, partial-load, and cancelled customer outcomes;
   test affordability and stranded-capital exposure.
4. Reconcile OCF, construction spending, fuel, receivables, tax credits,
   environmental work, interest, and dividend funding.
5. Test allowed versus earned ROE, regulatory lag, disallowance, and rate-case
   outcomes before capitalizing management plans.
6. Treat signed load, rate base, planned capex, EPS, dividends, and repurchases
   as diagnostic until project recovery and per-share return are evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test values rate base by project, allowed and
earned return, regulatory lag, customer funding, financing, timing, and
affordability. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether essential demand
and data-center load offset rates, construction inflation, debt, equity
dilution, political affordability, and customer-credit risk. Regulated
visibility is not current owner cash when the utility must spend first.

## Promotion boundary

`regional-regulated-utility-qualified; large-load-recovery-and-owner-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from load contracts,
customer credit/contributions, paid construction, in-service assets, approved
recovery, billed/collected revenue, allowed/earned return, debt/equity,
affordability, dividends, and diluted common residual. Load, rate base, planned
capex, EPS, dividends, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
