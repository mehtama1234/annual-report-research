# Regional relationship-bank valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves M&T Bank from annual-report evidence into a company-specific valuation, credit, liquidity, capital, and common-owner residual test. It keeps a regional relationship-deposit and commercial-bank model separate from national transaction banks, consumer-credit platforms, and ordinary bank screens.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/capital denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M&T Bank | Normalized common earnings and return on tangible common equity after deposit funding, credit losses, reserves, securities marks, operating cost, technology, regulatory capital, and dilution | Relationship deposits, treasury/payment systems, branches, technology, fraud/control infrastructure, credit reserves, retained CET1, and personnel | Deposit outflow/beta, CRE refinancing, regional unemployment, securities losses, NIM compression, criticized loans, fraud/cyber, or capital-return pressure | Earnings and buybacks rise while deposit cost, credit migration, liquidity, CET1, control spending, or tangible common value deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net income was approximately `$2.85B`; Q2 2026 net income was approximately `$818M`, taxable-equivalent net interest income `$1.804B`, net interest margin `3.70%`, and CET1 approximately `10.19%`.
- The control point is the operating relationship—household, business, municipal, and institutional deposits linked to lending, treasury, and payment services—not merely the ending loan balance.
- Loan growth must be reconciled to originations, repayments, yield, fees, delinquencies, nonaccruals, criticized/classified loans, charge-offs, reserves, collateral, and required capital.
- NIM is not a steady-state owner-return input until asset yield, deposit beta, funding cost, hedges, purchase-accounting accretion, and normalized rates are separated.
- Dividends and repurchases consume capital that may be needed for loans, reserves, liquidity, technology, or regulatory resilience. EPS from a smaller share count is not proof of better underwriting.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Track noninterest-bearing deposits, deposit beta, uninsured and brokered funding, transaction activity, concentration, and maturity; deposit growth purchased with pricing is not franchise growth.
2. Reconcile provision expense and allowance coverage to loan growth, portfolio mix, criticized assets, delinquency, charge-offs, recoveries, collateral, and macro assumptions.
3. Separate asset yield, funding cost, earning assets, securities duration, hedges, accretion, and seasonal effects before normalizing NIM.
4. Test efficiency gains against headcount, compensation, technology, fraud, compliance, service quality, and deferred control cost.
5. Evaluate dividends and buybacks after stress capital, liquidity, retained CET1, loan growth, and credit-cycle needs; retained capital is part of operating capacity.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style test values normalized common earnings or ROTCE after deposit pricing, credit losses, capital requirements, securities risk, technology, and control costs. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether regional employment, commercial real estate, property refinancing, rates, deposit confidence, and liquidity preserve the relationship franchise through a cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`regional-relationship-bank-qualified; deposit-credit-liquidity-capital-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from deposits and loans to funding cost, credit migration, reserves, liquidity, securities marks, CET1, technology/control investment, and diluted common value. EPS, NIM, deposits, loan growth, ROTCE, dividends, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
