# Regulated water/gas and merger-funding valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Essential Utilities from annual-report evidence into a company-specific rate-recovery, capital-funding, acquisition, merger, and common-owner residual test. It keeps Essential's combined water/wastewater and regulated-gas perimeter, PFAS cash, acquisition cohorts, and pending American Water combination separate from the existing pure regulated-water workbench.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Essential Utilities | Water/wastewater and gas cash after renewal/compliance capital, rate-case lag, customer contributions, collections, acquisitions, PFAS obligations, merger costs, debt, dividends, and dilution | Utility-system renewal, treatment, pipe/gas safety, construction work in progress, PFAS, customer-funded capital, acquired-system repair, merger integration, debt, and equity funding | Rate disallowance/lag, affordability and arrears, PFAS cost, gas working capital/weather, acquisition overpayment, merger approval/integration, rates, refinancing, or dilution | Rate base, connections, and merger scale rise while recovery, collections, earned returns, capital funding, affordability, or diluted common value deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net income was `$616.4M`, OCF `$1.0105B`, capital expenditures `$1.430B`, utility acquisitions `$57M`, and the screen after capital and acquisitions approximately negative `$476.5M`.
- The company invested a record `$1.4B` in infrastructure, while `$46.2M` of PFAS class-action settlement proceeds supported operating cash. Settlement proceeds are not recurring utility revenue.
- Water/wastewater and regulated gas carry different working-capital, weather, commodity-recovery, safety, and affordability burdens; gas purchased-cost recovery is not the same as common margin.
- Essential has pursued a merger with American Water. Approval and announced scale do not prove completed integration, regulatory recovery, customer affordability, financing, or per-share value creation.
- Acquisition cohorts include customer connections/equivalent dwelling units, purchase price, deferred maintenance, environmental obligations, rate approval, and required repair capital; connection growth is not owner cash.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile authorized rate increases, regulatory assets, earned return, disallowances, lag, and customer collections; authorized revenue is not collected cash.
2. Separate water, wastewater, and gas volumes, purchased-gas recovery, inventory/payables, weather, arrears, and customer affordability.
3. Remove PFAS settlement and insurance/recovery proceeds from recurring OCF while separately charging testing, treatment, remediation, legal, and operating obligations.
4. Test acquisitions through purchase price, acquired rate base, connection quality, deferred maintenance, repair capex, approval, bill impact, debt, and incremental cash.
5. Model the merger through approval, financing, integration, stranded cost, customer rates, synergies, capital allocation, equity issuance, and diluted common value.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style test asks what earned return on rate base, customer growth, regulatory recovery, gas/water mix, merger synergies, financing, and cost of capital the equity value requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether customers, regulators, debt markets, public budgets, weather, PFAS obligations, and gas affordability can fund the infrastructure cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`regulated-water-gas-merger-qualified; rate-recovery-capital-funding-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from water/gas capital spending through placed-in-service/rate-base treatment, regulatory recovery, customer payment, required renewal/compliance capital, PFAS obligations, acquisition return, merger cash, debt, and diluted common residual. Revenue, connections, rate-base plans, settlement proceeds, dividends, and OCF-less-capex remain diagnostic inputs.
