# Regulated water infrastructure valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This workbench moves American Water Works from essential-service and rate-base
evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests whether regulated
water renewal, compliance, resilience, and acquisitions create common-owner
returns after the financing gap, customer affordability, regulatory recovery,
environmental obligations, debt, and dilution.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| American Water Works | Regulated water and wastewater cash after renewal capex, rate-case lag, customer contributions, collections, acquisitions, PFAS/lead obligations, debt, dividends, and dilution | Treatment/distribution renewal, construction work in progress, maintenance, compliance, acquired-system remediation, customer-funded capital, regulatory assets, debt, and equity issuance | Rate disallowance or lag, customer arrears/affordability, PFAS or lead-service-line costs, acquisition overpayment, rates, drought, refinancing, or political resistance | Rate-base and connection growth continue while post-capex cash, earned returns, collections, affordability, regulatory recovery, or diluted per-share value weaken |

## Current evidence anchors

- 2025 operating revenue was `$5.140B`, operating cash flow `$2.059B`, and
  capital expenditures `$3.126B`, producing a negative post-capex diagnostic of
  about `$1.067B` before financing and other claims.
- The company added nearly `40,000` customer connections in 2025 and described
  approximately `$46–48B` of planned decade investment. Connection growth and
  planned investment are not equivalent to collected owner cash.
- Year-end long-term debt was approximately `$12.8B`, interest paid about
  `$568M`, dividends about `$633M`, and receivables/unbilled revenues increased
  by approximately `$145M`.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile authorized rate increases, regulatory assets, earned returns,
   disallowances, rate lag, and customer collections; authorized revenue is not
   automatically collected cash.
2. Separate maintenance, compliance, growth, construction work in progress,
   and customer-funded capital; do not treat all capex as discretionary growth
   or all customer contributions as shareholder return.
3. Test each acquisition cohort through purchase price, assumed liabilities,
   connections, repair capex, rate approval, bill impact, debt, and incremental
   cash.
4. Track PFAS, lead service lines, wastewater, drought, resiliency, arrears,
   disconnections, and affordability before relying on rate-base growth.
5. Compare dividends and equity issuance with cash after renewal and compliance
   capital, not only net income or adjusted EPS.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what earned return on rate base,
customer growth, regulatory recovery, capex, financing, and cost of capital the
equity value requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether customers,
regulators, debt markets, and public budgets can fund the infrastructure cycle
when compliance costs and rate increases arrive before full recovery.

## Promotion boundary

`regulated-water-qualified; rate-recovery-and-renewal-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from capital spending through
placed-in-service/rate-base treatment, regulatory recovery, customer payment,
required renewal and compliance capital, debt service, environmental claims,
and diluted common residual. Revenue, connections, rate-base plans, dividends,
and mechanical OCF-less-capex remain diagnostic inputs.
