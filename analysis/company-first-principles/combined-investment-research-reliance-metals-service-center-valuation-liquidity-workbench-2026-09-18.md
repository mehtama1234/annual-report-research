# Reliance metals service-center valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Reliance from current-period metals-distribution evidence into
a company-specific valuation, inventory, processing, acquisition, capital-return,
and common-owner cash test. It does not pool service-center economics with steel
production, fabricated components, or mining.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reliance | Collected processed-metals cash after mill purchases, steel/aluminum pricing, inventory, value-added processing, freight, branch labor, acquisitions, capex, debt, dividends, buybacks, and dilution | Service-center locations, processing equipment, inventory, acquisitions, automation, delivery fleet, working capital, and customer-service density | Metal-price reversal, inventory compression, mill availability, tariffs, project concentration, customer slowdown, acquisition integration, revolver use, or margin compression | Tons and price rise while value-added mix, inventory turns, collections, processing utilization, capex returns, leverage, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Reliance operated approximately `310` locations in `41` U.S. states and `10` countries outside the U.S.; average order size was `$3,120`, about `49%` of orders included value-added processing, and about `40%` were delivered within `24` hours.
- Full-year 2025 sales were `$14.3B`, tons sold `6.4M`, operating cash flow `$831.4M`, capex `$328.9M`, and buybacks `$594.1M`.
- Q2 2026 sales were `$4.63B`, pretax income `$429.8M`, diluted EPS `$6.29`, operating cash flow `$162.2M`, cash `$235.4M`, and total debt `$1.7B`.
- Q2 tons sold rose `10.8%` year over year and average selling price per ton rose `7.8%` sequentially; gross margin was `28.1%`.
- Reliance held about `17%` of the U.S. metals service-center market in 2025, up from about `15%` in 2024.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile tons to mill purchases, metal-price changes, FIFO or other inventory
   effects, value-added processing, customer pricing, and cash collections.
2. Separate plain resale from processing, cut-to-size, delivery, and rapid-turn
   service economics; ton growth alone is not proof of durable spread.
3. Keep inventory, receivables, branch density, fleet, processing equipment,
   acquisitions, capex, debt, dividends, and buybacks in the owner-cash denominator.
4. Test whether government projects or semiconductor/data-center demand are
   recurring, collected, and profitable after working capital and concentration.
5. Reconcile non-GAAP FIFO earnings, inventory gains or losses, acquisition costs,
   revolver use, SBC, taxes, and diluted shares before calling FCF surplus cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what tons, processing mix, metal spread,
inventory turns, share gains, capex, acquisition return, leverage, and cost of
capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether steel
and aluminum prices, tariffs, mill lead times, industrial demand, project awards,
and financing can preserve liquidity through a down-cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`reliance-metals-service-center-qualified; inventory-processing-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from tons and processed orders to
mill purchases, inventory turns, customer collections, processing utilization,
required capex, acquisition return, debt, claims, capital returns, and diluted common
residual. Tons, price, sales, FIFO earnings, adjusted EPS, guidance, and buybacks
remain diagnostic inputs.
