# Restaurant current-period refresh — Q2 2026

This refresh adds McDonald's and Chipotle to the restaurant research lane
without pooling them with the FY2025 CAVA, RBI, Wingstop, and Yum panel.
McDonald's and Chipotle have a June 30, 2026 Q2 filing surface; the other
restaurant cohort has not been forced into that period here.

The residuals are arithmetic screens, not owner cash. For both companies,
repurchases/dividends, maintenance versus growth, leases, taxes, franchisee or
company-store economics, support, working capital, SBC, and dilution must be
joined before any common-owner conclusion.

McDonald's Q2 filing explicitly states that franchised sales are not recorded
as company revenue, but are the basis for franchised revenues and an indicator
of franchisee health; its revenue combines company-operated sales with fees
from franchised restaurants. This is a critical denominator control. Chipotle's
filing instead describes `4,186` owned restaurants and `15` international
partner-operated restaurants, making corporate labor, food, real estate, and
new-unit investment more directly visible in its consolidated cash surface.

## New franchisee-health and revenue-perimeter evidence

McDonald's June 30 filing gives the franchise layer a more precise operating
denominator: 44,016 of 46,028 restaurants were franchised, while 2,012 were
company-owned and operated. Q2 franchised sales were `$34.451B`, but those sales
are not McDonald's reported revenue. They are the economic base on which rent,
royalty, and fee revenue is calculated. The filing also states that franchisees
operate independently and must fund much of the restaurant-level reinvestment.

That creates two separate tests. First, systemwide or franchised-sales growth
must convert into collected franchised rent, royalties, and fees. Second, the
franchisee's ability to fund remodels, technology, labor, and other required
investment must remain healthy; otherwise the franchisor can show attractive
asset-light margins while shifting stress into its customer base. The Q2 filing
supports the first denominator and the existence of the second risk, but does
not provide a same-period franchisee cash-collection, closure, delinquency, or
reinvestment schedule. No normalized owner-cash promotion is made.

This is the restaurant lane's clearest financial-shenanigans control so far:
do not capitalize `$34.451B` of system sales into McDonald's revenue, and do not
interpret a franchised margin improvement as proof that the entire system is
economically healthy. The missing join is franchisee-level cash health to
franchisor collections and required support/reinvestment.

## Why this changes the research route

McDonald's scale and franchising can create a strong royalty/property/control
point, but the current cash surface already shows that shareholder distributions
must be tested against reinvestment and other claims. Chipotle's more
company-operated profile makes corporate labor, food, real estate, and new-unit
capex more directly relevant to cash conversion. These are different burden
configurations, not a basis for a multiple comparison.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans tests

- Reconcile comparable-store sales and restaurant counts to corporate revenue,
  franchise fees, company-store sales, and collections.
- Separate refranchising/property proceeds, advertising funds, leases, and
  franchisee support from recurring franchisor cash.
- Test whether repurchases are funded by post-capex cash or by balance-sheet
  capacity; include SBC and diluted shares.
- Keep new-unit spending, remodels, maintenance, acquisitions, and other
  recurring “one-time” costs in the return hurdle.
