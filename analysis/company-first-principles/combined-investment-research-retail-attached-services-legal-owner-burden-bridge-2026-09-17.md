# Retail attached-services legal-owner and burden bridge

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

Q-06 is not only a question of whether advertising, membership, card,
marketplace, or fulfillment revenue exists. It is a control-point question:

`who owns the receivable or customer asset -> who performs the service -> who pays the costs -> how is the income classified -> what cash reaches the common owner?`

This bridge separates those roles for Target and Walmart and keeps attached-
service revenue out of normalized owner cash until the missing cost and
collection objects are joined.

## Legal-owner and accounting map

| Company / service | Reported surface | Legal owner or payer boundary | Burden carrier visible in filing | Financial-integrity risk if treated as owner cash |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Target / Roundel | H1 advertising revenue `$525M`; FY2025 `$915M` | Target owns the commercial relationship, but Roundel may be recorded in net sales or as a reduction of cost of sales/SG&A depending on arrangement | Integrated advertising team, vendor relationships, and shared company overhead; allocated service cost is not disclosed | Revenue may be netted against costs or classified across lines; applying a merchandise margin can overstate residual cash |
| Target / credit card | H1 credit-card profit sharing `$269M`; FY2025 `$522M` | TD underwrites, funds, owns receivables, controls risk and compliance; Target services accounts and markets the card and receives a share of profits | Target servicing/marketing burden is visible directionally; TD carries receivable funding and credit risk | Treating profit sharing as Target-owned card revenue ignores external asset ownership and cost/risk allocation |
| Target / other services | H1 other revenue `$347M`; FY2025 `$626M` | Includes Target Plus commissions, Shipt, membership, and related items; individual legal/cash routes are not separately disclosed | Fulfillment, technology, labor, working capital, tax, and capital burden are not allocated | Aggregating all other revenue as high-margin platform cash hides unlike payer and cost structures |
| Walmart / membership and other income | H1 `$3.904B`; FY2026 `$6.750B` | Consolidated Walmart/segment presentation; membership fees are deferred and recognized ratably; annual total includes rental/tenant income, recycling, gift-card breakage, and corporate/support items | Segment operating expenses are disclosed in aggregate, not by membership, advertising, rental, or other service | A mixed revenue pool can be mistaken for recurring membership cash or assigned a single service margin |
| Walmart / advertising and ecosystem | Advertising is described as higher-margin; Q2 release reports Walmart U.S. advertising growth and Walmart Connect growth | Certain advertising, fulfillment, and data arrangements can be recorded within eCommerce/net-sales presentation; no standalone legal-entity cash schedule | Fulfillment, technology, labor, and platform costs sit in broader segment expenses | Growth rates and margin commentary are not a cash receipt, incremental margin, or owner return |

## What is actually proven

1. Attached services are part of the customer control point and can influence
   reported mix and gross profit.
2. Target's credit-card economics have a named external owner and funder,
   making the profit-share route materially different from owned lending.
3. Target's Roundel accounting can move the same economic activity across
   revenue and expense presentation, so classification must be tracked before
   calculating margins.
4. Walmart's membership-and-other pool is material but heterogeneous and
   includes items beyond membership fees.
5. Deferred membership recognition is a timing control; it does not disclose
   membership-only cash collection or service profit.

## What remains unproven

- service-level collection and settlement cash;
- labor, fulfillment, technology, and overhead allocation;
- capital and working-capital requirements;
- taxes and legal-entity remittance;
- customer-credit losses or receivable ownership economics;
- common-owner residual after senior claims and dilution.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

These are reconciliation controls, not allegations of misconduct:

- Do not treat a profit-share receipt as owned-loan revenue.
- Do not compare Target Roundel revenue with merchandise gross margin without
  resolving whether the amount is gross or net in the filing presentation.
- Do not treat Walmart membership-and-other income as a pure subscription pool.
- Do not convert service growth rates into cash growth without collection,
  working-capital, tax, capex, and cost allocation.
- Do not subtract consolidated service costs from a service revenue pool unless
  the allocation is disclosed and period matched.
- Do not add attached-service revenue to OCF-less-property as if it were a
  separate owner-cash stream; it is already embedded in consolidated results.

## Promotion decision

Q-06 remains `evidence-insufficient` for standalone attached-service cash.
The bridge upgrades the control-point and legal-owner analysis, not the cash
denominator. The next promotion object is a legal-entity service schedule that
joins collection, allocated costs, capital, tax, and remittance. Until then,
attached-service revenue remains an operating-model observation and a labeled
valuation sensitivity only.
