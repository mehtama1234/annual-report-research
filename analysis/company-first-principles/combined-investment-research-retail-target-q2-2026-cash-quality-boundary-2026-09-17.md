# Target Q2 2026 cash-quality boundary

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Current-period control points

Target's August 1, 2026 Form 10-Q strengthens the retail CA-06/Q-04
denominator, but it does not provide a complete common-owner cash bridge.

| Object | H1 2026 disclosure | Safe interpretation |
|---|---:|---|

| Inventory movement | `$(945)M` use of cash | Growth-supporting working-capital burden, not a normalized margin conclusion |
| Accounts payable movement | `$612M` source of cash | Timing and supplier-finance/merchandise settlement must remain visible |
| Property and equipment | `$2.404B` cash outflow | Mixed property, store, technology and growth spending; maintenance split is not disclosed |

## QoE and owner-cash decision

The filing makes the main financial-shenanigans risk concrete: reported H1
OCF improvement includes payable leverage, while the gross supplier-finance
obligation cannot be treated as a cash-paid settlement amount. Target says that
the financial institutions may offer vendors an optional early-payment choice,
but the amount Target must remit and its payment date do not change; the
remittance date may be up to 120 days from invoice date. The `$994M`
tariff refund is recognized in cost of sales, and the prior `$593M`
interchange settlement gain demonstrates why period comparisons need an
exceptional-item bridge.

The safe screen is therefore:

`$4.519B OCF - $2.404B property spending`

with no further mechanical subtraction for the `$3.2B` supplier-finance
obligation, no annual lease substitution, and no current-period credit-card
profit-sharing margin imputation. A future promotion still requires a dated
supplier-finance settlement or accounts-payable roll-forward that can be
joined to the same-period cash flow. Maintenance/growth capital, payment-date
settlement, service costs, tax/lease cash, and diluted common-owner residual
remain open. The H1 service-revenue lines are collection-side observations,
not one-for-one owner cash, because Target does not disclose their allocated
fulfillment, labor, technology, lease, capex, or tax burden.

The increase from `$3.0B` to `$3.2B` of eligible supplier-finance obligations
is a lifecycle signal, not a cash-flow adjustment. Likewise, the `$1.0B` debt
repayment belongs in the financing and residual-claim map, not as a subtraction
from operating cash or property spending. These controls prevent the same
payable or debt movement from being counted as both working-capital support and
owner-cash burden.

Target's capital-allocation paragraph is also a policy observation, not a cash
allocation schedule: the stated priority to fund profitable growth and maintain
operations/assets does not identify how much of H1 property spending was
replacement, remodel, technology, supply-chain, or expansion capital. Dividends
and the absence of H1 repurchases are visible financing/residual observations,
but they do not convert the unallocated property line into normalized owner
cash.

### Lease and tax classification control — 2026-09-18

The Q2 10-Q's after-tax ROIC reconciliation adds a useful classification
control, but not a cash schedule. Target includes `$3.733B` of total operating
lease liabilities in invested capital, adds back `$172M` of trailing operating-
lease interest to operating income, and subtracts `$1.402B` of trailing income
taxes in its NOPAT calculation. The balance sheet separately reports `$3.332B`
of noncurrent operating-lease liabilities and `$3.587B` of operating-lease
assets. These figures show the lease and tax perimeter used in a return metric;
they do not identify H1 cash-paid lease amounts, cash taxes, lease principal or
interest classification in OCF, or the portion attributable to common-owner
residual cash. They must not be inserted into the H1 denominator as cash
deductions.

The filing's supplemental cash-flow information reports `$84M` of leased
assets obtained in exchange for new operating-lease liabilities and `$18M` of
leased assets obtained in exchange for new finance-lease liabilities. These
are noncash lease-origination disclosures, not H1 lease payments. The filing
also provides no “cash paid” lease or income-tax line that can be matched to
the H1 owner-cash screen. The exact boundary is therefore: lease and tax
burdens are identified, but period-matched cash deductions remain unproven.

The August 14 revolving facility adds a separate liquidity and financing
boundary. The credit agreement names Target Corporation as the borrower,
establishes `$4.0B` of aggregate commitments at the effective date, and allows
loan proceeds to be used for general corporate purposes. Because the agreement
was entered after the August 1 quarter-end and the checked materials do not
show a borrowing or draw, it cannot be inserted into H1 operating cash,
investing cash, or common-owner cash. The prior five-year and 364-day facilities
were terminated in connection with the new agreement, so the event changes the
available legal funding perimeter without proving incremental cash on hand.
The facility's leverage covenant and borrowing conditions are useful liquidity
stress inputs; they are not evidence that Target funded stores, inventory,
supplier finance, taxes, leases, dividends, or buybacks with revolver proceeds.

Status: `Q-04-qualified; Q-05-partial; Q-06-open; CA-06-partial; no-ranking`.
