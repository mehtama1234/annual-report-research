# TJX Q2 FY2027 settlement and capex boundary

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Why this matters

TJX's Q2 FY2027 Form 10-Q adds a current-period quality-of-earnings control
to the retail CA-06 denominator. The filing says six-month operating cash
flow was `$3.3B`, versus `$2.2B` in the prior-year period, and attributes the
increase in part to the receipt of credit-card interchange-fee settlement
cash and a decrease in prepaid expenses related to that receipt. It also
quantifies `$331M` of IEEPA tariff refunds, records `$112M` of incremental
year-end incentive-compensation and bonus accruals tied to the refunds, and
reports a `$219M` net Q2 benefit. These are disclosed drivers of the period
comparison, not normalized recurring owner cash.

The same filing says H1 capital spending primarily covered store improvements
and renovations, new stores, distribution centers, offices, and information
technology. TJX expects full-year FY2027 capital spending of approximately
`$2.2B–$2.3B`, but does not provide a same-period maintenance-versus-growth
dollar split in the reviewed filing. It does, however, disclose `$1.147B` of
cash paid for operating leases during the 26 weeks ended August 1, 2026.

## 2026-09-18 official Q2 source refresh

The official TJX Q2 FY2027 quarterly-results page, Q2 earnings release, and
conference-call route were rechecked on September 18, 2026. The release adds
current-period confirmation that Q2 operating cash flow was `$2.2B`, H1
shareholder returns were `$2.4B`, and the company plans to accelerate store
openings to 4% beginning in FY2028 while raising its long-term store target to
7,500. It repeats the `$331M` IEEPA refund and `$112M` related compensation
accrual, but does not provide a gross bank-receipt/legal-payment schedule for
the credit-card interchange settlement.

The reviewed Q2 materials also report 23 net store additions in the quarter,
but do not allocate H1 property additions between maintenance, replacement,
remodel, infrastructure, and growth. The official refresh therefore
strengthens the growth-direction and temporary-support controls without
changing the promotion result:
`temporary-support-visible; maintenance-open; owner-cash-open`.

## Gross cash received versus legal expenses

The filing separates the accounting gain from the bank-level receipt and legal
payment. The `$419M` non-recurring gain net of legal expenses is therefore not
used as a proxy for gross settlement cash in normalized owner cash.

## Lease and tax cash boundary

The same H1 filing reports `$11.154B` of operating-lease right-of-use assets,
`$1.714B` of current operating-lease liabilities, and `$9.732B` of
noncurrent operating-lease liabilities. Its cash-flow statement separately
shows a `$5M` increase in net operating-lease liabilities, a `$60M` increase
in income-tax recoverables, and a `$190M` increase in income-tax payables; it
does not disclose a cash-paid tax line. The H1 income-tax provision is `$887M`.

These are balance-sheet, provision, and working-capital controls. They do not
replace the separately disclosed `$1.147B` of operating-lease cash paid, and
the `$887M` provision or the net tax-account movements must not be treated as
cash taxes paid without a payment schedule.

## Common-owner and near-term liquidity boundary

The Q2 filing reports `$2.4B` of H1 financing cash outflows, primarily driven
by equity repurchases and dividend payments. TJX paid `$1.4B` to repurchase
and retire `8.9M` shares and paid `$1.0B` of common dividends during the first
six months. These are visible uses of cash and common-owner capital-allocation
claims; they are not additions to normalized owner cash and should not be
counted as a cash-generation measure.

TJX also says its `2.250%` ten-year `$1.0B` notes due September 2026 will be
repaid during the third quarter using operating cash flows. The company
reports `$1.5B` available under its credit facilities as of August 1, 2026.
This creates a dated near-term liquidity and cash-availability test: H1 OCF
cannot be evaluated without keeping the upcoming debt repayment, lease cash,
settlement effects, tariff support, and capital spending in the same perimeter.
The note maturity and stated facility availability do not prove that the
repayment has occurred or that the facility was drawn.

## Annual capex allocation control

TJX's FY2026 Form 10-K provides a company-level FY2027 planning allocation
behind the `$2.2B–$2.3B` full-year capital-spending guide: approximately
`$1.000B` for store renovations, `$992M` for offices and distribution centers
including information-technology systems, and `$222M` for new stores. The
three disclosed amounts sum to `$2.214B`, which is consistent with the rounded
guide. See the [TJX FY2026 Form 10-K capital-spending disclosure](https://investor.tjx.com/node/21241/html).

This improves the annual denominator: new stores are an explicit growth
component, while renovations and offices/distribution centers are disclosed
categories but are not labeled maintenance, replacement, or growth capital.
The allocation is also a forward annual plan, not a same-period allocation of
the `$1.159B` H1 FY2027 property additions. It must therefore be used as an
annual reference and sensitivity boundary, not as a direct H1 owner-cash
deduction or a maintenance-capital proof.

## Decision

This is a `Q-04/Q-05-qualified; CA-06-partial` upgrade. It strengthens the
financial-shenanigans and QoE warning that H1 OCF should not be annualized
without separating settlement and tariff effects, and it now supplies a
period-matched TJX lease-cash deduction candidate. The `$419M` figure is a net
accounting gain (`$470M` gross less `$51M` legal expense), not a separately
proven bank receipt, while the `$331M` refund has a visible `$112M` related
compensation burden; settlement cash/legal-expense and tax bridges remain open.
It does not label all capex
maintenance, and does not rank TJX against Target or Walmart.

The next promotion object remains a settlement cash/legal-expense schedule plus
a company-provided maintenance/growth allocation. Until then,
the correct label is `temporary-support-visible; maintenance-open;
owner-cash-open`.

The cash-flow statement provides a useful stop rule: because the settlement is
included in operating activities but not shown as a separately quantified cash
line, the `$390M` prepaid/other-current-assets movement cannot be substituted
for gross settlement proceeds. A working-capital bridge is required before any
part of H1 OCF can be removed or retained as normalized owner cash.
