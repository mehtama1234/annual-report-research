# Walmart Q2 FY2027 capex and tariff-refund boundary

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Current-period bridge

Walmart's July 31, 2026 Form 10-Q provides the most detailed current-period
retail capex surface in the cohort. It also makes the exceptional tariff
refund and the limits of management-defined free cash flow explicit.

| Object | H1 FY2027 disclosure | Safe interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Tariff refunds | Approximately `$2.9B` received in Q2 and recorded as a reduction of cost of sales | Large temporary gross-margin and cash-support item; not recurring operating economics |
| Refund accounting perimeter | Walmart accounts for tariff refunds as gain contingencies, recognizes recovery when realized or realizable, and says the `$2.9B` represents substantially all refunds requested | Recognition threshold and current-period receipt are explicit | No recurring margin, future refund receivable, or normalized owner cash may be inferred |
| Operating cash flow | `$19.710B` | Reported H1 OCF; subject to tariff support, inventory timing, and other working-capital effects |
| Capital expenditures | `$14.181B` | Gross property/equipment cash burden before any maintenance/growth allocation |
| Supply chain/customer-facing/technology/other | `$7.659B` | Mixed operating and growth investment; not a maintenance estimate |
| Store and club remodels | `$3.623B` | Potential maintenance and growth mix; no clean split disclosed |
| New stores/clubs, expansions and relocations | `$1.087B` | Narrow growth floor; not the full replacement burden |
| Management-defined free cash flow | `$5.529B` | OCF less property/equipment payments; Walmart says it excludes debt service and acquisitions |
| Segment comparability | Walmart updated certain corporate-overhead allocations beginning February 2026 and revised prior periods; it warns segment operating results are not directly comparable across formats and channels | Perimeter and denominator changes are disclosed | Segment margin, service revenue, and capital productivity cannot be pooled mechanically with TJX or Target |

## QoE and denominator decision

The filing says the H1 OCF increase was primarily higher operating cash,
partly offset by inventory-receipt timing, while H1 FCF declined because
capital expenditures increased `$2.8B` to support omnichannel growth. The
`$2.9B` tariff refund was partly invested in price and cost-mitigation
initiatives, so it should not be treated as a free cash distribution or
recurring gross-margin improvement.

The safe screen is:

`$19.710B OCF - $14.181B property/equipment payments = $5.529B reported FCF`

That screen is useful but not common-owner cash. It does not deduct debt
service, acquisitions, operating-lease cash, or an evidence-backed
maintenance reserve. It also cannot assign a single margin to Walmart+,
advertising, rental, fulfillment, and other income.

Status: `Q-04-qualified; Q-05-qualified-with-mixed-capex; Q-06-open;
CA-06-partial; no-ranking`.

The refund is therefore a realized gain-contingency event, not a recurring
price or procurement advantage. The segment-allocation revision is a separate
comparability control: even restated periods remain Walmart-specific and do
not establish a common service-cost or owner-cash denominator across retailers.
