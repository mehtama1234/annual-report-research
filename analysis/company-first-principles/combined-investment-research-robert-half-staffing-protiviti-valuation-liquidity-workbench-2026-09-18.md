# Robert Half staffing and Protiviti valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Robert Half from current-period evidence into a company-
specific valuation, labor-market, staffing-utilization, permanent-placement,
consulting, receivables, and common-owner cash test. It does not pool contract
talent, permanent placement, and Protiviti consulting into a generic services
multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Robert Half | Collected staffing and consulting cash after contractor wages, recruiter labor, placement costs, utilization, client collections, Protiviti delivery, technology, restructuring, debt, dividends, and dilution | Talent acquisition, branch and recruiter capacity, technology, training, Protiviti professionals, client development, working capital, and restructuring | White-collar hiring slowdown, bill-rate pressure, consultant bench, permanent-placement volatility, client concentration, receivable aging, recession, debt, or dilution | Sequential hiring improvement continues while billable utilization, placement fees, wage spread, Protiviti margin, collections, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Full-year 2025 revenue was `$5.379B`, net income `$133M`, and operating cash flow `$320M`; dividends declared per share reached `$2.36`.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$1.336B`, net income `$26M`, and diluted EPS `$0.26`; first-half revenue was `$2.637B`.
- Q2 revenue declined `2%` reported and `3%` adjusted year over year, while talent solutions delivered a third consecutive quarter of sequential adjusted growth and permanent placement grew `2.5%` year over year on an adjusted basis.
- The model has three distinct blocks: contract talent solutions, permanent placement talent solutions, and Protiviti consulting.
- Protiviti provides a partial countercycle through governance, risk, compliance, finance, technology, operations, and internal audit work rather than pure hiring volume.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile staffing revenue to hours, bill rates, contractor wages, utilization,
   placement fees, client acceptance, receivable aging, and cash collections.
2. Separate contract talent, permanent placement, and Protiviti by recurring demand,
   labor burden, project margin, client concentration, and cash timing.
3. Keep recruiters, branch capacity, technology, training, consultant bench,
   client development, working capital, restructuring, and dividends in the
   owner-cash denominator.
4. Test whether sequential improvement reflects real hiring demand or timing,
   seasonality, pricing, mix, and easy comparisons; new placements are not cash
   until clients pay.
5. Reconcile adjusted measures, severance or restructuring, contract assets,
   receivables, debt, SBC, dividends, and diluted shares before treating OCF as
   durable common-owner surplus.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what hiring growth, bill rates, wage
spread, consultant utilization, permanent-placement conversion, Protiviti margin,
reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style
stress test asks whether employer confidence, recession, automation, labor supply,
receivables, and enterprise compliance demand can preserve liquidity through a labor
cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`robert-half-staffing-protiviti-qualified; labor-cycle-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from hours, placements, and
consulting projects to client acceptance, collections, wage and recruiter cost,
utilization, required reinvestment, debt, claims, dividends, and diluted common
residual. Revenue, sequential growth, placements, adjusted EPS, guidance, and
dividends remain diagnostic inputs.
