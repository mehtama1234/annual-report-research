# Royalty and streaming valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Franco-Nevada from commodity and royalty evidence into a
company-specific contract, counterparty, acquisition, depletion, and liquidity
test. It distinguishes a royalty/stream portfolio from an operating miner or
producer. GEOs, commodity prices, adjusted EBITDA, operating cash, debt-free
status, dividends, and available capital are not normalized common-owner cash
by themselves.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Franco-Nevada | Probability-weighted cash from existing, development-stage, and optionality royalties/streams after operator production, payment, tax, portfolio, and common claims | Royalty and stream acquisitions, due diligence, operator/development funding where applicable, portfolio monitoring, taxes, and dividends | Gold/oil price decline, operator failure, permitting/political disruption, delayed asset start, Cobre Panama impairment, poor acquisition pricing, or counterparty concentration | GEOs or AUM grow while operator output, cash receipts, asset availability, acquisition returns, or per-share value deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was approximately `$1.560B`, operating cash `$1.494B`,
  productive-asset purchases `$2M`, and the screening residual `$1.492B` before
  royalty/stream acquisitions, investments, and dividends.
- FY2025 GEOs sold were `519K`, with approximately `85%` of revenue from
  precious metals; diversification does not eliminate gold-price dependence.
- FY2025 cash was about `$671M` and the company was debt-free at year-end, with
  approximately `$3.1B` of available capital. Available capital is optionality,
  not earned return.
- Q1 FY2026 operating cash was `$520.4M` and available capital `$3.4B`; the
  portfolio still requires asset-level production, payment, and acquisition-
  return evidence.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile profit and operating cash to commodity price, GEO mix, asset sales,
   settlements, taxes, receivables, and operator payment timing.
2. Read royalty percentages, stream purchase prices, inflation provisions,
   payment priority, audit rights, operator reporting, and termination terms.
3. Track each acquisition's cash paid, expected and actual GEOs, start date,
   operator capital, reserve life, impairments, and return on invested capital.
4. Rebuild revenue and cash by gold, silver, copper-linked streams, oil, gas,
   NGL, iron ore, and other commodities; diversification must change cash risk.
5. Separate producing, development-stage, exploration, and optionality assets;
   operator reserves are not guaranteed production.
6. Test Cobre Panama and other restricted assets for recoverability, timing,
   political/permitting risk, and whether the market is assigning unsupported
   value.
7. Charge acquisitions, taxes, dividends, investments, and diluted shares
   before treating the low direct-capex burden as owner cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what mid-cycle commodity prices,
probability-weighted production, asset starts, contract terms, acquisition
returns, and cost of capital the equity price requires. The Lyn Alden-style
stress test asks whether commodity cycles, operator financing, permitting,
political restrictions, and scarce attractive deals can be absorbed without
destroying portfolio value.

## Promotion boundary

`royalty-streaming-qualified; asset-production-and-acquisition-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period joins from each royalty/stream cohort to operator
production, GEOs sold, payment, commodity cash, asset availability, required
reinvestment, acquisition return, tax, dividends, and diluted common residual.
GEO growth, commodity prices, adjusted EBITDA, OCF, debt-free status, and
available capital remain diagnostic inputs.
