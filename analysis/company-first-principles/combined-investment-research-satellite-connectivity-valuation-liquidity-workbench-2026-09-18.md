# Satellite connectivity valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Iridium Communications into a company-specific satellite-
connectivity constellation and recurring-service valuation/liquidity object. It
keeps orbital network economics separate from terrestrial networking,
distributed power, merchant generation, and enterprise software. The object
tests service renewal after constellation replacement, ground infrastructure,
spectrum, launches, subscribers, government contracts, engineering/equipment
mix, debt, SBC, and diluted common claims.

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$871.7M`, service revenue `$634.0M`, OCF `$400.1M`, and
  capex `$100.3M`, leaving a listed-investment screen of about `$299.8M` before
  debt principal, constellation replacement reserve, and other claims.
- Billable subscribers reached `2.537M` at FY2025 year-end and `2.627M` by Q2
  2026. Subscriber growth must be separated by IoT, government, aviation,
  maritime, traffic, pricing, churn, and service margin.
- Iridium operates a 66-satellite low-earth-orbit constellation plus ground
  infrastructure, spectrum, and service integration. Low physical capex in one
  year is not a complete replacement denominator.
- Net debt was approximately `$1.678B` at FY2025 year-end; interest paid was
  `$90.2M`; SBC net of capitalized amounts was `$51.6M`.
- Engineering/support revenue was `$156.6M` and subscriber equipment revenue
  `$81.1M` in FY2025. These are strategically useful but more project- or
  hardware-dependent than recurring service revenue.
- The pending Rocket Lab transaction introduces closing, financing, launch,
  manufacturing, governance, integration, and future-reporting risk; synergies
  remain unearned until the combined cash structure is disclosed.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Build a constellation reserve from satellite age, replacement date, launch,
   insurance, ground-system, and service-continuity requirements.
2. Split service, government, engineering, equipment, and direct-to-device
   economics; subscriber counts are not equivalent to recurring owner cash.
3. Reconcile deferred revenue, billings, renewal, customer advances, refunds,
   partner share, and service delivery.
4. Test government concentration, appropriations, contract renewal,
   termination rights, payment timing, and mission-readiness cost.
5. Keep debt, SBC, dividends, buybacks, Rocket Lab integration, and satellite
   replacement claims in the common-owner bridge.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what service revenue per device,
subscriber retention, government renewal, direct-to-device margin, constellation
reserve, launch cost, debt cost, and cost of capital the valuation requires.
The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether remote connectivity demand,
government budgets, shipping/aviation, launch access, rates, spectrum rules,
and terrestrial competition preserve liquidity through a network replacement
cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`satellite-connectivity-qualified; constellation-replacement-and-service-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from subscriber and
government cohorts to service collection, churn, partner share, equipment and
engineering delivery, constellation/ground replacement, launch/insurance,
spectrum, debt, diluted shares, and common-owner residual. Subscribers,
service revenue, backlog, OEBITDA, OCF, FCF, dividends, and buybacks remain
diagnostic inputs.
