# Scholastic children’s reading and school-channel valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Scholastic from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests Book Fairs, Book Clubs, trade publishing, education solutions, entertainment, franchise IP, school and family relationships, sale-leasebacks, capital returns, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not pool Scholastic with institutional research publishers, newspapers, or general media platforms.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scholastic | Children’s reading habit and school-channel cash after Book Fairs, trade titles, education funding, franchise rights, entertainment, inventory, sale-leaseback effects, debt, and dilution | New titles, author and franchise rights, school-channel logistics, digital and entertainment production, inventory, working capital, facilities, debt, and shares | School and district budgets, title timing, retailer/channel risk, education weakness, sale-leaseback dependence, tender offers, leverage, or franchise underperformance | Book Fair and franchise revenue rise while school participation, title sell-through, collection, content reinvestment, recurring cash conversion, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2026 revenue was about `$1.582B`, adjusted EBITDA about `$151.5M`, and pro forma adjusted EBITDA about `$132.4M`.
- FY2026 Q4 Book Fairs revenue rose `5%` to about `$186.6M`, while Education revenue declined `13%` to about `$109.2M` and Entertainment revenue rose `42%` to about `$21.0M`.
- FY2026 Q3 sale-leaseback transactions helped move net cash to about `$90.6M` from net debt of about `$189.4M`; the company also announced a planned `$200M` tender offer.
- Q2 highlighted Book Fairs, Trade franchises, school channels, a Scholastic streaming app, and animated-series development, while Education remained exposed to funding volatility.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate Book Fair participation and sell-through, Trade title timing, Education funding, Entertainment production, and sale-leaseback proceeds.
2. Test whether franchise engagement across books, schools, families, and screens converts into recurring collected cash or episodic title and event revenue.
3. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA and FCF to inventory, content rights, production, school-channel costs, facility transactions, debt repayment, tender offers, and dilution.
4. Keep operating cash from the children’s platform separate from real-estate monetization and capital-return financing.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what Book Fair growth, franchise sell-through, school participation, Education recovery, Entertainment return, reinvestment, leverage, and cost of capital are embedded in the price. The stress test asks whether family reading habits and trusted school channels withstand education-budget volatility, retailer pressure, title timing, streaming competition, rates, and sale-leaseback dependence.

## Promotion boundary

`scholastic-qualified; school-channel-and-franchise-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from books, fairs, schools, and screens to collection, sell-through, content replacement, production return, facility economics, debt funding, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, Book Fair growth, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, guidance, sale-leaseback proceeds, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
