# Security trust and trust-recovery valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves CrowdStrike from recurring software growth into a
company-specific trust, retention, incident-cost, cloud-margin, deferred-
revenue, and dilution test. It focuses on the security-control point rather
than generic enterprise software. ARR, subscription revenue, adjusted free cash
flow, operating cash, customer prepayments, and repurchases are not normalized
common-owner cash by themselves.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CrowdStrike | Common cash after renewed subscription relationships, module expansion, cloud/AI delivery, R&D, support, incident remediation, legal/insurance, acquisitions, SBC, and dilution | Detection quality, telemetry/storage/inference, research, sales/support, incident response, customer remediation, internal software, acquisitions, and employee replacement | Trust event, renewal/concession pressure, cloud/AI cost inflation, customer budget cuts, deferred-revenue obligation, legal/remediation cash, or dilution | ARR grows while gross retention, module usage, gross margin, deferred-revenue conversion, incident trust, cash per diluted share, or SBC-adjusted residual deteriorates |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2026 revenue was `$4.81B`, subscription revenue `$4.56B`, operating cash
  `$1.61B`, and reported FCF `$1.24B`; FY2027 Q1 revenue was `$1.39B`, ARR
  `$5.51B`, operating cash `$590.9M`, and FCF `$468.5M`.
- The three-year diagnostic residual after property/equipment, capitalized
  software, and acquisitions rose from `$690M` to `$859M`, while SBC was
  `$649M`, `$861M`, and `$1.097B`; per-share cash therefore requires a real
  dilution bridge.
- Subscription revenue was approximately `95%` of FY2026 revenue. Deferred
  revenue is a future service obligation, not current-period profit.
- The July 19 Incident makes testing, quality, customer remediation, legal,
  insurance, support, and trust-recovery costs part of normalized durability.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile ARR to renewal, gross retention, net retention, module use,
   recognized revenue, deferred revenue, receivables, and collected cash.
2. Separate subscription growth from price, customer count, module expansion,
   concessions, Falcon Flex timing, and one-time contract effects.
3. Test subscription gross margin after telemetry, storage, cloud hosting, and
   AI-processing costs—not software gross margin in isolation.
4. Treat SBC as an economic claim through diluted shares, employee awards,
   tax-withholding repurchases, and discretionary buybacks.
5. Reconcile GAAP and adjusted measures to incident remediation, legal,
   insurance, support, restructuring, acquisition, and recurring quality costs.
6. Track identity, cloud, data, SIEM, and AI module adoption with support and
   sales cost per customer.
7. Charge future service obligations, R&D, acquisitions, debt, taxes, and
   dilution before promoting residual cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what renewal, module expansion,
pricing, gross margin, cloud/AI cost, R&D, incident burden, and cost of capital
the equity price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether security
budgets, customer liquidity, trust after an incident, cloud dependence, and
future service obligations can be funded through a more selective cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`security-trust-qualified; retention-incident-and-per-share-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period joins from ARR and subscription contracts to
renewal, module usage, deferred-revenue delivery, collection, gross margin,
incident/remediation cost, cloud/AI burden, SBC, acquisitions, and diluted
common residual. ARR, subscription revenue, adjusted FCF, OCF, and repurchases
remain diagnostic inputs.
