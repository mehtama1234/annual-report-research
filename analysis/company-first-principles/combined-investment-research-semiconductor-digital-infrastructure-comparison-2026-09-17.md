# Semiconductor process control vs digital infrastructure real estate

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This comparison tests three AI/physical-capacity control points without
treating any as an investment ranking: KLA's process-control workflow,
Equinix's interconnection-density platform, and Digital Realty's hyperscale
capacity and powered-land platform.

## Comparable cash surface

| Company | Control point | Latest annual revenue | OCF | Cash screen | Senior burden visible |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| KLA FY2026 | Inspection, metrology, yield management, installed service | `$13.579B` | `$4.143B` | OCF less PP&E: `$3.767B` | `$5.95B` senior notes, `$310M` SBC, `$2.290B` repurchases, customer/cycle/export risk |
| Equinix FY2025 | Interconnection density, powered capacity, network adjacency | `$9.217B` | `$3.911B` | OCF less recurring capex: `$3.627B`; OCF less total property capex, real-estate acquisitions, and business acquisitions: approximately `$(1.645B)` | `$21.4B` debt, `$1.5B` operating leases, `$498M` SBC, power/JV/growth-capex risk |
| Digital Realty FY2025 | Hyperscale powered capacity, data-center leases, connectivity | `$6.113B` | `$2.412B` | Core FFO approximately `$2.56B`; 2026 development capex guide `$3.25–$3.75B` | `$18.4B` debt, 4.9x net debt/adjusted EBITDA, recurring capex and leasing costs, JV/private-capital and dilution risk |

The screens are not comparable owner cash. KLA's PP&E screen may omit the R&D,
technical labor, inventory, and service reinvestment needed to preserve its
chokepoint. Equinix's recurring-capex screen may omit the growth capacity
required to preserve network relevance; its full-investment bridge shows the
capital intensity of expansion.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans contrast

| Diagnostic | KLA | Equinix | Digital Realty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue timing | Product acceptance and performance-obligation allocation; deferred system/service revenue | Recurring MRR, bookings, lease-up, and capacity delivery timing | Bookings and backlog convert only after construction, energization, and lease commencement |
| Working capital | Receivables and inventory uses can rise with shipments; deferred revenue can support or lag cash | Construction, power, rent, lease-up, and project funding can consume cash before stabilization | Development-in-process assets, leasing costs, partner contributions, and delayed rent can consume cash before stabilization |
| Capitalization | R&D, service organization, warranty, installation, and technical capability are the economic maintenance burden | Recurring capex, total property capex, acquisitions, JV/VIE capital, and non-GAAP AFFO adjustments | Development capex, leasing commissions, recurring capex, fund/JV capital, and Core FFO/AFFO adjustments |
| Owner claims | SBC, buybacks, senior notes, purchase commitments, and customer concentration | SBC, debt, leases, dividends, equity issuance, JV guarantees, and NCI | Debt, preferred claims, dividends, ATM issuance, JV/fund obligations, and dilution |
| Primary breaker | Service growth decouples from installed-base economics or cycle normalization overwhelms margin/cash | AFFO rises while per-share cash, project returns, or power/lease economics deteriorate | Backlog and bookings rise while lease commencement, stabilized yield, or FFO/share after financing and dilution weakens |

Neither company has a fraud finding. These are reconciliation prompts under the
Sloan/Schilit/Beneish-style discipline used throughout the project.

## Valuation expectation surface

- **KLA:** the existing September 2026 screen uses approximately `$238.4B` of
  equity value and `$3.767B` reported cash-after-PP&E, roughly `63x`. The bear,
  base, and bull normalized-cash cases are `$2.5B`, `$3.8B`, and `$5.0B`; the
  screen requires durable service growth and cycle resilience, not merely AI
  demand.
- **Equinix:** the existing September 2026 screen uses approximately `$102.0B`
  of equity value, about `27x` FY2025 AFFO and `28x` the recurring-capex screen.
  The required test is whether AFFO survives total growth capital, power,
  debt, leases, JV burden, SBC, and stabilization risk.
- **Digital Realty:** the existing September 2026 screen uses approximately
  `$68.2B` of equity value against approximately `$2.56B` of FY2025 Core FFO,
  or about `26.7x`. The key assumption is that `$3.25–$3.75B` of 2026
  development capex earns the stated stabilized yields without excessive
  leverage, dilution, power, or lease-up delay.

These are Damodaran-style expectation screens, not intrinsic-value conclusions.

## Lyn Alden-style stress transmission

| Stress | KLA transmission | Equinix transmission | Digital Realty transmission |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Higher rates / tighter credit | Customer fab capex defers; valuation multiple compresses; debt and commitments remain | Refinancing, development funding, lease-up hurdle, and equity cost rise | Refinancing and development hurdle rise; backlog may commence later |
| Inflation / power scarcity | Tool input, labor, tariff, and warranty costs pressure margin | Utility cost, construction, rent, and power availability pressure project returns | Power, construction, leasing, and energization costs pressure stabilized yields |
| Liquidity reversal | Inventory and receivables absorb cash as customers slow orders | Capacity spend and JV funding precede stabilized tenant cash | Development-in-process and partner-funded growth precede rent and cash |
| Customer concentration | Large foundry/customer decisions and export controls matter | Hyperscaler/customer concentration and alternative architectures matter | Hyperscaler deployment timing, backlog conversion, and customer bargaining power matter |

## Decision posture

KLA is the cleaner first control-point case because its reported annual cash
surface is positive after PP&E and service revenue is visible. Equinix is the
stronger interconnection-density reinvestment stress case, while Digital Realty
is the clearer hyperscale backlog-and-development-yield test. None is
promotable to a ranking until maintenance/growth economics, owner claims,
lease-up, and per-share cash are reconciled.

The [KLA FY2026 QoE forensic refresh](combined-investment-research-kla-fy2026-qoe-forensic-refresh-2026-09-17.md)
sharpens the first case. KLA's FY2026 filing shows a 19% single-customer
concentration, 87% international revenue, a `$1.1B` combined increase in
receivables and inventory, `$515M` of receivables factoring, `$5.97B` of
purchase commitments, and `$310M` of SBC against `$2.29B` of repurchases. The
`$3.767B` OCF-less-PP&E surface therefore remains a diagnostic denominator,
not normalized owner cash.

The [Equinix AFFO-to-owner-cash boundary](combined-investment-research-equinix-affo-owner-cash-boundary-2026-09-17.md)
now makes the REIT denominator explicit: `$3.627B` of OCF less management-
defined recurring capex sits beside a `(1.645)B` visible full-investment bridge
after total property capex and acquisitions. The difference is not a verdict
against Equinix; it is the required test of growth-capital returns, JV/VIE
burdens, lease-up, power, debt, and per-share dilution.

The [Digital Realty Core FFO-to-owner-cash boundary](combined-investment-research-digital-realty-ffo-owner-cash-boundary-2026-09-17.md)
completes the same diagnostic for the hyperscale-capacity counterpoint. FY2025
operating cash flow was `$2.412B`, development-in-process assets were about
`$4.977B`, and 2026 development capex was guided to `$3.25B–$3.75B`; backlog
and Core FFO therefore require a commencement, yield, partner-capital, debt,
and dilution bridge before they can be treated as common-owner cash.
