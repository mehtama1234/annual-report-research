# Copper, smelting, and by-product mine-system valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Southern Copper from current-period mining evidence into a company-specific valuation, copper, by-product, grade, recovery, smelting, jurisdiction, expansion, capex, and owner-cash test. It treats Southern Copper as a concentrated Peru/Mexico copper system with downstream processing, not as generic copper exposure.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Southern Copper | Collected copper, molybdenum, zinc, silver, and by-product mine and smelter cash after ore extraction, grade/recovery, processing, sustaining and expansion capex, royalties, taxes, jurisdiction, debt, dividends, and dilution | Mine development, concentrators, smelters, refineries, tailings, exploration, water and environmental infrastructure, Buenavista/Toquepala/Cuajone/La Caridad expansion, working capital, and community obligations | Copper and by-product prices, grade and recovery, Peruvian/Mexican permitting and community, water, smelter performance, labor, expansion delays, debt, and dilution | Production, price, adjusted EBITDA, net cash cost, FCF, dividends, or buybacks rise while grade, recovery, by-product credits, smelter output, permitting, expansion capex, jurisdiction, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net sales were `$13.420B`, net income `$4.3349B`, adjusted EBITDA `$7.8224B`, operating cash flow `$4.7521B`, and copper production `954,270` tons.
- At December 31, 2025, cash was `$4.3046B` and long-term debt `$6.7507B`.
- Q2 `2026` net sales were `$4.289B`, net income `$1.670B`, adjusted EBITDA `$2.856B`, and first-half operating cash flow `$3.683B`; cash at June 30 was `$5.665B` and long-term debt `$7.9944B`.
- Q2 copper production fell `3.5%` sequentially to `230,662` tonnes; Peruvian production fell `12.0%`, mainly from lower ore grades. Silver, zinc, and molybdenum production also declined.
- Q1 `2026` net sales were `$4.2514B`, adjusted EBITDA `$2.7128B`, operating cash flow `$1.6945B`, and capital investments `$441.9M`; net cash cost was `$(0.11)` per pound after by-product credits.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile payable copper, concentrates, smelter and refinery output, realized metal prices, by-product credits, inventory, receivables, and cash collection; production is not cash.
2. Separate Peru, Mexico, mine, smelter, refinery, copper, molybdenum, zinc, silver, and expansion cohorts; grade, recovery, ownership, and jurisdiction differ.
3. Test expansion and processing investments through capex, construction, commissioning, grade, recovery, by-product economics, permitting, water, community, and return on invested capital.
4. Keep adjusted EBITDA, net cash cost, price leverage, FCF, dividends, buybacks, and by-product credits separate from recurring collected common-owner cash.
5. Reconcile sustaining and growth capex, smelter maintenance, royalties, taxes, environmental and closure obligations, debt, interest, SBC, and diluted shares.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what copper and by-product prices, grades, recoveries, production, smelter utilization, reserve conversion, expansion timing, capex, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether commodity cycles, Peru/Mexico policy, community and water claims, labor, inflation, rates, and leverage preserve cash conversion.

## Promotion boundary

`southern-copper-copper-system-qualified; grade-recovery-smelter-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from mine and processing cohorts to payable metal, shipment, realized price, by-product credit, collection, sustaining and expansion capex, permitting and community obligations, debt service, taxes, dividends, and diluted common residual. Production, price, adjusted EBITDA, net cash cost, FCF, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
