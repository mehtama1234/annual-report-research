# Specialty construction contractors valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This workbench moves EMCOR, Comfort Systems USA, Quanta Services, MasTec, and
Primoris Services from infrastructure-demand evidence into company-specific
project-execution valuation objects. It keeps electrical/mechanical service,
utility construction, communications, and renewable/heavy-civil work separate.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EMCOR / Comfort Systems | Specialty electrical/mechanical execution cash after labor, materials, contract liabilities, retainage, acquisitions, and dilution | Craft labor, prefabrication, equipment, working capital, claims, acquisitions, SBC, and project support | Labor scarcity, customer concentration, bid error, supplier failure, contract-claim delay, acquisition integration, or margin compression | Revenue/RPO and contract liabilities rise while project margin, retainage collection, labor productivity, or diluted cash weakens |
| Quanta Services | Utility and infrastructure field-execution cash after percentage-of-cost estimates, contract assets, acquisitions, equipment, and claims | Craft labor, equipment, working capital, contract assets, maintenance capex, acquisitions, debt, and SBC | Cost-to-complete error, large acquisition integration, customer delay, supplier/labor shortage, project claim, or leverage | RPO and revenue rise while contract assets, cost estimates, acquisition cash, margin, or per-share residual deteriorate |
| MasTec / Primoris | Multi-lane infrastructure-project cash after fixed-price execution, renewables/power mix, working capital, losses, acquisitions, debt, and dilution | Equipment, labor, permits, contract assets, warranty/claims, project remediation, acquisitions, and debt | Challenged renewable or communications project, policy timing, customer concentration, margin reset, cash shortfall, or refinancing | Backlog rises while project losses, contract assets, receivables, capex, debt, or common cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- EMCOR Q2 2026 revenue was `$5.15B`, remaining performance obligations
  `$17.14B`, and contract liabilities approximately `$2.61B`; FY2025 operating
  cash flow was `$1.302B` and noncontrolling interests approximately `$1.273B`.
- Comfort Systems FY2025 revenue was approximately `$7.0B`, operating cash flow
  `$1.186B`, and Q2 2026 backlog `$14.06B`; Q2 cash included large billings
  and contract-liability effects that require normalization.
- Quanta FY2025 revenue was `$28.480B`, 2026 remaining performance obligations
  `$33.55B`, FY2025 operating cash flow `$2.230B`, and cash acquisitions about
  `$3.052B`; percentage-of-cost estimates covered `63.8%` of 2025 revenue.
- MasTec Q2 2026 backlog was `$21.391B`; six-month operating cash flow was about
  `$120.3M` against `$188.3M` of capex, with debt obligations `$2.753B`.
- Primoris FY2025 revenue was `$7.575B`, OCF `$470.4M`, capex `$129.9M`, and
  backlog `$11.9B`; Q2 2026 backlog reached `$13.9B` while the company recorded
  a `$24.2M` net loss and `$11.4M` adjusted EBITDA amid challenged renewable
  projects.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Trace backlog/RPO through labor, materials, measured progress, billing,
   retainage, acceptance, collection, warranty, and final margin.
2. Reconcile contract assets, contract liabilities, customer advances,
   receivables, and working-capital benefits; advances can fund cash before
   profitable completion.
3. Review cost-to-complete changes, loss provisions, claims, change orders,
   subcontractor exposure, and percentage-of-cost recognition.
4. Keep Quanta, EMCOR, and Comfort acquisition cash separate from recurring
   project cash; test acquired-company margin and cash return.
5. Treat backlog, adjusted EBITDA, OCF-less-capex, dividends, and repurchases
   as diagnostic or residual claims until project cash is collectible.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what backlog conversion, project
margin, labor productivity, acquisition return, reinvestment, and cost of
capital the equity value requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether
labor, material, rates, policy, customer capex, project claims, and working
capital can absorb an execution shock before backlog becomes cash.

## Promotion boundary

`specialty-contractor-qualified; project-margin-and-collection-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from funded work through
execution, billing, retainage/acceptance, collection, required equipment and
labor reinvestment, claims, debt, legal-entity availability, and diluted common
residual. Backlog, RPO, awards, contract liabilities, adjusted EBITDA, and
reported OCF remain diagnostic inputs.
