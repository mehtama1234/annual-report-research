# Stagwell AI agency platform valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Stagwell from current-period evidence into a company-specific
valuation, agency-retention, AI-workflow, acquisition, client-concentration, and
common-owner cash test. It does not pool agency labor, digital transformation,
advocacy, data, and software claims into a generic advertising multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stagwell | Collected net-revenue cash after pass-through costs, client retention, agency labor, digital transformation, AI tools, data, acquisitions, working capital, debt, and dilution | Agency talent, The Machine, Marketing Cloud, ID/data tools, technology, client acquisition, integration, and product development | Advertising slowdown, client loss, advocacy-cycle reversal, AI commoditization, acquisition integration, receivable aging, debt, or dilution | Net revenue and new business rise while retention, billable utilization, software conversion, collection, acquisition return, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Q2 2026 revenue was `$786M`, net revenue `$632M`, organic revenue growth `10%`, organic net-revenue growth `5%`, adjusted EBITDA `$109M`, and adjusted EBITDA margin `17%`.
- Q2 digital-transformation net revenue was `$107M`, up `18%` organically; quarterly net new business reached a record `$171M`.
- Q1 2026 revenue was `$704M`, net revenue `$585M`, adjusted EBITDA `$90M`, and net new business `$141M`.
- Full-year 2025 revenue was `$2.909B`, net revenue `$2.428B`, adjusted EBITDA `$422M`, adjusted EPS `$0.83`, and free cash flow `$187M`.
- The Marketing Cloud reportedly grew net revenue `230%` year over year, while advocacy and political-cycle exposure create a separate normalization issue.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile gross revenue to net revenue, pass-through media costs, client billing,
   receivables, retention, project acceptance, and cash collections.
2. Separate digital transformation, agency services, advocacy, data, The Machine,
   and Marketing Cloud by recurring revenue, labor burden, software cost, and client
   concentration.
3. Keep talent, engineers, AI/product development, technology, acquisitions,
   integration, working capital, debt, and SBC in the owner-cash denominator.
4. Normalize political and advocacy-cycle demand; record new business is not the
   same as durable revenue or collected margin.
5. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA, acquisition accounting, earn-outs, restructuring,
   contract assets, debt, buybacks, SBC, and diluted shares before treating FCF as
   common-owner surplus.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what net-revenue growth, client retention,
AI/software conversion, margin, acquisition return, reinvestment rate, leverage,
and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks
whether advertising budgets, AI substitution, political cycles, labor, receivables,
and refinancing can preserve liquidity through a communications cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`stagwell-ai-agency-platform-qualified; client-retention-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from client work and software/data
products to net collections, retention, labor and product cost, acquisition return,
debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, net new business, adjusted EBITDA,
guidance, AI claims, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
