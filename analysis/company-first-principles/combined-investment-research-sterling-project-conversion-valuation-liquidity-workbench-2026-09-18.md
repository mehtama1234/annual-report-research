# Sterling project-conversion valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench tests Sterling Infrastructure as a project-conversion platform:
signed work must become measured progress, billing, collection, margin, and
common-owner cash after cost-to-complete, claims, acquisitions, working
capital, capex, earn-outs, debt, leases, and dilution. Signed backlog is not
treated as funded cash or as a terminal-growth rate.

## Decision matrix

| Model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sterling | Signed and collected project cash after cost-to-complete, claims, acquisition cohorts, JV effects, debt, and dilution | Contract assets/retainage, labor/materials, fleet/property, project working capital, CEC/Stone Ridge integration, and equipment | Backlog delay, estimate revisions, customer concentration, acquisition funding, rates, cost inflation, or revolver use | Signed backlog grows while project collections, margins, acquired-cohort return, or diluted residual deteriorate |

## H1 2026 filed cash and backlog perimeter

The Q2 filing reports H1 operating cash flow of `$328.021M`, capital
expenditures of `$69.646M`, acquisition cash of `$139.985M`, and earn-out
payments of `$7.767M`:

```text
operating cash flow                         $328.021M
  - company capital expenditures             $69.646M
  - cash acquisitions                         $139.985M
  - earn-out payments                           $7.767M
  = residual after visible growth uses       $110.623M
```

The residual is a cash screen, not normalized owner cash. It is before any
maintenance/growth allocation, interest, taxes, leases, NCI, claims, project
rework, and unlisted integration costs. Adding `$27.406M` of non-cash stock
consideration would produce an economic-claim sensitivity of approximately
`$83.217M`, but that is not a GAAP cash-flow line.

The June 30 contract perimeter is also material: accounts receivable were
`$770.671M`, contract assets `$156.295M`, contract liabilities `$802.601M`,
and accounts payable `$316.019M`. Contract-asset retainage increased to
`$72.8M` from `$40.7M`; contract-liability retainage increased to `$197.1M`
from `$147.6M`. H1 revenue included `$138.9M` of changes in prior-period
contract estimates versus `$72.5M` in the prior-year period. These are project
accounting and future-collection controls, not cash return proof.

Sterling's Q2 presentation separates `$4.33B` of signed backlog from roughly
`$5.6B` of combined signed and unsigned backlog and more than `$1.4B` of future
phases. CEC and Stone Ridge account for approximately `$1.32B` of signed
backlog and `$1.24B` of unsigned awards; acquisition revenue was approximately
`21.5%` of Q2 revenue. The base valuation must use signed backlog only, keep
unsigned awards and future phases as bounded cases, and separate acquired from
organic conversion.

## Acquisition return hurdle

CEC's disclosed perimeter includes approximately `$442.937M` cash consideration,
`$79.458M` stock consideration, a possible earn-out, approximately `$227.8M`
of identifiable intangibles, and approximately `$308.9M` of goodwill. The
2025 reference contribution was approximately `$19.4M` after intangible
amortization and is not standalone free cash flow. A post-close cohort must
join revenue, gross profit, collections, contract assets/liabilities,
integration cost, earn-outs, capex, and diluted shares before the acquisition
receives terminal-growth or return credit.

## Valuation expectation screen — September 18, 2026

The market snapshot reports approximately `$16.153B` of equity value at
`$518.68` per share. Dividing that by twice the H1 mechanical OCF-less-capex
screen (`$516.750M`) gives approximately `31.3x`. This is a price-implied
expectation diagnostic, not a DCF, normalized FCF multiple, or investment
ranking. The price requires signed-backlog conversion, project-margin
durability, collection, successful CEC/Stone Ridge returns, and controlled
dilution after the visible growth uses.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

Use three cases:

- **Contracted:** signed backlog only, with conservative margin, billing, and
  collection assumptions.
- **Conversion:** a bounded share of unsigned awards, with acquired cohorts
  separately modeled and funded.
- **Stress:** delayed awards/future phases, lower project margins, slower
  collections, higher labor/material costs, rates, and full replacement capital.

Stress transmission runs through customer and hyperscaler capex, project
finance, rates, labor/material inflation, power availability, Sterling's
working-capital needs, and revolver burden. Backlog and adjusted EBITDA remain
diagnostics until cash/claim joins are evidenced.

## Promotion boundary

`project-conversion-qualified; acquisition-return-open; owner-cash-open;
no-ranking`

The next promotion object is a post-close CEC/Stone Ridge cohort schedule
connecting signed backlog, revenue, gross-margin estimate changes, billings,
contract assets/liabilities, retainage, collections, integration costs,
earn-outs, capex, debt, leases, and diluted shares.
