# Stryker–Inari post-close return bridge — pass 2 — 2026-09-17

## Decision

The Q2 2026 filing adds a post-close cash and claims perimeter around the
Inari acquisition, but it does not disclose a clean Inari-only revenue, margin,
or cash series. The correct upgrade is therefore `post-close perimeter
strengthened; acquisition return unproven; no-ranking`.

## Acquisition-to-current-period bridge

| Bridge object | Filed observation | Safe interpretation | Missing promotion evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Inari consideration, FY2025 | `$4.810B` net of cash acquired | named acquisition burden | acquired revenue, margin, and incremental return |
| Prior H1 investing cash context | approximately `$4.240B` including Inari and property purchases | major acquisition cash event was concentrated in comparison period | cohort cash generation after close |
| Current H1 investing cash use | approximately `$824M` | current property/investing burden is visible; no second Inari purchase payment reported | what portion is Inari maintenance/growth capital |
| Current H1 operating cash | `$1.842B` versus `$1.361B` prior year | consolidated operating cash improved | Inari contribution and working-capital allocation |
| Acquisition/integration costs | `$44M` | post-close integration burden remains visible | cash paid, recurrence, and Inari attribution |
| Structural/special charges | `$213M` | transformation and facility/product claims are material | recurrence and cash-paid schedule |
| Recall-related matters | `$52M` | quality claims are senior economic burdens | Inari versus legacy allocation and cash settlement |
| Medical-device regulation costs | `$1M` | regulatory remediation is visible | product/cohort allocation and persistence |
| Goodwill/other impairment | `$1M` | current impairment signal is small but observable | future impairment or platform economics |
| Debt maturity repayment | `$1.000B` | senior financing claim consumed current cash | financing allocation to acquisition and future refinancing |
| Ending cash and short-term investments | `$3.476B` versus `$4.100B` year-end | liquidity declined after current-period uses | distributable cash after claims and buffer |

The absence of a repeat acquisition payment is a timing observation, not a
return measurement. The required return sequence remains:

`Inari acquired revenue -> gross/operating margin -> amortization and integration -> quality/regulatory/recall cash -> working capital and R&D -> financing and dilution -> incremental post-close cash return`

## Quality-of-earnings and financial-shenanigans controls

1. Track acquisition/integration, structural, recall, and regulatory charges
   across 2025–2027. A charge described as special but repeated is an economic
   cost of the platform.
2. Keep the `$1.860B` identified intangible balance, `$3.191B` goodwill, and
   approximately 13-year weighted-average amortization period from the FY2025
   purchase allocation in the GAAP return context. Do not subtract goodwill as
   if it were current cash, but do not let adjusted EPS erase the return hurdle.
3. Reconcile acquired inventory and receivables to sales, margin, and cash
   settlement. Consolidated Stryker working capital cannot be assigned to Inari
   without a cohort or segment bridge.
4. Treat recalls, quality-system work, labeling, product exits, and regulatory
   remediation as senior claims before assigning post-close cash to owners.
5. Join debt repayment, dividends, SBC, diluted shares, and any future buybacks
   to the post-Inari common-owner residual.

## Promotion gate and thesis breaker

Promotion requires a same-entity Inari/Vascular cohort table containing revenue,
gross and operating margin, amortization, integration cash, inventory,
receivables, quality claims, R&D/sales burden, debt, dilution, and incremental
cash generation. Until that table exists, consolidated OCF and Vascular growth
are only perimeter evidence.

The thesis breaker is activated if Inari-attributable growth and adjusted EPS
rise while incremental post-close cash fails to cover maintenance/growth capital,
recurring quality claims, financing, and dilution. This is a falsifiable filing
test, not an allegation of accounting misconduct.
