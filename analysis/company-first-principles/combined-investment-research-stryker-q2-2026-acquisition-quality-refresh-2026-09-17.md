# Stryker Q2 2026 acquisition and quality-of-earnings refresh

## Purpose

This pass updates the medical-devices lane from a FY2025 acquisition-heavy
screen to a current post-Inari observation. It does not claim that Inari has
been proved value-creating or value-destroying. It separates the acquisition
cash event from subsequent operating, integration, structural, recall,
regulatory, financing, and liquidity effects.

## Post-acquisition cash sequence

Stryker's six-month 2025 investing cash flow included the Inari acquisition and
property purchases, totaling approximately `$4.240B`. In the six months ended
June 30, 2026, investing cash use was approximately `$824M`; the filing says
the prior-year period included cash paid to acquire Inari, while the 2026
period included property purchases. This is useful timing evidence: the large
FY2025 acquisition outflow is not repeated in the current half-year, but the
absence of a second purchase payment is not evidence that the acquired platform
has earned an adequate return.

Six-month operating cash flow increased to `$1.842B` from `$1.361B`. That
improvement must be read alongside the current-period charges and liquidity
changes below, not isolated as proof of post-close economics.

## Current-period adjustment and claim surface

Stryker's six-month 2026 adjusted operating-income reconciliation includes:

- `$44M` of acquisition and integration-related costs;
- `$213M` of structural optimization and other special charges;
- `$1M` of goodwill and other impairments;
- `$1M` of medical-device-regulation costs; and
- `$52M` of recall-related matters.

The filing separately identifies the nature of these categories, including
manufacturing integration, employee retention and workforce reductions,
facility closure or transfer, product-line exits, quality-system and labeling
work, inventory or asset write-offs, and recall estimates. These are not all
recurring in the accounting sense, but a repeated pattern would be an economic
cost of operating and expanding the clinical platform. The proper test is
cash-paid claims and remediation over several periods, not a one-period
adjusted EPS add-back.

## Financing and liquidity waterfall

Six-month 2026 financing cash use was approximately `$1.605B`, primarily driven
by repayment of `$1.000B` of maturing unsecured notes and dividend payments.
Stryker did not repurchase shares under its authorized program during the first
half of 2026; `$1.033B` remained authorized at June 30. Cash, cash equivalents,
short-term investments, and marketable securities were `$3.476B`, down from
`$4.100B` at December 31, 2025. The filing says Stryker expects to support
short-term liquidity through operating cash, commercial paper, and existing
credit lines.

This creates a better owner-cash control than simply subtracting FY2025
acquisition cash from OCF. The current waterfall is:

`post-Inari operating cash -> integration/quality/structural claims -> PP&E and working capital -> $1.0B debt maturity and dividends -> liquidity -> potential future repurchases`

The company has investment-grade access, but refinancing capacity is still an
owner claim when acquisition programs and remediation costs are ongoing.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans tests

1. **Acquisition cohort test:** identify Inari's post-close revenue, margin,
   inventory, amortization, integration cash, and incremental OCF; do not use
   the disappearance of the acquisition payment as the return measure.
2. **Adjustment recurrence test:** track acquisition/integration, structural,
   recall, regulatory, and product-line-exit charges across 2025–2027. A charge
   labeled special but repeated is economically recurring.
3. **Working-capital test:** reconcile procedure and product growth with
   inventory, receivables, payables, and cash settlement; avoid subtracting
   operating working-capital uses twice from OCF.
4. **Debt and dilution test:** connect debt repayment, future refinancing,
   SBC, diluted shares, dividends, and any resumed buybacks to common-owner
   cash.
5. **Clinical-quality test:** treat recalls, regulatory remediation, and
   cybersecurity or quality disruptions as senior claims before assigning cash
   to owners.

These are forensic controls, not findings of accounting misconduct.

## Interpretation

The Q2 2026 filing strengthens Stryker's medical-device control-point case but
does not close the central return question. The company has moved from a
large acquisition payment in the comparison period to a post-close period with
higher operating cash, ongoing integration and structural charges, recall and
regulatory costs, a `$1.000B` debt maturity repayment, and lower liquidity.

Current status:

`Stryker-post-Inari-period-updated; acquisition-return-open; quality-claims-visible; no-ranking`

The next decisive object remains a same-entity Inari cohort bridge connecting
acquired revenue and margin to integration cash, amortization, inventory,
quality claims, debt, and incremental cash generation.

The [Inari post-close return bridge pass 2](combined-investment-research-stryker-inari-acquisition-cohort-pass-2-2026-09-17.md)
now organizes the current-period cash and claim perimeter. It strengthens the
return test but does not assign consolidated Vascular results to Inari.
