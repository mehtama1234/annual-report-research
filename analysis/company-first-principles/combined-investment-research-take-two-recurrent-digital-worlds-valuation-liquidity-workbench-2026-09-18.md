# Take-Two recurrent digital worlds valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Take-Two Interactive from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests owned game worlds, full-game launches, recurrent consumer spending, net bookings, GTA, NBA 2K, Zynga mobile, player retention, development capitalization, licensing, deferred revenue, merger or acquisition costs, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not pool Take-Two with EA, Roblox, DraftKings, or physical franchise owners.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Take-Two | Owned digital-world cash after full-game launches, recurrent consumer spending, sports and mobile loops, player retention, development, platform fees, licensing, deferred revenue, debt, and dilution | Game development, live content, servers, user acquisition, marketing, licensing, acquisitions, deferred costs, debt, and shares | Tentpole delay, franchise concentration, mobile churn, development overspend, platform fees, licensing, merger disruption, or leverage | Net bookings and recurrent spend rise while player retention, franchise return, deferred-revenue quality, development cash conversion, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2026 net bookings rose `19%` to `$6.72B`, GAAP revenue rose `18%` to `$6.66B`, recurrent consumer spending rose `17%` to `78%` of bookings, and mobile contributed roughly half of bookings.
- Q1 FY2027 net bookings were `$1.39B`; recurrent consumer spending represented `84%` of bookings and revenue; mobile bookings were about `$762.3M`.
- FY2026 NBA 2K and Zynga supported recurring participation while GTA remained the dominant future launch catalyst; management reiterated `$8.0B-$8.2B` FY2027 bookings guidance.
- The model combines owned worlds, annualized sports, mobile habit loops, and launch-event concentration, making release execution and recurrent-spend durability separate tests.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate full-game launches, recurrent consumer spending, mobile, sports, catalog, platform fees, licensing, and deferred revenue.
2. Test player retention, content cadence, user acquisition, development return, and franchise concentration before treating net bookings as recurring owner cash.
3. Reconcile operating cash flow to capitalized development, live content, marketing, royalties, deferred revenue, acquisitions, debt, and dilution.
4. Keep GTA or any tentpole launch catalyst separate from the underlying NBA, mobile, and catalog participation engine.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what recurrent-spend growth, player retention, GTA launch, NBA 2K and mobile durability, development return, leverage, and cost of capital are embedded in the valuation. The stress test asks whether player fatigue, competition, launch delays, platform economics, licensing, regulation, and household entertainment budgets preserve collected cash.

## Promotion boundary

`take-two-qualified; recurrent-spend-and-tentpole-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from worlds and players to collection, retention, content and development return, deferred revenue, licensing, debt, claims, funding, and diluted common residual. Net bookings, revenue, recurrent spending, adjusted EBITDA, guidance, and capital returns remain diagnostic inputs.
