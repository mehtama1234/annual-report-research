# Talent advisory and professional services valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Korn Ferry into a company-specific talent-advisory,
executive-search, interim-capacity, consulting, digital, and RPO
valuation/liquidity object. It keeps professional-services economics separate
from enterprise workflow software, technical inspection, healthcare staffing,
and recurring route services. The object tests fee visibility after delivery
labor, utilization, collection, technology, acquisitions, debt, taxes, SBC,
and diluted common claims.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment/denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Korn Ferry | Solution-specific professional-fee cash after assignment delivery, professional compensation, interim labor, consultant utilization, RPO/Digital implementation, collection, acquisitions, debt, taxes, SBC, and diluted common residual | Executive Search, Professional Search & Interim, Consulting, Digital, and RPO mix; remaining fees, client retention, headcount, utilization, platform investment, acquisitions, contract assets, debt, and SBC | Hiring slowdown, delayed consulting, lower utilization, interim labor margin pressure, client concentration, receivable delay, acquisition funding, rates, and delivery/talent loss | Remaining fees, bookings, adjusted EBITDA, fee revenue, or OCF rise while delivery cost, utilization, collection, mix, acquisition return, or per-share residual deteriorates |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 OCF was `$364.4M`; PP&E spending `$62.5M`; Trilogy acquisition cash
  `$44.4M`; and SBC `$48.0M`, leaving an initial owner-cash screen of about
  `$209.5M` after PP&E, acquisition cash, and SBC.
- FY2026 fee revenue was approximately `$2.9B`; Q4 fee revenue `$759.8M`, net
  income `$73.1M`, and adjusted EBITDA `$129.5M`. Remaining fees were about
  `$1.9B`, but they require delivery, margin, and collection conversion.
- Q4 solution mix included Executive Search `$242.0M`, Professional Search &
  Interim `$149.1M`, and RPO `$97.6M`. Interim work carries more direct labor
  and lower margins than permanent placement, so mix matters.
- Trilogy closed for approximately `$44.4M` and expanded digital interim talent;
  Salo and ICS add related capacity. Acquired revenue does not prove acquired
  cash return until labor, integration, earn-outs, debt, and dilution are joined.
- Contract visibility is not owner cash: remaining fees can shift toward
  lower-margin interim or RPO delivery and remain subject to completion,
  acceptance, staffing, and collection.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile fee revenue to remaining fees, bookings, assignments, delivery,
   completion, collection, and contract duration.
2. Separate Search, Professional Search & Interim, Consulting, Digital, and RPO
   revenue, margin, labor burden, utilization, and renewal.
3. Track professional compensation, consultant headcount, interim labor,
   subcontractors, revenue per professional, and client concentration.
4. Treat platform retirement, restructuring, integration, SBC, and recurring
   capability costs as common-owner claims rather than automatic adjustments.
5. Keep remaining fees, adjusted EBITDA, acquisitions, dividends, repurchases,
   and diluted shares separate from collected cash after delivery.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what solution-level fee growth,
retention, utilization, compensation burden, interim margin, Digital/RPO
renewal, acquisition return, debt cost, and cost of capital the price requires.
The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether hiring, consulting, institutional
adaptation, rates, client liquidity, professional labor, and technology spend
preserve cash when discretionary budgets contract.

## Promotion boundary

`talent-advisory-professional-services-qualified; utilization-mix-and-collection-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from remaining fees and
assignments to delivery, utilization, professional/interim labor cost,
collection, solution-level renewal, acquisition return, debt, taxes, SBC,
diluted shares, and common-owner residual. Remaining fees, bookings, adjusted
EBITDA, fee revenue, OCF, FCF, dividends, and buybacks remain diagnostic
inputs.
