# Accessible-luxury brand-house valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Tapestry from current-period consumer evidence into a company-specific valuation, brand-investment, inventory, channel, and owner-cash test. It treats Coach and Kate Spade as accessible-luxury relationship businesses, not as interchangeable apparel wholesale brands.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tapestry | Collected brand-house cash after full-price sell-through, direct-to-consumer acquisition, inventory/markdowns, store and digital experience, design, marketing, licensing, tariffs, international operations, restructuring, debt, and dilution | Product design, leather and materials, stores, digital, customer acquisition, brand marketing, inventory, distribution, data/analytics, and international infrastructure | Brand heat, fashion/product failure, discounting, wholesale/channel inventory, China/FX, tariff and duty, customer-acquisition cost, strategic simplification, and dilution | Revenue, gross margin, customer acquisition, EPS, and free cash flow rise while full-price sell-through, repeat behavior, inventory, markdowns, brand investment, channel quality, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was about `$7.0B`, gross margin `75%`, operating margin `20%`, EPS `$5.10`, and free cash flow about `$1.3B`.
- Q3 FY2026 revenue was `$1.9B`, up `21%` reported and `19%` in constant currency; Coach grew `31%` reported and `29%` in constant currency; direct-to-consumer revenue grew `23%` in constant currency; and tariff/duty headwind was about `180` basis points.
- Q2 FY2026 revenue was `$2.5B`, up `14%`; gross margin was `75.5%`; inventory was `$896M` versus `$937M` a year earlier; and management raised full-year outlook.
- Q1 FY2026 acquired more than `2.2M` global customers, about `35%` of them Gen Z; Coach revenue grew `22%`; and Europe grew `32%` in constant currency.
- The post-Stuart Weitzman simplification and Coach-led mix are central to the current evidence; Kate Spade, direct channels, digital, North America, Greater China, and Europe remain separate demand and investment tests.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile reported revenue to full-price sell-through, units, average selling price, direct versus wholesale, returns, promotions, markdowns, and inventory aging; gross margin alone is not brand quality.
2. Separate Coach, Kate Spade, and disposed/restructured brand economics; Coach momentum cannot automatically support the whole house.
3. Test customer acquisition and Gen Z growth through repeat purchase, cohort contribution, retention, channel migration, marketing cost, and discount behavior.
4. Keep inventory reduction separate from sustainable demand, supplier commitments, store closures, restructuring, and working-capital timing.
5. Reconcile tariffs, duties, China/FX, licensing, product and store investment, Stuart Weitzman simplification, debt, and any acquisition/disposal claims.
6. Treat non-GAAP EPS, buybacks, dividends, and brand valuation as residual claims requiring inventory, brand investment, tax, debt, and diluted-share validation.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what full-price sell-through, repeat rate, customer-acquisition cost, store productivity, digital conversion, international growth, gross margin, inventory turns, brand reinvestment, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether status and self-expression spending survives household, currency, China, tariff, travel, and employment shocks while stores, inventory, design, marketing, and channel relationships still require funding.

## Promotion boundary

`tapestry-accessible-luxury-qualified; brand-sellthrough-inventory-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from brand demand to full-price sell-through, collection, repeat behavior, channel inventory, markdowns, store/digital investment, marketing, tariffs/FX, restructuring, debt, and diluted common residual. Revenue, gross margin, customer additions, EPS, free cash flow, Coach growth, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
