# Target Q2 2026 capex growth-driver boundary

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Finding

Target's public 2026 strategy and Q2 earnings release provide a stronger
qualitative classification of the current property-spending program, but not
a complete maintenance-versus-growth cash allocation.

Target's May 2026 strategy announcement says the company plans to invest
approximately `$5B` in 2026, including more than `130` full-store remodels,
more than `30` new stores, and investments in supply chain and technology. Its
Q2 2026 earnings release says quarterly capital expenditures were `$1.4B`, up
`27%` year over year, driven primarily by increased investments in store
remodels and new stores.

The company therefore supplies a current-period growth-driver signal:

```text
H1 property spending of $2.404B
  -> includes a material remodel/new-store program
  -> also includes supply chain, technology, and other property spending
  -> maintenance/replacement dollars remain unallocated
```

The Q2 release also reports `17` new stores in the quarter and `24` year to
date. These counts corroborate active expansion, but store count is not a
cash amount and cannot be used to estimate the cost of the new-store program.

## Q2 management-call boundary

On the Q2 earnings call, Target's CFO said approximately `$2.4B` had been
deployed in capital expenditures through the first half, up nearly `30%` year
over year, reflecting intentional incremental investments in new stores, full-
store remodels, supply chain and technology capabilities. Management also
said the company continued to expect approximately `$5B` of full-year CapEx.
This is stronger language than a generic capex guide because it explicitly
characterizes the H1 spending as incremental investment, but it remains a
management description rather than a project-level cash allocation.

The same call reports growth in attached or adjacent revenue streams: Roundel
gross billings nearly `20%`, Target Plus marketplace GMV more than `40%`, and
Target Circle 360 membership revenue more than `40%` year over year. The call
does not allocate the labor, fulfillment, technology, capital, tax, or other
costs supporting those streams. A targeted search of the transcript found no
maintenance, lease, or Shipt payment schedule.

## Interpretation boundary

This evidence supports a growth floor only in the directional sense that the
H1 property line is not pure maintenance. It does **not** support any of the
following calculations:

- treating the entire `$2.404B` H1 property line as growth;
- treating remodels as wholly growth or wholly maintenance;
- subtracting the `$5B` annual plan from H1 OCF as a period-matched burden;
- deriving a maintenance amount by subtracting reported new-store counts; or
- promoting the remaining property spending to normalized common-owner cash.

The correct status is:

`Target capex growth-driver boundary strengthened; maintenance allocation and common-owner residual remain unproven`

## Required next evidence

Promotion still requires a project-level or management capital schedule that
allocates the `$2.404B` H1 property cash spending among remodels, new stores,
supply chain, technology, replacement, and other categories with a period and
legal-entity perimeter. Lease/tax cash, attached-service cost/collection,
supplier-finance settlement, debt, dilution, and common-owner claims remain
separate gates.
