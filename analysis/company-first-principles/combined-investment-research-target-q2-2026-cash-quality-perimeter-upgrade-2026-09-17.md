# Target Q2 2026 cash-quality perimeter upgrade

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This is a current-period CA-06 upgrade for Target Corporation. It refreshes
the H1 cash, support, supplier-finance, capital-allocation, lease-liability,
and diluted-share perimeter without promoting reported free cash flow to
normalized common-owner cash.

## Same-period evidence

For the six months ended August 1, 2026, Target reported `$4.519B` of cash
provided by operating activities and `$2.404B` of expenditures for property and
equipment. The resulting derived cash-after-property screen is `$2.115B`.
The same cash-flow statement reports `$1.070B` of long-term-debt reductions,
`$1.034B` of dividends, `$3M` of share repurchases, and `$154M` of share-based
compensation expense.

The working-capital bridge includes `$945M` of inventory cash use and `$612M`
of accounts-payable support. Target separately reports `$3.2B` of vendor
obligations eligible for early payment under supplier-finance programs, up from
`$3.0B` at January 31, 2026. The obligation balance is not itself a cash-flow
adjustment.

Target's H1 net-sales table also provides the attached-service collection
perimeter: advertising revenue was `$525M`, credit-card profit sharing was
`$269M`, and other revenue was `$347M`. The other category includes Target
Plus commissions, Target Circle 360 and Shipt membership/service revenue,
rental income, and miscellaneous sources. These are reported same-entity
revenue objects, but Target does not allocate their fulfillment, labor,
technology, lease, capex, or tax burden, so they are not normalized owner cash.

## Temporary support and burden perimeter

Target recognized `$994M` of IEEPA tariff refunds in the six-month period as a
reduction to cost of sales. It states that the refund increased reported
operating income and after-tax EPS; this is a material current-period support
item and cannot be capitalized into recurring margin without future evidence.

The filing reports `$3.733B` of operating lease liabilities at August 1, 2026
and `$835M` of H1 tax provision. It does not provide a matched H1 cash-paid
operating-lease or cash-tax line in the current filing. These are claim and
provision markers, not additional deductions from OCF.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans controls

1. Separate tariff-refund support from underlying gross-margin and operating-
   income improvement; Target reports 19% H1 operating-income growth excluding
   the refund versus 94% including it.
2. Do not subtract the `$945M` inventory use or `$612M` payable support again
   from reported OCF; those are already reflected in the cash-flow statement.
3. Do not treat the `$3.2B` supplier-finance obligation balance as cash without
   a confirmed-invoice settlement roll-forward.
4. Keep property spending, lease liabilities, tax provision, debt reduction,
   dividends, repurchases, SBC, and service-level cash allocation distinct.
5. Treat the reported `$2.115B` screen as a denominator starting point, not
   owner cash or a valuation multiple.

## Valuation and liquidity implication

The Damodaran case requires a tariff-normalized gross-profit and cash range,
maintenance-versus-growth capex classification, and a period-matched common-
owner claim bridge. The Lyn-Alden-style stress removes tariff support, reverses
inventory/payable timing, raises lease and financing costs, and tests liquidity
through continued store, fulfillment, and digital investment.

## Decision

`Target-H1-August-2026-cash-quality-strengthened; normalized-owner-cash-open; CA-06-partial`
