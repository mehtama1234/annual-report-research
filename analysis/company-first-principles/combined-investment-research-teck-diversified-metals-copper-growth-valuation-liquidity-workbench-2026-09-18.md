# Teck diversified metals and copper-growth valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Teck Resources from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It separates copper growth, QB operating reliability, zinc and smelting support, steelmaking-coal cash flow, critical-minerals processing, merger execution, mine life, capex, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not pool Teck with pure copper, iron-ore, precious-metals, or steel producers.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Teck Resources | Diversified metals cash after copper grades and production, QB reliability, zinc and smelting economics, coal, sustaining/growth capex, merger execution, royalties, debt, and dilution | Mine development, sustaining capital, tailings and closure, processing, exploration, permitting, merger integration, debt, and shares | Copper-price reversal, QB instability, project delay, permitting, smelter economics, merger conditions, environmental claims, or capital overspend | Copper production and EBITDA rise while unit costs, QB recovery, mine-life replacement, smelting cash, merger economics, net cash, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- 2025 revenue was `$10.756B`, adjusted EBITDA was `$4.3B`, cash flow from operations was `$1.5B`, and year-end liquidity was `$9.1B`, including `$5.0B` of cash.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$3.605B`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.193B`, cash flow from operations was `$1.7B`, and liquidity reached `$10.3B` including `$6.1B` of cash.
- Q2 copper production was `135,900` tonnes, up `25%` year over year; the copper segment generated `$1.8B` of gross profit before depreciation and amortization, while zinc generated `$353M`.
- Trail Operations and possible germanium, gallium, and antimony expansion add a processing and critical-minerals object beyond mine output; the Anglo Teck merger adds a separate legal, regulatory, and integration perimeter.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile copper tonnes, grades, recoveries, realized prices, treatment charges, by-products, unit costs, and QB production before treating EBITDA as sustainable.
2. Separate spot-price windfalls, inventory draws, merger claims, asset values, and strategic optionality from recurring same-entity collected cash.
3. Test sustaining capital, tailings, closure, exploration, permitting, and project-development burdens against reported free cash flow and net cash.
4. Keep zinc, Trail processing, steelmaking coal, and critical-minerals optionality separate from the copper valuation denominator.
5. Reconcile merger consideration, regulatory conditions, synergies, funding, and dilution before promoting the Anglo Teck combination.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what copper production, prices, QB reliability, mine life, zinc and smelting contribution, capex, merger synergies, and cost of capital are embedded in the valuation. The stress test asks whether copper scarcity survives commodity volatility, permitting, geopolitical risk, processing bottlenecks, environmental obligations, merger execution, and the replacement burden of finite reserves.

## Promotion boundary

`teck-qualified; copper-qb-and-merger-return-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from tonnes and realized prices to collection, unit costs, mine replacement, sustaining capital, processing, claims, merger funding, debt, and diluted common residual. Production, EBITDA, OCF, FCF, dividends, guidance, and merger synergies remain diagnostic inputs.
