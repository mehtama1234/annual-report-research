# Teva generics and innovative-pharma transition valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Teva Pharmaceutical Industries from broad healthcare
evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It separates global
generics, biosimilars, innovative brands, long-acting and differentiated assets,
manufacturing, R&D, regulatory and patent exposure, transformation savings,
settlement claims, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not treat product
revenue, prescriptions, adjusted EBITDA, guidance, operating cash flow, free
cash flow, or pipeline milestones as normalized owner cash.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Teva | Global drug-access and portfolio-transition cash after generic and branded collections, price and volume erosion, manufacturing, R&D, regulatory compliance, patent and litigation claims, transformation, debt, and dilution | Plants and quality systems, complex generics and biosimilars, branded trials, launch and commercial spend, regulatory filings, working capital, settlements, debt, and SBC replacement | Generic price erosion, patent loss, failed launch, manufacturing or quality event, litigation settlement, access or reimbursement change, debt refinancing, FX, or dilution | Innovative brands grow while generic cash, manufacturing utilization, R&D return, patent life, litigation burden, leverage, or diluted per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Q2 2026 revenue was about `$4.1B`; management raised outlook for the three
  key innovative brands to roughly `$3.7B` combined for 2026.
- Q1 2026 revenue was about `$4.0B`, up `2%`; key innovative brands grew `41%`
  to `$838M`. Q4 2025 revenue was `$4.711B`, up `11%`.
- Teva is transitioning from generic-volume dependence toward branded
  neuroscience, immunology, long-acting, and differentiated assets while still
  carrying the manufacturing, debt, transformation, and market-access burden
  of global scale.
- Generic and biosimilar affordability is strategically important, but price
  erosion, competition, patent exposure, and reimbursement can overwhelm volume
  growth.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile product revenue to prescriptions, price and volume, channel
   inventory, gross-to-net deductions, payer and wholesaler collection, and
   generic competition.
2. Track innovative products from trial and regulatory spend through launch,
   persistence, access, manufacturing, commercial expense, and patent life.
3. Keep transformation savings, restructuring, litigation settlements, and
   adjusted add-backs separate from recurring cash costs.
4. Test whether complex-generic and biosimilar growth earns returns above the
   plant, quality, regulatory, and working-capital burden required to sustain it.
5. Treat debt paydown, dividends, or repurchases as residual claims only after
   manufacturing, R&D, settlements, compliance, and refinancing needs are funded.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what branded growth, generic price and
volume stability, launch success, patent duration, R&D return, reinvestment
rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress
test asks whether affordability, reimbursement, manufacturing, litigation,
rates, FX, and debt remain liquid through a portfolio or pricing shock without
confusing innovative revenue with durable common-owner cash.

## Promotion boundary

`teva-qualified; generics-to-innovation-transition-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from product sales to collection,
gross-to-net settlement, manufacturing and quality spend, R&D and launch return,
patent and litigation claims, debt, and diluted common residual. Revenue,
prescriptions, adjusted EBITDA, guidance, OCF, FCF, and milestones remain
diagnostic inputs.
