# Thrift-to-business-bank valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves WaFd from a strategic repositioning narrative into a
company-specific deposit, credit, capital, liquidity, and valuation test. It
separates transaction funding from expensive deposits and borrowings, legacy
real-estate credit from new business banking, and tangible common value from
repurchase-assisted EPS. Net income, ROATCE, NIM, efficiency, and buybacks are
not normalized common-owner cash by themselves.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WaFd | Normalized common earnings and tangible-book value after deposit funding cost, credit losses, branch and technology investment, compliance, capital requirements, and dilution | Relationship managers, underwriting, treasury systems, branches, technology, controls, loan-loss capital, and stress liquidity | Transaction-deposit runoff, higher deposit beta, regional real-estate losses, securities marks, wholesale funding, business defaults, or premature buybacks | Business-bank labels rise while deposits, fees, full-cost loan returns, credit quality, capital flexibility, or tangible value per share deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net income was approximately `$226M`, diluted EPS `$2.63`, ROATCE
  `9.20%`, assets `$26.7B`, and deposits `$21.4B`.
- Q3 FY2026 net income was approximately `$66.1M`, EPS `$0.84`, ROATCE `11.0%`,
  assets `$27.6B`, deposits `$20.9B`, loans `$20.0B`, NIM `2.81%`, and
  efficiency `53.69%`.
- Borrowings fell to about `$1.8B` at FY2025 year-end from `$3.3B`, but latest
  quarter borrowings plus junior subordinated debentures were about `$3.3B`;
  funding composition therefore remains an open control.
- Q2 FY2026 nonperforming assets improved to about `0.48%` of assets from
  `0.75%`, and delinquencies to about `0.78%` of loans from `1.07%`. WaFd also
  repurchased about `2.74M` shares, making capital-buffer and tangible-value
  tests essential.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile transaction and time deposits, deposit beta, rate paid, uninsured
   balances, borrowings, branch productivity, and treasury-service attachment.
2. Separate business banking from legacy mortgage, commercial-real-estate,
   construction, residential, and consumer loans by geography and collateral.
3. Track originations, repayments, purchases, sales, modifications, criticized
   loans, nonaccruals, NPAs, reserves, charge-offs, recoveries, and collateral.
4. Decompose NIM into loan yield, securities yield, deposit cost, borrowings,
   hedges, and mix; do not call temporary repricing structural improvement.
5. Reconcile efficiency gains to staffing, technology, credit, compliance,
   branch, fraud, and restructuring investment.
6. Compare repurchase price with tangible book, CET1/stress capital, liquidity,
   allowance coverage, and loan growth; EPS accretion is not resilience.
7. Test new business deposits, treasury fees, relationship-manager productivity,
   and loan returns after their full underwriting, staffing, and capital cost.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what deposit durability, business
customer attachment, NIM, efficiency, credit loss, ROATCE, tangible-book growth,
and cost of equity the market price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test
asks whether rates, deposit competition, regional employment, real-estate
refinancing, securities marks, and wholesale funding can be absorbed without
breaching liquidity or capital flexibility.

## Promotion boundary

`thrift-to-business-bank-qualified; deposit-credit-capital-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-period joins from deposits and funding cost to loan
mix, credit migration, reserves, capital, liquidity, full-cost business-bank
returns, tangible common value, and diluted residual. Net income, EPS, ROATCE,
NIM, efficiency, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
