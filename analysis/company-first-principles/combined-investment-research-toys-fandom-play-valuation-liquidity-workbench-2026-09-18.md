# Licensed collectibles and multiplatform play valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Funko and Spin Master from existing annual-report packets
into company-specific valuation, reinvestment, and liquidity tests. It does not
pool licensed collectibles, toy manufacturing, entertainment delivery, digital
games, or underlying intellectual-property ownership into one consumer ranking.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Funko | Licensed fandom-expression cash after royalties, guarantees, inventory, tariffs, channel support, DTC investment, debt, and dilution | Licensed property access, minimum guarantees, molds/tooling, inventory, freight/duty, DTC, stores, marketing, working capital, and debt service | Retailer destocking, tariff or refund reversal, royalty obligations, no revolver availability, covenant pressure, Loungefly rationalization, or refinancing | Core Collectibles and DTC demand remain positive while gross margin, inventory turns, royalty cash, debt paydown, or diluted common residual deteriorates |
| Spin Master | Portfolio cash across toys, entertainment, and digital games after product development, content, platform, inventory, tariffs, working capital, acquisitions, debt, and dilution | Toy innovation and tooling, content production and amortization, digital platform development, supply-chain diversification, inventory, marketing, and acquisition integration | Toy markdowns, retailer timing, tariff exposure, impairment, content underperformance, digital engagement decline, or working-capital reversal | Toy, entertainment, and digital growth persists while segment cash conversion, tariff-normalized margin, impairment burden, or per-share residual weakens |

## Current evidence anchors

- Funko FY2025 sales were `$908.2M`; Q2 2026 sales were `$207.7M`, gross
  margin was `56.6%`, cash was `$40.7M`, and debt was `$201.1M`.
- Funko's Q2 result included a `$25.4M` pre-tax tariff-refund and accrued-tariff
  release. It also sold `$22.1M` of tariff claims for `$19.2M`, using half the
  proceeds to reduce term debt. Those items are financing and policy
  adjustments, not normalized fandom owner cash.
- Funko reported more than `250` content-provider relationships, about `800`
  active licensed properties, and `$128.2M` of expected minimum guaranteed
  royalty payments at 2025 year-end. The guarantee schedule must remain in the
  reinvestment and liquidity denominator.
- Spin Master Q2 2026 revenue was `$436.4M`, toy revenue `$361.1M`, adjusted
  EBITDA `$51.6M`, and operating cash flow `$57.8M`. The quarter included a
  `$37.9M` toy-segment tariff refund, so reported margin and cash need a
  tariff-normalized sensitivity.
- Spin Master's Q4 2025 annual framing showed more than `$300M` of operating
  cash flow used across technology, supply-chain diversification, toy
  innovation, content, and digital platforms. That is a portfolio allocation
  claim, not proof that each format earns an equivalent return.

## Current primary-source cash and claim bridge

Funko's Q2 2026 SEC filing and earnings exhibit provide a clean temporary-
support boundary. Q2 sales were `$207.7M`, gross margin was `56.6%`, cash was
`$40.7M`, and total debt was `$201.1M`. The quarter's gross margin, net income,
adjusted net income, and adjusted EBITDA each included a `$25.4M` pre-tax
benefit from expected tariff refunds and release of accrued tariffs. Funko also
executed a `$22.1M` participation sale of tariff claims for `$19.2M`, using half
the proceeds to pay down its term loan. These amounts are visible policy,
financing, and balance-sheet events; they are not recurring fandom cash.

Spin Master's Q2 2026 official results provide a parallel but different
perimeter: revenue was `$436.4M`, operating cash flow was `$57.8M`, free cash
flow was `$19.2M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$51.6M`. The toy segment benefited
from a `$37.9M` tariff refund. Spin Master also repurchased `697,358` shares
for `$9.3M` during Q2, and declared a subsequent quarterly dividend. The
reported free-cash-flow and adjusted-EBITDA figures therefore remain subject
to tariff normalization, inventory and retailer timing, product/content
reinvestment, debt, and common-owner claims.

The two companies cannot be pooled: Funko's claim-sale proceeds and lender-
supervised debt structure are not equivalent to Spin Master's tariff refund and
portfolio cash flow. The correct common test is:

```text
licensed demand / toy sell-through
  -> collection and retailer timing
  -> inventory, royalties, tariffs, content and platform costs
  -> required reinvestment and debt service
  -> diluted common residual
```

The current lane status is therefore:

`licensed-play-current-period-qualified; tariff-and-reinvestment-normalization-open; no-ranking`

## Funko Q2 2026 operating-to-liquidity burden bridge

Funko's six-month cash-flow statement makes the headline improvement in cash
conversion materially less simple than adjusted EBITDA suggests. Net cash
provided by operating activities was `$23.630M`, versus cash used of `$44.442M`
in the prior-year period. The current-period bridge included `$30.541M` of
depreciation and amortization, `$5.196M` of equity compensation, a `$23.318M`
source from accounts receivable, a `$6.160M` inventory use, a `$5.992M`
accounts-payable use, a `$5.109M` accrued-royalties use, and a `$19.114M` use
from accrued expenses and other liabilities. The filing also says the H1
operating cash figure included `$9.0M` of interest payments. This is a reported
cash-flow bridge, not yet normalized owner cash: collections, liability
settlement, non-cash compensation, tariff support, and required product
investment remain separate objects.

The reinvestment burden is visible in investing cash flow. H1 net cash used in
investing was `$18.954M`, primarily purchases of tooling and molds. The filing
describes tooling and molds as the principal use of investing cash, so they
should remain in the cash denominator even though the company presents them as
capital expenditures rather than a recurring royalty or inventory cost.

The liquidity perimeter is stressed rather than merely leveraged. At June 30,
2026, Funko held `$40.7M` of cash and had `$198.3M` of debt under the Credit
Agreement net of unamortized debt-amendment fees, including `$124.6M` drawn on
the revolver and `$77.0M` on the term loan before the stated debt-cost
presentation. There was no remaining revolver availability. The `$40.7M` cash
balance includes `$19.2M` from the one-time sale of substantially all tariff
refund receivables, of which `$9.6M` was used to repay the term loan. The
remaining cash cannot be treated as recurring fandom cash or as unrestricted
common-owner liquidity.

The credit agreement requires cash above `$50.0M` to prepay the revolver, with
those prepayments permanently reducing commitments. It also requires quarterly
amortization, has a December 31, 2027 maturity, and carries loans at SOFR plus
`4.50%` at the filing date. Funko reported covenant compliance at June 30,
2026, but the amendment history includes covenant waivers and additional
cushion, and the company states that refinancing or additional capital may be
needed if operating cash or revolver availability is insufficient. This makes
refinancing, covenant headroom, and dilution part of the valuation object—not
an afterthought to adjusted EBITDA.

Royalty burden is also not captured by the current-period sales headline. The
Q2 10-Q reports `$34.1M` of accrued liabilities for ongoing and future royalty
audits, up from `$29.6M` at December 31, 2025, and a `$3.2M` reserve for
prepaid royalties estimated not to be recoverable through sales, up from
`$0.8M`. The filing confirms that license agreements can require minimum sales
levels or minimum guaranteed royalties and that guarantees may exceed what
actual sales recoup. The previously recorded FY2025 guarantee schedule remains
the best dated amount anchor; the Q2 filing strengthens the current reserve
and cash-burden evidence but does not provide a new full forward guarantee
schedule.

The current balance sheet adds a same-period burden control. At June 30, 2026,
Funko reported `$93.561M` of net accounts receivable, `$88.800M` of inventory,
`$11.9M` of excess-and-obsolete inventory reserves, `$58.206M` of accounts
payable, and `$54.712M` of accrued royalties. H1 operating cash was therefore
helped by a `$23.318M` reduction in net accounts receivable, while inventory
consumed `$6.160M`, accounts payable consumed `$5.992M`, and accrued royalties
consumed `$5.109M`. These balance-sheet levels and cash-flow movements must be
joined before treating the improvement as repeatable collection rather than a
period-specific working-capital release.

The debt amendment also makes the next filing testable. The Q2 filing requires
a minimum Qualified Cash balance of `$10.0M`; a minimum Fixed Charge Coverage
Ratio of `0.75x` for Q3 2026, `0.85x` for Q4 2026, `1.00x` for Q1 2027, and
`1.25x` from Q2 2027 onward; a maximum Net Leverage Ratio of `2.50x` beginning
in Q4 2026; and a six-month Q2 2026 minimum Consolidated EBITDA of `$15.1M`.
The covenant-cure right can waive certain tests after a voluntary prepayment of
more than `$10.0M`, but it cannot be treated as operating strength or available
owner cash.

The Q2 forward-guarantee search is classified as a searched negative for this
specific public filing object: no refreshed year-by-year minimum-guarantee
dollar table was located. That does not mean future guarantees are absent. It
means the FY2025 `$128.2M` schedule—`$40.6M` in 2026, `$49.9M` in 2027, and
`$37.7M` in 2028—must remain dated historical commitment evidence until a later
filing or license-level document updates it.

The current Funko test is therefore:

```text
reported sales and tariff-adjusted margin
  -> collectible demand and retailer collection
  -> inventory and receivable conversion
  -> royalty settlement and audit exposure
  -> tooling/mold reinvestment and interest
  -> revolver/covenant/refinancing constraints
  -> diluted common residual
```

No step in this bridge promotes H1 operating cash, adjusted EBITDA, the tariff
claim sale, or the `$40.7M` cash balance to normalized owner cash.

## Spin Master Q2 2026 operating-to-owner-cash bridge

Spin Master's Q2 release provides a stronger but more seasonal cash profile
than Funko's, while still leaving normalization work. Q2 operating cash was
`$57.8M` and Q2 investing cash use was `$38.6M`, producing reported free cash
flow of `$19.2M`. The same quarter included `$9.3M` of share repurchases,
`$8.1M` of lease payments, and `$75.0M` of Facility repayment. The company
explicitly identifies the `$37.9M` Q2 toy tariff refund as non-recurring and
excludes it from adjusted EBITDA. Cash, adjusted EBITDA, and free cash flow
must therefore be tested both before and after tariff normalization.

The H1 statement shows why the Q2 result cannot be annualized mechanically.
Operating cash was `$160.7M`, including `$108.6M` of net changes in non-cash
working capital, `$76.9M` of depreciation and amortization, `$11.8M` of
share-based compensation, `$25.6M` of income taxes paid, and `$7.1M` of
interest paid. Investing cash use was `$71.0M`, consisting of `$17.5M` of
property, plant and equipment, `$52.9M` of intangible-asset investment, and
`$0.6M` of portfolio investments. Financing cash use was `$145.5M`, including
`$167.0M` of loan repayment, `$16.1M` of lease-liability payments, `$17.2M` of
dividends, and `$15.0M` of share repurchases, partly offset by `$70.6M` of new
borrowings. Cash consequently fell from `$104.6M` at year-end 2025 to `$48.5M`
at June 30, 2026.

The balance-sheet perimeter keeps the working-capital burden visible. At June
30, 2026, Spin Master reported `$340.7M` of trade receivables, `$64.6M` of
other receivables, `$187.3M` of net inventory, `$393.5M` of trade payables and
accrued liabilities, `$167.9M` of current loans and borrowings, and total lease
liabilities of `$193.4M`. The company reported `$627.3M` of available liquidity,
made up of `$48.5M` cash and `$578.8M` under committed credit facilities. That
availability is a financing backstop, not owner cash; the facility amendment
added a `$225.0M` tranche and replaced the acquisition facility used for
Melissa & Doug.

The correct Spin Master normalization is therefore:

```text
toy sell-through / entertainment delivery / digital engagement
  -> sales allowances and retailer collection
  -> inventory and working-capital release or build
  -> tariff-normalized gross profit
  -> content, tooling, software, leases, and tax investment
  -> debt repayment and financing capacity
  -> dividends, repurchases, and diluted common residual
```

The H1 `$160.7M` operating-cash figure is not yet normalized owner cash: the
working-capital contribution, tariff refund, capitalized intangible investment,
lease payments, debt reduction, share-based compensation, and seasonal sell-in
cycle all require a same-period return test.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Remove tariff refunds, claim-sale proceeds, and tariff-accrual releases from
   recurring Funko and Spin Master cash before valuation.
2. Keep royalty guarantees, royalty audits, content amortization, impairment,
   markdowns, retailer allowances, and inventory reserves visible as cash or
   economic burdens even when adjusted EBITDA excludes them.
3. Reconcile direct-to-consumer growth and digital engagement to same-entity
   collection, fulfillment, platform costs, and working capital; sales mix alone
   is not owner cash.
4. Separate licensed-product economics from ownership of the underlying world:
   licensing breadth can support discovery and shelf refresh while also creating
   fixed guarantees and renewal risk.
5. Treat debt paydown, repurchases, or adjusted earnings as residual claims only
   after inventory, guarantees, tariffs, content, and required reinvestment are
   funded.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what licensed-property renewal,
collector conversion, toy sell-through, digital engagement, channel mix, margin,
reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style
stress test asks whether tariffs, freight, retailer bargaining power, household
discretionary pressure, licensing concentration, debt maturities, and platform
costs can compress liquidity before the cultural demand thesis is disproved.

## Promotion boundary

`licensed-play-qualified; tariff-normalization-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from licensed property or
brand engagement to sell-through, collection, inventory and royalty cash,
required reinvestment, debt service, and diluted common residual. Sales growth,
adjusted EBITDA, digital users, property count, tariff refunds, and buybacks
remain diagnostic inputs.

## September 18, 2026 valuation expectation refresh

The current market snapshot puts Funko equity value at approximately `$300.9M`
and Spin Master equity value at approximately `CAD 2.0B`. Against the visible
H1 mechanical screens, Funko's operating cash less tooling/molds was
`$4.676M`, or approximately `$9.352M` annualized, implying roughly `32.2x`
market capitalization to that screen. A debt-and-cash proxy using Funko's
`$201.1M` debt and `$40.7M` cash produces approximately `49.3x` against the
same annualized screen. Spin Master's H1 operating cash less PP&E and
intangible investment was approximately `$89.7M`, or `$179.4M` annualized,
implying approximately `11.2x` on the CAD market-cap snapshot before any
currency conversion.

These are expectation screens, not normalized owner-cash multiples. Funko's
screen still includes tariff/accrual support and a working-capital release,
while Spin Master's includes a large H1 working-capital contribution and the
Q2 toy tariff refund. Neither screen deducts the full royalty, content,
inventory, lease, tax, debt, or diluted-residual burden. The correct conclusion
is therefore a wide difference in market-implied recovery burden—not a ranking
of Funko against Spin Master.
