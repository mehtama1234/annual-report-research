# Printed-circuit-board and advanced-interconnect valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves TTM Technologies from current-period hardware evidence into a company-specific valuation, PCB, RF, microelectronics, backlog, capacity, customer, acquisition, working-capital, and owner-cash test. It treats TTM as an advanced interconnect and mission-systems manufacturer, not as a generic electronics supplier.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TTM Technologies | Collected PCB, substrate, RF, assembly, and mission-system cash after customer qualification, materials, labor, capacity, inventory, acquisitions, capex, debt, and dilution | Specialized facilities and equipment, PCB and substrate capacity, RF/microelectronics qualification, engineering, inventory, working capital, European acquisitions, customer support, and quality systems | AI/networking cycle, aerospace and defense procurement, customer concentration, qualification delays, utilization, materials, labor, acquisition integration, capex funding, debt, and dilution | Revenue, book-to-bill, backlog, data-center growth, adjusted EBITDA, or FCF rise while customer qualification, program acceptance, utilization, inventory, capacity, acquisition return, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 net sales were about `$2.9B`, non-GAAP net income `$259.0M`, and adjusted EBITDA `$456.3M` or `15.7%` of sales; the platform had about `18,200` employees, `1,300` customers, and `24` specialized facilities.
- Q2 `2026` net sales were about `$1.0B`, up `37%`; adjusted EBITDA `$166.8M` or `16.6%`; non-GAAP net income `$106.9M` or `$0.99` per diluted share.
- Q2 data-center and networking were `40%` of sales and grew `91%` year over year; aerospace and defense were `37%` of sales and program backlog exceeded `$1.7B`.
- Q1 `2026` net sales were `$846.0M`, adjusted EBITDA `$132.9M`, non-GAAP net income `$80.1M`, and book-to-bill `1.41`.
- Q4 `2025` net sales were `$774.3M`, adjusted EBITDA `$126.2M`, non-GAAP net income `$74.8M`, and book-to-bill `1.35`; announced Swiss Technology Group and ILFA acquisitions add integration and financing tests.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile boards, substrates, RF assemblies, program backlog, book-to-bill, customer qualification, shipments, acceptance, inventory, receivables, and cash; backlog is not cash.
2. Separate A&D, Commercial, RF&S Components, data-center/networking, medical, industrial, and customer cohorts; qualification and margin differ.
3. Test facility additions and Swiss Technology Group/ILFA acquisitions through purchase price, qualification, utilization, integration, customer retention, capex, and return on invested capital.
4. Keep adjusted EBITDA, book-to-bill, backlog, AI growth, FCF, and acquisition claims separate from recurring collected common-owner cash.
5. Reconcile laminate and materials, labor, quality, yield, freight, inventory, capitalized costs, debt, interest, taxes, SBC, and diluted shares.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what AI/networking demand, defense backlog, qualification, utilization, mix, pricing, capacity, acquisition return, capex, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether electronics cycles, defense procurement, customer concentration, materials, labor, rates, acquisition leverage, and capacity execution preserve cash conversion.

## Promotion boundary

`ttm-pcb-interconnect-qualified; qualification-backlog-capacity-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from PCB, RF, customer, and program cohorts to qualification, shipment and acceptance, collection, inventory, capacity and capex, acquisition integration, debt service, taxes, and diluted common residual. Revenue, book-to-bill, backlog, data-center growth, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, and acquisitions remain diagnostic inputs.
