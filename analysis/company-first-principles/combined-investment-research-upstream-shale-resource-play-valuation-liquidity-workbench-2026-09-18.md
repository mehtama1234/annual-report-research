# Upstream shale and resource-play valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves EOG Resources from upstream production evidence into a
basin, decline, reserve-renewal, infrastructure, and common-cash test. It
keeps EOG distinct from integrated oil, LNG export, midstream, oilfield
services, mining, and royalty/streaming models.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EOG Resources | Basin and well inventory cash after product-level realized prices, decline, drilling, reserve renewal, gathering, taxes, environmental claims, debt, SBC, and dilution | Maintenance and development drilling, facilities, exploration, gathering/processing access, land, reclamation, and reserve replacement | Oil/gas price decline, service-cost inflation, basis/transport constraint, impairment, reserve depletion, payout pressure, or environmental/regulatory burden | Production and FCF rise while reserve depth, production per share, mid-cycle cash, impairment, or capital needed to sustain the base deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 operating cash flow was `$10.040B`, total capital expenditures
  `$6.294B`, and company-defined FCF `$4.663B`; SBC was `$216M` and cash
  `$3.400B`.
- FY2025 production averaged `1,232.2 mboe/d`; crude and condensate were
  `521.9 mb/d`, NGLs `288.2 mb/d`, and natural gas `2,533 MMcf/d`. Product mix
  must remain separate from aggregate BOE.
- Proved reserves were `5,514 MMboe`, about `99%` in the United States. Reserve
  volume is not equivalent to economic value without decline, price, cost,
  infrastructure, and development-capital assumptions.
- FY2025 included approximately `$843M` of impairments, primarily Barnett and
  Woodford assets. EOG returned about `$4.7B` through dividends and repurchases
  while maintaining a policy to return at least `70%` of eligible cash after
  total capital expenditures.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Split oil, condensate, NGL, and gas production, realized price, basis,
   hedges, royalties, taxes, and unit costs before using BOE growth.
2. Separate maintenance drilling, development drilling, exploration,
   facilities, acquisitions, and reserve-replacement capital.
3. Track basin decline, type curves, well-level returns, proved reserve
   additions, inventory quality, production per share, and cash per share.
4. Reconcile gathering, processing, transportation, export, power, water,
   reclamation, and environmental obligations to field-level cash.
5. Analyze Barnett/Woodford and future impairments against original reserve,
   price, cost, and carrying-value assumptions.
6. Treat FCF, BOE, reserve volumes, dividends, repurchases, and adjusted cash
   flow as diagnostic until mid-cycle reserve-renewal cash is evidenced.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test values each basin by decline, product mix,
mid-cycle price, cost, drilling inventory, reserve life, sustaining capital,
taxes, gathering, and probability-weighted exploration. The Lyn Alden-style
stress test asks whether domestic energy security and flexible U.S. production
offset commodity cycles, service inflation, transport constraints, regulation,
and depletion. A low-complexity upstream operator still must continually buy
or drill its future production.

## Promotion boundary

`upstream-shale-qualified; reserve-renewal-and-mid-cycle-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from product production,
realized price, decline, reserve additions, drilling, maintenance and growth
capital, gathering, taxes, impairments, environmental obligations, debt, SBC,
and diluted common residual. BOE, reserves, FCF, adjusted cash flow,
dividends, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
