# Walmart Q2 FY2027 cash-quality perimeter upgrade

Research date: `2026-09-17`

## Purpose

This is a company-specific H1 retail upgrade for CA-06. It strengthens the
Walmart cash and quality-of-earnings perimeter without promoting Walmart's
non-GAAP free cash flow to normalized common-owner cash.

## Same-period evidence

Walmart's six months ended July 31, 2026 produced `$19.710B` of operating cash
flow and `$14.181B` of property-and-equipment payments, yielding management's
`$5.529B` free-cash-flow measure. Walmart explicitly says this measure does
not deduct debt service, contractual obligations, or business acquisitions.
The cash-flow statement also reports `$4.230B` of long-term debt issuance,
`$2.303B` of long-term debt repayment, `$3.945B` of dividends, and `$5.104B`
of share repurchases.

The segment note disaggregates the `$14.181B` H1 capital program into
Walmart U.S. `$10.453B`, Walmart International `$1.812B`, Sam's Club U.S.
`$664M`, and corporate/support `$1.252B`. This improves the legal-segment and
capital-allocation denominator, but the filing does not classify those amounts
as maintenance, remodel, fulfillment, technology, new-store, or growth
capital; the segment split is not a maintenance-capital schedule.

The H1 working-capital perimeter includes `$2.660B` of inventory cash use,
`$1.648B` of accounts-payable support, `$1.449B` of accrued-liability use,
and `$211M` of accrued-income-tax use. Supplier-financing obligations rose
from `$6.0B` at January 31, 2026 to `$6.4B` at July 31, 2026. This is a
qualified obligation movement, not a cash-flow adjustment.

The July 31 balance sheet reports `$15.650B` of operating lease right-of-use
assets and `$16.512B` of operating lease obligations, plus `$6.178B` of
finance lease right-of-use assets and `$6.832B` of finance lease obligations.
These are balance-sheet claim and asset controls, not H1 lease payments. The
`$211M` accrued-income-tax movement is already part of the reported operating-
cash bridge and is not a substitute for cash taxes paid. The filing provides
no separately matched H1 cash-paid lease or cash-tax schedule.

The filing also gives a current-period attached-service collection perimeter:
Sam's Club U.S. membership and other income was `$1.328B` for H1, up `8.5%`
year over year, and membership fees are deferred and recognized ratably over
the one-year term. Walmart further identifies selected advertising,
fulfillment-service, and data-insight arrangements within e-commerce and
ecosystem net sales, but does not separately quantify those revenue streams or
their allocated cost.

## Q2 management and presentation boundary

Walmart's official Q2 FY2027 earnings presentation says the year-over-year
free-cash-flow decline reflected a `$2.8B` increase in capital expenditures to
support the omnichannel growth strategy, partly offset by higher operating cash
flow. The presentation does not identify how much of that increase is
maintenance, replacement, remodel, fulfillment, technology, automation, or
new-store capital; the 10-Q's `$1.087B` new-store category remains the only
explicit growth floor in the H1 allocation table.

The Q2 earnings release and presentation also describe advertising,
marketplace, store-fulfilled delivery and membership as important growth and
economic drivers. Walmart states that global advertising is recorded either in
net sales or as a reduction to cost of sales depending on the arrangement.
This is an accounting-presentation control: advertising growth cannot be
converted into a comparable service-revenue or residual-cash series without
the arrangement-level gross/net classification and allocated fulfillment,
technology, labor, and other costs.

Neither the Q2 presentation nor the release supplies a matched H1 cash-paid
lease/tax schedule, supplier-finance settlement ledger, maintenance-capital
allocation, or ecosystem-service cost schedule.

## Quality-of-earnings controls

The quarter included approximately `$2.9B` of tariff refunds recorded as a
reduction to cost of sales. Walmart also recorded a `$0.4B` income-tax benefit
and `$0.5B` interest-expense reduction related to changes in unrecognized tax
benefits. These items materially affect current-period earnings and margin
comparability and must be isolated from recurring cash expectations.

The company reports `$11.696B` of H1 net income attributable to Walmart,
`$323M` attributable to noncontrolling interests, and 7,989 million diluted
weighted-average shares. The common-owner denominator therefore requires NCI,
share repurchases, dilution, debt, dividends, and contractual obligations to
remain visible.

## What this changes

Walmart now has a stronger period-matched cash-quality and financial-
shenanigans control surface: reported FCF is explicitly limited; inventory and
payable timing are visible; supplier-finance obligations are separately
identified; tariff refunds and tax-benefit effects are nonrecurring/support
markers; and the common-owner claim is distinguished from consolidated net
income.

This still does not close CA-06. The filing does not provide a matched H1 cash-
paid lease/tax schedule, a complete maintenance-versus-growth capex split,
service-level cost and collection allocation for the embedded ecosystem
offerings, supplier-finance settlement ledger, or a normalized common-owner
residual.

## Valuation and liquidity implication

The proper Damodaran input remains a range around reported OCF less property
spending, with tariff refunds, inventory/payable timing, capex growth, debt,
acquisitions, NCI, dividends, and repurchases explicitly carried. The
Lyn-Alden-style stress case reverses inventory support, removes tariff and tax
benefits, raises rates and working-capital funding needs, and tests the ability
to finance omnichannel capital investment without weakening the common-owner
residual.

## Decision

`Walmart-H1-FY2027-cash-quality-strengthened; normalized-owner-cash-open; CA-06-partial`
