# Warby Parker omnichannel eyewear valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench tests Warby Parker as an omnichannel eyewear, vision-care, and direct-brand platform. It keeps active customers, average revenue per customer, stores, exams, contacts, insurance, tariffs, lens mix, Intelligent Eyewear, retail expansion, working capital, and diluted common residual separate from reported revenue and adjusted EBITDA.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warby Parker | Owned eyewear and vision-care cash after frames, contacts, exams, stores, laboratories, insurance settlement, tariffs, technology, retail expansion, debt, and diluted common residual | Store openings and maintenance, optical laboratories, inventory, doctor and care staffing, technology, marketing, Intelligent Eyewear, working capital, tariffs, and shares | Consumer spending or traffic slowdown, tariff/input inflation, store payback failure, insurance collection pressure, clinical/regulatory issue, or wearable launch underperformance | Customers and revenue rise while repeat rate, ARPC, gross margin, store productivity, care attachment, inventory conversion, or diluted per-share cash deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 revenue was `$871.9M`, active customers `2.689M`, adjusted EBITDA `$95.2M`, net income `$1.6M`, operating cash flow `$110.8M`, and free cash flow `$43.7M`.
- Q2 2026 revenue was `$235.5M`, active customers `2.71M`, ARPC `$336`, adjusted EBITDA `$32.9M`, operating cash flow `$29.6M`, and free cash flow `$6.8M`; stores reached `352`.
- Contact lenses were `11.1%` of FY2025 revenue; eye exams and vision care were `6.4%`; insurance-backed orders were only `8.3%` of revenue despite insurance use in roughly `60%` of U.S. vision-care purchases.
- Q2 gross margin benefited from an `$11.8M` tariff-refund benefit, while management prepared Intelligent Eyewear without including launch revenue in guidance.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile active customers and ARPC to repeat purchases, contacts, exams, insurance settlement, refunds, and cash collection.
2. Separate store expansion from store productivity, fit/clinical attachment, fulfillment, laboratory, and staffing costs.
3. Keep tariff refunds, inventory, lens mix, Intelligent Eyewear investment, marketing, and technology costs in the persistence test.
4. Treat buybacks, stock compensation, store commitments, leases, debt, and diluted shares as common-owner residual claims.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what customer growth, ARPC, store productivity, care attachment, gross margin, insurance penetration, reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the valuation requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether households and insurers continue funding eyewear and vision care through tariff, employment, healthcare-budget, rent, and consumer-spending shocks.

## Promotion boundary

`warby-parker-qualified; eyewear-care-and-owned-demand-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from customers and stores to repeat collection, exams, contacts, insurance, inventory, store payback, required reinvestment, leases, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, active customers, ARPC, adjusted EBITDA, OCF, guidance, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
