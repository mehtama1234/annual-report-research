# Waterworks and municipal distribution valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Core & Main from annual-report evidence into a company-specific valuation, reinvestment, liquidity, and common-owner residual test. It keeps water, wastewater, storm-drainage, and fire-protection distribution separate from ordinary building-products distribution and the broader built-environment channel summary.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core & Main | Water-infrastructure distribution cash after project timing, inventory, supplier rebates, branch density, acquisitions/greenfields, interest, taxes, TRA, NCI, debt, and Class A allocation | Inventory and receivables, branch/fleet capacity, technology, acquisitions, greenfields, maintenance capex, integration, and working capital | Municipal funding delays, contractor cycle, inventory build, supplier-term reversal, acquisition leverage, debt refinancing, TRA payments, NCI distributions, or repurchases ahead of owner cash | Water-infrastructure growth continues while post-acquisition cash return, inventory quality, Holdings-to-Class-A conversion, or diluted residual deteriorates |

## Current evidence anchors

- Fiscal 2025 reported OCF of `$650M`, capex of `$46M`, acquisitions of `$61M`, and OCF less capex less acquisitions of about `$543M`; fiscal 2024 acquisitions were `$741M`, so one clean year is not normalized owner cash.
- Q1 2026 sales were `$1.910B`, gross margin `27.2%`, adjusted EBITDA `$226M`, and OCF only `$82M`; conversion must be observed through the seasonal working-capital cycle.
- More than `370` branches in `49` states serve over `5,000` suppliers and `60,000` customers. The control point is local availability, specification support, delivery, and national purchasing scale rather than proprietary manufacturing.
- Fiscal 2025 included `$14M` of equity compensation, `$11M` of acquisition expense, `$142M` of interest, a `$725M` tax receivable agreement payable, non-controlling interests, partnership exchanges, debt, and repurchases. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA is not Class A owner cash.
- Inventory rebates and purchase discounts included in inventory were `$54M` at fiscal 2025 year-end versus `$43M` a year earlier; rebate accruals, freight, lower-of-NRV reserves, and price/mix changes require a gross-margin and inventory-quality roll-forward.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Split volume, price, acquisitions, and greenfields; do not treat adjusted EBITDA growth as organic demand or acquisition return.
2. Roll inventory, receivables, supplier rebates, freight, and reserves against sales and gross margin; do not infer cash conversion from reported margin.
3. Reconcile consolidated cash to Holdings, then to Class A cash after NCI distributions, TRA payments, taxes, interest, debt repayment, and partnership-interest exchanges.
4. Keep recurring acquisition and integration spending in the competitive-advantage reinvestment denominator when the growth model depends on a steady deal cadence.
5. Test share repurchases against post-inventory, post-tax, post-TRA, post-NCI, and post-debt cash rather than treating buybacks as proof of surplus.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what branch density, product mix, municipal specification relationships, acquisition cadence, working-capital investment, and post-claim cash conversion the market price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether replacement infrastructure demand survives municipal budgets, federal appropriations, contractor backlogs, interest rates, supplier inflation, and project deferrals.

## Promotion boundary

`waterworks-distribution-qualified; inventory-project-collection-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from waterworks volume and price/mix to inventory and receivable collection, branch/acquisition or greenfield return, debt service, TRA and NCI cash claims, and diluted Class A residual. Sales, adjusted EBITDA, OCF, repurchases, municipal need, and infrastructure narratives remain diagnostic inputs.
