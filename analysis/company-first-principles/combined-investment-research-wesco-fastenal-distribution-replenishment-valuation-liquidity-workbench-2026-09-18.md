# Electrical distribution and embedded replenishment valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench advances the industrial-uptime lane from a cash-conversion
screen into company-specific valuation, reinvestment, liquidity, and thesis-
breaker tests. WESCO is treated as an electrical, communications, utility, and
data-center project channel; Fastenal is treated as an embedded replenishment,
industrial-vending, and point-of-use MRO system. They are not pooled merely
because both distribute industrial products.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WESCO | Project-channel gross profit and cash after receivable, inventory, supplier-term, branch/network, software, acquisition, debt, lease, and dilution burdens | Branches, leasehold improvements, equipment, internal-use software, IT, working capital, acquisitions, and maintenance/service capacity | Data-center or utility slowdown, customer concentration, working-capital reversal, supplier prepayment, acquisition leverage, rates, or dilution | Sales/backlog and adjusted earnings rise while receivables, inventory, supplier support, interest, or post-capex cash deteriorate |
| Fastenal | Embedded replenishment and service cash after inventory, vending/FMI, facilities, fleet, software, service labor, lease, tax, debt, and dilution burdens | FMI hardware, facilities/distribution, software/IT, fleet, manufacturing equipment, branch/site investment, and inventory | Manufacturing slowdown, large-account concentration, vending/service cost, inventory aging, labor, rates, or capital-return coverage | Sales, vending, and shareholder distributions rise while OCF conversion, inventory productivity, site economics, or diluted residual deteriorate |

## Same-period filed evidence

The H1 2026 screen reports:

| Field | WESCO | Fastenal |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Operating cash flow | `$275.1M` | `$644.1M` |
| Productive/property spend | `$51.6M` | `$123.0M` |
| Mechanical OCF less spend | `$223.5M` | `$521.1M` |
| Cash interest paid | `$172.3M` | `$2.0M` |
| Cash income taxes paid | `$154.6M` | `$203.0M` |
| Common dividends paid | `$48.8M` | `$550.9M` |
| Common repurchases | `$39.9M` | `$50.3M` |
| New operating-lease assets | `$139.6M` | `$63.0M` |

Cash interest and cash taxes are already inside operating cash flow and are not
subtracted again. Dividends, repurchases, share-settlement taxes, and lease
additions are separate claim or reinvestment surfaces. The `$223.5M` and
`$521.1M` figures are mechanical screens, not normalized owner cash.

WESCO's conversion is especially working-capital-sensitive: H1 cash flow
included a `$641.0M` receivables use, `$432.4M` inventory use, and `$726.0M`
accounts-payable support. Fastenal's H1 bridge included `$320.2M` receivables
use, `$7.7M` inventory release, `$84.1M` accounts-payable support, and `$28.6M`
accrued-expense support. These balance movements are diagnostic and must not be
added again as cash deductions.

The Q2 persistence screen adds a warning: WESCO Q2 operating cash was `$53.7M`
versus `$107.8M` in the prior-year quarter despite record sales, 13% organic
growth, approximately `$1.5B` of data-center sales, and backlog growth of about
60%. Fastenal Q2 operating cash was `$265.7M` versus `$278.6M`; OCF was 69.4%
of net income versus 84.4%, and the company returned approximately `$305M` to
shareholders. These are conversion and coverage tests, not manipulation
findings.

## Valuation expectation screen — September 18, 2026

The market snapshot reported approximately `$16.771B` of equity value for WESCO
at `$344.15` per share and `$56.375B` for Fastenal at `$49.01` per share. Using
two times the H1 mechanical cash screens as a deliberately simple annualized
reference produces:

| Company | Equity value | 2x H1 mechanical cash screen | Price / annualized screen |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| WESCO | `$16.771B` | `$447.0M` | approximately `37.5x` |
| Fastenal | `$56.375B` | `$1.042B` | approximately `54.1x` |

This is a Damodaran-style expectation surface, not a DCF, FCF multiple, or
investment ranking. The denominator is before unresolved maintenance/service,
lease, tax, acquisition, and dilution burdens. WESCO's multiple requires
receivable and inventory conversion plus project-channel durability; Fastenal's
requires sustained embedded-service economics and coverage of its large capital
returns after recurring reinvestment.

## Lyn Alden-style liquidity and QoE tests

Stress both models for industrial production, customer capex, power/data-center
timing, rates, inflation, supplier terms, working-capital reversal, and access
to credit. A demand signal is not a cash receipt. A backlog, vending location,
branch, customer-site deployment, or shareholder distribution is not proof of a
common-owner return.

The primary forensic prompts are receivable timing, inventory capitalization,
supplier-term support, acquisition contribution, lease additions, service labor,
and post-OCF capital returns. They remain Sloan-/Schilit-style follow-up flags;
no Beneish-style composite or fraud conclusion is assembled.

## Promotion boundary

`industrial-distribution-qualified; conversion-warning; owner-cash-open;
no-ranking`

Promotion requires another comparable quarter and same-entity joins from
customer demand or site deployment to collection, inventory/service burden,
maintenance versus growth capital, lease/tax cash, acquisition return, debt,
and diluted common residual. Reported OCF, mechanical OCF less property spend,
backlog, vending, and capital returns remain diagnostic.
