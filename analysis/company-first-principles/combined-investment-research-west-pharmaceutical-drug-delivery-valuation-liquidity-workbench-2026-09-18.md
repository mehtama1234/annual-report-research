# West Pharmaceutical drug-delivery components valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves West Pharmaceutical Services from current-period evidence
into a company-specific valuation, qualification, quality, reinvestment, and
common-owner cash test. It does not pool HVP Components, contract manufacturing,
drug-delivery systems, and commodity packaging into a generic medical-device
multiple.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| West Pharmaceutical Services | Collected drug-delivery and high-value-component cash after sterile manufacturing, customer qualification, validation, materials, labor, quality claims, capacity expansion, capex, debt, and dilution | HVP component tooling, molds, plants, validation, quality systems, development, capacity, contract manufacturing, and working capital | Therapy demand concentration, customer inventory correction, qualification delay, quality/regulatory event, plant interruption, materials/labor inflation, debt, or dilution | Injectable and biologic demand grows while HVP utilization, qualification economics, quality costs, capex returns, collections, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- Q2 2026 net sales were `$872.3M`, up `13.8%`; diluted EPS was `$2.15` and adjusted diluted EPS `$2.37`.
- Q1 2026 net sales were `$844.9M`, up `21.0%`; diluted EPS was `$1.92` and adjusted diluted EPS `$2.13`.
- Full-year 2025 net sales were approximately `$3.07B`, with HVP Components and product mix central to the operating read.
- The model is exposed to injectable and biologic therapy growth but monetizes containment, delivery, and high-value components rather than owning the underlying molecules.
- Sterile manufacturing, validation, customer qualification, quality reliability, and specialized engineering are required operating assets, not optional overhead.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Reconcile HVP component volume and mix to customer orders, qualification status,
   production acceptance, shipments, collections, and customer inventory levels.
2. Separate high-value components, delivery systems, contract manufacturing, and
   other businesses by margin, capacity, tooling, validation, and quality burden.
3. Keep molds, tooling, plants, validation, quality systems, development, capacity
   expansion, remediation, and working capital in the reinvestment denominator.
4. Test whether adjusted EPS removes recurring quality, warranty, remediation,
   restructuring, customer-transfer, or qualification costs.
5. Reconcile capex, debt, leases, claims, customer concentration, SBC, buybacks,
   and diluted shares before treating reported cash conversion as owner cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what injectable and biologic demand,
qualification win rate, utilization, mix, margin, capex, quality spending, and cost
of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether drug
pricing, customer inventory, regulation, sterile manufacturing, labor, materials,
and plant concentration can preserve liquidity through a therapy or quality cycle.

## Promotion boundary

`west-pharmaceutical-drug-delivery-qualified; qualification-quality-and-owner-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity, same-period joins from customer programs and
component shipments to qualification, acceptance, collections, required quality and
capacity spend, claims, debt, and diluted common residual. Revenue growth, HVP
volume, adjusted EPS, guidance, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
