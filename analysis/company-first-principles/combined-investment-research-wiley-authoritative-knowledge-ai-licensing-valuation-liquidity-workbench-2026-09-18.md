# Wiley authoritative knowledge and AI licensing valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves John Wiley & Sons from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests research publishing, learning content, institutional subscriptions, open access, AI licensing, recurring contracts, portfolio simplification, content rights, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not pool Wiley with newspapers, broadcasters, advertising agencies, or generic software platforms.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| John Wiley & Sons | Authoritative knowledge cash after research subscriptions, open-access settlement, learning content, AI licensing, editorial and platform costs, acquisitions, debt, and dilution | Editorial rights, journal and platform investment, courseware, technology, content acquisition, restructuring, working capital, debt, and shares | Research-budget pressure, open-access economics, AI rights leakage, learning weakness, acquisition integration, channel shifts, or leverage | AI revenue and adjusted EBITDA rise while recurring research retention, licensing collection, content rights, learning demand, cash conversion, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2026 revenue was about `$1.677B`, adjusted EBITDA about `$440M`, adjusted EPS about `$4.19`, operating cash flow about `$261M`, and free cash flow about `$195M`.
- Research represented approximately `64%` of FY2025 revenue and was about `96%` digital/online; Learning was approximately `35%` of revenue and about `60%` digital/online.
- FY2026 Q4 Research revenue was about `$296M`, up `5%` reported, while Learning revenue was about `$152M`, down `6%`; AI revenue reached about `$49M` for the year.
- Wiley has sold or exited University Services, most Wiley Edge, and CrossKnowledge, concentrating the platform around Research and a narrower Learning business.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate recurring research subscriptions, transformational agreements, open-access author funding, one-time AI licensing, and ordinary book or courseware sales.
2. Test retention, renewal, price, usage, institutional budget exposure, rights ownership, and collection before treating AI or digital revenue as durable.
3. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow to editorial investment, platform technology, content rights, restructuring, acquisitions, working capital, debt, and dilution.
4. Keep Research and Learning periods and economics separate; Research resilience does not prove Learning recovery.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what recurring research growth, open-access economics, AI licensing, learning stabilization, margin, reinvestment, and cost of capital are embedded in the valuation. The stress test asks whether universities, funders, corporate R&D, authors, and learners continue to fund trusted knowledge while AI changes rights economics, budgets, discovery, and distribution.

## Promotion boundary

`wiley-qualified; research-retention-and-ai-rights-collection-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from content and institutional usage to collection, renewal, rights ownership, editorial and platform reinvestment, acquisition return, debt, claims, and diluted common residual. Revenue, AI revenue, adjusted EBITDA, EPS, OCF, FCF, guidance, and licensing agreements remain diagnostic inputs.
