# Wyndham economy and midscale franchise valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Wyndham Hotels & Resorts from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests economy and midscale franchise fees, RevPAR, room growth, pipeline quality, ancillary revenues, franchisee health, FeePAR, brand technology, fee deferrals, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not pool Wyndham with premium lodging brands, owned hotel REITs, or broader asset-light platforms.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wyndham Hotels & Resorts | Everyday-traveler franchise cash after franchise fees, RevPAR, room openings, ancillary revenue, owner economics, technology, fee deferrals, taxes, debt, and dilution | Brand and technology, franchise support, pipeline development, loyalty and marketing, working capital, acquisitions, debt, and shares | Economy/midscale RevPAR weakness, franchisee distress, pipeline cancellation, Revo fee deferrals, weather, cross-border softness, or leverage | Rooms and pipeline grow while franchisee cash health, fee collection, RevPAR, ancillary contribution, debt service, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- FY2025 adjusted EBITDA was about `$718M`, adjusted diluted EPS `$4.58`, system-wide rooms grew `4%`, ancillary revenues reached an all-time high, and the pipeline reached `259,000` rooms.
- Q2 2026 U.S. RevPAR grew `2%`; rooms grew `4%` excluding Revo rooms; pipeline grew `4%` to approximately `261,000` rooms with a roughly `30%` FeePAR premium.
- 2025 produced `870` development contracts, up `18%`, while ancillary revenues increased `15%`; headline GAAP earnings were affected by impairment and other charges.
- The model is asset-light and fee-based but remains exposed to franchisee economics, economy/midscale travel, fee deferrals, pipeline funding, and capital returns.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate franchise fees, RevPAR, rooms, pipeline, ancillary revenue, owner economics, fee deferrals, and acquisitions.
2. Test whether pipeline additions are funded, opened, and productive, rather than treating signed rooms as collected recurring cash.
3. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA and FCF to franchisee support, technology, loyalty and marketing, FeePAR, debt, dividends, and dilution.
4. Keep economy/midscale demand and franchisee health separate from premium lodging and owned-asset hotel economics.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what rooms growth, RevPAR, ancillary revenue, FeePAR, franchisee retention, pipeline conversion, leverage, and cost of capital are embedded in the valuation. The stress test asks whether practical travel demand survives household pressure, rates, franchisee financing, weather, labor, fee deferrals, and cross-border weakness.

## Promotion boundary

`wyndham-qualified; economy-midscale-franchisee-and-pipeline-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from rooms and franchise activity to fee collection, RevPAR, franchisee health, pipeline opening, ancillary settlement, fee deferrals, debt, claims, funding, and diluted common residual. Rooms, pipeline, RevPAR, adjusted EBITDA, FCF, guidance, dividends, and repurchases remain diagnostic inputs.
