# Xerox installed-base and workflow-services valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Xerox from current-period evidence into a company-specific valuation object. It tests print equipment, post-sale revenue, managed print, IT solutions, workflow software, financing, page volumes, replacement cycles, Lexmark and ITsavvy integration, tariffs, cash flow, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not pool Xerox with cloud software, electronics manufacturers, or generic business services.

## Decision matrix

| Company/model | Correct valuation object | Reinvestment denominator | Liquidity stress | Filing-based thesis breaker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Xerox | Installed-office-device cash after equipment, post-sale supplies and service, workflow and IT attachment, financing, page volumes, acquisitions, tariffs, debt, and dilution | Hardware replacement, managed print, workflow technology, IT integration, inventory, financing receivables, restructuring, debt, and shares | Secular page decline, deferred replacement, tariff and memory costs, acquisition integration, financing losses, liquidity, or refinancing | Adjusted margin and post-sale revenue rise while pro forma equipment demand, page volume, cash conversion, financing quality, debt, or diluted common residual deteriorate |

## Current evidence anchors

- 2025 revenue was about `$7.022B`, with approximately `79%` post-sale revenue, operating cash flow of `$224M`, and free cash flow of `$133M`.
- Q2 2026 revenue was about `$1.922B`, adjusted operating income about `$203M`, operating cash flow `$37M`, and free cash flow `$11M`.
- Q2 Print and Other revenue was about `$1.733B`, IT Solutions `$194M`, equipment sales `$387M`, and post-sale revenue `$1.346B`.
- Xerox raised the Lexmark gross cost-synergy target to at least `$350M`; underlying pro forma revenue remained pressured despite reported integration-aided growth.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Separate equipment sales, post-sale supplies and service, page volume, IT Solutions, financing, acquisitions, and foreign exchange.
2. Reconcile reported growth and adjusted margin to Lexmark and ITsavvy integration, cost takeout, pro forma decline, and secular print pressure.
3. Test financing receivables, customer credit, inventory, tariffs, memory costs, restructuring, and cash conversion.
4. Determine whether workflow and managed services are durable collected cash or only an attachment claim around a shrinking device base.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The expectation test asks what post-sale retention, equipment replacement, page volumes, IT attachment, Lexmark synergies, margin, leverage, and cost of capital are embedded in the valuation. The stress test asks whether offices defer hardware, tariffs and memory costs rise, financing quality deteriorates, and service revenue can offset secular print decline and debt service.

## Promotion boundary

`xerox-qualified; post-sale-retention-and-integration-cash-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from devices and installed base to collection, post-sale retention, page volumes, financing receivables, workflow return, integration savings, debt, claims, funding, and diluted common residual. Revenue, adjusted operating income, post-sale mix, OCF, FCF, guidance, and synergies remain diagnostic inputs.
