# Zebra enterprise data-capture and workflow valuation/liquidity workbench

Research date: `2026-09-18`

## Purpose

This workbench moves Zebra from an uncovered technology packet into a company-specific valuation object. It separates Connected Frontline and Asset Visibility and Automation economics, installed-device dependence, supplies and services, customer workflow budgets, acquisitions, working capital, debt, and diluted common residual. It does not treat hardware revenue, device volume, segment growth, adjusted earnings, or repurchases as normalized owner cash.

## Current evidence anchors

- Q2 2026 revenue was about `$1.557B`, gross profit about `$825M`, operating income about `$321M`, net income about `$233M`, and diluted EPS about `$4.85`.
- Q2 2026 Connected Frontline revenue was about `$903M` with segment operating income of about `$219M`; Asset Visibility and Automation revenue was about `$654M` with segment operating income of about `$192M`.
- Q1 2026 revenue was about `$1.495B`; Connected Frontline revenue was about `$825M` and Asset Visibility and Automation revenue about `$670M`, providing a pre-step-up comparison rather than a single-quarter extrapolation.
- FY2025 revenue was about `$5.396B`, with Connected Frontline at about `$2.960B` and Asset Visibility and Automation at about `$2.436B`.
- FY2025 absorbed the Elo acquisition for about `$1.303B` and the Photoneo acquisition for about `$62M`; their contribution, integration cost, amortization, purchase accounting, and post-close capex must remain visible in the owner-cash bridge.

## QoE and financial-shenanigans prompts

1. Split device shipments from recurring supplies, service, software, and workflow attachment; installed-base growth is not cash until usage, renewal, collection, and support cost are joined.
2. Test channel inventory, customer deployment timing, rebates, returns, and receivables aging against reported revenue and segment margins.
3. Keep Elo and Photoneo acquisition price, acquired revenue and margin, integration spend, amortization, purchase accounting, and post-close capex separate from organic growth.
4. Treat customer modernization budgets as a demand variable: retail, logistics, healthcare, manufacturing, and field operations can defer deployments even when long-run workflow dependence is strong.
5. Reconcile inventory, components, warranty and quality obligations, R&D, software delivery, SBC, debt, and diluted shares before accepting adjusted earnings or repurchases as owner cash.

## Damodaran/Lyn Alden application

The Damodaran-style expectation test asks what installed-base retention, device replacement, supplies and service attachment, software monetization, segment margin, acquisition return, reinvestment rate, and cost of capital the price requires. The Lyn Alden-style stress test asks whether frontline customers can fund modernization through a slowdown, whether channel and inventory financing absorb cash, and whether components, tariffs, rates, acquisitions, or debt reduce liquidity before the workflow moat appears in common-owner returns.

## Promotion boundary

`zebra-enterprise-workflow-qualified; installed-base-and-channel-open; no-ranking`

Promotion requires same-entity joins from devices and workflow deployments to supplies, services, software, customer collections, channel inventory, required reinvestment, acquisition return, claims, funding, and diluted common residual. Revenue, segment operating income, adjusted EPS, device volume, backlog, and buybacks remain diagnostic inputs.
