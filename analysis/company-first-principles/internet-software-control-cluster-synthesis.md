## What This Cluster Is Really About

This cluster is not about "internet software" as a single industry.

The real question is: as more work, commerce, entertainment, software creation, and machine activity moves online, who controls the points where digital activity must be allowed, protected, routed, executed, governed, or monetized?

Every digital action has a hidden dependency chain. A user, employee, developer, application, bot, AI agent, or creator tries to do something. That action needs identity, permission, traffic movement, security, infrastructure, runtime, data access, discovery, and sometimes payments. The more the internet becomes automated and participatory, the more valuable those control points become.

The companies in this cluster are not interchangeable. They sit at different points in the digital dependency chain.

## First-Principles Mechanism

Start with a digital action.

Someone or something tries to interact:

- an employee logs into a SaaS app
- a shopper loads a checkout page
- an API sends data to another system
- a bot tries to scrape, attack, or transact
- an AI agent makes a request
- a developer deploys code
- a creator publishes a Roblox experience
- a user enters a game world

Before that action can safely complete, several questions must be answered:

- Who or what is making the request?
- Is it authorized?
- Where should the request go?
- Is the destination protected?
- Should traffic be blocked, accelerated, inspected, logged, or transformed?
- Does code need to run near the user?
- Does the customer need infrastructure to build the application in the first place?
- If the interaction is social or participatory, how are safety, age, incentives, and monetization governed?

That is the cluster's core mechanism: more digital activity creates more demand for trusted control layers.

## Why "Internet Software" Is Too Blunt

The AnnualReports industry label puts these companies near each other, but the economic jobs are different.

Cloudflare, Akamai, and Fastly all touch the edge, but they are not the same:

- Cloudflare is trying to be the broad traffic-control platform.
- Akamai is converting delivery heritage into security and distributed cloud.
- Fastly is the narrower programmable-edge case.

Zscaler and Fortinet both sell security, but they are not the same:

- Zscaler is cloud-delivered policy for distributed access.
- Fortinet is security convergence with hardware, services, branch, OT, and network enforcement attached.

DigitalOcean and Roblox are both internet software, but they are almost opposites:

- DigitalOcean supports builders underneath the application.
- Roblox owns a visible participatory world where the application is the culture.

The main conceptual insight is that digital complexity fragments into different control jobs.

## Customer Insight Across The Set

The common customer behavior is loss of manual control.

As digital systems expand, organizations and users can no longer manually govern all the interactions happening around them.

An enterprise cannot manually inspect every login, API call, bot request, AI-agent action, and data movement. That creates demand for Zscaler, Cloudflare, Fortinet, Akamai, and Fastly.

A developer cannot manually assemble every infrastructure layer from scratch while also building the product. That creates demand for DigitalOcean.

A parent, user, creator, or platform operator cannot manually govern every interaction inside a participatory digital world. That creates Roblox's safety, discovery, age, creator, and monetization burden.

Each company reduces a different control anxiety.

Cloudflare reduces traffic-path anxiety. The customer is asking: can one platform help make internet-facing activity faster, safer, routed, inspected, and ready for AI-agent traffic?

Akamai reduces reliability-transition anxiety. The customer is asking: can an existing edge network still protect and place workloads in a world where plain delivery is less differentiated?

Fastly reduces customization anxiety. The customer is asking: can I control application behavior close to the user without rebuilding my whole stack?

Zscaler reduces access anxiety. The customer is asking: which humans, machines, devices, workloads, apps, and agents are allowed to touch which resources?

Fortinet reduces enforcement anxiety. The customer is asking: how do I secure real branch, industrial, carrier, and infrastructure environments where policy still needs deployed enforcement points?

DigitalOcean reduces builder anxiety. The customer is asking: can I use cloud and AI infrastructure without becoming a hyperscaler specialist?

Roblox reduces participation anxiety. The users want social play and identity; creators want monetization; parents and regulators want safety; management has to keep the world open enough to grow and controlled enough to survive.

## Industrial / Operating Insight Across The Set

The control layer is not economically uniform.

Cloudflare's machine is platform breadth. Its economics depend on whether more products attach to the same traffic path and whether that breadth turns into operating leverage rather than permanent spending.

Akamai's machine is transition. It has cash flow and a large installed edge network, but delivery is under pressure. The strategic operating question is whether security and CIS contracts grow fast enough to offset the old delivery drag.

Fastly's machine is focused edge execution. It has improving gross margins, security growth, and RPO growth, but top-ten customer concentration means its edge position is less diversified. A few large architecture decisions can move the model.

Zscaler's machine is recurring policy utility. ARR, RPO, deferred revenue, large-customer cohorts, and cash flow matter because the product becomes embedded in the daily governance of enterprise access.

Fortinet's machine is hybrid enforcement. Product revenue, service revenue, deferred revenue, billings, and free cash flow all matter because the model blends hardware refresh, subscriptions, branch deployments, OT environments, and security services.

DigitalOcean's machine is simplified access. The model works if the company lets smaller builders and AI-native customers use infrastructure easily. The risk is that AI inference creates heavier capacity commitments and larger-customer dependence.

Roblox's machine is governed participation. Engagement, bookings, creator payouts, safety costs, recommendation algorithms, and age policy all interact. A change that improves long-term platform health can hurt short-term monetization.

This is why "software infrastructure" should not be treated as automatically clean or asset-light. Every control point carries a different burden.

## Societal Insight Across The Set

The internet is moving from pages and apps toward automated, governed, participatory systems.

In the older internet, the main question was whether a user could reach a site or app. In the newer internet, the questions multiply:

- Is the actor human or machine?
- Is the machine authorized?
- Is the API safe?
- Is the data protected?
- Can an AI agent act on behalf of someone?
- Where should code run?
- Who governs identity and safety inside digital worlds?
- Which infrastructure lets smaller builders participate?

Cloudflare and Zscaler show that machine traffic and AI agents need routing, identity, access, and policy. Fortinet shows that cyber control is moving into factories, branches, carriers, and AI infrastructure, not only office IT. DigitalOcean shows that AI creation is spreading beyond large enterprises. Roblox shows that digital life is becoming participatory, economic, and socially governed rather than simply consumed. Akamai and Fastly show that performance, placement, and protection remain hidden prerequisites for every visible interface.

The broader societal shift is that trust is becoming infrastructure. The economy is putting more decisions, relationships, work, play, and transactions into digital systems. That makes the invisible systems deciding "allowed or blocked," "fast or slow," "safe or unsafe," "governed or ungoverned," more important.

## Cluster Thesis

This cluster proves that digital activity creates several different control markets, not one internet-software market.

The winners monetize different choke points:

- traffic path
- application delivery
- edge execution
- access policy
- physical enforcement
- builder infrastructure
- participation governance

The common thread is that more digital activity creates more need for trusted intermediaries. The sharper insight is that those intermediaries have different economics because they control different parts of the dependency chain.

## What Would Weaken The Cluster Thesis

The thesis weakens if:

- AI traffic does not create durable incremental security, routing, or governance demand.
- edge growth fails to convert into operating leverage.
- enterprises consolidate control into hyperscalers instead of independent platforms.
- smaller builders do not sustain AI-infrastructure demand.
- Fortinet's hardware-attached model loses relevance against cloud-native policy platforms.
- Roblox engagement fails to translate into bookings after safety and algorithm changes.
