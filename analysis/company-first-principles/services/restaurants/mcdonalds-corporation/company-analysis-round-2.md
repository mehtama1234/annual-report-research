# McDonald's Corporation Round 2 Analysis

Date baseline: 2026-08-30

This round exists because the first pass was too broad. It named franchise, loyalty, and cash flow, but it did not explain the parts that make McDonald's work right now.

The better question is simple: what is McDonald's doing to make people keep coming back, and what proof would show that it is working?

## Corrected Answer

McDonald's makes money from repeated food visits, busy restaurant sites, franchise rent and fees, app data, and campaigns that make people show up.

The food sale is what customers see. Under that are five important parts:

- good locations that create rent and fees;
- loyalty data that may make people visit more often;
- visible low-price offers that answer the "fast food got too expensive" complaint;
- store technology that can cut waiting and mistakes;
- event meals, like the Grinch socks, that turn a meal into something people talk about and collect.

The Q2 2026 numbers stop us from giving a lazy positive answer. U.S. comparable sales rose, but guest counts were down. So the question is not just whether sales rose. The question is whether more people came, or whether price and mix covered up weak visits.

## What The First Pass Missed

The first pass missed behavior. It saw loyalty sales and active users, but it did not ask whether loyalty changes visits. McDonald's says U.S. MyMcDonald's Rewards members more than double their visits in the first year after joining. That is the center of the loyalty question. The caveat is obvious: maybe the people who joined were already frequent customers.

The first pass missed price trust. The `$5 Meal Deal` is not only a cheap bundle. It is a public answer to people who felt fast food had become too expensive. McValue adds app offers, Buy One Add One for `$1`, and local franchisee deals. The risk is that customers stack discounts, buy little else, and hurt restaurant profit.

The first pass underweighted property. McDonald's is not a pure restaurant operator. The parent company collects rent and fees from many franchised restaurants. That helps explain why parent-company profit can look strong even when store-level work is hard.

The first pass missed local store technology. Ready on Arrival and Google Cloud edge computing matter only if they make restaurants work better: shorter waits, fewer wrong orders, fewer broken machines, and less wasted labor.

The first pass missed the Grinch socks. Sherwood reported McDonald's biggest sales day on record and about 50 million sock pairs sold globally. That is not a side note. It shows a restaurant can briefly act like a mass merchandise seller.

## New Deep Questions

### Did loyalty actually make people come more often?

McDonald's says U.S. rewards members more than double visits in the first year after joining. That claim matters only if joining changed behavior. The next pass must compare each member's visits before and after joining, then compare those members with similar people who did not join.

Weak proof would be app sales rising while total visits stay weak. Strong proof would be members still visiting more often after the first wave of sign-up offers ends.

### Did the `$5 Meal Deal` repair trust, or only buy cheap visits?

The deal is McDonald's answer to customers who felt fast food had become too expensive. The important test is what customers did next. If they came back later for normal-price food, the deal may have repaired trust. If they bought only the discounted bundle and waited for the next coupon, it bought low-profit traffic.

The next pass must look for repeat visits, extra items added to the order, and franchisee profit after app coupons are stacked on top.

### Who pays when McDonald's promises value?

The word value hides the bill. The discount can be paid by franchisees, McDonald's, suppliers, workers, or customers through higher prices elsewhere. This is the pressure question.

The next pass must track whether franchisees are still making enough after rent, wages, food, discounts, remodels, and technology. Parent cash is not enough proof if operators are weaker.

### Are U.S. sales rising for the right reason?

Q2 2026 U.S. sales rose even though guest counts were down. That means the dollars looked better than the visit count. The next pass must split growth into visits, price, and order size.

Strong proof would be positive guest counts after McValue. Weak proof would be higher checks hiding fewer customers.

### Does Ready on Arrival improve the store?

Ready on Arrival matters only if it helps workers make food at the right time. The test is not whether the technology sounds advanced. The test is wait time, order accuracy, labor per order, equipment downtime, and line speed in normal busy stores.

If franchisees pay for new systems but do not see better store results, the technology becomes another cost, not a benefit.

### Did the Grinch socks create real demand or just a one-day spike?

Sherwood reported McDonald's biggest sales day ever and about 50 million sock pairs sold globally. That is worth studying because a restaurant briefly became a sock seller.

The next pass must ask whether new or lapsed customers came in, whether they returned after the socks were gone, and whether the campaign made profit after product, labor, and marketing costs.

## Rule For The Next Company

The next company analysis should not start and end with revenue, EPS, and management quotes. It should answer these plain questions:

- What repeated customer behavior does the company depend on?
- What physical or digital thing does the company control?
- Which reported number may look good without proving real demand?
- What odd fact reveals customer desire better than management language?
- Who pays when the company tries to keep prices low or traffic high?
