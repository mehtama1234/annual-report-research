# Access Instead Of Ownership Comparison

Date: 2026-08-11
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Plain Claim

Some strong businesses are not really selling a product unit.

They are selling continued access.

The customer is paying for the right to keep entering a system that offers:

- lower prices
- a workout routine
- entertainment
- travel recognition
- premium status
- gaming and hospitality rewards

The important shift is:

the customer does not need to own more things each time.

The customer needs to stay inside the access system.

## Why This Comparison Matters

The archive already has pages on:

- paid relationship and habit loops
- closed loops versus open routing
- status and wallet systems

What it did not yet have as a standalone proof page is a direct comparison of businesses where access itself is the core product.

This memo asks:

Which companies are strongest when the customer keeps paying for entry rather than for outright ownership each time?

## Access System Map

| Pattern | Best company examples | What the customer is paying to access | Main burden |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value access | Costco | lower prices, stock-up confidence, convenience | margin discipline, renewal quality, traffic, assortment relevance |
| Wellness access | Planet Fitness | affordable routine, confidence, low social friction | retention, pricing sensitivity, franchise execution |
| Entertainment access | Netflix, Disney | deep content library, franchises, discovery, bundled attention | content cost, churn, format shifts, ad balance |
| Travel-network access | Marriott | repeat stay recognition, broad branded choice, loyalty benefits | travel-cycle sensitivity, owner economics, loyalty fatigue |
| Wallet and rewards access | Caesars, American Express | one identity, one rewards balance, one premium or gaming relationship | engagement retention, rewards cost, regulation, partner economics |

## What The Comparison Clarifies

### 1. Access can be more important than ownership when repeat behavior matters.

The Costco member does not need to own the warehouse.

The Planet Fitness member does not need to own the equipment.

The Netflix user does not need to own each movie.

The Marriott guest does not need to own a vacation property.

The American Express cardholder does not need to renegotiate value on every swipe.

The recurring benefit is continued entry into the system.

### 2. Access businesses win when they reduce the customer's setup cost for the next use.

Costco reduces the effort of hunting for value.

Planet Fitness reduces the friction of deciding where to work out.

Netflix reduces the friction of choosing what to watch.

Marriott reduces the friction of picking a stay.

Caesars reduces the friction of moving between physical and digital entertainment.

American Express reduces the friction of choosing a premium spend surface.

### 3. Access can be value-based, premium, or identity-driven.

Costco is value-heavy.

Planet Fitness is low-friction and mass-market.

Netflix is utility plus culture.

Disney is franchise and destination access.

Marriott is travel recognition and loyalty.

American Express is premium identity and benefits.

Caesars is rewards-linked entertainment access.

This matters because it shows access is a business model shape, not one consumer segment.

### 4. The strongest access systems often add layers over time.

Netflix adds advertising and broader entertainment formats.

Disney ties streaming to parks, sports, and merchandise.

Costco adds digital convenience to the warehouse habit.

Caesars adds digital wallet behavior to physical resort visitation.

American Express layers travel, dining, and merchant benefits onto payments.

Access systems become stronger when the next adjacent use still fits the same customer identity.

### 5. Access models can look simple while hiding significant operational burden.

These are not effortless dues collectors.

Costco still runs large-format retail operations.

Planet Fitness still depends on franchise health and equipment cycles.

Netflix and Disney still carry content and programming cost.

Marriott still depends on owner economics and brand standards.

Caesars still carries property and gaming complexity.

American Express still carries credit, merchant, and network risk.

The model is attractive not because burden disappears, but because recurring access makes the demand side more durable.

## Stronger Conclusion

The stronger conclusion is that access has become a major product in its own right.

The customer increasingly pays for:

- access to value
- access to routine
- access to entertainment
- access to travel identity
- access to a wallet or benefits network

The key cross-company insight is:

When repeat use matters more than one-time ownership, the company that controls access can build a stronger relationship than the company selling isolated transactions.

## Aha Moments

### 1. Costco and American Express look opposite on the surface, but both are access businesses.

One sells entry into savings.

The other sells entry into premium treatment.

### 2. Planet Fitness is closer to Netflix than taxonomy suggests.

Both are recurring-access systems where the customer mainly pays to keep the option of use available at low friction.

### 3. Disney is moving from media ownership toward ecosystem access.

The important question is no longer just whether a consumer buys a title or a ticket.

It is whether the consumer stays inside the Disney relationship across formats.

### 4. Caesars shows that access can include the wallet itself.

The relationship becomes stronger when money, rewards, and identity move together across physical and digital surfaces.

## Thesis Breakers

This access-instead-of-ownership thesis weakens if:

- customers stop valuing continued entry enough to justify the recurring fee or relationship
- digital convenience makes some access intermediaries less necessary
- access systems become too expensive relative to the perceived benefit
- content, rewards, or operating costs rise faster than the relationship value they support
- the customer still belongs, but actual use intensity keeps weakening
