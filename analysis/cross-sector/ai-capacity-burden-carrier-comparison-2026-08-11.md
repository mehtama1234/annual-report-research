# AI Physical Capacity And Burden Carrier Comparison

Date: 2026-08-11
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Plain Claim

AI is not only a software trend. It is a physical-capacity buildout.

The first AI proof memo showed the broad chain: cloud demand, accelerators, networking, power, cooling, skilled field execution, utilities, and generation.

This memo adds the next layer: who captures cleaner economics, who carries the burden, and where the bottlenecks move after the first wave of chip and data-center demand.

The useful question is:

When AI demand becomes real infrastructure, who owns the bottleneck and who has to fund, build, lease, power, inspect, or maintain it?

## Backlog Conversion Analysis

Backlog is the bridge between AI enthusiasm and economic proof.

The backlog signals are strong but not identical:

- Quanta's total backlog rose from `$43.98B` at 2025 year-end to `$53.4B` in Q2 2026. That says grid, power, load-center, and infrastructure work is moving into executable commitments.
- Digital Realty's backlog rose from `$817M` at its share in Q4 2025 to `$1.4B` at its share in Q2 2026, with a separate July hyperscale leasing signal of `$410M` annualized GAAP rent at 100% share. That says data-center demand is turning into contracted rent, but also into financing and development obligations.
- Equinix's Q2 2026 annualized gross bookings grew `23%`, and monthly recurring revenue grew `11%`. That says its demand is not only project backlog; it is recurring digital-infrastructure use.
- Vertiv's Q4 2025 backlog of `$15.0B`, Q4 book-to-bill of `2.9x`, and Q2 2026 organic sales growth of `18%` show the conversion from orders into sales, margins, and cash flow.
- Eaton's guidance raises and Q2 2026 organic growth of `14%` show that electrical capacity demand is already affecting revenue and full-year expectations.
- KLA is less backlog-driven in the packet, but its revenue progression from `$3.30B` in Q2 FY26 to `$3.66B` in Q4 FY26 shows process-control demand staying strong through the reporting window.
- Applied Materials is also less backlog-driven than the infrastructure equipment names. Its nine-month FY2026 Semiconductor Systems revenue grew 10% year over year and AGS revenue grew 18%, but the cash bridge includes elevated capex and a $698M contract-liability contribution. The evidence supports demand and service momentum, not cycle immunity.
- ASML has more backlog visibility than KLA or Applied Materials: year-end backlog was €38.8B, while 2025 installed-base-management sales reached €8.193B. That visibility is valuable but not equivalent to cash because shipment, customer acceptance, export-license, supplier, and cancellation risks remain.
- Lam's support layer is large but less contractual than ASML's backlog: fiscal 2026 support-related revenue was `$8.347B`, while deferred revenue declined to `$2.43B` and the company held about `$490M` of Japanese shipments in inventory pending acceptance. This supports installed-base value but requires a stricter cash and acceptance test.

The key insight:

Backlog quality depends on where the company sits. Equipment and contractors must deliver. Data-center REITs must fund and power capacity. Interconnection platforms must keep ecosystem density growing. Process-control suppliers must stay indispensable through capex cycles.

## Power And Skilled-Labor Bottleneck

The AI buildout is increasingly constrained by things that do not look like software:

- power availability
- electrical gear
- cooling systems
- substations
- transmission and distribution
- field crews
- permits
- powered data-center land
- interconnection density
- advanced semiconductor inspection

Quanta is the clearest skilled-labor proof point. Its Q2 2026 backlog of `$53.4B` and RPO of `$33.6B` mean the market is not only ordering equipment. It needs qualified teams to build and maintain electric and communications infrastructure.

Digital Realty and Equinix show the real-estate version of the same constraint. Customers do not only need square feet. They need powered, connected, resilient, financeable data-center capacity.

KLA shows the upstream manufacturing version. AI infrastructure fails before deployment if the chip-manufacturing process cannot deliver yield, precision, inspection, and process control.

Vertiv and Eaton show the equipment and systems version. More compute density means more power distribution, backup power, thermal management, cooling, and capacity expansion.

## Company-Level Strategy Reads

### Quanta

Quanta is trying to be the field-execution company for electrification, grid modernization, power generation, communications, load-center growth, and critical infrastructure. The strongest evidence is Q2 2026 revenue of `$9.6B`, RPO of `$33.6B`, total backlog of `$53.4B`, and management emphasis on craft-skilled execution.

The strategy works if backlog converts into profitable projects without labor, safety, acquisition, or project-execution problems.

### Digital Realty

Digital Realty is trying to be a global data-center capacity and capital-structure platform. The evidence is Q2 2026 revenue of `$1.9B`, Core FFO per share excluding net promote of `$2.13`, backlog of `$1.4B` at its share, and July hyperscale leases worth `$410M` annualized GAAP rent at 100% share.

The strategy works if leasing demand converts into attractive development returns after power, capex, financing, and joint-venture costs.

### Equinix

Equinix is trying to be the interconnection-dense platform layer of data-center infrastructure. The evidence is 2025 AFFO per share of `$38.33`, Q2 2026 AFFO per share of `$11.78`, monthly recurring revenue growth of `11%`, annualized gross bookings growth of `23%`, more than `500,000` interconnections, and a record `9,700` net interconnections added in Q2 2026.

The strategy works if customer ecosystem density keeps producing recurring revenue and interconnection value faster than capex and power demands rise.

### KLA

KLA is trying to own the semiconductor process-control choke point. The evidence is Q2 FY26 revenue of `$3.30B`, Q3 FY26 revenue of `$3.415B`, Q4 FY26 revenue of `$3.66B`, and fiscal 2026 revenue of `$13.58B`.

The strategy works if inspection, yield, packaging, and advanced-node precision remain non-discretionary for semiconductor customers.

### Applied Materials

Applied Materials is trying to control multiple qualified materials-engineering
steps and then monetize the installed base through services, spares, and
factory-automation software. Nine-month FY2026 Semiconductor Systems revenue
was `$18.146B` at a `34.0%` operating margin; AGS revenue was `$5.005B` at a
`29.2%` margin. The strategy works if customers continue investing in leading-
edge logic, memory, and packaging, while contract liabilities convert cleanly,
inventory remains productive, and R&D and facility spending do not consume the
incremental cash.

### ASML

ASML is trying to control lithography qualification at the most advanced
semiconductor nodes. The evidence is 2025 sales of `€32.667B`, gross margin of
`52.8%`, backlog of `€38.8B`, and installed-base-management sales of `€8.193B`.
The strategy works if EUV productivity improves, customers keep funding new
fabs, backlog converts without major push-outs, and R&D and supplier investment
preserve the technical lead. The risks are unusually concentrated: the largest
customer was `23.9%` of sales, the two largest were `38.0%`, and China was
`29.1%`.

### Lam Research

Lam is trying to capture the process-intensity layer in deposition, etch,
clean, packaging, and support. Fiscal 2026 systems revenue was `$14.885B` and
support-related revenue was `$8.347B`; operating cash was `$5.858B` against
about `$922M` of capital/investing cash use. The strategy works if process
complexity expands Lam's tool content and the installed base produces durable
support cash. The main tests are China at `34%` of revenue, four customers at
`16%`, `15%`, `12%`, and `12%`, and lower cash conversion than fiscal 2025.

### Teradyne

Teradyne is trying to capture the test and validation layer after chip
fabrication and before system deployment. Q2 FY2026 Semiconductor Test revenue
was `$1.122B`, compared with `$107M` of Product Test and `$100M` of Robotics.
The strategy works if AI, HBM, compute, and high-speed systems require more
test content and Teradyne retains qualified positions. The main tests are
receivables, customer advances, acquisitions, robotics margins, and whether
the current valuation assumes peak semiconductor-test demand.

### Vertiv And Eaton

Vertiv and Eaton are turning AI compute demand into power and electrical systems demand. Vertiv shows this through `$15.0B` of Q4 2025 backlog and Q2 2026 adjusted free cash flow of `$925M`. Eaton shows it through 2025 sales of `$27.448B`, Q2 2026 sales of `$8.531B`, organic growth of `14%`, and raised full-year organic-growth guidance of `11-13%`.

The strategy works if equipment demand keeps converting into margins, cash flow, and capacity expansion without supply-chain or project-timing drag.

## Aha Moments

### 1. AI capacity is not one bottleneck.

KLA, Vertiv, Eaton, Quanta, Digital Realty, and Equinix are all AI infrastructure names, but they do not sell the same thing. The bottleneck moves from semiconductor precision to power systems, thermal systems, field crews, powered real estate, and interconnection density.

### 2. The best economics may sit one layer away from the obvious owner.

Digital Realty carries more development and financing burden than Equinix appears to carry in the packet evidence. Quanta carries project-execution and labor burden. KLA, Vertiv, and Eaton may capture attractive economics because their products or services are hard to avoid when the physical buildout happens.

### 3. Real estate taxonomy hides the AI story.

Digital Realty is classified as `REIT - Office Real Estate` in the packet, but the useful business role is data-center capacity and hyperscale leasing. Equinix is a REIT, but the moat is interconnection and ecosystem density. Sector labels are too blunt for AI infrastructure.

### 4. Backlog is necessary but not sufficient.

Backlog shows demand, not finished economics. The question for every AI physical-capacity company is whether backlog converts into revenue, margins, free cash flow, and acceptable returns after labor, power, capex, and supply-chain burden.

## Thesis Breakers

The AI physical-capacity thesis weakens if:

- Quanta backlog grows but project margins, cash flow, safety, or labor execution deteriorate.
- Digital Realty signs leases but development returns weaken because of power scarcity, financing costs, construction inflation, or joint-venture complexity.
- Equinix loses interconnection growth or recurring revenue momentum while capex rises.
- KLA revenue rolls over sharply because semiconductor customers defer process-control spend or export restrictions overwhelm demand.
- Applied Materials revenue and margin roll over, AGS does not support the tool cycle, contract liabilities fail to convert, or China restrictions and customer concentration overwhelm process-equipment demand.
- ASML backlog fails to convert, EUV productivity or customer acceptance weakens, service growth slows with tool utilization, or export controls and customer concentration overwhelm the lithography scarcity thesis.
- Lam support revenue weakens with utilization, deferred revenue and Japan-held inventory fail to convert, China restrictions overwhelm other regions, or deposition/etch process-intensity growth fails to translate into cash.
- Teradyne Semiconductor Test revenue rolls over, receivables and advances weaken cash conversion, Robotics remains structurally unprofitable, or the valuation remains above normalized cash even after AI-test demand matures.
- Vertiv and Eaton backlog or order strength fails to convert into revenue, margin, and free cash flow.
- GE Vernova's RPO and customer deposits rise while Prolec losses, Wind contract losses, inventory, contract assets, or warranty claims absorb the cash.
- Hubbell revenue grows through price and acquisitions while unit volume, distributor turns, acquisition returns, or cash per diluted share weaken.
- Powell bookings and backlog rise while contract liabilities fund current cash, receivables expand, project margins weaken, or the Jacintoport expansion carries excess fixed cost.
- AI workloads become materially less power-, cooling-, network-, or data-center-intensive than current company evidence implies.
- Utility interconnection delays, permitting, public opposition, or power prices slow customer deployment enough to strand capacity plans.
