# AI physical capacity and control layers: forensic comparison

Date: 2026-09-13

This comparison uses local packets for Generac, Vertiv, F5, KLA, Equinix, and
Digital Realty, with Quanta, Sterling, MasTec, Comfort Systems, and EMCOR as
field-execution controls. It tests
the AI-infrastructure narrative through durability, reinvestment, and earnings
quality rather than treating backlog or AI exposure as value creation.

## The lane question

Where does AI demand become monetizable scarcity: chips and process control,
application/security control, power and cooling, interconnection and real
estate, or field execution? Which layer has pricing power, and which layer must
fund the physical burden before revenue is recognized?

| Layer | Companies | Evidence | Burden to restore to the economics |

## What the evidence currently supports

AI infrastructure is a full physical system. The strongest packets show demand
pulling through process control, application delivery, power, cooling,
interconnection, powered land, and field construction. Vertiv's Q4 2025 organic
orders grew 252% with a 2.9x book-to-bill and its Q2 2026 adjusted free cash flow
was $925M. Equinix's recurring interconnection economics and Digital Realty's
backlog show a different monetization path from equipment shipments. Generac's
Q2 C&I sales rose 29%, but its $71M tariff-refund benefit makes the quarter a
particularly useful earnings-quality test.

The evidence supports structural demand, not a uniform investment conclusion.
KLA's process-control position may be harder to defer than general equipment.
F5 has cleaner margins and cash than physical operators but retains meaningful
services and systems revenue. Equinix has ecosystem density; Digital Realty has
powered capacity and lease backlog. Quanta and Sterling have extraordinary
visibility but carry the hardest labor and project-conversion burden.

## Forensic earnings review

### Demand indicators versus recognized economics

Backlog, orders, book-to-bill, RPO, MRR, interconnections, and pipeline are
useful leading indicators, but they are not interchangeable. A booked project
can be delayed, repriced, canceled, or expensive to execute. A recurring lease
can require large up-front power and construction investment. An equipment
order can be recognized before its working-capital and warranty burden is
fully visible.

The next filing bridge must therefore tie each leading indicator to recognized
revenue, gross margin, operating cash flow, free cash flow, receivables,
inventory, customer advances, capex, and return on invested capital.

### Generac: tariff benefit and mix transition

Generac's FY2025 sales fell 2% to $4.21B, while Q2 2026 sales rose 11% to
$1.17B. C&I growth and hyperscale agreements support a real transition away
from residential standby dependence. But the Q2 margin and guidance read was
materially helped by approximately $71M of pretax tariff refunds.

The falsifiable question is whether large-megawatt capacity expansion creates
durable margins after the refund disappears, residential demand remains cyclical,
and manufacturing, dealer, warranty, and inventory requirements rise.

### Vertiv: unusually strong conversion, but execution still matters

Vertiv reported Q2 2026 sales of $3.274B, adjusted operating margin of 22.6%,
and adjusted free cash flow of $925M, with $5.6B of liquidity and net cash. That
combination is stronger evidence than backlog alone. Still, the Q4 2.9x book-to-
bill and $15B backlog create a future conversion test: supply-chain congestion,
multi-phase deployment, pricing, warranty, and capacity investment can change
the realized return.

### Equinix and Digital Realty: recurring revenue does not eliminate capex

Equinix's Q2 2026 MRR grew 11%, while AFFO per share reached $11.78. Digital
Realty reported Q2 revenue of about $1.9B and $1.4B of backlog at its share,
with additional July hyperscale leases worth $410M annualized GAAP rent at
100% share. These are strong ecosystem and lease signals.

The Damodaran test is whether incremental powered capacity earns an adequate
return after construction, power procurement, financing, depreciation,
maintenance, and lease-up risk. The Alden test is whether power scarcity and
network density remain durable through a weaker capital market. AFFO and FFO
must be reconciled to recurring maintenance and expansion capital, not treated
as unconstrained owner cash.

The [Equinix deep memo](../deep-company-pages/equinix-inc.md) makes that burden
explicit: FY2025 OCF was $3.911B, recurring capex $284M, but total property and
equipment capex $4.311B, real-estate acquisitions $994M, and debt $21.4B. The
network-density thesis therefore depends on the return from new powered
capacity, not simply on recurring revenue or interconnection count.

The [Digital Realty deep memo](../deep-company-pages/digital-realty-trust-inc.md)
makes the counterpoint explicit: 4Q25 bookings and the 2026 development-yield
guide are demand evidence, but DLR still has to fund recurring capex and leasing
costs plus $3.25B-$3.75B of development capex through debt, partner capital,
and future rent. Backlog conversion and powered-capacity returns therefore need
to be tested against financing and dilution, not only against lease demand.

### Amazon: cloud growth with the largest directly visible capital bill

Amazon is the cohort's broad demand owner. It captures revenue from consumer
shopping, seller services, advertising, subscriptions, and AWS, so AI demand
can be funded by more than cloud billing alone. In FY2025, AWS produced
$128.725B of revenue and $45.606B of operating income, while consolidated
operating cash flow was $139.514B.

The burden is unusually visible. Amazon spent $128.320B on net cash property
and equipment in FY2025. In the first six months of 2026, it spent $96.3B on
cash capital expenditures and $39.8B on acquisitions and other investments.
The latest 10-Q also reports $28.7B invested in OpenAI preferred stock. These
investments may be strategically sound, but they are not the same as cash
earned by AWS. Amazon's Q2 2026 trailing twelve-month free cash flow was
negative $7.604B after property spending.

Amazon therefore adds a necessary control case to the AI comparison. Cloud
customer demand can be strong while common-owner cash is weak during a major
capacity build. The key test is incremental operating income and cash after
server renewal, power, data-center leases, financing, strategic investments,
and dilution. This is a stronger test than AWS growth alone.

### F5 and KLA: cleaner control layers, different cyclicality

F5's FY2025 revenue was about $3.088B, gross margin about 81.4%, operating cash
flow about $949.7M, and services revenue about $1.579B. Its high-margin control
position is attractive, but services and systems remain material. The question
is whether AI/security attach creates recurring software economics without
proportionate service labor.

KLA's FY2026 revenue reached $13.58B, and its process-control position appears
harder to cut because yield loss is more expensive than inspection. The risk is
not absence of a moat; it is normalization. Track equipment revenue, service and
installed-base revenue, customer capex, gross margin, R&D, share repurchases,
working capital, and the next semiconductor investment cycle.

The [F5 deep memo](../deep-company-pages/f5-inc.md) sharpens the software-side
control test. F5's FY2025 $949.7M OCF included a $200.5M deferred-revenue
contribution, while $231.5M of SBC and $171.1M of acquisition cash sit outside
the headline operating number. Its October 2025 cyber incident also makes
trust, renewal, remediation, and insurance costs part of the economics. F5 is
cleaner than physical capacity owners, but its recurring layer must still be
tested for services labor, deferred obligations, and customer retention.

### Quanta and Sterling: backlog-to-cash is the burden test

Quanta's Q2 total backlog was $53.4B and RPO was $33.6B. Sterling's Q2 backlog
was $4.33B, with 92% of E-Infrastructure backlog mission-critical. MasTec's Q2
18-month backlog was $21.4B across communications, clean energy, power delivery,
and pipeline infrastructure. Comfort Systems' 2025 backlog reached $11.94B and
then $14.06B in Q2 2026; EMCOR's
remaining performance obligations reached $13.25B at 2025 year-end and $17.14B
in Q2 2026. These numbers show that AI and grid investment reaches a broad
field-execution layer, not just a small set of specialist contractors.

The forensic question is whether project margin and cash conversion survive the
labor bottleneck. Track billings, unbilled receivables, contract assets,
customer advances, project claims, change orders, safety costs, equipment,
subcontractors, and retention. Future-phase opportunities must not be allowed
to substitute for signed, profitable work.

### Comfort Systems and EMCOR: labor scarcity meets backlog accounting

The local [Comfort Systems packet](../../extracted/industrial-goods/general-contractors/comfort-systems-usa-inc/company-packet.md)
and [EMCOR packet](../../extracted/industrial-goods/general-contractors/emcor-group-inc/company-packet.md)
add an important contradiction test. Comfort Systems reported 2025 revenue of
$9.10B, net income of $1.02B, operating cash flow of $1.19B, and backlog of
$11.94B; by Q2 2026 its backlog was $14.06B. EMCOR reported 2025 revenue of
$16.99B, net income of $1.27B, and year-end RPO of $13.25B; Q2 2026 RPO was
$17.14B. Both packets identify skilled labor, project execution, and
subcontractor capacity as constraints.

The evidence is directionally strong but not yet a full owner-cash conclusion:
the packets do not currently contain a complete local raw filing chain for
multi-year capex, working capital, contract assets, claims, SBC, debt, and
acquisition returns. The next filing read should reconcile backlog growth to
revenue recognized, gross-margin movement, cash collected, contract assets and
liabilities, retainage, safety costs, and maintenance investment. If backlog
rises while cash conversion or project margin deteriorates, these companies
demonstrate that labor scarcity can create revenue visibility without clean
owner economics.

## Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

Alden's durability lens favors KLA's yield-protection necessity, Equinix's
ecosystem density, and grid/power reliability work over purely speculative
compute expansion. Damodaran's lens asks which company can reinvest at high
returns as the market grows, and whether the market price already discounts a
long period of exceptional growth. Our forensic lens restores tariffs,
services, working capital, capex, labor, warranty, and financing costs that
headline AI narratives often omit.

The cleanest current control candidates are KLA at a technical chokepoint,
F5 at enterprise application control, and Equinix at dense interconnection.
Vertiv is the strongest equipment conversion case but must prove backlog
realization. Generac is a transition case with a clear temporary-benefit risk.
Quanta, Sterling, MasTec, Comfort Systems, and EMCOR demonstrate demand
pull-through while carrying more of the physical and labor burden than the cleaner
control layers. MasTec provides the broad diversified contractor sample; Sterling
provides the concentrated mission-critical sample; Comfort Systems currently has
the clearest packet-level labor-squeeze signal; and EMCOR provides a larger
diversified sample against which to test whether scale improves or merely hides
that burden.

## Falsifiers and next filing watchlist

The thesis weakens if:

- backlog or orders rise while gross margin and free cash flow fall;
- tariff, tax, or other policy benefits explain a disproportionate share of
  margin expansion;
- power, labor, equipment, or construction capex rises faster than contracted
  pricing and cash collections;
- data-center leases or interconnections grow without adequate return on new
  powered capacity;
- software/security revenue requires steadily increasing services labor;
- semiconductor process-control demand proves more deferrable than assumed; or
- field contractors recognize revenue while contract assets, claims, safety
  costs, or unbilled receivables accumulate.
- contractor backlog grows faster than staffing, productivity, or collected
  cash, especially at Comfort Systems and EMCOR.
- MasTec's backlog and adjusted EBITDA expand while operating cash remains below
  capex and debt rises through acquisitions.

### MasTec: diversification does not solve the cash bridge

The [MasTec deep memo](../deep-company-pages/mastec-inc.md) adds the broad
contractor control case. Its Q2 2026 backlog was $21.391B and six-month segment
EBITDA was $779.3M, but six-month operating cash flow was only about $120.3M
against $188.3M of capex. Accounts receivable and contract assets reached about
$4.23B combined, while debt obligations were $2.753B and cash was $315.6M.

MasTec is the diversification comparison to Sterling's more concentrated
mission-critical mix: four operating lanes can smooth demand, but they also make
mix, customer concentration, acquisition contribution, and project-estimate
quality harder to see. The evidence supports a durable physical-demand lane, not
a clean owner-cash conclusion. The next filing must show backlog converting
without contract assets and debt outrunning collections.

### NextEra: the utility can recover capital, but shareholders still fund growth

The [NextEra deep memo](../deep-company-pages/nextera-energy-inc.md) adds the
regulated-generation layer. FPL's rate-base mechanism is cleaner than contractor
backlog: approved investment can earn an allowed return. But the consolidated
company invested $19.389B in the first six months of 2026 against $7.276B of
operating cash flow, with debt, tax-credit proceeds, capitalized interest, NCI,
and project guarantees filling the funding gap. NextEra therefore demonstrates
that regulated recovery improves cash-flow visibility without making growth
capital free or eliminating financing and affordability risk.

For the next filing window, capture organic orders, book-to-bill, backlog/RPO
definitions, revenue conversion, gross margin, GAAP and adjusted profit,
operating cash flow, free cash flow, inventory, receivables, contract assets,
customer advances, capex, maintenance capex, power availability, interconnection
growth, utilization, services mix, warranty, labor, and customer concentration.

The cross-sector conclusion is conditional: AI's most defensible economics may
settle at scarce control points, but every control-point thesis must be tested
against the physical system that makes it possible.

## Valuation: demand must survive the capacity bill

Value each layer from the cash it retains after the capacity needed to serve
the demand. Chips and process-control tools require different assumptions from
data-center real estate, power equipment, and contractors. The model should
separate customer orders, delivered revenue, utilization, maintenance and
growth capital, inventory, warranty, power, leases, financing, acquisitions,
and dilution.

Use normal, delayed-build, lower-utilization, and lower-price cases. The current
valuation is fragile if it requires rapid AI demand, full capacity utilization,
stable pricing, and uninterrupted capital returns while ignoring the cash
required for power, cooling, replacement, and customer concentration. The
relevant denominator is owner cash after the physical system is funded, not
the headline size of the AI opportunity.

The [Generac deep memo](../deep-company-pages/generac-holdings-inc.md) adds the
backup-power layer beneath the data-center story. Its C&I and data-center
backlog supports a real physical-control thesis, but FY2025 cash after capex
fell to roughly $268M and Q2 2026 margin included a material tariff-refund
benefit. Generac therefore tests whether resilience demand becomes durable
owner cash after inventory, warranty, factory capacity, legal costs, and debt.
