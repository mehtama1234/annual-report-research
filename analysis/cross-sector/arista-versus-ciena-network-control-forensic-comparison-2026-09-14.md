# Arista Versus Ciena: Network-Fabric Control and the Cash Cost of AI Demand

Date baseline: `2026-09-14`

Evidence base: Arista and Ciena fiscal `2025` annual filings, the latest
reported quarter materials preserved in the archive, and their company
dossiers. This is a control-point and cash-quality comparison, not a current
market-price ranking.

## Executive conclusion

Arista and Ciena both benefit from the growth of data movement, but they occupy
different parts of the network:

- Arista controls switching, routing, network operating software, and
  observability inside data centers, campuses, and AI fabrics.
- Ciena controls optical transport, routing, switching, and network management
  across long-haul, metro, carrier, and data-center connections.

Arista has the cleaner reported economics in the current evidence. Fiscal 2025
Arista operating cash flow was `$4.372B` against `$119.5M` of property,
equipment, and intangible purchases. Ciena fiscal 2025 operating cash flow was
`$806.1M`, capital purchases were `$140.8M`, and the Nubis acquisition used
`$231.1M`; the mechanical residual after both was about `$434M`.

The difference is not only margin. Arista's cash conversion was supported by a
`$2.452B` increase in deferred revenue, while Ciena's latest growth phase
consumed working capital and included a `$72.4M` inventory-obsolescence
provision. Arista has less physical burden but greater hyperscaler and
architecture concentration. Ciena has more hardware, inventory, and production
burden but can benefit from broader optical-network demand.

## 1. Control point and customer dependency

| Company | Control point | Customer replacement work | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arista | Data-center and AI-fabric switching architecture plus EOS/CloudVision | Topology redesign, software migration, testing, retraining, performance validation | Hyperscaler concentration and architecture change |
| Ciena | Optical transport, routing, switching, and network automation | Network qualification, optical testing, field integration, acceptance, and service transition | Backlog conversion, inventory, receivables, and carrier/cloud cycle |

Both companies gain value from qualification and operating dependence. Arista's
software and telemetry make the fabric more embedded. Ciena's optical systems
and network integration create a broader physical qualification burden.

## 3. Revenue visibility is not the same as cash quality

### Arista

Arista's deferred-revenue increase was a major operating-cash contributor. This
can reflect strong customer prepayment and contract demand. It also means that
future service, support, and delivery obligations remain. The next test is
whether deferred revenue converts into recognized revenue and gross profit as
the AI-network cycle matures.

Arista's accounts receivable, inventory, and other assets consumed cash despite
the strong deferred-revenue inflow. High margins therefore do not eliminate
working-capital risk.

### Ciena

Ciena's fiscal 2025 orders were `$7.8B` and year-end backlog was `$5.0B`, but
backlog is not cash. The order-to-cash path is:

`orders -> backlog -> production -> shipment -> acceptance -> receivable collection -> cash`

During the first nine months of fiscal 2026, receivables and inventory consumed
cash, while inventory-obsolescence provisions rose. Ciena's growth therefore
requires direct testing of inventory quality, customer acceptance, payment
terms, and receivable factoring.

## 4. Alden lens: liquidity and cycle durability

Arista has more liquidity and lower capital intensity, but its demand is more
concentrated in large cloud and AI-fabric customers. A spending pause or
architecture change can affect a large portion of growth quickly.

Ciena has a broader network role and may benefit from carrier, cloud, and
long-haul demand, but it carries more inventory, production, warranty, R&D, and
customer-acceptance risk. Its liquidity stress can appear before revenue falls
because inventory and receivables absorb cash.

The stress tests are different:

- Arista: can cash per diluted share remain strong when deferred-revenue growth
  slows and hyperscaler purchasing normalizes?
- Ciena: can backlog convert to cash without inventory reserves, receivable
  factoring, and working-capital consumption rising?

## 5. Damodaran lens: translate the AI-network narrative

| Narrative | Arista required evidence | Ciena required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| AI networking | AI-fabric revenue, customer concentration, ports, mix, margin | Cloud-provider revenue, optical growth, orders, backlog, and acceptance |
| Control point | EOS, CloudVision, installed architecture, renewal and cross-sell | Optical qualification, Blue Planet, services, and network integration |
| Visibility | Deferred revenue, backlog, billings, and conversion | Orders, backlog, cancellation terms, shipment, and collection |
| Asset-light | Capital purchases, outsourced manufacturing, inventory, and warranty | Production capacity, inventory, component commitments, and warranty |
| Cash quality | Deferred-revenue reversal, AR, inventory, SBC, acquisition cash | AR, inventory, factoring, obsolescence, SBC, and acquisitions |

Arista's lifecycle risk is a high-margin platform attracting competition and
customer bargaining power. Ciena's lifecycle risk is a strong optical cycle
requiring continued R&D and production investment before demand normalizes.

## 6. Who captures the cleaner economics?

| Layer | Cleaner position | Burden |
|---|---|---|
| Data-center fabric | Arista's switching and software control | Hyperscaler concentration, product transitions, and SBC |
| Optical transport | Ciena's qualification and network performance | Inventory, production, obsolescence, warranty, and receivables |
| Network software | Arista EOS/CloudVision and Ciena Blue Planet | R&D, support, adoption, and hardware attachment |
| AI demand | Both benefit from capacity and traffic growth | Customer capex cycles and architecture shifts |
| Cash conversion | Arista currently shows stronger margin and residual cash | Deferred revenue and working-capital reversal risk |
| Physical capacity | Neither owns the full carrier or data-center system | Suppliers, customers, and infrastructure owners fund the larger stack |

Arista captures the cleaner economics in the current evidence because it sells a
high-margin control layer with low capital purchases. Ciena remains valuable but
has more physical and working-capital burden. That judgment is conditional on
Arista's deferred-revenue cash converting cleanly and on customer concentration
not weakening its pricing power.

## 7. Valuation expectations

This comparison does not use current market values. The correct bridges differ:

### Arista

`equity value -> normalized owner cash after deferred-revenue normalization, SBC, acquisition cash, and dilution`

### Ciena

`equity value -> normalized owner cash after backlog conversion, inventory, factoring, capex, acquisitions, SBC, and dilution`

Arista deserves a higher-quality multiple only if its software and architecture
control remain durable through a cloud-capex pause. Ciena deserves a high
multiple only if backlog converts into cash without the working-capital and
obsolescence costs seen in the current checkpoint.

## 8. Filing-based falsifiers

### Arista

- deferred revenue reverses while revenue growth slows;
- a small number of customers drive an increasing share of sales;
- receivables and inventory grow faster than revenue;
- gross margin falls as AI product mix normalizes;
- SBC and dilution absorb owner-cash growth; or
- CloudVision and EOS do not deepen customer dependence.

### Ciena

- backlog rises while shipments, collections, and cash do not;
- inventory reserves rise as AI-network demand remains strong;
- factoring or payment-term changes support operating cash;
- gross margin depends on tariff refunds or temporary mix;
- cloud concentration increases without contract protection; or
- convertible debt and SBC absorb per-share value.

## Conclusion

Arista and Ciena show why “AI networking” is not one economic category.
Arista controls the data-center fabric and software architecture with cleaner
reported cash economics. Ciena controls optical transport and carries more
inventory, production, and acceptance burden.

The next filings should test Arista's deferred-revenue normalization and
hyperscaler concentration alongside Ciena's backlog-to-cash conversion and
inventory quality. The valuable control point is not established by demand
growth alone; it is established when the control layer produces durable cash per
diluted share after the specific burden required to keep it working.
