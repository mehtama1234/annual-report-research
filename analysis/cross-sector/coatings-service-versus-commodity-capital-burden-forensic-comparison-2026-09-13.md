# Coatings service versus commodity capital burden: forensic comparison

Sherwin-Williams adds a controlled-distribution and contractor-workflow
contradiction to the Ecolab/Nucor materials lane.

| Company | Control point | FY2025 OCF | Capex/acquisition burden | Core question |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Sherwin-Williams | Professional coatings, branch density, formulation, and technical workflow | $3.452B | $0.798B / $1.211B | Can pricing, repaint demand, and pro share gains support a premium after debt and acquisition returns? |
| Ecolab | Embedded water, hygiene, food-safety, and compliance service | $2.953B | $1.048B / $1.621B | Do service relationships convert acquisition and field investment into recurring cash? |
| Nucor | EAF steel, downstream fabrication, and domestic production capacity | $3.234B | $3.422B / $0.002B | Can utilization, spreads, and project returns overcome commodity cyclicality and heavy capex? |

The economic hierarchy is not simply “service good, commodity bad.”
Sherwin-Williams and Ecolab control customer workflows, but both must fund
field labor, plants, branches, technology, acquisitions, and working capital.
Nucor has a more visible physical-capacity burden, but its disciplined balance
sheet and downstream mix can create value when the cycle and project returns
cooperate.

## Q2 2026 Sherwin-Williams checkpoint

Sherwin-Williams’ latest quarter supports the workflow thesis but narrows the
claim. Paint Stores Group same-store sales grew `4.2%`, with mid-single-digit
pricing and low-single-digit volume growth. Consolidated gross margin was
`49.2%`, slightly below the prior year, because Suvinil diluted the mix and raw
material costs increased. Consumer Brands sales rose `21.5%`, primarily because
of the Suvinil acquisition; this is acquired growth, not a clean organic
proof-point.

The cash and balance-sheet test is less comfortable than the earnings headline.
Six-month operating cash was `$1.487B`, while capital expenditures were
`$246.7M`, acquisitions were `$64.9M`, dividends were `$394.6M`, and share
repurchases were `$1.837B`. Receivables reached `$3.571B` and total debt
`$12.072B`, against `$293.5M` of cash. Sherwin-Williams can still have a
valuable professional distribution relationship, but the owner’s residual must
be measured after working-capital growth, acquisition integration, interest,
and the financing used to support repurchases.

This makes the comparison more precise: Ecolab’s service economics depend on
field execution and acquisition returns; Sherwin-Williams’ depend on price,
availability, and contractor share; Nucor’s depend on utilization and project
returns. Each control point can protect gross profit while still producing a
less clean common-owner cash stream than the headline margin implies.

## Forensic distinction

Sherwin-Williams' $1.443B residual after capex and acquisitions is positive but
below its $2.446B of 2025 capital returns. Ecolab's residual was approximately
$283M after a larger acquisition/affiliate outlay. Nucor's residual was
approximately negative $190M after PP&E and acquisitions. The correct reading
is therefore:

`workflow control -> pricing and retention -> gross profit -> maintenance and
growth capital -> acquisition return -> debt and capital returns`

not:

`high gross margin -> free cash flow -> justified premium multiple`.

## Business economics: service control versus physical capacity

Sherwin-Williams and Ecolab monetize embedded customer workflows: product
selection, formulation, compliance, replenishment, and field support. Nucor
monetizes steel production, scrap access, downstream fabrication, and domestic
capacity. The first two can retain a customer relationship without owning the
entire downstream project; Nucor carries more of the plant, energy, inventory,
labor, and cycle burden directly.

The correct comparison is not gross margin. It is customer dependence, price
and retention, volume and utilization, working capital, maintenance and growth
capital, acquisition cost, debt, and cash per continuing share.

## Valuation: workflow cash after the capital burden

Value Sherwin-Williams and Ecolab from organic fee or product growth after
branch, plant, field labor, service, acquisition, and debt costs. Value Nucor
from mid-cycle steel spreads and utilization after sustaining capital, energy,
working capital, environmental obligations, and project returns. Use normal,
weak-cycle, and successful-investment cases rather than applying a service
multiple to commodity cash.

The price is demanding if it requires professional-channel growth, service
pricing, high steel utilization, and acquisition returns at the same time. The
useful denominator is owner cash after the system that produces the revenue is
maintained.

## What to check next

Track organic growth, retention, price, volume, utilization, raw-material and
energy cost, branch or field productivity, inventory, receivables, capex,
acquisition cohorts, debt, goodwill, and diluted shares. The conclusion
strengthens only when customer control survives a weaker cycle and the cash
bridge remains positive after renewal and growth spending.

## Alden and Damodaran synthesis

The Alden lens favors Sherwin-Williams and Ecolab when maintenance,
regulation, and customer complexity protect demand through weak liquidity; it
favors Nucor when domestic capacity, energy economics, and industrial policy
support utilization. The rate and credit cycle still reaches all three through
housing, industrial projects, customer budgets, raw materials, and debt.

The Damodaran lens asks whether the next dollar of branch, field-service,
plant, or steel capacity earns more than the cost of capital. Sherwin-Williams
has the most attractive customer-interface economics of the three, but its
current price requires durable professional-channel growth and disciplined
acquisition integration. Ecolab requires service cash to catch up with a high
multiple. Nucor requires through-cycle project returns rather than one strong
steel year.
