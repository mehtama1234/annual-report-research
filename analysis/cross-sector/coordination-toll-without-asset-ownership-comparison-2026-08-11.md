# Coordination Toll Without Asset Ownership Comparison

Date: 2026-08-11
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Plain Claim

Some of the cleanest businesses in the archive do not own most of the underlying asset or risk.

They own the coordination layer.

They get paid to:

- route demand
- place risk
- organize transactions
- manage membership
- simplify search
- keep the customer inside one workflow

The common pattern is not manufacturing, lending, or property ownership.

It is orchestration.

## Why This Comparison Matters

The archive already has pages on:

- relationship owners versus burden carriers
- interface ownership
- status and wallet systems
- institutional overhead

What it did not yet have as a standalone proof page is a direct comparison of coordination tolls that sit between a customer and a fragmented underlying system.

This memo asks:

Which companies capture recurring economics by making a messy system easier to navigate without owning most of the underlying asset base?

## What The Comparison Clarifies

### 1. Fragmented systems create attractive coordination tolls.

Travel supply is fragmented.

Insurance markets are fragmented.

Merchant acceptance and premium spend behavior are fragmented.

The company that makes those systems easier to navigate can capture recurring economics without owning every underlying asset.

### 2. Customer workflow control can matter more than direct asset ownership.

Booking does not need to own most of the rooms.

Marriott does not need to own most of the hotels.

Marsh, Aon, and Gallagher do not need to carry most of the underwriting risk.

American Express does not need to make the goods it finances or rewards.

The moat often sits in the repeated decision surface instead.

### 3. Coordination tolls look cleaner when the underlying burden sits elsewhere.

Hotel owners carry property maintenance and real-estate burden.

Insurers carry underwriting and balance-sheet exposure.

Travel suppliers carry the fixed-cost burden of aircraft, rooms, or fleets.

Merchants carry inventory and operating complexity.

The coordination layer captures part of the economics by simplifying the transaction without owning all of that burden.

### 4. Loyalty and repeat behavior strengthen the toll.

Booking is moving deeper into merchant mix, payments, direct behavior, and app engagement.

American Express reinforces spend through membership and benefits.

Marriott reinforces repeat-stay behavior through loyalty.

Insurance brokers reinforce repeat renewal and advisory dependence through client relationships and market knowledge.

The best versions of this model make the next decision easier than starting over.

### 5. Coordination businesses are still exposed to trust and relevance risk.

These are not effortless toll roads.

If the customer no longer trusts the intermediary, or finds an easier direct path, the value can weaken quickly.

That is why brand, data, loyalty, platform usefulness, and advisory credibility all matter so much.

## Stronger Conclusion

The stronger conclusion is that some of the archive’s best economics sit in coordination layers that organize fragmented systems.

These companies do not need to own every hotel room, every insurance liability, every merchant inventory item, or every travel seat.

They need to own the workflow that helps the customer decide, book, place, pay, or renew.

The key cross-company insight is:

When a market is fragmented and the customer needs help navigating it repeatedly, the coordination layer can become more valuable than a large share of the underlying asset base.

## Aha Moments

### 1. Booking and Marsh are farther apart by taxonomy than by business logic.

One routes travel demand and the other routes insurance demand, but both monetize search, comparison, trust, and placement in fragmented markets.

### 2. Marriott belongs partly in the same family as platforms and brokers.

It looks like a hotel company, but much of the value comes from brand, standards, and loyalty coordination layered on top of owner capital.

### 3. American Express is partly a coordination business, not just a lender or card issuer.

Its membership model works because it coordinates merchants, rewards, data, status, and spend behavior in one closed loop.

### 4. The best coordination tolls lower friction before the next transaction happens.

That is why direct mix, loyalty membership, merchant mix, and renewal behavior matter so much across this group.

## Thesis Breakers

This coordination-toll thesis weakens if:

- customers increasingly bypass the intermediary and go direct
- loyalty or membership loops stop improving repeat behavior
- merchants, hotel owners, or insurers recapture more of the economics from the coordination layer
- advisory or placement businesses lose trust or pricing power
- platform relevance weakens because the workflow becomes easier to self-manage
