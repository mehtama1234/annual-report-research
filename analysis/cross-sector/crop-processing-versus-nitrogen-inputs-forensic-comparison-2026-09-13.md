# Agricultural inputs and crop protection: Bunge, CF Industries, Nutrien, Mosaic, and FMC

Date: 2026-09-13

This comparison asks whether four agricultural businesses create owner value in
the same way. Bunge moves crops through origination, storage, processing, and
merchandising. CF converts natural gas and industrial capacity into nitrogen.
Nutrien combines fertilizer production with retail and agronomy. Mosaic converts
phosphate and potash inputs and distributes fertilizer in Brazil. FMC develops
crop-protection chemistry. All serve food production, but they carry different
financial burdens.

## Business economics: start with the control point

| Question | Bunge Global | CF Industries |
|---|---|---|
| What the customer depends on | Crop sourcing, processing capacity, logistics, and market access | Reliable ammonia and nitrogen production with access to competitive gas |
| Main physical burden | Inventory, receivables, seasonal debt, plants, and commodity-price exposure | Plants, gas input, outages, environmental work, and sustaining capital |
| FY2025 OCF | $844M | $2.752B |
| FY2025 PP&E spending | $1.723B | $950M |
| Acquisitions | $4.201B Viterra cash outflow | No comparable acquisition in the filed bridge |
| OCF less listed uses | Negative $5.080B after PP&E and Viterra | $1.802B after PP&E |

The difference is not that one company is “asset-light.” Bunge carries more
working capital and used a large acquisition to change its network. CF carries
large process plants and is more exposed to gas costs, utilization, and outage
risk. The correct owner-cash denominator is therefore different.

Mosaic adds a fourth burden pattern. Its FY2025 operating cash flow was $825M
against $1.359B of PP&E spending, leaving a negative $535M screen. Inventory
was $3.363B. The issue was not absence of fertilizer demand; sulfur, ammonia,
phosphate-rock costs, utilization, Brazil execution, and inventory absorbed the
cash.

## First-principles questions

1. Is the control point scarce because customers lack alternatives, or because
   the current price environment is tight?
2. Which cost must be funded before the company can distribute cash: inventory
   and debt at Bunge, or plant renewal and gas supply at CF?
3. Does scale improve returns after integration and capital, or merely increase
   the amount of capital at risk?
4. What happens when commodity prices normalize while replacement costs remain
   high?

## Lyn Alden lens

Both companies connect agriculture to energy, trade, and geopolitics. Bunge is
more exposed to crop flows, working-capital funding, and trade-policy changes.
CF is more directly exposed to energy input prices, nitrogen supply, and plant
availability. In both cases, strategic importance can coexist with weak
shareholder returns if debt or renewal capital absorbs the cash.

## Damodaran lens

Value Bunge by segment: origination and merchandising, processing, Viterra
synergies, and the capital needed to support inventory and receivables. Value CF
by cycle-normalized nitrogen prices, gas cost per ton, utilization, sustaining
capital, and separately the probability-weighted Blue Point project.

The market should not capitalize Bunge's acquisition-adjusted cash or CF's
strong nitrogen margin as permanent without evidence across a weaker period.
For each company, forecast revenue, operating margin, reinvestment, financing
cost, and share count rather than applying a multiple to adjusted EBITDA alone.

## Forensic questions to carry forward

- Bunge: Do Viterra synergies appear in cash after inventory, receivables,
  seasonal debt, integration spending, and goodwill? How much of reported
  trading performance is mark-to-market or timing?
- CF: What is realized nitrogen price less gas and other cash costs? How much of
  PP&E spending is sustaining? Does Yazoo return on schedule, and does Blue
  Point earn a return after partner funding and construction cost?
- Both: Are buybacks made below a conservative estimate of intrinsic value, and
  are dividends covered after all necessary renewal spending?

## Working conclusion

Bunge is a network and working-capital case whose 2025 owner-cash picture was
dominated by Viterra funding. CF is a focused production case with a cleaner
filed cash screen, but its earnings remain sensitive to nitrogen prices, gas
costs, utilization, and outages. The next useful evidence is not another demand
statement. It is a multi-year bridge showing what cash remains after the costs
each business must bear to preserve its control point.

## Nutrien adds the integration test

Nutrien combines upstream fertilizer with retail and agronomy. Its FY2025
operating cash flow was $4.007B and management free cash flow was $1.979B, but
inventory was $6.977B, receivables were $5.675B, debt was $10.736B, and supplier
financing helped operating cash. That makes Nutrien a useful middle case:

| Model | Potential benefit | Main proof required |
|---|---|---|
| CF focused producer | Cleaner exposure to plant utilization and gas-cost advantage | Mid-cycle nitrogen price, gas cost, sustaining capital, and outage history |
| Nutrien integrated system | Potash scale plus retail access and proprietary products | Segment cash, supplier-financing normalization, mine renewal, retail working capital, and portfolio exits |
| Mosaic conversion system | Phosphate and potash production with Brazil distribution | Sulfur, ammonia, and rock costs; inventory liquidation; curtailments; Brazil cash; and sustaining capital |
| FMC crop-protection chemistry | Active ingredients, formulations, registrations, and technical route to market | Patent expiry, generic pricing, product renewal, registration spending, factoring, restructuring, and debt |
| Bunge crop-flow network | Origination, processing, logistics, and customer access | Viterra cash synergies after inventory, receivables, debt, and integration |

Integration should earn its place only if the combined business produces more
resilient owner cash after the added capital and financing burden. FMC is the
distinct science-and-lifecycle case: its FY2025 continuing operating cash flow
was negative $6M against $843M of adjusted EBITDA, so product renewal and
financial repair must be proven together.

## What to check next

The next filing should separate mid-cycle price, volume, utilization, input
cost, inventory, receivables, supplier financing, sustaining capital, growth
projects, product registrations, patent or generic pressure, and debt. For
Nutrien and Bunge, test whether integration reduces working-capital and
financing burden rather than only increasing scale. For FMC, test whether
product renewal and restructuring produce cash after registrations, factoring,
and debt. The thesis strengthens only when the reported improvement survives a
normal crop, fertilizer, and chemical-price environment.
