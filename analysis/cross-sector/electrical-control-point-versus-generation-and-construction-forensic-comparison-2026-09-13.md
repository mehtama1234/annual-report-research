# Electrical control point versus generation and construction: who captures AI power economics?

Eaton, Cummins, Vertiv, Generac, and EMCOR sit at different points in the same electricity buildout. Comparing them by headline growth obscures who controls the scarce interface and who carries the capital or execution burden.

| Company | Control point | FY2025 mechanical screen | Main burden |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Eaton | Electrical distribution, protection, controls, and aerospace systems | OCF $4.472B less PP&E $919M less acquisitions $1.490B = **$2.063B** | Acquisition goodwill, working capital/unbilled revenue, separation costs, debt, capacity expansion |
| Cummins | Engines, generators, distribution, service, and microgrids | **$2.374B** | Truck cyclicality, warranty, emissions liabilities, inventory, Accelera transition charges |
| Vertiv | Data-center power and cooling integration | **$709M** after PP&E | Working-capital reversal, customer concentration, warranty, acquisitions, capacity execution |
| Generac | Standby/C&I generation and resilience channels | **$268M** after capex | Residential cycle, tariff timing, inventory, warranty, hyperscale pricing |
| EMCOR | Electrical/mechanical field execution | **$167M** after capex before other normalization | Labor scarcity, project estimates, contract assets, backlog conversion, acquisition effects |

## The control-point hierarchy

Eaton is closest to the electrical architecture that every data center and grid upgrade needs, so its products may be harder to substitute than a generator order. Cummins and Generac monetize the reliability edge, but their equipment remains more negotiable with large buyers. Vertiv controls an integrated power-and-cooling workflow inside the facility. EMCOR captures execution scarcity where customer capex becomes installed infrastructure.

The strongest control point is not automatically the best investment. Eaton’s FY2025 capital returns exceeded its post-capex/acquisition residual, while EMCOR’s backlog visibility coexists with thin post-capex cash. Cummins has a better mechanical residual but carries heavier warranty and emissions history. Quality and price must be evaluated together.

## The shared forensic test

All five names require the same bridge:

`orders/backlog -> shipment or project completion -> billed revenue -> collected cash -> maintenance/growth capital -> owner cash`

Eaton is the clearest case where the bridge currently has a warning sign: unbilled receivables rose to $759M from $330M and 2025 operating cash absorbed $777M across receivables, inventory, and unbilled receivables. That may be normal growth, but the next filing must show conversion. Cummins has a parallel test through inventory, service attach, and warranty. Vertiv and EMCOR require backlog-to-cash reconciliation, while Generac requires channel and tariff normalization.

## Valuation conclusion

Eaton’s approximately $165.7B equity value is about 80x its FY2025 mechanical residual and exceeds even a $148.5B bull case in the current scenario register. Cummins trades near 32.5x its screen and is approximately a base-case valuation. This contrast matters: Eaton may have the cleaner control point while Cummins offers the less demanding price. A superior moat does not eliminate the need for a sufficient return.

## Thesis breakers

1. Data-center projects are delayed or redesigned before backlog converts.
2. New manufacturing capacity causes price competition rather than durable scarcity.
3. Working capital and contract assets grow faster than revenue and operating cash.
4. Acquisition returns fail to exceed the cost of capital, producing goodwill impairment or leverage drag.
5. Utilities and grid owners eventually relieve the bottleneck, reducing the reliability premium.
