# Embedded quality and water versus hygiene and commodity capital

Date baseline: 2026-09-13. This comparison is a research artifact, not an
investment recommendation.

## The lane question

Which layer captures the cleaner economics behind essential physical systems:
measurement and product-quality control, site-embedded hygiene and water
service, or steel production and downstream capacity?

| Company | Economic control point | FY2025 cash bridge | Burden carrier |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Veralto | Water analytics, monitoring, coding, inspection, and traceability workflows | $1.077B OCF less $63M PP&E = $1.014B | Acquisition integration, R&D, customer qualification, installed support, and measurement commoditization |
| Ecolab | Embedded hygiene, water treatment, pest, and compliance service | $2.953B OCF less $1.048B PP&E less $1.621B acquisitions = $283M | Field labor, service density, chemistry inputs, acquisition cohorts, working capital, and debt |
| Nucor | EAF steel production, scrap network, downstream fabrication, and domestic capacity | $3.234B OCF less $3.422B PP&E less $2M acquisitions = $(190)M | Mills, energy, scrap, labor, environmental compliance, trade policy, and steel-cycle utilization |
| CECO Environmental | Engineered industrial air, water, thermal, and process-control systems | $5.861M OCF less $11.343M PP&E = $(5.482)M | Project execution, contract assets, working capital, customer acceptance, backlog timing, acquisitions, and integration |

## What the comparison shows

Veralto’s measurement and workflow model currently converts cash more cleanly
than Ecolab’s service-heavy model and Nucor’s capital-heavy production model.
That does not establish superior long-term returns. Veralto’s advantage may be
partly lifecycle timing: low PP&E does not mean low reinvestment if customer
qualification, R&D, software, service, and acquisitions are required to defend
the installed relationship.

Ecolab carries the most visible labor and customer-site burden. Nucor carries
the greatest physical capital and cycle burden. Veralto appears to capture the
highest measurement/control value per dollar of PP&E, but its key risk is that
bolt-on acquisitions and operating-system savings are capitalized as if they
were organic recurring growth.

CECO carries the heaviest project-execution and reporting-denominator burden.
Its 2025 revenue and net income rose while GAAP operating cash fell to $5.9M;
the packet’s $9.6M FCF figure therefore requires a precise reconciliation of
non-GAAP adjustments, divestiture effects, and working capital. Backlog is a
valuable demand signal, not a substitute for collected cash.

## Alden and Damodaran lenses

The Alden lens favors Veralto’s exposure to water scarcity, public-health
standards, traceability, and essential measurement, while recognizing that
industrial liquidity and customer budgets still govern the cycle. Ecolab has
similar non-discretionary demand but more field execution and input exposure.
Nucor has strategic domestic capacity and trade-policy support, but the highest
commodity and reinvestment sensitivity.

The Damodaran lens asks whether each narrative becomes durable owner cash:

- Veralto: prove organic growth, acquired-cohort returns, conversion after
  working capital, and the durability of cost-optimization savings.
- Ecolab: prove service pricing and field productivity cover labor/input costs
  after Ovivo and other acquisitions.
- Nucor: prove project returns and reserve-like productive capacity through a
  steel down-cycle, not merely during policy-supported pricing.

## Forensic questions and possible shenanigans

These are denominator and incentive tests, not misconduct allegations:

- Does Veralto’s FCF-conversion metric exclude acquisitions, R&D, restructuring,
  or recurring installed-support investment?
- Does Ecolab’s organic growth hide field labor, route density, or customer
  working-capital burdens behind pricing and mix?
- Does Nucor’s contract-sales visibility obscure price formulas, scrap pass-
  through, customer credit, and utilization risk?
- Does CECO’s adjusted FCF exclude recurring integration, transaction,
  contract-asset, warranty, or project-working-capital costs?
- Are buybacks made from recurring cash, or while management is still funding
  acquisitions, new mills, and technology/product qualification?
- Does goodwill grow faster than the cash earnings of the acquired control
  point?

## Current conclusion and falsifiers

Veralto currently captures the cleanest reported economics in this lane, but
only conditionally. It must demonstrate that low PP&E is not being replaced by
high acquisition and operating-system dependence. Ecolab remains the stronger
customer-site relationship case but with a much heavier labor and acquisition
burden. Nucor owns the most tangible physical capacity, yet FY2025 shows how
quickly that capacity can consume owner cash in a weak cycle.

CECO is the project-execution counterexample: strategic environmental demand
and backlog can be real while the statutory cash denominator is negative. It
should not be promoted from interesting infrastructure exposure to clean
compounder until the Thermon and backlog-to-cash bridges are visible.

## Business economics and valuation

Veralto controls measurement, monitoring, coding, inspection, and traceability
workflows. Ecolab controls embedded water, hygiene, food-safety, and compliance
service. Nucor controls steel production and downstream capacity. CECO sells
engineered projects and carries more acceptance, contract-asset, and execution
risk. These are different control points and should not share one margin or
cash denominator.

Value Veralto and Ecolab from recurring workflow cash after acquisitions,
service labor, R&D, working capital, and debt. Value Nucor from mid-cycle steel
cash after plant renewal and utilization risk. Value CECO from probability-
weighted project cash after contract assets, acceptance, acquisitions, and
integration. The price should be tested under normal, weak-cycle, and
capacity-investment cases; low PP&E alone does not prove low economic capital.

The next filings should compare organic growth, acquired revenue, gross and
operating margin, working capital, R&D/service investment, PP&E, goodwill,
debt, SBC, buybacks, and cash after all required reinvestment on a like-period
basis.
