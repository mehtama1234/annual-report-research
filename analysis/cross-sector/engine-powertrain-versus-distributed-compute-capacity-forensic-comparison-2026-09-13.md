# Engine powertrain versus distributed compute capacity: who owns the reliability premium?

Cummins, Generac, and Vertiv all benefit from the same macro impulse—digital load is making electricity continuity more valuable—but they occupy different control points.

| Company | Control point | FY2025 cash screen | Core quality question |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Cummins | Engines, generator platforms, distribution, service, microgrid-adjacent power | OCF $3.621B less PP&E $1.235B less acquisitions $12M = **$2.374B** | Does a cyclical engine OEM convert data-center demand into durable service cash after warranty, emissions, inventory, and transition costs? |
| Generac | Standby and C&I generator platform, controls, and resilience channel | OCF less capex = **$268M** | Does hyperscale/C&I growth outrun residential cyclicality, tariff timing, inventory, and warranty burden? |
| Vertiv | Data-center power, cooling, racks, and capacity integration | OCF less PP&E = **$709M** | Can backlog and pricing persist after working-capital support reverses and capacity/warranty costs normalize? |

## Business economics: the shared macro thesis is real, but the cash claims differ

Cummins is the broadest physical platform: truck and industrial engines, components, distribution, power generation, and Accelera. Generac is a more concentrated resilience OEM with stronger direct exposure to standby demand. Vertiv sells the interior electrical and thermal infrastructure of the data center. The same AI buildout therefore appears as Power Systems growth at Cummins, C&I/hyperscale demand at Generac, and power/cooling backlog at Vertiv.

The forensic distinction is that none of these is automatically a recurring-revenue software business. The buyer can renegotiate equipment pricing, dual-source, defer projects, or internalize controls. Recurrence must be demonstrated through service attach, installed-base retention, replacement cycles, deposits, and cash collection.

## What Cummins adds

Cummins makes the reliability stack more complete. Its FY2025 filing shows $3.621B of operating cash against $1.235B of PP&E capex, but also $5.822B of inventory, $5.818B of receivables, a $2.778B warranty accrual, and $6.792B of long-term debt/capital leases. It also recorded $458M of Accelera actions. The company can be a durable AI-power beneficiary and still have a materially lower normalized owner-cash denominator than EBITDA suggests.

Generac is more directly exposed to the data-center/resilience order cycle, so its key checks are backlog quality, tariff normalization, residential recovery, and factory capacity. Vertiv has the strongest “inside the compute hall” positioning, but its key checks are backlog-to-cash conversion, customer concentration, inventory and receivables, warranty/capacity investment, and whether acquired businesses earn back their integration cost.

## Cross-company thesis breakers

1. Data-center demand remains high but buyers use scale to push equipment prices down.
2. Grid investment catches up, reducing the premium for distributed generation and backup.
3. AI model efficiency or project delays compress the build cycle faster than capacity can be redeployed.
4. Warranty, emissions, tariffs, or component shortages absorb the apparent gross-margin expansion.
5. Backlog grows while receivables, inventory, and capex grow faster than operating cash.

## Research conclusion

The preferred control point is not simply the company closest to the hyperscaler. Durable economics rise with the difficulty of substitution and the length of the customer relationship. Vertiv owns a technically embedded interior workflow; Cummins owns a global installed base and service network but faces heavier cyclicality and liability; Generac has strong resilience demand but a narrower product and channel mix. The next step is to compare realized service economics and cash conversion, not headline Power Systems or backlog growth.

## Definitions and the common chain

**Installed base** means equipment already operating at customer sites. It can
produce service and replacement revenue, but only if the equipment remains in
use and the customer continues to pay for support.

**Backlog** means orders or contracted work not yet recognized as revenue. It
shows demand visibility, not collected cash or completed profit.

**Warranty burden** is the expected cost of repairing or replacing products
after sale. It is an ordinary economic cost of equipment reliability, even when
the cash is paid later.

The three companies should be read through the same chain:

`customer power need -> specification and order -> production and shipment -> installation and utilization -> service / warranty / replacement -> collection -> required reinvestment -> common-owner cash`

The chain prevents a data-center order, a generator backlog number, or a power-
systems revenue increase from being treated as recurring cash before the
equipment is installed, accepted, used, serviced, and paid for.

## Company-specific operating evidence

| Company | What the FY2025 figures show | What they do not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cummins | $3.621B OCF, $1.235B PP&E capex, $2.374B screen; $5.822B inventory, $5.818B receivables, $2.778B warranty accrual, and $6.792B long-term debt/capital leases | That power-generation demand is independent of truck and industrial cycles, or that Accelera and emissions transition spending will earn an adequate return |
| Generac | $4.209B sales, $438M OCF, and approximately $268M after capex; C&I and data-center demand improved while residential demand remained more cyclical | That backlog and data-center qualification will convert into stable margin and cash without tariff, inventory, warranty, and customer pricing pressure |
| Vertiv | Approximately $8.01B sales, $1.21B OCF, $501M PP&E capex, and $709M after PP&E; Q2 2026 adjusted FCF was approximately $925M | That backlog, pricing, and working-capital support will persist after capacity expansion, acquisitions, warranty costs, and customer concentration are normalized |

The screens are not directly comparable. Cummins owns a broad powertrain and
service system, Generac has a more concentrated generator and resilience
portfolio, and Vertiv sells the power and cooling systems inside data centers.
Their exposure to AI demand is therefore an indirect, direct, and interior-
facility exposure respectively.

## Cummins: broad installed base with multiple burdens

Cummins' control point is the combination of engines, generators, components,
distribution, parts, and service. The company can earn from the original
equipment, maintenance, replacement, and customer uptime. Its installed base
is valuable because customers need compatible parts and technical support.

The same breadth creates a difficult normalization problem. Cummins' inventory
and receivables are large because the company must supply a global industrial
network. Warranty accruals are material because equipment failure can produce
direct repair cost and reputational damage. Emissions regulation can require
new engineering, certification, and product transition spending before the new
products generate sufficient margin.

The $2.374B FY2025 residual is therefore a screening number. It precedes the
cost of preserving the distribution and service network, warranty experience,
emissions compliance, Accelera transition, debt, and working-capital capacity.
Data-center demand can improve Power Systems without eliminating truck,
construction, mining, and industrial cyclicality.

## Generac: resilience demand with a sharper channel test

Generac controls a generator and power-resilience product platform, including
residential standby, commercial and industrial systems, controls, and service.
The durable claim is that customers pay to avoid power interruption. The
cyclical claim is that residential replacement and new-home demand can weaken,
while large customers negotiate pricing and project timing.

Generac's FY2025 screen of approximately $268M after capex is much smaller than
its sales base. The difference requires examination of inventory, warranty,
factory capacity, tariffs, customer/channel working capital, and the cost of
qualifying products for data-center and other critical-load applications.

The strongest evidence would be C&I and data-center growth accompanied by stable
gross margin, collections, service attach, and warranty performance. The weak
evidence would be backlog or orders rising while residential weakness, channel
inventory, concessions, or factory investment absorb the cash.

## Vertiv: embedded data-center power and cooling

Vertiv controls an integrated facility layer: power distribution, thermal
management, racks, monitoring, and related services. The customer is not only
buying a generator; it is buying a coordinated system that keeps high-density
compute operating within temperature, power, and uptime limits.

That location inside the data center can produce pricing and service value, but
it also creates execution burden. Vertiv must manufacture and install complex
systems, manage suppliers, carry inventory, support customers, and expand
capacity ahead of demand. Acquisitions and working-capital movements can make
cash look stronger or weaker than the underlying installed workflow.

Vertiv's FY2025 $709M after-PP&E screen and 2026 quarterly adjusted FCF are
encouraging, but the proper test is GAAP cash after inventory and receivable
normalization, warranty and capacity costs, acquisition return, and the capital
needed to serve larger deployments. The data-center customer may be powerful
enough to capture some of the economics through volume and pricing negotiations.

## Alden lens: reliability demand and liquidity location

The macro thesis is that data-center growth, grid stress, extreme weather,
industrial continuity, and distributed generation make power reliability more
valuable. The liquidity question is where the cost sits when customers delay
projects or rates rise.

- Cummins carries a broad manufacturing, inventory, warranty, debt, and
  regulatory burden. It has more ways to earn service cash but more cyclical
  end markets.
- Generac carries a sharper residential and C&I demand mix, with product,
  channel, tariff, and qualification risk.
- Vertiv carries the most direct AI-facility exposure, with customer
  concentration, installation, supplier, acquisition, capacity, and working-
  capital risk.

In a liquidity contraction, a customer may delay a new data center, a utility
may defer grid work, or a homeowner may delay a generator replacement. The
company with the strongest order book is not necessarily the most resilient if
it has already built inventory, hired labor, or borrowed to expand capacity.

## Damodaran lens: convert reliability narrative into returns

For Cummins, forecast engine and power-system volumes separately, then model
service attach, warranty, emissions investment, inventory, receivables, debt,
and Accelera. A data-center growth assumption must not be allowed to replace a
through-cycle view of trucks and industrial equipment.

For Generac, separate residential, C&I, and data-center demand; model channel
inventory and pricing; and charge factory, warranty, tariff, and qualification
spending against incremental gross profit.

For Vertiv, model backlog conversion, product and service margin, customer
concentration, working capital, capacity capex, warranty, acquisitions, and
the expected life of data-center demand. Backlog is an input to revenue, not a
terminal value.

The common reinvestment question is:

`incremental reliability revenue - incremental equipment, service, warranty, labor, working-capital, and capacity cost -> return on invested capital`

If incremental returns do not exceed the cost of capital, higher demand can
increase revenue while reducing owner value.

## Forensic earnings-quality comparison

| Test | Cummins | Generac | Vertiv |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue timing | Equipment shipment, dealer/customer inventory, service, and power-system project timing | Channel orders, residential replacement, C&I qualification, and tariff/price timing | Backlog conversion, project installation, customer acceptance, and product/service mix |
| Cash timing | Inventory, receivables, payables, warranty, and emissions spending | Inventory, channel terms, warranty, and factory investment | Receivables, inventory, supplier terms, contract advances, warranty, and acquisitions |
| Main adjustment risk | Accelera actions, emissions transition, warranty, and restructuring | Adjusted EBITDA, tariff or legal items, warranty, and residential normalization | Adjusted FCF, acquisition integration, capacity spending, and working-capital support |
| Capital claim | Manufacturing, service network, R&D, inventory, and debt | Factory, inventory, product qualification, warranty, and channel support | Capacity, installation, supplier commitments, service labor, acquisitions, and debt |
| Falsifier | Power Systems grows but consolidated cash and returns weaken | C&I/backlog grows but margin and collections do not | Backlog grows but cash, margins, and acquisition returns deteriorate |

These are not allegations of accounting misconduct. They are tests for whether
the preferred operating measure remains representative after normal equipment
and service costs are included.

## Valuation expectations

The current scenario register places Cummins at approximately 32.5x its
$2.374B FY2025 screen, Generac at approximately 41.6x its $268M screen, and
Vertiv at approximately 142.5x its $709M screen. Those multiples are not a
ranking of business quality.

Cummins' lower screen multiple reflects a broader, more cyclical business and
requires service cash, Power Systems growth, warranty control, and a successful
transition without a large emissions or Accelera cash burden. Generac's price
requires C&I and data-center growth to offset residential cyclicality and to
produce more than a one-cycle order improvement. Vertiv's price requires a
substantial continuation of AI/data-center capacity growth, backlog conversion,
pricing, margin, and cash generation after capacity and acquisition investment.

The market-implied hurdle is highest for Vertiv. A strong control point does not
make a 142x mechanical residual automatically reasonable; the company must grow
normalized cash materially and preserve returns while funding the bottleneck.

## Conclusion

Vertiv has the most direct position inside the AI data-center physical system,
but it also carries the highest market-implied cash hurdle. Cummins has the
broadest installed-base and service system, but its economics remain exposed to
several non-AI cycles and regulatory costs. Generac has a clear reliability
need, but its cash outcome depends on whether commercial and data-center demand
can offset residential and channel volatility.

The durable investment conclusion requires more than high demand. It requires
each company to collect cash, preserve product reliability, fund the required
technical and physical capacity, and earn an adequate return on that capital.
