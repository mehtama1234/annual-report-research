# Healthcare distribution peer synthesis: owner cash versus flow scale

Date: 2026-09-13  
Companies: McKesson, Cencora, Cardinal Health

This is a source-grounded peer synthesis from the three existing deep memos.
It is not a recommendation or a claim of misconduct. The purpose is to hold
the comparable economics constant: all three are essential healthcare
intermediaries, but their 2025 cash and capital stories differ materially.

## The peer question

Do these companies earn durable owner cash from controlling pharmaceutical and
provider distribution, or does the apparent quality of the business depend on
vendor terms, working-capital timing, acquisition spending, legal claims, and
adjusted-earnings presentation?

The economic chain is:

`manufacturer access -> regulated logistics -> inventory and receivables -> provider/pharmacy workflow -> gross-profit spread -> cash conversion -> legal, debt, and reinvestment capacity`

Revenue is a poor cross-peer denominator because pharmaceuticals are largely
pass-through volume. The better comparison is gross profit, normalized working
capital, cash after sustaining capital, and the return on incremental specialty
or service investment.

## Like-for-like FY2025 screen

Amounts are $ billions. Fiscal year-ends differ: McKesson March 31, Cencora
September 30, and Cardinal June 30. “Residual” is a screening bridge, not a
steady-state owner-earnings claim.

| Company | Revenue | OCF | Capex | Acquisitions / investments | Residual after listed uses | Equity value snapshot |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| McKesson | 359.1 | 6.085 | 0.537 PP&E + 0.322 software | 0.024 acquisitions | **5.202** | 105.1 |
| Cencora | 321.3 | 3.875 | 0.668 | 4.096 acquired companies + 0.196 equity investments + 0.035 other | **(1.085)** | 62.4 |
| Cardinal Health | 222.6 | 2.397 | 0.547 | 5.250 acquisitions | **(3.400)** | 55.4 |

The arithmetic makes the central distinction visible. McKesson's listed 2025
residual is positive because its acquisition cash outlay was low in that year.
Cencora and Cardinal were simultaneously distributors and serial acquirers;
their post-growth-investment residuals were negative. That does not prove the
acquisitions are bad. It means the valuation must treat acquired specialty and
service cash as an uncertain future return, not as current distributable cash.

### The three-year residual path matters more than the latest ranking

| Company | 2023 residual after listed uses | 2024 residual after listed uses | 2025 residual after listed uses | What the path says |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| McKesson | $3.734B | $3.355B | $5.202B | The current screen is strong, but the increase must be tested against the $3.7B payable contribution, specialty working capital, settlements, and portfolio changes |
| Cencora | $1.300B | $2.898B | $(1.085)B | Cash conversion looked positive before the large RCA and related acquisition program; the specialty thesis now requires cohort-level cash returns |
| Cardinal | $2.363B | $3.251B | $(3.400)B | The 2025 acquisition program overwhelmed current-year post-capex cash; future service-platform cash must replace the outlay and contract-transition effects |

The path prevents a common analytical mistake: calling McKesson the “cleanest
business” solely because its latest residual is positive, or calling Cencora
and Cardinal weak solely because they invested heavily in one year. The correct
judgment is conditional on reinvestment cadence. If acquisitions are a normal
part of preserving specialty access, then acquisition cash belongs in the
through-cycle owner-cash denominator. If the companies can reduce acquisition
intensity after building the platform, the negative year may be a temporary
growth investment. The next filings must show gross profit, cash, and returns
from the acquired cohorts rather than only revenue or adjusted EPS.

### Per-share quality and capital allocation

The parent-level scorecard should track more than OCF and EPS:

| Test | McKesson | Cencora | Cardinal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main presentation metric to challenge | Adjusted EPS and OCF after payable timing | Adjusted EPS versus GAAP, LIFO credits, and acquisition add-backs | Non-GAAP operating earnings, contract-transition normalization, and acquisition adjustments |
| Capital return that needs funding proof | Buybacks and portfolio actions after settlements and specialty investment | Buybacks and dividends after RCA/OneOncology, litigation, and debt | Buybacks and dividends while acquisitions and debt funding remain large |
| Dilution / claim to track | SBC, debt, litigation, NCI, and software investment | SBC, debt, litigation, NCI, and acquisition consideration | $244M SBC, debt, goodwill, litigation, and acquired-platform claims |
| Falsifier | OCF remains payable-supported while gross profit and normalized cash lag | Acquired gross profit fails to cover integration, working capital, legal, and financing cost | Service acquisitions need impairment or margin support while post-growth cash remains negative |

This is not an allegation of accounting misconduct. It is an incentive test:
revenue, adjusted EPS, and acquisition growth can all look favorable while the
capital needed to support the distribution relationship and the acquired
service layer rises faster than common-owner cash. A fair comparison must charge
SBC, debt, legal cash, working-capital funding, and acquisition consideration
before interpreting buybacks as evidence of undervaluation.

| Company | Cash supplied by payables | Cash used by receivables | Cash used by inventory | What must be normalized |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| McKesson | +3.7 | (3.935) | (2.270) | Vendor-payment timing, rebate/chargeback flows, specialty growth inventory, customer credit, and settlement timing |
| Cencora | +3.7 | (1.9) | (1.3) | Higher inventory and scheduled supplier payments, quarter-end terms, DSO/DIO/DPO, and credit losses |
| Cardinal | +2.732 | (0.833) | (1.816) | OptumRx contract transition, new-customer onboarding, vendor terms, inventory funding, and receivables quality |

The payable contribution is not inherently improper. A growing distributor may
legitimately receive supplier credit while inventory expands. The forensic
question is persistence and symmetry: when growth slows or terms normalize,
does operating cash remain proportional to gross profit, or does the prior cash
benefit reverse?

## Earnings-quality and financial-engineering tests

### McKesson: positive residual, but flow economics can flatter cash

McKesson's FY2025 OCF increased to $6.085B, but the filing bridge includes a
$3.7B accounts-payable contribution while receivables and inventory used cash.
The correct question is not whether the cash is real—it is whether it is
repeatable after supplier terms, inventory funding, customer credit, litigation,
and specialty investment normalize. Its portfolio pruning and Apollo-related
activity also require separation of sale proceeds, stranded costs, and removed
earnings.

**Breaker:** revenue and adjusted EPS rise while gross profit and normalized
cash do not, or future OCF remains increasingly payable-supported.

### Cencora: specialty growth is acquisition-funded and legally encumbered

Cencora's $3.9B Retina Consultants of America acquisition sits inside a
$4.096B acquired-company outlay. After capital expenditures and the broader
acquisition/investment cash, the strict FY2025 residual was approximately
negative $1.085B. FY2025 adjusted diluted EPS of $16.00 versus
GAAP diluted EPS of $7.96 makes classification essential: amortization and
impairments may be non-cash, but serial acquisition and integration are part of
the chosen operating model. The $3.881B accrued litigation liability and $404M
litigation-liability cash decrease are capital claims against the platform.

**Breaker:** RCA or later cohorts fail to produce incremental gross profit and
cash after integration, physician retention, working capital, legal cash, and
debt cost; or specialty/GLP-1 growth expands volume while gross-profit dollars
and normalized cash stagnate.

### Cardinal: transition and service adjacencies need to replace lost flow

Cardinal's OptumRx contract expiration affected both revenue comparability and
negative working capital unwind. The company then spent $5.250B on acquisitions
while reporting $2.397B of OCF. FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS of $6.45
versus non-GAAP EPS of $8.24 and the later $184M Navista/ION goodwill
impairment make the acquired service-platform return test unusually important.

**Breaker:** replacement growth does not offset contract loss on gross profit
and cash, or acquired platforms require repeated impairment, integration cost,
margin support, or debt while post-growth cash remains negative.

## Alden and Damodaran synthesis

### Alden: essential demand does not eliminate liquidity risk

All three distribute medically necessary products, so demand durability is
stronger than in a discretionary channel. But they are also large balance-sheet
flow machines with thin spreads. A liquidity shock can come from customer
defaults, inventory funding, supplier terms, rates, reimbursement, shortages,
tariffs, or legal settlements even if patient demand remains intact.

The more robust operator is the one that can preserve fill rate, compliance,
credit discipline, and investment capacity when working capital expands and
financing becomes less forgiving.

### Damodaran: value the operating layers separately

Each peer should be decomposed into:

`low-margin distribution volume -> specialty/oncology gross profit -> provider and technology services -> working-capital requirement -> acquisition return -> legal/debt claim -> owner cash`

McKesson currently offers the strongest reported residual, but the market value
also requires durable specialty/service economics. Cencora's and Cardinal's
negative post-growth residuals mean the market is underwriting future acquired
cash, not merely capitalizing current distribution cash. The valuation cases
should vary gross-profit spread, DSO/DIO/DPO, acquisition cadence, cohort
returns, legal cash, debt cost, dilution, and the normalized maintenance
technology requirement.

## Research conclusion

The peer set supports a differentiated conclusion:

1. **McKesson is the cleanest current cash conversion screen**, not because
   distribution is intrinsically high-return, but because 2025 listed growth
   spending was low relative to OCF. The unresolved issue is whether its cash
   remains strong after working-capital and legal normalization.
2. **Cencora is the clearest specialty-acquisition test.** It may control a
   more valuable physician and manufacturer relationship, but the current
   residual is negative and the adjusted/GAAP gap is large.
3. **Cardinal is the strongest contradiction case.** Its broader service
   platform may become more valuable, but it must replace a contract-related
   flow disruption and earn back a large acquisition program while carrying
   working-capital, litigation, debt, and impairment risk.

The durable economic-control thesis is therefore conditional: these companies
control an essential channel, but channel control becomes attractive only when
gross-profit spread, counterparty credit, normalized working capital, legal
cash, acquisition returns, and financing capacity all survive together.
