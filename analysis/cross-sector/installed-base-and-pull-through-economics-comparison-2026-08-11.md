# Installed-Base And Pull-Through Economics Comparison

Date: 2026-08-11
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Plain Claim

Some businesses look like product sellers at first glance.

But the real economics start after the first placement, onboarding, or referral win.

Once the system is in place, the company can keep earning through:

- consumables
- accessories
- service
- resupply
- repeat testing
- recurring monitoring

The first sale opens the door.

The pull-through is the business.

## What The Comparison Clarifies

### 1. The initial sale often understates the real business.

Intuitive can look like a capital-equipment company until procedure growth and accessories are put next to installed-base scale.

Thermo Fisher can look like an instrument seller until recurring consumables and workflow services are put next to the portfolio.

DexCom can look like a device company until repeated sensor usage becomes the core lens.

AdaptHealth can look like equipment distribution until the resupply loop becomes visible.

The better question is not only what gets sold first.

It is what has to keep flowing after adoption.

### 2. Pull-through economics are strongest when the system becomes part of routine behavior.

Hospitals repeat procedures.

Labs repeat tests.

Patients repeat monitoring.

Home-care patients repeat resupply.

Researchers and production sites repeat consumables usage.

That is why recurring demand often sits behind a workflow rather than inside a simple subscription contract.

### 3. Installed base matters most when usage intensity matters too.

An idle installed base is weaker than a heavily used one.

That is why Intuitive's procedure growth matters more than placements alone.

It is why Abbott's diagnostics and chronic-care growth matter more than category labels alone.

It is why DexCom's recurring sensor use matters more than one-time onboarding.

The real signal is not only whether the customer entered the system.

It is whether the customer keeps using it enough to generate pull-through.

### 4. This model appears across very different operating settings.

It appears in:

- procedure rooms
- hospitals
- laboratories
- research sites
- patient homes
- app-linked chronic-care routines

That is useful because it shows the model is not tied to one taxonomy bucket.

It is an economic shape:

place the system once, then keep earning as the workflow repeats.

### 5. Pull-through can be cleaner than the initial hardware or equipment story.

The first sale may require heavier selling, onboarding, capital budgeting, or referral work.

But the ongoing revenue can become:

- more recurring
- more predictable
- more embedded
- more scalable

This is why installed-base businesses often deserve to be interpreted through follow-on usage rather than first-sale optics.

## Stronger Conclusion

The stronger conclusion is that some of the archive's best businesses are not one-time product businesses at all.

They are recurring pull-through systems disguised as product companies.

What makes them strong is that the original placement creates permission for:

- repeated use
- repeated procurement
- repeated monitoring
- repeated service
- repeated resupply

That pattern deserves to be treated as a distinct economic model in the archive.

## Aha Moments

### 1. Intuitive and AdaptHealth are more alike than they first appear.

One lives in the procedure room.

The other lives in the patient home.

Both rely on the idea that the first placement creates a stream of follow-on usage and supply demand.

### 2. DexCom sits between a device company and a consumables company.

The real economics look more recurring than the product label suggests because the sensor keeps getting used and replaced inside the care routine.

### 3. Thermo Fisher and BD show that healthcare workflow can monetize through many small repeated actions rather than one dramatic purchase.

The system deepens through consumables, testing, medication systems, and ongoing procurement.

### 4. Abbott is easiest to understand when breadth is treated as multiple pull-through loops running at once.

Diagnostics, devices, nutrition, and chronic-care categories all create their own recurring use patterns.

## Thesis Breakers

This installed-base-and-pull-through thesis weakens if:

- usage intensity stops following placements or onboarding
- recurring attachment rates compress materially
- reimbursement or budget pressure limits follow-on usage
- service and consumable layers become easier to substitute
- the initial placement becomes too expensive relative to the recurring economics it generates
