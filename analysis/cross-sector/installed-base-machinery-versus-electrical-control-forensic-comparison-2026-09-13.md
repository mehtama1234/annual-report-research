# Installed-base machinery versus electrical control: who owns the physical AI bottleneck?

Caterpillar, Cummins, and Eaton all benefit when digital infrastructure creates more physical demand, but they monetize different parts of the system.

| Company | Economic control point | FY2025 mechanical screen | Principal burden |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Eaton | Electrical distribution, protection, controls, and power quality | **$2.063B** after PP&E and acquisitions | Acquisition goodwill, unbilled receivables, capacity capex, debt, separation costs |
| Cummins | Engines, generator platforms, distribution, and service | **$2.374B** after PP&E and acquisitions | Warranty, emissions, truck cycle, inventory, Accelera cleanup |
| Caterpillar | Equipment ecosystem, dealers, finance, parts, and services | **$8.871B** after PP&E and acquisitions | Dealer inventory, Cat Financial credit, machinery inventory, tariffs, high capital returns |

## Business economics: first-principles distinction

Eaton sells the electrical architecture that must be present when a load connects to the grid. Cummins sells generation and powertrain capacity that protects or supplies the load. Caterpillar sells the machines and financing network that build, power, mine, and maintain the broader physical economy. Eaton has the most direct electrical-control-point claim; Caterpillar has the broadest installed-base network; Cummins bridges engines, distributed power, and service.

## Why Caterpillar’s larger cash number is not automatically safer

Caterpillar’s $8.871B residual is much larger in absolute terms, but its revenue base and capital system are also much larger. The company spent approximately $7.978B on dividends and repurchases, while Cat Financial held about $25.2B of net finance receivables and inventory was $18.1B. The number is not comparable to Eaton’s or Cummins’ residual without adjusting for credit, dealer, and working-capital burdens.

## Who controls the valuable relationship?

- Eaton controls specification and electrical qualification at a project’s power-distribution interface.
- Cummins controls uptime through equipment, service, and a global installed base, but large buyers can negotiate generator pricing.
- Caterpillar controls customer productivity through dealer density, proprietary parts, financing, and fleet standardization; the relationship is deeper but more capital-intensive.

## Risks and accounting tests: shared falsification path

For all three, test:

`order/backlog -> shipment -> dealer/customer utilization -> billing -> cash -> required reinvestment -> capital returns`

Eaton currently shows unbilled-receivable pressure; Cummins shows warranty/emissions and inventory burden; Caterpillar shows dealer inventory, finance receivable, and tariff timing. The next filing that most improves confidence is the one that shows cash conversion while these burdens remain stable—not merely another record order number.

## Valuation conclusion

The current register places Eaton at approximately 80x its FY2025 screen, Caterpillar at approximately 42.7x, and Cummins at approximately 32.5x. Eaton may have the cleanest bottleneck; Caterpillar has the broadest network; Cummins has the less demanding relative multiple. The market is charging for quality in all three, but it is charging Eaton for a much more aggressive growth and reinvestment outcome.

## 2026 operating checkpoint

The latest quarter strengthens all three demand narratives, but it also makes
the conversion tests more important:

| Company | Latest evidence | What remains unproven |
|---|---|---|
| Eaton | Q2 2026 sales `$8.531B`, organic growth `14%`, Electrical Americas rolling orders up `41%`, and backlog about `$24.1B` | Whether backlog becomes collected cash after unbilled receivables, inventory, acquisitions, capacity spending, and Mobility separation costs |
| Cummins | Q2 2026 revenue `$9.5B`, Power Systems sales up `19%`, first-half operating cash flow about `$1.8B`, and inventory near `$6.4B` | Whether data-center power growth remains profitable after warranty, service, emissions, inventory, and transition costs |
| Caterpillar | Q2 2026 sales `$20.5B`, operating margin `20.9%`, Power & Energy sales `$8.238B`, and Q1 backlog near `$63B` | Whether dealer inventory, Cat Financial credit, tariff recoveries, capex, and capital returns remain covered through a slower cycle |

Vertiv provides the closest direct comparison. Q2 2026 sales were `$3.274B`,
adjusted operating margin was `22.6%`, and adjusted free cash flow was `$925M`.
Vertiv therefore shows the strongest recent data-center-specific cash
conversion in the cohort, but it also carries a sharp backlog, acquisition,
capacity, service-labor, and customer-concentration test. The difference is
not that Vertiv is “asset-light” while the others are physical businesses.
The difference is where each company must fund the next unit of growth.

The common 2026 test is:

`orders or backlog -> production and acceptance -> billed revenue -> collection -> warranty/service/credit burden -> cash after reinvestment`

Eaton's main warning is unbilled receivables and acquisition-funded growth.
Cummins' is inventory and warranty/emissions liability. Caterpillar's is the
dealer and financing balance sheet. Vertiv's is the reversal risk in working
capital and the return on the PurgeRite and capacity investments. A record
quarter at any one company does not settle the comparison; the cleaner
economics belong to the company that converts demand into cash while its
company-specific burden remains stable.
