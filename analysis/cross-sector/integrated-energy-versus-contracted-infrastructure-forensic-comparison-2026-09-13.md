# Integrated energy versus contracted infrastructure: forensic comparison

Date baseline: 2026-09-13. This comparison is a research artifact, not an
investment recommendation.

## Business economics: the lane question

Does integrated upstream/downstream scale produce cleaner owner cash than
contracted infrastructure, or does it simply move commodity, refining, capex,
and transition risk inside a larger system?

| Company | Control point | FY2025 visible cash bridge | Main burden |
|---|---|---:|---|
| ExxonMobil | Integrated reserves, production, refining, chemicals, specialty products, and logistics | $51.970B OCF less $28.358B PP&E = $23.612B | Commodity prices, depletion, reserve replacement, refining cycle, transition, and $37.5B common returns |
| ConocoPhillips | Low-cost upstream reserves and production with LNG optionality | $19.796B OCF less $12.553B capital program = $7.243B | Direct commodity and depletion exposure, reserve replacement, asset sales, project execution, and ~$9.0B common returns |
| EOG Resources | U.S.-weighted drilling inventory, reserve depth, field execution, and gathering access | $10.040B OCF less $6.294B total capital expenditure = $3.746B screen; $4.663B management FCF | Direct oil/NGL/gas price exposure, basin decline, reserve replacement, impairments, infrastructure access, and $4.7B shareholder returns |
| Cheniere | LNG liquefaction trains, export docks, contracts, and know-how | $5.539B less $3.078B = $2.461B | Derivative marks, train construction, contracts, $22.5B debt, and $3.175B buybacks/dividends |
| Energy Transfer | Gathering, transport, storage, fractionation, and export corridors | $10.149B less $6.303B = $3.846B | $68.3B debt, NCI waterfall, growth capex, and utilization/contract risk |
| NextEra | FPL rate base plus NEER generation/development platform | Existing NextEra memo and source ledger | Large capex, tax-credit/hedge dependence, NCI, regulation, and funding |

## What changes when the system is integrated?

Exxon owns more of the chain and can redirect capital toward advantaged
reservoirs, products, or geographies. That can reduce dependence on any one
counterparty or bottleneck. But it also makes the owner responsible for every
capital cycle: drilling, processing, refinery maintenance, chemicals, product
inventory, environmental obligations, and transition investment. Cheniere and
Energy Transfer have narrower control points, but their contracted or network
economics must still be tested against expansion capital, debt, and ownership
claims.

ConocoPhillips isolates the upstream question. It carries less downstream
capital and can look cleaner when commodity prices and production execute, but
the 2025 capital program left a $7.243B screening residual against roughly
$9.0B of common returns. The upstream model therefore does not eliminate the
capital-allocation contradiction; it makes reserve replacement and asset-sale
dependence more visible.

EOG adds a more U.S.-weighted independent-producer control. Its 2025 free cash
flow was $4.663B, close to the 2023 and 2024 levels of $5.108B and $5.367B, but
its earnings can move sharply with realized oil prices: Q4 2025 free cash flow
was $978M, Q1 2026 was $1.493B, and Q2 2026 was $2.799B. The main question is
whether the 70% return policy is funded after reserve replacement, not whether
the company can distribute cash in a strong price quarter.

### Q2 2026 Exxon checkpoint

Exxon’s Q2 result sharpens the comparison rather than closing it. GAAP earnings
were `$14.5B`, operating cash flow was `$23.6B`, cash capital spending was
`$6.8B`, and reported free cash flow was `$17.2B`. The company returned `$9.4B`
to shareholders in the quarter. Upstream produced `$7.927B` of earnings and
Energy Products produced `$5.465B`, but the upstream earnings bridge attributed
about `$4.650B` to price and `$1.140B` to advantaged volume growth. Production
of `4.514M` oil-equivalent barrels per day was below the prior-year quarter’s
`4.630M`, mainly because Middle East disruption more than offset Permian and
Guyana growth.

This creates a precise cross-sector test. Exxon generated much more current
cash than the contracted infrastructure names, but the cash is exposed to
commodity price and margin conditions and must fund a larger physical asset
base. The next comparison should therefore use mid-cycle price assumptions,
maintenance and reserve-replacement capital, and the actual cash cost of new
production. A quarter with high prices and lower production can be excellent
for cash while still leaving the long-term replacement question unresolved.

## Alden and Damodaran lenses

The Alden lens asks which system remains durable through inflation, rates,
geopolitical stress, and energy scarcity. Exxon has resource and integration
optionality; Cheniere has export scarcity; Energy Transfer has connected
network density; NextEra has regulated rate-base exposure plus development
optionality. None is immune to liquidity conditions or capital intensity.

The Damodaran lens asks whether the narrative becomes a sustainable owner-cash
stream. Exxon requires normalized commodity, refining, and chemical margins;
Cheniere requires train-level physical cash after maintenance capex and hedge
settlements; Energy Transfer requires a common-unit waterfall after debt,
minority claims, and project capital; NextEra requires returns on new rate-base
and competitive generation investment after financing and tax-credit effects.

## Forensic denominator questions

These are presentation and denominator tests, not accusations of misconduct:

- Does OCF benefit from inventory, receivable, payable, contract-advance, or
  derivative timing that reverses later?
- Is capex genuinely growth capital, or is recurring maintenance being labeled
  discretionary so distributions look better?
- Does consolidated cash belong to common owners after NCI, affiliates,
  preferred claims, insurance capital, and project-level debt?
- Are buybacks funded from normalized cash, asset sales, or balance-sheet
  flexibility that should instead protect the physical system?
- Does backlog, contracted volume, or rate base earn an adequate return after
  construction, financing, utilization, and operating risk?

## Current conclusion and falsifiers

Exxon is the useful contradiction case: its scale and integration are more
diversified than a single LNG or pipeline asset, yet the FY2025 residual
declined and common returns exceeded the simple residual. The cleanest owner
cash will belong to the system that demonstrates stable post-maintenance cash,
declining incremental capital intensity, and leverage discipline—not the one
with the largest production, capacity, or adjusted EBITDA headline.

ConocoPhillips is the direct upstream control: if it produces cleaner cash
after reserve replacement than Exxon after all-in integration capital, the
upstream specialization earns its place; if not, the apparent simplicity is
only narrower disclosure of the same physical-economy burden.

The lane thesis weakens if Exxon production growth requires disproportionate
capital, Cheniere's train returns depend on recurring exclusions, Energy
Transfer's coverage depends on consolidated cash unavailable to common units,
or NextEra's growth requires persistent external funding without adequate
project returns. EOG's thesis weakens if production growth is purchased by
under-replacing reserves or if the 70% return policy forces debt, asset sales,
or weaker future drilling quality.

## Valuation: commodity, contract, and rate-base cash are different claims

Value Exxon, ConocoPhillips, and EOG from mid-cycle commodity cash after
reserve replacement and sustaining capital. Value Cheniere and Energy Transfer
from contracted or corridor cash after train, pipeline, maintenance, debt,
minority, and project funding requirements. Value NextEra from regulated and
competitive project returns after financing, tax-credit, construction, and
regulatory-lag assumptions.

Use normal commodity, lower-utilization, delayed-project, and higher-rate cases.
The price is fragile if it requires both high commodity prices and rapid growth
capital, or if it treats adjusted distributable cash as unrestricted common
cash before debt, NCI, and maintenance needs are paid.
