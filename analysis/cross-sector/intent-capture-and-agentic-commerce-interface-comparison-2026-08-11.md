# Intent-Capture And Agentic Commerce Interface Comparison

Date: 2026-08-11
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Plain-English Claim

One of the archive's newer but already concrete patterns is that commerce discovery is moving closer to the moment of intent and farther away from the old model of "go to one site, browse manually, then check out."

The emerging fight is over who captures intent when discovery, recommendation, and even checkout begin to happen inside AI assistants, conversational interfaces, and machine-mediated commerce flows.

## Core Question

Which companies are preparing for a world where product discovery and purchase happen through AI-mediated surfaces rather than only through traditional websites or apps?

## Why These Four Belong Together

These companies play different roles in the same shift:

- Criteo is trying to monetize intent when product discovery happens inside commerce media and AI interfaces.
- Shopify is building the catalog, merchant, payment, and protocol layer that lets AI-mediated commerce actually execute.
- Etsy is adapting a discovery-heavy marketplace so its supply can be surfaced through AI shopping and agentic checkout flows.
- Airbnb shows the same shift in travel, where app behavior, flexible payments, and AI-assisted support widen the interface beyond a normal destination site.

The stronger conclusion is that discovery, recommendation, and transaction are moving closer together.

## Exact Mechanism

### 1. Criteo shows that AI interfaces are already entering the commerce-ad stack

Criteo is the strongest direct proof in the repo that AI-mediated shopping is already being commercialized.

In Q2 `2026`, management said the company had become OpenAI's first advertising-technology partner, with more than `2,000` brands advertising on ChatGPT across seven countries. The packet also says Criteo positions itself around the path from product discovery to purchase rather than around generic audience reach.

That means conversational interfaces are no longer just traffic risk.

They are becoming commerce inventory.

### 2. Shopify shows that agentic commerce needs an execution layer, not just discovery

Shopify's packet makes clear that the company is preparing for AI-mediated buying rather than only website commerce.

Fiscal `2025` GMV was `$378.4 billion`, and Shopify Payments penetration was `65.6%`. The company explicitly highlighted Catalog, Sidekick, and Universal Commerce Protocol, while management described `2025` as laying the rails for a new era of AI commerce.

The important point is that AI discovery alone does not complete a purchase.

Someone still needs to provide:

- structured product data
- inventory and merchant logic
- checkout
- payments
- post-purchase workflow

That is why the execution layer may gain value as discovery fragments.

### 3. Etsy shows how marketplaces are adapting when intent starts elsewhere

Etsy's packet is useful because it sits much closer to differentiated consumer demand than Criteo or Shopify do.

In `Q4 2025`, Etsy reported `$3.59 billion` of consolidated GMS and `$881.6 million` of consolidated revenue. More importantly for this theme, management explicitly described AI-agent commerce with Google, Microsoft, Stripe, and ChatGPT-linked checkout experiences.

That is strong evidence that the marketplace does not assume discovery will remain confined to Etsy.com.

It is adapting so unique supply can still be found and purchased when the first consumer interaction begins inside an assistant or search layer.

### 4. Airbnb shows the same shift outside retail goods

Airbnb matters because the same pattern appears in travel.

In `Q1 2026`, app-booked nights grew `22%` and represented `63%` of total nights booked. About `20%` of global gross booking value came from `Reserve Now, Pay Later` bookings. In `Q2 2026`, nearly `45%` of issues beginning with the AI assistant were resolved without a human agent, while support cost per booking declined about `16%`.

This matters because travel discovery, trust, checkout, and service are also compressing into one smarter interface.

The category is different, but the operating direction is similar: less static browsing, more managed intent flow.

## The Stronger Conclusion

The stronger conclusion is that one of the next important economic layers in commerce is not just who owns the destination site.

It is who remains useful when intent, recommendation, and purchase begin to happen inside machine-mediated interfaces.

In practice, the winning stack may split into different roles:

1. the company that captures intent
2. the company that translates intent into a purchasable object
3. the company that executes checkout and fulfillment
4. the company that preserves trust when discovery is no longer direct

That is a more useful way to read the shift than saying only that "AI changes search."

The common pattern is that the interface gets smarter, but the burden of making that intelligence transactable still sits with the platform and merchant infrastructure layer.

## Why This Pattern Matters

This pattern matters because it changes the meaning of customer ownership.

The old model assumed that the company with the website, app, or marketplace homepage controlled discovery.

The new model is messier:

- intent may begin elsewhere
- discovery may be mediated by AI
- checkout may be embedded
- recommendation may happen before the customer ever sees the merchant's own surface

That means value may move toward companies that make supply legible, trusted, and executable in many environments rather than only in one owned destination.

## Consumer And Cultural Meaning

The consumer is becoming more willing to let software narrow the choice set.

That can look like:

- asking an assistant for a product
- clicking an AI-linked recommendation
- accepting a suggested booking flow
- using a platform that already knows saved preferences, payment choices, or trust history

This makes the interface more convenient, but it also means more buying behavior may be shaped before the customer reaches a merchant's own homepage.

## Industrial And Operating Pressures

The opportunity is large, but the pressures are real.

- Criteo faces retailer concentration and dependence on third-party interface shifts.
- Shopify has to keep merchant execution useful even if discovery control keeps moving outward.
- Etsy has to preserve differentiated supply, trust, and relevance while ceding some first-contact discovery to outside layers.
- Airbnb has to widen the trip interface without letting trust, support, and supply quality erode.

The broader lesson is that AI-mediated commerce does not remove infrastructure needs.

It increases them.

## Disconfirming Evidence And Limits

This pattern would weaken if:

- consumers continue to prefer direct destination-site browsing for high-intent categories
- AI surfaces fail to generate reliable conversion economics
- merchants resist allowing third-party agents or assistants to intermediate checkout
- platform trust, fraud, or fulfillment problems rise faster than the convenience benefit

There is also a boundary condition:

not every category will migrate at the same speed.

Unique goods, travel, high-trust purchases, and merchant-controlled brands may still keep more value in direct surfaces than commodity search-like categories do.
