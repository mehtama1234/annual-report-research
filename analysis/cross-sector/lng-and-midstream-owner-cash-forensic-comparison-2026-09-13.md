# LNG and midstream infrastructure: owner-cash forensic comparison

Date baseline: 2026-09-13. This comparison extends the power and energy lane
with Cheniere and Energy Transfer. It is a research artifact, not an
investment recommendation.

## Business economics: the lane question

Which energy intermediary captures the cleaner economics: the LNG export
conversion chokepoint, the diversified midstream corridor, or the regulated and
competitive asset owner? The answer depends less on whether demand is durable
than on how much capital, leverage, commodity exposure, and ownership leakage
are required to convert that demand into owner cash.

| Company | Control point | FY2025 visible cash bridge | Main burden |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Cheniere | Liquefaction trains, export docks, contracts, and operating know-how | $5.539B OCF less $3.078B PP&E = $2.461B before returns | Derivative marks, LNG/contract risk, train construction, $22.5B debt, $3.175B buybacks/dividends |
| Energy Transfer | Connected gathering, transport, storage, fractionation, and export corridors | $10.149B OCF less $6.303B productive assets = $3.846B before equity claims | $68.3B debt, affiliate/NCI waterfall, growth capex, contract/utilization and commodity-flow risk |
| ONEOK | Integrated NGL, gas, storage, and logistics network | Existing ONEOK memo and source ledger | Acquisition integration, commodity optimization, capex, and leverage |
| NextEra | FPL rate base plus NEER generation/development platform | Existing NextEra memo and source ledger | Very large capex, tax-credit/hedge dependence, NCI, regulatory and funding burden |

The key result is that Cheniere and Energy Transfer both have real physical
control points, but neither is a frictionless toll road. Cheniere's scarcity is
more concentrated and its reported earnings more exposed to derivative marks;
Energy Transfer has more network diversification but a larger consolidated
debt and ownership waterfall.

This is not a free-cash-flow ranking. Cheniere's PP&E includes growth and
maintenance; Energy Transfer's productive-asset denominator includes a very
large expansion program. The table is a pressure test against narratives that
use adjusted EBITDA, DCF, or contracted capacity without showing the capital
needed to keep and expand the system.

## What the Alden lens adds

Both companies benefit from a durable energy-security and infrastructure thesis:
U.S. gas and NGL production needs connected takeaway, global customers need
reliable energy, and export/fractionation/storage bottlenecks can earn scarcity
returns. Liquidity conditions still matter. High rates raise the hurdle for
new trains, terminals, pipelines, and storage; weaker counterparties can turn
contracted volume into collection risk; and leverage makes a temporary volume
shock more consequential to equity holders.

Cheniere is the more concentrated scarcity asset. Its durability depends on
export capacity, train availability, contract quality, and the continued gap
between global demand and competing liquefaction supply. Energy Transfer is the
more diversified corridor. Its durability depends on network utilization,
counterparty credit, tariff and contract durability, and whether its project
pipeline earns more than its debt and equity capital.

## What the Damodaran lens adds

The narrative-to-numbers translation differs:

- Cheniere's narrative requires train-level returns, normalized physical margin,
  maintenance-versus-growth capex, and a reconciliation of DCF to owner cash.
- Energy Transfer's narrative requires segment-level return on incremental
  capital, contract and volume durability, leverage stabilization, and a full
  common-unit waterfall after minority and affiliate claims.

In both cases, the lifecycle question is decisive. A commissioned asset can
look like a mature cash compounder while the company is still in a heavy
expansion phase. A project that is “contracted” is not automatically valuable
if the contract covers operating cost but not the return on construction
capital.

## Forensic questions and possible shenanigans

Neither dossier establishes fraud or misconduct. The relevant risks are
presentation and denominator risks:

- adjusted EBITDA or DCF may exclude recurring maintenance, implementation,
  amortization, or project costs;
- derivative marks may make GAAP earnings diverge sharply from physical cash;
- fee-based or take-or-pay language may obscure counterparty, volume, renewal,
  or tariff risk;
- consolidated OCF may overstate cash attributable to common equity when
  minority interests, preferred claims, affiliates, and joint ventures are
  material;
- buybacks or distributions may continue while debt and growth capex rise; and
- backlog and contracted capacity may be presented as economic profit before
  construction, financing, and utilization are proven.

These are falsifiable through subsequent filings. The next comparable dataset
should capture realized physical margin, contract coverage, train/pipeline
availability, maintenance and growth capex, debt maturities, interest paid,
counterparty concentration, distributions, share/unit count, and post-project
cash returns.

## Control point versus burden carrier

The relationship map is asymmetric:

`global energy demand -> producer / customer commitment -> export or corridor access -> conversion / transport -> cash owner`

Cheniere owns the conversion bottleneck and carries train, construction, and
derivative burdens. Energy Transfer owns the connected routing layer and carries
network expansion, leverage, affiliate, and equity-waterfall burdens. The
customer receives reliability and optionality; the infrastructure owner must
finance the physical system before the demand is fully monetized.

The strongest current conclusion is therefore conditional: energy-infrastructure
control points can be durable, but the cleaner economics belong to the company
that demonstrates declining incremental capital intensity, stable cash after
maintenance, and leverage discipline—not necessarily the company with the
largest capacity, backlog, or adjusted EBITDA.

## Valuation: contracted cash still needs replacement and leverage discipline

Value LNG and midstream businesses from cash after maintenance capital, debt
service, contract obligations, throughput or utilization, and the growth
capital required to preserve the system. Cheniere's contracted liquefaction,
Energy Transfer's corridor and processing assets, and an integrated producer's
commodity exposure should not share one multiple or one cash denominator.

Use normal commodity, lower-utilization, delayed-project, and higher-financing-
cost cases. A contracted project deserves a premium only when the counterparty,
contract terms, construction cost, and collection path support the return. The
market is over-crediting visibility when it capitalizes adjusted distributable
cash while excluding sustaining capital, leverage, NCI claims, derivative
settlement, or the next expansion's funding requirement.

## Filing-based thesis breakers

The lane thesis weakens if:

- new capacity grows faster than cash after maintenance capex;
- leverage rises while distributions are defended as “covered” by adjusted DCF;
- realized margin depends on favorable derivative marks, commodity spreads, or
  temporary contract terms;
- counterparty collections, utilization, or contract renewals deteriorate;
- project costs or schedules slip without a corresponding return reset; or
- capital allocation favors buybacks, distributions, or headline expansions
  over balance-sheet resilience.
