# Mission-critical workflow versus passive connectivity

Date baseline: 2026-09-13. This comparison is a research artifact, not an
investment recommendation.

## Business economics: the lane question

Where does connectivity become the valuable relationship: passive site and
capacity ownership, active interconnection, or the institutional workflow that
must operate under stress?

| Company | Control point | FY2025 cash screen | Main burden |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Motorola Solutions | Public-safety networks, devices, video, dispatch, evidence, and managed workflows | $2.837B OCF less $265M PP&E = $2.572B before major acquisitions | Government procurement, integration, cybersecurity, acquisition pace, support labor, and public-sector budgets |
| Equinix | Vendor-neutral interconnection density and powered data-center capacity | $3.911B OCF less $4.311B total property capex less $994M real-estate acquisitions less $251M business acquisitions = $(1.645)B growth bridge | Power, construction, leases, debt, JV/VIE claims, customer lease-up, and dilution |
| F5 | Enterprise application delivery, security, and support workflow | Existing F5 dossier and source ledger | Subscription transition, support labor, product R&D, SBC, cyber retention, and integration |

## What the comparison shows

Equinix controls a dense physical network but carries the largest capital burden.
Motorola controls the active institutional workflow and converts a much larger
portion of OCF after PP&E, but its acquisition program means the mature cash
screen is not the same as platform-expansion owner cash. F5 sits between the
two: less physical capex, but product, support, cybersecurity, and customer-
retention obligations remain real.

The relationship becomes more valuable as the cost of failure rises. A data
center needs reliable interconnection; a public-safety agency needs a system
that responders can use during an emergency; an enterprise needs traffic and
security tools to remain available. The owner captures cleaner economics only
if the recurring workflow persists after required reinvestment and acquisition
spend.

## Alden and Damodaran lenses

The Alden lens favors all three when digital and institutional resilience are
structural rather than discretionary. It also exposes different liquidity
risks: Equinix is rate-, power-, and construction-sensitive; Motorola is
procurement-, acquisition-, and public-budget-sensitive; F5 is software-cycle
and customer-retention-sensitive.

The Damodaran lens requires separating:

- installed-base recurring service from hardware refresh and project revenue;
- organic workflow expansion from acquired revenue;
- recurring capex from growth capex and acquisitions; and
- consolidated cash from debt, lease, JV, SBC, and common-owner claims.

## Forensic questions

These are denominator and incentive tests, not misconduct allegations:

- Does Motorola’s backlog and software/services growth convert to cash after
  implementation, support, cybersecurity, earnouts, and acquired-company costs?
- Does Equinix’s AFFO/recurring-capex framing understate the investment required
  to preserve network density and deliver new powered capacity?
- Does F5’s subscription transition improve cash after support labor and SBC, or
  simply move revenue recognition and deferred revenue across periods?
- Are buybacks made after the company proves incremental returns, or before the
  next capital cycle is visible?

## Current conclusion and falsifiers

Motorola currently appears to capture the cleanest mature cash in this lane,
but only if acquisition-adjusted returns remain high and the installed workflow
continues to compound. Equinix owns a stronger physical scarcity asset but must
earn its way through a much heavier growth-capital burden. F5’s position depends
on proving that software transition and security retention create owner cash
without hidden support and dilution costs.

The next filings should compare organic growth, backlog quality, recurring
revenue, customer retention, capex, acquisitions, contract assets, debt, lease
claims, SBC, and cash per installed relationship.

## Valuation: recurring workflow after the infrastructure burden

Value Motorola and F5 from recurring workflow and service cash after support,
security, R&D, acquisitions, SBC, and dilution. Value Equinix from normalized
cash after recurring capital, powered-capacity expansion, leases, debt, joint
venture claims, and lease-up. Their control points may all be durable, but
their required reinvestment and financing are not interchangeable.

Use normal, slower-growth, delayed-capacity, and higher-rate cases. A recurring
revenue multiple is misleading when the company must keep building capacity or
buying capabilities just to preserve the customer relationship. The relevant
denominator is cash per installed relationship after the system remains
available and secure.
