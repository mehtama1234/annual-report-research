# Retail formats: value discovery, attached services, and shareholder cash

Research date: `2026-09-15`

## The comparison question

How do different retail formats turn customer traffic and supplier relationships
into shareholder cash after inventory, labor, stores, fulfillment, leases, and
capital spending?

The question is not whether a low price attracts a customer. It is whether the
company can repeatedly buy goods at a low enough cost, sell them before their
value fades, and fund the stores and distribution network that make the format
work. The comparison also prevents a common error: treating positive sales and
earnings growth as proof that inventory and store returns are healthy.

## What the three companies contribute

TJX is the off-price case in this comparison. Its local source packet reports
FY2026 net sales of `$60.4B`, comparable sales growth of `5%`, diluted EPS of
`$4.87`, and more than `5,200` stores. Q3 FY26, Q4 FY26, and Q1 FY27 each had
positive comparable sales, while Q1 FY27 had `$14.3B` of sales, `6%` comparable
sales growth, and a `12.0%` pretax margin.

Target is the focused mass-retail case. Its FY2025 sales declined `1.7%` to
`$104.8B` and operating income declined `8.1%` to `$5.1B`, while Roundel
advertising, membership, marketplace, and same-day delivery grew. In Q2 2026,
a `$994M` tariff refund added `$1.65` to EPS and 3.7 percentage points to
quarterly gross margin. Target's vendor-income receivable was `$542M` at
January 31, 2026 and was identified as a critical audit matter.

Walmart is the scale mass-retail case. Its Q2 FY2027 revenue was `$187.9B`,
global advertising grew `38%`, global membership fee revenue grew `17%`, and
capital spending rose as the company funded omnichannel growth. For the first
six months, operating cash flow was `$19.710B`, capital spending was `$14.181B`,
and company-defined free cash flow was `$5.529B`.

These facts establish three different operating systems. They do not establish
that one format always earns the best return. The decisive evidence remains
transactions, inventory turns, markdowns, shrink, vendor terms, store and
fulfillment capital, leases, and cash per diluted share.

## How the economics work

| Question | TJX off-price | Target mass retail | Walmart mass retail | Filing test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What creates customer traffic? | Changing branded assortment and a visible discount | Planned assortment, convenience, private brands, and store/digital access | Low prices, broad availability, stores, pickup, and delivery | Transactions, repeat visits, ticket, and traffic |
| What creates gross margin? | Low purchase cost plus fast sell-through | Merchandise margin plus vendor programs and attached services | Retail spread plus advertising, membership, marketplace, and financial services | Purchase cost, vendor income, rebates, markdowns, freight, and shrink |
| Where is the main risk? | Buying too much or losing the value gap | Weak merchandise demand, vendor-income collection, tariffs, and working-capital timing | Low-price investment, fulfillment cost, inventory, and heavy capital spending | Inventory age, turns, margin bridge, and cash timing |
| What must be funded? | Stores, distribution, inventory, systems, leases, and labor | Stores, supply chain, digital fulfillment, inventory, leases, and labor | Stores, automation, delivery, inventory, technology, leases, and labor | Capex, lease obligations, working capital, and payables |
| What is the durable asset? | Buying network, vendor access, store density, and customer habit | Customer relationship, supplier access, stores, data, and attached services | Customer frequency, store coverage, supplier scale, data, and attached services | Returns on incremental capital and customer retention |

The format can look asset-light when viewed only through the income statement.
It is not asset-free. Inventory, stores, distribution centers, leases, labor,
and technology all sit ahead of shareholder cash.

## What must be compared before a conclusion

1. **Traffic quality:** positive comparable sales driven by transactions are
   stronger than growth driven only by price or mix.
2. **Merchandise discipline:** faster turns with stable or improving gross
   margin are stronger than margin expansion accompanied by slower inventory.
3. **Store returns:** mature-store cash and new-store cash should be separated.
   A growing footprint can dilute returns even while total profit rises.
4. **Funding:** operating cash flow should be reconciled for inventory, vendor
   payment timing, leases, taxes, and maintenance capital.
5. **Customer value:** a discount is useful only when the merchandise is good
   enough to bring the customer back. Value perception is an operating variable,
   not a slogan.

6. **Attached revenue:** advertising, membership, marketplace, vendor programs,
   and delivery can improve relationship economics, but their revenue is not
   automatically incremental owner cash. Subtract fulfillment, service,
   discounts, data and technology, seller support, and customer-acquisition cost.

## First-pass conclusion

TJX currently provides evidence of a strong off-price system: positive
comparable sales across the recent quarter chain, large scale, and continued
margin and guidance strength. Target provides a more difficult repair case:
attached services and traffic improved, but vendor-income collection, a large
tariff refund, inventory, payables, and higher capital spending complicate the
normalized margin read. Walmart shows the strongest relationship and scale, but
its store and fulfillment network requires substantial reinvestment before
advertising, membership, and marketplace growth becomes owner cash.

The cleanest conclusion is not that one format is always superior. TJX appears
to control buying flexibility and repeated value-seeking traffic. Target must
prove that its relationship layers repair merchandise economics without relying
on temporary vendor or tariff benefits. Walmart controls the broadest shopping
relationship, but carries the largest physical and capital burden. The next
filings should show whether each system turns that control into cash after
inventory, markdowns, shrink, leases, fulfillment, store capital, and dilution.
