# Platform economics: merchant workflow, marketplace trust, and advertising decisioning

## Business economics: purpose and control point

Shopify, eBay, The Trade Desk, and Amazon are all described as technology
platforms, but they control different relationships and carry different costs.
Shopify
helps merchants create and operate a commerce business. eBay coordinates
buyers and sellers around differentiated inventory. The Trade Desk helps
advertisers decide where to place open-internet advertising.

The useful comparison is not which company has the largest activity metric. It
is which control point creates retained economics after the costs needed to
keep the relationship functioning.

The common chain is:

`participant need -> platform workflow -> activity or transaction -> retained revenue -> operating cost -> reinvestment -> common owner cash`

Amazon adds the broadest and most capital-intensive case. It connects consumer
demand, seller access, advertising, fulfillment, subscriptions, and cloud
computing, but it also owns or leases much more of the physical and computing
capacity required to make those relationships work.

## Control-point comparison

| Company | Relationship controlled | Activity denominator | What the company retains | Main burden |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shopify | Merchant operating workflow | GMV and merchant count | Subscription, payments, merchant services, and attached financial products | Payment processing, merchant credit, chargebacks, support, AI, SBC, dilution |
| eBay | Buyer–seller discovery and trust | GMV, buyers, sellers, listings, transactions | Transaction fees, advertising, shipping and other services | Fraud, transaction losses, marketing, software renewal, seller incentives, SBC |
| The Trade Desk | Buyer-side advertising decisioning | Gross platform spend and advertiser budgets | Advertising decisioning revenue and data/measurement services | Publisher and identity access, receivables, supplier settlement, hosting, AI, SBC |
| Amazon | Consumer demand, seller access, fulfillment, advertising, subscriptions, and cloud | Retail sales, seller services, ad sales, and AWS usage | AWS, advertising, seller services, subscriptions, and retail contribution | Data centers, fulfillment, leases, labor, inventory, legal, strategic investments, and dilution |

Shopify controls the merchant's own operating system more directly than eBay,
but it also becomes more exposed to payment and merchant-credit risk. eBay has
less control over the seller's business, but its differentiated supply and
trust systems can create buyer and seller dependence. The Trade Desk has a
valuable workflow position, but advertisers and publishers remain connected
through external platforms and relationships that can change.

## eBay current checkpoint: stronger activity, weaker trust-cost signal

eBay's Q2 2026 results improve the activity case: revenue was $3.134B and GMV
was $22.398B, each up 15% as reported and 14% on an FX-neutral basis. The
quarterly take rate was 13.99%, unchanged from the prior-year quarter, while
first-party advertising revenue rose 25% to $570M. This means the quarter's
growth did not require a higher headline fee on every dollar of GMV.

The forensic result is less comfortable. First-half transaction losses were
$271M versus $167M in the prior-year period. eBay attributed $68M of the
increase to unfavorable buyer and seller fraud and recovery rates and $32M to
higher volume. Active buyers were 136M, up 2%, while Q2 GMV rose 15%. The
platform is therefore monetizing more activity per buyer, but the filing does
not yet show whether that reflects healthy frequency and category mix or a
more concentrated and higher-risk transaction base.

First-half continuing operating cash flow was $1.519B and property spending
was $295M, but the improvement was helped by $794M less cash tax and better
working-capital movements. After the $338M of stock compensation and the $39M
of acquisitions recorded in the first half, the simple residual is about
$787M before dividends, buybacks, debt service, and the $1.4B cash Depop
purchase completed after quarter end. eBay therefore has improving operating
momentum and a simultaneously rising test for trust costs, dilution, and
acquisition payback.

## Same activity metric, different meaning

### Shopify: GMV through a merchant system

Shopify's FY2025 GMV was $378.4B against $11.556B of revenue. Subscription
solutions were $2.752B and merchant solutions were $8.804B. Shopify Payments
penetration was 65.6%.

GMV shows merchant activity, not Shopify's retained revenue. The important
question is whether payments and other merchant solutions add gross profit and
cash after third-party processing costs, refunds, chargebacks, merchant
balances, and credit losses. Higher Payments penetration can deepen the
relationship while changing the margin and risk profile.

### eBay: GMV through a two-sided marketplace

eBay's FY2025 GMV was $79.609B against $11.100B of revenue. The take rate was
13.94%, up from 13.77% in 2024. The take rate is a useful retained-revenue
measure, but it does not show the quality of the transactions or the cost of
protecting them.

eBay recorded $396M of transaction losses in 2025. A higher take rate can be
economically weak if it increases seller fees, reduces listing quality, or
causes buyers and sellers to migrate. The platform must preserve both sides of
the market.

### The Trade Desk: gross advertising spend through a decision system

The Trade Desk's FY2025 gross platform spend was about $13.4B against $2.896B
of revenue. Its retained percentage of spend is not the same as a marketplace
take rate because the company operates in a chain involving publishers, data
providers, identity systems, supply-path partners, and advertisers.

The key question is whether the decisioning and measurement service remains
valuable after supplier payments, data costs, hosting, AI investment, and
changes in publisher or identity access.

### Amazon: several relationships on one infrastructure base

Amazon's FY2025 net sales were $716.924B, including $269.287B from online
stores, $172.162B from third-party seller services, $68.635B from advertising,
$49.619B from subscriptions, and $128.725B from AWS. AWS generated $45.606B of
segment operating income, while North America generated $29.619B and
International generated $4.750B.

The operating system is unusually broad, but the cash burden is equally broad.
FY2025 operating cash flow was $139.514B against $128.320B of net cash property
spending and $3.841B of acquisition and other investment activity. Amazon also
carried $101.538B of present-value lease liabilities. The company controls more
of the customer and seller relationship than the narrower platforms, but it
must prove that the additional physical and computing assets earn enough cash
to compensate common owners.

## Comparable cash bridge

| FY2025 measure, USD millions | Shopify | eBay continuing operations | The Trade Desk | Amazon |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue | 11,556 | 11,100 | 2,896 | 716,924 |
| Operating cash flow | 2,033 | 2,009 | 992.7 | 139,514 |
| PP&E / platform infrastructure | 26 | 525 | 197.0 | 128,320 net cash property spending |
| Capitalized software | included in company disclosure | 134 platform development | 12.8 | included in technology and infrastructure |
| Acquisitions | 56 | 208 | not central to current screen | 3,841 acquisitions and other investments |
| Simple cash after listed investment | 1,951 | 1,276 | 782.9 | 7,353 |
| Stock-based compensation | 449 | 607 | 490.6 | 19,467 |
| Other central burden | $1.784B loans and merchant cash advances | $396M transaction losses | receivables and payables timing | $101.538B present-value lease liabilities; $128.320B net property spending |

The comparison is not a ranking of business quality. Shopify's low PP&E
spending does not eliminate payment, credit, settlement, or platform costs.
eBay's higher property spending reflects a different infrastructure profile,
while its trust and transaction-loss burden is more visible. The Trade Desk
has high cash conversion before equity cost, but its cash bridge is sensitive to
receivables, partner settlement, and a high stock-compensation load.
Amazon's cash conversion is the opposite comparison: operating cash is large,
but the physical and computing reinvestment bill is also large enough to leave
only a small residual in FY2025 and a negative company-defined FCF result in
the latest twelve months through Q2 2026.

The residuals also have different confidence levels. Shopify's screen excludes
some financial-product and investment complexity. eBay's continuing cash fell
from $2.414B in 2024 to $2.009B in 2025, so the apparent marketplace recovery
needs a working-capital and tax explanation. The Trade Desk's screen must be
tested against the cash benefit from payables and the cost of maintaining
advertiser and publisher access.

## Who captures the cleaner economics?

### Shopify

Shopify can capture a larger share of the merchant workflow because it sits at
storefront creation, checkout, payment, order data, and operating tools. The
subscription component is easier to understand as recurring software revenue.
Merchant solutions are more transaction-linked and can grow faster, but they
carry payment costs and financial risk.

The cleaner economics are in the software relationship only if merchants renew
and Shopify does not need to subsidize growth through payment pricing or credit.
The company earns more attachment by processing payments and extending finance,
but that attachment also makes merchant failure partly its financial problem.

### eBay

eBay can capture fees without owning inventory or most fulfillment assets. Its
cleaner economics depend on unique supply, search relevance, trust, and repeat
participation. The platform avoids the full physical burden of a retailer, but
it funds fraud controls, customer support, marketing, transaction losses, and
software.

The cleanest eBay economics appear in categories where buyers have strong intent
and sellers cannot easily reach the same demand elsewhere. The weak point is
standardized merchandise, where a buyer may compare many channels and a seller
may treat eBay as one advertising and distribution option.

### The Trade Desk

The Trade Desk can earn high margins because it does not own media inventory or
consumer distribution. The control point is measurement, bidding, identity,
and workflow integration. Its cleaner economics depend on advertisers and
publishers continuing to treat its decisioning as independent and useful.

The burden is less physical but not absent. Supplier settlement, data rights,
hosting, AI compute, privacy rules, receivables, and stock compensation can
consume the apparent margin. The platform is cleaner when it retains pricing
power without taking on inventory or credit risk.

## Burden transfer

“Asset-light” means that a company does not own some assets directly. It does
not mean the economic burden has disappeared.

| Burden | Shopify | eBay | The Trade Desk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inventory | Merchant funds inventory | Seller funds inventory | Publisher owns ad inventory |
| Fulfillment | Merchant and partners, with platform connections | Seller and carriers; some shipping programs | Publisher and supply partners |
| Payment / settlement | Shopify and payment partners; chargebacks | eBay and payment partners; transaction losses | Advertiser, publisher, data, and supply settlement |
| Credit | Merchant loans and cash advances | Buyer/seller payment and fraud exposure | Advertiser and partner collection exposure |
| Demand acquisition | Merchant tools and discovery partners | Marketing, category demand, buyer traffic | Advertiser budgets and media access |
| Maintenance investment | Software, support, AI, security | Platform, search, trust, support, capitalized development | Software, data, hosting, identity, AI |
| Equity burden | SBC and dilution | SBC and large buybacks | SBC and large buybacks |

The party that owns the relationship may not be the party carrying the most
capital. Shopify shifts inventory and fulfillment to merchants. eBay shifts
inventory to sellers. The Trade Desk shifts media ownership to publishers. The
platform still pays the cost required to make the external assets usable.

## Earnings-quality tests

### Activity versus retained economics

For all three companies, separate activity from revenue, revenue from gross
profit, and gross profit from owner cash. A larger denominator can make growth
look impressive while the retained percentage or cash conversion weakens.

### Working-capital timing

Review receivables, payment liabilities, customer balances, payables, deferred
revenue, refunds, and chargebacks. Cash received before the platform has
completed its obligation is not unrestricted owner cash. A payable increase can
support current operating cash and reverse later.

### Equity compensation

SBC is added back in the operating-cash reconciliation, but employees still
receive value and continuing shareholders may be diluted. Shopify recorded
$449M, eBay $607M, and The Trade Desk $490.6M of FY2025 SBC. Compare these
amounts with diluted shares, actual repurchases, and the cash that remains after
buybacks.

### Capitalized technology

Capitalized software is an asset because it is expected to benefit future
periods. It is also evidence that some platform labor is not currently shown as
expense. Measure additions, amortization life, replacement cycle, and whether
capitalized costs are rising as the platform matures.

### Acquisition and financial-product expansion

Shopify's merchant credit and financial products and eBay's category
acquisitions change the risk profile. A platform that expands into financing,
payments, or new categories can increase total revenue while also increasing
losses, capital needs, regulation, and operating complexity.

## Macro and durability comparison

| Exposure | Shopify | eBay | The Trade Desk | Amazon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consumer liquidity | Direct through merchant sales | Direct through buyer demand and resale | Indirect through advertiser budgets | Direct through retail demand and seller health |
| Interest rates | Merchant credit and small-business formation | Consumer and seller spending | Corporate advertising budgets and valuation multiple | Consumer demand, cloud budgets, financing, and valuation multiple |
| Inflation | Merchant costs and payment volume | Prices, shipping, and seller margins | Media, data, and technology costs | Wages, delivery, power, equipment, and fulfillment costs |
| Regulation | Payments, lending, privacy, AI | Consumer protection, payments, trade, AI | Privacy, identity, competition, advertising transparency | Antitrust, labor, privacy, payments, tax, consumer protection, and AI |
| External dependency | Merchant survival and discovery channels | Carriers, sellers, buyers, payment systems | Publishers, identity, data, supply platforms | Sellers, carriers, labor, power, equipment, cloud customers, and regulators |

The most durable platform is not necessarily the one with the highest growth.
It is the one whose participants continue to need the workflow during stress,
whose counterparty remains healthy, and whose balance sheet can fund trust,
security, and product renewal without destroying owner returns.

## Valuation expectations

The price-implied question differs by company:

- Shopify requires continued high GMV growth, payment attachment, merchant
  retention, and credit quality after a large valuation expansion.
- eBay requires a recovery in cash conversion, continued category liquidity,
  advertising growth, and controlled transaction losses despite a mature
  marketplace profile.
- The Trade Desk requires sustained advertiser adoption, stable take economics,
  continued CTV and identity access, and a recovery from the 2026 growth
  slowdown without a large increase in equity or infrastructure cost.
- Amazon requires continued AWS, advertising, seller-service, and retail growth
  after charging the recurring data-center, fulfillment, lease, strategic-
  investment, legal, and dilution burden.

Do not value GMV, gross spend, or merchant count directly. Value the future cash
retained after the platform pays for the systems that create and protect the
activity.

## Cohort conclusions

1. Shopify has the deepest merchant workflow attachment but carries the most
   explicit financial-product expansion risk.
2. eBay has the clearest two-sided trust and differentiated-supply test, but
   its relationship is more exposed to user migration and transaction quality.
3. The Trade Desk has the lightest physical burden and potentially cleanest
   margin structure, but its control point depends on access to external media,
   identity, data, and advertiser budgets.
4. Activity metrics are leading indicators. Common cash requires take-rate
   quality, contribution margin, working-capital discipline, platform renewal,
   equity-cost treatment, and participant health.
5. The cleanest economics belong to the platform that controls a repeated
   workflow while leaving counterparties healthy enough to keep supplying the
   activity.
