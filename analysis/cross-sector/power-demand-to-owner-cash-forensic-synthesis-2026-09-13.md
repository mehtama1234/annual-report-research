# Power demand to owner cash: cross-sector forensic synthesis

Date baseline: 2026-09-13

## Business economics: the question

The archive now contains enough evidence to separate the “more electricity is
needed” narrative into distinct economic layers. AI, electrification, industrial
reshoring, and digital infrastructure may create real demand, but the investment
outcome depends on where the demand is controlled and who funds the system before
cash is collected.

The chain is:

`large-load demand -> regulatory approval / contract -> generation and transmission -> equipment -> field installation -> powered capacity -> recurring or transactional cash`

The same demand can create very different shareholder economics at each step.

This is not a ranking. It identifies where the economic burden migrates.

## 1. Regulated recovery is the cleanest funding mechanism, not free capital

FPL is the strongest example of a formal recovery architecture. Investment in
generation, transmission, and distribution can enter rate base and earn an allowed
return, subject to regulatory approval and customer affordability. This is cleaner
than a contractor's unsigned award or a developer's origination pipeline.

NextEra's Q2 2026 filing also shows why the mechanism should not be confused with
owner cash. In H1 2026, NEE generated $7.276B of operating cash flow but spent
$19.389B on capital expenditures, independent-power investments, and nuclear fuel.
It issued $15.566B of long-term debt, increased short-term debt by $3.431B, sold
assets, and issued equity. FPL's rate base creates a recovery path, but shareholders
still bear financing, construction, dilution, regulatory, and political risk.

The forensic test is to separate:

`earned regulated ROE + ordinary customer growth + approved rate-base additions`

from reserve amortization, storm-cost recovery, tax-credit timing, hedge marks,
and financing transactions. NextEra's H1 2026 results included $196M after-tax of
FPL reserve-surplus amortization, $1.173B of clean-energy tax-credit proceeds,
$403M of NEER capitalized interest, and large favorable hedge and prior-year-XPLR
effects. These are disclosed accounting and financing mechanisms; they are not
automatically improper, but they are not interchangeable with durable operating
cash.

## 2. Midstream integration captures more optionality but inherits more complexity

ONEOK illustrates the layer between regulated utility infrastructure and field
execution. Its connected system can monetize gathering, processing, fractionation,
transportation, storage, refined products, crude, and export routes. Integration
can create stronger utilization and optimization than a single pipeline tariff.

The cost is that “fee-based” becomes a mixed category. ONEOK's filing identifies
percent-of-proceeds contracts, NGL and product spreads, location differentials,
optimization and marketing, retained product, and affiliate economics. FY2025
adjusted EBITDA was $8.020B, but the reconciliation included $516M from
unconsolidated affiliates and a $386M deduction for equity earnings. Total debt
was $33.965B against $78M of cash.

ONEOK's $2.447B of FY2025 cash after capex is meaningful evidence of owner-cash
capacity, but it must fund dividends, debt service, and the next project cycle. The
midstream control point is therefore cleaner than a contractor backlog, but less
clean than a simple regulated recovery stream.

## 3. Equipment makers capture bottleneck value before the build is complete

Vertiv, Generac, KLA, and F5 occupy different equipment or control interfaces.
Their strongest evidence is not just AI language or orders. It is pricing,
installed-base pull-through, gross margin, service/consumables, and cash conversion
after inventory, warranty, R&D, and factory investment.

Equipment makers may capture a better slice of the economics than field contractors
because a differentiated product or process-control interface can scale with less
labor. But they remain exposed to customer capex cycles and supply-chain constraints.
The next filing must distinguish booked demand from shipped product, recognized
revenue, inventory, customer advances, warranty, and free cash flow.

## 4. Powered real estate converts demand into recurring rent, but not effortless AFFO

Equinix and Digital Realty own or control a denser relationship: powered space,
interconnection, network adjacency, and tenant deployment workflow. This can create
switching costs and recurring revenue after a facility is live.

The burden arrives earlier and at larger scale. Development requires power access,
construction, equipment, financing, lease-up, and tenant concentration. AFFO/FFO
therefore needs a sustaining-capex and development-return bridge. A lease backlog or
annualized rent figure proves demand; it does not prove that the next dollar of
powered capacity earns an adequate return.

## 5. Contractors monetize scarcity last and carry the most execution burden

Quanta, Sterling, MasTec, Comfort Systems, and EMCOR are closest to the labor and
project bottleneck. Their backlog can be highly valuable because permits, skilled
crews, electrical coordination, site knowledge, and schedule reliability are hard
to assemble quickly.

But the accounting distance from backlog to owner cash is also greatest. Revenue
may be recognized over time using cost-to-cost estimates while contract assets,
claims, retainage, receivables, subcontractor obligations, equipment, and working
capital accumulate. The correct sequence is:

`signed work -> measured progress -> gross profit -> billed cash -> collected cash -> capex/acquisition residual`

The recent company cases make the contradiction visible:

- Sterling's FY2025 operating cash flow was $440M against $77M capex and $482M cash acquisitions, leaving a negative residual after the CEC acquisition.
- MasTec's H1 2026 operating cash flow was about $120M against $188M capex while debt obligations were $2.75B and receivables plus contract assets were about $4.23B.
- Comfort Systems and EMCOR show strong backlog and RPO, but their next proof remains contract-asset, claims, retainage, labor, and collection conversion.

Contractors may be essential to the build while still earning lower risk-adjusted
returns than the relationship owners above them.

The table changes the interpretation of “power demand.” NextEra can show a
large construction deficit even with a formal recovery mechanism. ONEOK shows a
positive annual residual but carries substantial debt and affiliate complexity.
Vertiv shows stronger product economics but must separate working-capital release
from repeatable cash. Equinix has the clearest recurring customer relationship in
the group but the heaviest physical investment requirement. The contractors can
show very large backlog while producing negative or acquisition-dependent
residual cash.

The next filing pass should not force these figures into one multiple. It should
reconcile each company within its own model: regulated return after financing;
midstream cash after leverage and maintenance capital; equipment cash after
working-capital normalization and warranty; property cash after sustaining and
growth capital; and contractor cash after contract assets, claims, acquisitions,
and dilution.

## The burden-transfer pattern

The power build moves burden across the stack:

`utility customer / ratepayer -> utility balance sheet -> developer and tax-credit market -> equipment inventory and factories -> contractor labor and working capital -> data-center tenant or industrial customer`

No layer makes the burden disappear. It changes who has to finance it and who has
the contractual mechanism to recover it.

The cleaner economic controllers tend to own one of three mechanisms:

1. A regulated recovery path with an allowed return.
2. A differentiated product or workflow that customers cannot easily defer.
3. A dense network or relationship that captures repeated usage after the asset is built.

The burden carriers tend to own the construction, inventory, labor, property, or
commodity exposure before the control point has fully matured.

## Alden and Damodaran synthesis

Through Lyn Alden's lens, electricity and infrastructure demand are durable because
they are physical prerequisites for modern life and production. But liquidity
determines the timing and financing of the build. Existing regulated assets and
dense interconnection may be more resilient than unbuilt backlog, speculative
origination, or highly leveraged expansion.

Through Damodaran's lens, the narrative must be translated into incremental return
on capital. “AI demand” becomes investable only when the company can show customer
commitment, project completion, utilization, pricing, cash generation, and returns
above the cost of capital. High growth financed by debt, tax-credit proceeds,
partner capital, dilution, or asset sales may still create value, but the valuation
must include those financing claims.

Our forensic lens adds the denominator discipline: adjusted EBITDA, backlog, rate
base, megawatts, RPO, annualized rent, and tax-credit proceeds are different forms
of visibility. None is owner cash without the associated capital and burden bridge.

## Cross-sector thesis breakers

The combined thesis weakens if:

- utility rate recovery is delayed or politically constrained while construction spending continues;
- development backlog grows faster than project starts, customer funding, or operating cash;
- tax credits, hedge gains, reserve amortization, or asset sales become recurring substitutes for operating earnings;
- midstream volume and optimization margins weaken while debt and growth capex remain high;
- equipment orders rise while inventory, warranty, receivables, or factory capex absorb the cash;
- data-center rent or interconnection growth fails to cover sustaining and development capital; or
- contractor backlog rises while contract assets, claims, retainage, debt, labor cost, and receivables grow faster than collected cash.

## Next filing work

For each layer, capture the same minimum bridge:

`demand indicator -> recognized revenue / rate-base addition -> margin or allowed return -> operating cash -> required capital -> financing and dilution -> residual owner cash`

The next high-value work is not another AI narrative. It is a comparable filing
table for NextEra, ONEOK, Vertiv, Equinix, MasTec, Sterling, Comfort Systems, and
EMCOR that separates organic growth, acquired growth, maintenance capital, growth
capital, partner/NCI claims, and cash after financing burden.
