# Pre-Collected Demand And Obligation-Backed Visibility Comparison

Date: 2026-08-11
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Plain-English Claim

One of the archive's more important recurring patterns is that some businesses do not wait to get paid after the value is delivered.

They get paid earlier.

The useful question is not only who has recurring demand.

It is who collects cash, deferred revenue, deposits, stored value, or partner-funded obligations before the full service is consumed.

## Core Question

Which companies build stronger visibility and operating control because customers, partners, or ecosystems fund part of the relationship in advance?

## Why These Five Belong Together

These companies are in different sectors, but they all benefit when part of the economic relationship is funded before the last unit of service is delivered.

- Starbucks carries prepaid stored value and reward liabilities inside a daily routine business.
- Delta sells miles into a partner ecosystem and carries deferred loyalty obligations while collecting large co-brand remuneration.
- VeriSign runs a critical internet toll system with large prepaid deferred-revenue balances.
- Fortinet pairs hardware and security subscriptions with a very large deferred-revenue pool.
- Zscaler shows the cleaner cloud-native version of the same idea, with deferred revenue and ARR backing an access-governance platform.

The stronger conclusion is that pre-collected demand often makes a business more durable, more legible, and easier to scale than the surface category implies.

## Exact Mechanism

### 1. Starbucks shows how a daily routine business becomes partially prepaid

Starbucks is useful because the surface business looks simple.

A customer buys coffee.

But the packet says cards can be activated in stores, online, and through the app, and that unredeemed card balances plus unredeemed Stars sit in deferred revenue.

That means some of the demand is pre-funded before the next drink is poured.

The routine is not only behavioral.

It is partly financed in advance.

### 2. Delta shows that loyalty can become a pre-funded business inside the business

Delta's filing chain makes this pattern explicit.

American Express remuneration totaled `$8.2B` in `2025`, and management expects that figure to grow toward `$10B` over the next few years.

The filing also says `12%` of revenue miles flown in `2025` were from award travel.

In Q2 `2026`, American Express remuneration reached `$2.4B`, up `16%`.

That means the airline is not just flying passengers and then collecting fares.

It is also selling a future travel-and-status currency into a payments ecosystem before the full travel obligation is used.

### 3. VeriSign shows the toll-franchise version of the same pattern

VeriSign is the cleaner infrastructure case.

Deferred revenue was `$1.3845B` at the end of `2025`, then rose to `$1.4294B` and `$1.4489B` through the first half of `2026`.

Against fiscal `2025` revenue of `$1.6566B`, those balances are large enough to matter structurally, not cosmetically.

The point is not just that VeriSign is recurring.

It is that a meaningful part of the economic relationship is collected and recognized over time while the company keeps the naming-and-resolution layer working.

### 4. Fortinet shows how hybrid infrastructure can still carry a giant obligation pool

Fortinet matters because it stops this memo from becoming only a consumer or contract-software story.

Fiscal `2025` deferred revenue was `$7.12B`.

Current deferred revenue rose from `$3.636B` at the end of `2025` to `$3.7263B` in Q1 `2026` and `$3.8418B` in Q2 `2026`.

The company still sells appliances and faces hardware cycles, but the larger structure is increasingly supported by service obligations and recurring commitments already on the books.

That makes the model more stable than a simple firewall box vendor would look from the outside.

### 5. Zscaler shows the software-native version of pre-collected trust demand

Zscaler is the purest control-layer version here.

Deferred revenue reached `$2.351B` in FY2026 Q1, `$2.355B` in FY2026 Q2, and `$2.477B` in FY2026 Q3, while ARR rose from `$3.204B` to `$3.525B`.

That matters because the company is being paid to keep access, policy, identity, and AI-governance controls running across a future service period.

The customer is not buying a one-time object.

The customer is pre-funding governed continuity.

## The Stronger Conclusion

The stronger conclusion is that some of the archive's better businesses are not only recurring.

They are pre-collected.

In plain English:

- the customer or partner funds part of the relationship early
- the company then carries an obligation to deliver or honor the service over time
- that structure creates better visibility, stronger retention logic, and often better cash economics than a pure point-of-sale model

This is why deferred revenue, stored value, deposits, and partner-funded obligations should be treated as strategic signals, not just accounting labels.

The common pattern is that these companies collect economic commitment before the full use event is complete.

## Why This Pattern Matters

This pattern matters because it changes how durability should be read.

Two companies can both look recurring.

But the one that gets funded earlier often has:

- better visibility
- stronger customer lock-in
- more room to invest ahead of delivery
- a clearer economic buffer than a pure pay-after-use model

That does not guarantee strength.

But it usually changes the operating posture.

## Consumer And Cultural Meaning

These packets also support a broader behavioral read.

- consumers increasingly accept stored-value, wallet-like, and reward-backed routines
- travelers accept ecosystem currencies that function beyond one flight purchase
- businesses increasingly pre-commit to security and infrastructure continuity instead of buying one-off fixes
- the digital economy keeps rewarding narrow layers that can charge in advance for trusted continuity

The larger social point is that trust and routine often become strongest when the relationship is funded before the next decision has to be remade from scratch.

## Industrial And Operating Pressures

This pattern is valuable, but it creates obligation.

- Starbucks must still serve the drink well enough that prepaid and reward behavior stays healthy.
- Delta still has to deliver the flight network, seat availability, and premium experience behind the loyalty economics.
- VeriSign has to keep a critical internet layer available and governable.
- Fortinet and Zscaler must keep policy, security, and service continuity strong enough to justify contract renewal and deferred obligations.

The larger lesson is that pre-collected economics are only attractive if the operator can keep the trust layer intact after collecting the money.

## Disconfirming Evidence And Limits

This pattern would weaken if:

- deferred revenue or stored-value pools stopped translating into durable retention
- pre-collected balances grew while service quality deteriorated
- partner-funded obligations became less profitable because redemption, support, or delivery costs rose too fast
- customers moved back toward pay-as-you-go behavior in categories where routine had looked strong

There is also a conceptual limit:

pre-collected demand is not automatically good demand.

It can mask future execution problems if the service obligation becomes harder to fulfill than the early cash made it appear.
