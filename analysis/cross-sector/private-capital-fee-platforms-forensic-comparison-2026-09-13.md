# Private-capital fee platforms: Blackstone versus Apollo and public-market managers

Blackstone adds the major private-markets comparator to Apollo, BlackRock, and
T. Rowe Price. The common label “asset manager” hides materially different
cash, incentive, liquidity, and balance-sheet claims.

| Company | Primary control point | FY2025 headline evidence | Main denominator |
|---|---|---|---|
| KKR | Alternative-asset management linked to Global Atlantic insurance, private wealth, strategic holdings, and asset-based finance | Q2 2026: `$796.5B` AUM, `$638.4B` FPAUM, `$1.214B` quarterly FRE, `$1.539B` TOE, `$1.493B` ANI, `$143B` uncalled commitments | FRE after compensation and dilution, plus separately valued insurance economics and probability-weighted carry; do not capitalize ANI or consolidated GAAP income as if all were recurring fees |
| Blackstone | Private-capital distribution, perpetual/insurance/wealth channels, and realizations | ~$1.275T AUM; ~$5.7B FRE; ~$7.1B DE; $239.4B inflows; $125.6B realizations | FRE plus conservative performance cash, less partner, compensation, tax, NCI, debt, and clawback claims |
| Apollo | Origination, credit, retirement services, and Athene spread economics | Existing Apollo dossier separates FRE, SRE, ANI, insurance assets/liabilities, and HoldCo claims | FRE + normalized SRE/ANI, not one consolidated FCF multiple |
| Brookfield | Global fundraising, operating-asset deployment, insurance solutions, and principal capital allocation | Approximately $5.4B FY2025 distributable earnings before realizations; approximately $6.0B including realizations; approximately $180B insurance assets in Q1 2026 | Fee earnings plus operating cash and insurance spread after partner/NCI claims, debt, tax, reinvestment, and capital requirements |
| Carlyle | Private-equity, credit, and AlpInvest fundraising and investment workflow | Approximately $1.2B FY2025 FRE; approximately $1.7B DE; approximately $477B AUM; approximately $337B fee-earning AUM | FRE after recurring costs plus probability-weighted carry, credit outcomes, partner claims, tax, and dilution |

| T. Rowe Price | Active-management relationship and public-market product shelf | Existing T. Rowe dossier tests outflows, fee compression, and cost rigidity | Normalized earnings under flow and fee-rate pressure |

## Business economics: the core comparison

Blackstone and Apollo can grow through private credit, insurance, and wealth
channels even when classic private-equity realizations slow. That is a source
of durability and a source of hidden complexity. AUM can grow while fees fall,
liquidity terms lengthen, marks remain optimistic, or common shareholders absorb
more compensation and partner claims.

BlackRock and T. Rowe have more transparent public-market flow and fee-rate
signals, but they face daily fee compression, passive substitution, and visible
outflow risk. Their assets are more liquid; their revenue may be less
transactional but also less protected by private-market lockups.

## Alden and Damodaran synthesis

The Alden lens favors private-capital platforms when institutional balance-sheet
complexity and demand for alternative funding rise. It asks whether liquidity
stress is being transferred to funds, insurers, investors, or the manager's
reputation. The public-market lens asks whether flows and fee rates survive
market competition and lower-cost substitutes.

The Damodaran lens requires a component valuation. FRE deserves a recurring
multiple; realized performance income deserves a lower or explicitly
probability-weighted multiple; spread earnings require credit, funding,
insurance, and required-capital normalization. Capitalizing DE, ANI, or GAAP
income as if every dollar were recurring management fee cash is the central
financial-engineering error to avoid.

## Risks, accounting tests, and what to check next

Test fee-paying AUM against net flows, fee rates, product mix, and retention.
Keep realizations, marks, carried interest, clawbacks, partner allocations,
insurance spread, and consolidated fund activity separate from recurring fee
income. Reconcile compensation, SBC, partner and NCI claims, debt, taxes,
liquidity, statutory capital, and diluted shares before calling distributable
earnings common-owner cash. The next filing should show whether new capital is
actually fee-paying, whether fee rates hold, and whether realizations convert
to parent cash without transferring risk to partners, policyholders, or debt.

KKR makes the comparison concrete. In Q2 2026, its `$1.214B` FRE was supported
by `$1.250B` of management fees, while realized performance income contributed
`$212M` after the disclosed compensation allocation and Insurance Operating
Earnings contributed `$288M`. Those streams belong in different valuation
columns. KKR's `$796.5B` AUM also included `$143B` of uncalled commitments and
`$72B` of committed capital not yet paying fees; these are useful indicators of
future revenue, but they are not current fee cash. The insurance column must
also preserve Global Atlantic's liabilities, asset risk, liquidity, and
statutory-capital burden.

The next filings should compare net fee-earning AUM, fee rates, gross and net
flows, realizations, loss and reserve experience, carried-interest clawbacks,
SBC and partner economics, debt, NCI, liquidity terms, and per-share cash
conversion.

Brookfield adds a broader capital-allocator case. Its FY2025 distributable
earnings before realizations were approximately `$5.4B`, total distributable
earnings approximately `$6.0B`, and net income approximately `$3.2B`. Q1 2026
capital raised was approximately `$67B`, including approximately `$44B` of
insurance capital, and insurance assets reached approximately `$180B`. These
figures show fundraising and platform scale, but common-owner cash requires a
separate bridge for partner and noncontrolling claims, insurance liabilities,
asset-level debt, marks, taxes, reinvestment, and parent liquidity. Brookfield's
larger opportunity set is also a larger denominator problem.

Carlyle adds the more concentrated fee-plus-carry case. FY2025 fee-related
earnings were approximately `$1.2B`, distributable earnings approximately
`$1.7B`, and realized net performance revenue approximately `$357M` on
approximately `$337B` of fee-earning AUM. Q1 2026 showed approximately `$300M`
of FRE but a GAAP net loss of approximately `$132.2M`; Q2 showed approximately
`$358M` of FRE and `$115M` of realized net performance revenue. The fee engine
was steadier than GAAP earnings, but the difference identifies separate risks:
recurring fees, marks, realizations, partner claims, and carry timing.
