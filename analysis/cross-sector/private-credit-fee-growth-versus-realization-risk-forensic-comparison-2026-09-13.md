# Private-credit fee growth versus realization risk: forensic comparison

Ares adds a credit-heavy, fundraising-led alternative-manager model to the
Blackstone/Apollo comparison. KKR adds a broader platform with insurance,
strategic holdings, and monetization economics; the comparison therefore
needs to separate the fee engine from each firm's additional balance-sheet and
performance claims.

## Business economics: what the comparison changes

Ares appears less dependent on classic realizations than Blackstone, but its
credit concentration means “recurring fee” does not equal “low risk.” Fee
income may persist while credit performance deteriorates, because the manager
can continue earning fees on committed or managed capital. The eventual test is
whether borrowers repay, funds distribute, investors renew, and new capital
arrives at acceptable returns.

The Q2 comparison makes the difference measurable. Ares raised `$36.4B` and
deployed `$35.9B`, with `$170.0B` of available capital and `$92.6B` of AUM not
yet paying fees. KKR reported `$143B` of uncalled commitments and `$72B` of
committed capital not yet paying fees, but its current earnings also include
insurance operating earnings and realized performance income. Ares therefore
has the clearer credit-deployment test; KKR has the broader but more complex
component valuation. Neither company's AUM or dry powder should be treated as
current owner cash.

Blackstone's larger perpetual and wealth channels may improve capital
permanence, while Apollo's insurance and retirement platform adds spread and
origination economics. Those features diversify revenue but also add liquidity,
duration, regulatory, and balance-sheet claims.

## Alden and Damodaran synthesis

The Alden lens follows where liquidity risk sits. Private credit can replace
bank balance-sheet capacity, but a tightening cycle moves stress into borrowers,
funds, insurers, and manager reputation. The Damodaran lens asks whether each
manager earns attractive incremental returns on new FPAUM, deployed capital,
insurance assets, and acquisitions after compensation and required capital.

The cleanest recurring denominator is FRE after taxes, compensation, partner
economics, and corporate costs. Realized Income, DE, SRE, ANI, and GAAP income
must be decomposed into recurring fees, realized performance, marks, spread
income, credit losses, and claims. Capitalizing all of them at one premium
multiple is the central financial-engineering error.

Future filings should compare FPAUM and fee rates, gross/net fundraising,
available capital, deployment, realization and distribution rates, nonaccruals,
losses, reserve/valuation policy, carried-interest clawbacks, SBC, partner and
NCI claims, debt, insurance capital, and per-share cash conversion.
