# Recreation, Lifestyle, And Occasion Demand Comparison

Date: 2026-08-11
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Plain Claim

Consumer spending is not simply strong or weak.

The better question is whether the purchase gives the buyer a role.

In this lane, the useful roles are:

- traveler
- loyalty member
- host
- guest
- gift-giver
- fiance or spouse
- design-led homeowner
- restaurant diner
- hospitality operator

The evidence does not say every discretionary category is safe. It says certain kinds of spending remained more defensible when they were tied to travel, status, hosting, gifting, ritual, identity, or shared experiences. The same evidence also shows the pressure underneath the demand: housing rates, tariffs, inventory, food cost, labor, route density, property ownership, and geopolitical disruption.

## Packet Inputs Used

This comparison relies on the packet sections that make recreation, lifestyle, and occasion demand visible:

- annual report takeaways

The memo should be read as a synthesis of those packet inputs rather than as a loose claim that consumers still spend on experiences.

## Common unit and relationship cash bridge

The cohort can now be compared on a common economic sequence without pretending
that the accounting denominators are identical:

`customer activity -> unit or partner economics -> labor/property/inventory burden -> maintenance and growth capital -> financing and dilution -> common-owner cash`

| Model | Company | Filed cash screen | What the parent controls | Burden that must be charged before comparison |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Growth-stage direct operator | CAVA | FY2025 OCF `$184.8M` less PP&E `$158.7M` = about `$26.1M`; 439 restaurants, AUV about `$2.934M` | Menu, guest relationship, pricing, store data, labor execution, and openings | Food, wages, occupancy, pre-opening, maintenance, leases, technology, and the capital needed to mature 73 new units |
| Mature direct operator | Darden | FY2026 OCF `$1.853B` less `$734M` capex = about `$1.119B` before other claims | Full-service brands, menu, service, locations, guest relationship, and direct store cash | Food, labor, occupancy, maintenance versus growth capex, leases, Chuy's integration, debt, and traffic |
| Franchise platform | RBI / YUM / Wingstop | RBI `$1.297B`; YUM `$857M`; Wingstop `$87.1M` after listed parent investments | Brand standards, royalty contracts, digital/loyalty, development, and system data | Franchisee labor, food, rent, remodels, financing, local execution, support, advertising funds, and parent leverage |
| Branded hotel fee platform | Marriott | FY2025 OCF `$3.212B` less capital/technology `$604M` and CitizenM acquisition `$350M` = about `$2.245B` screen | Brand, reservation, loyalty, co-brand, revenue management, and owner distribution | Loyalty liability, technology, guarantees, contract acquisition, owner health, debt, and diluted shares |
| Hotel property owner | Host | FY2025 OCF `$1.510B` less property capex `$644M` = about `$866M` before debt and distributions | Hotel locations, rooms, property condition, renovation, and residual real-estate value | Labor, insurance, utilities, management fees, renewal cycle, interest, refinancing, asset value, and REIT/OP claims |

These screens are not an investment ranking. CAVA and Darden record more of the
restaurant sale and cost stack themselves. RBI, YUM, and Wingstop report a
smaller parent revenue base because franchisees record most store sales, but
the parent can still carry debt, acquired stores, technology, guarantees, and
support obligations. Marriott shifts most building capital to hotel owners;
Host retains the physical asset and its full renewal cycle.

The useful cross-company test is therefore whether the control point earns a
return after the burden it cannot avoid. For the direct operators, compare
traffic, price/mix, store-level margin, maintenance capex, and mature-unit
returns. For the franchise platforms, compare royalties with franchisee
openings, closures, remodels, and operator financing. For the hotel cases,
compare fee cash or property cash with loyalty/guarantee claims or renewal,
labor, and refinancing requirements. This completes the structural comparison;
the remaining work is current filing updates and unit-level evidence where the
companies do not disclose a complete partner return.

## Franchisee evidence: what is disclosed and what is still missing

The franchise comparison has an important evidence boundary. Parent-company
cash is observable. The cash return of the operator who staffs, leases,
finances, remodels, and runs the restaurant is usually not reported in a
standard, comparable form. System sales and royalty growth therefore cannot
be treated as proof that the network is healthy.

| Company | Disclosed parent-side evidence | Evidence of parent-side burden | Missing operator proof | Current reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RBI | 2025 system-wide sales were about `$46.762B`; more than `95%` of restaurants were franchised. | 2025 operating cash flow was `$1.714B`; RBI spent `$265M` on PP&E and `$152M` on acquired franchised restaurants; long-term debt was `$13.25B`. | Comparable franchisee cash return, closure and delinquency rates, remodel funding per store, and operator debt capacity. | The royalty model is real, but parent acquisitions and leverage show that the system is not costless to support. |
| YUM | More than `63,000` restaurants and approximately `1,500` franchisees; 2025 franchise and property revenue was `$3.473B`. | 2025 operating cash flow was `$2.010B`; capital spending was `$371M`; franchise-restaurant acquisitions were `$782M`; long-term debt was `$11.872B`. | Franchisee margin after rent, labor, debt, and remodels; closures, delinquency, and the return earned on acquired or refranchised stores. | Strong parent cash does not settle whether the franchise base can fund the next wave of units and remodels without recurring corporate help. |
| Wingstop | `2,999` of `3,056` restaurants were franchised at year-end 2025, or about `98%`; system-wide sales rose `12.1%`. | Domestic same-store sales declined `3.3%`; parent operating cash flow fell to `$153.1M`; PP&E and restaurant acquisitions used `$66.0M`; long-term debt was about `$1.221B`. | Franchisee store profit, closures, remodel funding, and store-level debt or financing availability. | The high-royalty model has strong operating leverage, but the 2025 gap between system growth and domestic same-store demand makes operator health the central unresolved test. |
| Domino's | FY2025 ended with `22,142` stores; more than `85%` of U.S. retail sales were digital; global net store growth was `776`. | Corporate also carries supply centers, technology, support, inventory, facilities, debt, and buyback obligations. | Comparable franchisee return after delivery labor, local rent, store debt, support fees, and remodeling; closure and delinquency trends. | Store growth and digital activity show network reach, not necessarily adequate operator returns. Supply-chain volume must be tested together with franchisee liquidity. |

This is not a request for a perfect private-company dataset. The practical
next-filing test is narrower: look for openings and closures, same-store
transactions rather than only ticket, remodel and development deferrals,
acquired-store performance, franchisee assistance, delinquency or defaults,
and any disclosure that makes the operator's cash burden measurable. Until
that evidence appears, the correct cohort conclusion is **qualified**, not
proven. The parent may have a durable control point while the weakest part of
the network is quietly losing the ability to reinvest.

## What This Adds To The Archive

The earlier fandom and participation memo proved that consumers still spend to become fans, players, members, beauty participants, and identity buyers.

This memo extends that pattern into physical life:

- Travel spending is not just a hotel night; Marriott and Hilton show branded mobility, loyalty, status, pipeline growth, and owner confidence.
- Home spending is not just furniture; RH shows taste and status, while Williams-Sonoma shows hosting, cooking, gifting, and design services.
- Jewelry is not just a hard good; Signet shows rituals, milestones, holidays, bridal, fashion, and self-expression.
- Restaurants and hospitality are not just end markets; Sysco shows the route-density, sourcing, product-cost, and local-case infrastructure required for those experiences to happen.

The stronger conclusion is:

When consumers are selective, the most defensible spending often attaches to identity, occasion, membership, hosting, travel, or social participation. But the earnings quality depends on where the company sits. Relationship owners can have cleaner models. Merchandisers carry inventory and cost pressure. Support-layer companies carry labor, freight, sourcing, and volume execution.

## Company-Level Strategy Reads

### Marriott

Marriott is trying to be the default branded-travel network. The key evidence is not just its room count. The stronger evidence is the combination of `1,779,936` rooms at 2025 year-end, about `610,000` pipeline rooms, loyalty members booking about `75%` of U.S. room nights and `68%` of global room nights, Q2 2026 RevPAR growth of `3.4%`, and new U.S. co-branded card agreements.

The strategy is to own repeat travel behavior while property owners carry much of the physical hotel burden.

### Hilton

Hilton is trying to compound hospitality demand through brands, fees, pipeline conversion, and global room growth. The key evidence is `9,158` properties and `1,351,351` rooms at 2025 year-end, `520,500` pipeline rooms, Q2 2026 RevPAR growth of `3.9%`, a `541,300` room pipeline, and `6.1%` net unit growth.

The strategy is to turn travel demand and owner demand into an asset-light growth engine.

### Airbnb

Airbnb owns the host-guest interface rather than the lodging asset. The 2025
platform had more than `5M` hosts and more than `2.5B` guest arrivals across
over `220` countries and regions. Q2 2026 revenue grew `17%` and support cost
per booking declined about `16%` as nearly `45%` of issues beginning with the
AI assistant were resolved without a human agent.

The operating advantage is relationship density and trust without hotel
property ownership. The risk is that the platform inherits responsibility for
safety, refunds, tax, local regulation, and service quality even when hosts
fund the physical stay. The next filing must show whether app use, repeat
bookings, host quality, and support efficiency produce higher contribution
after stock compensation and product investment.

### RH

RH is trying to become a luxury taste and place-making system, not a normal home furnishings store. The key evidence is its filing language around Products, Places, Services, and Spaces; RH Hospitality inside `25` Design Galleries; and the sharp difference between Q3 FY25 operating income of `$105.9M` and Q1 FY26 operating income of `$34.2M` with a net loss.

The [RH dossier](../deep-company-pages/rh-inc.md) treats orders placed as a
leading measure rather than cash, and separates core product retail from
hospitality, galleries, inventory, leases, and destination investment.

The strategy can be powerful when affluent home demand is active, but the burden is real because housing, interest rates, and high-ticket timing can quickly hit earnings.

### Williams-Sonoma

Williams-Sonoma is trying to own the better-home relationship across cooking, entertaining, decorating, gifting, design, loyalty, e-commerce, and B2B. The key evidence is fiscal 2025 comparable brand revenue growth of `3.5%`, operating margin of `18.1%`, diluted EPS of `$8.84`, and Q1 2026 comparable brand revenue growth of `4.8%`.

The strategy is not only to sell home goods. It is to use a multi-brand platform to serve the occasions and routines that happen inside the home, while managing tariffs and inventory tightly enough to protect margins.

### Signet

Signet is trying to own emotionally important jewelry occasions through brand love, connected commerce, assortment discipline, and price architecture. The key evidence is Q1 FY27 same-store sales growth of `1.8%`, merchandise AUR growth of about `5%`, Q3 FY26 same-store sales growth of `3.0%`, Bridal AUR growth of `6%`, and Fashion AUR growth of `8%`.

The strategy works if consumers keep treating bridal, holidays, gifting, and self-purchase as meaningful occasions. It weakens if engagement behavior, wedding spend, diamond economics, or discretionary trade-down break the model.

### Wayfair

Wayfair is the logistics-mediated home case. FY2025 revenue grew `5.1%` to
`$12.5B`; Q2 2026 active customers reached `21.7M`, orders delivered reached
`10.6M`, and Perigold grew more than `35%`. In Q3 2025, repeat customers placed
`80.1%` of orders and LTM revenue per active customer was `$578`.

The strategy is to turn fragmented supplier inventory into a usable digital
selection and delivery service. That can take share from weaker retailers, but
the parent still funds advertising, technology, delivery, fulfillment, returns,
leases, and customer support. Wayfair's adjusted EBITDA cannot be treated as
owner cash until those costs, stock compensation, and capitalized software are
included.

### Sysco

Sysco is trying to be the operating backbone for restaurants and foodservice customers. The key evidence is Q3 FY26 sales growth of `4.7%`, U.S. Foodservice volume growth of `2.3%`, U.S. local volume growth of `3.3%`, and gross profit growth of `6.5%` to about `$3.8B`.

The strategy is not consumer-facing, but it is consumer-sensitive. Sysco benefits when people travel, eat out, gather, and spend on hospitality, but it must convert that activity through route density, sourcing, labor, and product-cost discipline.

### CAVA

CAVA is the company-operated control case beneath the franchise and distribution
layers. It owns the guest relationship, menu, labor execution, site decisions,
and store-level outcome. FY2025 restaurant-level profit was `$285.0M`, but
operating cash flow was `$184.8M`, PP&E purchases were `$158.7M`, and
consolidated net income was `$63.7M`.

The conclusion is conditional. CAVA's restaurant-level economics may support
attractive new-unit returns, but the parent must retain enough cash after
opening 73 restaurants, maintaining existing stores, paying leases, funding
technology, and absorbing corporate costs. AUV and restaurant-level profit
cannot substitute for a common-owner cash bridge.

### Darden

Darden is the mature full-service direct-operator case. FY2025 sales increased
`6.0%` to `$12.1B`, blended same-restaurant sales increased `2.0%`, adjusted
EBITDA was about `$2.0B`, and the company returned about `$1.1B` to shareholders.
Q1 FY2026 sales increased `10.4%` to `$3.0B`, with blended same-restaurant sales
up `4.7%`, but brand results differed: Olive Garden and LongHorn were positive
while Fine Dining was slightly negative.

Darden captures the full restaurant relationship and most of the sales, but it
also keeps the direct operating burden. Its cleanest test is sales and guest
traffic converted into restaurant margin, operating cash, maintenance capital,
new-unit returns, acquisition integration, lease-adjusted obligations, and
diluted cash per share. This makes Darden the necessary comparison against
franchise-heavy brands, where the parent may report cleaner royalties because
franchisees fund more of the store system.

### Chipotle

Chipotle adds a more mature company-operated comparison. FY2025 comparable
sales fell `1.7%` because transactions fell `2.9%` while average check rose
`1.2%`. In Q2 2026, transactions improved `1.0%` and comparable sales rose
`2.2%`, but restaurant-level margin fell to `25.2%` from `27.4%`.

This is an important earnings-quality result: customer activity improved, but
less store profit remained from each sales dollar. The three-year cash record
also shows the capital choice directly: FY2025 operating cash flow was
`$2.114B`, restaurant capex was `$666.3M`, and repurchases were `$2.426B`.
The investor must test whether buybacks are appropriate while store margins,
Chipotlane returns, labor, food, and opening economics remain unsettled.

## Newly completed role tests

### Booking: travel distribution above the physical network

Booking adds the digital interface above hotels, alternative accommodations,
flights, and rental cars. FY2025 revenue was `$26.917B`, while segment adjusted
EBITDA less capex was `$9.852B`. In Q2 2026, gross bookings were `$50.957B`,
room nights were `325M`, merchant gross bookings were `$36.996B`, merchant mix
reached `73%`, and supply reached about `4.7M` properties.

This is not the same economics as Marriott or Hilton. Booking controls search,
comparison, reservation, payment, and repeat traveler behavior without owning
most of the room supply. The merchant shift can deepen the relationship, but it
also brings supplier remittance, refunds, chargebacks, support, tax, and
working-capital obligations. The decisive test is retained contribution per
booking after marketing and payment cost, not gross booking value.

### Airbnb: host-supplied lodging and trust control

Airbnb adds the non-branded lodging case. FY2025 revenue was about `$12.2B`,
GBV was `$91.3B`, nights and seats booked were `533.0M`, and operating cash
flow was `$4.646B`. Q2 2026 revenue was `$3.6B`, GBV was `$27.2B`, nights and
seats booked were `148.3M`, and adjusted EBITDA was about `$1.3B`.

Airbnb controls search, identity, reviews, payments, cancellation rules,
support, and dispute resolution, while hosts fund the property, cleaning,
maintenance, local permits, and much of the guest service. This is cleaner than
property ownership but not burden-free. The platform must pay for trust and
safety, support, refunds, marketing, payment processing, tax compliance, and
regulatory availability.

The owner-cash test is:

`active host supply × booking conversion × retained take rate -> contribution
after payment, support, trust, marketing, refunds, tax, and insurance -> SBC,
product investment, and diluted cash per share`

GBV and nights booked are not revenue, and revenue is not owner cash. The
company's `$1.592B` of 2025 stock-based compensation and disclosed estimated
withholding-tax liabilities of `$199M` show why the platform's cash and adjusted
measures need an explicit common-owner bridge.

### Hyatt: a different branded-owner network

Hyatt adds a third large hospitality control point. FY2025 ended with `1,528`
hotels and resorts and `372,763` rooms; adjusted EBITDA was `$1.159B`. Q2 2026
RevPAR rose `5.9%`, gross fees were `$324M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$297M`, and
the pipeline reached about `154,000` rooms.

Hyatt supports the asset-light relationship thesis, but the corporate company
still depends on developers and owners financing rooms, maintaining standards,
and remaining financially healthy. Pipeline rooms are future fee capacity, not
current owner cash. The next check is base-fee durability after incentive fees,
loyalty cost, technology, guarantees, acquisitions, debt, and dilution.

### Planet Fitness: participation at an affordable price

Planet Fitness adds a service participation model rather than another product
or travel network. FY2025 revenue grew `12.1%`, adjusted EBITDA reached
`$551.6M`, and the system ended with about `20.8M` members and `2,896` clubs.
By Q2 2026, revenue was `$365.2M`, adjusted EBITDA `$152.8M`, same-club sales
growth had slowed to `1.7%`, and the network reached `2,930` clubs.

About `90%` of clubs are independently owned. Corporate Planet Fitness owns the
brand, membership architecture, franchise system, and support layer; local
franchisees carry rent, labor, equipment, cleaning, and service execution. The
Q2 slowdown makes the correct question clear: can member retention, attendance,
price, and franchisee returns support the royalty stream without continuous
unit growth?

### CAVA: direct restaurant ownership below the franchise layer

CAVA supplies the missing direct-operator comparison. Its FY2025 same-restaurant
sales grew `4.0%` and AUV was about `$2.934M`, but restaurant-level profit margin
slipped to `24.4%` from `25.0%`. Sales and store profit can improve while the
parent still spends heavily on new stores and carries lease, labor, food, and
maintenance claims.

The owner-cash test is:

`guest transactions and spend -> store contribution -> labor, food, rent, and
delivery -> corporate costs -> maintenance and growth capex -> lease and
dilution claims -> cash per share`

This makes CAVA complementary to Domino's and RBI. Domino's and RBI transfer
more store-level burden to franchisees; CAVA retains more operating control and
more direct economics, but also retains more capital and execution risk.

Together, Airbnb, Booking, Hyatt, Planet Fitness, CAVA, and Chipotle complete seven distinct roles:

| Role | Company | Primary activity measure | Owner-cash test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Travel distribution | Booking | Gross bookings, room nights, merchant mix, direct/app use | Retained contribution after marketing, payment, support, refunds, tax, and SBC |
| Host-guest marketplace | Airbnb | GBV, nights/seats, active supply, app use, take rate, repeat booking | Contribution after trust, support, payments, refunds, tax, marketing, SBC, and product investment |
| Branded hospitality | Hyatt | RevPAR, gross fees, rooms, pipeline, loyalty | Fee cash after support, loyalty, technology, acquisitions, debt, and dilution |
| Affordable participation | Planet Fitness | Members, same-club sales, attendance, clubs, retention | Corporate royalty after franchisee health, support, technology, SBC, debt, and buybacks |
| Company-operated restaurant | CAVA | Transactions, same-restaurant sales, AUV, restaurant-level profit, store growth | Parent cash after food, labor, rent, openings, maintenance, corporate costs, leases, SBC, and growth capex |
| Mature company-operated restaurant | Chipotle | Transactions, average check, same-restaurant sales, restaurant margin, Chipotlane share, store openings | Parent cash after food, labor, occupancy, digital complexity, capex, leases, SBC, and buybacks |

The broader pattern is now more precise: participation demand can be captured
by the interface, the brand, the physical operator, or the recurring service.
The company with the cleanest economics is the one that retains the valuable
relationship while requiring another party to fund the heaviest physical
burden—and while keeping that partner healthy.

## Aha Moments

### 1. Experience demand creates two very different winners.

Marriott and Hilton can monetize travel through brands, loyalty, fees, pipeline, and owner demand. Sysco monetizes related social and hospitality activity through food distribution. One owns the customer relationship more directly; the other carries operating infrastructure. Both may benefit from the same human behavior.

### 2. The home is both a shelter category and a social stage.

RH shows home as taste, status, and destination. Williams-Sonoma shows home as cooking, entertaining, gifting, design, and everyday hosting. That means home spending should not be read only through housing turnover. Some spending follows housing cycles, but some follows identity and hosting behavior.

### 3. Jewelry competes with travel but also behaves like a ritual.

Signet explicitly matters because its risk language recognizes experiential spending competition. That makes the comparison with Marriott and Hilton useful. The question is not whether the consumer picks products or experiences. The question is which occasions are socially important enough to keep funding.

### 4. The cleaner consumer story often hides a burden carrier.

Travel loyalty sounds clean at Marriott and Hilton, but hotel owners carry property burden. Social eating sounds like consumer fun, but Sysco carries sourcing, freight, labor, and margin execution. Home identity sounds aspirational, but RH and Williams-Sonoma carry inventory, tariff, and housing-rate exposure.

Company-operated restaurants make the burden visible in the parent company. CAVA
and Darden can retain the full store relationship and upside, but the investor
must charge food, labor, occupancy, openings, maintenance, leases, acquisition
integration, and corporate overhead against restaurant-level profit.

## Thesis Breakers

The role-based consumer thesis weakens if:

- Marriott and Hilton show RevPAR pressure, weaker pipeline conversion, or owner reluctance at the same time.
- Airbnb GBV or nights booked grow while take rate, repeat booking, host quality, contribution, or diluted cash per share weakens.
- Williams-Sonoma comparable brand revenue and operating margin weaken while inventories keep rising.
- RH demand remains weak even after housing or rate conditions improve.
- Wayfair active customers or orders grow while repeat behavior, contribution per order, delivery economics, or diluted cash per share weakens.
- Signet Bridal and Fashion both weaken while AUR growth fails to offset lower traffic or unit demand.
- Sysco local volume turns negative while consumer-facing travel and occasion companies still sound confident.
- CAVA same-restaurant sales or transactions weaken while new-store spending and lease commitments remain elevated, or restaurant-level profit fails to convert into parent cash after capex.
- Chipotle transactions weaken again or restaurant-level margin remains compressed while openings, Chipotlane construction, and repurchases remain high.
- Tariffs, commodity costs, labor, or freight absorb so much gross profit that demand no longer converts into earnings.
