# Replenishment Without Subscription Comparison

Date: 2026-08-11
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Plain-English Claim

One of the archive's recurring patterns is that some of the strongest repeat businesses do not mainly look like memberships or classic subscriptions.

They look like refill, resupply, reorder, and recurring inventory flow.

## Core Question

Which companies build durable repeat economics because customers or institutions have to keep coming back for replenishment rather than because they signed up for an obvious subscription product?

## Why These Four Belong Together

These four businesses operate in different categories, but all depend on the same repeat mechanism:

- Chewy keeps pet-parent demand recurring through Autoship, prescriptions, pharmacy, and care management.
- AdaptHealth keeps recurring home-medical demand moving through equipment access, consumable resupply, referral channels, and payor routing.
- Cencora keeps drug and healthcare-product flow continuous for pharmacies, providers, and health systems.
- Sysco keeps food and kitchen inventory moving for restaurants, hospitality, healthcare, and institutional buyers.

The stronger conclusion is that replenishment can be as powerful as subscription, even when it is not labeled that way.

## Exact Mechanism

### 1. Chewy turns reorder behavior into a durable consumer relationship

Chewy is the clearest consumer-facing proof that replenishment can behave like a subscription without relying mainly on a formal membership fee.

Fiscal `2025` net sales reached `$12.60 billion`, active customers reached `21.3 million`, and record free cash flow reached `$562 million`. In `Q3 2025`, about `84%` of customer sales came from Autoship.

The key point is that the relationship is thick because the customer keeps needing more of the same care stack:

- food
- prescriptions
- wellness items
- account-managed convenience

### 2. AdaptHealth shows that recurring care is often a resupply system

AdaptHealth's packet makes the healthcare version explicit.

As of `2025-12-31`, the company serviced about `4.3 million` patients annually through about `640` locations and completed about `38,500` equipment and supply deliveries per day. The packet says Sleep Health covers `CPAP` and `BiLevel` supply and resupply, while Respiratory Health and Wellness at Home also produce recurring replenishment economics.

That means the customer relationship is not only about installing equipment once.

It is about keeping the patient supplied and functioning over time.

### 3. Cencora shows replenishment at institutional scale

Cencora gives the same pattern in a more industrial form.

Fiscal `2025` revenue reached `$321 billion`, adjusted operating income reached `$4.2 billion`, and adjusted free cash flow reached `$3.0 billion`.

The packet describes a pure institutional-demand business in which pharmacies, providers, and health systems rely on recurring replenishment and dependable drug flow.

The repeat engine is not patient subscription language.

It is the fact that care sites must keep getting product on time.

### 4. Sysco shows that foodservice is also a replenishment system

Sysco broadens the pattern beyond healthcare and pet care.

The packet says restaurants, hospitality venues, healthcare kitchens, education, and other institutional buyers need dependable replenishment rather than one-off transactions. In FY2026 Q2, sales increased `3.0%`, U.S. Foodservice volume increased `0.8%`, and U.S. local volume increased `1.2%`.

That proves the same basic logic:

the buyer may not think of the relationship as a subscription,

but the operation depends on repeat fill, route reliability, and inventory continuity.

## The Stronger Conclusion

The stronger conclusion is that one of the archive's better hidden repeat-economics patterns is replenishment without subscription.

These companies win because:

1. the customer or institution keeps needing refills
2. interruption is costly or inconvenient
3. the supplier becomes embedded in the routine
4. repeat flow becomes more important than one-time acquisition

That is a different economic model from classic media subscription or paid membership, but it can be just as durable.

| Sysco | recurring foodservice flow and route economics | route execution, inflation, customer churn, restaurant and hospitality cyclicality |

The shared pattern is that the economics look cleaner from the outside than the operating burden underneath.

## Why This Pattern Matters

This matters because it improves how repeat behavior is read in the archive.

A lot of recurring economics do not show up as:

- membership fees
- monthly subscriptions
- obvious recurring software revenue

They show up as reorder frequency, deliveries per day, prescription flow, route density, and inventory continuity.

That makes replenishment a hidden relationship model.

## Consumer And Societal Meaning

These packets also show different kinds of routine dependence:

- pets create managed household care routines
- chronic illness creates home-care and equipment routines
- healthcare systems create institutional drug-flow routines
- kitchens and hospitality systems create food-flow routines

The social meaning is that much of the economy runs on keeping recurring needs quietly supplied.

## Industrial And Operating Pressures

The replenishment model is durable, but it is not easy.

- Chewy has to protect retention and keep prescriptions, service, and Autoship quality high.
- AdaptHealth has to manage reimbursement, rollout complexity, and field-service productivity.
- Cencora has to manage customer concentration, policy exposure, and the margin effects of drug mix.
- Sysco has to manage routing, inflation, volume, and operating leverage in a cautious demand environment.

The broader lesson is that replenishment businesses often carry heavy logistics, working-capital, and service burdens beneath the repeat revenue surface.

## Disconfirming Evidence And Limits

This pattern would weaken if:

- reorder frequency declines materially
- substitution or channel-switching becomes easier than expected
- service failures make the repeat relationship less sticky
- institutions become less dependent on outsourced flow management

There is also a limit:

not all replenishment loops have the same resilience. Pet care, chronic care, pharmaceuticals, and foodservice each have different sensitivity to consumer stress, reimbursement pressure, or end-market weakness.
