# Selective Consumer Proof

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Plain Claim

The filings do not show one simple consumer story. They show a selective consumer.

People are still spending when the purchase feels valuable, useful, habitual, expressive, healthy, comfortable, or tied to an occasion. They are pulling back or forcing reinvestment when products feel too expensive, too generic, or too easy to delay.

## Exact Evidence

| Company | Exact packet facts | What it proves |
|---|---|---|

Households pay upfront for trusted low-price access, then repeat the habit. |

Q1 2026 net sales rose `11.1%` to `$3.2B`; comparable sales rose `5.3%`. | Beauty demand is not only replenishment. It is discovery, identity, salons, app behavior, marketplace breadth, loyalty data, and media monetization. |
| `e.l.f. Beauty` | FY2026 net sales grew `25%` to `$1.6365B`; FY2027 Q1 net sales grew `36%` to `$479.4M`; gross margin reached `83%`; adjusted EBITDA was `$168.2M`, or `35%` of net sales. Management called it the `30th consecutive quarter` of net sales growth and raised FY2027 growth guidance to `18%` to `20%`. | Consumers are still spending aggressively in beauty when the product is accessible, trend-aware, culturally active, and distributed through strong retailer plus e-commerce channels. |
| `Crocs` | 2025 revenue was about `$4.04B`; the brand sold in more than `85` countries; only `47.9%` of 2025 revenue came through wholesale, implying majority DTC by revenue. Q2 2026 revenue was `$1.179B`; the Crocs Brand surpassed `$1.0B` of quarterly revenue for the first time; DTC revenue grew `12.0%`. | Comfort and casual self-expression are not niche. ## Causal Chain

1. Households are dealing with price pressure and weaker sentiment.
2. They still spend when the purchase solves a clear problem: cheaper access, daily routine, beauty, comfort, health, travel, gifting, or identity.
3. Companies with strong value propositions or cultural relevance can grow even while weaker middle-market or generic offers struggle.
4. Upstream suppliers and packaging companies feel the pressure through volume, pricing, inventory, plant economics, and customer affordability.

## Stronger Conclusion

The right question is not "is the consumer strong or weak?" The right question is "what does the consumer refuse to give up, and what are they willing to trade down, delay, or abandon?"

Costco proves value access. Ulta and e.l.f. prove discovery and self-presentation. Crocs and Deckers prove comfort and identity. General Mills proves that even staples need renewed value. Graphic Packaging proves that the pressure reaches the supply chain.

## What Would Weaken This

- If value retailers slow while premium identity brands also slow, the selectivity thesis may become broad weakness.
- If beauty growth depends mainly on one acquisition or launch cycle, the cultural-demand read may be too broad.
- If packaging weakness reflects company-specific capital mistakes more than customer demand, it should not be generalized.
- If staples regain volume without value reinvestment, the pressure thesis would need revision.

## Curiosity Questions

- Which purchases are defended because they save money?
- Which purchases are defended because they express identity?
- Which purchases are defended because they are recurring household routines?
- Which upstream suppliers are the earliest warning that the consumer is weakening?
