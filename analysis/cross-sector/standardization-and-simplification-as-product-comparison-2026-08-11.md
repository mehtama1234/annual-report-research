# Standardization And Simplification As Product Comparison

Date: 2026-08-11
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Plain Claim

Some companies are not mainly selling a thing.

They are selling a simpler way to operate.

They get paid because customers want:

- fewer vendors
- fewer workarounds
- fewer approval bottlenecks
- fewer procurement headaches
- fewer safety gaps
- fewer discharge and replenishment failures

The common pattern is standardization.

These businesses win by making a messy workflow more uniform, easier to repeat, and harder to break.

## Why This Comparison Matters

The archive already has pages on:

- institutional overhead and control-plane economics
- continuity and maintenance economics
- hidden operating infrastructure

What it did not yet have as a standalone proof page is a direct comparison of companies that sell simplification itself.

This memo asks:

Which companies get paid because customers would rather standardize the workflow than keep managing complexity alone?

## What The Comparison Clarifies

### 1. Simplification is a real product, not a byproduct.

These companies are not only selling software, uniforms, inventory, or equipment access.

They are selling fewer decisions, fewer exceptions, and fewer breakdowns.

That is why they can become deeply embedded.

### 2. Standardization crosses digital and physical systems.

ServiceNow standardizes digital workflow.

Palo Alto standardizes digital defense.

Cintas standardizes workplace readiness.

Grainger and Ferguson standardize procurement and field execution.

AdaptHealth standardizes a messy home-care continuation layer.

This matters because it shows the same economic logic repeating far beyond software.

### 3. Fragmentation creates the demand for simplification.

Too many internal processes help ServiceNow.

Too many security tools help Palo Alto.

Too many vendors and SKUs help Grainger.

Too many local contractor variables help Ferguson.

Too many post-discharge steps help AdaptHealth.

The more fragmented the underlying system, the more valuable the standardization layer can become.

### 4. Standardization can improve economics even when the frontline work stays messy.

The customer workflow gets cleaner even if the operator still carries real burden:

- route density at Cintas
- inventory and branch execution at Grainger and Ferguson
- reimbursement and field complexity at AdaptHealth
- platform integration at ServiceNow and Palo Alto

That is why simplification can be valuable without implying the business itself is easy.

### 5. Standardization often becomes a moat because nobody wants to reopen the mess.

Once a company becomes the default workflow or default supply channel, switching away means reintroducing complexity.

That is often a stronger source of stickiness than branding alone.

## Stronger Conclusion

The stronger conclusion is that standardization is one of the archive’s underappreciated business models.

Customers repeatedly pay for a cleaner operating pattern when the alternative is:

- too many handoffs
- too many suppliers
- too many exceptions
- too many fragmented tools

The key cross-company insight is:

When complexity becomes painful enough, the company that makes the workflow more uniform can become one of the most durable toll collectors in the system.

## Aha Moments

### 1. ServiceNow and Grainger are closer than taxonomy suggests.

One standardizes internal work.

The other standardizes recurring procurement.

Both win because customers do not want to keep re-solving the same operational mess.

### 2. Cintas belongs in the same conversation as enterprise software more often than people think.

Its real product is not apparel. It is standardized workplace readiness.

### 3. AdaptHealth shows that healthcare growth often depends less on breakthrough science than on who can make the handoff chain simpler.

### 4. Ferguson proves that standardization can still be local and physical.

Not every simplification layer is a central software dashboard. Sometimes it is branch density, fleet, and product availability.

## Thesis Breakers

This standardization-and-simplification thesis weakens if:

- customers decide the simplification layer is not worth the added cost
- switching away becomes easier than the current moat implies
- fragmented alternatives improve enough that the standardizer loses differentiation
- the operator’s own complexity grows faster than the customer value it creates
- margin quality weakens because standardization demand does not translate into pricing power or retention
