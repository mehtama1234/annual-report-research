# Abbott Laboratories — installed diagnostics, chronic-care devices, and portfolio breadth

Related first-principles explanation: [Procedure MedTech, clinical adoption, and owner cash](../first-principles/procedure-medtech-clinical-adoption-and-owner-cash.md).

Date baseline: 2026-09-13

## The investment question

Abbott Laboratories operates across Medical Devices, Diagnostics, Nutrition,
and Established Pharmaceuticals. It supplies products used repeatedly by
providers, laboratories, patients, and institutions. Its control point is the
installed diagnostic, monitoring, and treatment workflow rather than a single
hospital or one drug patent.

The investment question is whether this breadth produces durable cash per share
after product development, manufacturing and quality requirements, laboratory
and device adoption, reimbursement, working capital, acquisitions, litigation,
stock compensation, and replacement capital.

## Q2 2026 operating checkpoint

Q2 reported sales were `$12.593B`, up `13.0%`, but comparable sales growth was
`4.8%`. Medical Devices sales were `$5.853B`, up `7.9%` excluding currency,
while Diagnostics sales were `$3.092B`; the latter includes Exact Sciences
from its March 23 acquisition date. Abbott reported GAAP diluted EPS of `$0.53`
and adjusted diluted EPS of `$1.31`. That unusually wide gap makes the
acquisition and special-item bridge central to the current analysis rather than
a footnote.

The first-half cash statement was strong before acquisition spending: operating
cash was `$3.803B`, property and equipment purchases were `$896M`, and
dividends and repurchases were `$2.200B` and `$1.225B`. But Abbott spent
`$19.962B` on business acquisitions and technologies, issued `$19.827B` of
long-term debt, and ended June with `$5.104B` of cash. The post-acquisition
owner-cash position is therefore very different from the reported operating
cash-flow number.

Exact Sciences contributed `$1.0B` of sales in the first six months, while its
earnings contribution was not material to Abbott’s consolidated net earnings.
Goodwill increased by `$11.4B` and gross amortizable intangible assets by
`$12.3B`; Abbott estimates annual intangible amortization of `$2.6B` in 2026.
These are not evidence that the deal will fail, but they establish a measurable
return hurdle: cancer-diagnostics growth must eventually cover the purchase
price, new debt, integration, research, commercial support, and the economic
cost of acquired amortization.

The current evidence supports strong underlying Medical Devices execution, but
it weakens any claim that Abbott’s 2026 growth is already cleanly organic or
that adjusted EPS is a reliable owner-cash measure. The next filings must show
Exact Sciences revenue, operating income, test volumes, reimbursement, cash
interest, integration costs, and whether diagnostic growth remains after the
acquisition comparison fades.

Abbott can look more stable than a concentrated medtech or pharmaceutical
company, but diversification can also hide different lifecycles and returns
inside one adjusted-EPS number.

## What Abbott actually controls

The operating chain is:

`clinical or nutritional need -> product adoption -> installed workflow or recurring use -> reimbursement/procurement -> replenishment and cash`

Abbott controls product development, manufacturing, sales channels, clinical
evidence, installed diagnostic and monitoring systems, and recurring product
relationships. It does not control hospital budgets, payer decisions,
physician choice, patient adherence, diagnostic utilization, regulatory
approval, or the outcome of a clinical intervention.

In 2025, Medical Devices generated $19.290B of sales, Diagnostics $9.888B,
Nutrition $8.820B, and Established Pharmaceuticals $6.330B. The portfolio
therefore combines high-growth device categories, diagnostics and testing,
nutrition, and more mature pharmaceutical products. Each has a different
reinvestment need and different exposure to competition and price pressure.

## Three-year reported bridge

Amounts are $ billions. Abbott's reported free-cash-flow screen is operating
cash flow less PP&E spending. The final column subtracts business acquisitions
as well, because acquisitions are a recurring way the portfolio changes.

| Fiscal year | Sales | Net earnings | Operating cash flow | PP&E capex | Reported FCF | Acquisitions | Residual after acquisitions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 40.109 | 5.723 | 7.261 | 2.202 | 5.059 | 0.877 | 4.182 |
| 2024 | 41.952 | 13.402 | 8.558 | 2.207 | 6.351 | 0.000 | 6.351 |
| 2025 | 44.328 | 6.524 | 9.566 | 2.171 | 7.395 | 0.105 | 7.290 |

The reported cash picture is strong, but 2024 net earnings were affected by a
large tax-related comparison item, so that year's earnings should not be used
as a normal profit denominator. In 2025, operating cash increased to $9.566B,
while capital spending remained approximately $2.2B. Abbott says capital
spending supports manufacturing and R&D facilities, information technology,
and laboratory instruments placed with customers.

Working capital still matters. In 2025, trade receivables used $652M of cash,
prepaid and other assets used $1.295B, while inventories provided $195M and
trade payables and other liabilities provided $954M. The net cash result was
legitimate, but the liability and asset movements should be tested over time
before treating the full increase as recurring margin improvement.

## Where the economics sit

### Medical Devices: procedures and chronic-care continuity

Medical Devices includes cardiovascular, diabetes, neuromodulation, and other
products whose value can recur through procedures, sensors, replacement parts,
and patient monitoring. Abbott benefits when the product becomes part of a
clinical protocol or a patient's ongoing disease-management routine.

The burden is evidence, regulatory approval, quality, sales support, hospital
capital budgets, reimbursement, and competition. A growing installed base is
useful only if utilization, replenishment, service, and margin remain attractive
after the cost of maintaining clinical trust.

### Diagnostics: installed systems plus recurring tests

Diagnostics can create a two-part relationship: the instrument or system enters
the laboratory, then tests and consumables repeat. The economics depend on
requisition volume, reimbursement, menu breadth, uptime, quality, and the cost
of placing and supporting systems.

The 2026 Exact Sciences acquisition expands Abbott's diagnostic footprint but
also makes current growth less purely organic. The investor should separate
legacy Core Laboratory and point-of-care growth from acquired revenue,
integration cost, and any required research or commercial investment.

### Nutrition and Established Pharmaceuticals: breadth with different quality

Nutrition adds institutional and household demand, but it can face category
competition, input cost, retailer or channel pressure, and product-safety
requirements. Established Pharmaceuticals provide geographic and access
breadth, but they are more exposed to generic competition, pricing limits, and
country-specific regulation than a differentiated device platform.

Portfolio breadth is valuable when it shares distribution, evidence, quality,
and customer relationships. It is less valuable when mature businesses consume
capital or management attention without supporting attractive incremental
returns.

## Earnings quality and forensic questions

### 1. Adjusted EPS must be reconciled to GAAP and cash

Abbott reports adjusted earnings to exclude specified items, including
acquisition-related amortization and other significant effects. Some exclusions
help compare operating periods; others may represent recurring costs of owning a
portfolio built through acquisitions.

Track GAAP EPS, adjusted EPS, amortization, restructuring, litigation,
integration, tax items, stock compensation, weighted-average shares, and cash
after acquisitions. If adjusted earnings grow while acquisition-adjusted cash
per share does not, the portfolio's economic return is weaker than the headline.

### 2. Installed instruments are not automatically high-return assets

Capital spending includes laboratory instruments placed with customers. Those
placements may generate recurring tests and strengthen retention, but they also
tie up capital and can require service, replacement, and pricing concessions.

The next filings should show instrument placements, utilization, recurring
consumables, service economics, customer concentration, and return on deployed
equipment where available. Revenue growth without better utilization or cash
return can indicate that Abbott is buying access rather than earning a moat.

### 3. Exact Sciences changes the growth denominator

The acquired diagnostics business can add testing volume and strategic breadth.
It can also add integration costs, goodwill, intangible amortization, and a
different reimbursement profile. Current organic growth should be shown both
including and excluding acquired operations, with the acquired business's cash
return tracked separately.

The Q2 2026 filing makes the denominator explicit. Exact Sciences added `$1.0B`
of sales in the first six months, but its earnings contribution was not material
to consolidated net earnings. Abbott paid approximately `$20.6B`, funded mainly
with `$20.0B` of new long-term debt, and recorded `$11.4B` of goodwill and
`$12.3B` of additional gross amortizable intangibles. The acquisition may be
strategically correct, but it cannot be treated as an immediate contribution to
normalized cash per share.

### 4. Quality and litigation are part of device economics

Medical devices and diagnostics require quality systems, clinical evidence,
recalls, remediation, regulatory submissions, and legal defense. A product
event may first appear as a reserve or special charge, but the economic loss can
also include lost physician trust, delayed launches, lower utilization, and
additional manufacturing or monitoring cost.

Do not remove recurring quality and compliance spending from normalized owner
cash merely because it is labeled unusual in one year.

### 5. Working capital and foreign cash affect conversion

Abbott holds a substantial portion of cash outside the United States and has
global receivables, inventory, and supply chains. Track receivable days,
inventory turns, customer allowances, supplier terms, currency effects, tax
payments, and cash location. A strong consolidated cash number is less useful if
working capital is funded by suppliers or cash cannot be deployed where the
owner needs it without cost.

### 6. Debt, pensions, and buybacks compete with innovation

Abbott ended 2025 with an investment-grade credit profile and expects operating
cash to exceed capital expenditures and dividends. It also funded $309M to
defined-benefit pension plans in 2025, paid $4.1B of dividends, and had debt
principal maturities including $3.0B in 2026 and $7.4B in 2031 and thereafter.

The balance sheet is resilient, but the investor should still test whether
dividend growth, debt repayment, acquisitions, and buybacks leave enough cash
for product renewal, quality, evidence, and manufacturing capacity.

## Lyn Alden lens

Abbott benefits from durable healthcare needs: chronic disease monitoring,
cardiovascular intervention, diagnostics, nutrition, and access to medicines.
The cycle appears through hospital budgets, research and development funding,
consumer affordability, government reimbursement, currency, and cost of
capital.

The strongest version of the thesis is a portfolio where installed devices and
diagnostics generate recurring use while nutrition and pharmaceuticals provide
cash diversity. The weaker version is a diversified set of businesses that
requires continuous product replacement, acquisitions, and regulatory spending
to maintain reported growth.

## Damodaran lens and valuation

The narrative is “a diversified healthcare platform with recurring device,
diagnostic, nutrition, and pharmaceutical demand.” Translate it into:

`product adoption -> utilization and replenishment -> reimbursement and price -> quality-adjusted margin -> working capital -> sustaining and growth capex -> acquisition and litigation claims -> common cash per share`

At the September 11, 2026 market snapshot, Abbott's equity value was
approximately $177.7B. A normalized owner-cash screen is:

| Normalized annual owner cash | Multiple | Implied equity value |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $5.5B bear case | 18x | $99B |
| $7.5B base case | 24x | $180B |
| $9.5B strong case | 28x | $266B |

These are scenario outputs, not price targets. The base case requires Medical
Devices and Diagnostics to sustain organic growth, Exact Sciences to integrate
well, recurring consumables to support the installed base, mature businesses to
remain cash-generative, and quality and litigation costs to stay manageable.

The current market value therefore assumes that portfolio breadth improves the
quality of earnings rather than merely increasing the number of businesses in
the consolidated report.

## Incentives and thesis breakers

Management incentives emphasize sales, adjusted EPS, operating margin, cash
flow, innovation, growth, and capital deployment. Those measures can reward
real improvements, but they can also favor acquisitions, adjusted exclusions,
instrument placements, or buybacks before product-level returns are proven.

The thesis weakens if:

- device or diagnostics growth slows after acquisition effects normalize;
- instrument placements rise without recurring test, consumable, or service
  revenue;
- reimbursement or hospital-budget pressure reduces utilization and pricing;
- Exact Sciences integration adds cost without acceptable cash returns;
- quality, recall, litigation, or regulatory costs recur and are repeatedly
  excluded from adjusted results;
- working-capital support reverses while adjusted EPS remains strong; or
- dividends and buybacks crowd out product renewal, capacity, or debt resilience.

## Evidence grades and next filing questions

- **Filed fact:** 2025 sales, segment sales, GAAP net earnings, operating cash
  flow, PP&E spending, acquisitions, working-capital movements, pensions,
  dividends, and debt obligations are anchored to the 2025 Form 10-K.
- **Current operating evidence:** Q1 and Q2 2026 sales, organic growth, segment
  performance, and Exact Sciences integration claims come from the preserved
  packet and quarterly filings; they require later cash confirmation.
- **Inference:** Abbott controls recurring provider and patient workflow more
  directly than a distributor, but less completely than a single high-switching-
  cost procedure platform.
- **Unresolved:** installed-base utilization, organic growth after acquisition,
  device and diagnostics reimbursement, quality cost, acquisition returns,
  working-capital normalization, and common cash per share after compensation.

The next filings should answer: Did Medical Devices and Diagnostics grow
organically? Did recurring tests, sensors, and consumables grow with the
installed base? Did Exact Sciences earn above its funding cost? Did cash per
share rise after capex, acquisitions, pensions, dividends, litigation, and
stock compensation? Did mature pharmaceuticals and nutrition fund rather than
obscure the higher-growth portfolio?

The next quarter should also reconcile the `$0.53` GAAP EPS and `$1.31` adjusted
EPS spread, including acquisition amortization, integration, legal reserves,
tax effects, and any other excluded items. A repeated spread of that size would
mean the adjusted measure is describing a portfolio with materially different
owner economics from the GAAP measure.
