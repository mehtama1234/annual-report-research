# ABM Industries — outsourced facility operations, labor burden, and cash quality

Date baseline: 2026-09-13  
Primary annual filing: [ABM FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/771497/000077149725000031/abm-20251031.htm)  
Annual report: [ABM FY2025 annual report](https://materials.proxyvote.com/Approved/000957/20260126/10KWP_624591/INDEX.HTML?page=1)  
Companion packet: [ABM company packet](/home/mehtama1/git-repo/annual-report-research/extracted/industrial-goods/business-services/abm-industries-inc/company-packet.md)

## Bottom line

ABM provides facility services, engineering, transportation support, parking,
aviation services, janitorial work, and technical operations. Customers hire
ABM to keep airports, warehouses, campuses, commercial buildings,
manufacturing sites, semiconductor facilities, and other complex environments
operating.

FY2025 revenue was approximately $8.7B, new sales bookings were $1.9B, net
income was $162.4M, operating cash flow was $234.4M, capital expenditures were
$79.3M, and cash acquisitions were $36.7M. The simple OCF-less-capex-and-
acquisitions screen was $118.4M. ABM also paid $59.0M to settle contingent
consideration, which makes the economic acquisition burden larger than the
investing-cash line alone.

Current judgment: ABM has recurring customer relationships and useful exposure
to technical facilities, battery storage, airports, and data-center-adjacent
sites. But it is fundamentally a labor and execution business. The company
must recruit, retain, schedule, train, insure, and supervise a large workforce,
then collect from customers while absorbing wage, claims, and service-mix
pressure. Its bookings and technical mix matter only if they raise cash per
employee after those obligations.

## What ABM controls

`facility operating need -> service contract -> labor and technical execution -> site uptime -> renewal, expansion, and new-site award`

ABM controls site staffing, process management, engineering support, service
quality, customer coordination, and the ability to combine routine and
technical services. It does not control customer occupancy, airport traffic,
facility budgets, wage markets, weather, labor availability, or the capital
spending decisions of semiconductor and data-center customers.

The control point is local execution at scale. A customer can switch providers,
but a change can disrupt staffing, safety, airport operations, clean rooms,
maintenance routines, and site knowledge. That creates retention value without
creating a software-like margin structure.

## Recurring contracts and bookings

ABM closed FY2025 with record revenue of about $8.7B and new sales bookings of
$1.9B. FY2026 Q1 revenue was $2.2B with 5.5% organic growth, and Q2 revenue was
$2.3B with 6.1% organic growth plus 2.3% acquisition growth. Q2 adjusted EBITDA
was $131.7M, and first-half bookings were $1.2B.

Bookings are useful only when they become staffed, profitable, collected
contracts. The conversion chain requires:

1. customer award and defined scope;
2. successful hiring or transfer of the workforce;
3. wage and benefit rates that the contract can recover;
4. service quality, safety, and uptime during delivery;
5. customer acceptance and timely payment; and
6. renewal or expansion at an acceptable return.

Technical Solutions can have better growth and margin potential than routine
facility services, but Q1 2026 showed weather delays and less favorable service
mix affecting results. The next filings should separate recurring site service,
project work, transportation, and technical operations.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. Labor is the main operating liability

ABM’s business requires a large distributed workforce. Wages, benefits,
overtime, recruitment, training, absenteeism, turnover, workers’ compensation,
and management supervision are necessary costs. A contract may appear recurring
while its economics deteriorate when wage increases exceed pricing or when
vacancies force overtime and temporary labor.

Track revenue per employee, labor hours per site, wage recovery, turnover,
open positions, overtime, workers’ compensation claims, and operating margin by
service line. Adjusted EBITDA is incomplete if it improves by deferring hiring
or reducing service quality.

### 2. Receivables and unbilled work affect cash

Trade receivables and costs incurred in excess of amounts billed used $109.8M
of cash in FY2025. This can reflect ordinary contract timing, but it creates a
liquidity requirement and exposes ABM to customer disputes, retainage, and
collection risk.

The next filing should show receivable aging, unbilled costs, customer
concentration, contract assets, collection days, bad-debt provision, and cash
conversion by segment. Revenue growth without billing and collection is weaker
than revenue followed by cash.

### 3. Acquisition economics include contingent consideration

ABM paid $36.7M for businesses and equity securities in FY2025 and $114.3M in
2024. It also paid $59.0M to settle contingent consideration in 2025. That
payment is acquisition-related even though it appears in financing cash flow.

The correct acquisition test includes purchase price, earnouts, integration,
customer retention, acquired labor, site margins, working capital, debt, and
cash generated after the deal. Acquisitions should not be called optional if
they are needed to maintain growth or technical capability.

### 4. Adjusted profit can hide service-mix change

FY2025 net income was $162.4M, while adjusted measures exclude selected items.
The gap may be reasonable, but the recurring cost of contract transitions,
restructuring, claims, acquisition integration, and technical capability
investment must remain in a long-term owner-cash model.

Service mix also matters. Higher-value technical work may improve margins, but
routine facilities work remains part of the customer relationship and carries
the wage and staffing burden. A mix shift should be tested with gross profit,
labor cost, renewal, and cash per site.

### 5. Low capex does not mean low reinvestment

ABM’s visible capex was $79.3M in FY2025, but the company also invests through
labor recruiting, training, vehicles, site equipment, technology, safety,
insurance, and acquisitions. Some operating costs preserve the service
franchise rather than create a balance-sheet asset.

The owner-cash bridge must therefore charge the recurring people and operating
systems required to win and retain contracts, not only PP&E.

## Alden macro and durability lens

ABM is exposed to airport traffic, manufacturing and distribution, education,
commercial occupancy, semiconductor production, battery storage, data-center
services, wages, inflation, and customer outsourcing decisions. Facility
continuity can remain important in a downturn, but customers can rebid,
combine vendors, reduce scope, or delay discretionary projects.

Liquidity depends on customer collections, labor flexibility, insurance claims,
debt and leases, acquisition funding, and the ability to absorb contract
transitions. Technical-facility demand can be durable, but the balance sheet
does not receive the same protection as a regulated utility or a software
contract with high gross margin.

## Damodaran narrative-to-numbers lens

The narrative is “ABM is a recurring facility-operations platform gaining
technical exposure as buildings and industrial sites become more complex.” The
model must specify:

1. recurring site service versus project revenue;
2. technical, aviation, manufacturing, education, and business mix;
3. bookings conversion and renewal;
4. wage, benefit, turnover, and productivity assumptions;
5. receivables and unbilled-cost requirements;
6. capex, vehicles, technology, safety, and insurance needs;
7. acquisition and contingent-consideration returns; and
8. normalized cash per employee and per site.

ABM is a mature services company in a mix-upgrade and technical-expansion
lifecycle. The valuation deserves a higher multiple only if technical work and
recurring site relationships improve cash returns without making the labor
burden more volatile.

The September 11, 2026 market snapshot showed equity value of approximately
$2.931B. The screen below uses OCF less capex and acquisitions, while the bear

The current value is above the base screen. It requires sustained organic

cash conversion after claims, leases, acquisitions, and contingent payments.

## Incentives, thesis breakers, and next filings

Management has an incentive to emphasize bookings, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted
EPS, organic growth, and technical-services opportunity. These metrics are
useful, but the shareholder test is cash per employee and per site after labor,
claims, equipment, working capital, acquisitions, and debt.

The thesis weakens if:

- bookings grow while hiring, wage recovery, or contract margins deteriorate;
- revenue rises but receivables and unbilled costs grow faster;
- technical projects repeatedly miss schedules or require costly transitions;
- WGNSTAR or other acquisitions add sales without acquired cash returns;
- adjusted EBITDA improves through exclusions that recur every year;
- workers’ compensation, insurance, safety, or legal claims rise; or
- customer rebids and outsourcing reductions weaken renewal and pricing.

The next filings should show labor productivity, turnover, wage recovery,
contract renewal, bookings conversion, service mix, receivable aging, unbilled
costs, insurance claims, capex, vehicle and lease commitments, acquisition
cohorts, contingent payments, debt, and shareholder returns.

## Evidence grade

- Control point: **B+** — site execution, service quality, and technical labor
  create switching friction, but contracts can be rebid.
- Operating economics: **B-** — recurring demand is real, while labor and mix
  create low-margin execution risk.
- Cash conversion: **B** — OCF is positive, but receivables, acquisitions,
  contingent consideration, and people investment matter.
- Balance-sheet resilience: **B** — liquidity is adequate, but claims, leases,
  debt, and customer timing remain material.
- Capital allocation: **B-** — technical acquisitions may improve mix, but
  returns and contingent payments need proof.
- Valuation: **B-** — current value requires technical growth and better cash
  per employee than the historical base demonstrates.
