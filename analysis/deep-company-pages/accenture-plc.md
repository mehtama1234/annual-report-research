# Accenture — enterprise implementation, AI services, and cash per employee

Date baseline: 2026-09-13  
Primary annual filing: [Accenture FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737325000217/acn-20250831.htm)  
Annual report: [Accenture FY2025 Annual Report](https://investor.accenture.com/~/media/Files/A/accenture-v4/investors/home/annual-report-2025.pdf)

## Bottom line

Accenture helps large organizations design, implement, operate, and change
technology and business processes. It controls client access, implementation
know-how, trained labor, industry methods, partner relationships, and the
ability to carry a transformation program from strategy into operation. It
does not own most of the software, cloud infrastructure, or customer assets
that create the underlying technology demand.

FY2025 revenue was $69.67B, new bookings were $80.6B, generative-AI bookings
were $5.9B, GAAP operating margin was 14.7%, adjusted operating margin was
15.6%, GAAP diluted EPS was $12.15, and free cash flow was approximately
$10.9B. Operating cash flow was $11.474B, capital expenditures were $600M,
and purchases of businesses and investments were $1.471B.

The cash profile is strong, but the recurring-quality claim needs care. In
FY2025, Accenture recorded $2.094B of stock-based compensation, spent $3.3B
on acquisitions, research and development, and learning and development, and
recognized $615M of business-optimization costs. Receivables and contract
assets used $1.021B of cash, while deferred revenue provided $707M.

Current judgment: Accenture is a high-quality enterprise-services intermediary
with a large trained workforce and strong cash conversion. The main risk is
not a simple accounting trick. It is that “AI bookings,” acquisitions, adjusted
margin, utilization, and workforce investment can make implementation demand
look more recurring and scalable than it is. The valuation must charge labor,
training, retention, acquisitions, stock dilution, and client budget cycles.

## What Accenture controls

`enterprise problem -> advisory and design -> implementation -> managed operation -> renewal, expansion, and new transformation work`

Accenture controls the client relationship, project-management process,
technical talent, industry expertise, partner ecosystem, delivery centers,
and change-management capability. It does not control client IT budgets,
vendor product strategy, cloud pricing, employee retention, or whether a
client builds capability internally.

The control point is often the trusted implementation layer. A client may be
able to buy software from several vendors, but it still needs people and
processes to redesign workflows, migrate data, manage risk, train employees,
and operate the result. That creates switching cost, but the service remains
labor-intensive and competitive.

Accenture’s own free-cash-flow measure was $10.9B in FY2025 because it
subtracts capex but not acquisitions. The broader screen subtracts business
purchases as a possible recurring capability investment. Neither figure is
free of labor, training, stock compensation, working-capital, or shareholder
claims.

The 2024 residual was depressed by $6.583B of acquisitions. That year should
not be used as a normal cash base without separately measuring the acquired
businesses’ subsequent revenue, margin, employee retention, and cash return.

## AI bookings and implementation economics

Generative-AI bookings of $5.9B in FY2025 and advanced-AI bookings of $2.2B in
FY2026 Q1 show that clients are paying for assessment, design, integration,
data work, model deployment, governance, and workflow change. Bookings are not
the same as revenue, and revenue is not the same as durable recurring cash.

The AI chain must be proven in order:

1. a client identifies a business use case and funds the program;
2. Accenture wins a defined project or managed-service contract;
3. trained people and technology deliver the implementation;
4. the client accepts the work and pays;
5. the system enters recurring operation or creates expansion work; and
6. Accenture earns a return after training, attrition, delivery risk, and
   pricing pressure.

The next filings should separate advanced-AI bookings from broader consulting,
implementation, and managed services; show conversion to revenue; identify
renewal or managed-service attachment; and report margin after the people and
technology required to deliver the work.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. Bookings are a leading indicator, not earnings

FY2025 bookings of $80.6B exceeded revenue, and book-to-bill was about 1.2.
This supports future work, but the company must still staff, price, execute,
and collect it. Large bookings can contain projects with different duration,
margin, cancellation, and client-concentration risk.

Track bookings by consulting versus managed services, geography, industry,
contract length, backlog conversion, cancellations, revenue recognition, and
gross margin. A high book-to-bill with falling utilization or pricing is weaker
than one with stable delivery economics.

### 2. Stock compensation is an employee cost and dilution claim

FY2025 stock-based compensation was approximately $2.094B. It is noncash in
the current period, but it compensates employees and can require future share
issuance or repurchase. A free-cash-flow-to-net-income ratio of 1.4 is not
entirely unrestricted owner cash when the company must replace employee equity
and buy back shares to limit dilution.

The next proxy and 10-K should connect compensation expense, shares issued,
shares repurchased, diluted shares, retention, and employee productivity. The
right per-share test is cash after the economic cost of maintaining the
workforce and share count.

### 3. Acquisitions are both growth and labor replacement

Accenture spent $6.583B on businesses and investments in FY2024 and $1.471B
in FY2025. It also says acquisitions are an engine for organic growth because
they add skills, capabilities, and industry expertise. This can be rational,
but it means “organic” growth may depend on a workforce and client base first
acquired by the company.

Test acquired revenue, acquired employees, attrition, utilization, cross-sell,
integration costs, goodwill, intangible amortization, and cash return on each
cohort. A capability acquisition is valuable only if it improves client
retention, pricing, delivery capacity, or per-share cash after the purchase
price.

### 4. Business optimization costs may recur

FY2025 business-optimization costs were $615M, including employee severance
and asset impairments. Such costs can be appropriate during a workforce or
portfolio reset, but a repeated “one-time” cost is part of the operating model.
Accenture also invested heavily in learning and development and AI skills.

The owner-cash bridge should retain recurring retraining, severance, bench,
recruiting, and facility costs needed to keep the workforce aligned with
technology demand.

### 5. Working capital reflects client billing and delivery timing

Receivables and contract assets used $1.021B of cash in FY2025, while deferred
revenue provided $707M. Those movements can be normal as projects scale. They
also show why revenue growth does not automatically become cash in the same
period.

Track days sales outstanding, unbilled receivables, contract assets, deferred
revenue, milestone billing, client concentration, and collection disputes. A
client paying in advance supports liquidity, but it also creates future
delivery obligations.

## Alden macro and durability lens

Accenture is exposed to enterprise technology budgets, consulting demand,
interest rates, wage inflation, currency, outsourcing decisions, and the pace
at which clients adopt AI. In a difficult economy, clients may defer strategy
work but retain mandatory implementation, cybersecurity, cloud operations, or
cost-reduction programs.

Liquidity is strong when the company can flex hiring, utilization, acquisitions,
and shareholder returns without damaging delivery quality. The key macro risk
is not only a recession; it is a change in the amount of human labor required
per unit of client output. AI could increase demand for implementation work,
but it could also reduce billable hours or pressure pricing.

## Damodaran narrative-to-numbers lens

The narrative is “Accenture is the trusted enterprise transformation and AI
implementation layer.” The model must specify:

1. consulting versus managed-services revenue and margin;
2. bookings conversion and cancellation;
3. billable utilization, pricing, wage cost, and employee attrition;
4. AI-related revenue, renewal, and delivery cost;
5. acquisitions, acquired growth, goodwill, and integration cost;
6. training, R&D, business optimization, and stock compensation;
7. receivables, contract assets, deferred revenue, and cash conversion; and
8. a terminal growth rate consistent with enterprise budgets and labor
   productivity.

Accenture is a mature professional-services platform in a technology-
transition lifecycle. Its valuation deserves a premium for trust, breadth,
and delivery scale only if AI increases durable cash per employee rather than
merely increasing bookings and required training.

## Incentives, thesis breakers, and next filings

Management has an incentive to emphasize bookings, AI bookings, adjusted
margin, free-cash-flow conversion, and the scale of the trained AI workforce.
Those measures are informative, but the shareholder test is cash per diluted
share after workforce replacement, acquisitions, and client delivery costs.

The thesis weakens if:

- AI bookings rise but conversion to revenue, margin, or managed services does not;
- utilization and pricing fall as AI tools reduce billable labor requirements;
- acquisitions supply most growth while per-share cash earnings stagnate;
- stock compensation and repurchases rise together without productivity gains;
- receivables and contract assets grow faster than revenue or collections slow;
- business-optimization and retraining costs recur at elevated levels; or
- clients build internal AI and implementation capabilities faster than Accenture
  can maintain its trusted delivery role.

The next filings should disclose bookings conversion, AI revenue and margin,
utilization, attrition, wage inflation, acquired versus organic growth,
contract-asset aging, deferred revenue, stock compensation, diluted shares,
training spend, acquisitions, and cash returned after those claims.

## Evidence grade

- Control point: **B+** — client trust, delivery scale, and trained expertise
  are valuable, but software vendors and internal teams remain alternatives.
- Operating economics: **B+** — margins and cash are strong, while labor and
  pricing determine whether AI improves productivity or reduces billable work.
- Cash conversion: **B+** — FY2025 cash was strong, but acquisitions, working
  capital, stock compensation, and optimization costs require normalization.
- Balance-sheet resilience: **A-** — cash and liquidity are strong, while
  acquisition funding and employee claims remain relevant.
- Capital allocation: **B** — acquisitions and shareholder returns are large;
  capability purchases require return evidence.
- Valuation: **B** — current value requires the AI transition to raise durable
  cash per employee and per diluted share.
