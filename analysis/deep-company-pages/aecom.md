# AECOM — design and program-management control, backlog quality, and project cash conversion

Date baseline: 2026-09-14  
Primary evidence: [AECOM company packet](../../extracted/industrial-goods/engineering-construction/aecom/company-packet.md), [AECOM FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/868857/000086885726000008/aecom2025_arx10k.pdf), [AECOM Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/868857/000086885726000014/acm-20260403.htm), and [AECOM source ledger](../../extracted/industrial-goods/engineering-construction/aecom/source-ledger.md)

## Investment question

AECOM provides infrastructure consulting, design, advisory, engineering, and
program-management services. It earns fees before and during construction of
transportation, water, facilities, defense, energy, and data-center projects.
It generally does not own the finished infrastructure.

The investment question is whether AECOM's design relationships, technical
standards, win rate, and program-management position produce durable owner cash
after pass-through costs, labor, project claims, receivables, contract assets,
international collection problems, acquisitions, debt, buybacks, and
dividends.

## What AECOM controls

The operating sequence is:

`public or private capital authorization -> advisory, planning, design, permitting, and engineering -> procurement and program management -> construction coordination -> acceptance and operation -> billing and collection -> follow-on program work`

AECOM controls technical expertise, design standards, permitting knowledge,
client relationships, program coordination, and access to large infrastructure
pursuits. Customers control the capital budget, project approval, funding,
scope, and payment. Contractors and subcontractors perform much of the
physical work, while AECOM carries professional-liability, estimate, claim,
collection, and labor risk.

## Definitions

- **Gross revenue** includes amounts billed under project arrangements,
  including some reimbursable and pass-through costs. **Adjusted net service
  revenue (NSR)** is intended to show the fee-bearing economic work after
  removing specified pass-through costs.
- **Backlog** is expected future revenue from awarded work. It is not profit,
  cash, or an unconditional customer commitment.
- **Book-to-burn** compares awards and bookings with revenue burn. A result over
  `1.0x` increases visibility, but it does not prove adequate margin or cash
  collection.
- **Contract assets** represent work recognized before the right to bill is
  unconditional. They are a cash claim that still depends on approvals and
  collection.
- **Recurring-capital residual** here means operating cash flow less payments
  for capital expenditures. Acquisitions, joint-venture investments, claims,
  working capital, debt, buybacks, and dividends remain separate burdens.

| FY2025 | Revenue `$16.140B`; adjusted NSR `$7.573B`; backlog about `$24.8B` | Operating cash `$821.6M`; capex `$136.7M`; residual about `$684.9M`; acquisitions `$212.5M`; stock compensation `$61.4M` |
| Q1 FY2026 | Revenue `$3.831B`; adjusted NSR `$1.851B`; backlog `$25.962B`; book-to-burn `1.5x` | Adjusted operating margin and EPS improved despite a U.S. federal shutdown of `43` days |
| Q2 FY2026 | Revenue `$3.801B`; adjusted NSR `$1.948B`; backlog `$26.204B` | Adjusted operating margin `16.5%`; free cash flow was negative `$27M` because Middle East collection and claim timing delayed cash |

AECOM's record backlog and margin expansion are meaningful. The quality test
is whether adjusted NSR, not just gross revenue, grows with acceptable
receivable and contract-asset behavior. A higher backlog can contain more
subcontractor and reimbursable work without creating the same owner economics.

## Three-year cash bridge

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Capital expenditures | Recurring-capital residual | Acquisition cash | Other investing burden |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2023 | `$696.0M` | `$105.6M` | `$590.4M` | none | JV investment about `$59.8M` |
| FY2024 | `$827.5M` | `$119.6M` | `$707.9M` | `$18.7M` | JV investment about `$55.1M` |
| FY2025 | `$821.6M` | `$136.7M` | `$684.9M` | `$212.5M` | JV investment `$55.6M`; deconsolidation outflow `$45.4M` |

The recurring-capital residual stayed strong, but 2025 cash included a favorable
`$334.4M` source from accounts receivable and contract assets after a `$500.8M`
use in 2024. Accounts payable used `$333.6M`, contract liabilities used
`$210.5M`, and accrued expenses provided `$145.2M`. That pattern shows why
AECOM's reported free cash flow cannot be projected without a working-capital
normalization.

The company also paid `$212.5M` for acquisitions and invested in joint
ventures while repurchasing about `$388.4M` of stock and paying `$133.6M` of
dividends in FY2025. Common-owner cash therefore depends on both project
conversion and management's capital-allocation choices.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering risks

### Gross revenue can hide pass-through intensity

AECOM's adjusted NSR is a necessary control measure. Compare gross revenue
growth with adjusted NSR growth, gross margin, segment operating margin, and
cash generation. If gross revenue accelerates because of reimbursable costs
while adjusted NSR and cash per share do not, the headline growth is less
valuable than it appears.

### Backlog and book-to-burn do not remove project risk

AECOM reported a long run of book-to-burn above `1.0x` and a backlog near
`$26B` in Q2 FY2026. Work still requires staff, customer approvals, change
orders, subcontractor coordination, billing, and collection. Review project
losses, claims, unapproved change orders, contract assets, DSO, and backlog
conversion by Americas and International operations.

### Receivables and claims are the immediate cash test

DSO rose from `74` days at September 30, 2025 to `83` days at March 31, 2026
in the packet. Q2 FY2026 free cash flow was negative `$27M` because Middle East
payment timing and claim resolution delayed collections, even while operating
performance and backlog improved. This is not proof of improper accounting;
it is proof that owner cash depends on project administration and customer
payment, not only on margin.

### International operations carry asymmetric downside

Americas design margins were strong, while International NSR and gross profit
were weaker because of Asia and Middle East conditions. A global footprint can
increase the addressable market, but it also adds currency, political,
contract-claim, legal, and collection risk. A normalized valuation should not
apply the strongest Americas margin to the whole portfolio without evidence.

### Acquisitions and joint ventures reduce the clean-cash denominator

AECOM paid `$212.5M` for businesses in FY2025 and invested in unconsolidated
joint ventures. Distributions from joint ventures can support operating cash,
but capital calls and deconsolidation transactions can use cash. Track acquired
NSR, margin, retention, integration spending, goodwill, JV distributions, and
the return on capital actually retained by AECOM.

### Adjusted metrics and capital returns need reconciliation

Management targets adjusted EPS, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted operating income,
free cash flow, and a long-term `100%+` free-cash-flow conversion goal. These
are useful operating measures but do not replace GAAP cash. FY2025 also included
`$61.4M` of stock compensation, `$166.9M` of interest paid, buybacks, and
dividends. Check cash per diluted share after all of these claims.

## Balance-sheet resilience and capital requirements

At September 30, 2025, AECOM had about `$1.586B` of cash and roughly `$2.744B`
of gross debt. The Q2 FY2026 release reported cash of about `$1.013B` and debt
around `$2.745B`. Debt was manageable relative to operating cash, but the
company remains exposed to refinancing, interest, working-capital funding,
letters of credit, insurance, professional liability, and project guarantees.

The balance sheet also contains contract assets, goodwill, operating leases,
and interests in joint ventures. Capital requirements are therefore not just
the `$136.7M` of FY2025 capex. AECOM needs cash to employ technical staff,
support project execution, finance receivables, resolve claims, invest in
technology, and maintain client credibility.

## Lyn Alden lens

AECOM sits between public budgets and private infrastructure capital. Water,
transportation, defense, grid, and resilience programs can be supported by
long-term public need, but award and payment timing depend on fiscal policy,
appropriations, interest rates, and political execution.

Private data-center, semiconductor, life-sciences, and energy projects are
more directly linked to corporate financing conditions. When liquidity is
available, design and program-management work can expand before construction
spending peaks. When rates rise or project returns weaken, backlog can remain
visible while starts, claims, and collections become slower.

The company is relatively asset-light compared with a self-performing
contractor, but that does not mean risk-free. Its scarce assets are skilled
people, client trust, project knowledge, and the ability to carry receivables
until customers approve and pay.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative becomes:

`public and private infrastructure capital × project complexity × AECOM design and program-management share × technical talent and win rate -> adjusted NSR -> labor, subcontractors, claims, working capital, taxes, debt, and reinvestment -> owner cash`

The valuation should separately model:

1. adjusted NSR by Americas and International operations;
2. advisory, design, program management, and construction-management mix;
3. gross revenue versus fee-bearing economic work;
4. backlog and book-to-burn conversion, project estimates, claims, DSO, and
   contract assets;
5. acquisition and joint-venture returns;
6. debt, interest, working-capital funding, insurance, and professional
   liabilities; and
7. stock compensation, buybacks, dividends, and diluted cash per share.

At the September 14, 2026 market snapshot, AECOM equity value was about
`$8.7B`. The valuation register uses FY2025 recurring-capital residual of about
`$684.9M`, but normalizes the base case for receivable volatility, international
claims, acquisitions, JV investment, debt, SBC, and capital returns. The
question is whether the market is paying for durable technical control or for
the continuation of a favorable infrastructure spending cycle.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Management is incentivized to emphasize backlog, book-to-burn, adjusted NSR,
adjusted margin, adjusted EPS, adjusted EBITDA, free-cash-flow conversion,
win rate, and buybacks. Pair each with gross-to-net revenue reconciliation,
receivables, contract assets, claims, project estimates, debt, acquisitions,
SBC, and diluted shares.

The thesis breaks if:

- gross backlog grows while adjusted NSR, margin, or cash conversion weakens;
- DSO and contract assets keep rising without a matching improvement in
  collections or customer approvals;
- Middle East, Asia, or other international claims consume recurring cash;
- advisory and AI investment raises cost without higher win rate, pricing, or
  adjusted NSR per employee;
- acquisitions and JV investments require more cash than the acquired work
  returns;
- public funding or private project financing slows enough to reduce starts and
  book-to-burn;
- adjusted EPS rises mainly through buybacks while cash per diluted share does
  not; or
- the company repeatedly relies on one-time working-capital releases to meet
  free-cash-flow targets.

## Bottom line

AECOM controls an important upstream relationship in infrastructure. It helps
customers define, permit, design, fund, and coordinate projects before physical
assets are built. That gives it less direct capital intensity than a self-
performing contractor, but it still bears labor, professional, claim,
receivable, and execution burdens.

The reported demand story is strong: backlog is growing, book-to-burn remains
above one, and Americas margins are improving. The forensic question is cash
quality. FY2025 benefited from a large receivable and contract-asset release,
while Q2 FY2026 showed how Middle East collection timing could turn strong
operating performance into negative quarterly free cash flow. Durable owner
economics require adjusted NSR growth, stable project estimates, normalized
working capital, contained international claims, high-return acquisitions, and
cash per diluted share that improves after debt, SBC, buybacks, and dividends.
