# Affirm Holdings — purchase-moment underwriting, merchant conversion, and credit burden

As of 2026-09-14. Affirm's fiscal year ends June 30. Dollar amounts are in
millions unless explicitly identified otherwise.

## How to read this memo

Affirm is not ordinary application software. It provides payment choice,
underwriting, merchant conversion, installment loans, a card network, loan
servicing, and funding infrastructure.

Gross merchandise volume (GMV) is the dollar value of transactions on the
platform, net of refunds. It is an activity measure. Revenue is made up of
merchant fees, interest and fee income, card-network revenue, servicing and
other income, less transaction costs. Revenue less transaction costs (RLTC) is
the important operating denominator because transaction costs include funding
costs, provision for credit losses, and loss on loan originations.

Adjusted operating income excludes stock compensation, commercial-agreement
warrant expense, amortization, restructuring, and selected other items. It can
show operating leverage, but it is not a substitute for credit-adjusted cash
earnings or common-owner return.

## Investment question

The question is whether Affirm can become a durable payment and
merchant-conversion network while pricing consumer credit, funding loans,
absorbing fraud and losses, servicing receivables, and controlling dilution.

Affirm can create value in two different ways. It can help a merchant convert
a customer who would otherwise delay or abandon a purchase. It can also earn a
return for underwriting and financing that purchase. The first is a network
and software proposition. The second is a credit and funding proposition.
They must not be valued as if they carry the same risk.

## Control point

Affirm controls the payment decision at checkout:

1. **Offer design:** monthly installments, Pay-in-X, 0% APR, or interest-
   bearing options.
2. **Approval and underwriting:** which consumers qualify and how much credit
   to extend.
3. **Merchant conversion:** whether the financing offer changes whether the
   sale occurs and which merchant offer wins.
4. **Data and optimization:** transaction, repayment, fraud, and offer data
   improve approval, pricing, and merchant-program selection.
5. **Repeat use:** Affirm Card and wallet integrations can turn one checkout
   tool into a recurring payment relationship.

The control point is stronger when merchants cannot reproduce Affirm's
approval, financing, and conversion system. It is weaker when merchants can
use banks, card networks, PayPal, Apple Pay, Shopify, Synchrony, or their own
financing without losing conversion.

The Q3 shareholder letter says delinquencies rose in line with expectations,
with roughly half of the increase due to a lower denominator associated with
tax-refund seasonality. This is not evidence that credit quality is broken,
but it also does not justify ignoring the increase. The correct test is cohort
loss performance after seasoning.

The quarter also benefited from strong capital-markets conditions. Affirm
priced a third revolving ABS transaction under a 5% blended yield in March and
reported a lower average cost of funds. That is positive evidence, but it is
partly external. Normalized value must survive higher funding costs and weaker
securitization demand.

## Revenue and credit bridge

Merchant network revenue depends on GMV, product mix, merchant pricing,
consumer credit risk, loan term, and whether the transaction occurs through
Affirm Card or another direct channel. Merchant network revenue as a
percentage of GMV declined to 2.3% for Q3 and 2.4% for the nine-month period,
from 2.5% in the comparable prior-year periods. At the same time, 0% APR GMV
and direct-to-consumer activity increased.

Higher GMV therefore does not automatically produce a higher merchant fee
rate. Mix, incentives, negotiated pricing, and direct-to-consumer growth can
reduce monetization while the network becomes more useful.

Separate:

- merchant fees for conversion;
- interest income from loans held for investment;
- gains or losses on loan sales;
- funding costs and hedge results;
- provision for expected credit losses;
- card-network and servicing revenue;
- merchant incentives and loss on originations.

The economic result is the spread after expected credit loss and funding cost,
plus merchant and network revenue, less operating expense, technology,
servicing, compliance, and dilution.

## Credit quality and reserve judgment

Affirm uses an allowance for expected lifetime credit losses. The allowance
depends on contractual term, historical losses, consumer payment history,
recoveries, current economic conditions, and behavioral assumptions. Loans are
generally charged off when contractual principal reaches 120 days past due.

For the nine months ended March 31, 2026, provision for losses was $573M,
compared with $460M in the prior-year period. The allowance roll-forward
showed $557M of provision for loan losses and $496M of charge-offs. The
increase is not automatically a problem because the loan book grew, but it is a
material earnings input and must be viewed with originations, delinquencies,
charge-offs, recoveries, and vintage data.

Questions for each filing:

1. Are reserve assumptions conservative relative to consumer leverage,
   unemployment, inflation, and refinancing conditions?
2. Is the mix shifting toward longer-term, larger, or higher-risk loans?
3. Are charge-offs and recoveries developing as the reserve model predicted?
4. Is provision rising because the book is larger, credit is weaker, or the
   forecast changed?
5. Do loan-sale gains depend on buyer pricing that may not persist?

## Funding and balance-sheet resilience

Affirm funds loans through warehouse facilities, securitizations, sale and
repurchase agreements, and other arrangements. At March 31, 2026, funding debt
and securitization principal outstanding was about $7.8B, with $12.9B of
borrowing capacity. U.S. warehouse facilities had $5.4B of capacity and $1.7B
drawn; Canadian and U.K. facilities had $814M of capacity and $552M drawn. An
unsecured $330M revolver was undrawn.

Securitization reduces direct funding concentration but does not remove credit
or liquidity risk. Affirm retains residual interests that absorb losses after
required distributions and continues servicing the loans. The company must
maintain collateral quality, funding access, hedge effectiveness, advance
rates, covenant compliance, and buyer demand for asset-backed debt.

The stress test is combined: credit losses rise, loan buyers demand more
yield, warehouse advance rates fall, and consumers borrow less. The balance
sheet can look liquid at period end while the next origination cohort requires
more capital and produces lower returns.

## 0% APR and merchant conversion

Affirm's Q2 FY2026 `Big Nothing` event is useful evidence for the merchant
conversion thesis. Participating merchants saw roughly a 5% GMV lift overall,
and roughly a 27% lift when promoted on Affirm surfaces. More than 65% of
approved U.S. applications were presented with a 0% APR offer, and 39% of
quarter transactions used 0% APR products.

The result supports the claim that financing design can change demand. It does
not prove durable owner cash. The merchant may fund the subsidy, Affirm may
accept lower fees, the consumer may shift purchases forward, or the event may
attract a different credit mix. The next proof is repeat purchase,
contribution after funding and credit costs, merchant renewal, and performance
after the promotion ends.

## Affirm Card and network expansion

Affirm Card moves the company closer to a broader payment network. Q3 FY2026
card GMV was about $2.1B, up 146%, and active cardholders more than doubled to
4.4M. Card growth can increase frequency and data density, but it also broadens
exposure to authorization, fraud, credit behavior, network economics, and
customer-acquisition costs.

Test active-card retention, purchase frequency, merchant mix, authorization
quality, losses, interchange or network revenue, funding needs, and incremental
contribution after rewards and marketing. A large cardholder count without
positive contribution can increase complexity without improving the control
point.

## Stock compensation and financial engineering

For the nine months ended March 31, 2026, Affirm recorded $379M of total stock
compensation. About $237M was expensed in operating costs and $141M was
capitalized into property, equipment, and software. Capitalizing SBC does not
remove the employee cost; it places part of it on the balance sheet and moves
the expense into future depreciation or amortization.

Affirm also recorded $143M of commercial-agreement warrant expense in the
nine-month cash-flow reconciliation. Warrant expense is not a normal cash
operating cost in the period, but the related equity claim matters to common
owners. Include warrants, RSUs, options, convertible notes, and share-settlement
taxes in per-share value.

Adjusted operating income can rise because credit and network economics improve,
but it can also look stronger because SBC, warrant expense, and amortization
are excluded. The proper question is whether GAAP earnings and cash per fully
diluted share improve after the equity claims are charged.

## Lyn Alden, Damodaran, and forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** Affirm is exposed to household cash flow, consumer debt
  service, unemployment, interest rates, funding-market liquidity, and the
  willingness of asset-backed investors to finance installment receivables.
  Strong spending supports growth but can also increase future credit exposure.
- **Damodaran lens:** translate the story into GMV, RLTC, merchant fee rate,
  product mix, loan yields, expected loss, funding cost, servicing cost,
  operating expense, reinvestment, capital needs, and fully diluted shares.
  Value the merchant network and card option separately from the credit spread.
- **Forensic lens:** do not treat GMV as revenue, loan-sale gains as recurring
  spread, reserve releases as operating improvement, adjusted income as owner
  earnings, SBC capitalization as free labor, or securitization as elimination
  of risk.

## Valuation framing

Affirm should be valued through two engines:

1. **Network and conversion:** merchant fees, card-network revenue, merchant
   services, and repeat use after direct costs.
2. **Credit and funding:** interest income, loan discounts, servicing, expected
   loss, funding cost, hedges, retained residuals, and required capital.

The first engine earns a higher payment-network or software multiple only if it
produces cash independently of favorable credit spreads. The second engine
requires normalized loss and funding assumptions, not the current quarter's
best securitization yield.

Charge stock compensation, commercial warrants, capitalized SBC, technology
investment, taxes, and required capital. RLTC growth can be rapid while common-
owner value grows slowly if the company issues shares or accepts weaker credit
economics to maintain growth.

### Specialized two-engine valuation screen

Affirm is intentionally kept outside the common owner-cash register. Its
operating cash flow includes loan funding, receivable movements, loan sales,
securitization activity, and other credit-balance-sheet changes that do not
have the same meaning as cash from an ordinary service or software company.

The latest Q3 FY2026 figures provide a useful operating anchor. Quarterly RLTC
was `$498M`, or about `$1.992B` annualized. Quarterly GAAP operating income was
`$103M`, or about `$412M` annualized. The nine-month filing also recorded
`$379M` of stock compensation, including `$141M` capitalized, and `$143M` of
commercial-agreement warrant expense. Those equity claims remain relevant even
when adjusted operating income excludes them.

At the 2026-09-15 market snapshot, Affirm's equity value was about `$25.543B`.
An initial specialized screen uses normalized fully diluted operating earnings
of `$400M`/`$1.000B`/`$1.600B` in bear/base/bull cases at `25x`/`30x`/`35x`,
implying approximately `$10B`/`$30B`/`$56B`. These are not owner-cash cases or
price targets. The base case requires Affirm to move from roughly `$412M` of
annualized current GAAP operating income to `$1.0B` while keeping credit loss,
funding, capital, warrant, and dilution costs under control. The market is
therefore paying for substantial improvement in both the network engine and
the credit engine, not merely for current GMV growth.

The next upgrade is a model that reports, by product and vintage, merchant and
card contribution after expected loss, funding, servicing, incentives, fraud,
capital, warrants, and fully diluted shares. Until that evidence is available,
the screen should remain specialized and visibly more uncertain than the
ordinary company rows.

## Evidence grades and thesis breakers

| Question | Current grade | What would upgrade or weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Merchant conversion control point | B+ | Repeated conversion lift and merchant renewal after promotions |
| Consumer repeat behavior | B+ | Card and installment frequency grow without weaker vintages |
| Credit-adjusted economics | B- | Losses, recoveries, and RLTC remain strong through a weaker cycle |
| Funding resilience | B- | Funding remains available at acceptable spreads through stress |
| Merchant fee quality | B- | Fee rate and retention hold as 0% APR and card mix change |
| Card expansion | B- | Contribution remains positive after losses, rewards, and fraud |
| GAAP earnings quality | C+ | GAAP earnings and cash per diluted share improve after SBC and warrants |
| Capital allocation | C+ | Repurchases or reinvestment are funded from normalized owner cash |

Thesis breakers:

1. Delinquencies, charge-offs, or lifetime-loss assumptions rise faster than
   RLTC and merchant revenue.
2. Funding costs rise or securitization demand weakens enough to compress the
   credit engine.
3. Merchant fee rates decline because competition or incentives absorb the
   value of conversion.
4. 0% APR promotions pull demand forward without improving repeat contribution
   or merchant retention.
5. Affirm Card grows users and GMV but produces poor contribution after losses,
   rewards, fraud, and acquisition costs.
6. Loan-sale gains, reserve changes, or warrant marks contribute a growing
   share of reported improvement.
7. Capitalized SBC and unrecognized awards rise faster than cash earnings per
   diluted share.
8. Top partners, funding providers, or loan buyers gain leverage to reduce
   Affirm's economics or restrict growth.
