# AGNC Investment: Agency MBS carry, leverage, and book-value discipline

## What the company does

AGNC Investment is a mortgage REIT that invests primarily in Agency residential
mortgage-backed securities and TBA positions. It finances those assets with repo
and other borrowing, hedges interest-rate and duration exposure, earns net spread
and dollar-roll income, and distributes a large portion of the result to common
shareholders.

The product is balance-sheet management. AGNC must continuously decide how much
leverage, duration, hedge protection, mortgage exposure, and liquidity risk the
equity can carry. Agency guarantees reduce borrower credit risk; they do not
protect shareholders from rate, spread, prepayment, funding, or book-value loss.

FY2025 was a strong carry and book-value period, with economic return on tangible
common equity approximately `22.7%` and total stock return approximately `34.8%`.
The portfolio was approximately `$94.8B`, at-risk leverage approximately `7.2x`,
and net spread and dollar-roll income approximately `$1.535B`.

Q1 2026 demonstrates why the annual result cannot be extrapolated. Net spread and
dollar-roll income remained approximately `$475M`, but comprehensive loss was
approximately `$200M`, economic return was approximately `-1.6%`, and tangible
book value per share fell from `$8.88` to `$8.38`. Q2 2026 recovered: net income
and comprehensive income were approximately `$603M`, economic return was `6.7%`,
book value rose to `$8.58`, and spread income was approximately `$462M`.

Spread income and economic return therefore answer different questions. The
first shows current portfolio carry. The second includes changes in the value of
the assets, hedges, and equity.

## Where the economics sit

### Agency MBS portfolio

AGNC's portfolio was approximately `95%` 30-year fixed-rate Agency RMBS and TBAs
at year-end 2025. The Agency guarantee reduces direct borrower credit loss, but
the securities still change value when Treasury rates, mortgage spreads,
prepayment expectations, and liquidity change.

Track coupon, premium or discount, asset yield, weighted-average life, duration,
prepayment speed, TBA positions, mortgage spreads, and realized and unrealized
gains. A larger portfolio is not better if leverage and duration risk grow faster
than sustainable spread.

### Repo funding and leverage

At-risk leverage of approximately `7.4x` means a relatively small percentage
change in financed assets can materially affect common equity. Repo financing
also creates maturity, haircut, collateral, and counterparty risks.

Track funding cost, repo maturity, haircuts, unencumbered assets, liquidity,
counterparty concentration, margin calls, and the ability to reduce leverage.
The relevant stress is whether AGNC can fund the portfolio and its hedges while
preserving book value when lenders become more cautious.

### Hedges and mortgage duration

Interest-rate hedges reduce some risk but do not create a fixed outcome. Mortgage
assets can shorten when borrowers refinance and lengthen when they remain locked
into old loans. Hedge basis and convexity can leave the company exposed even if
the headline hedge ratio appears adequate.

Measure hedge notional, duration gap, swap and Treasury positions, hedge cost,
counterparty exposure, collateral, prepayment speeds, and TBA exposure. The
portfolio must be evaluated under falling, rising, and volatile rate paths.

### Distribution and shareholder capital

The dividend is part of the product for many mortgage-REIT investors. It is not
proof of sustainable earnings. Compare dividend per share with net spread income,
economic return, comprehensive income, book-value movement, and share issuance.

A dividend that exceeds sustainable spread can reduce equity. Equity issuance can
replenish capital, but it may dilute existing shareholders or transfer book value
depending on the issue price relative to tangible book value.

## Earnings quality and forensic review

### Carry versus comprehensive income

Q1 2026 is the key forensic case: spread and dollar-roll income was approximately
`$475M`, yet comprehensive loss was approximately `$200M` and economic return
negative `1.6%`. A carry-only analysis would have missed the capital loss.

Build a quarterly bridge from net spread and dollar-roll income to net income,
comprehensive income, tangible book value, and total shareholder return. Separate
coupon income, dollar-roll income, premium amortization, realized gains, market
marks, hedge results, funding costs, and operating expenses.

### Dividend coverage

Test dividends against normalized spread income after funding and hedge cost, not
against a single strong quarter. A dividend can appear covered by current carry
while book value is being eroded by market losses. Repeated book-value decline
requires a lower sustainable distribution even if the nominal dividend remains
unchanged.

### Leverage and dilution

Leverage can lift economic return during favorable spread and rate conditions. It
also magnifies losses. Track leverage against book value, asset duration, hedge
coverage, repo access, and liquidity.

Read at-the-market issuance, preferred equity, repurchases, and share count. A
company can preserve the portfolio by issuing equity, but the economic cost may
be borne by existing holders. The correct per-share test is book value and future
spread income after the new capital, not just portfolio size.

### Mark-to-market and financial engineering

Mortgage REIT accounting includes market values and derivative results. The risk
is not that marks are inherently improper; it is that current spread income is
presented without equal attention to the capital value that supports future
dividends.

Compare reported marks with observable Agency MBS pricing, rates, mortgage
spreads, hedge results, and subsequent book-value movements. Do not treat the
`22.7%` FY2025 economic return as a normalized permanent rate.

## Balance-sheet resilience and macro conditions

The Lyn Alden lens follows Treasury rates, mortgage spreads, repo liquidity,
housing finance, and collateral. AGNC may benefit when funding remains available,
spreads are favorable, and hedges protect book value. It is vulnerable when repo
haircuts rise, rates gap, mortgage spreads widen, or prepayments change duration
faster than the hedge book adjusts.

Stress cases should include:

- rapid rate increases and decreases;
- mortgage-spread widening;
- higher prepayment or extension risk;
- repo cost increases and collateral calls;
- counterparty or liquidity stress;
- book-value decline at 7x-plus leverage;
- dividends exceeding sustainable spread;
- equity issuance below tangible book value;
- forced asset sales and hedge basis failure.

The relevant resilience measure is not only current spread. It is the ability to
preserve tangible book value and funding access while continuing to meet hedging,
margin, and dividend obligations.

## Damodaran lens: translate the carry narrative into numbers

The narrative is that AGNC can earn a durable high dividend by financing Agency
MBS, hedging rates, and managing leverage. That narrative requires:

1. Asset yield exceeds repo and other funding cost after hedge expense.
2. Mortgage spreads and prepayments remain manageable across rate paths.
3. Leverage increases equity return without producing unacceptable book-value
   volatility or liquidity risk.
4. Dividends remain below normalized economic earnings.
5. Repo and hedge counterparties remain available during market stress.
6. Equity issuance and capital actions protect, rather than transfer value away
   from, existing shareholders.
7. The manager can maintain positive total return after dividends, marks, and
   dilution.

Value AGNC from normalized spread income, tangible book value, leverage,
duration, hedge effectiveness, funding, and dividend sustainability. A current
yield multiple is inadequate because the equity value can change materially even
when carry is stable.

## Cash conversion and distributable earnings

For AGNC, common-owner cash is not operating cash flow less capital spending. The relevant starting point is normalized net spread and dollar-roll income after repo funding, hedge expense, operating costs, and the economic cost of maintaining the portfolio. That amount must then be tested against changes in tangible book value, leverage, margin requirements, and the capital needed to withstand a stressed market.

The distribution bridge is: asset interest and dollar-roll income -> funding and hedge costs -> operating expenses -> normalized distributable earnings -> dividend. A dividend is durable only when this bridge remains positive across rate, spread, prepayment, and repo scenarios. If dividends are paid while book value declines or equity must be issued below tangible book value, the nominal distribution is not the same as durable owner cash.

## Thesis breakers and filing tests

| Question | Evidence supporting the thesis | Evidence weakening it |
|---|---|---|
| Is spread income durable? | Asset yield exceeds funding and hedge cost across rate paths | Repo or hedge cost consumes carry |
| Is book value protected? | Tangible book value grows or remains stable after dividends | Repeated book-value decline despite positive spread income |
| Is leverage controlled? | At-risk leverage and duration remain within stress capacity | Leverage rises to preserve dividends or magnifies losses |
| Are hedges effective? | Hedge results offset rate and duration changes | Basis, convexity, or counterparty losses leave equity exposed |
| Is funding resilient? | Repo access, haircuts, liquidity, and counterparties remain stable | Margin calls, shorter maturities, higher haircuts, or forced sales |
| Are dividends sustainable? | Distribution is covered by normalized economic earnings | Dividends exceed carry and require capital issuance |
| Are capital actions fair? | New shares are issued above book or at accretive terms | Dilutive issuance or repurchases transfer value from existing holders |

## Bottom line

AGNC controls a levered Agency MBS portfolio, its financing, hedge book, and
distribution policy. Its cleanest economic feature is a transparent spread and
housing-finance market structure. Its unavoidable burden is leverage: funding,
duration, prepayment, mortgage spread, hedge, liquidity, and book-value risk are
all concentrated in common equity.

FY2025 was strong, but Q1 2026 showed that approximately `$475M` of spread income
can coexist with a negative economic return and falling book value. Q2 2026
recovered, but one recovery does not establish a through-cycle result. Future
filings must show carry after funding and hedges, book-value movement, leverage,
repo terms, prepayment, hedge effectiveness, dividend coverage, and dilution.

**Evidence grade:** Strong for FY2025 and Q1-Q2 2026 reported portfolio, spread,
economic-return, book-value, leverage, and dividend figures; medium for hedge and
funding durability; unresolved for through-cycle repo, rate, spread, prepayment,
and capital-return behavior.
