# Air Products and Chemicals — industrial-gas contracts, project capital, and the cost of a reset

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed GAAP figures unless explicitly identified otherwise; dollar amounts are in millions.

## How to read this memo

Industrial gases are gases such as oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, and helium used
in manufacturing, refining, health care, electronics, and space programs. An
on-site or pipeline plant is built near one customer or a connected customer
network and supplies gas continuously. Merchant gas is produced for delivery
by truck or other distribution channels. GAAP is the accounting rule set used
in the filed financial statements. Adjusted earnings exclude selected charges;
they can help describe operating performance, but they are not a substitute
for cash actually received. Capital expenditure means cash invested in plants,
pipelines, equipment, and other productive assets.

## Investment question

Air Products has a strong core business when a customer depends on a reliable
gas supply and the company owns the plant, pipeline, logistics, and operating
knowledge required to provide it. The central question is whether that core
business can produce durable cash for shareholders after the company funds new
plants, joint ventures, project-finance obligations, debt, dividends, and
large clean-energy projects whose economics have now been reset.

## What the filing and recent company record reveal

- The core industrial-gas model can have durable customer relationships because
  an on-site plant or pipeline is part of the customer’s production process.
  That relationship is valuable only if contract pricing, energy pass-through,
  utilization, and plant returns cover the capital required to maintain it.
- FY2025 project spending was concentrated in NEOM and clean-energy complexes
  in Louisiana and Alberta, while core plants also required maintenance and
  replacement spending. The company therefore had both a mature operating base
  and a large new-project program at the same time.
- In FY2026 management exited the Louisiana Clean Energy Complex and the
  Arizona/Casa Grande liquid-hydrogen facility and reduced its capital guidance.
  The reset is evidence that demand, customer commitments, timing, cost, or
  returns were weaker than the earlier plan required. It does not by itself
  prove that the core industrial-gas assets are impaired, but it weakens the
  case for treating the prior project pipeline as earned future value.
- Management’s adjusted operating results remained positive while GAAP results
  included major project and asset charges. The analyst must identify which
  charges are genuinely non-recurring and which arise from a recurring pattern
  of approving, funding, and later abandoning large projects.
- The engineering-materials separation may make the remaining industrial-gas
  earnings easier to analyze, but it can also move debt, pension obligations,
  stranded costs, tax assets, or shared infrastructure between entities. The
  separation documents must be read before assigning a clean multiple.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Does adjusted operating profit describe cash economics?

Reconcile adjusted operating income to GAAP operating income and then to
operating cash flow. Identify project cancellations, impairments, restructuring,
contract changes, pension items, and recurring compensation. A charge can be
non-cash in the current year while still representing cash that shareholders
previously funded or must fund later.

### 2. How much of the cash flow belongs to the core business?

Separate mature industrial-gas operations from NEOM, Louisiana, Alberta,
hydrogen, and equity-affiliate investments. Report revenue, operating income,
cash flow, capital spending, partner funding, and debt for each group. A
consolidated cash-flow number is not enough when one project consumes capital
for years before it produces customer cash.

### 3. Are long-term contracts economically protective?

Read contract duration, minimum purchase obligations, energy-index formulas,
take-or-pay terms, termination rights, customer credit, and renewal economics.
“Contracted” does not mean risk-free. The customer can delay a plant, reduce
production, renegotiate, or become unable to pay; the supplier can remain tied
to an underused asset.

### 4. Who bears the risk in joint ventures and non-recourse structures?

Map Air Products’ equity, guarantees, completion support, offtake commitments,
partner funding, debt service, and cash distributions. Non-recourse debt may
limit direct creditor claims on the parent, but the parent can still lose its
equity, provide support, or face reputational and contractual obligations.

### 5. Is the dividend sustainable after the reset?

FY2025 dividends of $1.589B exceeded operating cash flow less management capex
and acquisitions by about $1.867B. Test the dividend against maintenance capex,
project commitments, debt maturities, pension funding, separation costs, and
cash actually available at the parent. A long dividend record does not prove
that the current capital plan funds the dividend.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** industrial gases are physical infrastructure with energy,
  materials, labor, and financing requirements. Customer dependence can support
  pricing and contract duration, but inflation and high interest costs reduce
  the value of future cash unless returns exceed the full cost of capital.
- **Damodaran lens:** value the mature industrial-gas base, growth projects,
  joint ventures, and the planned separation independently. Use explicit
  reinvestment and return-on-capital assumptions. Do not capitalize adjusted
  earnings as though project losses and capital needs have disappeared.
- **Forensic lens:** the main category errors are treating contract visibility
  as guaranteed profit, adjusted income as owner cash, partner-funded project
  spending as free capital, non-recourse debt as no risk, and canceled projects
  as isolated one-time events.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $64.93B of equity value at
$291.43 per share. Against the FY2025 residual of negative $1.867B, a current
owner-cash multiple is not meaningful. A normalized range of $0.5B / $1.2B /
$2.2B at 15x / 18x / 22x implies roughly $7.5B / $21.6B / $48.4B of equity
value before assigning separate value to projects or net debt. The market
therefore requires a successful core-business recovery, materially lower
project spending, and credible separation economics.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Core industrial-gas returns remain below the cost of capital after maintenance
   capex and energy costs.
2. New project cancellations, impairments, or restructuring charges recur.
3. NEOM or other joint ventures require further parent cash, guarantees, or
   debt support despite non-recourse labels.
4. Customer contracts do not provide enough minimum volume or energy-cost
   recovery to protect plant utilization and margins.
5. The engineering-materials separation allocates debt, shared costs, or tax
   liabilities unfavorably to the remaining company.
6. Dividends continue to exceed cash available after maintenance capex and
   mandatory project or debt funding.

Next filing checks: segment operating income; contract terms and utilization;

separation allocation documents; receivables; adjusted-to-GAAP reconciliation;
