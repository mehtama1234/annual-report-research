# Alliant Energy Corporation — regulated rate base, large-load contracts, and the affordability constraint

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed GAAP figures unless explicitly identified otherwise; dollar amounts are in millions.

## How to read this memo

In this memo, a control point means the specific customer relationship, asset,
or process that gives a company the ability to earn money. GAAP means the accounting rules used in the filed financial statements.
Operating cash flow is cash produced by day-to-day operations. Rate base means
the investment regulators allow the utility to recover from customers while
earning an approved return. The negative residual below is only a cash-timing
and investment screen; it is not a complete measure of value for a regulated
utility, whose investment is normally recovered over many years through rates.

## Investment question

Alliant is the clean regulated-rate-base contrast to Constellation, Vistra, and
NRG. It serves a local electric and gas franchise and is using customer-specific
structures, advance ratemaking, and a large capital plan to accommodate data
centers and industrial demand. The question is whether contracted load becomes
earned common-equity returns without shifting excessive construction, rate-lag,
affordability, or financing risk to ratepayers and shareholders.

The mechanical residual was approximately negative $987M in 2023, negative
$1.082B in 2024, and negative $1.314B in 2025 as investment accelerated. This
does not prove value destruction: a regulated utility can create value when
approved investment earns a return above its funding cost and is recovered in
customer rates. It does prove that OCF-minus-capex cannot be used as the sole
valuation denominator for Alliant.

## What the filing and IR record reveal

- Alliant controls a regulated local customer franchise, reliability obligation,
  and the ratemaking process through which investment can be recovered.
- Company materials report approximately 3.4 GW of contracted data-center load,
  a projected roughly 60% increase in demand by 2031 versus 2025, and a $13.4B
  2026–2029 capital plan. These are valuable leading indicators, not yet proof
  of earned returns.
- Individual-customer rates, advance ratemaking, and customer-specific
  agreements can reduce the risk that existing customers subsidize new large
  loads. The proof is in the final tariffs, deposits, contributions, recovery
  timing, and what happens if the customer delays.
- A smaller regional utility can experience a large system effect from data
  centers: the incremental load is material relative to Alliant’s roughly 5.5 GW
  peak demand base. Concentration therefore cuts both ways—greater visibility,
  but more dependence on a few projects and regulators.
- The social contract is part of the economics. Management emphasizes
  affordability and below-national-average rates; aggressive capital recovery
  can weaken the very political permission on which rate-base value depends.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Is rate-base growth earning its cost of capital?

Reconstruct the capital plan by project, in-service date, approved rate base,
allowed return, construction-work-in-progress treatment, debt/equity funding,
and rate-case lag. A larger asset base is not value creation if regulatory
recovery is delayed, disallowed, or diluted by financing.

### 2. Are large-load commitments real and customer-funded?

Inspect service agreements, deposits, minimum bills, contribution-in-aid,
termination fees, interconnection milestones, and curtailment rights. The
question is not whether 3.4 GW is announced; it is whether the customer funds
the incremental system before Alliant commits irreversible capital.

### 3. Who bears affordability and stranded-capital risk?

If a data-center project is delayed, the regulator may protect customers rather
than shareholders. Model the outcome under energized load, delayed load,
partial load, and cancellation. Compare customer-specific recovery with broad
rate socialization.

### 4. Is the dividend funded by durable regulated earnings?

The $521M dividend sits beside a negative $1.314B mechanical residual. That is
normal for a growing utility only if the dividend is supported by earnings,
capital-market access, and a credible future recovery mechanism. It should not
be described as free cash yield.

### 5. Does the capital plan outrun the balance sheet?

Test debt growth, interest coverage, equity issuance, credit metrics, rate-base
growth, and customer bill impact. Higher rates can make an apparently attractive
load-growth project value-destructive even when demand itself is genuine.

## Incentives, regulatory recovery, and cohort position

Alliant's growth narrative can reward several different outcomes: a larger
rate base, more contracted load, approved capital spending, earnings growth,
and dividend continuity. None of those outcomes alone proves that common
shareholders earn an adequate return. The relevant test is whether each major
project enters service on time, receives a fair allowed return, is funded at a
reasonable cost, and remains affordable enough that regulators and customers
continue to support it.

Customer-specific structures can protect existing ratepayers, but only if the
customer provides binding credit support or contributions before Alliant takes
the full construction risk. The filing record should therefore be read for
what happens after delay, curtailment, cancellation, or cost overrun—not only
for the announced gigawatts.

Alliant belongs in the [utility rate-base, merchant-power, and large-load
first-principles essay](../first-principles/utility-rate-base-merchant-power-and-large-load.md)
and the [power cohort comparison](../cross-sector/nuclear-scarcity-versus-regulated-and-merchant-power-forensic-comparison-2026-09-13.md).
Compared with NRG, Alliant controls a regulated franchise and recovery process
rather than a retail/merchant customer-and-generation stack. Its cleaner
revenue visibility comes with rate-lag, affordability, debt, equity issuance,
and regulatory-disallowance risk.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** the local franchise is durable, but a capital-intensive
  utility is exposed to rates, inflation, financing availability, and political
  affordability. Essential demand does not immunize the balance sheet.
- **Damodaran lens:** value regulated rate base using allowed return, growth,
  regulatory lag, financing, and customer affordability. Treat contracted
  large-load projects as options until they are approved, financed, energized,
  and earning.
- **Forensic lens:** the key category errors are treating contracted load as
  revenue, rate-base additions as current cash, dividends as FCF yield, and
  management capital plans as proof of incremental ROIC.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $17.55B of equity value at
$67.26 per share. A mechanical post-investment screen is not appropriate for a
regulated utility; for orientation only, normalized distributable owner cash
of $0.5B / $0.75B / $1.0B at 16x / 20x / 24x implies $8B / $15B / $24B. The
market is therefore underwriting successful rate-base growth and large-load
recovery, not simply FY2025 cash conversion.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Large-load contracts are delayed, curtailed, or cancelled after capital is
   committed.
2. Regulators disallow, defer, or reduce recovery of major projects.
3. Rate increases damage affordability and political support for the capital
   plan.
4. Debt and equity funding costs rise faster than allowed returns.
5. Construction delays, reliability costs, environmental obligations, or
   retirement liabilities absorb the expected project return.
6. Customer-specific structures prove weaker than management’s “growth pays
   its own way” framing.

Next filing checks: rate-base additions and in-service dates; approved versus
requested recovery; construction-work-in-progress; debt/equity funding;
customer contributions and deposits; load energization; bill impact; OCF;
capital expenditures; asset-retirement obligations; and dividend coverage.
