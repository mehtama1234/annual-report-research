# Alphabet Inc. — search distribution, cloud infrastructure, and the cost of staying ahead

Research date: 2026-09-13  
Evidence window: FY2025 and Q2 2026  
Ticker: GOOGL / GOOG  
Evidence grade: A for FY2025 and Q2 2026 reported figures; B for the future
return on AI infrastructure, the durability of search monetization under AI
interfaces, and the long-run economics of Other Bets.

## Bottom line

Alphabet controls several valuable points in the information and computing
system: search intent, video attention, mobile distribution, advertising
measurement, cloud infrastructure, AI models, and custom computing hardware.
Search and YouTube generate demand and data; Google Cloud sells computing and
AI services to enterprises; the same technical infrastructure supports both
internal products and external customers.

FY2025 was very profitable. Revenue was $402.836B, operating income was
$129.039B, net income was $132.170B, and operating cash flow was $164.7B.
Capital expenditures were $91.4B, leaving an operating-cash-after-capex screen
of about $73.3B before acquisitions, investments, debt principal, and other
claims. That screen is meaningful, but it should not be treated as permanent
owner cash because Alphabet expects a very large increase in infrastructure
spending in 2026.

The latest quarter makes the capital question more urgent. Q2 2026 revenue was
$119.8B and operating income was $40.8B, while management raised full-year
capital-spending guidance to approximately $195B-$205B and said 2027 spending
would increase significantly again. The business can fund a large build from
its existing cash engine, but the investment case now requires proof that AI
capacity creates enough additional operating income and cash after servers,
networking, data centers, power, depreciation, research, talent, and legal or
regulatory constraints.

This is not evidence of accounting misconduct. The forensic concern is simpler:
current search economics can make a large future investment program appear
affordable even if the incremental return on that program is lower than the
return on the older advertising business.

## What Alphabet actually controls

The primary chain is:

`user question or viewing need -> Search/YouTube/mobile distribution -> attention and intent data -> advertiser demand -> measured conversion -> cash`

The enterprise chain is:

`developer or business workload -> Google Cloud/AI service -> usage or contract -> technical infrastructure utilization -> recurring service revenue`

Alphabet controls search ranking and presentation, advertising auctions,
YouTube distribution, Android and related mobile services, cloud services,
AI models, selected chips and systems, and large-scale data and measurement
capabilities. It does not control user preferences, advertiser budgets,
distribution partners, device manufacturers, cloud customer workloads, energy
availability, specialized technical labor, regulators, or the cost of advanced
chips and data-center construction.

The company therefore has two different economic qualities inside one group:

- Google Services monetizes user attention and commercial intent, mainly through
  advertising, subscriptions, platforms, and devices.
- Google Cloud monetizes technical infrastructure and enterprise workloads,
  but requires large up-front capacity and continuing product investment.

The reported segment structure helps, but Alphabet also reports Alphabet-level
activities that primarily include shared AI research and development. An
outside investor should not assume that all AI costs have been assigned to the
segment that will eventually receive the revenue.

Alphabet's revenue grew 15% in 2025. Operating income grew 15% as well, while

not whether the legacy business generated cash; it clearly did. The question is
whether the return profile remains similar after the capital base nearly
doubles from 2025 levels.

Google Services remains the economic center of the company. Google Cloud
operating income rose from $6.112B in 2024 to $13.910B in 2025, but Cloud also
incurred $44.795B of disclosed costs and expenses, including technical
infrastructure and employee compensation. Other Bets and Alphabet-level costs
consume a meaningful amount of operating profit, although they can support
long-term research and new products.

Alphabet says Alphabet-level activities primarily reflect shared AI research
and development. This is strategically understandable, but it matters for
valuation: the current Search profit funds research and infrastructure whose
future revenue may be distributed across Search, Cloud, YouTube, devices, or
new products. A sum-of-the-parts model should use a range for these shared
costs, not assign them away as if they disappear.

## Advertising economics

FY2025 Google advertising revenue was $294.691B:

- Google Search and other: $224.532B
- YouTube ads: $40.367B
- Google Network: $29.792B

Search and other revenue grew $26.4B from 2024 to 2025. Paid clicks increased
6% and cost per click increased 7%. Those two measures show both demand and
pricing, but they do not alone prove that AI search will preserve the same
economics. An AI response can change the number of result pages, external
links, commercial queries, ad formats, traffic-acquisition payments, and the
cost of serving each query.

The Google Network declined $567M in 2025 as AdSense weakness outweighed AdMob
growth. That is an important contrast: Alphabet's strongest advertising
position is not equally strong across every distribution channel.

Advertising traffic acquisition costs were 20.3% of the relevant advertising
base in 2025, down from 20.7% in 2024. A lower rate is helpful, but the company
still depends on distribution partners and pays them for traffic. Mobile
defaults, browser arrangements, device access, and regulatory remedies are
therefore part of the economics of Search.

## Google Cloud and AI: demand versus capital return

Google Cloud revenue was $58.705B in FY2025, up from $43.229B in 2024. Cloud
operating income was $13.910B. Q2 2026 Cloud revenue grew 82% year over year to
approximately $20.6B, according to the quarter materials, while the filing
reported consolidated Q2 revenue of $119.8B and operating income of $40.8B.

The operating evidence is encouraging: enterprise demand, AI services, custom
TPUs, partner GPUs, and Gemini products are creating revenue. The capital
evidence is more demanding:

- FY2025 capital expenditures were $91.4B, mainly technical infrastructure.
- Management expected 2026 capital expenditures near $180B in the annual
  report and later raised the Q2 range to about $195B-$205B.
- Q2 2026 data-center and technical-infrastructure spending is now large enough
  to make quarterly free cash flow negative.
- The 2025 10-K says the company expects infrastructure investment to increase
  materially in 2026.

The correct question is not whether Cloud revenue is growing. It is whether
incremental Cloud and AI gross profit, after power, servers, networking,
depreciation, employees, support, and financing, earns more than the return
required on the new infrastructure. Cloud backlog or enterprise adoption is
evidence of demand; it is not evidence of the final return on capital.

## Cash conversion and reinvestment

Alphabet's FY2025 operating cash flow of $164.7B was supported by net income,
depreciation, stock compensation, deferred taxes, and working-capital changes.
The cash-capex screen is:

`$164.7B operating cash flow - $91.4B capital expenditures = approximately $73.3B`

That is a useful first calculation, not a final owner-cash answer. It does not
subtract acquisitions, strategic investments, debt principal, or the cash
required for working-capital growth. It also uses one year's operating cash
against a capital program that is changing quickly.

Alphabet repurchased $45.4B of Class A and Class C shares in 2025. Buybacks
reduce share count only if the repurchase price and timing are sensible and if
the company is not simultaneously issuing stock compensation or borrowing to
fund capital allocation. The investor should compare actual diluted shares,
shares withheld for taxes, grants, and repurchases rather than relying on the
authorized program.

The 2025 cash-flow bridge also includes $24.953B of stock-based compensation,
$21.136B of depreciation, and $8.348B of deferred-income-tax expense. The
depreciation number is not a reason to subtract it again from operating cash
flow, but it shows that the existing asset base is already consuming earnings
over time. The question is whether new servers and data centers will produce
cash before they require replacement.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering tests

### Non-operating and investment activity

Alphabet holds marketable and non-marketable securities. Non-marketable
securities carried at December 31, 2025 totaled about $68.687B, including
approximately $64.094B accounted for under the measurement alternative and
$4.593B of equity-method investments and other items. Changes in fair value or
observable transactions can affect other income and equity without representing
cash generated by Search or Cloud.

For valuation, separate operating profit, cash interest, realized investment
returns, unrealized investment adjustments, and equity-method results. A strong
investment gain can increase net income while leaving the operating business
unchanged.

### Stock compensation and dilution

Stock-based compensation was $24.953B in 2025. It is a real employee cost even
though the cash-flow statement adds it back. Alphabet must be evaluated on cash
per diluted share after grants and tax-withholding share sales, not only on
total operating cash.

The multi-class structure also matters. Class B shares have ten votes per
share, so voting control is concentrated even when economic ownership is spread
across the public classes. This does not make the accounting improper; it
changes the governance and capital-allocation checks for minority owners.

### Useful lives and depreciation

Technical infrastructure is depreciated over estimated useful lives. If the
useful life is lengthened, current depreciation falls and operating income rises;
if it is shortened, current earnings fall. Alphabet's expanding server and
network base makes this estimate economically important. The investor should
compare depreciation with replacement capex, asset age, utilization, and the
speed of chip and system obsolescence.

### Deferred taxes and tax-law effects

Alphabet reported $8.348B of deferred-income-tax expense in the 2025 cash-flow
reconciliation. Changes in tax law also allowed accelerated depreciation and
immediate expensing of some domestic research costs. These rules can improve
near-term cash taxes and therefore operating cash, but the benefit is a timing
effect unless the tax advantage is permanent. Track cash taxes, deferred-tax
balances, and whether future capital spending keeps generating similar benefits.

### Legal, competition, and distribution costs

Alphabet faces antitrust, privacy, data-use, copyright, AI-governance, and
consumer-protection matters. The key economic risk is not only a fine. A remedy
could change default distribution, data combination, ad placement, revenue
sharing, or the ability to use one product to strengthen another.

## Balance-sheet resilience

Alphabet ended FY2025 with a large liquid-asset base and manageable debt
relative to operating cash. The balance sheet gives it more ability than most
companies to fund technical infrastructure during a tighter credit market.
That strength should not be confused with high returns on every dollar spent.

The balance sheet also carries investments, leases, purchase commitments,
energy obligations, and technical infrastructure that can become difficult to
reduce quickly. A data center built for a specific workload may have lower
value if demand, chip economics, or model architecture changes. The downside
test is therefore asset redeployment and cash return, not just solvency.

## Lyn Alden lens: liquidity and durability

Search, video, mobile services, cloud computing, and business software have a
durable role in a modern economy. But their sensitivity differs:

- Search advertising depends on commercial activity and advertiser competition.
- YouTube depends on attention, content supply, ad budgets, and subscriptions.
- Cloud depends on enterprise workloads, developer usage, and IT budgets.
- AI infrastructure depends on power, chips, data centers, technical labor, and
  financing capacity.

Higher rates can reduce advertising and enterprise technology budgets while
raising the value assigned to near-term cash relative to distant AI profits.
Alphabet's cash and low dependence on external financing provide resilience,
but the company is choosing to expose more cash to a large infrastructure cycle.

The durable asset is not simply “AI leadership.” It is the combination of
distribution, data, technical talent, models, custom chips, enterprise
relationships, and cash generation. Each component can weaken separately.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The AI-first narrative should be translated into separate forecasts:

1. Search: query volume, paid clicks, cost per click, commercial intent, AI
   response cost, traffic acquisition cost, and regulatory distribution changes.
2. YouTube: viewing time, ad load, creator economics, subscriptions, content
   cost, and user monetization.
3. Cloud: usage, price, contract duration, backlog conversion, gross margin,
   support cost, infrastructure utilization, and capital per dollar of revenue.
4. AI products: paid seats, API usage, model cost, retention, inference cost,
   pricing, and cannibalization of existing Search or Cloud revenue.
5. Infrastructure: server and networking replacement, data-center construction,
   power, leases, depreciation, useful lives, and supply commitments.
6. Corporate capital: acquisitions, investments, buybacks, stock compensation,
   debt, taxes, and the multi-class governance structure.

The competitive-advantage period should differ by business. Search distribution
and advertiser data may remain strong while individual AI models become more
competitive. Cloud demand may grow while infrastructure returns fall. A
consolidated multiple hides these differences; a sum-of-the-parts model with a
shared-cost range is more appropriate.

## Incentives and governance questions

The investor should read the proxy for the precise performance measures used in
executive compensation. Growth in revenue, Cloud backlog, AI adoption, or
adjusted earnings can encourage spending before returns are demonstrated. A
stronger scorecard would connect compensation to cash per diluted share,
incremental returns, reliability, regulatory outcomes, and product retention.

The multi-class voting structure means the economic owner and the voting owner
are not identical. Minority shareholders need to examine related-party
transactions, board independence, capital allocation, and whether management
can pursue long-duration projects without a conventional change-of-control
constraint.

## What would prove this wrong?

The positive interpretation would weaken if:

- AI search reduces commercial intent or advertiser pricing faster than new AI
  products replace the lost economics;
- Cloud revenue grows but gross margin and cash return decline as infrastructure
  and support costs rise;
- technical infrastructure capex remains near or above operating cash for years
  without a measurable increase in operating income and cash per share;
- Cloud backlog grows but converts slowly, is concentrated, or requires price
  concessions and capacity commitments that lower returns;
- useful-life assumptions, depreciation, or investment marks materially support
  earnings while replacement cash requirements rise;
- YouTube viewing grows while creator payments, content cost, or ad load weaken
  retained economics;
- antitrust or privacy remedies impair default distribution, data combination,
  ad measurement, or cross-product attachment;
- Other Bets and shared AI research consume rising amounts of profit without
  credible evidence of future revenue; or
- stock compensation, buybacks, and multi-class control leave common cash per
  share below the growth in total company cash.

## Valuation discipline

At the September 11, 2026 market snapshot, GOOGL was approximately $338.50 per
share and the market capitalization was about $4.140T. The finance feed showed a
trailing price/earnings ratio near 17, but that ratio is not enough for this
case because the company is entering a period of exceptional capital spending.

The valuation should use three linked cases:

- **Lower case:** Search remains profitable, but AI monetization is slower and
  a large share of cash is absorbed by infrastructure, leases, research, and
  legal costs.
- **Base case:** Search and YouTube remain durable, Cloud earns a good return,
  and AI infrastructure eventually increases operating income enough to restore
  cash after capex.
- **Higher case:** AI expands Search, Cloud, subscriptions, and enterprise
  software without materially reducing advertising economics, and the capital
  base earns returns above its financing and replacement cost.

The market price requires the base-to-higher case. The key sensitivity is not a
small change in the advertising multiple; it is whether the next $100B-plus of
technical infrastructure produces an acceptable incremental return. The model
must also give value to liquid investments without allowing their unrealized
gains to substitute for operating cash.

Alphabet has a cleaner starting cash engine than Amazon because Search and
YouTube require less physical fulfillment capacity. It has a similar emerging
problem: AI and Cloud require a much larger technical asset base. The key
difference from The Trade Desk is that Alphabet owns the consumer distribution
and much of the infrastructure; this increases control but also increases the
capital and regulatory burden.

## Final interpretation

Alphabet is not merely an advertising company adding AI. It is a mature cash
business choosing to become a large-scale infrastructure and enterprise-AI
provider. That choice may protect its distribution and create a second major
profit engine. It may also lower the quality of consolidated owner cash if
capital spending grows faster than the cash returns from new services.

The durable evidence is strong Search distribution, YouTube reach, technical
capability, cash generation, Cloud growth, and enterprise demand. The unresolved
evidence is the return on the new infrastructure, the effect of AI interfaces
on commercial search, the allocation of shared AI costs, and the effect of
regulatory remedies on distribution and data.

The next decisive evidence is not another AI adoption statistic. It is a
multi-year bridge from new infrastructure investment to Cloud and AI revenue,
operating income, depreciation, cash flow, and cash per diluted share.
