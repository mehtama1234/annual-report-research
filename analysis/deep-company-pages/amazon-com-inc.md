# Amazon.com, Inc. — a broad control system with a very large capital bill

Research date: 2026-09-13  
Evidence window: FY2025 and Q2 2026  
Ticker: AMZN  
Evidence grade: A for FY2025 and Q2 2026 reported financials; B for the
long-run profitability of newer initiatives, the allocation of shared
infrastructure, and the future return on AI-related investments.

## Bottom line

Amazon controls several connected relationships at once: consumer shopping,
third-party sellers, advertising, fulfillment, subscriptions, and cloud
computing. This is more important than the label “online retailer.” A customer
can enter through shopping, a seller can pay for access to demand and logistics,
an advertiser can pay for placement near purchase intent, and an enterprise can
buy computing capacity. Amazon reports each revenue stream separately, but the
operating system is partly shared.

The business produced $79.975B of operating income and $139.514B of operating
cash flow in FY2025. It also spent $128.320B on net cash property and equipment
and $3.841B on acquisitions and other investments. The resulting simple cash
screen was about $7.353B before debt principal, lease principal, and other
claims. Amazon's own free-cash-flow measure was $11.194B because it subtracts
net property-and-equipment purchases but does not subtract acquisitions or debt
principal.

The central forensic conclusion is therefore precise: Amazon has powerful
relationship control and a strong operating profit engine, but common-owner
cash depends on whether enormous technology, fulfillment, data-center, and AI
investment earns an adequate return. The clean economics are visible in AWS,
advertising, and seller services. The burden is visible in fulfillment,
inventory, leases, labor, legal exposure, stock compensation, and capital
spending. The consolidated result can look safer than any one investment
program because the businesses share infrastructure and cash resources.

This is not evidence of misconduct. It is a reason to value Amazon through
segment profit, cash after the full capital bill, investment marks, dilution,
and future return on capital rather than through revenue growth or headline
operating cash flow alone.

## What the company actually controls

The operating chain is:

`consumer need -> selection and availability -> purchase -> delivery and repeat use -> seller demand -> advertising demand -> infrastructure density`

AWS is a related but distinct chain:

`developer or enterprise workload -> cloud adoption -> usage and long-term contract -> infrastructure utilization -> recurring service revenue`

Amazon controls the customer interface, product selection, seller tools,
advertising placement, fulfillment network, delivery promise, cloud services,
and much of the data and software connecting those activities. It does not
control consumer budgets, seller survival, labor availability, delivery costs,
cloud customer spending, energy and equipment prices, regulators, or the return
on every new category it enters.

The useful economic distinction is between activity and retained economics.
Gross merchandise sold by a seller is activity flowing through the system;
third-party seller services revenue is the amount Amazon records for commissions
and related services. Advertising revenue is not the same as advertising
profit, because the company must maintain the shopping and measurement systems
that make the placement useful. AWS revenue is service revenue, but its margin
depends on utilization, pricing, power, servers, networking, depreciation, and
customer contracts.

## FY2025 reported baseline

Amounts are $ billions unless noted.

| Measure | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Net sales | 574.785 | 637.959 | 716.924 |
| Operating income | 36.852 | 68.593 | 79.975 |
| Net income | 30.425 | 59.248 | 77.670 |
| Operating cash flow | — | 115.877 | 139.514 |
| Net cash property/equipment spending | — | 77.658 | 128.320 |
| Acquisitions and other investment activity | — | 7.082 | 3.841 |
| Operating cash less the two listed uses | — | 31.137 | 7.353 |
| Amazon-defined free cash flow | — | 38.219 | 11.194 |

Operating income grew faster than sales from 2024 to 2025, but the cash left
after property spending fell sharply. This is not automatically negative:
Amazon may be investing for future AWS and fulfillment revenue. It does mean
the investor must prove that the investment is productive instead of treating
the current operating margin as the final economic result.

## Revenue mix and segment profit

Amazon's FY2025 revenue by disclosed product or service group was:

| Revenue group | FY2025 revenue | Change from 2024 | What it represents |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Online stores | $269.287B | 9% | Product and transactional digital-media sales recorded gross |
| Physical stores | $22.561B | 6% | Physical-store sales |
| Third-party seller services | $172.162B | 10% | Commissions, fulfillment/shipping fees, and other seller services |
| Advertising services | $68.635B | 22% | Sponsored, display, and video advertising |
| Subscription services | $49.619B | 12% | Prime and other digital subscriptions |
| AWS | $128.725B | 20% | Compute, storage, database, and related cloud services |
| Other | $5.935B | 9% | Including shipping, healthcare, licensing, and card arrangements |

The two reported segments that generated most FY2025 operating income were AWS
at $45.606B and North America at $29.619B. International generated $4.750B.
AWS represented about 18% of sales but about 57% of segment operating income;
that comparison is useful, but it is not a fully separated company valuation
because shared technology and infrastructure costs are allocated by usage.

Amazon states that the majority of technology and infrastructure costs are
allocated to AWS, while shared costs can support several offerings. This
allocation is reasonable for internal management, but it limits how precisely
an outside investor can assign all data-center and software investment to one
segment. The correct response is to use ranges and test consolidated returns,
not to assume that AWS owns every dollar of infrastructure profit.

## Cash conversion: what is real and what is timing

Amazon's operating cash flow is substantial and is tied to cash received from
consumers, sellers, developers, enterprises, content creators, and advertisers.
The 2025 bridge also shows important timing effects:

- Accounts payable supplied $11.231B of cash.
- Inventory used $3.002B of cash.
- Accounts receivable and other used $7.333B.
- Accrued expenses and other used $5.019B.
- Unearned revenue used $214M.

The accounts-payable contribution deserves special attention. Amazon states
that holiday sales usually make cash and accounts payable high at December 31,
and that payables generally decline in the first three months as vendors and
sellers are paid. A rough screen that removes the $11.231B payable benefit
would leave operating cash close to the $128.320B net cash property-spending
number, before acquisitions and other claims. This is not a normalized cash
flow calculation, because other working-capital items also change. It is a
clear reason to inspect the first quarter after year-end rather than treating
the December cash balance as permanent.

Amazon's definition of free cash flow is useful but limited. It excludes cash
acquisitions, debt principal, finance-lease principal, and some other claims.
The 10-K itself says free cash flow is not residual cash available for
discretionary spending. The archive therefore keeps both measures visible:

`operating cash flow - net cash property spending = company-reported FCF`

`operating cash flow - net property spending - acquisitions/other investments = broader cash screen`

Neither measure alone answers how much cash can be distributed. Fulfillment
capacity, data centers, lease commitments, and replacement spending can make a
large part of the investment recurring.

## The full capital burden

Cash property and equipment purchases were $131.819B in 2025, partly offset by
$3.499B of proceeds and incentives. Amazon says most cash capital spending went
to technology infrastructure, primarily AWS, and additional fulfillment
capacity. Property and equipment net on the balance sheet rose to $357.025B
from $252.665B in 2024.

The balance sheet also records economic commitments outside the single cash
capex line:

- Cash paid for operating leases was $15.038B.
- Assets acquired under operating leases were $19.930B.
- Property and equipment acquired under finance leases was $2.911B.
- Finance-lease principal repayments were $1.557B.
- Property and equipment acquired but not yet paid increased by $10.155B.
- Present-value lease liabilities were $101.538B, consisting of $89.252B of
  operating-lease liabilities and $12.286B of finance-lease liabilities.

These figures should not be added mechanically to the $128.320B cash-capex
measure; that would double count some obligations. They show why a
cash-capex-only valuation can understate the resources committed to the
fulfillment and data-center system. The next review should separate
maintenance, capacity growth, AWS server renewal, fulfillment automation,
lease-funded assets, and assets acquired but not yet paid.

## Earnings quality and possible financial-engineering traps

### Investment gains are not operating earnings

Amazon reported $17.336B of total non-operating income in 2025. Net income was
$77.670B, while operating income was $79.975B. The relationship looks benign
at the consolidated level because taxes and other items offset much of the
non-operating amount, but the investment result can still make net income less
representative of recurring operating performance. The operating-income and
cash-flow series are the better starting point.

The analyst must separate realized investment returns, unrealized changes,
equity-method activity, and cash received from the underlying businesses. The
2025 cash-flow reconciliation included $2.7B invested in Anthropic convertible
notes. Those investments can be strategically sensible, but their valuation
and accounting are not the same as cash earned by retail, advertising, or AWS.

### One-time operating charges may recur

FY2025 operating income included a $2.5B charge related to an FTC lawsuit
settlement and $2.7B of estimated severance costs, including $1.8B recorded in
Q3. The company also recorded approximately $1.3B of asset impairments, mainly
related to physical stores and operating leases.

These costs may be non-recurring in amount, but the underlying risks are not
necessarily one-time. Regulation, labor restructuring, store closures, and
asset underuse can return. The right adjustment is to show the charge, explain
why it occurred, and test its frequency; simply adding it back every year
would overstate cash economics.

### Depreciation and useful lives affect the story

Property-and-equipment depreciation and amortization was $41.9B in 2025.
Amazon changed an estimate for server and networking-equipment useful lives;
the change increased 2025 depreciation and amortization by $1.4B and reduced
net income by $1.0B, primarily in AWS. This change reduced current earnings,
so it is not evidence that management inflated 2025 profit. It does show that
useful-life assumptions materially affect reported AWS earnings and that future
replacement spending must be compared with accounting depreciation.

### Stock compensation and share count

Stock-based compensation expense was $19.467B in 2025, down from $22.011B in
2024. The diluted weighted-average share count rose from 10.721B in 2024 to
10.827B in 2025, and Amazon made no common-stock repurchases in 2023, 2024, or
2025. The lower stock-compensation expense is positive, but the share count
still matters when translating company growth into cash per owner share.

Stock compensation is a labor cost paid through ownership rather than cash
payroll. It can be appropriate for retaining technical staff, but it is not
free to existing owners. Track grants, vesting, dilution, and the value of
shares issued alongside operating cash flow.

## Balance-sheet resilience and liquidity

Cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities totaled $123.029B at
December 31, 2025, including $3.296B restricted. Long-term debt was $65.648B
on the balance sheet and face value of long-term debt was $68.836B. Amazon
also had present-value lease liabilities of $101.538B and future operating and
finance lease commitments.

The balance sheet is resilient in a simple liquidity sense: cash and liquid
securities are large relative to debt, and the business generates operating
cash. The more important risk is allocation. If AI and cloud capacity spending
continues to rise while retail and advertising cash is required to fund it,
the balance sheet may remain strong while common-owner returns weaken.

Q2 2026 makes that issue more immediate. Revenue was $200.606B, operating
income was $27.5B, and AWS revenue was $42.232B, up 37% year over year. For the
six months, cash capital expenditures were $96.3B and operating cash flow was
$71.419B. Amazon also reported $39.8B of acquisition and other investment
cash payments, including $28.7B invested in OpenAI preferred stock. The
trailing twelve-month measure showed $161.403B of operating cash flow against
$169.007B of net property spending, producing negative $7.604B of Amazon-
defined free cash flow before acquisition and investment cash.

The Q2 2026 data does not prove that the AI investment is poor. It does prove
that the valuation must include financing and investment needs while the
return evidence is still developing.

## Lyn Alden lens: liquidity, durability, and physical limits

Amazon has durable demand in convenience, essential goods, business software,
and cloud infrastructure. The durability is conditional rather than absolute.
Consumers can reduce discretionary purchases, sellers can leave or fail,
enterprises can reduce cloud usage, and regulators can limit how Amazon uses
its position across connected markets.

Liquidity matters through several channels. Higher rates can slow consumer and
business spending, raise the cost of data-center and fulfillment investment,
and reduce the value assigned to distant AI growth. At the same time, Amazon's
large cash balance and operating scale give it more financing flexibility than
smaller competitors.

The physical constraint is central: servers, networking equipment, power,
data centers, warehouses, transportation, labor, and leases are required to
turn demand into service. The company controls the interface and software, but
it does not eliminate the physical capital bill.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative “Amazon monetizes a large ecosystem” must be translated into
separate forecasts:

1. North America retail: unit growth, price, mix, fulfillment productivity,
   labor, and capital per incremental order.
2. International retail: local density, currency, country profitability,
   payment risk, and the path from scale to durable operating income.
3. Advertising: advertiser retention, conversion measurement, seller demand,
   privacy and regulatory limits, and incremental infrastructure cost.
4. Seller services: seller volume, commission rates, fulfillment adoption,
   payment losses, and seller dependence on Amazon.
5. AWS: usage, price, contract duration, utilization, depreciation, power,
   customer concentration, and AI workload economics.
6. Corporate capital: maintenance capex, growth capex, leases, strategic
   investments, debt, stock compensation, and share count.

The competitive-advantage period should not be one number for the whole group.
AWS, advertising, retail logistics, and subscriptions face different threats
and require different replacement spending. A consolidated model should show
which segment funds the others and whether that transfer remains economically
fair to common owners.

## Incentives and governance questions

The 10-K discloses a long-term focus on free cash flow and customer experience,
but the investor should inspect the proxy scorecard for the precise weighting
of operating income, sales, free cash flow, stock performance, strategic goals,
and capital allocation. A plan that rewards growth or adjusted measures can
encourage investment before returns are visible.

Jeff Bezos adopted a trading plan covering up to 15 million shares through
February 2027, and CEO Andy Jassy adopted a plan covering up to 142,224 shares
through December 2026. Trading plans are not evidence of improper conduct;
they are relevant to governance and ownership alignment because they affect
the practical ownership exposure of senior leaders.

## What would prove this wrong?

The current positive interpretation would weaken if:

- AWS growth requires steadily rising capex without higher return on invested
  capital or durable operating margin;
- advertising growth depends on weaker disclosure, forced seller spending, or
  rising measurement and regulatory costs;
- North America operating gains reverse when fulfillment wage, delivery, lease,
  or inventory costs normalize;
- accounts payable repeatedly supplies year-end cash that reverses in the next
  quarter;
- inventory, receivables, or seller-related payment losses grow faster than
  underlying activity;
- investment marks or equity-method gains become a major part of net income;
- OpenAI, Anthropic, or other strategic investments consume cash without a
  credible return or create additional funding obligations;
- lease-funded and unpaid property commitments rise faster than cash earnings;
- stock compensation and a rising share count absorb the benefit of operating
  growth; or
- antitrust, labor, privacy, tax, or consumer-protection remedies reduce the
  ability to combine consumer, seller, advertising, and fulfillment data or
  economics.

## Valuation discipline

At the September 11, 2026 market snapshot, AMZN was approximately $256.78 per
share with an equity value of about $2.800T. That value is roughly 20.7 times
FY2025 earnings, but the earnings multiple is not sufficient because 2025 net
income included material non-operating income and because 2026 investment
spending rose sharply.

The useful valuation cases are:

- **Lower case:** treat only a conservative portion of current operating cash
  as distributable after maintenance capex, leases, working-capital reversal,
  strategic investments, and dilution.
- **Base case:** give AWS and advertising continued growth, but charge a large
  recurring capital bill and require incremental returns above the cost of
  capital.
- **Higher case:** assume AI and cloud investment materially increase operating
  income and cash after the investment phase, while retail and advertising
  maintain their relationship control.

The market price requires the higher case to become credible. The valuation is
not disproved by negative Amazon-defined FCF in the latest twelve months, but
the burden of proof is high because the capital bill is now large enough to
absorb operating cash and require new debt or investment financing.

## Comparison bridge

Amazon should be compared with Shopify, eBay, and The Trade Desk by the
relationship controlled, not by a single “platform” label.

| Company | Relationship controlled | Main activity denominator | Retained economics | Main burden |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Amazon | Consumer demand, seller access, fulfillment, ads, cloud | Retail sales, seller services, ad sales, AWS usage | AWS, ads, seller services, subscriptions, and retail contribution | Data centers, fulfillment, leases, labor, inventory, legal, dilution |
| Shopify | Merchant workflow and payments | Merchant GMV | Subscription, merchant services, and payments spread | Payment risk, merchant credit, support, SBC, and platform investment |
| eBay | Buyer-seller discovery and trust | Marketplace GMV | Fees and advertising | Fraud, transaction losses, software, seller retention, and SBC |
| The Trade Desk | Buyer-side advertising decisions | Gross advertising spend | Platform revenue | Publisher/identity dependence, receivables, hosting, and SBC |

Amazon captures more layers but carries more physical and capital burden. The
cleaner economics of a narrower platform do not automatically make it safer;
the broader system may have stronger cross-subsidy capacity. The comparison
question is whether Amazon's additional control produces enough incremental
cash after the additional assets and obligations.

## Final interpretation

Amazon is a high-quality operating system with a low-quality shortcut: the
shortcut is to treat every connected revenue stream as if it has the same
margin, capital need, and risk. The filings do not support that simplification.

The durable part is the repeated relationship with consumers, sellers,
advertisers, developers, and enterprises. The uncertain part is the amount of
cash that remains after Amazon expands the physical and computing capacity that
supports those relationships. AWS and advertising make the economics cleaner;
fulfillment, leases, labor, working capital, legal exposure, strategic
investments, and dilution make the consolidated owner-cash question harder.

The next decisive evidence is not another revenue record. It is incremental
cash return on the 2025-2026 investment program, shown separately for AWS,
fulfillment, and strategic AI investments, with working-capital reversals and
share dilution kept visible.
