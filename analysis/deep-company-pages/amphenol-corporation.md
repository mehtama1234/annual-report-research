# Amphenol: Connector Content, Acquisition Compounding, and Tariff-Adjusted Cash

Date baseline: `2026-09-14`

Evidence base: Amphenol fiscal `2025` Form 10-K, fiscal `2025` Q4, fiscal `2026`
Q1 and Q2 filed materials, and the company packet. The dated scenario register
preserves a September 14, 2026 equity-value input; it is an expectations
screen, not a live quote or price target.

## Bottom line

Amphenol supplies connectors, fiber-optic interconnects, high-speed cables,
antennas, power-distribution systems, sensors, and related assemblies. These
products sit inside data centers, networks, vehicles, aircraft, defense
systems, and industrial equipment.

The control point is product content that is difficult to substitute after a
system has been designed, qualified, and deployed. A connector is not valuable
because it is visible to the end user. It is valuable when signal integrity,
power density, reliability, form factor, and qualification matter.

The operating record is strong. Fiscal 2025 sales were `$23.1B`, operating cash
flow was `$5.375B`, capital expenditures were `$996.6M`, and company-defined
free cash flow was `$4.393B`. The forensic contradiction is capital allocation:
Amphenol used `$3.819B` for acquisitions in 2025, after `$2.156B` in 2024 and
`$970M` in 2023. The business can generate large cash, but the per-share result
depends on whether repeated acquisitions create returns above their price and
integration cost.

Q2 2026 adds a second quality issue. Sales rose `55%` to `$8.758B`, organic
growth was `30%`, and adjusted operating margin was `29.8%`, but the quarter
included an `$80M` net benefit from recovery of IEEPA tariffs. The underlying
demand was strong; the reported margin should still be normalized for that
policy recovery.

## 1. What Amphenol controls

Amphenol controls the physical connection between components and systems. Its
products manage:

- electrical and optical signal transmission;
- power distribution and busbar connection;
- radio-frequency transmission and antennas;
- cable routing and assemblies; and
- sensing in vehicles, industrial systems, and equipment.

The customer dependency comes from design-in, qualification, reliability,
engineering support, and the risk of changing a component after a system has
been validated. The switching cost is strongest in high-speed data, aerospace,
defense, automotive, and industrial applications where a connection failure
can impair the whole system.

The control point is not universal. Generic low-specification connectors can be
replaceable. The investment case depends on the share of sales tied to
specification, engineering, qualification, and system performance.

## 2. Three-year cash and acquisition bridge

| Year | Operating cash flow | Capital expenditures | Acquisition cash | Diagnostic residual after capex and acquisitions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | `$2.529B` | `$372.8M` | `$970M` | `$1.186B` |
| 2024 | `$2.815B` | `$665.4M` | `$2.156B` | Approximately `($7M)` |
| 2025 | `$5.375B` | `$996.6M` | `$3.819B` | `$559M` |

The residual is a diagnostic rather than a company-defined free-cash-flow
measure. It charges acquisition cash against the year in which it was paid.
Acquisitions may create future value, but they are not maintenance capex and
should not disappear from an owner-cash analysis.

The three-year sequence shows why the company requires a cohort-based analysis.
Operating cash rose sharply, but acquisition spending also became a large use
of cash. The question is whether acquired businesses produce enough future
operating cash and margin to justify their price.

## 3. Current operating sequence

The packet reports:

- FY2025 sales of `$23.1B`, up `52%` reported and `38%` organically;
- FY2025 adjusted operating margin of `26.2%` and free cash flow of `$4.4B`;
- Q4 2025 sales of `$6.4B`, up `49%` reported and `37%` organically;
- Q1 2026 sales of `$7.620B`, up `58%`, with organic growth of `33%`;
- Q2 2026 sales of `$8.758B`, up `55%`, with organic growth of `30%`;
- Q2 orders of `$10.7B` and book-to-bill of `1.23:1`; and
- Q2 2026 operating cash flow of `$1.6B` and free cash flow of `$1.2B`.

CommScope CCS contributed about `$871.5M` of net sales from the acquisition date
through March 31, 2026. Management expected the acquired business to contribute
about `$4.6B` of 2026 sales and `$0.30` of adjusted diluted EPS accretion.

Reported growth must therefore be divided into organic growth, acquisition
revenue, price, mix, tariff recovery, and volume.

## 4. Earnings quality and financial engineering

### Tariff recovery

Q2 2026 included an `$80M` net benefit from recovery of IEEPA tariffs, or about
`$0.04` per share. The benefit may be legitimate compensation for prior costs.
It is not evidence of recurring product pricing power.

The normalized margin test is:

`reported operating income - tariff recovery - other policy benefits = underlying operating income`

The underlying quarter can still be excellent. The point is to prevent a
temporary recovery from being capitalized as a permanent margin improvement.

### Acquisition accounting

Amphenol's decentralized acquisition model is a central part of the strategy.
The company completed five acquisitions during 2025 and closed the CommScope
CCS transaction in January 2026.

The forensic acquisition bridge should track:

- purchase price and cash paid;
- acquired revenue and operating income;
- purchase accounting and amortization;
- integration cost and restructuring;
- acquired working capital and inventory;
- customer retention and cross-selling; and
- return on the incremental invested capital.

Adjusted EPS accretion is not the same as economic return. Accretion can result
from financing, timing, purchase accounting, or a comparison with a weak prior
period. The acquired business must earn an adequate return after integration,
capital spending, and dilution.

### Working capital and orders

Orders and book-to-bill provide visibility. They do not prove collected cash.
The next filings should reconcile orders to shipments, revenue, receivables,
inventory, customer deposits, and cash. A rising book-to-bill with weakening
collections or inventory turns would reduce earnings quality.

## 5. Alden lens: durability and liquidity

Amphenol benefits from broad electronics proliferation rather than one end
market. Datacom, autos, aerospace, defense, industrial systems, mobile devices,
and communications networks have different cycles.

Its liquidity is stronger than that of a carrier or plant-heavy commodity
producer, but acquisition spending creates a distinct financing risk. The main
stress points are:

- a slowdown in IT datacom and AI infrastructure;
- acquisition prices rising faster than returns;
- tariff and trade-policy reversals;
- customer concentration in large platforms;
- inventory and supplier commitments;
- margin pressure when high-growth mix normalizes; and
- debt or equity funding for acquisition activity.

The durability test is whether the company can maintain organic growth and
returns when acquisition activity slows and the datacom cycle becomes less
exceptional.

## 6. Damodaran lens: translate the content-density narrative

The narrative is that electronics density, AI, electrification, and defense
modernization increase Amphenol's content per system. The required numbers are:

1. organic growth by end market and product category;
2. content per system and price versus volume;
3. customer concentration and design-win durability;
4. gross and operating margin after tariff recoveries;
5. acquisition price, organic growth, and return by cohort;
6. capital expenditures needed for manufacturing capacity;
7. working capital, inventory, and orders-to-cash conversion;
8. SBC, diluted shares, debt, and acquisition financing; and
9. normalized owner cash per diluted share after acquisition spending.

The lifecycle question is whether Amphenol is still compounding through
fragmented electronics markets or whether acquisition competition and customer
scale will reduce returns on the next deal.

## 7. Control point versus burden carrier

| Layer | Amphenol captures | Amphenol carries |
|---|---|---|
| High-speed interconnect | Qualification, signal integrity, and design-in | R&D, quality, warranty, and customer-specific engineering |
| Power and busbar systems | Content from higher power density | Manufacturing capacity, metals, inventory, and price pressure |
| Aerospace and defense | Long qualification cycles and mission-critical reliability | Program timing, government budgets, compliance, and concentration |
| IT datacom | AI and bandwidth content growth | Hyperscaler cycle, supplier commitments, and customer bargaining power |
| Acquisition platform | New markets, customers, and product capability | Purchase price, integration, amortization, debt, and dilution |
| Diversification | Less dependence on one end market | Capital allocation complexity and mixed segment cycles |

Amphenol captures a cleaner economics layer than a carrier or field contractor,
but its acquisition program and manufacturing footprint are real burdens. The
best economics sit in qualified, specification-heavy content—not generic volume
alone.

## 8. Valuation readiness

The proper bridge is:

`equity value -> organic normalized owner cash -> acquisition returns -> SBC and dilution -> required growth and multiple`

Bear, base, and bull cases should vary organic growth, IT datacom demand,
tariff normalization, acquired-business returns, capex, working capital, debt,
SBC, and the cash multiple. A valuation based on adjusted EPS accretion or
book-to-bill alone would omit the cash cost and return risk of acquisitions.

### Dated valuation screen

The register records approximately `$108.2B` of equity value against bear,
base, and bull normalized owner-cash cases of `$1.8B`, `$3.0B`, and `$4.5B`,
valued at `25x`, `35x`, and `45x`. The implied values are `$45.0B`, `$105.0B`,
and `$202.5B`. The recorded value is near the base case, so the key question
is whether acquisition-led growth earns adequate returns after integration,
capex, working capital, and dilution.

## 9. Next-filing watchlist and thesis breakers

1. Does organic growth remain strong after CommScope revenue is separated?
2. Does adjusted margin hold after the `$80M` tariff recovery rolls off?
3. Does acquired revenue produce the promised `$0.30` EPS accretion without
   rising integration and amortization costs?
4. Do acquisitions earn returns above the purchase price and cost of capital?
5. Does book-to-bill convert into shipments, revenue, collections, and cash?
6. Do receivables and inventory grow slower than sales?
7. Does capex remain productive as IT datacom capacity expands?
8. Does customer concentration rise in the AI and hyperscale business?
9. Does SBC and acquisition financing reduce cash per diluted share?
10. Does organic growth persist if AI-network spending slows?

The thesis breaks if acquisition growth masks weak organic performance, tariff
recovery is mistaken for recurring margin, book-to-bill fails to convert, or
acquisitions consume the cash generated by the operating business.

## Conclusion

Amphenol is a strong physical-content control point. It monetizes the increase
in signal, power, antenna, sensor, and connection density across digital and
industrial systems.

Its economics are cleaner than those of a network owner because it does not fund
the entire system. They are less simple than pure software economics because
manufacturing, inventory, customer concentration, tariffs, and acquisitions
remain material. The next filings should be read for organic growth, normalized
margin, acquisition returns, and owner cash per diluted share—not the headline
sales growth or adjusted EPS accretion alone.
