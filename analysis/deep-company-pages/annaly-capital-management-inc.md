# Annaly Capital Management — Agency-MBS carry, repo leverage, and book-value risk

As of 2026-09-14. FY2025 figures are filed figures unless explicitly
identified otherwise; dollar amounts are in millions unless stated otherwise.

## How to read this memo

Annaly is a mortgage REIT. It buys mortgage assets, finances them, hedges
interest-rate and spread exposure, services some mortgage assets, and
distributes the remaining earnings and capital-market result to shareholders.

The central economic equation is:

`asset yield + dollar-roll and servicing income - repo and other funding cost - hedge cost - operating expense - losses and book-value change`

Agency guarantees reduce residential-credit risk. They do not remove rate,
spread, prepayment, funding, leverage, collateral, liquidity, or dilution risk.

GAAP net income is not the correct standalone denominator. Read net interest
income, economic net interest income, earnings available for distribution (EAD),
comprehensive income, book value per share, economic leverage, and dividends
together.

## Investment question

The question is whether Annaly can earn a sustainable return on common equity
after the funding and hedge structure is fully charged, while preserving book
value and avoiding dilutive equity issuance.

Annaly has a liquid Agency-MBS platform and a mortgage-servicing business. Its
advantage is access to collateral, trading scale, financing relationships,
hedging expertise, and portfolio management. Its disadvantage is that a small
change in rates, mortgage spreads, prepayments, repo haircuts, or hedge
effectiveness can overwhelm a quarter of coupon carry.

the underlying asset coupon remains unchanged.

## What Annaly controls

Annaly controls four linked decisions:

1. **Asset selection:** Agency MBS, mortgage servicing rights, residential
   mortgage loans, credit-risk-transfer assets, and other housing-finance
   exposures.
2. **Funding:** repo counterparties, secured financing, maturities, collateral,
   haircuts, and the amount of leverage.
3. **Risk transfer:** swaps, swaptions, Treasury positions, TBA derivatives,
   and other hedges that shape duration and basis risk.
4. **Capital allocation:** dividends, ATM equity issuance, portfolio growth,
   deleveraging, and share repurchases.

The company does not control the Federal Reserve, mortgage borrower
prepayments, repo-market liquidity, mortgage spreads, or the behavior of hedge
counterparties. These external variables determine whether the portfolio
manager's decisions produce durable common returns.

## Spread income is not owner cash

Net interest income can be positive while comprehensive income and book value
fall. A mortgage REIT receives coupon and financing spread, but the financed
securities can decline in value when rates or mortgage spreads move. Hedges can
offset some of that change and create their own gains or losses.

The correct sequence is:

- measure asset yield and dollar-roll income;
- subtract economic funding cost, including the swap component;
- measure servicing and operating income;
- include derivative and investment marks;
- measure book-value movement per share;
- add dividends and subtract dilution.

The resulting economic return is the shareholder result. Dividend coverage is
only one part of it.

## Repo leverage and liquidity

At June 30, 2026, Annaly had $86.9B of repurchase agreements with a weighted
average rate of 3.85% and weighted average remaining maturity of 33 days. The
company reported a roughly 3% weighted-average repo haircut and $90.2B of
financial assets and cash pledged against liabilities.

Short repo maturity against longer-duration mortgage assets creates rollover
and margin risk. A lender can require additional collateral, reduce financing,
or refuse to renew. The company can be forced to sell assets at an unfavorable
price even when the eventual mortgage cash flows remain intact.

Track repo maturity, weighted funding rate, haircuts, unencumbered assets,
cash, collateral calls, counterparty concentration, and the amount of risk
that can be reduced without crystallizing a book-value loss.

## Hedges, duration, and prepayments

Hedges reduce rate exposure but do not eliminate risk. Their effectiveness
depends on duration, convexity, mortgage basis, collateral, counterparty terms,
and the relationship between the hedge instrument and the mortgage asset.

Prepayments change the risk dynamically. When rates fall, borrowers may
refinance and return principal sooner, reducing the period during which Annaly
earns the asset yield and forcing reinvestment at lower yields. When rates rise,
prepayments can slow and the asset duration can extend while repo funding still
requires renewal.

The next filing must be read for prepayment speed, asset duration, hedge
coverage, swap and Treasury positions, TBA exposure, mortgage spreads, and
premium amortization. Stable leverage does not mean stable risk if duration or
prepayment behavior changes.

## EAD, dividend, and book value

For Q2 2026, EAD was $0.79 per average common share and the common dividend was
$0.75. This was the ninth consecutive quarter in which EAD exceeded the
dividend, according to management. That supports current payout coverage.

The dividend must still be compared with:

- economic net interest income after funding and hedges;
- realized and unrealized book-value changes;
- common shares issued through ATM programs;
- leverage and liquidity needs;
- asset and hedge reinvestment requirements.

A dividend can be covered for a period while the economic value per share
declines. Conversely, a quarter with weak GAAP income can preserve or improve
book value through favorable hedge and spread movements. The shareholder owns
both the distribution and the residual capital.

## Capital allocation and dilution

Annaly raised approximately $447M of common equity through ATM sales during Q2
2026. Shares outstanding increased from 707.0M at December 31, 2025 to 750.6M
at June 30, 2026. New equity can be accretive when issued above book value and
used to acquire assets at attractive returns. It can transfer value from
existing holders when issued below book value or when the incremental portfolio
does not earn its funding and hedge cost.

The company also has a $1.5B common-stock repurchase authorization through
2029, but no shares were repurchased during the first half of 2026. The
relevant test is not the authorization size. It is whether management buys
below a defensible book-value estimate while the balance sheet can support the
purchase without reducing liquidity.

## Lyn Alden, Damodaran, and forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** Annaly is directly exposed to rates, liquidity, housing
  finance, repo funding, collateral haircuts, and central-bank policy. A
  tightening can raise funding cost and reduce asset value at the same time.
  A falling-rate environment can improve financing conditions but increase
  prepayment and reinvestment risk.
- **Damodaran lens:** value normalized spread income and book value separately.
  The required return is a return on tangible equity after funding, hedging,
  operating expense, expected book-value volatility, and dilution. A current
  dividend is not a perpetuity unless the underlying spread survives.
- **Forensic lens:** reconcile EAD to GAAP income, comprehensive income, book
  value, derivative marks, premium amortization, TBA income, repo funding,
  ATM issuance, and dividends. Do not treat favorable marks as recurring
  spread or leverage as a harmless scale variable.

## Valuation framing

Annaly should be valued with two linked methods:

1. **Book-value method:** estimate normalized tangible book value per share and
   apply a discount or premium based on asset quality, leverage, hedge quality,
   management, liquidity, and expected return on equity.
2. **Distribution method:** estimate normalized EAD after funding, hedges,
   operating expense, and dilution, then apply a payout multiple consistent
   with rate, spread, and funding volatility.

The methods should converge. A high dividend yield with a persistent book-value
decline is not a high-return investment. A temporary book-value gain produced
by favorable marks does not establish a stable distribution stream.

## Evidence grades and thesis breakers

| Question | Current grade | What would upgrade or weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agency liquidity and portfolio control | B+ | Stable returns through changing rate and spread conditions |
| Current dividend coverage | B+ | EAD remains above dividend without leverage or dilution escalation |
| Funding resilience | B- | Repo access, haircuts, and collateral remain stable in stress |
| Hedge quality | B- | Book value remains protected across rate and basis moves |
| Sustainable spread | B- | Economic NII survives higher repo cost and prepayment changes |
| Per-share compounding | C+ | Book value and dividends grow after ATM issuance |
| Capital allocation | C+ | Equity issuance is accretive and repurchases occur below intrinsic book value |

Thesis breakers:

1. Economic return turns negative repeatedly even while management reports
   positive carry.
2. Repo haircuts, rates, maturities, or counterparty access deteriorate.
3. Hedge losses fail to offset mortgage-spread or duration losses.
4. Prepayments accelerate and force lower-yield reinvestment.
5. EAD falls below the dividend and management uses leverage or equity issuance
   to preserve the payout.
6. Book value per share declines after repeated ATM issuance.
7. Preferred claims, secured financing, or collateral requirements reduce the
   common equity buffer.
8. The portfolio expands while return on common equity falls after all funding,
   hedge, and operating costs.
