# Applied Materials: materials-engineering control point with cycle, China, and customer concentration

## Bottom line

Applied Materials sells equipment used in semiconductor manufacturing and
services the installed base. Its economic value comes from qualified process
steps: deposition, etch, rapid thermal processing, chemical mechanical
planarization, metrology, inspection, packaging, and ion implantation. A
customer does not buy only a machine; it qualifies a process, tool, recipe,
service response, and expected yield result.

That supports a strong control-point thesis, but it does not make the company
cycle-proof. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $28.5B and cash from operations was
$8.0B, but capital spending rose to $2.26B. In the first nine months of fiscal
2026, operating cash flow was $5.57B and capital spending was $1.99B. The
period also benefited from a $698M increase in contract liabilities, while
China represented 30% of fiscal 2025 revenue and two customers represented
about 34% of the first nine months of fiscal 2026 revenue. The correct
question is whether future owner cash remains strong after a semiconductor
capital-spending downturn, export restrictions, customer concentration, and
the continuing cost of research and process qualification.

## How to read the company

`fab process requirement -> Applied tool and recipe -> customer qualification -> wafer output and yield -> installed-base service -> cash`

Applied has two principal segments:

- **Semiconductor Systems:** capital equipment used in chip fabrication. In
  fiscal 2025 it produced $20.798B, or 73% of revenue. Its fiscal 2025 mix was
  67% foundry, logic, and other; 26% DRAM; and 7% NAND.
- **Applied Global Services:** services, spare parts, and factory-automation
  software for the installed base. It produced about $6.4B in fiscal 2025.
  The 200mm equipment business moved into Semiconductor Systems in fiscal 2026,
  so segment comparisons need that reporting change understood.

The company does not control semiconductor manufacturers' fab budgets, memory
pricing, export policy, or customer technology choices. It controls technical
equipment, process knowledge, installed-base support, and the ability to help
customers achieve yield and throughput targets.

## Operating evidence

| Fiscal year ended October | Revenue | Net income | Operating cash flow | Capex | OCF less capex |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2023 | $26.517B | $6.856B | $8.700B | $1.106B | $7.594B |
| 2024 | $27.176B | $7.177B | $8.677B | $1.190B | $7.487B |
| 2025 | $28.368B | $6.998B | $7.958B | $2.260B | $5.698B |

Fiscal 2025 revenue increased 4%, but operating cash flow declined because of
higher income-tax payments and inventory. The cash-after-capex screen fell
more sharply because the company was investing in capacity and facilities.
This is not automatically poor capital allocation: the question is whether
the additional capacity produces durable tool demand and service economics.

For the nine months ended July 26, 2026, revenue was $24.037B, operating cash
flow was $5.568B, and capital spending was $1.988B. Semiconductor Systems
revenue was $18.146B, up 10% from the comparable period; AGS revenue was
$5.005B, up 18%. Segment operating margins were 34.0% for Semiconductor
Systems and 29.2% for AGS. The third quarter alone had $9.115B of revenue,
including $7.040B from Semiconductor Systems and $1.781B from AGS.

## Earnings quality and forensic review

### 1. High margins reflect real technical content, but not a guaranteed floor

Fiscal 2025 Semiconductor Systems operating margin was 35.5% and AGS margin
was 28.1%. In the first nine months of fiscal 2026 those margins were 34.0%
and 29.2%. Management attributed the improvement to volume, product mix,
average selling prices, material and manufacturing costs, and stronger service
and spare-parts demand, partly offset by higher research and development.

The test is whether margin remains high when leading-edge demand slows, older
node demand weakens, customer mix changes, or export restrictions remove
available sales. A margin decline should be separated into price, mix,
utilization, manufacturing cost, warranty, service cost, and R&D rather than
described simply as semiconductor cyclicality.

### 2. Operating cash flow is positive, but contract liabilities need tracing

The nine-month 2026 cash-flow statement included a $698M increase in contract
liabilities. Contract liabilities rose from $2.566B at October 26, 2025 to
$3.271B at July 26, 2026. The company recognized approximately $1.8B of
revenue during the period from opening contract liabilities.

Advance billings and customer payments are legitimate cash sources. They are
not the same as permanently earned profit. The follow-up test is whether the
liability balance converts into revenue and gross profit without rising faster
than shipments, whether technical sign-off delays create contract assets, and
whether customer deposits reverse if tools are delayed or orders change.

### 3. Revenue timing has different risk by segment

Semiconductor Systems revenue is recognized at a point in time. AGS revenue
for tangible goods is also generally point-in-time, while service agreements
are recognized over time; most of that over-time revenue is recognized within
12 months. This means quarterly results can be affected by tool shipment,
customer acceptance, service start dates, and the movement of 200mm equipment
between segments.

Two customers represented approximately 19% and 15% of fiscal 2025 revenue.
For the first nine months of fiscal 2026, two customers represented about 20%
and 14%. No other customer exceeded 10% in that 2026 period. A large customer
can therefore affect revenue, pricing, receivables, inventory commitments, and
capacity utilization at the same time.

### 4. Inventory and technology transitions can create delayed losses

At July 26, 2026 inventory included $1.866B of customer-service spares,
$2.323B of raw materials, $1.191B of work in process, and $1.184B of finished
goods. This stock supports service availability and manufacturing lead times,
but it also carries obsolescence risk when process generations, customer
orders, or export rules change.

The filing specifically warns that inventory bought for demand that does not
materialize, or equipment made obsolete by rapid technology change, can create
excess and obsolete-inventory charges and underutilized production capacity.
The review should compare inventory growth with revenue, orders, tool
shipments, service growth, and write-downs across a full semiconductor cycle.

### 5. SBC is material to owner cash

Fiscal 2025 share-based compensation was $660M, up from $577M in 2024 and
$490M in 2023. The first nine months of fiscal 2026 recorded $545M. As of
July 26, 2026, $1.2B of unrecognized compensation expense remained, to be
recognized over a weighted average of 2.7 years.

The owner test is:

`OCF - capex - economic cost of continuing dilution -> per-share cash`

Buybacks must be evaluated against shares issued through equity plans and the
price paid. A reduction in share count does not make the compensation cost
irrelevant.

## Alden lens: strategic necessity inside a liquidity cycle

The durable part of Applied's position is technical qualification and the
economic cost of poor yield, low throughput, or delayed process adoption. The
cyclical part is that the customer must first fund a fab, equipment upgrade,
memory transition, or advanced-packaging line.

The principal macro risks are:

- semiconductor capital-expenditure reductions after a capacity buildout;
- memory and foundry/logic mix changes;
- export licensing, tariffs, and retaliatory measures affecting China and
  other locations;
- customer concentration and customer-specific order changes;
- supply-chain and inventory commitments; and
- the need to maintain high R&D, field engineering, and service capability.

China revenue was $8.529B, or 30% of fiscal 2025 revenue, down from 37% in
2024. The 10-K says export licensing is uncertain and can limit sales,
displace Applied with domestic or foreign competitors, and make forecasting
more difficult. The 2026 10-Q repeats that the rules are evolving. This is a
direct business variable, not a distant geopolitical footnote.

## Damodaran lens: convert the story into normalized cash

The valuation chain is:

`installed process position -> tool placements -> customer utilization and technology transitions -> AGS service/spares -> R&D and facility reinvestment -> owner cash`

The fiscal 2025 cash-after-capex screen was $5.698B, versus $7.594B in 2023
and $7.487B in 2024. The first nine months of fiscal 2026 produced $3.580B
after capex. The valuation should not capitalize the 2023-24 level without
testing whether the lower 2025 result reflects temporary investment or a
normalized increase in capital intensity.

At the September 14, 2026 market snapshot, market capitalization was about
$343.2B. A simple screen therefore values the equity at roughly 60 times
fiscal 2025 cash after capex. That is not a price target. It says the market
requires sustained growth, high margins, strong AGS conversion, and successful
technology transitions while accepting China and customer concentration risk.

Illustrative equity-value cases:

| Normalized owner cash | Multiple | Implied equity value |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $4.5B bear | 25x | $112.5B |
| $5.8B base | 30x | $174.0B |
| $7.2B bull | 35x | $252.0B |

The base case is deliberately below the current market value. To justify the
current price, the analysis needs either a materially higher sustainable cash
level, a longer growth runway than these cases assume, or a lower required
return. The next step is a reverse valuation using revenue growth, segment
margins, R&D, capex, working capital, SBC, and the terminal multiple.

## Possible financial-engineering questions

These are tests, not accusations:

- Are advance billings and contract-liability growth being treated as durable
  cash without testing future delivery and acceptance obligations?
- Do customer concentration and point-in-time tool revenue make quarter-end
  shipment timing unusually important?
- Are inventory and customer-service spares rising ahead of demand and later
  producing write-downs?
- Does capital spending remain elevated after the current facility buildout,
  reducing owner cash below the reported free-cash-flow narrative?
- Does SBC and the unrecognized award balance offset buybacks on a per-share
  basis?
- Are China restrictions, legal settlements, or product reconfiguration costs
  being treated as one-time events when the policy environment is recurring?

## What would prove this wrong

Downgrade the control-point thesis if future filings show that:

1. Semiconductor Systems revenue and margin fall sharply with a normal fab
   spending slowdown and AGS does not provide meaningful support.
2. AGS growth depends mainly on new tool placements rather than a durable,
   productive installed base.
3. Contract liabilities grow while revenue conversion, collections, or gross
   profit weaken.
4. Inventory, receivables, or customer-specific commitments rise faster than
   revenue and produce recurring write-downs.
5. R&D, field service, and facility capex consume most incremental gross
   profit.
6. China restrictions remove a large product set or force structurally lower
   margins, while domestic competitors gain capability.
7. The two largest customers reduce purchases or demand materially better
   commercial terms.
8. Share issuance offsets buybacks and normalized cash per share fails to grow.
