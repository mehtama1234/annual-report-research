# Arista Networks: AI-Fabric Control, Deferred Revenue, and Hyperscaler Concentration

Date baseline: `2026-09-14`

Evidence base: Arista fiscal `2025` Form 10-K, fiscal `2025` Q4, fiscal `2026`
Q1 and Q2 results materials, and the company packet. The fiscal 2025 cash-flow
figures come from the filed annual cash-flow statement. The dated scenario
register preserves a September 14, 2026 equity-value input; it is an
expectations screen, not a live quote or price target.

## Bottom line

Arista sells Ethernet switching, routing, network operating software, and
network-management tools used in cloud, data-center, campus, WAN, and AI-fabric
environments. Its control point is the architecture that determines how large
volumes of data move between servers, storage, accelerators, and users.

The business has unusually strong reported economics for a networking company.
Fiscal 2025 revenue was `$9.006B`, GAAP net income was `$3.511B`, operating cash
flow was `$4.372B`, and purchases of property, equipment, and intangible assets
were `$119.5M`. Fiscal 2025 operating cash flow therefore exceeded reported
capital purchases by approximately `$4.252B` before acquisitions and other
investing activity.

The forensic contradiction is that the cash result was supported by a `$2.452B`
increase in deferred revenue, while accounts receivable used `$746.4M`, inventory
used `$412.5M`, and other assets used `$937.4M`. Deferred revenue is cash or
billings received before all contracted service is delivered. The cash is real,
but it creates future service obligations. Arista is a cleaner control-point
case than Ciena on capital intensity, but its cash quality still needs working-
capital and customer-concentration analysis.

## 1. What Arista controls

Arista controls a network architecture through three connected layers:

- switching and routing hardware that directs traffic;
- EOS, its network operating software; and
- CloudVision, observability, automation, and network-management tools.

The customer dependency is created by design qualification, operating software,
network configuration, telemetry, staff training, and the risk of changing the
architecture of a live data center. A customer can buy alternative hardware,
but replacing a qualified fabric can require testing, topology changes,
software migration, operational retraining, and performance risk.

The control point is stronger when Arista governs a large installed fabric and
when the software and telemetry are used across the network. It is weaker if
customers treat switches as interchangeable hardware or if hyperscalers change
architecture and supplier mix.

## 3. Current operating sequence

The packet reports:

- Q4 2025 revenue of `$2.488B`, with GAAP EPS of `$0.75`;
- Q1 2026 revenue of `$2.709B`, with operating cash flow of `$1.69B`;
- Q2 2026 revenue of `$3.036B`, up `37.7%` year over year;
- Q2 2026 non-GAAP operating margin of `49.9%`; and
- cumulative shipments of `150M` ports by the end of 2025.

These results support a strong AI-network demand case. They do not prove that
the current growth rate will persist through a hyperscaler spending pause or a
change in network architecture.

## 4. Earnings quality and cash conversion

### Deferred revenue

The 2025 cash-flow statement shows a `$2.452B` increase in deferred revenue.
This was a major contributor to operating cash flow. Deferred revenue is not a
bad signal: it can show customer prepayment and strong contract demand. It does
mean that current cash includes payment for service, support, or other promises
that Arista must fulfill later.

The next filings should show whether deferred revenue continues to convert into
recognized revenue and gross profit without rising cancellation, concession,
service, or warranty cost.

### Working capital

Fiscal 2025 operating assets consumed cash:

- accounts receivable used `$746.4M`;
- inventory used `$412.5M`; and
- other assets used `$937.4M`.

This pattern is consistent with rapid growth and increased operating scale. It
also means cash conversion is not simply “high margin equals high cash.” The
analyst must separate customer prepayment from cash tied up in receivables,
inventory, prepayments, and other operating assets.

### Stock-based compensation

SBC was `$439.2M` in fiscal 2025, up from `$355.4M` in 2024 and `$296.8M` in
2023. SBC is non-cash when recorded, but it transfers value through employee
shares or options and can require buybacks to limit dilution.

The owner-cash test is:

`reported operating cash -> capital purchases -> acquisitions -> SBC and dilution -> cash per diluted share`

Arista's high operating cash generation remains strong after this test, but the
per-share result depends on diluted shares and repurchase policy rather than
reported cash alone.

### Acquisition spending

The `$300M` fiscal 2025 business combination may strengthen observability,
security, or network capability. It should be analyzed separately from required
capital spending. An acquisition creates value only if the acquired product
improves customer retention, cross-sell, margin, or network-control depth after
integration cost.

## 5. Alden lens: durability and liquidity

Arista benefits from durable data traffic, AI-fabric requirements, cloud
infrastructure, and the need to operate increasingly complex networks. Its
liquidity position is stronger than a carrier's because it does not finance
spectrum, nationwide access, or a large property portfolio.

The main durability risks are concentration and cycle timing:

- hyperscaler customers may represent a large share of revenue;
- a customer can change network architecture or supplier allocation;
- AI-network spending can be paused or re-timed;
- inventory and supplier commitments can become excessive during a cycle peak;
- product transitions can create obsolete equipment; and
- software and SBC investment must continue even when shipments slow.

The stress test is whether Arista can preserve gross margin, customer
qualification, and owner cash through a period of slower hyperscaler spending.

## 6. Damodaran lens: translate the AI-network narrative

The narrative is that Arista controls the AI-fabric and client-to-cloud
architecture. The required numbers are:

1. revenue growth from hyperscale, enterprise, campus, routing, and software;
2. customer concentration and customer-specific architecture dependence;
3. port shipments, product mix, pricing, and gross margin;
4. EOS and CloudVision adoption, renewal, and incremental margin;
5. deferred revenue conversion and contract terms;
6. receivables, inventory, supplier commitments, and obsolescence;
7. R&D required to maintain switching and routing leadership;
8. SBC, diluted shares, and buybacks; and
9. normalized owner cash per diluted share relative to market value.

The lifecycle question is whether Arista is still gaining share in a long AI-
network buildout or is approaching a period where very high margins attract
competition and customer bargaining power increases.

## 7. Control point versus burden carrier

| Layer | Arista captures | Arista carries |
|---|---|---|
| Network architecture | Qualification, software embedment, and switching design | Customer concentration and architecture change |
| EOS and CloudVision | Automation, observability, and installed workflow | R&D, support, security, and product-renewal cost |
| AI-fabric demand | High growth and high-margin system sales | Hyperscaler cycle, pricing pressure, and supply commitments |
| Asset-light model | Low property and capital-purchase burden | Inventory, receivables, outsourced manufacturing, and warranty |
| Recurring cash | Customer prepayment and deferred revenue | Future service and support obligations |
| Capital allocation | Cash available for acquisitions and repurchases | SBC, acquisition integration, and dilution |

Arista captures a cleaner network-control layer than a telecom carrier or
data-center landlord. It still carries hardware-cycle and customer-concentration
risk that a pure software company would not.

## 8. Valuation readiness

The scenario register preserves an approximately `$254.7B` equity-value
snapshot for this valuation screen. The proper bridge is:

`equity value -> normalized owner cash -> required cash yield -> AI-network growth, margin, reinvestment, and dilution required`

| Case | Normalized owner-cash screen | Applied multiple | Implied equity value | What the price requires |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bear | `$2.5B` | `35x` | `$87.5B` | Hyperscaler spending pauses, deferred revenue normalizes, working capital and SBC remain meaningful, and margins fall from current levels |
| Base | `$4.0B` | `50x` | `$200.0B` | AI-fabric growth continues, EOS/CloudVision deepen the installed workflow, and cash per diluted share grows despite concentration and reinvestment |
| Bull | `$5.5B` | `65x` | `$357.5B` | Arista sustains exceptional growth and margins, expands beyond its largest customers, and converts growth into cash without material dilution or inventory loss |

The snapshot sits above the base case in this screen and below the bull case.
That does not make the stock automatically unattractive; it states the burden
of proof. A valuation based only on the `49.9%` Q2 non-GAAP margin would ignore
the possibility that product mix, customer concentration, deferred-revenue
timing, working capital, or the AI cycle changes. The scenario register is a
screen, not a target price.

## 9. Next-filing watchlist and thesis breakers

1. Does revenue remain diversified beyond a small number of hyperscalers?
2. Does deferred revenue convert into revenue and cash without rising credits?
3. Do receivables and inventory grow slower than revenue over a full cycle?
4. Does inventory obsolescence or supplier commitment risk rise after AI demand
   expectations change?
5. Does gross margin remain strong when product mix normalizes?
6. Does EOS and CloudVision adoption increase recurring economics rather than
   only support hardware sales?
7. Does SBC decline as a percentage of revenue, with diluted shares controlled?
8. Do acquisitions produce measurable customer and cash returns?
9. Does operating cash remain strong after deferred-revenue timing reverses?
10. Does owner cash per diluted share grow through a hyperscaler capex pause?

The thesis breaks if customer concentration rises while architecture changes,
deferred revenue reverses, receivables and inventory consume cash, margins rely
on temporary product mix, or SBC absorbs the increase in owner cash.

## Conclusion

Arista is one of the strongest control-point companies in the data-movement
cohort. It captures network architecture, operating software, and observability
without owning the full access network or data-center property.

Its reported cash economics are unusually strong, but the cash bridge contains
important qualifications: deferred revenue supported operating cash, working
capital consumed cash, SBC was substantial, and customer concentration creates
cycle risk. The investment case is therefore cleaner than Ciena's physical and
inventory burden, but it still requires proof that AI-fabric demand becomes
durable cash per diluted share across the cycle.
