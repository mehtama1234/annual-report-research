# Arthur J. Gallagher & Co. Full Forensic Analysis Memo

Date baseline: 2026-09-13  
Primary source: [Gallagher 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354190/000162828026008662/ajg-20251231.htm)  
Companion packet: [Gallagher company packet](/home/mehtama1/git-repo/annual-report-research/extracted/financial/insurance-brokers/arthur-j-gallagher-co/company-packet.md)

## Bottom line

Gallagher is the important contradiction to Aon and Marsh: it owns the same
high-value insurance-broker and risk-management workflow, but its growth model
is materially more acquisition-dependent. FY2025 revenue was $13.942B and
operating cash flow was $1.930B, while the filing reports $15.766B of cash paid
for acquisitions net of cash and restricted cash acquired. Goodwill acquired
during the year was $9.904B. The company also reported 6% organic fee growth.

The attractive business is the recurring client, producer, renewal, and risk-
management relationship. The forensic question is whether the shareholder
return is being created by organic compounding or by repeatedly issuing debt,
equity, and cash to buy revenue. A 6% organic-growth figure is meaningful, but
it does not make the acquisition program maintenance-free.

## What Gallagher controls

`client risk problem -> brokerage / risk-management advice -> placement and
renewal workflow -> recurring fee and commission relationship`

Gallagher's control point is distribution, expertise, producer relationships,
and the operational workflow around risk. It does not primarily warehouse
underwriting claims like Chubb. That makes its capital burden structurally
lighter than a carrier's, but the roll-up adds a different burden: acquisition
cash, integration, earnouts, goodwill, acquired intangible amortization, debt,
and the need to retain acquired producers and clients.

| 2025 | 13.942 | 1.930 | 0.145 | 15.766 | 0.667 | 9.904 |

The 2025 residual after capex and acquisitions is approximately **negative
$13.981B**. That is not a claim that the operating business destroyed value;
it is the correct reminder that Gallagher's growth capital was extraordinary
in 2025, primarily because AssuredPartners closed. It also means a simple
OCF multiple would be misleading unless the model separately estimates the
incremental cash earnings and return on the acquired platform.

The filing says 33 acquisitions closed in 2025, with approximately $3.562B of
annualized acquired revenue. That revenue run-rate is not the same as cash
earnings: integration cost, producer compensation, client retention, working
capital, interest, and amortization must be tested.

## Earnings quality and possible presentation risks

### Organic growth versus acquired growth

The reported 6% organic fee growth is the strongest evidence that the platform
has a living operating engine. The next test is whether organic growth remains
positive after the AssuredPartners comparison period, and whether it is driven
by client retention, rate/exposure, new business, pricing, and producer
productivity rather than simply market-wide insurance pricing.

### Acquisition accounting is not misconduct, but it is the central risk

Goodwill rose to $22.593B and net amortizable intangible assets to $10.684B at
year-end. Those balances are normal consequences of a broker roll-up; they are
also where future impairment, amortization, and return-on-acquisition risk sit.
The company should be judged on cash return on acquired revenue and on per-share
value after debt and equity consideration, not on adjusted EBITDA alone.

### Cash-flow denominator risk

2025 operating cash flow includes $1.122B of depreciation and amortization,
$148M of deferred-compensation and restricted-stock amortization, $49M of
stock-based and other noncash compensation, and a $491M payment of acquisition
earnouts in excess of original estimates. Those are not all recurring owner
cash adjustments. The cash-flow bridge must retain earnouts, integration spend,
producer retention, interest, and acquisition funding as real claims.

### Balance-sheet and dilution risk

Long-term debt was $12.873B. Gallagher issued common stock as part of
acquisition consideration, and the filing says it may use cash, new debt,
credit facilities, or common stock for future deals. That flexibility is useful
in a stressed market, but it can turn growth into a transfer from existing
shareholders if the acquired cash return is below the cost of capital.

## Alden macro/liquidity lens

The favorable macro case is rising institutional risk complexity: cyber,
catastrophe, regulation, benefits, supply-chain exposure, and demand for
outsourced risk expertise. Higher rates can also increase the value of advice
around capital structure and risk financing.

The offset is corporate budget sensitivity and financing cost. A recession can
slow new business, reduce exposures and premiums, lengthen receivable
collections, and raise producer-retention pressure. Higher rates make the
acquisition hurdle more demanding. Gallagher's liquidity outcome therefore
depends less on underwriting float than on whether recurring fee cash can fund
debt service and a normalized acquisition program.

## Damodaran lens

The correct model is a two-stage operating and reinvestment model:

`organic fee growth -> margin after producer/talent cost -> tax/capex/working
capital -> acquisition investment -> incremental acquired cash earnings ->
debt, dilution, and owner cash`

The key input is not the headline revenue CAGR. It is the return on incremental
acquisition capital after earnouts, integration, amortization, financing, and
shares issued. Gallagher can deserve a premium multiple if the acquired
relationships compound organically and returns remain high. It deserves a
lower multiple if reported growth requires increasingly large deals or if
goodwill grows faster than per-share cash earnings.

## Valuation expectation and thesis breakers

At the September 11, 2026 market snapshot, AJG was approximately $240.32 per
share with an equity value near $61.594B. The market is therefore capitalizing
the future acquired platform, not merely the negative FY2025 residual. A
reasonable scenario range should use normalized post-AssuredPartners cash,
not FY2025 OCF and not a mechanical negative value.

| Case | Normalized owner cash | Multiple | Implied equity value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | $1.1B | 24x | $26.4B |
| Base | $1.7B | 30x | $51.0B |
| Bull | $2.3B | 36x | $82.8B |

The base case is below the synchronized market value and requires evidence that
AssuredPartners converts into durable organic fee cash.

## Q2 2026 current forensic checkpoint

Q2 2026 reported total company revenue was about `$3.955B` and adjusted
revenue about `$3.947B`; adjusted diluted EPS was about `$2.84`. The quarter
supports continued earnings momentum after the AssuredPartners transaction,
but the headline quarter cannot answer whether the acquired platform is
earning its purchase price.

The current test is:

`acquired producers and clients -> retained fee revenue -> producer and support
cost -> integration and earnout cost -> debt and dilution -> incremental cash
per share`

Separate reported growth into organic brokerage growth, acquired revenue,
pricing or exposure effects, and risk-management revenue. Track client and
producer retention, revenue per producer, compensation, margins, receivables,
earnout payments, debt, interest, goodwill, amortization, and diluted shares.

## Incentives, acquisition return, and cohort position

Gallagher's growth model creates a clear incentive test: acquired revenue can
increase reported scale immediately, while the costs of retaining producers,
integrating systems, paying earnouts, servicing debt, and issuing shares arrive
over time. The relevant management scorecard is cash per diluted share after
those claims, not acquired revenue or adjusted EPS alone.

The $15.766B of 2025 acquisition cash and $9.904B of acquired goodwill make
the next few filings more important than the current annual margin. Assured-
Partners should be followed by cohort: revenue retention, producer retention,
organic growth after anniversary, margin after compensation, earnout cash,
incremental working capital, debt reduction, and cash return on the purchase
price. If those measures improve, the roll-up can create value; if they do not,
organic growth may be subsidizing a low-return acquisition program.

Gallagher belongs in the [insurance-broker roll-up versus organic-fee
comparison](../cross-sector/insurance-broker-rollup-versus-organic-fee-forensic-comparison-2026-09-13.md)

Compared with Aon and Marsh, Gallagher has greater acquisition and integration

does not avoid talent, retention, debt, or transaction risk.

| Valuation margin of safety | C | The price is supported by normalized post-acquisition cash rather than perpetual M&A |

Next filings should show organic growth after the acquired-revenue comparison
period, acquired versus internal fee growth, producer and client retention,
cash paid for acquisitions and earnouts, goodwill and intangible changes,
receivables, compensation, debt, interest, SBC, diluted shares, and cash after
the normalized acquisition program.

## Thesis breakers

- organic growth falls below 3% while acquisition spending remains elevated;
- acquired producers or clients fail to retain, or acquired revenue does not
  convert into incremental margin and cash;
- debt rises faster than recurring cash earnings, or equity issuance hides
  per-share stagnation;
- goodwill impairments, earnout payments, integration costs, or restructuring
  recur as a permanent cost of the model; or
- receivables, compensation, legal/conduct costs, or cyber failures reveal that
  the relationship moat is less durable than the multiple assumes.

This is not a misconduct finding. The financial-engineering concern is the
more ordinary one: a high-quality workflow can still produce poor shareholder
returns when growth is purchased at an excessive price.
