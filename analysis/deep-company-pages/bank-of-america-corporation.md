# Bank of America: deposit franchise, consumer credit, and the cost of scale

## What the company does

Bank of America combines consumer banking, small-business banking, commercial
banking, wealth management, investment banking, trading, payments, and card
lending. Its control points are checking and savings relationships, payment
flows, customer data, wealth relationships, corporate lending, and market
liquidity.

The deposit franchise is valuable because deposits can provide a lower-cost and
more stable funding source than wholesale borrowing. The advantage is not
permanent. Customers can move cash when rates change, and the bank must pay
more to retain deposits when competitors offer higher yields.

The bank’s burden is unusually broad: credit losses, liquidity, regulatory
capital, operational resilience, fraud, cyber risk, employee compensation,
technology, legal remediation, and market and counterparty exposure. The right
question is whether the deposit and client relationships create risk-adjusted
common earnings after those costs through a full credit cycle.

## FY2025 operating baseline

| Measure | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue, net of interest expense | $98.6B | $101.9B | $113.1B |
| Net income | $26.5B | $27.1B | $30.5B |
| Diluted EPS | $3.08 | $3.21 | $3.81 |
| Average deposits | $1.93T | $1.92T | $1.98T |
| Year-end deposits | $1.92T | $1.97T | $2.02T |
| Average loans and leases | $1.05T | $1.06T | $1.14T |
| Year-end loans and leases | $1.05T | $1.10T | $1.19T |

The company reported broad-based 2025 growth, but revenue and earnings should be
split into net interest income, investment-banking and markets fees, card and
payments income, wealth management, and other noninterest revenue. A record
quarter in markets or investment banking is not a permanent earnings base.

The three-year series shows recovery, not uninterrupted operating improvement.
Revenue rose from `$98.6B` in 2023 to `$113.1B` in 2025, while net income rose
from `$26.5B` to `$30.5B`. The 2024 result was still affected by deposit-cost
pressure and the cost of carrying the balance sheet at higher rates. The bank
therefore needs a capital and credit test alongside the earnings trend.

| Capital and credit measure | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Standardized CET1 ratio | 11.8% | 11.9% | 11.4% |
| Return on average assets | 0.84% | 0.83% | 0.89% |
| Return on average tangible common equity | 13.5% | 12.9% | 14.2% |
| Net charge-offs / average loans | 0.36% | 0.57% | 0.50% |
| Allowance / loans and leases | 1.27% | 1.21% | 1.12% |
| Provision for credit losses | $4.4B | $5.8B | $5.7B |

The 2025 return improvement came with a lower standardized CET1 ratio and a
lower allowance ratio than in 2023. That is not automatically a weakness: the
ratio can reflect balance-sheet mix, regulatory requirements, reserve releases,
and capital returns. It does mean that earnings growth must be read with credit
losses, risk-weighted assets, tangible common equity, and the capital required
to support additional loans.

The common-owner denominator is normalized earnings after credit losses,
funding costs, employee and technology expense, legal and compliance spending,
required capital, and prudent balance-sheet growth. Bank free cash flow is not
calculated like industrial free cash flow because loans, deposits, securities,
and capital are operating resources.

## Where the economics sit

### Deposit and consumer relationships

Bank of America served approximately 69 million consumer and small-business
clients through about 3,600 financial centers, 15,000 ATMs, and digital
platforms with roughly 49 million active users, including approximately 41
million mobile users.

These relationships can generate deposits, card spending, payments, mortgages,
consumer loans, small-business services, and wealth referrals. The control
point is the customer relationship and payment flow, not the branch count alone.

The test is whether active users, direct deposits, balances, payment volume,
product penetration, and retention grow without disproportionate rewards,
marketing, fraud, servicing, and technology expense.

### Net interest income

Net interest income is the difference between interest earned on loans and
securities and interest paid on deposits and other funding. The spread depends
on loan yields, deposit repricing, funding mix, credit quality, and the yield
curve.

Bank of America’s 2025 average deposits were about $1.98T and average loans and
leases about $1.14T. A large deposit base can lower funding cost, but deposit
beta—the percentage of market-rate increases passed to depositors—can rise
quickly when customers have alternatives.

Future filings should separate balance growth from rate and mix effects. Loan
growth that requires expensive deposits or weak underwriting is not necessarily
value creating.

### Global Markets and investment banking

Markets and investment banking benefit from volatility, issuance, mergers,
trading volumes, and client risk appetite. These businesses diversify consumer
banking, but they are cyclical and can produce unusually high or low quarterly
revenue.

The valuation must normalize sales and trading, investment banking, and other
market-sensitive fees. The bank should not be credited with a permanent return
on a favorable capital-markets cycle.

### Wealth, payments, and technology

Wealth management and payments can produce fee income with lower credit risk
than lending, but they require advisor compensation, rewards, fraud controls,
technology, and customer service. Digital scale can reduce transaction cost,
but technology is also a required control and resilience expense.

AI and digital investment should be judged by cost per account, fraud losses,
service quality, client retention, payment volume, and risk-control outcomes—not
only by the number of active users or the size of the technology budget.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering review

### 1. Credit provisions can move earnings before losses are known

The bank’s provision for credit losses reflects expected losses, portfolio mix,
delinquencies, charge-offs, macroeconomic forecasts, and reserve changes. A
reserve release can increase earnings without improving the underlying credit
portfolio; a reserve build can reduce earnings before actual losses occur.

The analysis should reconcile reserve coverage, charge-offs, nonaccrual loans,
early- and late-stage delinquencies, commercial real-estate exposure, card
balances, auto loans, small-business credit, and macroeconomic assumptions.

### 2. Consumer strength can be delayed credit weakness

Management reported resilient spending and improving card delinquency trends in
the latest quarter. That is useful evidence, but the stress can appear later if
unemployment rises, excess savings fall, or revolving balances grow faster than
income.

Track payment rates, revolving utilization, delinquency migration, net
charge-offs, loss severity, credit scores, and reserve coverage by product. A
stable aggregate charge-off ratio can hide deterioration in a growing or recently
acquired portfolio.

### 3. One-time investment gains distort comparisons

The 2024 comparison included a large gain related to Visa shares and a
contribution of Visa shares to the JPMorgan Chase Foundation. Bank of America
must also be evaluated for securities gains, tax benefits, legal settlements,
restructuring items, and other nonrecurring income.

The correct comparison is recurring pre-provision net revenue, credit costs,
capital, and common earnings—not headline year-over-year revenue alone.

### 4. Technology is both investment and maintenance

Technology spending supports digital banking, payment processing, cybersecurity,
fraud prevention, regulatory reporting, data infrastructure, and AI. A bank
cannot reduce it as freely as a discretionary marketing expense.

If management presents technology as future productivity investment, the test is
whether efficiency improves after the full cost of implementation, employee
training, model risk, controls, and system replacement. Cutting necessary
technology can improve current expense ratios while increasing future loss and
regulatory risk.

### 5. Capital returns are constrained by regulation

Dividends and repurchases must be evaluated after stress capital requirements,
the stress capital buffer, risk-weighted asset growth, market-risk capital,
liquidity requirements, and possible rule changes. A buyback can increase EPS
while reducing the capital buffer needed for a credit or market shock.

The relevant measure is common equity created per share after capital returns,
not EPS accretion by itself.

### 6. Incentives can favor scale over risk-adjusted value

The proxy should be used to connect compensation to revenue, net income,
return on tangible common equity, efficiency, client growth, conduct, credit
quality, and capital. Incentives weighted toward volume, deposits, or adjusted
earnings can encourage growth that consumes capital or creates later losses.

## Balance-sheet resilience

Bank of America’s resilience comes from deposit scale, liquidity, diversified
revenue, capital, and a broad client network. Its vulnerabilities are deposit
competition, unrealized securities losses, commercial real-estate exposure,
consumer credit, wholesale funding, operational events, and regulatory capital
changes.

The balance-sheet review should separate:

- insured and uninsured deposits and their repricing behavior;
- loans by product, collateral, geography, and delinquency stage;
- securities duration and unrealized losses;
- liquidity sources and stressed cash outflows;
- derivatives and counterparty exposures;
- common equity, tangible common equity, CET1, and stress capital;
- legal, operational, cyber, and fraud reserves.

The bank can be liquid and profitable while still earning below its cost of
equity if it holds excess low-yield assets or must retain more capital than the
market expected.

## Lyn Alden macro, liquidity, and durability lens

The bank is directly tied to the credit and liquidity cycle. Higher rates can
increase asset yields but raise deposit costs and reduce the value of fixed-rate
securities. A recession can reduce loan demand, investment banking, and wealth
fees while increasing credit losses. Market stress can raise trading revenue and
counterparty risk at the same time.

The durable part is the client network, payment infrastructure, deposit base,
and scale of compliance and technology investment. The fragile part is the
balance-sheet leverage: a small loss rate applied to a very large asset base can
consume years of earnings.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative is that Bank of America can convert deposit scale, client trust,
payments, wealth, and technology into durable risk-adjusted returns.

That narrative requires:

1. deposits remain stable at an acceptable cost;
2. net interest income survives repricing and yield-curve changes;
3. loan growth earns more than its expected loss and capital requirement;
4. markets and investment-banking revenue normalize above a weak-cycle floor;
5. technology and compliance spending create productivity without weakening
   controls;
6. common earnings grow after required capital retention and dilution.

Bank of America is a mature financial institution. Growth is valuable only when
incremental loans, deposits, accounts, and payment volume earn above the cost
of capital after credit, technology, compensation, liquidity, and regulatory
burdens.

## Valuation screen

The market feed showed an equity value of approximately $457.3B. The following
screen uses normalized net income, not industrial owner cash:

| Case | Normalized net income | Earnings multiple | Implied equity value | Required interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bear | $27B | 12x | $324B | Higher deposit costs, credit normalization, weaker markets, capital pressure |
| Base | $30.5B | 14x | $427B | FY2025 earnings broadly persist with normal credit and capital returns |
| Bull | $35B | 16x | $560B | Deposit and payments growth, strong markets, stable credit, higher ROTCE |

The current market value is above the base screen and below the bull case. The
price therefore requires continued operating improvement and confidence that
credit losses, deposit costs, and regulatory capital will not absorb the recent
earnings gains.

## Thesis breakers and future filing tests

| Question | Evidence that would support the thesis | Evidence that would weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| Is the deposit franchise valuable? | Balances grow with controlled deposit beta and stable liquidity | Customers migrate to higher-yield alternatives and funding cost rises |
| Is loan growth profitable? | Net interest income and risk-adjusted return rise after losses and capital | Loan growth increases reserves, charge-offs, or capital faster than earnings |
| Is consumer credit healthy? | Payment rates, delinquencies, reserves, and charge-offs remain controlled | Revolving balances rise while payment rates and reserve coverage weaken |
| Are markets results durable? | Fees normalize above a through-cycle floor without excess risk | Earnings rely on one-time gains or unusually favorable volatility |
| Is technology creating value? | Cost per account, fraud, service, and efficiency improve after spending | Technology expense rises while controls, outages, fraud, or remediation worsen |
| Are buybacks safe? | CET1 and stress capital remain strong after distributions and growth | Capital rules or losses force issuance, payout cuts, or balance-sheet contraction |

## Bottom line

Bank of America owns a valuable deposit, payments, wealth, and corporate-client
network. Its scale can spread technology, compliance, and liquidity costs across
a large customer base. That does not eliminate the banking burden; it magnifies
the effect of small changes in credit losses, deposit pricing, and capital
requirements.

The 2025 and 2026 evidence supports a strong operating setup, but the valuation
requires more than resilient consumer spending and record markets. Future
filings must show that common earnings remain attractive after normalizing
capital-market revenue, funding costs, consumer credit, technology, and stress
capital.

**Evidence grade:** Strong for FY2025 and Q2 2026 reported revenue, earnings,
deposits, loans, client scale, and credit trends; medium for through-cycle net
interest income and risk-adjusted returns; unresolved for the full effect of
future capital rules, deposit repricing, and consumer credit normalization.
