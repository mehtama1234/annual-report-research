# Baxter International Inc. Full Forensic Analysis Memo

Date baseline: 2026-09-13  
Primary source: [Baxter 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/10456/000162828026007733/bax-20251231.htm)  
Companion packet: [company-packet.md](/home/mehtama1/git-repo/annual-report-research/extracted/healthcare/medical-instruments-supplies/baxter-international-inc/company-packet.md)

## Bottom line

Baxter controls a recurring hospital and institutional supply relationship across
IV solutions, infusion systems, injectable pharmaceuticals, connected care,
patient-support equipment, surgery, and drug compounding. Its products are
less glamorous than a procedure platform, but they are embedded in care
delivery and therefore can have meaningful switching and reliability value.

The current economics are a reset, not a clean compounder story. Baxter sold
Kidney Care for $3.8B in cash in January 2025, repaid $3.81B of debt during the
year, and still ended 2025 with $9.48B of debt and finance-lease obligations.
Continuing-operations operating cash flow was $951M against $513M of capital
expenditures, leaving roughly $429M before debt service, dividends, and other
claims. Net loss attributable to shareholders was $957M, including $2.09B of
special-item drag. The central question is whether the remaining portfolio
can restore reliable margins and cash conversion after quality events,
inventory costs, impairments, and separation dis-synergies—or whether
“adjusted” earnings are carrying a permanently higher operating burden.

This memo identifies reconciliation tests; it does not establish fraud.

## What Baxter actually controls

The economic chain is:

`hospital and home-care demand -> product availability and clinical reliability -> installed workflow -> recurring consumables, service, and pharmaceuticals -> Baxter cash`

Baxter's control point is institutional workflow and availability. Hospitals,
ambulatory sites, pharmacies, dialysis centers, and patient homes need products
to arrive reliably and function safely. But Baxter does not control hospital
budgets, reimbursement, labor, procurement concentration, or the full clinical
outcome. Product holds and quality remediation can therefore destroy trust
faster than ordinary demand weakness.

## Q2 2026 recovery checkpoint

The Q2 2026 results provide a useful test of the post-Kidney-Care recovery, but
these figures are company-reported quarterly and first-half results from the IR
release and packet; they are not a replacement for the FY2025 audited bridge.

| Measure | Q2 2026 evidence | What it supports | What remains unresolved |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Worldwide sales | Approximately $2.96B, up 5% reported and organically | Demand for the remaining hospital and institutional portfolio remained positive | Sales growth does not establish margin recovery or adequate return on the remaining capital |
| Quarterly free cash flow | Approximately $181M | Cash generation continued after the reset year | Management free cash flow must be reconciled to GAAP operating cash, capex, quality spending, and working-capital movement |
| First-half free cash flow | Approximately $257M | The result was not limited to one quarter | It is still too short a period to reverse the FY2025 VAT-receivable and inventory normalization questions |
| Medical Products & Therapies sales | Approximately $2.1B, up 7% reported and 5% organically | IV solutions and related products remained the main operating engine | The Novum IQ hold and higher-cost inventory still affect the quality of the recovery |
| Healthcare Systems & Technologies sales | Approximately $801M, up 4% reported | Patient-support and front-line systems remained active | Planned portfolio exits and hospital equipment timing can change the mix |
| Inventory | $2.316B at June 30 versus $2.232B at December 31, 2025 | Inventory continued to require cash after the year-end cleanup | The next filing must show aging, reserves, held product, and whether inventory growth matches sales |

The checkpoint changes the conclusion in a limited way. It supports the claim
that end-market demand and cash generation have not collapsed. It does not yet
prove that Baxter has restored a durable margin structure or that the higher-
cost inventory and product-quality burdens are finished. The next 10-Q should
show GAAP operating cash flow, capex, inventory reserves, product holds,
quality/remediation cash, debt reduction, and recurring special charges. A
quarter with higher sales but flat gross margin, rising inventory, or repeated
quality costs would be a weak recovery even if adjusted EPS improves.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. Special-item normalization is the entire investment case

2025 net loss attributable to Baxter stockholders was $957M; management says
special items adversely affected the result by $2.09B, or $4.08 per diluted
share. Continuing-operations loss was $900M. Removing special items may be
reasonable for a divestiture/reset year, but the burden is proving that the
items are genuinely nonrecurring.

Separate: Kidney Care separation costs, Front Line Care goodwill impairment,
deferred-tax valuation allowances, product-quality remediation, restructuring,
legal and environmental reserves, stranded costs, and recurring manufacturing
or compliance investment. If the same categories recur every year, adjusted
EPS is not a clean measure of economic earnings.

### 2. Divestiture proceeds repaired liquidity but changed the denominator

The Kidney Care sale generated $3.8B of cash, but it also removed a business,
altered segment mix, created dis-synergies, and left Baxter with indemnities
and transition obligations. In 2025, investing cash flow from discontinued
operations included $3.305B of sale proceeds. Those proceeds must not be
blended with continuing operating cash when valuing the post-sale business.

The next filing should reconcile the remaining portfolio's organic growth,
gross margin, corporate cost allocation, stranded costs, working capital, and
capital intensity against the pre-sale period.

### 3. Quality events are economic costs, not merely presentation items

The packet identifies the Novum IQ LVP shipment-and-installation hold and
higher-cost inventory produced at the end of 2025. The 2025 filing also
records a $485M goodwill impairment tied to Front Line Care and substantial
special items. These are not proof that revenue is fictitious; they are proof
that quality, product holds, forecast errors, and portfolio decisions can
change the durability of the installed relationship.

Track product returns, reserves, remediation spending, regulatory commitments,
lost sales, customer concessions, warranty trends, and inventory aging. A
recovery in reported sales without normalized quality and inventory cash would
be a weak recovery.

### 4. Working-capital improvement may not repeat

Management attributes part of the 2025 operating-cash improvement to
collections of value-added-tax receivables and improved working capital. That
is a legitimate source of cash, but it is not a permanent margin improvement.
The next review should reverse one-time receivable collections and test
inventory turns, supply-chain normalization, payables, and product-hold
effects.

### 5. Capital investment is partly defensive

2025 capital expenditures were $513M, focused on production efficiency, quality
systems, and manufacturing capacity. That can support future growth, but in a
healthcare supplier it may also be mandatory maintenance or remediation
capital. The valuation should not award all capex a growth return; it should
split sustaining quality/compliance capital from capacity expansion.

## Alden lens: healthcare durability meets balance-sheet constraint

Demand for IV solutions, infusion, injectable drugs, and acute-care equipment
is structurally durable, but Baxter's ability to monetize that demand depends
on reliability, hospital budgets, manufacturing availability, and debt
capacity. At year-end, debt and finance-lease obligations totaled $9.48B,
with $2.715B of projected interest payments in contractual obligations. Credit
ratings were BBB-/Baa3 with stable outlook after downgrades during 2025.

The liquidity chain is:

`clinical necessity -> procurement continuity -> product quality -> recurring volume and pricing -> cash conversion -> debt reduction and reinvestment`

The sale proceeds improved liquidity, but high leverage raises the cost of a
quality setback. If cash is directed primarily to debt service and remediation,
less remains for innovation, capacity, and customer support.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to valuation

The model should value the post-Kidney-Care portfolio, not capitalize legacy
revenue. Translate:

`installed hospital workflow -> recurring consumables/drug volume -> price and mix -> quality-adjusted margin -> working capital -> sustaining versus growth capex -> debt service -> equity value`

At the September 11, 2026 market snapshot, BAX equity value was approximately
$12.33B. That is about 13.0x 2025 continuing-operations OCF and 28.7x the
$429M residual after capex and developed-technology investments. These are
screening ratios, not price targets. The second ratio reveals the market's
dependence on margin recovery, working-capital normalization, and declining
debt burden.

The reverse valuation should vary organic sales, product-hold recovery,
quality/remediation cost, gross margin, working capital, sustaining capex,
interest expense, debt repayment, and the timing of stranded-cost removal.

## Incentives and thesis breakers

Management is naturally rewarded for adjusted EPS, operational sales growth,
cost savings, debt reduction, free cash flow, and portfolio simplification.
Those metrics can improve through divestiture, add-backs, working-capital
release, or lower investment before the remaining product system is truly
healthy.

The thesis is materially weakened if future filings show:

- continuing “special” quality, restructuring, separation, or compliance
  charges;
- revenue recovery without normalized gross margin, inventory turns, and cash;
- Novum IQ or other product issues causing holds, concessions, reserves, or
  customer loss;
- debt reduction stalls while interest and mandatory quality capital consume
  the post-sale cash stream; or
- segment reorganization makes comparisons look better without resolving
  stranded costs and post-divestiture dis-synergies.

## Evidence grade and unresolved questions

- **Filing fact:** continuing-operations cash flow, capex, debt, separation
  proceeds, special items, quality reserves, and working-capital changes are
  anchored to the 2025 Form 10-K.
- **Disclosed/packet-backed:** Q2 2026 sales, margin-recovery, product-quality,
  and management-outlook claims come from company materials and require later
  cash confirmation.
- **Inference:** Baxter controls embedded hospital supply workflows, but quality
  remediation and post-divestiture stranded costs can consume the same durable
  demand economics that make the platform valuable.
- **Unresolved:** the next filing must show normalized gross margin, inventory
  turns, quality cash, debt reduction, and recurring capex after working-capital
  benefits fade. Repeated special charges or stagnant debt reduction would break
  the recovery case.
