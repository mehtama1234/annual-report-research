# BHP — copper-centered diversified mining, iron-ore cash, and long-cycle allocation

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are IFRS figures in U.S. dollars; dollar
amounts are in millions unless otherwise stated. BHP's fiscal year ended June
30, so its FY2025 is not a calendar-year period.

## How to read this memo

Net operating cash flow is cash generated by the operating businesses. BHP's
free cash flow measure is net operating cash flow less net investing cash flow;
its capital-and-exploration measure separately identifies the cash required for
productive assets and exploration. Underlying EBITDA and underlying attributable
profit are adjusted measures, not owner cash. BHP also pays dividends to
non-controlling interests, and its joint ventures and large projects mean that
consolidated production is not identical to cash belonging to BHP shareholders.

## Investment question

BHP is a diversified mining platform built around Australian iron ore, copper,
coal, and potash options. Its current strategic direction is increasingly
copper-centered: copper supplied 51% of Underlying EBITDA in the FY2026
half-year results, while iron ore remains a major cash source. The question is
whether BHP can redeploy iron-ore cash into copper and potash growth at returns
that exceed the value of dividends, while keeping debt, operating, social,
environmental, and partner burdens under control.

The three-year record is the central denominator test: free cash flow was
$5.636B in FY2023, $11.903B in FY2024, and $5.342B in FY2025. Operating cash
was $18.701B, $20.665B, and $18.692B across those years. The sharp difference
between operating cash and free cash flow shows why a mining company's price
must be tested against capital spending, not only against EBITDA or production.

## What the annual and current-period record reveals

- BHP produced about 2 million tonnes of copper in FY2025 and described it as a
  record year. Copper is becoming the center of the portfolio's long-term
  narrative, but current cash still depends on iron ore, coal, and the cost
  structure of existing assets.
- FY2025 capital and exploration expenditure was $9.794B, including $4.5B in
  copper, $1.6B in potash, and $3.2B in steelmaking materials. This allocation
  makes the growth thesis measurable: the company is already spending heavily
  before future projects prove their returns.
- BHP paid $1.8B of Samarco settlement obligations in FY2025. Legacy claims
  therefore compete with dividends and new projects for cash even when they are
  discussed separately from operating performance.
- Net debt rose to $12.924B from $9.120B in FY2024. Management increased its
  target net-debt range to $10B-$20B while arguing that stronger copper output
  and asset quality improve debt capacity. That is a balance-sheet choice, not
  proof that every growth project earns its cost.
- BHP's FY2026 half-year results reported $9.372B of net operating cash flow and
  $2.906B of free cash flow after $5.263B of capital and exploration spending.
  Copper represented 51% of Underlying EBITDA. The current evidence supports a
  copper shift, but it also shows that higher copper exposure does not remove
  capital intensity.
- Portfolio recycling is part of the model. The FY2026 nine-month review cited
  about $4.8B from the Antamina silver stream, Carajas divestment, and earlier
  asset transactions. Proceeds can improve liquidity, but asset sales should
  not be treated as recurring owner cash.
- BHP reports economic contribution and payments to governments because the
  social and fiscal footprint is large. Taxes, royalties, host-country rules,
  labor, water, closure, and environmental obligations are part of the cost of
  retaining the operating permission.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Is free cash flow being measured after the full renewal burden?

Reconcile $5.342B of free cash flow to operating cash, PP&E, exploration,
equity-accounted funding, acquisitions, asset sales, Samarco payments, leases,
and non-controlling interests. Treat the $9.794B capital-and-exploration figure
as a starting point, then test whether other investing cash is required to keep
the economic system productive.

### 2. Is copper growth creating value or only changing the story?

For each copper asset, track production, grade, recovery, realized price, unit
cost, sustaining capital, growth capital, ownership, and project schedule. A
record production year matters only if cash per BHP share rises after capital and
claims.

### 3. What does the FY2024-to-FY2025 cash decline mean?

Free cash flow fell from $11.903B to $5.342B while operating cash fell only from
$20.665B to $18.692B. Separate lower prices, higher capital, Vicuña funding,
Samarco payments, working capital, taxes, and dividends. This bridge identifies
whether the lower residual is cyclical or a new capital baseline.

### 4. Are project returns visible before capital is committed?

For copper, potash, and mine-life extensions, record original capital, revised
capital, expected production, ramp date, ownership, operating cost, taxes,
closure cost, and expected return. Compare those returns with the cash yield on
buybacks or dividends at the time of approval.

### 5. Are joint ventures and non-controlling interests obscuring owner cash?

Separate production and EBITDA from cash received by BHP. Include dividends to
non-controlling interests, equity-accounted investments, partner funding, and
project-level debt. Consolidated size is not the same as common-owner exposure.

### 6. Is management rewarded for cash quality?

Review whether annual and long-term incentives emphasize production, EBITDA,
return on capital, safety, cash flow, debt, or share performance. A scorecard
that rewards volume or adjusted profit before full-cost returns can encourage
growth that looks successful before the final capital bill arrives.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** BHP sits inside energy security, electrification, food
  security, China-linked industrial demand, trade policy, and resource
  nationalism. Its balance sheet is strong enough to fund projects, but the
  cycle and the size of the capital program determine how much flexibility is
  left in a downturn.
- **Damodaran lens:** value iron ore, copper, coal, potash, and project options
  by lifecycle. Use mid-cycle prices, production decline, sustaining capital,
  reserve renewal, probability-weighted project timing, ownership, taxes, debt,
  and closure obligations. Do not capitalize FY2024's unusually high residual
  as the normal cash base.
- **Forensic lens:** the principal risks are treating record copper production
  as value creation, treating asset-sale proceeds as recurring cash, ignoring
  non-controlling distributions, and using free cash flow without testing the
  full project and legacy-investment burden.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $441.62B of equity value at
$87.15 per ADR. Against FY2025 free cash flow of $5.342B, the screen is about
82.7x. A normalized owner-cash range of $10B / $18B / $26B at 15x / 20x / 25x
implies $150B / $360B / $650B. The current price requires a substantial and
sustained increase from FY2025 free cash flow, a successful copper-led shift,
and project returns that justify the capital and valuation already embedded in
the shares.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Copper production rises without higher cash per share after capital, taxes,
   partner claims, and dividends.
2. Iron-ore or coal prices normalize while costs, royalties, and renewal capital
   remain high.
3. Potash or copper projects require more capital, start later, or earn below
   the required return.
4. Net debt moves above the stated range or dividends remain above sustainable
   free cash flow.
5. Asset-sale proceeds, working-capital releases, or adjusted measures continue
   to support the apparent cash return.
6. Samarco, closure, water, environmental, labor, or host-country obligations
   rise faster than provisions and owner cash.
