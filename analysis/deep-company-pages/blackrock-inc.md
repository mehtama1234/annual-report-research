## Bottom line

BlackRock controls a capital-allocation platform rather than a single fund
product. The control points are ETF distribution and liquidity, institutional
index and active mandates, private-market origination, technology and data,
and the relationships that connect these products to advisers, institutions,
retirement plans, and governments.

The platform is economically attractive: FY2025 total revenue was $24.216
billion, adjusted operating margin was 44.1%, technology and subscription
revenue was $1.981 billion, and reported net inflows were a record $698
billion. Q2 2026 AUM reached approximately $15.3 trillion, with quarterly net
inflows of $192 billion and an adjusted operating margin of 45.9%.

The central forensic conclusion is conditional. AUM growth is not the same as
economic growth: market appreciation can lift AUM without new client control,
while fee-rate compression, product mix, distribution costs, acquisition
amortization, contingent consideration, stock compensation, and private-market
marks determine what reaches owners. BlackRock deserves a platform premium
only if net flows, fee-paying AUM, technology monetization, and post-acquisition
cash returns compound faster than the burdens introduced by HPS, Preqin, and
other strategic expansion.

## What the company actually controls

BlackRock's system is:

`client relationship -> product shelf and distribution -> AUM and net flows -> fee revenue -> technology/data cross-sell`

Private markets add a second loop:

`origination and underwriting -> private assets -> realizations/marks -> fee-paying AUM and performance fees`

The first loop is comparatively transparent. ETFs and institutional mandates
have observable AUM, flows, fee rates, and market performance. The second loop
is more complex: private-market valuation, realization timing, incentive fees,
co-investments, acquisition consideration, and insurance or balance-sheet
partners can make reported earnings less directly comparable to fee revenue.

BlackRock's durable advantage is distribution and operating infrastructure at
scale. Its risk is that scale becomes a reason to buy growth at an inadequate
return, or that a broader product set makes the preferred adjusted metrics less
representative of the economic claims on cash.

The operating bridge should not begin with AUM alone. It should decompose:

- market movement and foreign exchange;
- gross subscriptions, redemptions, and net flows;
- average AUM rather than period-end AUM;
- fee rate by product and channel;
- ETFs/index, active, alternatives, cash, and technology mix; and
- performance fees and other non-recurring revenue.

Technology and subscription revenue of $1.981 billion in FY2025 is strategically
important because it offers a higher-value monetization layer than simply
collecting a percentage of public-market AUM. The test is incremental margin
and retention: revenue growth is not attractive if it requires proportionate
implementation, sales, data, or acquisition spending.

## Cash conversion and required capital

### FY2025 reported bridge

| Item | $ millions |
| --- | ---: |
| GAAP net cash from operations | 3,927 |
| Capital expenditures | (375) |
| Acquisitions | (3,496) |
| Screening cash after capex and acquisitions | **56** |

The $56 million screen is deliberately conservative and not a normalized
owner-cash estimate. BlackRock spent approximately $3.1 billion on Preqin and
$369 million on HPS in 2025, while also investing in technology and making
other investments. Treating a transformational acquisition as recurring
maintenance capex would be too harsh; ignoring it would be too generous. The
correct question is whether the acquired fee-paying AUM, technology revenue,
and operating synergies earn an adequate return on the capital deployed.

### Three-year acquisition-adjusted diagnostic

The filed cash-flow statements support the same screen across the prior years:

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Capital expenditures | Cash acquisitions | Diagnostic residual |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2023 | `$4.165B` | `$344M` | `$189M` | `$3.632B` |
| 2024 | `$4.956B` | `$255M` | `$2.936B` | `$1.765B` |
| 2025 | `$3.927B` | `$375M` | `$3.496B` | `$56M` |

The decline does not prove that BlackRock's mature fee platform deteriorated.
It shows that the company moved a large amount of cash into GIP, Preqin, HPS,
and related platform expansion. The investment case must therefore carry two
separate denominators: mature recurring corporate cash and the return required
on acquisition capital. FY2025 stock compensation of approximately `$1.307B`
and contingent consideration make the per-share bridge more demanding still.

BlackRock also presents a “cash flows excluding CIPs” measure because cash
flows of certain sponsored investment products can overwhelm the corporate
cash signal. CIPs and variable-interest-entity balances must be separated from
cash available to BlackRock shareholders. The company ended 2025 with about
$11.5 billion of consolidated cash, but the relevant corporate liquidity is
not simply every dollar shown in the consolidated balance sheet.

The FY2025 cash-flow statement also included approximately $1.307 billion of
stock compensation, $1.126 billion of depreciation and amortization, and a
large fair-value adjustment for contingent consideration. These are not all
equivalent: stock compensation is a real dilution claim, amortization reflects
past acquisition capital, and contingent-consideration remeasurement is a
valuation-sensitive liability. Each must be retained in the owner-cash bridge
with a clear treatment.

### A better platform cash-flow framework

`GAAP OCF`

`- maintenance technology and operating capex`

`- recurring acquisitions or investment commitments required to defend the platform`

`+/- realized performance fees, investment marks, and CIPs/VIE normalization`

`- dilution-equivalent stock compensation`

`= normalized corporate owner cash`

The result should be shown as a range, not a single adjusted number. A base
case can capitalize recurring public-market and technology fees, while a
separate private-markets case assigns lower confidence to unrealized marks and
higher reinvestment needs during the build-out phase.

## Earnings-quality and financial-engineering tests

### 1. AUM can grow without client control improving

Separate market beta from net new money. A rising S&P 500, bond rally, or
currency movement can increase AUM and headline revenue even when clients are
redeeming or moving to lower-fee products. Track net flows by product, average
AUM, organic base fee growth, and fee rate in basis points.

The strongest evidence of durable economics is simultaneous growth in net
flows, fee-paying AUM, average revenue per dollar of AUM, and cash conversion.
The weakest is AUM growth driven mainly by markets, acquisitions, or a shift
into lower-fee index products.

### 2. Adjusted margin is useful but not self-authenticating

FY2025 GAAP operating margin was approximately 29.1%, versus a 44.1% adjusted
operating margin. The gap reflects acquisition-related amortization,
contingent consideration, and other adjustments. Some exclusions may be
reasonable for period comparability; they are not automatically irrelevant to
owners.

Amortization records the consumption of acquired customer relationships and
technology. If BlackRock must repeatedly buy platforms to sustain growth, the
charge is economically recurring even if accounting treats it as non-cash.
Contingent consideration can move with BlackRock's share price and acquired
asset performance. A recurring pattern of “one-time” integration or
acquisition costs should be deducted from a durable earnings case.

### 3. Private-market marks and performance fees need a realization ledger

HPS added approximately $165 billion of AUM and $118 billion of fee-paying AUM
in July 2025; Preqin was acquired in March 2025. These transactions expand the
platform but also introduce private valuation, realization, incentive-fee,
integration, and contingent-payment questions.

For every quarter, record:

- unrealized versus realized investment gains;
- performance-fee revenue and reversal risk;
- fee-paying AUM versus total AUM;
- valuation marks versus exit proceeds;
- contingent consideration remeasurement; and
- cash acquisition cost, integration cost, and incremental fee revenue.

Private-market AUM is not a free option. It requires underwriting talent,
distribution, data, operations, and credibility through a realization cycle.

### 4. CIPs and VIEs can obscure the corporate cash denominator

BlackRock consolidates certain investment products or sponsored structures for
accounting purposes. Their asset and liability movements can be very large
without representing capital that management can distribute to shareholders.
The “excluding CIPs” presentation is therefore analytically useful, but it
must reconcile cleanly to GAAP and not be used to remove ordinary corporate
expenses.

The balance-sheet test is to separate corporate cash, investments, debt, and
working capital from separate-account assets, VIE liabilities, and client
product cash flows. Corporate liquidity and shareholder distributions should
be modeled from the first layer.

### 5. Stock compensation and buybacks require a share-count test

FY2025 stock compensation was approximately $1.307 billion. BlackRock also
paid roughly $3.347 billion in dividends and subsidiary distributions and
repurchased about $1.951 billion of shares. The relevant test is not whether
repurchases are large; it is whether they reduce diluted shares after employee
issuance and acquisition-related stock consideration.

Track basic shares, diluted shares, weighted-average shares, unvested awards,
shares issued for acquisitions, and repurchases at different prices. A buyback
that merely offsets compensation or acquisition dilution is not the same as a
per-share capital return.

## Alden macro and durability lens

BlackRock is exposed to global market levels, rates, credit spreads,
retirement savings, and institutional risk budgets. That makes it a powerful
beneficiary of financialization but not a low-beta operating business.

The durability case rests on several reinforcing relationships: clients need
low-cost beta and liquidity; institutions need risk analytics and portfolio
technology; advisers need distribution and model portfolios; and private
markets need origination plus a trusted allocator. During stress, demand for
risk management and liquidity can rise, but market declines can reduce AUM and
fee revenue at the same time.

The macro questions are:

- What happens to fee revenue if equity markets fall 30% and rates remain high?
- Do fixed-income and cash inflows offset equity redemptions?
- Does private-market fundraising slow before public-market AUM recovers?
- How much of technology revenue is contractually recurring versus project or
  implementation dependent?
- Are acquisitions a response to durable client demand or a way to manufacture
  growth when organic flows weaken?

## Damodaran narrative-to-numbers lens

The narrative is “one integrated platform monetizing every important layer of
capital allocation.” To earn that narrative, the model must specify:

1. sustainable net-flow growth by product;
2. fee-rate retention after mix and price competition;
3. technology revenue growth and incremental margin;
4. private-market fee-paying AUM and realization economics;
5. recurring acquisition/reinvestment requirements;
6. normalized corporate owner cash after dilution; and
7. a terminal growth rate consistent with global financial-market growth.

BlackRock appears to be a mature public-market franchise with a newer
reinvestment phase in private markets and technology. The mature core may
justify a high-quality multiple; the newer businesses should be valued on
incremental invested capital and evidence of cash returns, not on AUM alone.

## Management incentives and capital allocation

Management has a rational reason to pursue acquisitions: a broader platform
can increase distribution, retention, and wallet share. It also creates a
rational risk of empire building because AUM and adjusted revenue can rise
before returns on acquisition capital are visible.

The capital-allocation scorecard should include:

- organic fee-paying AUM growth before acquisitions;
- incremental revenue and operating income from HPS and Preqin;
- acquisition cash cost plus contingent consideration;
- amortization and integration costs versus initial underwriting;
- diluted share count after stock consideration and SBC;
- dividends and buybacks after required reinvestment; and
- return on incremental technology and private-markets capital.

## Thesis breakers and next-filing questions

The thesis weakens if:

- net flows remain weak while AUM rises only through market appreciation;
- fee rates fall faster than operating expenses can be removed;
- technology revenue grows but retention, margins, or implementation costs deteriorate;
- private-market marks rise without corresponding realizations or cash distributions;
- HPS, Preqin, or future acquisitions fail to earn their acquisition cost;
- adjusted operating income expands while GAAP-to-adjusted exclusions recur;
- buybacks fail to reduce diluted shares; or
- CIPs/VIE and corporate cash are repeatedly presented in ways that blur
  shareholder liquidity.

The next filing should capture average AUM, net flows by product, fee rates,
performance fees, realized/unrealized marks, fee-paying AUM, acquisition
synergies, contingent consideration, stock compensation, diluted shares,
corporate cash, debt, CIPs/VIE balances, capex, acquisitions, and cash
available after distributions.

## Evidence grade

- Operating platform: **A-** — scale, flows, AUM, and technology revenue are
  well disclosed, but market beta must be isolated.
- Cash conversion: **B** — GAAP OCF is clear, but CIPs/VIEs and acquisition
  intensity require a platform-specific bridge.
- Earnings quality: **B** — adjusted metrics are useful but the GAAP gap,
  amortization, contingent consideration, SBC, and private marks matter.
- Acquisition underwriting: **B-** — HPS and Preqin are strategically logical;
  return evidence remains incomplete.
- Capital allocation: **B** — large distributions coexist with substantial
  acquisition and technology reinvestment.
- Valuation: **B-** — use a core-fee plus growth-platform model, not a single
  AUM multiple or unadjusted adjusted-EPS multiple.

platform's mature fee cash and acquisition-funded growth must be modeled
separately. SBC and stock-funded consideration also remain owner claims even
