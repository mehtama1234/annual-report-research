# Booking Holdings — travel distribution, payments, and owner cash

Date: `2026-09-14`. Primary evidence: [Booking company packet](../../extracted/services/transportation-services/booking-holdings/company-packet.md) and [Booking source ledger](../../extracted/services/transportation-services/booking-holdings/source-ledger.md).

## Investment question

Booking is a travel marketplace and transaction platform. It connects
travelers with hotels, alternative accommodations, flights, rental cars, and
other travel services. It does not own most of the rooms, aircraft, or rental
fleets. Its control point is discovery, comparison, reservation, payment,
localization, loyalty, and the ability to route a traveler through several
parts of a trip.

The investment question is whether Booking can keep the traveler relationship
and earn durable cash after marketing, payment processing, customer support,
fraud, refunds, taxes, technology, stock compensation, and regulatory costs.
The second question is whether the shift toward merchant bookings, payments,
mobile, and the Connected Trip improves economics or mainly increases the
amount of transaction and settlement risk Booking carries.

## What the company controls

The operating sequence is:

`travel intent -> search and comparison -> supply selection -> reservation and
payment -> trip completion -> support and refund -> repeat booking -> cross-sell`

Booking controls the consumer interface and much of the transaction workflow.
Suppliers provide rooms, flights, cars, local service, and physical quality.
Travelers provide demand but remain sensitive to price, income, exchange rates,
airfare, safety, and geopolitical events.

The company therefore owns demand capture more directly than a hotel brand, but
it does not own the underlying supply. The marketplace is valuable only when
there is enough trustworthy supply at a price travelers accept and enough
traveler demand for suppliers to remain on the platform.

## Definitions

- **Gross bookings:** the value of travel reservations before the amount
  retained by Booking and the amount paid or owed to suppliers.
- **Merchant booking:** Booking collects payment from the traveler and later
  remits the supplier's share. This can increase control and payment revenue,
  but it also increases settlement, refund, chargeback, and working-capital
  responsibility.
- **Agency booking:** the supplier generally collects the travel payment while
  Booking receives a commission. This can be simpler economically but gives
  Booking less control over payment and the customer relationship.
- **Take rate:** retained revenue divided by gross bookings. A higher take rate
  is not automatically better if it requires more marketing, payment, support,
  refunds, or credit exposure.
- **Connected Trip:** the effort to connect lodging, flights, cars, payments,
  loyalty, and other travel services through one traveler relationship.

The evidence supports a broadening interface: more merchant payment control,
more direct app behavior, more alternative supply, and more cross-vertical
travel. It does not yet prove that each adjacent product earns the same margin
as core accommodation distribution.

## The merchant shift

The move from agency to merchant bookings changes both economics and risk.

Under an agency model, Booking may receive a commission after the supplier
collects from the traveler. Under a merchant model, Booking collects the full
transaction and later pays the supplier. Merchant bookings can improve payment
attachment, customer control, and the ability to sell a complete trip. They can
also produce larger gross cash movements without increasing retained revenue
by the same amount.

The forensic bridge is:

`merchant gross booking -> traveler collection -> supplier remittance -> fee,
payment cost, refund, fraud, and support -> retained contribution`

Track merchant mix, payment processing expense, funds held for suppliers,
refunds, chargebacks, working capital, and cash per booking. A rise in merchant
mix is strategically meaningful, but it is not free cash.

## Supply, demand, and network quality

### Supply

Booking.com had approximately `4.7M` properties at June 30, 2026. Supply
growth can improve destination choice and conversion, but listing count alone
does not measure availability, quality, pricing, cancellation behavior, or
regulatory compliance.

Track active properties, available room nights, conversion, cancellation,
review quality, supplier retention, commissions, and local restrictions.

### Demand

App, direct, and loyalty behavior can reduce dependence on paid search and
improve repeat economics. The packet reports Q2 mobile mix in the high-50%
range, B2C direct mix in the mid-60% range, and Genius participation in the
high-50% range. These are useful indicators, but direct demand is valuable only
if it produces profitable repeat bookings after discounts, rewards, marketing,
payment, and support.

### Cross-vertical expansion

Flights, rental cars, alternative accommodation, payments, and other travel
services can increase the number of decisions Booking helps coordinate. The
incremental value depends on attachment rate, contribution per transaction,
customer acquisition cost, support, cancellations, and supplier economics.

The Connected Trip is therefore a testable operating claim, not a slogan. The
next filings should show whether a traveler who books one category uses more
categories, returns more often, and costs less to serve.

## Cash conversion and earnings quality

Booking's reported revenue is a better denominator than gross bookings, but
reported revenue still must be reconciled to cash and obligations. The company
has a low physical-capital model, yet it invests in software, marketing,
payments, customer support, trust systems, and acquisitions.

The central questions are:

1. Is revenue growth coming from more profitable retained contribution or from
   a change in booking presentation and merchant mix?
2. Do supplier funds, deferred revenue, or customer prepayments create a cash
   benefit before the related travel is completed?
3. Are payment, refund, fraud, tax, and chargeback costs rising with merchant
   control?
4. Does stock compensation reduce cash per diluted share even when adjusted
   EBITDA less capex looks strong?
5. Does marketing intensity fall as direct, app, and loyalty behavior improves?
6. Do acquisitions and technology spending generate higher contribution, or
   merely higher gross bookings?

The appropriate owner-cash bridge is:

`retained travel revenue -> payment, marketing, support, fraud, tax, and
technology costs -> working-capital normalization -> software and acquisition
investment -> SBC and dilution -> common-owner cash`

## Macro and liquidity lens

Travel is discretionary. It is affected by employment, household income,
exchange rates, airfare, fuel, interest rates, geopolitical events, and local
regulation. Booking's global footprint diversifies individual markets but
increases exposure to many legal and tax systems.

The platform is light in property but dependent on marketplace liquidity. In a
downturn, suppliers may need bookings more urgently while travelers reduce
spending. That can create more competition for demand and more pressure on
commission, marketing, refunds, and support.

The durability test is whether direct repeat demand, supply density, and
payment integration allow Booking to protect contribution and liquidity when
travel volume and supplier health weaken.

## Damodaran narrative-to-numbers bridge

The narrative is that Booking can become the primary digital interface for a
trip. Translate it into:

`active supply × available inventory × search conversion × booking frequency ×
average booking value × retained take rate -> contribution after service cost`

Forecast separately:

- accommodation versus flights, cars, and adjacent services;
- agency versus merchant mix;
- direct/app/loyalty behavior versus paid acquisition;
- payment and fintech attachment;
- support, refund, fraud, and tax cost;
- technology and AI investment;
- acquisition spending and integration; and
- diluted shares and cash per share.

The lifecycle question is whether the company is still gaining control of a
growing travel workflow or is paying more to defend a mature search and
booking position against suppliers, regulators, and competing interfaces.

## Valuation framing

Use normalized retained contribution and owner cash, not gross bookings. A
scenario should stress:

- travel volume and average booking value;
- take rate and merchant mix;
- direct and app acquisition cost;
- payment, refund, fraud, and tax claims;
- alternative accommodation and flight attachment;
- AI-supported customer service savings;
- stock compensation and buyback price; and
- the market multiple applied to normalized cash.

The market should pay more for a durable travel interface than for a simple
referral site, but the premium requires evidence that the interface lowers
acquisition cost, increases repeat behavior, and retains contribution after
payment and support obligations.

### Initial owner-cash screen — 2026-09-14

The FY2025 Form 10-K reports `$9.409B` of operating cash flow and `$322M` of
PP&E purchases. Booking also reports about `$613M` of stock-based compensation.
The initial common-owner measure is therefore approximately `$8.474B` after
PP&E and SBC. The comparable figures were about `$6.469B` in FY2023 and
`$7.295B` in FY2024. These figures are not gross bookings: they are a cash
bridge after the platform's operating and capital claims, but they still need
working-capital, supplier-funds, payments, tax, acquisition, and dilution
normalization.

FY2025 common-stock repurchases were about `$6.440B` and dividends were about
`$1.248B`, or roughly `$7.688B` together. Long-term debt was about `$18.736B`.
The platform therefore returned almost all of the SBC-adjusted residual while
carrying a large debt and settlement system. Buybacks are not automatically
bad, but their value depends on take rate, direct behavior, and per-share cash
continuing to grow after the travel cycle turns.

A finance quote retrieved at `2026-09-15 00:15 UTC` showed a share price of
`$175.35` and equity value of approximately `$135.020B`. This is a dated market
input, not filing evidence or a price target.

| Screen | Bear | Base | Bull |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Normalized common-owner cash | `$7.000B` | `$9.000B` | `$11.000B` |
| Equity cash multiple | `12x` | `15x` | `18x` |
| Implied equity value | `$84.0B` | `$135.0B` | `$198.0B` |

The base case assumes travel demand and merchant mix remain healthy while
marketing, payment, refund, supplier, and support costs stay controlled. The
bear case assumes the interface must spend more to retain supply and demand,
with debt and buybacks limiting flexibility. The bull case requires direct
repeat behavior, profitable alternative accommodation and flight attachment,
and sustained cash per diluted share. The dated market value is approximately
the base case, leaving little room for lower take rate or higher acquisition
cost. This screen is not a price target.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Management can emphasize gross bookings, room nights, merchant mix, mobile
share, direct share, Genius participation, adjusted EBITDA less capex, and
Connected Trip adoption. Pair each measure with retained revenue, contribution,
supplier funds, marketing, payment cost, refunds, support, SBC, diluted shares,
and cash per share.

The thesis breaks if:

1. Gross bookings grow while retained take rate or contribution per booking
   weakens.
2. Merchant mix increases gross cash movement while settlement, refunds,
   chargebacks, or working capital absorb the benefit.
3. App and direct behavior grow only through discounts or loyalty cost.
4. Alternative accommodation, flights, cars, or adjacent products add volume
   without profitable attachment.
5. Regulatory or tax changes remove supply or create liabilities above recorded
   reserves.
6. Travel volume remains strong but marketing and supplier incentives rise
   faster than retained revenue.
7. Adjusted cash improves while SBC, acquisition cash, debt, or diluted shares
   prevent per-share value from improving.
