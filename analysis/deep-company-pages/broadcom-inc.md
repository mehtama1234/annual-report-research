# Broadcom Inc. — scaled silicon and infrastructure-software control

Research date: 2026-09-14  
Evidence window: FY2025 and FY2026 Q3  
Ticker: AVGO  
Evidence grade: A for reported financials; B for the durability of custom-AI demand, VMware retention, and the long-run balance between semiconductor concentration and software recurrence.

## Bottom line

Broadcom controls several narrow but important layers of computing infrastructure: custom AI accelerators, Ethernet and networking silicon, connectivity components, and infrastructure software used in private cloud, mainframe, cybersecurity, enterprise systems, and VMware environments. The economic case is not simply “AI growth.” It is that a small number of customers need Broadcom’s designs to make large systems work, and that the company can add software revenue to a deployed technical base.

The company also carries risks that a pure software analysis would miss. Semiconductor revenue depends on a small number of large customers, design cycles, product transitions, manufacturing capacity, and customer capital spending. Infrastructure software includes acquisition amortization, integration, renewals, pricing changes, and debt from the VMware transaction. A high adjusted margin can therefore coexist with substantial owner claims from acquisition-related amortization, stock compensation, interest, and future reinvestment.

FY2025 revenue was $63.887B, operating cash flow was $27.537B, and free cash flow was $26.914B. In Q3 FY2026, revenue was $29.591B, operating cash flow was $14.197B, and free cash flow was $13.665B. Semiconductor solutions were 70% of Q3 revenue and infrastructure software was 30%. AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7B in the quarter, up 221% year over year. These are powerful results, but they are also a high comparison point that increases the burden of proof for future growth.

## What Broadcom controls

The operating chain is:

`customer architecture -> Broadcom design and software integration -> outsourced manufacturing or installed software -> system deployment -> renewal, expansion, and next design cycle`

Broadcom’s control is strongest when its component is designed into a customer’s architecture, when replacing it requires qualification and software changes, and when the customer needs Broadcom to support a full technical roadmap. Custom accelerators and networking products can have high switching costs after design-in. VMware and other infrastructure software can have renewal and expansion value when they manage critical workloads.

Broadcom does not control hyperscaler budgets, customer architecture choices, foundry capacity, advanced packaging, export rules, open-source alternatives, or the price customers will accept. It also does not control whether the VMware transition produces durable retention or encourages customers to migrate away.

FY2025 operating cash flow increased from $19.962B in FY2024 to $27.537B, but
that comparison should not be treated as a clean organic trend. FY2024 included
approximately $26B of cash paid for acquisitions, principally VMware, while
FY2025 was the first full annual period after the transaction. The low PP&E
requirement makes the company-defined free-cash-flow margin look unusually high;
the economic acquisition cost, integration work, debt, interest, and dilution
must remain visible alongside it.

The annual comparison also changes how to read the software contribution. A
larger software share can improve recurrence and margins, but the VMware purchase
price was paid before the full retention, pricing, cross-sell, and cash-return
record was available. The correct test is not whether FY2025 cash flow rose; it
is whether incremental cash after integration, interest, recurring product
investment, and dilution justifies the capital committed.

## Two engines, two tests

### Semiconductor solutions

The semiconductor business provides the current growth engine. Custom AI accelerators and AI networking can become valuable control points because hyperscalers need performance, power efficiency, bandwidth, and system-specific design. Broadcom’s role is narrower than a general-purpose processor supplier but can be deeply embedded in a customer’s architecture.

The central risk is concentration. A few customers can account for a large share of demand, and a design win can be followed by a product transition, internal development, price negotiation, or a change in system architecture. AI orders and management forecasts must be reconciled to shipped products, revenue, gross margin, receivables, inventory, supplier commitments, and free cash flow.

The investor should also separate AI semiconductor growth from the rest of the semiconductor portfolio. Wireless, broadband, storage, industrial, and networking products have different cycles and margins. A single company-wide margin can hide a mix shift toward a very strong AI period or a later decline in older products.

### Infrastructure software

The software business can provide a more recurring base and can reduce dependence on each new chip cycle. VMware gives Broadcom access to private-cloud and virtualization infrastructure that customers may find difficult to replace quickly. Mainframe, cybersecurity, enterprise, and storage software add other installed relationships.

But software recurrence must be measured, not assumed. The filings and future disclosures should be tested for renewal rates, cancellations, customer migration, price increases, contract duration, deferred revenue, RPO, sales compensation, support cost, product investment, and free cash flow after interest. VMware pricing and packaging can increase near-term revenue while weakening long-term retention. A recurring invoice is not automatically a durable customer relationship.

## Earnings quality and cash conversion

Broadcom reports both GAAP and non-GAAP results. Its non-GAAP measures exclude amortization of acquisition-related intangible assets, stock compensation, restructuring and other charges, acquisition costs, integration costs, and tax adjustments. These exclusions can help compare ongoing operations, but several are recurring costs of the business model.

Acquisition amortization is a non-cash accounting charge, but it records the consumption of acquired customer relationships and technology. If Broadcom must keep buying businesses to replace lost growth, the economic acquisition cost has not disappeared. Stock compensation is also a claim on owners even when it does not reduce current-period cash. The correct owner measure is cash after sustaining investment, acquisition requirements, debt service, and dilution.

Working capital deserves special attention during AI growth. Revenue can rise before cash if receivables expand. Suppliers can finance part of the growth through payables, while inventory and purchase commitments can rise before demand is proven. The investor should compare revenue growth with receivables, inventory, contract liabilities, supplier commitments, and cash conversion over several quarters.

## Lyn Alden lens: liquidity, concentration, and funding

Broadcom benefits from large technology budgets and from the need to keep critical systems operating. It is not immune to tighter liquidity. Hyperscaler capital expenditure, interest rates, customer financing capacity, foundry availability, and the semiconductor cycle determine how quickly design demand becomes shipments and cash.

The VMware acquisition also changed the funding profile. Debt, interest, integration, and capital returns compete for the same cash. A strong liquidity position can let Broadcom continue research, support customers, and retire debt during a downturn. The stress test is whether free cash flow remains adequate if AI growth slows, software retention weakens, or a major customer delays a product program.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative is: Broadcom owns high-value infrastructure control points, combines semiconductor growth with software recurrence, and can compound cash through disciplined acquisition and capital allocation.

That narrative must become measurable assumptions:

- AI accelerator and networking revenue after the current buildout;
- number and economic importance of major semiconductor customers;
- gross margin and cash conversion by semiconductor category;
- design-win duration, replacement risk, and customer concentration;
- VMware renewal, migration, pricing, and net retention;
- software deferred revenue, RPO, support cost, and product investment;
- acquisition amortization, integration costs, and the return on VMware capital;
- stock compensation, diluted shares, debt, interest, dividends, and buybacks;
- sustaining research and development and required supplier commitments;
- terminal cash generation after AI growth normalizes.

At a market value of approximately $1.728T on September 14, 2026, Broadcom was valued at roughly 64 times FY2025 free cash flow. A simple screen is:

| Normalized annual owner-cash screen | Multiple | Implied equity value |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $22B bear case | 30x | $660B |
| $30B base case | 40x | $1.200T |
| $40B strong case | 50x | $2.000T |

These are scenario outputs, not price targets. The base case requires AI semiconductor cash to remain high after the cycle matures, the software base to retain customers after VMware pricing changes, acquisition and interest costs to decline relative to cash generation, and dilution to remain controlled. The current price therefore embeds a demanding combination of growth, durability, and capital allocation.

## Thesis breakers and next evidence

The thesis weakens if AI semiconductor revenue depends on one or two customers whose programs are delayed; if revenue grows while receivables, inventory, or supplier commitments grow faster; if semiconductor gross margin falls as customers gain bargaining power; if VMware pricing causes cancellations or migration; if infrastructure software growth is mostly acquired or price-driven; if stock compensation remains a large recurring claim; if debt and interest limit capital allocation; or if management repeatedly uses adjusted earnings to exclude costs required to preserve the platform.

The next filings should answer: Did Q4 AI revenue and total revenue meet guidance without a working-capital distortion? Did AI gross margin and cash conversion remain strong? What share of software revenue renewed, and how did VMware retention change after pricing and packaging actions? Did debt decline? Did diluted shares fall after repurchases? Did the company disclose enough customer and product detail to test concentration rather than asking investors to infer durability from total revenue?
