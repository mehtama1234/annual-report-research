# Bunge Global — crop-flow infrastructure, working capital, and Viterra integration

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed U.S. GAAP figures; dollar amounts
are in millions.

## How to read this memo

Origination means buying crops from farmers or local suppliers. Crush means
processing oilseeds into meal and oil. Mark-to-market means recording an asset
or liability at its current market value even before the related sale is
completed. Working capital is cash tied up in inventory, receivables, and
payables. A value-at-risk or hedging measure describes price exposure; it does
not prove that a trading position will be profitable.

## Investment question

Bunge connects farmers to food, animal feed, biofuels, and industrial customers
through origination, storage, transport, processing, merchandising, and risk
management. The Viterra combination enlarged that network. The question is
whether the additional scale creates durable margins and cost savings after
inventory financing, commodity-price marks, freight, debt, integration costs,
working-capital swings, and the capital needed for new processing facilities.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Bunge operates four economically different lanes: soybean processing and
  refining, softseed processing and refining, other oilseeds and specialty
  ingredients, and grain merchandising and milling. These should not be valued
  as one uniform commodity margin.
- The Viterra combination expanded origination, grain handling, logistics,
  processing, and customer reach. Management reported more than $70M of cost
  synergies by year-end 2025. The next test is whether synergies become cash
  after integration costs and working-capital funding.
- Q1 2026 shows the accounting presentation risk: diluted EPS was $0.35 while
  adjusted EPS was $1.83. Commodity, freight, foreign-exchange contracts, and
  readily marketable inventory can create GAAP timing differences. Those marks
  are part of the business, not automatically noise, and must be reconciled to
  realized cash results.
- Q2 2026 adjusted total EBIT improved to about $665M, with stronger soybean and
  softseed processing. A strong quarter can reflect temporary crush spreads,
  regional crop conditions, or policy changes rather than durable network power.
- Biofuel policy is a real demand variable. Renewable volume obligations,
  renewable diesel, and sustainable aviation fuel can increase demand for
  soybean and other oils, but policy can change and new supply can compress
  margins.
- Traceability is becoming a commercial requirement. Monitoring soy and palm
  sourcing may protect access to regulated and reputation-sensitive markets, but
  it also adds systems, supplier, and compliance cost.
- Viterra increased debt, inventory, receivables, goodwill, and integration
  complexity. The larger network can redirect crops when trade routes or weather
  change, but that flexibility is valuable only if it produces returns above the
  financing and asset costs.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. What is the recurring processing and merchandising margin?

Separate realized crush spreads, origination margins, freight, storage,
specialty ingredients, biofuel-related demand, and mark-to-market changes.
Compare realized cash margins with reported segment EBIT across several crop and
price environments.

### 2. Are adjusted results removing normal business risk?

Reconcile GAAP earnings to adjusted earnings. Identify derivative marks,
inventory revaluations, foreign exchange, restructuring, integration costs,
asset sales, and acquisition accounting. A recurring cost cannot be treated as
one-time merely because management labels it an adjustment each quarter.

### 3. Does Viterra earn its purchase price?

Track acquired revenue, EBIT, cash flow, synergies, integration spending,
working capital, capex, debt, goodwill, and return on invested capital. Compare
the original synergy schedule with realized cash savings, not only adjusted EBIT.

### 4. Is working capital controlled through the cycle?

Track inventory days, basis and flat-price exposure, receivable aging, supplier
payables, collateral, seasonal borrowing, and cash released when commodity
prices fall. A fall in inventory can increase cash temporarily while reducing
future trading capacity.

### 5. Who carries policy and traceability costs?

Measure the cost of sourcing controls, certifications, supplier monitoring,
lower-carbon investments, and customer-specific requirements. Test whether
customers pay for those costs or whether they reduce Bunge’s margin.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** food, feed, and fuel are physical systems exposed to
  weather, energy, trade, inflation, interest rates, and geopolitics. Network
  density can improve resilience, but large inventory and debt make liquidity
  important when prices move quickly.
- **Damodaran lens:** value processing assets, merchandising, specialty
  ingredients, and Viterra synergies separately. Use mid-cycle spreads and
  explicit working-capital and capex requirements; do not value the company on
  revenue or a peak commodity margin.
- **Forensic lens:** the main category errors are treating revenue as value
  added, adjusted EPS as cash, mark-to-market changes as irrelevant, inventory
  as a free asset, synergies as earned before integration, and debt-funded
  acquisitions as immediately accretive.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $23.52B of equity value at
$122.41 per share. The FY2025 post-acquisition residual was negative, so a
current cash multiple is not meaningful. A normalized range of $0.5B / $1.0B /
$1.5B at 12x / 15x / 18x implies $6B / $15B / $27B. The market therefore
requires a normalized cash recovery, successful Viterra integration, stable
working-capital funding, and durable processing or merchandising margins.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Viterra synergies remain adjusted earnings rather than realized cash.
2. Crush spreads, origination margins, or specialty-ingredient returns revert
   without enough network benefit to cover debt and fixed assets.
3. Inventory and receivables absorb cash during a price or crop-flow shock.
4. Debt, collateral, or seasonal financing becomes expensive or unavailable.
5. Biofuel policy changes reduce oilseed demand or compress the value of new
   feedstock investments.
6. Goodwill or acquired assets require impairment after the combination.

Next filing checks: segment EBIT to cash; mark-to-market reconciliation;

traceability costs; dividends; and buybacks after acquisition funding.
