# Cameco Corporation — nuclear-fuel contracting, inventory, and Westinghouse exposure

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed IFRS figures in Canadian dollars
unless explicitly identified otherwise; dollar amounts are in millions.

## How to read this memo

Uranium is the fuel input used in nuclear reactors. Conversion changes uranium
into a chemical form that can be enriched. Fuel services include conversion,
fabrication, and related processing. An equity-method investment is a company
owned partly by Cameco whose profit is recorded as Cameco’s share of the
investment’s earnings rather than as consolidated revenue and expenses. Owner
cash here means operating cash flow after productive-asset spending; it is a
screen, not a complete valuation measure.

## Investment question

Cameco is a nuclear-fuel platform with uranium mines, procurement and inventory
management, fuel-services conversion, long-term customer contracts, and a
strategic stake in Westinghouse. The question is whether nuclear fuel scarcity
and long-term reactor demand become durable cash for Cameco shareholders after
mine sustaining capital, conversion investment, delivery timing, inventory,
joint-venture claims, Westinghouse project risk, and political restrictions.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Cameco’s production was 21.0 million pounds on its share basis in 2025, but
  deliveries were 33.0 million pounds. The difference shows why production
  volume alone cannot explain annual revenue or cash. Procurement, inventory,
  contract timing, and customer delivery obligations matter.
- The company reported long-term commitments to deliver about 230 million
  pounds after completing 2025 deliveries, with average annual deliveries of
  about 28 million pounds over the following five years. This provides demand
  visibility, but it also creates an obligation to source or produce material
  at an acceptable cost.
- Uranium inventory ended 2025 at 9.7 million pounds with an average cost of
  $61.85 per pound. A rising market can increase the economic value of existing
  inventory, but the cash result depends on when inventory is bought, sold, and
  replaced.
- Fuel Services produced 14.0 million kgU in 2025, including record UF6 output
  at Port Hope. Conversion capacity makes Cameco less dependent on mine output,
  but the conversion business has its own contracts, maintenance, regulation,
  and pricing cycle.
- Westinghouse makes the earnings stream less transparent than a mining-only
  company. Q2 2026 and the first half of 2026 were weaker partly because
  Cameco’s share of Westinghouse earnings fell. Reactor construction and service
  timing can therefore change reported earnings without a matching change in
  uranium production.
- Cameco’s balance sheet gives it time to manage a long contracting cycle. That
  is valuable, but it does not eliminate project execution, regulatory,
  sanctions, export-control, or reactor-customer risks.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. How much profit is cash from uranium deliveries?

Reconcile profit attributable to owners to operating cash flow. Separate
non-cash equity-method earnings, contract timing, inventory purchases and
releases, receivables, taxes, and working capital. A strong cash year can result
from selling inventory accumulated earlier and may require later replenishment.

### 2. Does contract coverage improve returns or transfer price risk?

Read the contract mix: fixed-price, escalated-price, market-related, floor and
ceiling terms, delivery dates, volume flexibility, and penalties. Long-term
commitments reduce demand uncertainty, but Cameco may still bear uranium-price,
production, procurement, and replacement-cost risk.

### 3. Are mines being maintained at the reported cost?

Separate sustaining capital from expansion capital and compare planned versus
actual production, grades, recovery, operating cost, reclamation, and mine-life
assumptions. Uranium reserves are not current cash; they become cash only after
permits, labor, processing, capital, and customer delivery are secured.

### 4. What does Westinghouse contribute to common cash?

Track Cameco’s share of Westinghouse earnings, distributions, project margins,
working capital, guarantees, construction claims, and required investment.
Equity-method earnings can increase reported profit without appearing as
consolidated revenue or operating cash in the same period.

### 5. Does the balance sheet remain resilient under a delayed cycle?

Test cash, debt maturities, joint-venture funding, inventory purchases, mine
capital, conversion maintenance, dividends, and political restrictions under
lower uranium prices or delayed reactor projects. The current balance sheet is
strong, but the test is whether it stays strong while the company honors its
contract book.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** nuclear fuel is a strategic physical input with long lead
  times, limited qualified supply, and policy-driven demand. Energy security can
  support long contracts and investment, but political intervention, high
  financing costs, and project delays can reduce the cash value of scarcity.
- **Damodaran lens:** value uranium mining, Fuel Services, inventory and
  contracting, and Westinghouse separately. Use mid-cycle uranium prices and
  explicit sustaining capital. Assign project value only after modeling timing,
  capital, probability of completion, and the share belonging to Cameco.
- **Forensic lens:** the main category errors are treating uranium demand as
  current cash, production as deliveries, contract backlog as margin, inventory
  gains as recurring earnings, equity-method profit as consolidated cash, and
  Westinghouse optionality as though all reactor projects already earn returns.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately US$42.02B of equity value at
US$96.68 per share. Using an approximate 0.72 CAD/USD conversion, FY2025’s
CAD $1.075B residual is about US$0.77B, or roughly 54x that screening residual.
A normalized range of US$0.45B / US$0.80B / US$1.30B at 25x / 30x / 35x implies
US$11.25B / US$24.0B / US$45.5B. The current market value therefore requires
more than the filed 2025 residual: it requires sustained uranium pricing,
successful contract execution, credible mine supply, and meaningful value from
Westinghouse and Fuel Services.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Uranium contract prices or volumes fail to support mine and conversion
   returns after sustaining capital.
2. Production shortfalls force expensive procurement or inventory drawdown.
3. Mine expansion, permitting, reclamation, or operating costs rise faster than
   realized contract prices.
4. Westinghouse earnings, distributions, or project margins weaken while the
   market continues to assign strategic value to the investment.
5. Sanctions, export controls, national fuel policies, or customer delays
   restrict deliveries or cash movement.
6. Inventory and receivables absorb cash as reported earnings remain strong.
