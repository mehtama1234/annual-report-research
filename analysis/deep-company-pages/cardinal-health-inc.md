# Cardinal Health, Inc. Full Forensic Analysis Memo

Date baseline: 2026-09-13  
Primary source: [Cardinal Health FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/721371/000072137125000079/cah-20250630.htm)  
Companion packet: [company-packet.md](/home/mehtama1/git-repo/annual-report-research/extracted/healthcare/drug-stores/cardinal-health-inc/company-packet.md)

## Bottom line

Cardinal Health is an institutional healthcare channel: it distributes
pharmaceuticals and medical products to pharmacies, hospitals, health systems,
ambulatory surgery centers, laboratories, physician offices, and patients at
home. Its valuable control point is continuity of supply plus specialty,
logistics, at-home, nuclear/precision-health, and provider workflow services.

FY2025 revenue was $222.6B, but revenue is not the economic denominator.
Operating cash flow was $2.397B, while the company spent $547M on capital
expenditures and $5.3B on acquisitions. It also returned $765M through buybacks
and $494M through dividends, using $2.9B of new long-term debt proceeds and an
$800M term loan to fund part of the acquisition program. Cardinal's recurring
distribution franchise may be durable, but its reported 2025 cash economics
were shaped by the unwinding of negative working capital associated with an
expired OptumRx contract, acquisitions, opioid payments, and a customer
contract transition.

The core question is whether Cardinal is becoming a higher-quality specialty
and services platform, or whether acquisition intensity and working-capital
normalization are making adjusted EPS look cleaner than sustainable owner cash.
This memo identifies tests and does not establish fraud.

## What Cardinal controls

The economic sequence is:

`manufacturer access -> regulated distribution and inventory -> provider/pharmacy workflow -> specialty, logistics, and at-home services -> cash`

Cardinal operates primarily through Pharmaceutical and Specialty Solutions and
Global Medical Products and Distribution, with Other containing nuclear and
precision health, at-home solutions, and OptiFreight logistics. The platform
is embedded in care delivery, but suppliers, large customers, reimbursement,
tariffs, and regulation retain bargaining power.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. The OptumRx contract expiry distorted both sales and cash

Revenue fell 2% as reported in FY2025 because of a previously communicated
customer contract expiration; management described 18% growth excluding that
effect. Operating cash flow included the unwinding of negative working capital
associated with the expired contract and ordinary vendor-payment timing.

This is a legitimate transition effect, not evidence of manipulation. The
forensic test is to separate lost contract revenue and working capital from
organic growth, new-customer onboarding, and recurring cash. A business can
show strong comparable growth after a contract exit while its balance sheet is
still normalizing.

### 2. Acquisitions dominate the 2025 cash story

Cardinal deployed approximately $5.3B on acquisitions, including MSO-platform
and Advanced Diabetes Supply-related expansion. The acquired service layer may
improve the moat, but it creates goodwill, integration, physician/customer
retention, and working-capital risk. Track purchase-price allocation, acquired
gross profit, revenue retention, integration costs, impairment, and capital
return by cohort.

The key bridge is:

`acquisition price -> incremental gross profit -> integration and working capital -> recurring cash -> debt reduction`

### 3. GAAP versus non-GAAP divergence is economically material

FY2025 GAAP operating earnings were approximately $2.3B and non-GAAP operating
earnings approximately $2.8B; GAAP diluted EPS was $6.45 versus non-GAAP EPS of
$8.24. The gap may reflect real impairments, acquisition, litigation, and
portfolio items, but a recurring acquisition-and-remediation program can make
those “adjustments” part of the operating model.

The next filing should classify each adjustment by recurrence, cash cost,
business segment, and whether it is required to maintain the strategic platform.

### 4. Working capital and litigation are central, not peripheral

FY2025 cash flow included an $833M increase in trade receivables, a $1.816B
inventory cash use, and a $2.732B accounts-payable contribution. Cardinal also
paid $403M related to opioid litigation in the quarter chain and disclosed
substantial settlement exposure. The company can be operationally essential
while still having cash conversion that varies sharply with vendor terms,
customer contracts, inventory, and legal payments.

### 5. Goodwill impairment tests the service-platform thesis

The Q3 FY2026 packet records a $184M pre-tax goodwill impairment in the Navista
& ION reporting unit. Although it is outside FY2025, it is an important forward
test of the acquisition strategy. A service adjacency should not be valued as
a durable platform until the acquired reporting units demonstrate cash returns
without repeated impairment or margin support.

## Alden lens: essential channel, thin spread, large financial flow

Cardinal's demand is resilient because healthcare products must move, but the
business funds enormous inventories and receivables on thin spreads. At
June 30, 2025 cash was $3.9B, and trade payables were approximately $34.7B.
Liquidity depends on supplier terms, customer credit, bank facilities,
securitized receivables, and continued operating cash flow.

The durability chain is:

`essential care demand -> channel volume -> supplier/customer terms -> gross-profit spread -> normalized cash -> debt, legal, and reinvestment capacity`

Higher rates, tariffs, product shortages, reimbursement changes, or customer
contract loss can pressure cash even when end demand remains strong.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to valuation

The valuation should split:

`pharmaceutical distribution volume -> specialty and branded mix -> medical-product/logistics services -> working-capital requirement -> acquisition returns -> legal and debt claims -> owner cash`

At the September 11, 2026 market snapshot, CAH equity value was approximately
$55.36B. That is about 23.1x FY2025 operating cash flow and about 29.9x the
$1.85B residual after capex but before acquisitions. Once acquisitions are
included, current-year residual is negative. The valuation therefore requires
both normalized working capital and successful acquisition cohorts.

The reverse valuation should vary gross-profit margin, contract churn,
specialty and at-home growth, DSO/DIO/DPO, opioid/legal cash, tariffs,
acquisition integration, capex, debt cost, buybacks, and share dilution.

## Incentives and thesis breakers

Management is naturally rewarded for revenue growth, non-GAAP operating profit,
EPS, adjusted free cash flow, acquisitions, service-platform expansion, debt
reduction, and capital returns. Those measures can improve through contract
mix, working-capital timing, leverage, and add-backs without producing a
proportionate increase in durable owner cash.

The thesis is materially weakened if future filings show:

- revenue and adjusted EPS growth without normalized gross-profit and cash
  growth;
- vendor terms supplying cash while receivables, inventory, or customer credit
  stress rises;
- acquired service platforms require recurring impairment, integration cost,
  or margin subsidies;
- opioid, tariff, contract-transition, or compliance cash costs recur as
  “non-GAAP” exclusions; or
- acquisitions and buybacks continue to require debt while post-growth cash
  remains negative.

## Evidence grade and unresolved questions

- **Filing fact:** FY2025 segment results, operating cash flow, capex,
  acquisitions, contract transition, working capital, litigation, goodwill, and
  debt are anchored to the annual filing.
- **Disclosed/packet-backed:** specialty, at-home, and latest-quarter outlook
  claims are management-reported until gross-profit and cash conversion confirm
  them.
- **Inference:** Cardinal controls a high-volume healthcare distribution and
  service workflow, but earns thin spreads while carrying inventory, credit,
  contract, legal, and regulatory burdens.
- **Unresolved:** the next filing must distinguish vendor-term cash support from
  durable margin and cash, and show acquired-platform returns after litigation,
  contract transition, and working-capital normalization.

## Primary-filing verification and acquisition-cash bridge

acquisition payments of **$5.250B**, share-based compensation of **$244M**, and

acquisition returns, contract transition, working-capital timing, and debt
funding. Revenue scale remains a weak proxy for shareholder economics.
