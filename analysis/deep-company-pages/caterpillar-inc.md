# Caterpillar Inc. — the installed-base machine, dealer balance sheet, and the cost of backlog

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed GAAP figures unless explicitly identified otherwise; dollar amounts are in millions.

## Investment question

Caterpillar is a high-quality physical-infrastructure franchise, but the investment case is more complicated than “record backlog plus AI power demand.” The company combines heavy equipment, Power & Energy, mining, construction, dealers, Financial Products, and a large aftermarket base. The central question is whether its control over the installed machine ecosystem produces durable owner cash through the cycle, or whether dealer inventory, financing receivables, tariffs, capex, and financial leverage absorb the apparent backlog quality.

Related first-principles explanation: [Caterpillar installed base and machine economics](../first-principles/caterpillar-installed-base-machine-economics.md).

## Q2 2026 checkpoint: record revenue, but a more demanding cash test

Q2 2026 sales and revenues reached `$20.5B`, the first quarter above `$20B` in
Caterpillar's history. GAAP profit per share was `$7.77`, adjusted profit per
share was `$8.17`, and operating profit margin was `20.9%`. Power & Energy
sales were `$8.238B`, Construction Industries sales were `$8.346B`, and
Resource Industries sales were `$4.648B`.

Those figures strengthen the demand case, but they do not resolve the owner-cash
question. The quarter included expected recovery of about `$392M` of IEEPA
tariff costs. That recovery may compensate for real prior costs, but it should
not be capitalized as permanent pricing power. The normalized margin test is:

`reported operating profit - tariff recovery - other temporary policy effects = underlying operating profit`

Q1 2026 management commentary put backlog near `$63B`, above the `$51.2B` at
the end of FY2025. The increase improves visibility, but it also raises the
working-capital and manufacturing test. The next filing should reconcile the
backlog increase with dealer inventory, customer acceptance, receivables,
factory throughput, and cash collections. A record backlog can coexist with
inventory accumulation or customer-financing growth.

Caterpillar also repurchased about `$5.0B` of shares in Q1 2026 and spent
another `$2.2B` on capital returns in Q2. This is a material management-choice
signal. Buybacks can be sensible when normalized cash is durable, but they
reduce flexibility if tariffs, dealer support, Cat Financial losses, or the
planned increase in capital spending weaken cash later in the cycle. The next
quarterly bridge should therefore show cash after capex, finance-receivable
funding, dealer support, dividends, repurchases, and warranty obligations—not
only adjusted profit per share.

## What the annual report reveals

- Firm backlog rose to approximately $51.2B from $30.0B at year-end 2024, with $19.3B not expected to be filled in 2026. The backlog is meaningful but time-distributed; only part of it supports near-term earnings.
- Power & Energy was the largest source of backlog growth, connecting Caterpillar to data-center prime power and grid demand. That is a valuable adjacency, but it remains equipment revenue with manufacturing and service obligations.
- Services revenue was approximately $24B, giving Caterpillar an installed-base layer that can stabilize equipment cycles. The key question is service attach, price realization, component availability, and whether services margins remain strong when equipment utilization softens.
- Dealers are independent and economically important. Caterpillar can influence dealer inventory but does not own the full channel balance sheet. The filing says machine dealer inventory decreased about $500M in 2025 while total dealer inventory increased about $900M; 2026 expectations include more than $1B of machine dealer inventory build in Q1. This creates a direct channel-stuffing/timing test without implying wrongdoing.
- Cat Financial makes equipment more affordable and supports sales, but it converts equipment demand into a credit portfolio. Additions to finance receivables were $15.329B in 2025 and collections were $13.515B. The allowance for credit losses was $277M, and write-offs were $148M. This is an earnings-quality and cycle-risk lens, not merely a financing footnote.
- Tariffs are material. Management estimated about $2.6B of 2026 tariff impact before mitigation, while Q2 2026 included $392M of expected IEEPA tariff recoveries. Any margin comparison must separate durable price/cost power from policy timing and customer pass-through.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Is backlog real demand or channel timing?

Backlog is firm by Caterpillar’s definition, but equipment can still be delayed, reprioritized, or delivered into a dealer system before end-user utilization is proven. Track dealer inventory, machine rental utilization, order cancellations, customer advances, and receivables against backlog. A backlog increase paired with dealer inventory and receivable growth is weaker than an increase paired with end-user cash collections.

### 2. Is Financial Products a stabilizer or a hidden cyclical lever?

Cat Financial is a strategic moat because financing helps customers buy expensive equipment. It also creates credit losses, funding requirements, residual-value risk, and an earnings stream that can look service-like in good conditions. The next filing should test delinquencies, charge-offs, allowances, used-equipment values, funding spreads, and whether financing growth is required to support unit volume.

### 3. Does the installed base actually produce cleaner cash?

Services revenue and parts are attractive because they repeat across a large fleet. But the installed base also requires field labor, parts inventory, warranty support, dealer density, and engineering. A service margin expansion that comes with rising inventory and warranty claims is not the same as a clean recurring platform.

### 4. Are capital returns pro-cyclical?

Repurchases of $5.190B and dividends of $2.788B nearly consumed the post-capex residual. The company can sustain this while cash is strong, but the test is whether repurchases slow before a downturn or continue while debt, dealer support, and Financial Products reserves rise. Capital allocation should be evaluated across a full cycle, not during record backlog.

### 5. Are tariff recoveries being mistaken for structural pricing power?

The Q2 2026 tariff recovery improves reported economics, but it is not a permanent operating advantage. Model 2026 margins both with and without policy recoveries, then test whether customers accept durable price increases after tariffs fade.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** Caterpillar monetizes real-economy capital formation, energy intensity, infrastructure scarcity, and the replacement value of an installed machine fleet. It is exposed to commodity cycles, rates, industrial policy, dealer credit, and global liquidity. The durability comes from network and installed-base density, not from immunity to the cycle.
- **Damodaran lens:** separate mature machinery earnings, services, Power & Energy growth, Resource Industries commodity exposure, and Cat Financial. Backlog is a narrative input; owner cash requires explicit reinvestment, credit losses, inventory, and capital-return assumptions. The lifecycle is late-cycle in some equipment markets even if data-center power creates a new growth leg.
- **Forensic lens:** the key possible “shenanigan” is not a single accounting trick. It is the risk that backlog, dealer inventory, financing growth, tariff recovery, and adjusted margins collectively make a cyclical system look more recurring and less capital-intensive than it is.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $378.6B of equity value, or about 42.7x FY2025 mechanical owner cash of $8.871B. A normalized range of $6.5B / $9.0B / $11.5B at 25x / 32x / 38x produces $162.5B / $288B / $437B. The current price therefore requires something close to the bull case: sustained backlog conversion, strong services, successful Power & Energy growth, controlled Financial Products losses, and continued capital returns despite higher capex.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Dealer inventory rises faster than end-user demand and later reverses.
2. Cat Financial delinquencies, charge-offs, or residual-value losses rise with rates or used-equipment weakness.
3. Power & Energy backlog converts at lower margins than the narrative implies.
4. Tariff recoveries or price realization fade before manufacturing costs normalize.
5. Capital returns remain aggressive while post-capex cash falls and debt/funding needs rise.

Next filing checks: dealer inventory by region; finance receivable aging and allowance coverage; service revenue and margin; backlog cancellations and delivery timing; tariff recoveries versus underlying price; inventory turns; warranty roll-forward; and repurchases relative to normalized cash.
