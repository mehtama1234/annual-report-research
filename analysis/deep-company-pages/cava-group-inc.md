# CAVA Group, Inc. Full Forensic Analysis Memo

Date baseline: 2026-09-13  
Primary source: [CAVA 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1639438/000162828026011296/cava-20251228.htm)  
Companion packet: [company-packet.md](/home/mehtama1/git-repo/annual-report-research/extracted/services/restaurants/cava-group-inc/company-packet.md)

## Bottom line

CAVA is the restaurant lane's company-operated control case. It owns the
guest relationship, menu, labor execution, real-estate decisions, data, and
store-level operating outcome rather than collecting a royalty from an
independent franchisee. That creates more direct upside if the concept scales,
but also means the parent retains the labor, occupancy, opening, maintenance,
training, food, delivery, and lease burden.

The 2025 filing shows real operating progress: 439 restaurants, 4.0% same-
restaurant sales growth, $2.934M AUV, $285.0M of restaurant-level profit, and
$184.8M of operating cash flow. But restaurant-level profit margin slipped to
24.4% from 25.0%, while $158.7M of PP&E purchases and $134.7M of debt-security
purchases made reported investing cash flow negative. Consolidated net income
was only $63.7M, and the company carried $624.9M of operating-lease payments
in its disclosed cash commitments.

The clean thesis is a differentiated, healthy-food occasion with strong unit
economics and substantial runway. The hard question is whether mature-store
cash returns remain attractive after corporate overhead, pre-opening costs,
technology, manufacturing, lease commitments, stock compensation, and the
capital required to open 73 restaurants in a year.

## Data lineage warning

The local bounded packet says 367 locations and 58 net openings. Those figures
correspond to the preceding fiscal-year snapshot; the current SEC 2025 Form
10-K reports 367 beginning restaurants, 73 openings, one closure, and 439
ending restaurants. The memo uses the 2025 10-K for FY2025 numbers and retains
the packet only as contextual source material. This is itself a useful control:
annual-report packets must be date-checked before cross-company comparisons.

## What CAVA controls

The economic sequence is:

`brand and guest occasions -> restaurant traffic and AUV -> food/labor/occupancy execution -> store contribution -> openings and corporate cash`

CAVA participates directly in eat-in, takeout, catering, loyalty, and digital
occasions. Unlike RBI, YUM, and Wingstop, the parent does not transfer most
restaurant-level economics to franchisees. It can learn directly from store
operations and control hospitality, but it also pays the full cost of getting
that experience delivered.

The $26.1M after-PP&E figure aligns with management's reported free-cash-flow

The debt-security purchases are treasury management rather than restaurant
capex, but they explain why cash declined even while operating cash flow
improved.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. The 2024 tax benefit makes GAAP growth comparisons misleading

2024 net income was $130.3M, including an $80.1M benefit from release of a
valuation allowance. CAVA's 2025 net income of $63.7M therefore looks like a
large decline, but adjusted net income for 2024 excluding that benefit was
$50.2M. The proper comparison is recurring operating performance and cash,
not unadjusted 2024 EPS versus 2025 EPS.

This is disclosed tax accounting, not evidence of a shenanigan. It is a
classic quality-of-earnings normalization requirement.

### 2. Restaurant-level margin is useful but incomplete

Restaurant-level margin declined 60 basis points to 24.4% even as AUV rose to
$2.934M and same-restaurant sales increased 4.0%. Food, beverage, and
packaging were $348.7M, labor was $301.9M, occupancy was $83.6M, and other
operating expenses were $150.1M. Other operating expense includes repairs,
utilities, recruiting, card fees, delivery fees, marketing, and distribution.

The margin decline may reflect investments in wages, menu pricing, growth,
delivery, or mix. It does not by itself indicate manipulation. The next filing
should separate price from traffic, new-store maturation from mature-store
performance, and wage/food inflation from productivity.

### 3. Growth capex can make operating cash look better than owner economics

Operating cash flow rose 14.8% to $184.8M, but PP&E purchases rose 46.8% to
$158.7M as openings accelerated. The company expects 2026 capital expenditures
to be higher than 2025. A business can report rising OCF while its true
owner-cash return remains modest if every year requires a large opening and
technology program.

The key test is cohort economics:

`opening investment -> ramp and mature AUV -> restaurant profit -> maintenance capex and lease burden -> payback and return on incremental capital`

### 4. Equity compensation is small relative to sales but material to early
shareholder economics

Equity-based compensation was $15.2M in 2025 and $13.6M in 2024. CAVA had
116.1M shares outstanding at year-end versus 115.1M a year earlier, and
tax-withholding cash flows related to RSUs were $24.7M in 2024. In a high-
multiple growth company, dilution and option/RSU economics should be charged
against owner returns even when reported cash flow adds back the expense.

## Alden lens: demand durability versus fixed operating commitments

CAVA's Mediterranean/wellness positioning may create a durable food occasion,
but the macro transmission is direct. Consumers can trade down, while the
company still pays wages, rent, food, utilities, technology, and lease
obligations. At year-end, cash and fixed-income investments totaled roughly
$393M, with $624.9M of operating-lease payments disclosed across the life of
the commitments.

The durability chain is:

`affordable repeat demand -> traffic and frequency -> store labor productivity -> mature-store margin -> opening returns -> self-funded growth`

The balance sheet is currently less debt-stressed than the franchisors, but
lease commitments and expansion capex are real fixed claims. A consumer
slowdown or labor/food shock would hit CAVA's P&L and cash directly rather than
first appearing in a franchisee's finances.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to valuation

The model should not capitalize AUV or restaurant count directly. It should
translate:

`439 stores -> mature-store sales and traffic -> restaurant-level margin -> G&A/pre-opening/technology -> maintenance and growth capex -> free cash flow -> incremental return on new stores`

At the September 11, 2026 market snapshot, CAVA's equity value was approximately
$6.615B and the price was $55.88. That is roughly 35.8x 2025 operating cash
flow and about 253x the $26.1M after-PP&E residual. These are screening ratios,
not a price target. They show that the market requires a long runway of
successful openings and margin durability; current accounting earnings cannot
support the valuation without growth.

The reverse valuation should vary same-store traffic, AUV, restaurant-level
margin, opening cadence, store build cost, ramp period, maintenance capex,
lease cost, G&A leverage, SBC dilution, and terminal unit density. If growth
slows, the valuation is exposed because the current residual is largely being
reinvested.

### Valuation screen

The scenario register uses approximately `$6.615B` of equity value for the
screen:

| Case | Normalized owner-cash screen | Applied multiple | Implied equity value | What must be true |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bear | `$40M` | `40x` | `$1.6B` | Mature-store traffic weakens, margin remains under pressure, and openings consume cash without adequate payback |
| Base | `$100M` | `50x` | `$5.0B` | CAVA reaches a larger mature-store base, holds acceptable AUV and margin, and growth capex becomes more self-funded |
| Bull | `$180M` | `60x` | `$10.8B` | New-store cohorts earn strong returns, traffic expands beyond price, corporate costs leverage, and the brand supports a long runway |

The current snapshot is above the base-case value and below the bull case.
That makes the central investment question explicit: the market requires a
large improvement from the `$26.1M` 2025 cash-after-PP&E screen. The bull case
cannot be supported by restaurant count alone; it requires mature-store cash
returns, disciplined opening costs, stable leases, margin resilience, and
limited dilution. This is a scenario screen, not a price target.

## Incentives and thesis breakers

Management is naturally rewarded for restaurant openings, AUV, same-store
sales, restaurant-level margin, adjusted EBITDA, total revenue, and stock-price
growth. Those measures can improve while mature-store returns weaken if new
units, price, or adjusted exclusions dominate the scorecard.

The thesis is materially weakened if future filings show:

- same-store sales driven by price while traffic and frequency decline;
- AUV rising but restaurant-level margin falling through food, labor,
  occupancy, delivery, or maintenance pressure;
- new-store cohorts requiring more capital or taking longer to reach mature
  economics;
- operating cash flow repeatedly failing to cover PP&E, lease obligations,
  technology, and SBC-adjusted owner needs; or
- corporate G&A, pre-opening expense, impairment, or manufacturing investment
  scaling faster than restaurant-level profit.

## Evidence grade and unresolved questions

- **Filing fact:** FY2025 three-year financials, cash flow, PP&E, leases,
  repurchases, SBC, tax accounting, and restaurant-level margin are anchored to
  the Form 10-K.
- **Disclosed/packet-backed:** 2026 comparable sales, AUV, traffic/check mix,
  openings, and management's growth framing come from the earnings materials and
  company packet.
- **Inference:** CAVA controls a differentiated restaurant brand and operating
  format, but unlike a franchisor it carries the labor, food, occupancy, and
  build-out burden directly.
- **Unresolved:** the next filing must show mature-store cash returns and cohort
  payback after the current opening pace, while separating price from traffic and
  growth capex from maintenance capex. Repeated residual cash shortfalls or
  slowing cohorts would break the growth valuation.
