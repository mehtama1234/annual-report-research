## Bottom line

Cencora connects drug manufacturers, pharmacies, health systems, physician
practices, and patients through wholesale distribution, specialty access,
logistics, commercialization, and related services. Its control point is
reliable product flow and access coordination, not drug discovery or insurance
underwriting.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was about `$321.3B`, operating cash flow was about `$3.875B`,
capital expenditures were about `$668M`, and cash used for the Retina Consultants
of America acquisition and related investments was about `$4.292B`. The strict
post-growth residual was therefore approximately negative `$1.085B` before
other owner claims. This is the central forensic finding: Cencora can produce
large recurring operating cash while still requiring substantial external
capital or balance-sheet capacity to fund specialty expansion and acquisitions.

GAAP diluted EPS was `$7.96` versus adjusted diluted EPS of `$16.00` in fiscal
2025. The difference included a large PharmaLex goodwill impairment and other
portfolio-reset items. Some adjustments may be non-cash, but the gap means the
analyst must not treat adjusted EPS as the sole measure of economic quality.

Current judgment: Cencora owns an important healthcare access relationship and
has resilient product flow, specialty depth, and provider embedment. The return
depends on thin-spread discipline, working-capital control, acquisition returns,
litigation and liability management, and whether specialty and service growth
increase cash per diluted share rather than only revenue and adjusted EPS.

## What Cencora controls

`manufacturer supply -> wholesale and specialty distribution -> provider, pharmacy, or physician workflow -> patient access -> payment and replenishment`

Cencora controls logistics, inventory availability, specialty handling,
manufacturer relationships, provider and pharmacy relationships, reimbursement
support, commercialization services, and parts of the physician-practice
workflow. It does not control drug pricing policy, manufacturer strategy,
reimbursement rates, customer concentration, product shortages, or the cost of
the drugs it distributes.

The business has different economic layers:

- U.S. Healthcare Solutions carries the largest distribution volume and the
  lowest-spread, highest-working-capital characteristics.
- Specialty distribution and physician-administered products can have more
  service content and stronger relationships, but they carry product,
  reimbursement, concentration, and acquisition risks.
- International Healthcare Solutions adds wholesale, logistics, and
  commercialization activity across different currencies, regulations, and
  payment systems.
- OneOncology and related services move the company closer to physician
  workflow and specialty care, but they also add operating complexity and
  acquisition integration claims.

The cash screen is deliberately strict. Cencora's operating cash is real, but
the company uses capital for inventory, receivables, technology, facilities,
specialty expansion, acquisitions, equity investments, dividends, debt service,
and repurchases. If specialty access is built through acquisitions, acquisition
cash is part of the reinvestment requirement.

The company-defined free-cash-flow figure should be reconciled to operating
cash, capital spending, acquisition cash, litigation payments, working-capital
changes, and debt funding. A large distribution company can show strong free
cash flow in a year when it reduces inventory or receives favorable supplier
terms; that does not establish the normal cash required to support growth.

In Q3, reported gross profit margin was about `4.26%`. Management said gross
profit benefited from a higher LIFO credit and OneOncology, while increased
GLP-1 sales pressured margin because those products carry lower gross margins.
This is exactly why revenue growth is a weak summary measure for Cencora:
small changes in gross margin, inventory accounting, product mix, and customer
terms can materially change earnings.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. The GAAP-to-adjusted gap is economically important

Fiscal 2025 adjusted diluted EPS of `$16.00` was approximately twice GAAP
diluted EPS of `$7.96`. The gap included PharmaLex goodwill impairment, deal and
integration expenses, amortization, and other items.

Goodwill impairment is non-cash in the period recorded, but it is evidence that
management's earlier purchase price or growth assumptions did not hold. Deal and
integration expenses may be recurring when acquisitions are a core growth
method. Amortization is non-cash but reflects acquired intangible assets that
were paid for with capital.

The model should show GAAP income, adjusted income, each adjustment, cash paid,
whether the cost recurs, and the acquisition cohort that created the asset. An
adjustment is not economically irrelevant simply because it does not repeat in
the same accounting label.

### 2. Distribution scale creates working-capital dependence

Cencora buys and holds pharmaceuticals, sells to providers and pharmacies, and
collects under customer and manufacturer terms. Receivables, inventory,
accounts payable, rebates, returns, and litigation liabilities can all move
operating cash independently of current-period gross profit.

The critical bridge is:

`gross profit -> receivables and inventory -> supplier terms and rebates -> collected cash -> debt and acquisition capacity`

Track days sales outstanding, inventory turns, inventory reserves, payable days,
customer concentration, product returns, chargebacks, and the effect of GLP-1
volume. A rise in sales with a larger working-capital requirement may be
economically sound, but it lowers the cash available to fund acquisitions and
shareholder returns.

### 3. GLP-1 growth can reduce margin quality

GLP-1 products can add substantial revenue and unit volume while carrying lower
gross margins. This creates a potential mismatch between headline growth and
cash return. The analyst should separate GLP-1 revenue, margin, inventory,
payment terms, customer mix, and any product or regulatory exposure.

The question is not whether GLP-1 volume is good or bad. It is whether the
incremental gross profit after working capital, service cost, credit risk, and
capital requirements exceeds the return from other uses of the balance sheet.

### 4. Specialty services add value but also liabilities

OneOncology and other specialty services can deepen Cencora's role in physician
and patient workflows. They can raise gross profit and add access, data, and
service relationships that ordinary wholesale distribution does not provide.

They also introduce physician-practice integration, reimbursement, staffing,
compliance, customer concentration, and acquisition-return risk. The next
filings should show the contribution of OneOncology to revenue, operating
income, cash flow, integration costs, and working capital. Management commentary
about strategic fit is not evidence of return until cash results appear.

### 5. LIFO credits and litigation gains need normalization

Q2 and Q3 fiscal 2026 gross profit benefited from LIFO credits. A LIFO credit
can be a valid accounting result of inventory-cost changes, but it is not the
same as recurring distribution productivity. Cencora also reports gains from
antitrust litigation settlements in its adjusted reconciliations.

The normalized model should show gross profit before and after LIFO effects,
settlement gains, and product-mix changes. It should compare those adjustments
with actual cash receipts, legal costs, and the probability of recurrence.

### 6. Acquisition funding changes the owner denominator

Cencora used debt and other financing to fund the RCA acquisition and later
completed OneOncology. Acquisitions can improve specialty access and provider
relationships, but they increase interest expense, goodwill, intangible
amortization, integration costs, and the risk of future impairment.

The required return is on total purchase price plus integration and working
capital, not on management's adjusted EPS accretion. Compare acquired operating
income and cash flow with purchase cash, incremental debt, interest, NCI or
equity claims, and additional capital requirements.

## Balance sheet and liquidity

Cencora's scale and supplier relationships support access to financing, but the
business carries substantial inventory, receivables, debt, acquisition
commitments, litigation liabilities, and thin operating spreads. Liquidity can
look strong while the company remains dependent on supplier terms and timely
customer collections.

The stress case is not only a collapse in drug demand. It is a combination of
slower collections, higher inventory, product shortages, lower manufacturer
prices, weaker specialty reimbursement, higher interest costs, litigation
payments, and continued acquisition requirements. The balance sheet must fund
the distribution system before manufacturers and customers settle all claims.

## Alden lens: liquidity and durability

Cencora benefits from non-discretionary medicine flow, provider and pharmacy
embedment, global scale, and the difficulty of replacing a compliant specialty
distribution network. Its durability is different from a branded drug company:
it depends on logistics, contracts, service reliability, working capital, and
access relationships rather than patent protection.

Macro risks include drug pricing policy, reimbursement, interest rates,
manufacturer concentration, customer concentration, inventory funding,
regulatory enforcement, litigation, and currency. In a liquidity stress, the
company may continue serving patients while common-equity returns fall because
working capital and debt consume more cash.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative is: “Cencora is a scaled healthcare-access platform that can
increase value by moving from low-spread distribution into specialty products,
provider workflows, and manufacturer services.” The valuation model must
specify:

1. distribution revenue, gross margin, product mix, and customer concentration;
2. GLP-1 volume, margin, inventory, payment terms, and policy exposure;
3. specialty revenue, physician workflow, reimbursement, and service cost;
4. receivables, inventory, payables, rebates, returns, and working-capital need;
5. RCA, OneOncology, and other acquisition price, integration, and cash return;
6. LIFO, litigation, impairment, amortization, and recurring-adjustment policy;
7. debt, interest, supplier financing, litigation reserves, and liquidity; and
8. shares, repurchases, dividends, NCI or equity claims, and cash per share.

Cencora is in a scale-and-specialty expansion phase. A premium valuation requires
specialty and service economics to improve the margin and cash profile without
making the balance sheet more fragile or requiring repeated acquisition funding.

## Valuation screen

The September 11, 2026 market snapshot showed equity value of approximately
`$62.4B`. The screen begins with the strict FY2025 residual of negative `$1.085B`
after capital expenditures and RCA-related acquisition and investment cash. It
then tests normalized common cash if specialty growth matures and acquisition
spending becomes less intense.

| Case | Normalized common owner cash | Equity cash multiple | Implied common equity value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | `$2.0B` | `22x` | `$44B` |
| Base | `$3.0B` | `24x` | `$72B` |
| Bull | `$4.0B` | `26x` | `$104B` |

The current market value sits between the bear and base cases. The market is
requiring Cencora to convert its large operating scale and specialty strategy
into substantially more common cash than the strict FY2025 post-growth bridge
shows. The central question is whether that improvement comes from durable
operating economics or from continuing adjustments and balance-sheet funding.

## Thesis breakers and next filings

The thesis weakens if:

- revenue growth continues while gross margin, working capital, or cash per
  share deteriorates;
- GLP-1 volume expands but lower margin, inventory, credit, or policy exposure
  reduces common returns;
- OneOncology or RCA fails to generate cash after integration, interest, and
  provider-workflow costs;
- goodwill impairment and “one-time” integration costs recur across cohorts;
- LIFO credits or litigation gains support reported margin more than operations;
- receivables and inventory grow faster than sales while supplier terms tighten;
- debt and acquisition spending consume operating cash; or
- repurchases increase while diluted shares or common cash per share do not
  improve.

The next filings should provide:

1. gross margin by major product and specialty category;
2. GLP-1 volume, margin, inventory, payment terms, and customer mix;
3. OneOncology and RCA revenue, operating income, cash flow, and integration;
4. receivable days, inventory turns, payables, rebates, and returns;
5. LIFO, litigation, impairment, amortization, and adjusted-EPS bridges;
6. debt, interest, supplier financing, litigation reserves, and liquidity; and
7. cash per diluted share after acquisitions, dividends, and repurchases.

## Evidence grade

- **High confidence:** FY2025 revenue, GAAP and adjusted EPS, operating cash,
  capital spending, RCA acquisition and investment cash, PharmaLex impairment,
  Q1-Q3 FY2026 revenue and adjusted EPS, LIFO disclosures, GLP-1 margin mix,
  and OneOncology commentary.
- **Medium confidence:** the normalized owner-cash cases and the division of
  working-capital needs between maintenance and growth.
- **Open:** full cash returns from RCA and OneOncology, long-run GLP-1 margin,
  litigation and LIFO normalization, customer concentration, and cash per
  diluted share after future acquisitions.
