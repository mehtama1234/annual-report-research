# CF Industries Holdings — nitrogen production, gas-cost advantage, and low-carbon capital

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed U.S. GAAP figures in U.S. dollars;
dollar amounts are in millions unless otherwise stated.

## How to read this memo

Ammonia is the basic nitrogen product made from natural gas, air, and plant
equipment. Nitrogen fertilizer products such as urea and UAN are made from
ammonia and sold to farmers and distributors. Utilization measures how much of
available plant capacity operated. Realized price is the average selling price
actually received, not a quoted benchmark price. Sustaining capital keeps the
existing plants operating; growth capital adds or changes capacity. Adjusted
EBITDA is management's profit measure before interest, tax, depreciation, and
selected items; it is not cash available to common shareholders.

## Investment question

CF converts natural gas and large industrial plants into ammonia and other
nitrogen products. Its advantage may come from North American gas access,
large integrated facilities, logistics, and high plant reliability. The central
question is whether those advantages produce acceptable owner cash through a
full fertilizer cycle after maintenance spending, outages, environmental
obligations, buybacks, dividends, and low-carbon project funding.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- FY2025 gross ammonia production was about 10.1 million tons. Available
  ammonia capacity utilization was 98% in the first half of 2026, showing that
  reliability and plant availability are major economic variables.
- The Yazoo City incident reduced expected 2026 gross ammonia production to
  about 9.5 million tons, with recovery directed into the first half of 2027.
  A single outage therefore affects both volume and the cost of serving
  customers while capacity is unavailable.
- Higher nitrogen selling prices increased revenue and adjusted EBITDA, but
  natural gas is a major input. The right margin test is realized nitrogen
  price minus gas and other cash costs, not revenue growth alone.
- CF returned about $1.7B to shareholders in 2025 while funding plant spending
  and Blue Point development. That is attractive if cash is above maintenance
  needs; it is aggressive if current margins are near the top of the cycle.
- Blue Point, a low-carbon ammonia joint venture with JERA and Mitsui, gives CF
  exposure to carbon capture, permits, construction, offtake, tax support, and
  partner economics. Its value should be modeled separately from the existing
  nitrogen plants until construction cost and contracted cash returns are
  clear.
- The decision to leave the Donaldsonville electrolyzer project and emphasize
  a carbon-capture-led route is an important capital-allocation signal. It
  suggests management is comparing decarbonization projects on economics, not
  treating every clean-energy project as equally valuable.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Are margins caused by price, cost, or execution?

Rebuild each year from ammonia and downstream product volumes, realized prices,
natural-gas costs, other input costs, utilization, outages, and freight. A
strong price environment can make an ordinary plant look like a permanently
high-return asset.

### 2. What is sustaining capital?

Reconcile the $950M PP&E spending line to management's sustaining-capital
measure. Identify turnaround work, environmental compliance, reliability
spending, debottlenecking, and Blue Point or other growth investment. Owner
cash is overstated when growth and maintenance spending are treated as one
small recurring number.

### 3. Does adjusted EBITDA convert to cash?

Bridge adjusted EBITDA to operating cash flow and then to cash after capital
spending. Track working capital, cash taxes, interest, pension or benefit
obligations, litigation, and joint-venture funding. The Q1 2026 litigation
settlement gain is a reminder to isolate non-recurring items.

### 4. Does the outage reveal operating concentration?

Track Yazoo's repair cost, insurance recovery, lost volume, customer service
cost, restart timing, and any change in the plant's long-term economics. Compare
planned utilization with actual production and maintenance downtime across all
plants.

### 5. Will low-carbon ammonia earn a return?

Separate Blue Point construction spending, partner contributions, tax credits,
offtake pricing, carbon-capture performance, operating costs, and debt or
guarantees from the legacy business. Require a project return estimate and
actual cash milestones before assigning full value to the project.

### 6. Are shareholder returns durable?

Test dividends and buybacks against mid-cycle cash after sustaining capital,
debt maturities, outage reserves, and committed projects. A lower share count
helps only if the repurchases were made below intrinsic value and did not reduce
needed plant investment or increase financial risk.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** fertilizer links food production, energy inputs, trade,
  and geopolitics. North American gas and reliable industrial capacity can be
  strategic advantages, but the company remains exposed to energy prices,
  global supply additions, and policy changes.
- **Damodaran lens:** value legacy nitrogen plants on mid-cycle realized prices,
  gas costs, utilization, and sustaining capital. Value Blue Point as a
  probability-weighted project with explicit construction cost, timing,
  offtake, tax support, and operating risk.
- **Forensic lens:** the main errors are treating adjusted EBITDA as owner cash,
  treating high utilization as proof of permanent pricing power, treating
  buybacks as value creation without a cycle test, and adding low-carbon
  project value before the capital and return evidence exists.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $20.48B of equity value at
$133.07 per share. Against the FY2025 $1.802B OCF-less-PP&E screen, that is
about 11.4x. A normalized range of $1.0B / $1.5B / $2.0B at 12x / 15x / 18x
implies $12B / $22.5B / $36B. The current value therefore requires cash above
the bear case and assumes that North American cost advantage, utilization,
nitrogen pricing, and project execution remain favorable.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Nitrogen prices fall while natural-gas and operating costs remain high.
2. Global capacity additions or trade normalization remove the current supply
   tightness.
3. Yazoo recovery is delayed, costs more than expected, or exposes broader
   reliability problems.
4. Sustaining capital is materially above the reported or guided level.
5. Blue Point requires more capital, receives weaker offtake economics, or
   fails to deliver expected carbon performance and policy support.
6. Buybacks continue through a weak fertilizer cycle while debt and plant
   renewal needs rise.

Next filing checks: realized prices by product; gas cost per ton; production,
utilization, outages, and turnaround spending; sustaining versus growth
capital; inventory and receivables; Blue Point commitments and partner cash;
environmental obligations; debt; and buybacks at different commodity prices.
