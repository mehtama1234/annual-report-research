## Bottom line

Cheniere controls a scarce conversion point between U.S. natural gas and global
energy demand: liquefaction trains, storage, docks, pipelines, long-term
contracts, and the operating knowledge required to run them safely. That is a
stronger control point than simply owning gas molecules, but it is not a pure
toll road. The company remains exposed to feed-gas and destination economics,
derivative marks, train availability, regulatory permission, construction
execution, and very large capital cycles.

The 2025 filing shows a real cash-generating platform: $5.539B of operating
cash flow against $3.078B of property and equipment spending. But the same year
returned $2.724B through repurchases and $451M through dividends. The simple
post-capex residual was approximately $2.461B, below those visible capital
returns, before debt repayment, working-capital volatility, and other claims.
This does not prove an unsustainable policy; it proves that “distributable cash
flow” and owner cash after maintaining and expanding the system are not the
same denominator.

Current judgment: Cheniere is a strategically durable infrastructure asset with
high operating leverage and an unusually valuable export position. The thesis
requires a normalization of contract cash flows, maintenance versus growth
capex, derivative volatility, debt, and train-completion economics. It should
not be valued from GAAP net income in a single commodity-price or hedge-mark
year.

## What the company controls

Cheniere's economic chain is:

`U.S. gas access -> liquefaction trains -> storage and loading -> contracted LNG delivery -> global power / industrial / heating demand`

Sabine Pass has six operating trains with approximately 30 mtpa of capacity.
Corpus Christi has three operating trains at approximately 15 mtpa, with Stage
3 adding brownfield capacity. The relationship is valuable because a customer
needs reliable molecules at a destination and cannot easily substitute a
licensed, connected liquefaction and export system in the short run.

The control point is therefore physical, contractual, regulatory, and
operational at once. The burden is equally real: high fixed-cost trains,
feed-gas procurement, maintenance turnarounds, marine logistics, safety,
permitting, construction, debt, and weather or geopolitical disruption.

it does mean that incremental trains must be analyzed on returns after their
full construction and financing burden.

## Cash conversion and owner-cash bridge

### 2025 reported bridge

| Item | $ millions |
|---|---:|
| Net cash from operations | 5,539 |
| Less: PP&E spending | (3,078) |
| Residual before financing and capital returns | **2,461** |
| Repurchases | (2,724) |
| Dividends | (451) |
| Simple residual after visible returns | **(714)** |

The bridge is deliberately not labeled free cash flow. PP&E spending includes
both maintenance and growth capital, while operating cash flow can benefit from
contract advances, working-capital timing, and hedge settlements. Conversely,
some expansion capital may create durable future cash flow and should not be
treated as recurring maintenance. The right question is whether each train and
related infrastructure package earns an adequate return on the capital
deployed, not whether all capex is immediately distributable.

Management's $5.3B of 2025 Distributable Cash Flow is a useful management
metric, but it is not interchangeable with consolidated OCF less PP&E. The
definition, noncontrolling interests, derivative settlements, and project
capital adjustments must be reconciled before using it for valuation or payout
capacity.

## Earnings quality and possible financial engineering

### Derivative marks can invert the GAAP narrative

The 2026 first-quarter packet is the clearest warning: Cheniere reported a large
GAAP net loss while adjusted EBITDA and distributable cash flow remained
positive, primarily because of derivative revaluation. That distinction can be
economically legitimate when derivatives hedge contracted physical exposure,
but it makes earnings quality dependent on whether the hedge portfolio is
understood rather than merely excluded.

Required tests:

- Reconcile realized derivative cash settlements to unrealized mark changes.
- Separate hedges of contracted volumes from merchant or optimization
  positions.
- Compare hedge gains/losses with feed-gas costs, LNG sales, and contract
  margins over a full cycle.
- Test whether adjusted EBITDA removes recurring volatility that is actually a
  cost of the business model.

### DCF adjustments can hide the capital cycle

The company's DCF metric is useful for comparing distributions but can obscure
the difference between maintaining a commissioned train and finishing a new
train. A construction-heavy year can show strong DCF while shareholder cash is
being consumed by PP&E spending, debt service, working capital, or buybacks.
The forensic denominator is consolidated OCF, total PP&E cash spending, debt
movement, and equity returns together.

### Contracted visibility is not zero-risk visibility

Long-term sale and purchase agreements reduce volume risk, but they do not
eliminate counterparty credit, destination, force-majeure, regulatory, or
operational risk. The next filing should be read for take-or-pay terms,
credit-support requirements, contract concentration, cancellation or
renegotiation behavior, and the proportion of future capacity already sold.

### Buybacks require a cycle-adjusted hurdle

Returning $2.724B while cash fell to $1.099B is not automatically imprudent;
the company may have committed contracted cash flows and debt capacity. But the
buyback program should be compared with train-completion needs, leverage,
refinancing, and the normalized value of the equity. A repurchase made against
temporary hedge earnings or peak LNG sentiment can transfer value from
remaining shareholders to selling shareholders.

## Capital structure and Lyn Alden durability lens

Cheniere's durability comes from infrastructure scarcity, energy-security
importance, global diversification of demand, and the difficulty of replicating
licensed liquefaction and export systems. Its fragility comes from the same
capital intensity that creates the moat. Higher rates raise the cost of new
trains and refinanceable debt; weak global demand or abundant alternative LNG
capacity can reduce utilization or contract economics; and a construction or
operational failure has disproportionate consequences.

The Alden-style macro questions are:

- Does global energy demand and geopolitical diversification create a durable
  floor under U.S. LNG exports?
- Can Cheniere fund expansion from internally generated cash without relying on
  favorable debt markets and aggressive capital returns?
- How much of reported cash is exposed to nominal commodity prices, hedge
  settlement timing, and working-capital release?
- Does the export bottleneck remain scarce after competing projects are built?

Debt near $22.5B and cash near $1.1B require utilization, contract quality, and
disciplined capital allocation to remain favorable through a liquidity shock.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The bullish narrative is that Cheniere owns irreplaceable U.S. LNG export
capacity, has a long runway of contracted demand, is completing brownfield
expansion at attractive returns, and can compound cash per share through
buybacks.

The skeptical narrative is that the apparent moat is capital-intensive,
commodity-adjacent, and partly financed by debt. The market may capitalize peak
contract and hedge economics while treating growth capex as if it were already
earning steady returns.

The valuation case requires commissioned and incremental trains to achieve
sustainable utilization and margin after feed-gas, shipping, maintenance, and
overhead; growth capex to decline relative to OCF after Stage 3 completion;
normalized DCF to reconcile to cash available after maintenance capital and
debt obligations; and buybacks to reduce share count without starving expansion
or weakening liquidity. No market-implied valuation is asserted here; the next
valuation pass should use a low/base/high normalized owner-cash range and a
higher required return than a pure regulated toll road.

## Thesis breakers and next filing tests

The thesis weakens if train availability, utilization, or realized margin falls
while adjusted DCF remains stable only because of exclusions or hedge marks;
Stage 3 increases capital employed without proportional cash flow; debt rises
or cash falls while buybacks continue through a weaker LNG cycle; counterparties
renegotiate or default; maintenance capex is repeatedly presented as
discretionary growth spending; regulation reduces export optionality; or global
LNG supply growth removes the scarcity premium before new capital is recovered.

The next annual filing should capture train-level capacity and availability,
contracted versus merchant volumes, realized margin, derivative cash versus
marks, maintenance and growth capex, debt maturities, counterparty exposure,
share count, and cash returned per dollar of normalized owner cash.
