# Citigroup: global transaction network, transformation burden, and the proof required for a turnaround

## What the company does

Citigroup operates five reportable businesses: Services, Markets, Banking,
Wealth, and U.S. Consumer Cards. Services includes treasury and trade solutions
and securities services. Markets provides sales, trading, financing, and
prime-brokerage services. Banking includes investment banking and corporate
lending. Wealth combines banking, lending, investment, custody, and trust
services. U.S. Consumer Cards is primarily a credit-card business.

The proposed control point is a global institutional network: a multinational
client can use Citi to move cash, settle securities, finance trade, manage
foreign exchange, raise capital, and access markets across jurisdictions. That
network can be valuable because replacing local licenses, operating accounts,
settlement connections, compliance processes, and trained staff is difficult.
It is not automatically a moat. The bank must prove that the network earns an
adequate return after technology, controls, liquidity, capital, credit, and
employee costs.

## FY2025 and Q2 2026 baseline

| Measure | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue, net of interest expense | $78.1B | $80.7B | $85.2B |
| Net income | $9.2B | $12.7B | $14.3B |
| Diluted EPS | $4.04 | $5.94 | $6.99 |
| Provision for credit losses and benefits/claims | $9.2B | $10.1B | $10.3B |
| RoTCE | 4.9% | 7.0% | 7.7% |
| CET1 ratio | 13.4% | 13.6% | 13.2% |

The three-year series shows a real improvement in reported earnings and return,
but it does not yet prove that the turnaround has reached its target quality.
Provision expense remained above `$9B` in each year, while 2025 revenue included
notable items and unusually strong capital-markets conditions. The question is
whether the Services and Wealth relationship businesses can carry the cost of
transformation, controls, and global liquidity after Markets and Banking are
normalized.

| Average deposits | — | approximately 7% above the prior year in Q2 2026 |
| Average loans | — | approximately 4% above the prior year in Q2 2026 |

Citi described 2025 as a turning point, with record revenue in each of the
five businesses, positive operating leverage for a second consecutive year,
and more than $17.6B returned to shareholders. The reported numbers require
qualification: 2025 included notable items, the business mix is changing as
divestitures finish, and the strongest 2026 quarters include unusually strong
Markets and Banking conditions. The correct baseline is therefore a range of
normalized common earnings, not the annualized Q2 result.

Q2 2026 was materially stronger than FY2025: revenue rose to $24.8B, net
income was $5.8B, non-interest revenue was $7.6B, and operating expenses were
$14.2B. Citi repurchased approximately $4.0B of common stock in the quarter
and reported a 12.8% CET1 ratio. Capital fell from the year-end level as
repurchases, dividends, higher risk-weighted assets, and the Banamex and Russia
transactions offset earnings and other capital benefits. A high payout during
a strong quarter is not evidence that the new earnings level is yet durable.

## Where the economics sit

### Services: the cleanest control point, with a large control burden

Services is the strongest candidate for durable economics. Treasury and trade
solutions earn fees and spreads from client cash management, payments, trade,
and working-capital activity. Securities services earn fees from custody,
fund administration, settlement, collateral, and related infrastructure. The
client relationship can expand across products, and switching a global
operating network is operationally costly.

The burden is significant. Services requires local licenses, resilient
technology, anti-money-laundering controls, sanctions screening, cyber defense,
settlement accuracy, liquidity, and staff in many jurisdictions. A service
revenue increase is valuable only if incremental revenue exceeds the cost of
those controls and the capital committed to the operating balance sheet.

Future filings should show revenue growth by business, average deposits and
loans, fee rates, expense growth, operational incidents, client balances,
cross-border activity, and RoTCE. A large amount of client assets or payment
volume can grow while owner economics remain weak if pricing is competitive or
control costs rise faster than revenue.

### Markets: valuable platform, cyclical earnings

Markets benefits from client trading, financing, volatility, issuance, and
macro uncertainty. It diversifies Citi's revenue and uses the same global
institutional relationships as Services. It also consumes risk capital,
collateral, technology, compensation, and control resources.

The valuation should normalize fixed income, currencies, commodities, and
equities revenue over a full cycle. A record quarter can reflect market
volumes, volatility, client positioning, and spread conditions that cannot be
repeated. The relevant test is whether the platform earns an attractive return
after compensation and risk capital in both active and quiet markets.

### Banking: improving franchise, exposed to capital-markets timing

Banking includes mergers and acquisitions, debt and equity capital markets,
advisory, restructuring, and corporate lending. Q2 2026 revenue increased 34%
year over year, and 2025 was described as Citi's best year for M&A revenue.
Those are useful signs of client engagement, not proof of a permanent fee
stream.

Track backlog and announced transactions against completed transactions,
underwriting inventory, leveraged-finance credit, reserve changes, loan growth,
fees paid to employees, and capital consumed. If announced activity rises but
completed fees, credit quality, or risk-adjusted returns do not, the narrative
is ahead of the economics.

### Wealth: relationship expansion with execution risk

Wealth can produce fee, spread, lending, custody, and trust revenue with less
direct credit exposure than card lending. Citi reported continued growth and
increased asset gathering from existing clients. The test is whether client
assets, net flows, lending, and recurring fees grow faster than advisor and
platform compensation, acquisition costs, and service expense.

Market appreciation can inflate assets under management without proving net
new client value. Separate market movement from net flows and distinguish
custody or low-fee balances from higher-margin advisory and lending products.

### U.S. Consumer Cards: clear revenue engine, concentrated credit burden

Cards monetize spending, revolving balances, interchange, annual fees, and
interest income. They also carry charge-offs, fraud, rewards, marketing, and
regulatory cost. Prime-heavy exposure can reduce loss severity but does not
remove the risk that payment rates fall, revolving balances rise, or losses
appear after a credit cycle turns.

The filing review must track delinquencies by vintage, payment rate, revolving
utilization, net charge-offs, reserve coverage, credit-score mix, rewards cost,
and acquisition economics. A growth quarter can be value destructive if the
bank buys balances with rewards and marketing whose lifetime return does not
cover expected losses and capital.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering review

### The turnaround has a denominator problem

Citi reports RoTCE, adjusted revenue, adjusted EPS, and notable-item
exclusions. Those measures are useful for separating unusual items, but the
investor must ask which costs are truly nonrecurring. Transformation spending,
separation costs, legal work, control remediation, technology replacement, and
stranded costs may decline, but a global bank must continue to spend on
controls and resilience.

The clean comparison is common earnings after recurring technology,
compensation, compliance, credit, liquidity, and capital costs. Repeatedly
excluding these expenses would overstate the earnings power of the proposed
new Citi.

### Divestitures can improve the story without proving organic returns

The sales of legacy businesses, the Russia exit, and the Banamex transaction
reduce complexity and may release capital. They also change revenue mix,
comparability, stranded cost allocation, tax, and capital. Cash or accounting
effects from a sale should not be treated as recurring operating earnings.

Future filings should reconcile reported revenue and expenses after each sale,
show residual costs, identify capital released, and disclose whether Services,
Markets, Banking, Wealth, and USCC actually improve on an organic basis.

### Credit provisions are estimates, not cash losses in the same period

The allowance for credit losses uses portfolio data and economic forecasts.
Provision releases can raise earnings without improving the book; reserve builds
can lower current earnings before losses are realized. Citi's card and
corporate-credit exposures should be tested by vintage, geography, product,
collateral, and macro assumption.

Compare charge-offs with reserve coverage, delinquency migration, nonaccrual
loans, criticized assets, loan growth, and the direction of the economic
forecast. A strong quarter with lower provisions is not necessarily a durable
improvement in underwriting.

### Capital returns may be early relative to the proof of the turnaround

Repurchases reduce the share count and can increase EPS, but they also reduce
CET1 capital. Citi's Q2 2026 CET1 ratio fell to 12.8% as repurchases,
dividends, higher risk-weighted assets, and transactions offset earnings.
Assess buybacks against stress capital, binding regulatory ratios, liquidity,
and the possibility that the bank needs to fund growth or absorb losses.

The right question is not whether a repurchase is EPS-accretive. It is whether
the bank is buying shares below intrinsic value while retaining enough capital
to support the full operating and regulatory burden.

### Technology can be growth investment or required maintenance

AI-assisted coding, service agents, onboarding, underwriting, wealth workflows,
and tokenized payments may reduce cost or improve client service. The same bank
must fund cybersecurity, model governance, data quality, regulatory reporting,
resilience, fraud prevention, and system replacement. Those costs are not
optional simply because management calls them transformation.

The evidence should be cost per account or transaction, error rates, fraud,
outages, regulatory findings, employee productivity, and total implementation
cost. More AI deployment without lower full-cost service or stronger controls
does not establish value creation.

## Balance-sheet resilience and macro conditions

Citi's global footprint gives it diversified client flows but creates more
jurisdiction, currency, liquidity, sanctions, and operational exposure than a
primarily domestic bank. At June 30, 2026, Citi reported average high-quality
liquid assets of roughly $641B, average net outflows of $568B, and an LCR of
113%. It also reported approximately $1.1T of available liquidity resources.

Liquidity is not the same as profitability. High-quality liquid assets protect
against withdrawals but may earn less than loans or trading assets. The bank
must maintain liquidity at regulated entities where it may not be freely
transferable across borders. Stress tests should cover deposit flight,
collateral calls, market losses, currency moves, and simultaneous client
withdrawals.

The Alden lens therefore focuses on the quality of funding, the liquidity
buffer, the ability to operate when dollar funding tightens, and the losses
that can pass through a leveraged balance sheet. The global network is more
durable than a single quarter of trading income, but the network's resilience
depends on trust, settlement, capital, and regulatory execution.

## Damodaran lens: translate the turnaround into numbers

The narrative is that Citi can simplify the firm, complete remediation, and
convert its global institutional network into a mid-teens RoTCE business with
more durable operating leverage.

That narrative requires:

1. Services growth to exceed the full cost of global controls and technology.
2. Markets revenue to normalize above a weak-cycle floor after compensation and
   risk capital.
3. Banking and Wealth to grow through client relationships rather than a single
   favorable issuance or asset-price cycle.
4. USCC credit losses, rewards, and funding costs to remain consistent with
   profitable growth.
5. Transformation and divestitures to reduce recurring cost without creating
   stranded expense or weaker controls.
6. CET1 capital and liquidity to support growth and shareholder returns through
   a stress cycle.

Citi is a mature institution undergoing portfolio and operating-model repair.
The growth rate in revenue is less important than incremental RoTCE after
capital, controls, credit, and technology. The valuation should pay for proven
returns, not the full value of a target state that has not yet persisted.

## Valuation screen

The September 14, 2026 market feed showed an equity value of approximately
$240.9B. This is a normalized net-income screen rather than industrial owner
cash.

| Case | Normalized common earnings | Earnings multiple | Implied equity value | Required interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bear | $13B | 10x | $130B | Markets normalize lower, credit and controls remain expensive, turnaround stalls |
| Base | $17B | 12x | $204B | Services and Banking improve, but full-cycle returns remain below the strongest quarter |
| Bull | $21B | 14x | $294B | Mid-teens RoTCE becomes durable, capital returns continue, and control costs fall without new failures |

The current value is above the base earnings screen and below the bull case.
It therefore embeds meaningful confidence that the 2026 earnings improvement
will persist and that Citi will approach its target return profile. Book value,
tangible book value, and future RoTCE should be used alongside the earnings
screen because bank value depends on the return earned on required equity.

## Cash conversion and distributable earnings

For Citi, owner cash is not the bank's cash balance or operating cash flow. The relevant bridge is net income after credit provisions and operating costs -> earnings retained to support risk-weighted assets, liquidity and regulatory capital -> capital available at the parent -> dividends and repurchases. A turnaround is only distributable if the bank can fund remediation, technology, credit growth and stress-capital requirements before returning excess capital.

Track common shares, tangible common equity, CET1, risk-weighted assets, subsidiary dividends, regulatory buffers, and capital returns together. If adjusted earnings rise while capital needs, remediation spending, credit reserves or dilution absorb the improvement, reported earnings have not yet become durable common-owner cash.

## Thesis breakers and future filing tests

| Question | Evidence that supports the thesis | Evidence that weakens it |
|---|---|---|
| Is Services a durable control point? | Recurring fee and spread growth, stable client balances, improving RoTCE after control costs | Volume grows while pricing, outages, remediation, or expense consume the gain |
| Is the turnaround real? | Reported and adjusted results converge as transformation costs fall and controls remain strong | “Notable” and transformation exclusions recur or residual costs remain hidden |
| Are Markets earnings durable? | Through-cycle revenue covers compensation and risk capital | Revenue depends on unusually high volatility or inventory marks |
| Is credit controlled? | Delinquencies, charge-offs, reserves, and criticized assets remain consistent with loan growth | Card vintages, corporate credit, or reserve coverage deteriorate after growth |
| Are divestitures value creating? | Capital and stranded costs fall, with organic core growth visible | Sale proceeds or accounting gains mask weak continuing returns |
| Are buybacks safe? | CET1 remains resilient after distributions and risk-weighted-asset growth | Payouts force weaker buffers, slower investment, or future issuance |
| Does technology create value? | Lower full-cost servicing and fraud with no deterioration in controls | Spending and AI claims rise without measurable productivity or resilience |

## Bottom line

Citi owns a potentially valuable global institutional network, and Services is
the clearest place where that network can produce durable economics. Markets,
Banking, and Wealth add client breadth but make earnings more sensitive to
capital-market conditions. USCC can produce strong returns but carries a
concentrated consumer-credit and rewards burden.

The 2025 evidence supports real operational improvement, and Q2 2026 is strong
evidence that the platform can produce much better quarterly returns. It does
not yet prove that the bank has permanently converted remediation spending,
divestitures, and target-state claims into mid-teens through-cycle RoTCE. The
next proof is recurring earnings after full control costs, stable credit, CET1
resilience after distributions, and organic growth in Services and Wealth.

**Evidence grade:** Strong for reported FY2025 and Q2 2026 revenue, earnings,
segment direction, liquidity, and capital; medium for recurring Services and
Banking returns; unresolved for through-cycle Markets income, residual
transformation cost, and target-state RoTCE.
