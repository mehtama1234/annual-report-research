# Colgate-Palmolive: habitual use, science-supported brands, and clean cash conversion

## What the company does

Colgate-Palmolive sells oral-care, personal-care, home-care, and pet-nutrition
products. Oral Care represented about 44% of 2025 sales, Personal Care 17%,
Home Care 16%, and Hill's Pet Nutrition supplied a separate specialty category.

The valuable relationship is built around repeated use, product efficacy,
professional recommendation, distribution, and brand trust. Toothpaste,
toothbrushes, personal-care products, and pet nutrition are not identical
businesses, but they share a routine purchase pattern and a need for reliable
product performance.

The main burden is the cost of protecting that position: research and product
development, advertising, retailer support, manufacturing, packaging, global
distribution, input costs, and the working capital required before collection.
The key question is whether daily use and category leadership produce owner cash
after that recurring support, or whether pricing and adjusted metrics are doing
more of the work than the brand suggests.

## FY2025 and recent-quarter starting point

| Measure | FY2025 | Q1–Q2 FY2026 | What it says |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Net sales | $20.382B | Q2 $5.361B | Scale is broad but category-specific |
| Organic sales | Up 1.4% | Q1 +2.9%; Q2 +2.4% | Growth is positive but not high growth |
| Net cash from operations | $4.198B | Better free cash flow in Q1 | Cash generation is a central strength |
| Base-business EPS | $3.69 | Q1 $0.97; Q2 $0.99 | Underlying earnings remained positive |
| Gross margin | — | Q1 60.6%; Q2 61.5% | Margin expansion is supporting earnings |

The fiscal 2025 result shows a focused daily-use consumer system rather than a
portfolio relying on one broad discretionary trend. The company reported
positive organic growth and record operating cash. Walmart represented about
11% of 2025 sales, so retailer concentration remains relevant even with a
strong brand portfolio.

The recent sequence is constructive but requires separation. Q4 FY2025 GAAP
EPS was negative $0.05 because of a skin-health impairment, while base-business
EPS was $0.95 and organic sales rose 2.2%. Q1 FY2026 organic sales rose 2.9%
with base-business EPS of $0.97. Q2 organic sales rose 2.4%, base-business EPS
was $0.99, and gross margin reached 61.5%.

This supports operating stability, not a conclusion that all reported profit
is equally clean. The impairment is non-cash in the period, but it is evidence
that portfolio assets can be worth less than management previously assumed.

## How activity becomes cash

The cash chain is:

`habit or professional recommendation -> product selection -> retailer or veterinary channel -> shipment and collection -> gross profit after inputs, packaging, and trade support -> advertising, research, plants, and overhead -> operating cash -> reinvestment and shareholder cash`

The chain has several separate tests.

- Price can raise sales while reducing unit volume or household penetration.
- Category leadership can coexist with retailer bargaining power and private-label
  pressure.
- Advertising is an expense, but reducing it can weaken future demand.
- A specialty pet product can have better category quality while still carrying
  input, channel, and private-label risk.
- A GAAP impairment can distort one period, while adjusted earnings can hide
  the economic cost of an earlier overinvestment.

Read sales with volume, price, mix, market share, gross margin, advertising,
research, inventory, receivables, operating cash, capital spending, dividends,
repurchases, stock compensation, debt, and diluted shares.

## What the recent quarters show

### Q4 FY2025: strong operations with an impairment warning

Organic sales rose 2.2% and base-business EPS was $0.95. GAAP EPS was negative
$0.05 because of a skin-health impairment.

The right conclusion is two-sided. The recurring operating business continued to
grow, but the impairment is not irrelevant. It says that at least one portfolio
asset did not support its prior carrying value. Future return-on-capital work
must exclude the impaired asset from the old narrative and test whether the
remaining portfolio earns acceptable returns.

### Q1 FY2026: balanced growth and brand support

Net sales were $5.324B, organic sales rose 2.9%, base-business EPS was $0.97,
and gross margin was 60.6%. Management highlighted broad-based growth, improved
free cash flow, and year-over-year advertising increases.

The important point is that advertising support and cash generation were not
treated as opposing signals. A clean consumer franchise should be able to fund
brand support while still generating cash, but this has to persist through
input-cost and retailer cycles.

### Q2 FY2026: margin expansion with pricing and mix still visible

Net sales were $5.361B, organic sales rose 2.4%, base-business EPS was $0.99,
and gross margin reached 61.5%. Management maintained sales and GAAP EPS
guidance while updating gross-margin and base-business EPS guidance.

The quarter reinforces category durability. It does not eliminate the need to
separate volume from price and mix, or to test whether gross-margin improvement
is retained after advertising, innovation, and input costs are fully funded.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering questions

### 1. Does category leadership produce volume, or only price?

Toothpaste and toothbrush market-share leadership is valuable only if it remains
connected to unit demand, repeat purchase, distribution, and acceptable gross
profit. Organic sales growth can be composed mostly of price and mix.

The next filings should bridge organic growth into volume, price, mix, currency,
and acquisitions, then compare those measures with market share and household
penetration.

### 2. Is the impairment isolated or evidence of portfolio misallocation?

The skin-health impairment should not be treated as a recurring cash expense,
but it should remain in the capital-allocation record. Management paid for or
developed an asset that did not retain its expected value.

Track further impairments, restructuring, brand exits, acquisition premiums,
and segment return on capital. A clean daily-use franchise can still destroy
value through weak portfolio allocation.

### 3. Are gross-margin gains durable after brand support?

Q1 and Q2 margin improvement is useful evidence, but gross margin can be helped
by price, mix, productivity, input timing, foreign exchange, or lower trade
spending. The company also needs to keep advertising and product development
strong enough to protect future demand.

The test is a margin bridge that shows price, volume, mix, commodities,
packaging, freight, productivity, advertising, and trade investments together.

### 4. Does Hill's improve portfolio quality or add another risk set?

Hill's Pet Nutrition may have attractive specialty and science-supported
economics. It also has separate veterinary, pet-owner, input, distribution, and
private-label dynamics.

Segment growth should be read with segment margin, inventory, channel exposure,
innovation spending, and capital needs. The higher-quality category should prove
its returns after those costs rather than receive an automatic premium.

### 5. Is retailer concentration manageable?

Walmart represented about 11% of 2025 sales. Scale and category leadership can
improve negotiating position, but a large retailer can still demand price,
promotion, shelf support, and service.

Track customer concentration, trade-spending rates, receivables, inventory,
private-label competition, and market-share changes by channel. A brand is not
fully independent of the retailer that controls access to the consumer.

## Lyn Alden lens: macro, liquidity, and durability

Daily-use oral care and personal hygiene are more resilient than discretionary
consumer categories. Pet nutrition and home care have their own demand and input
cycles, but the overall system is supported by repeated need rather than a
single occasional purchase.

The stress sequence is:

`household pressure or retailer bargaining -> trade-down, lower volume, or higher promotion -> gross-margin pressure -> more advertising and innovation required -> weaker owner cash if the response is underfunded or overfunded`

Foreign exchange, input costs, packaging, freight, labor, tariffs, and interest
rates affect how much cash survives. Liquidity is also strategic: Colgate must
continue funding science, advertising, product quality, and manufacturing
through a period when consumers or retailers resist price increases.

The durable part is routine use, efficacy, habit, and professional adjacency.
The fragile part is the assumption that habit prevents substitution, private
label, retailer pressure, or poor portfolio allocation.

## Damodaran lens: narrative-to-numbers, reinvestment, and lifecycle

The narrative is that category leadership, science-based innovation, brand
penetration, advertising, Hill's, and operating execution can produce a durable
consumer cash compounder.

Translate it into:

1. volume, price, mix, market share, and penetration by category;
2. gross margin after input, packaging, trade, advertising, and freight costs;
3. research, product development, and capacity spending required to defend
   efficacy and category leadership;
4. segment growth and return on capital, especially Hill's and skin health;
5. operating cash after working capital, capital spending, stock compensation,
   dividends, repurchases, debt, and impairment-related portfolio repair;
6. diluted-share change and the cost of buybacks relative to owner cash.

Colgate is a mature compounder if its existing brands can grow modestly while
the company reinvests enough to preserve category leadership. A higher multiple
requires confidence that the cash stream is durable and that new investment is
incremental rather than merely replacing lost relevance.

## Illustrative valuation screen

The local packet does not preserve a live market-price snapshot suitable for a
current recommendation. An illustrative owner-cash screen using normalized
owner-cash cases of `$3.0B`, `$3.6B`, and `$4.2B` at `18x`, `22x`, and `26x`
produces implied equity values of `$54B`, `$79.2B`, and `$109.2B`.

These are not price targets. They express the expectation burden for a mature
consumer company:

- the bear case assumes low volume, retailer pressure, and heavier brand or
  input costs;
- the base case assumes modest growth, stable gross margin, and continued cash
  conversion;
- the bull case assumes sustained category leadership, Hill's strength, and a
  long runway for reinvestment at high returns.

Rebuild the screen from the next annual filing with operating cash flow,
capital spending, working capital, advertising, research, stock compensation,
dividends, buybacks, debt, and diluted shares.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Read the proxy for the weight placed on sales, base-business EPS, organic sales,
free cash flow, market share, gross margin, return on capital, and shareholder
returns. Adjusted EPS can be useful for removing an impairment, but it can also
make a poor acquisition or recurring restructuring cost less visible.

The thesis weakens if:

- organic sales growth remains price-led while volume and household reach fall;
- market share weakens despite higher advertising and innovation spending;
- gross-margin gains reverse when input costs or trade support normalize;
- private label takes share in oral care, home care, or pet nutrition;
- Hill's growth requires more capital and promotion than its returns justify;
- further impairments or portfolio exits show that capital was allocated poorly;
- operating cash weakens while dividends and repurchases continue;
- buybacks offset stock compensation without reducing diluted shares.

3. Gross-margin bridge for commodities, packaging, freight, productivity,

4. Research, product-development, quality, advertising, and capacity investment

5. Hill's growth, margin, channel mix, inventory, and required capital.
6. Operating cash flow, inventory, receivables, capital spending, impairment,

   shares.
7. Evidence that guidance is met through volume and durable cash rather than
   price, mix, timing, or adjusted exclusions.

## Bottom line

Colgate is a stronger daily-use and science-supported consumer case than a
generic “defensive staples” label suggests. Habit, efficacy, professional
recommendation, and category leadership help the company generate cash while
still funding advertising and innovation.

The conclusion remains conditional. The business must prove that organic growth
contains enough volume, that gross-margin gains survive full brand support, that
Hill's earns its capital, and that the skin-health impairment is a contained
portfolio error rather than a broader allocation problem.

**Evidence grade:** Strong for FY2025 sales, organic growth, operating cash,
segment mix, Q4 FY2025 through Q2 FY2026 operating trends, gross margin, and the
impairment; medium for normalized owner cash and long-run category durability;
unresolved for future volume mix, private-label pressure, Hill's returns, and
the return on continued brand investment.
