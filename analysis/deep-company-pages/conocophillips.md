## Bottom line

ConocoPhillips is best understood as a global upstream portfolio with LNG
optionality and a shareholder-return overlay, not as a downstream-heavy
integrated major. Its control point is a large, geographically diversified
base of low-cost reserves and production, plus the technical and commercial
capability to allocate capital toward the best reservoirs and projects.

The 2025 filing presents strong cash conversion: $19.796B of operating cash
flow and a disclosed $12.553B capital program, leaving a $7.243B screening
residual. But common distributions were approximately $9.0B, including $5.018B
of repurchases and $3.995B of common dividends. The “return 45% of CFO” story
is therefore not the same as cash available after the full capital program.
The difference is manageable only if the capital program is partly growth,
asset dispositions are not required to sustain returns, and low-cost
production remains durable through the commodity cycle.

The pattern is the important evidence. Cash from operations remained near
$20B, but earnings fell and the capital program increased. The post-program
residual declined from $8.7B to $7.2B while distributions remained above it.
This does not establish an unsustainable payout; it establishes that the
underwriting denominator must distinguish sustaining capital, growth projects,
acquisition/disposition effects, and commodity-cycle cash.

## Control point and operating mix

2025 production was approximately 2,375 MBOED, with the Lower 48 contributing
approximately 1,484 MBOED. Alaska, the Lower 48, Canada, Europe/Middle East/
North Africa, and Asia Pacific form a diversified upstream portfolio. The
company also has LNG offtake and project exposure, but its economics are still
primarily determined by realized crude, gas, and NGL prices, production cost,
decline rates, and the return on development capital.

The control point is resource depth plus low-cost operating capability. The
burden carrier is depletion: every barrel or equivalent produced today reduces
future inventory unless reserve replacement and development capital earn an
adequate return.

## Earnings quality and possible financial engineering

The cleanest forensic issue is denominator choice rather than GAAP recognition.
Management emphasizes CFO, adjusted earnings, free cash flow, and a percentage
return framework. The filing separately discloses a $12.553B capital program,
which is more informative than treating all CFO as distributable. Dispositions
also matter: the company disposed of approximately $3.2B of assets in 2025 and
is targeting $5B by year-end 2026. Asset sales can high-grade the portfolio,
but they can also fund returns while shrinking the productive base.

Required tests:

- Reconcile production growth with reserve replacement, finding and
  development cost, decline rates, and the capital program by basin.
- Separate base maintenance from growth, LNG, appraisal, and major-project
  spending; test returns on each cohort after price and schedule risk.
- Compare asset-sale proceeds with the cash required to replace the disposed
  production and reserves.
- Track whether the 45% CFO return framework leaves the balance sheet resilient
  at mid-cycle prices rather than only in a strong price environment.
- Test working capital, derivatives, and tax effects that may make CFO look
  smoother or stronger than recurring owner cash.

These are falsifiable presentation tests, not allegations of misconduct.

## What the cash numbers mean in an upstream company

Operating cash flow is cash collected and paid through the business during the
period, after operating costs, interest, and cash taxes, subject to working
capital timing. It is not automatically cash that can be distributed. An
upstream company must spend capital to replace depletion, develop approved
reserves, meet abandonment obligations, and fund projects that management says
will increase future production.

For this memo, owner cash is a conservative analytical quantity rather than a
company-reported metric:

`owner cash = operating cash flow - sustaining capital - required restoration or other unavoidable cash commitments`

The $7.243B 2025 residual subtracts the entire disclosed capital program from
operating cash flow. That is a useful upper-bound screen, but it is not yet
normalized owner cash because the filing's total program includes both
sustaining and growth spending. If the company can defer a growth project
without reducing the existing production base, that spending should be shown
separately in the valuation model. If a project is needed to offset decline, it
belongs in sustaining capital even if management labels it growth.

Commodity normalization is equally important. Reported earnings combine
production volume, realized crude/gas/NGL prices, hedges, royalties, taxes,
cost inflation, impairments, and one-time asset transactions. A three-year
average of reported earnings is not automatically a mid-cycle estimate. The
model should state the reference prices, production level, unit costs, tax
rate, sustaining capital, and reserve-replacement requirement used to derive
normalized owner cash.

## Management incentives and capital-allocation tests

The likely incentive conflict is not necessarily improper accounting. It is the
choice of measurements and timing that determine whether shareholders see
production growth, adjusted earnings, cash returns, or per-share value as the
main success measure. Buybacks can improve per-share results, but only create
value when the repurchase price is below a reasonable estimate of normalized
per-share value and when the company is not sacrificing reserve replacement or
balance-sheet resilience.

The 2025 evidence creates four tests for the proxy statement and subsequent
filings:

1. **Performance measures.** Identify the exact annual and long-term incentive
   metrics, their weights, threshold/target/maximum levels, and any adjustment
   for commodity prices, acquisitions, dispositions, or unusual items. Compare
   them with production growth, reserve replacement, unit costs, return on
   capital, operating cash flow after capital, safety, and
   emissions/remediation obligations.
2. **Per-share versus enterprise progress.** Reconcile repurchase volume and
   average price with diluted shares, net debt, production per share, reserves
   per share, and owner cash per share. A lower share count is not proof of
   value creation if the company paid a high price or funded the repurchase by
   selling productive assets.
3. **Portfolio high-grading.** For each material disposition, record the sold
   production, reserves, cash flow, tax effects, and sale proceeds. Compare
   those amounts with the cost and expected output of replacement projects.
   Selling higher-cost assets may improve near-term unit statistics, but the
   shareholder result depends on the value of what was sold and retained.
4. **Short-term cash-return pressure.** Test whether the stated return policy
   remains funded after sustaining capital, restoration obligations, debt
   service, and committed project spending under lower reference prices. If
   distributions exceed that amount, identify whether the difference came from
   asset sales, beginning cash, new borrowing, or recurring excess cash.

These tests require the proxy statement and detailed compensation tables; the
current company packet does not preserve a local proxy artifact. The correct
conclusion today is that the incentive channel is an open diligence item, not
that management compensation is either aligned or misaligned.

## Lyn Alden durability lens

The durable case is energy security, global demand, geopolitical redundancy,
low-cost resource positions, and the fact that supply cannot be expanded
quickly when investment has been constrained. The fragile case is higher rates,
commodity volatility, OPEC/geopolitical shifts, methane and carbon regulation,
electrification, and a shrinking pool of projects that can earn an adequate
return after transition and decommissioning costs.

Compared with ExxonMobil, ConocoPhillips has less downstream diversification but
less capital tied up in refining and chemicals. That can produce cleaner
operating accountability in a favorable upstream cycle, but it also leaves
cash more directly exposed to commodity prices and production decline.

## Damodaran narrative-to-numbers lens

The bullish narrative requires low-cost production growth, reserve replacement
above depletion, LNG optionality, disciplined high-grading, and buybacks made
below normalized value. The skeptical narrative says the company converts
finite reserves into current cash, sells assets to maintain payout optics, and
capitalizes a favorable commodity period as if it were a perpetual annuity.

For a mechanical screen, normalized owner-cash cases are: bear $5.5B × 12 =
$66B, base $8.0B × 15 = $120B, and bull $10.5B × 18 = $189B. The scenarios are
not a price target; they force the model to show what commodity, production,
capital-intensity, and reserve-replacement assumptions are required.

The multiple is not the main conclusion. The main conclusion is the required
commodity and reinvestment assumption behind each value. The bear case assumes
that a larger share of cash is consumed by sustaining capital and lower prices.
The base case assumes that the existing portfolio produces approximately $8B
of recurring owner cash after the capital needed to keep the business viable.
The bull case assumes both strong realized prices and enough low-cost growth to
justify a higher multiple. Before comparing any case with market
capitalization, the model must use the same date for equity value, add net debt
and other claims, account for non-controlling interests where relevant, and
divide by the correct diluted share count. None of those market-value inputs
are asserted in this memo.

## Thesis breakers and next filing tests

The thesis weakens if production growth requires rising capital per barrel,
reserve replacement falls below depletion, asset sales fund ordinary returns,
realized prices or LNG economics normalize below the base case, or buybacks
continue while debt and project obligations rise. The next filing should
capture proved-reserve changes, production and decline by basin, finding and
development cost, capital-program mix, asset-sale replacement, LNG project
economics, emissions/remediation obligations, debt maturities, and repurchase
prices/share count.
The proxy should also be added to the local evidence chain so the incentive
tests above can be completed with disclosed performance metrics rather than
assumptions.
