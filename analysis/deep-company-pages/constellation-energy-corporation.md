# Constellation Energy Corporation — nuclear scarcity, data-center contracts, and the capital cost of reliable power

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed GAAP figures unless explicitly identified otherwise; dollar amounts are in millions.

## How to read this memo

GAAP means the accounting rules used in the filed financial statements.
Operating cash flow is the cash produced by day-to-day operations. Property,
plant, and equipment (PP&E) is the physical generation infrastructure. The
post-capex residual below subtracts the cash needed to maintain or expand that
infrastructure; it is a screening measure, not management’s definition of free
cash flow.

## Investment question

Constellation owns one of the clearest scarce assets in the AI-power chain: a large nuclear and clean-generation fleet that can provide reliable electricity and emissions attributes when new grid supply is difficult to build. The investment question is whether that scarcity becomes durable common-equity cash after nuclear maintenance, relicensing, fuel, decommissioning, hedge collateral, working capital, debt, and the capital needed to restart or extend plants.

## What the filing reveals

- Constellation’s core control point is scarce, reliable clean generation, not generic renewable capacity. Nuclear output can be contracted to customers that value reliability and emissions attributes.
- The Microsoft PPA supporting the restart of the Crane Clean Energy Center illustrates the model: a customer contract helps justify a plant restart, but the project still carries construction, regulatory, operating, and timing risk.
- The filing explicitly states that energy delivered but not yet billed is estimated; unbilled customer revenue was $1.305B. This is normal utility accounting, but it is a key cash-conversion test when market contracts and large-load growth accelerate.
- Commodity derivatives are recorded at fair value with realized and unrealized effects in revenue or expense. GAAP net income can therefore move materially before the underlying power is delivered and cash settles.
- Nuclear assets bring obligations beyond ordinary capex: fuel, spent-fuel management, decommissioning trust funding, license renewal, outage planning, reliability requirements, and potential regulatory remediation.
- The Calpine acquisition closed in January 2026, after the FY2025 filing. The 2025 denominator is therefore a standalone baseline, while the current valuation is a pro forma platform claim. Integration, debt, divestitures, synergies, and fleet mix must be tested in the 2026 filings.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. How much FY2025 OCF was normalized cash?

The recovery from negative 2023/2024 OCF to $4.237B is encouraging but unusually large. Decompose it into hedge settlements, receivables, collateral, nuclear production, customer advances, tax credits, and working capital. The next filing should show whether cash remains positive after capex without relying on favorable derivative timing.

### 2. Are hedge marks being confused with operating economics?

Competitive power companies use derivatives to manage future generation and fuel exposure. Unrealized hedge gains or losses can dominate GAAP earnings even though cash settlement occurs later. Value contracted generation from expected settled cash and availability, not one quarter’s mark.

### 3. Does the PPA create value after full nuclear capital?

Long-term PPAs with Microsoft, Meta, or other customers can improve visibility, but the relevant return is after restart/life-extension capex, maintenance, fuel, insurance, regulatory compliance, decommissioning, and financing. A high contracted price is not sufficient if the plant requires uneconomic renewal capital.

### 4. What does Calpine add after integration?

The current thesis capitalizes the enlarged fleet, but the standalone FY2025 cash bridge does not contain Calpine’s earnings or integration costs. Track pro forma EBITDA to cash, debt reduction, divestiture proceeds, hedge profile, plant availability, and whether synergies are operational or merely presentation-level.

### 5. Are large-load contracts genuinely customer-funded?

Data-center demand can support generation economics, but the risk is that the utility builds before the load is energized. Test take-or-pay terms, customer credit, interconnection milestones, capex contributions, curtailment rights, and the allocation of stranded-capital risk.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** nuclear and reliable clean power are scarce physical assets in a capital-intensive, inflation- and policy-sensitive economy. The demand is durable, but higher rates, outage risk, regulation, fuel, and construction timing determine whether scarcity becomes shareholder cash.
- **Damodaran lens:** separate mature fleet cash, contracted PPA cash, merchant generation, tax credits, and restart/life-extension options. Calpine is a new lifecycle and acquisition cohort; it cannot be valued as though its future cash were already present in FY2025.
- **Forensic lens:** the highest-risk category errors are treating hedge marks as cash, unbilled energy as collected revenue, PPAs as risk-free contracts, and pro forma acquisition EBITDA as current owner cash.

## Management incentives and capital allocation

The proxy statement should establish whether annual and long-term awards reward
adjusted earnings, operating cash flow, free cash flow, EBITDA, generation,
capacity factors, safety, emissions, share price, return on invested capital,
or per-share value. Record each metric's definition, weights,
threshold/target/maximum levels, and exclusions for derivative marks, hedge
settlements, acquisitions, integration, tax items, outages, and unusual
charges.

The incentive tests are:

- Does a generation or EBITDA target charge nuclear fuel, outages, relicensing,
  spent-fuel, decommissioning, and required maintenance capital?
- Does an adjusted earnings measure exclude derivative losses or gains that are
  part of ordinary hedging, or recurring Calpine integration and financing
  costs?
- Does a growth target count a signed PPA, a data-center commitment, or a
  capacity addition before the asset is operating and earning cash?
- Are capital returns evaluated after hedge collateral, nuclear obligations,
  debt service, and required customer or project investment?
- Are acquisitions measured by post-integration cash per share and return on
  capital, rather than by pro forma EBITDA or fleet size?

The Calpine transaction makes this important. Management may be rewarded for
reported scale or synergies before the acquired fleet's capex, hedge profile,
working capital, debt, and integration cost are visible. The proxy and 2026
filings must show whether the compensation system charges those burdens.

## Balance-sheet resilience and owner cash

Constellation's balance sheet must be tested under both lower power prices and
adverse hedge movements. A merchant generator may owe collateral when market
prices move even if its long-term physical assets remain valuable. The analysis
should track cash and restricted cash, hedge collateral, margin requirements,
debt maturities, interest cost, nuclear fuel obligations, decommissioning
trusts, spent-fuel commitments, purchase obligations, and Calpine acquisition
financing.

The relevant stress sequence is:

`lower power price or outage -> hedge and working-capital cash use -> reduced cash after capex -> debt and collateral requirement -> ability to preserve nuclear reliability and common distributions`

A PPA can reduce price exposure but does not remove outage, construction,
regulatory, counterparty, or replacement risk. A customer contribution can
reduce initial capital but should be reconciled with availability obligations,
service cost, and the risk of stranded capacity.

The next filing should present pro forma debt, interest coverage, hedge
collateral, liquidity availability, nuclear obligations, Calpine cash flow,
capex, and capital returns together. A positive GAAP net income or a large
contracted revenue balance is not enough to establish resilience.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $100.9B of equity value. That is about 79x the FY2025 mechanical post-capex/acquisition residual of $1.274B. A normalized range of $0.8B / $1.6B / $2.6B at 28x / 35x / 42x produces $22.4B / $56.0B / $109.2B. The current price therefore requires a successful Calpine integration, durable nuclear scarcity pricing, strong PPA economics, and a materially higher normalized post-capex cash denominator than FY2025 alone supports.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Calpine integration increases leverage without producing stable cash after capex.
2. Hedge settlements or power prices normalize below the assumed earnings base.
3. Nuclear outages, relicensing, fuel, or decommissioning costs rise faster than PPA pricing.
4. Data-center projects are delayed, curtailed, or fail to fund the required generation investment.
5. Unbilled revenue, receivables, collateral, or customer concentration grow faster than cash.

Next filing checks: pro forma Calpine OCF and capex; hedge settlement versus marks; nuclear capacity factors and outage costs; PPA customer contributions; unbilled revenue; debt and interest; spent-fuel/decommissioning obligations; and capital returns after integration.
