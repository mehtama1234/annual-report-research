# CrowdStrike Holdings: Security Control, Trust Recovery, and Owner Cash

Date baseline: `2026-09-14`

Evidence base: CrowdStrike fiscal `2026` Form 10-K, fiscal `2026` Q3 and Q4
results materials, and fiscal `2027` Q1 Form 10-Q and results materials in the
company packet. The packet was collected on August 10, 2026. No full earnings-
call transcript is saved locally.

## Bottom line

CrowdStrike sells security software that helps organizations identify attacks,
control access, investigate incidents, and protect endpoints, cloud systems,
identities, data, and AI workflows. An endpoint is a laptop, server, or other
device connected to an organization's systems. The company's control point is
not ownership of the customer's hardware. It is the security data, detection
logic, policy, and response workflow that customers use to decide whether a
system or identity can be trusted.

The operating record is strong. Fiscal 2026 revenue was `$4.81B`, subscription
revenue was `$4.56B`, operating cash flow was `$1.61B`, and reported free cash
flow was `$1.24B`. In fiscal 2027 Q1, revenue was `$1.39B`, annual recurring
revenue (ARR) was `$5.51B`, operating cash flow was `$590.9M`, and free cash
flow was `$468.5M`.

The central question is whether this cash represents durable owner economics or
whether part of it is supported by customer prepayments, stock compensation,
and unusually high demand for security consolidation. Owner cash means cash
left for common shareholders after the spending required to keep the business
competitive and operating. It is not identical to management's adjusted free
cash flow.

## 1. What CrowdStrike controls

CrowdStrike controls a software layer with four connected functions:

- collecting security events from endpoints, cloud systems, identities, and
  data environments;
- analyzing those events to identify suspicious activity;
- enforcing policies and supporting investigation and response; and
- storing the history needed for compliance, threat hunting, and future model
  improvement.

The customer dependency is measurable. Replacing the platform can require
reinstalling sensors, moving security data, rebuilding detection rules,
retraining analysts, changing incident-response procedures, and proving that
the replacement provides adequate coverage. That creates switching cost, but
it does not make the company immune to replacement. A serious security event,
weak product coverage, price pressure, or a better consolidated platform can
reduce renewal and expansion.

CrowdStrike also sells additional modules, including identity security, cloud
protection, data security, Next-Gen SIEM, and AI-related security tools. A
module is an additional product sold to an existing customer. Module growth is
valuable only if it increases customer value and cash without creating equal
support, computing, sales, and research costs.

### Three-year owner-cash diagnostic

The filed cash-flow statements allow a consistent residual after property and
equipment, capitalized internal-use software, and cash acquisitions:

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Property/equipment and capitalized software | Cash acquisitions | Diagnostic residual |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2024 | `$1.166B` | `$226M` | `$239M` | `$690M` |
| 2025 | `$1.382B` | `$314M` | `$310M` | `$758M` |
| 2026 | `$1.612B` | `$371M` | `$382M` | `$859M` |

The residual improved, but stock-based compensation was `$649M`, `$861M`, and
`$1.097B` in those years. That does not mean the cash is fictitious; it means
the owner-cash result must be tested after the economic cost of maintaining the
employee base and controlling dilution. The July 19 Incident also makes
remediation and trust-recovery spending part of the durability test.

ARR is the annualized value of subscription contracts that are active at a
point in time. It is not revenue already earned, and it is not cash that can
be spent without providing future service. Net new ARR is the addition to that
annualized base from new and expanded contracts, net of reductions. It should
be tested against renewal, usage, pricing, gross margin, and collected cash.

## 3. Earnings quality and cash conversion

### Revenue quality

Subscription revenue was about `95%` of fiscal 2026 revenue. This is a favorable
revenue structure because it reduces dependence on one-time hardware or project
sales. The important follow-up is whether the subscription is economically
recurring: customers must renew, keep the platform deployed, buy additional
modules, and accept pricing that covers detection, storage, support, and
research costs.

Deferred revenue is cash collected before the company has delivered all of the
contracted service. It improves current cash flow but creates a future service
obligation. A rising deferred-revenue balance is useful evidence of demand, but
it must not be treated as current-period profit.

### Stock-based compensation

Stock-based compensation (SBC) is compensation paid through shares or rights to
receive shares rather than entirely in cash. The accounting expense is real,
but it does not leave the bank account on the grant date. The economic cost is
future dilution or cash used to repurchase shares that offset that dilution.

CrowdStrike's owner-cash analysis therefore needs both reported cash flow and:

- SBC as a percentage of revenue and gross profit;
- diluted shares outstanding over time;
- shares issued to employees;
- shares repurchased for employee tax withholding; and
- discretionary buybacks after operating needs and acquisitions.

The company can report higher free cash flow and higher earnings per share at
the same time that the value per original share improves little if dilution is
being offset at a high cash cost.

### The July 19 incident and normalized costs

The packet notes that management's non-GAAP measures repeatedly exclude costs
associated with the July 19 Incident and related matters. Non-GAAP income is a
management-defined profit measure that removes selected expenses from GAAP
(generally accepted accounting principles) results.

The correct question is not whether every incident cost repeats each year. It
is whether the incident reveals a recurring cost of maintaining trust: testing,
quality control, customer remediation, insurance, legal work, support labor,
and product-process investment. A cost can be unusual in timing while still
being necessary to operate the business safely.

## 4. Alden lens: durable need versus liquidity and trust risk

Security spending has a strong structural reason to exist. Organizations must
protect systems, meet regulatory obligations, investigate attacks, and limit
operational interruption. That demand can remain important during an economic
slowdown.

Liquidity means the ability to fund obligations when cash receipts are delayed
or conditions worsen. CrowdStrike's software model has less property and
equipment burden than a carrier, data-center owner, or hardware supplier. The
main liquidity risks are different:

- customers can delay expansion or reduce modules;
- a security incident can increase remediation and legal cash costs;
- cloud hosting, data storage, and AI inference costs can rise with usage;
- sales and support hiring can be increased before revenue is collected; and
- customer prepayments can make current cash look stronger than the future
  service burden.

The durability test is whether the company can preserve renewal, detection
quality, and cash generation while security budgets become more selective.

## 5. Damodaran lens: translate the platform narrative into numbers

The narrative is that CrowdStrike is becoming a broad security and AI-trust
platform. The numbers required to support that narrative are:

1. subscription growth after separating price, customer count, and module use;
2. gross retention and net retention, which measure customer loss and expansion;
3. ARR growth converted into recognized revenue and collected cash;
4. gross margin after cloud hosting, storage, telemetry, and AI-processing cost;
5. sales efficiency and the payback period for new customer acquisition;
6. research and development needed to preserve detection quality;
7. SBC and diluted shares per dollar of revenue and owner cash;
8. incident, legal, remediation, and insurance costs;
9. customer concentration and public-sector or partner dependence; and
10. the price paid for growth through the required normalized owner-cash yield.

The lifecycle question is whether CrowdStrike is still building a platform or
is approaching a stage where growth requires more modules, sales effort, and
AI infrastructure for each incremental dollar of revenue. A high valuation
requires durable growth, strong incremental margins, and limited dilution; it
cannot be justified by ARR alone.

## 6. What the market must be assuming

The dated scenario register preserves a September 14, 2026 equity-value input
and a normalized owner-cash screen. It is not a live quote or a price target;
it shows what the recorded valuation requires.

### Dated valuation screen

The register records approximately `$215.934B` of equity value against bear,
base, and strong normalized owner-cash cases of `$0.700B`, `$1.100B`, and
`$1.600B`, valued at `30x`, `45x`, and `60x`. The corresponding implied equity
values are `$21.0B`, `$49.5B`, and `$96.0B`.

The recorded equity value is far above the base screen. That does not prove the
market is wrong; it identifies the burden of proof. CrowdStrike must show
durable renewal after the incident, module adoption, gross margin after cloud
and AI-processing costs, and per-share cash after SBC, remediation,
acquisitions, and dilution. Refresh the screen when the next filing and a
synchronized market snapshot are available.

The bridge should show:

`equity value -> normalized owner cash -> implied cash yield -> growth and margin required`

Bear, base, and bull cases should vary renewal, module expansion, gross margin,
incident-related costs, cloud and AI-processing costs, SBC, diluted shares,
taxes, and the multiple applied to owner cash. A case using management's
adjusted free cash flow without an SBC and incident-cost treatment is incomplete.

## 7. Control point versus burden carrier

| Layer | CrowdStrike may capture | CrowdStrike still carries |
|---|---|---|
| Security telemetry | Data and workflow embedment | Storage, processing, privacy, and data-residency cost |
| Detection and response | High switching cost and trust | False positives, missed threats, product liability, and incident response |
| Platform consolidation | More modules per customer and lower vendor count | Integration risk, sales complexity, and customer budget resistance |
| AI security | New policy and monitoring demand | Rapid research spending, uncertain usage economics, and claims that must be proven |
| Recurring contracts | Visibility and customer prepayment | Future service obligation, renewal risk, and deferred revenue runoff |
| Asset-light structure | Lower property and maintenance capex | Dependence on cloud suppliers, talent, and stock-based compensation |

The company captures a cleaner economic layer than a physical network owner,
but it does not eliminate the burden. It moves the burden into trust,
software quality, data processing, specialized labor, and dilution.

## 8. Next-filing watchlist and thesis breakers

1. Does ARR growth remain supported by gross retention and customer expansion,
   rather than only new-logo sales or contract timing?
2. Does subscription gross margin hold after telemetry, storage, cloud, and AI
   processing costs are included?
3. Does deferred revenue grow in a way that supports future revenue without
   masking weaker collections or cancellation activity?
4. Do receivables grow more slowly than revenue and billings?
5. Does SBC fall as a percentage of revenue, and does diluted share count stop
   rising after employee awards and tax-withholding repurchases?
6. Are July 19 incident, remediation, legal, insurance, and customer-support
   costs isolated, declining, and transparently reconciled?
7. Do customers expand across identity, cloud, data, and AI modules while
   support and sales cost per customer remains controlled?
8. Does Falcon Flex expansion produce durable committed revenue or merely shift
   timing and discounting between products?
9. Does the company retain trust after future incidents, with stable renewal and
   reference-customer evidence?
10. Does owner cash per diluted share grow faster than reported adjusted free
    cash flow?

The thesis breaks if a major incident causes sustained renewal deterioration,
if ARR growth requires rising concessions and sales cost, if cloud and AI costs
compress gross margin, if deferred revenue and receivables stop converting to
revenue and cash, or if SBC absorbs the per-share benefit of growth.

## Conclusion

CrowdStrike is a strong example of a software control point: customers pay to
make security state visible, enforceable, and auditable across increasingly
complex systems. The company has demonstrated large recurring revenue and high
reported cash generation.

The unresolved investment question is quality of owner cash per diluted share.
That requires separating customer prepayment from earned profit, adjusted
profit from recurring operating cost, and headline cash generation from SBC,
incident remediation, cloud usage, and future service obligations. The next
filings should be read for trust retention and cash quality, not only ARR.
