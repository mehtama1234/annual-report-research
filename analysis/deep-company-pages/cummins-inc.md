# Cummins Inc. — engine franchise, distributed power, and the cash cost of reliability

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures below are filed GAAP figures unless explicitly identified otherwise; dollar amounts are in millions.

## Investment question

Cummins is a useful test of whether AI infrastructure demand is reaching a durable physical control point. The company has a cyclical engine and components franchise, a distribution/service network, and a fast-growing Power Systems business selling generators, controls, and microgrid-adjacent equipment into grid-constrained data centers. The key question is not whether power demand is real. It is whether the current mix shift produces durable owner cash after capex, inventory, warranty, emissions compliance, transition write-offs, and the capital required to support a large installed base.

The bridge is therefore a screen, not a valuation. Cummins generated $3.621B of operating cash but spent $1.235B on PP&E, leaving $2.386B before net acquisition cash; after the $12M acquisition outflow, the mechanical residual is $2.374B. The important tension is that this cash generation coexists with $458M of Accelera actions in 2025, $240M of electrolyzer goodwill/inventory charges in Q3, and a warranty balance that is nearly as large as one year of residual cash.

## Q2 2026 checkpoint: the power mix is improving, but the balance sheet still carries the proof burden

Q2 2026 revenue reached a record `$9.5B`, net income was `$932M`, and EBITDA
was `$1.7B`, or `17.5%` of sales. Power Systems sales rose `19%` and
Distribution sales rose `9%`, with management attributing the strength mainly
to data-center-related power demand. Engine sales were `$3.1B`, Components
sales were `$2.9B`, Distribution sales were `$3.3B`, Power Systems sales were
`$2.3B`, and Accelera sales were `$145M`.

Management raised 2026 revenue guidance to growth of `10%–13%` and EBITDA
guidance to `18.0%–18.5%`. These are meaningful improvements in the operating
case. They do not remove the requirement to test the mix. Power Systems growth
can offset truck-cycle weakness, but the company must still fund generator
capacity, service labor, inventory, warranty, and emissions compliance.

The Q2 filing reported first-half operating cash flow of about `$1.8B`, cash and
marketable securities of approximately `$3.9B`, and inventory near `$6.4B`.
Inventory was therefore large relative to the quarterly earnings gain. The
next filing should reconcile the record sales sequence to inventory turns,
receivable days, customer deposits, backlog, and cash collected. A data-center
order becomes durable owner cash only after the equipment ships, the customer
accepts it, the receivable is collected, and warranty and service obligations
remain within the expected return.

The quarter also illustrates why adjusted margins need a transition bridge.
Q1 included `$199M` of charges tied to the sale of the low-pressure fuel-cell
business, while 2025 included `$458M` of Accelera actions. Narrowing the
portfolio can improve future returns, but repeated charges should remain in the
economic denominator until the transition stops consuming cash and management
can show returns on the remaining investment.

## What the annual report actually says

- The five-segment model matters: Engine, Components, Distribution, Power Systems, and Accelera create different lifecycle and reinvestment profiles. Engine and Components remain exposed to North American truck replacement and freight cycles; Distribution and Power Systems are the current data-center and uptime beneficiaries.
- Management attributed record Distribution and Power Systems performance to demand for reliable backup power, particularly from data centers. The economic claim is credible, but it is an equipment-and-service claim, not a recurring software claim.
- The 2025 filing shows the cost of transition optionality. Cummins fully impaired electrolyzer goodwill, wrote down inventory, and recorded total Accelera charges of $458M. This is evidence of discipline only if future investment actually becomes narrower and returns improve; repeated “strategic” charges would be a quality-of-earnings warning.
- The filing also describes a $2.0B emissions-related charge in Q4 2023 and $1.9B of settlement payments in 2024, alongside recall, extended-warranty, mitigation, testing, and compliance obligations. This history makes emissions certification a core underwriting variable, not a footnote.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Is the data-center mix durable or a negotiated OEM surge?

Power Systems growth is real, but hyperscalers are large buyers with dual-sourcing leverage. Test realized price, gross margin, backlog cancellation terms, customer concentration, deposits, warranty burden, and whether service revenue rises with the installed fleet. A generator shipped into a data center is not equivalent to a contracted recurring platform until maintenance, controls, and replacement economics are demonstrated.

### 2. Does working capital validate demand?

Receivables and inventory are each roughly $5.8B. The research packet reports Q2 2026 inventory near $6.4B and receivables above $6.5B, so the next filings should test whether growth is converting to cash or whether Cummins is building ahead of uncertain truck and data-center orders. Inventory write-downs reached $119M in 2025, including the Accelera cleanup.

### 3. Is warranty risk under-reserved?

The product-warranty accrual was $2.778B at year-end; 2025 payments were $730M, and standard warranties issued were $632M. The question is not misconduct; it is whether emissions complexity, new engine launches, and high-output generator deployments make historical failure rates a poor guide. Track accrual additions, claims paid, campaign charges, and changes in warranty duration by segment.

### 4. Are transition losses being made to look nonrecurring?

Accelera actions totaled $458M in 2025 after $312M of strategic-reorganization charges in 2024. Treat “one-time” language skeptically until the business stops requiring inventory, fixed-asset, contract-termination, and severance cleanup. The bear case is not that zero-emission technology fails; it is that the profitable legacy base repeatedly funds experiments without a measurable return hurdle.

### 5. Does reported owner cash survive the full capital stack?

The residual is before dividends, debt repayment, joint-venture funding, pension or compliance cash, and the cost of maintaining global service capacity. Cummins also receives equity-method distributions and has substantial debt. Normalize the residual through a truck downcycle and a data-center capacity buildout before assigning a terminal multiple.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** Cummins monetizes the physical bottleneck beneath digitization: dependable electrons, fuel flexibility, engines, service labor, and replacement parts. This is a real-economy beneficiary of rising power intensity, but the moat is manufacturing and installed-base reach rather than monetary scarcity. Inflation, rates, commodity inputs, and capex cycles still matter.
- **Damodaran lens:** Split the company into lifecycle engines. Mature Engine/Components cash deserves normalized-cycle assumptions; Power Systems deserves a growth case only if backlog converts at durable margins and service attach rates; Accelera should be valued as an option with explicit future cash burn, not as proof of current growth.
- **Forensic lens:** the largest risks are denominator quality and liability timing: working-capital investment, warranty accruals, emissions obligations, inventory cleanup, and recurring transition charges can make EBITDA growth look cleaner than owner cash.

## Valuation framing at the current market snapshot

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $77.1B of equity value. That is about 32.5x the mechanical FY2025 owner-cash screen of $2.374B. A conservative normalized range of $1.8B / $2.6B / $3.4B implies equity-value cases at 24x / 30x / 36x of $43.2B / $78.0B / $122.4B. The base case is therefore approximately fully valued, not obviously cheap: it requires Power Systems growth to offset truck cyclicality without a repeat of emissions, warranty, inventory, or Accelera surprises.

## Management incentives and capital allocation

The proxy statement should establish whether annual and long-term awards
emphasize sales, adjusted earnings, operating cash flow, free cash flow,
return on invested capital, share price, segment margin, product launches,
safety, emissions performance, or customer service. The exact definitions,
weights, threshold/target/maximum levels, and exclusions matter because
Cummins is managing businesses at different lifecycle stages.

The incentive tests are:

- Does a Power Systems revenue or backlog goal include warranty, inventory,
  customer concentration, and service cost?
- Does an adjusted earnings measure exclude Accelera restructuring, inventory,
  goodwill, emissions, or legal costs that recur as part of the transition?
- Does a cash-flow measure charge working-capital investment, emissions
  compliance, warranty payments, and capital needed for the installed service
  network?
- Are acquisitions, joint ventures, and equity-method investments evaluated on
  cash return after capital and dilution rather than revenue or strategic
  positioning?
- Are repurchases made only after warranty, emissions, inventory, transition,
  and debt needs are funded?

An incentive plan that rewards revenue or adjusted EBITDA can encourage a
larger generator and components book before customer utilization, warranty
experience, and cash collection are known. An incentive plan tied to cash per
diluted share and return on invested capital gives more weight to the full
burden, but the proxy must show whether that is actually the design.

For each major product or transition investment, compare the promised return
with realized margin, inventory, warranty, service attach, working capital,
cash, and diluted shares. The $458M of 2025 Accelera actions and history of
emissions-related charges make recurring exclusions a central diligence item.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Power Systems growth decelerates while customer concentration and price concessions rise.
2. Data-center orders convert into receivables and inventory faster than operating cash.
3. Warranty accruals, recalls, or emissions-compliance spending rise faster than unit volume.
4. Accelera records another large cleanup or requires continued capital despite narrowed strategy.
5. PP&E capex remains above $1.2B while normalized OCF falls toward $3B or below.

Next filing checks: segment margin and backlog quality; inventory turns and receivable days; warranty roll-forward; Accelera cash versus non-cash charges; Power Systems service attach; and whether common repurchases resume only after those obligations are funded.
