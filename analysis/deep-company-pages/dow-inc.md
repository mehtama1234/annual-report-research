# Dow — integrated chemicals, spread-cycle exposure, and restructuring claims

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed U.S. GAAP figures in U.S. dollars;
dollar amounts are in millions unless otherwise stated.

## How to read this memo

Polyethylene is a plastic resin made from ethylene. A spread is the difference
between the selling price of a chemical product and the cost of its key input.
Integrated production links feedstock, chemical conversion, utilities, and
downstream products within one operating system. Operating EBITDA is a
management measure that excludes interest, tax, depreciation, and selected
items; it is not cash available to common shareholders. Sustaining capital keeps
assets operating, while growth capital adds capacity or changes the portfolio.

## Investment question

Dow owns a global network of chemical plants and application businesses serving
packaging, coatings, infrastructure, mobility, electronics, and consumer
products. Its possible advantage is feedstock flexibility, integration, scale,
technical know-how, and customer qualification. The central question is
whether those advantages produce acceptable through-cycle owner cash after
spread volatility, plant renewal, environmental obligations, pensions, debt,
restructuring, and the large Transform to Outperform program.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Dow operates six global businesses across Packaging & Specialty Plastics,
  Industrial Intermediates & Infrastructure, and Performance Materials &
  Coatings. Their spread, utilization, and capital requirements differ.
- Packaging & Specialty Plastics is the largest earnings lever. Q2 2026 showed
  how quickly polyethylene prices and self-help actions can change segment EBIT.
  The same sensitivity makes a single strong quarter weak evidence of durable
  economics.
- The 2025 base year was weak: sales were about $40.0B, net loss was $2.4B,
  continuing operating cash flow was $1.062B, and gross debt was $18.161B.
  The business therefore entered the restructuring program with a real balance-
  sheet constraint, not just a temporary earnings miss.
- Transform to Outperform targets at least $2B of near-term operating EBITDA
  improvement on top of an earlier $1B structural cost program. The critical
  test is cash after implementation costs, plant closures, lost volume,
  environmental spending, and any required replacement capacity.
- Cash provided by continuing operations was driven partly by cash earnings,
  advance customer payments tied to long-term supply contracts, and dividends
  from equity-method investments. These sources should not be valued equally.
- The company expects substantial capital spending while also carrying
  pensions, environmental liabilities, restructuring cash, and dividends. A
  lower capex target can protect liquidity but can also defer renewal.
- Dow's materials are embedded in downstream products, but customer
  qualification does not eliminate commodity pricing. The analysis must separate
  technical differentiation from the underlying price of polyethylene and other
  chemical products.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. What is the normalized chemical spread?

Bridge segment revenue and EBIT by selling price, volume, feedstock, energy,
utilization, product mix, freight, and currency. Test polyethylene and other
spreads at mid-cycle levels. A rebound caused by a short supply disruption
should not receive a permanent multiple.

### 2. Does operating EBITDA become cash?

Reconcile operating EBITDA to continuing operating cash flow, including working
capital, advance payments, affiliate dividends, pensions, taxes, interest,
restructuring, and environmental spending. Keep cash received from customers or
equity-method affiliates visibly separate from internally generated margin.

### 3. Is Transform to Outperform value creation?

Track announced savings, implementation cash, workforce reductions, site exits,
volume loss, severance, environmental closure cost, and capital avoided. Savings
count only after the business proves that the remaining plants can serve
customers without higher quality, freight, or reliability costs.

### 4. Is capital spending maintenance or growth?

Separate the $2.479B PP&E spend, $157M gas-field development, climate projects,
turnarounds, reliability work, and expansion. Compare depreciation with the
physical replacement needs of the network. Under-spending can improve current
cash while reducing future capacity or increasing later repair costs.

### 5. Are affiliate dividends and customer advances repeatable?

Identify the source, ownership, tax, and distribution history of equity-method
dividends. Track advance payments from customers against contract obligations,
future delivery, pricing, and working-capital reversal. Cash is cleaner when it
comes from collected margin rather than a claim on future performance.

### 6. Can debt and dividends coexist with weak cash?

Test dividends, debt maturities, pension contributions, environmental claims,
and restructuring cash against mid-cycle operating cash after renewal capital.
Capital returns should not be treated as evidence of durability when funded by
asset sales, debt, or deferred maintenance.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** Dow is exposed to global industrial activity, energy and
  feedstock prices, trade routes, inflation in replacement cost, and the rate
  environment. Scale and integration improve resilience only if leverage and
  renewal spending remain manageable.
- **Damodaran lens:** value each segment on mid-cycle spreads, volume,
  utilization, reinvestment, and terminal economics. Value Transform to
  Outperform as a probability-weighted restructuring plan, not as immediate
  EBITDA. Include pension, environmental, debt, and affiliate claims.
- **Forensic lens:** the major risks are treating operating EBITDA as owner cash,
  treating advance payments or affiliate dividends as recurring margin, calling
  restructuring savings permanent before implementation costs end, and
  overlooking deferred maintenance in a large capital program.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $21.05B of equity value at
$29.03 per share. Against the FY2025 negative $1.606B post-capital screen, a
current cash multiple is not meaningful. A normalized range of $1B / $2B / $3B
at 10x / 12x / 15x implies $10B / $24B / $45B. The current value requires a
substantial recovery in cash after capital, not merely the $2B EBITDA target.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Polyethylene and other spreads normalize before cost savings become durable.
2. Transform to Outperform savings require recurring cash charges or reduce
   capacity and customer service.
3. Capital spending stays high while operating cash remains weak.
4. Advance customer payments or affiliate dividends reverse.
5. Inventory and receivables grow faster than sales and margin.
6. Debt, pension, environmental, and dividend claims prevent the recovery cash
   from reaching common shareholders.
