# Dycom Industries, Inc. — fiber and critical-facility execution, backlog quality, and acquisition-funded scale

Date baseline: 2026-09-14  
Primary evidence: [Dycom company packet](../../extracted/industrial-goods/heavy-construction/dycom-industries-inc/company-packet.md), [Dycom FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/67215/000006721526000008/dy-20260131.htm), [Dycom Q1 FY2027 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/67215/000006721526000025/dy-20260502.htm), and [Dycom source ledger](../../extracted/industrial-goods/heavy-construction/dycom-industries-inc/source-ledger.md)

## Investment question

Dycom is a specialty contractor for fiber, wireless, utility-support, locating,
outside-plant construction, and now inside-facility electrical, security, and
building systems. It is a field-execution company at the point where digital
capacity becomes installed infrastructure.

The investment question is whether Dycom's skilled workforce, customer
relationships, backlog, and expanding critical-facility capabilities can
produce durable owner cash after fleet and equipment capital, receivables,
contract assets, customer concentration, acquisition financing, debt,
insurance claims, stock compensation, and dilution.

## What Dycom controls

The operating sequence is:

`fiber, wireless, utility, or data-center requirement -> engineering and permit -> award or master-service agreement -> crews, equipment, materials, and subcontractors -> construction and acceptance -> billing and collection -> next work order or facility phase -> cash`

Dycom controls field labor, routing and construction knowledge, operating-company
coverage, customer-specific execution, equipment, permitting capability, and
the ability to work across aerial, underground, wireless, and inside-facility
systems. Customers control project priorities, volumes, cancellations, and
payment. Dycom carries safety, weather, labor, equipment, working-capital,
insurance, and project-completion risk.

## Definitions

- **Backlog** is management's estimate of uncompleted services under customer
  contracts. For many master-service agreements, it is estimated from prior
  work and customer indications; customers may not be committed to specific
  volumes.
- **Book-to-bill** compares new awards with revenue. A ratio above `1.0x`
  improves visibility but does not establish margin or cash quality.
- **Outside plant** is the network infrastructure outside a facility, including
  aerial and underground fiber and wireless work. **Inside plant** is the
  cabling, electrical, security, and building-system work within a facility.
- **Recurring-capital residual** here means operating cash flow less capital
  expenditures. Acquisitions, debt, insurance, working capital, stock
  compensation, and buybacks remain separate owner claims.

The fiscal labels matter. Dycom's FY2026 ended January 31, 2026, while the
company packet's annual-report label is `2025`. The dossier uses the filing
periods explicitly so the annual and quarter numbers are not mixed.

Backlog quality also differs by segment. Communications backlog is largely
master-service-agreement work where future volumes can change. Building Systems
backlog is more closely tied to remaining performance obligations under firm
fixed-price or modified fixed-price orders. These should not receive the same
certainty weight.

## Three-year cash bridge

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Capital expenditures | Recurring-capital residual | Acquisition cash |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2024 | `$259.0M` | `$218.5M` | `$40.5M` | `$122.9M` |
| FY2025 | `$349.1M` | `$250.5M` | `$98.6M` | `$183.9M` |
| FY2026 | `$642.5M` | `$240.8M` | `$401.7M` | `$1.629B` |

FY2026 cash generation improved materially, but the Power Solutions acquisition
consumed more than four times the recurring-capital residual. Q1 FY2027 then
used `$24.6M` of operating cash, with receivables using `$283.9M` and contract
assets using `$80.5M`, partly offset by a `$169.4M` increase in accounts
payable. That is a direct demonstration that rapid revenue and backlog growth
require cash before the customer pays.

The acquisition was valued at about `$1.95B` and funded with approximately
`$1.645B` of cash, `$351M` of newly issued shares, and assumed seller debt of
`$64.8M`. The cash and dilution burden therefore persists after the transaction
closes. National Technology Integrators adds another inside-facility expansion
path and must be evaluated separately when the transaction closes.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering risks

### Backlog under master-service agreements is not committed volume

Dycom states that many customers are not contractually committed to procure
specific service volumes. Backlog estimates use prior activity, customer
information, expected scope, and timing assumptions. Cancellations, delays,
permitting, engineering changes, weather, and customer priorities can change
the number.

The strongest evidence is therefore executed work, customer-funded starts,
revenue, margin, billing, and collection—not the total backlog headline.

### Customer concentration is both a moat and a risk

FY2025 revenue included approximately `20.1%` from AT&T, `12.1%` from Lumen,
`8.5%` from Comcast, `7.3%` from Charter, and `7.4%` from another customer.
Large customers can provide repeat work and help Dycom plan crews, but a
spending change by one carrier can affect utilization, backlog, receivables,
and pricing simultaneously.

### Receivables and contract assets are the immediate cash test

Q1 FY2027 revenue reached `$1.965B`, but operating cash was negative because
receivables and contract assets expanded. DSO was `96` days, down from `111`
days a year earlier, but the absolute cash use was still large because the
business had grown rapidly. Track billed and unbilled receivables, customer
concentration, retainage, contract liabilities, collections, and the reversal
of the working-capital investment.

### Equipment capital is not optional growth spending

FY2026 capex was `$240.8M`, and management says equipment supports customer
demand and replacement cycles. A contractor can look asset-light compared with
an infrastructure owner while still needing fleets, aerial equipment,
trenching equipment, locating systems, facilities, and technology. Separate
replacement capital from capacity expansion and test utilization and resale
values.

### Acquisition accounting creates an adjustment gap

The Power Solutions purchase created customer-relationship, backlog, and trade-
name intangible assets of about `$1.284B` in the Q1 FY2027 filing. Amortization
will reduce GAAP earnings, while adjusted EBITDA may exclude it. The cost is
still evidence of the price paid for customer access and capability. Test
retention, cross-selling, data-center awards, margin, integration cost, and
cash return rather than accepting adjusted earnings as proof.

### Insurance and safety liabilities remain economic costs

At May 2, 2026, Dycom had accrued insurance claims of about `$116.4M` and
operating lease liabilities of about `$183.2M`. Safety and fleet risk are part
of the operating model even when the reported result is adjusted for individual
claims or acquisition costs.

## Balance-sheet resilience and capital requirements

At May 2, 2026, Dycom had `$538.8M` of cash, about `$2.816B` of debt, and an
`$800M` revolving facility. The debt structure included a `$1.54B` Term Loan A,
`$800M` Term Loan B, and `$500M` of 4.50% senior notes. The company also had
surety bonds of about `$653M` and standby letters of credit of about `$53.6M`.

Liquidity must therefore be evaluated against working capital, equipment
replacement, insurance claims, surety capacity, acquisition debt, and customer
concentration. A large cash balance does not eliminate the obligation to fund
crews and equipment before collecting from customers.

## Lyn Alden lens

Dycom benefits from durable digital-capacity requirements: fiber density,
wireless networks, data-center interconnection, private secure networks, and
critical-facility systems. Those needs can persist even when individual
telecom carriers reduce spending.

The business is still highly liquidity-sensitive. Carrier capex, hyperscaler
projects, BEAD timing, interest rates, permitting, labor availability, and
customer financing determine starts and collections. Acquisition debt adds a
second sensitivity: demand can be durable while the equity return is weak if
the company overpays for capability at the top of a project cycle.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative becomes:

`digital and critical-facility demand × customer relationship × skilled crew and equipment capacity × award and execution share -> contract revenue -> labor, subcontractors, equipment, insurance, working capital, taxes, debt, and acquisitions -> owner cash`

The valuation should separately model:

1. Communications versus Building Systems revenue and margin;
2. master-service-agreement backlog versus firm fixed-price backlog;
3. organic growth versus Power Solutions and NTI acquired growth;
4. receivables, contract assets, DSO, retainage, and customer concentration;
5. fleet and equipment replacement versus growth capex;
6. debt, interest, surety, insurance, and lease burdens; and
7. stock consideration, SBC, buybacks, and diluted cash per share.

At the September 14, 2026 market snapshot, Dycom equity value was about
`$8.84B`. The valuation register uses FY2026 recurring-capital residual of
`$401.7M`, but normalizes the cases for the Power Solutions acquisition, Q1
working-capital draw, debt, customer concentration, equipment capital,
insurance, and dilution. The market must prove that backlog growth is not just
acquisition and customer-volume optimism.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Management is incentivized to emphasize backlog, book-to-bill, adjusted EBITDA,
adjusted EPS, free cash flow, customer duration, acquisition synergies, and
share repurchases. Pair each with organic revenue, segment margin, DSO,
contract assets, equipment capital, debt, insurance, customer concentration,
SBC, and diluted shares.

The thesis breaks if:

- backlog grows through master-service estimates while customer volumes or
  starts weaken;
- receivables and contract assets consume cash faster than revenue growth;
- Power Solutions or NTI add revenue without incremental cash after debt,
  integration, and employee retention costs;
- customer concentration causes a simultaneous drop in volume, pricing, and
  collections;
- equipment and fleet spending rises faster than utilization and project
  margin;
- BEAD, fiber, or data-center work is delayed by customer funding, permitting,
  or regulation;
- insurance, safety, or surety claims require more capital than modeled; or
- adjusted EPS rises while cash per diluted share falls after acquisitions,
  debt, SBC, and buybacks.

| Is leverage manageable? | Debt declines while Dycom funds equipment, insurance, and acquisitions | Acquisition-led growth requires repeated refinancing before cash returns appear |

## Bottom line

Dycom controls a scarce field-execution relationship in digital infrastructure.
Its labor, routing, permitting, equipment, and operating-company network turn
fiber and critical-facility demand into functioning systems. The shift from
outside-plant communications to inside-facility building systems expands the
addressable work but also increases acquisition and integration risk.

The reported demand signal is strong: backlog reached `$11.906B`, book-to-bill
was about `2.2x`, and customers were extending contract durations. The cash
test is less clean. FY2026 acquisition cash was `$1.629B`, Q1 FY2027 operating
cash was negative `$24.6M` as receivables and contract assets expanded, and debt
was about `$2.816B`. The durable thesis requires backlog to convert through
collection, Power Solutions to earn its price, equipment capital to remain
productive, customer concentration to stay manageable, and cash per diluted
share to rise after debt, insurance, SBC, and acquisition claims.
