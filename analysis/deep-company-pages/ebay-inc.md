# eBay — marketplace trust, take rate, advertising, and owner cash

As of 2026-09-14. FY2025 figures are filed GAAP figures unless explicitly
identified otherwise; dollar amounts are in millions.

## How to read this memo

eBay operates a marketplace where independent sellers list goods and buyers
search, evaluate, pay for, and receive them. The company generally does not
own the merchandise. Its economic role is to provide access to demand, seller
tools, payment and transaction services, trust systems, advertising, and in
some cases shipping support.

GMV means gross merchandise volume: the total value of paid transactions that
flow through the marketplace, including shipping and taxes, before returns or
cancellations are removed. Revenue is the amount eBay retains for its services.
Take rate is revenue divided by GMV. It measures monetization of activity, not
the profitability of the entire transaction.

GAAP is the accounting rule set used in the filed statements. Non-GAAP profit
excludes selected items. It can help explain management's operating view, but
it does not remove stock compensation, technology investment, transaction
losses, marketing, or the cost of keeping buyers and sellers on the platform.

## Investment question

eBay controls a marketplace relationship built around seller supply, buyer
discovery, trust, category expertise, and transaction infrastructure. Its
strongest positions are in collectibles, motors parts and accessories, luxury,
refurbished goods, apparel, sneakers, and other categories where unique supply
and buyer interest matter.

The investment question is whether that relationship can produce durable cash
after seller incentives, payment processing, fraud and transaction losses,
marketing, product development, platform hosting, stock compensation,
advertising investment, taxes, acquisitions, and the risk that buyers or
sellers move to other channels.

The 2025 residual of $1.276B fell below the 2023 and 2024 screens even though

than a conclusion that the platform weakened permanently. The 10-K identifies

included $2.5B of repurchases. A large buyback can reduce the share count, but
it does not improve the underlying marketplace economics if cash conversion is
weakening.

## What eBay controls

eBay's control point has four linked parts:

1. **Supply:** sellers list new, used, collectible, refurbished, and hard-to-
   find goods that are not always available in a standardized retail catalog.
2. **Discovery:** buyers can search and compare a large, changing inventory.
3. **Trust:** ratings, seller history, payment processes, buyer protection,
   returns, fraud controls, and dispute systems reduce transaction uncertainty.
4. **Monetization:** eBay charges transaction fees and sells promoted listings
   and other advertising services to sellers.

The control point is strongest when a category has unique supply, knowledgeable
buyers, and repeated seller participation. It is weaker where products are
standardized and buyers can compare identical items on Amazon, brand sites,
Google, social commerce, or local resale platforms.

eBay does not control the seller's merchandise, the buyer's income, the
carrier's service, or all demand discovery. It therefore has a relationship
control point but not the same physical fulfillment control as a retailer that
owns warehouses and delivery operations.

## Narrative-to-numbers bridge

| Management narrative | Metric to inspect | Economic proof still required |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Recommerce and C2C | Used-goods GMV, seller retention, disputes, returns, trust cost | Supply growth must remain healthy without excessive incentives or fraud losses |

| Higher take rate | Revenue/GMV by category, payments, shipping, ad mix | Higher monetization must not reduce GMV, seller retention, or buyer conversion |
| AI-enabled discovery | Search conversion, relevance, support cost, product-development expense | AI must improve conversion or lower cost after compute, data, and employee expense |

In 2025, revenue grew 8% and GMV grew 7%, with take rate increasing from
13.77% in 2024 to 13.94%. The increase was helped by advertising penetration
and a U.K. shipping program, while lower fees connected to the U.K.
consumer-to-consumer initiative partly offset revenue. This is a useful
contradiction: strategic programs can improve activity or user relevance while
reducing near-term monetization per transaction.

## Q2 2026 current checkpoint

The second quarter supplies stronger activity evidence, but it also exposes a
larger trust-cost test.

| Q2 / first-half measure | Current evidence | What it means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Q2 revenue | $3.134B, +15% reported and +14% FX-neutral | Retained revenue grew with the marketplace, not only through currency |
| Q2 GMV | $22.398B, +15% reported and +14% FX-neutral | The activity denominator improved materially |
| Q2 take rate | 13.99%, unchanged year over year | Q2 growth was not dependent on a higher headline take rate |
| First-half take rate | 13.95% versus 13.89% | Monetization improved modestly, but the change is not large enough to establish durable pricing power |
| Active buyers | 136M, +2% year over year | Buyer count grew more slowly than GMV, so frequency, mix, or average transaction value requires follow-up |
| First-party advertising | $570M, +25% reported and +24% FX-neutral | Advertising was a major revenue driver; attribution changes helped conversion and monetization |
| Total advertising | $596M, 2.7% of GMV | The ad layer is material, but the filing does not by itself prove incremental seller demand |
| First-half transaction losses | $271M versus $167M | Losses grew faster than GMV; $68M was attributed to fraud and recovery-rate changes and $32M to volume |
| First-half continuing OCF / capex | $1.519B / $295M | Reported free cash flow was $1.224B before acquisitions, financing, and dilution |
| First-half SBC | $338M | SBC equaled about 22% of continuing OCF and remains an owner-cost adjustment |
| Depop acquisition | $1.4B cash paid, closed July 30 | The recommerce thesis now carries a large acquisition payback requirement |

The cash comparison needs two qualifications. First, first-half operating cash
flow was up from $415M in the prior-year period because cash taxes fell by
$794M and working-capital movements improved; this is not the same as proving
that the marketplace permanently converted more of its revenue into cash.
Second, the quarter's $326M company-defined free cash flow deducts property
and equipment but not stock compensation, the Depop purchase, dividends,
buybacks, or debt repayment. A stricter owner-cash view subtracts the $338M of
first-half SBC and the $39M of acquisitions already recorded in the period,
before considering the $1.4B Depop payment after quarter end.

The most important contradiction is therefore: activity is broadening, but the
cost of protecting transactions is rising faster than activity. The filing
states that Q2 transaction-loss growth came from unfavorable buyer and seller
fraud and recovery rates as well as higher volume. The next test is whether
the loss rate per GMV falls as controls improve, or whether the company is
accepting more claims, chargebacks, and protection expense to support growth.

The buyer data also needs discipline. Active buyers rose 2% while Q2 GMV rose
15%. That can be good if existing buyers are purchasing more often or buying
higher-value goods, but it can also indicate concentration in categories or
customers. Management should disclose enough cohort and category data to
separate healthy engagement from a narrow volume mix.

The Depop acquisition should not be credited as value creation on the closing
date. It adds a new audience and fashion supply, but the required proof is
incremental active buyers, seller supply, repeat purchase, contribution after
marketing and trust costs, and cash returns above the $1.4B purchase price.

## Trust, fraud, and transaction losses

Trust is not only a brand feature; it is a cost center and a balance-sheet
exposure. eBay has to identify fraud, handle payment disputes, manage returns,
protect buyers, and decide how much loss to absorb to maintain participation.
The 2025 cash-flow statement included $396M of transaction losses, compared with
$353M in 2024 and $360M in 2023. The analyst must determine whether this line
reflects a stable cost per GMV, a changing fraud environment, a product or
policy change, or growth in higher-risk transaction categories.

The correct denominator is not only transaction-loss dollars. Track losses as
a percentage of GMV, seller and buyer complaints, refunds and cancellations,
payment disputes, reserve changes, and the effect on repeat activity. A
marketplace can report strong GMV while weakening if the quality of that GMV
requires rising loss absorption or incentives.

## Advertising and take rate

Promoted Listings allow sellers to pay for greater visibility on or away from
the marketplace. This can be an efficient monetization layer because eBay knows
the item, category, buyer intent, and transaction result. But advertising can
also create a conflict: sellers may need to pay simply to restore visibility,
while buyers receive less relevant results.

The filing says first-party advertising revenue is recognized when the
promotion service is performed. That timing is relatively straightforward, but
the economic test is harder: did advertising cause an incremental sale, or did
it move a seller's own demand from an unpaid placement to a paid placement?
Measure seller return on ad spend, ad penetration, organic conversion, take
rate, and seller retention together.

The company also warns that AI agents and chatbots could change how consumers
search for products and how advertising is purchased. If discovery moves
outside eBay, the platform could lose both buyer traffic and the information
that supports its advertising products.

## Cash conversion and investment portfolio

eBay's operating cash flows arise primarily from cash received from customers
on its marketplace platforms, offset by sales and marketing, employee
compensation, and payment-processing expenses. Continuing operating cash flow
fell to $2.009B in 2025 from $2.414B in 2024, even as revenue rose.

The 2025 cash flow also included substantial purchases and maturities of
investments. These movements can make total investing cash look favorable, but
they are treasury management rather than proof of marketplace earnings. The
owner-cash denominator should separate operating cash, property investment,
acquisitions, equity-investment distributions, and investment-portfolio
turnover.

eBay capitalized $134M of platform development costs in 2025, compared with
$108M in 2024. Capitalized development is an asset on the balance sheet, but
the software is amortized over roughly one to three years. The short life means
the company must continually spend to maintain the platform. A low reported
property-capital number does not mean the marketplace has no maintenance cost.

## Management incentives and owner claims

Stock-based compensation was $607M in 2025, equal to about 30% of continuing
operating cash flow. eBay also issued $141M of common stock and repurchased
$2.5B. Repurchases may more than offset annual issuance, but the analyst must
track diluted shares, award vesting, future unearned compensation, and whether
buybacks were made at a price supported by normalized cash rather than a strong
market period.

The company had $902M of unearned stock-based compensation at the end of 2025,
to be expensed through 2030. This is not a current cash payment, but it is a
future expense and a potential source of dilution. It should remain in the
owner-return analysis.

Management can be rewarded for GMV, active buyers, revenue, advertising, or
adjusted EPS before the platform proves seller health and cash per share. The
compensation plan and buyback authorization should therefore be read alongside
GMV quality, take rate, transaction losses, and continuing operating cash.

## Lyn Alden, Damodaran, and forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** eBay is less capital-intensive than owned retail, but it
  remains exposed to consumer liquidity, credit conditions, cross-border trade,
  shipping, fees, and trust during weaker economic periods. A cash-rich balance
  sheet helps, but declining activity, rising fraud, and seller migration can
  weaken the platform before the income statement fully reflects it.
- **Damodaran lens:** translate marketplace narrative into GMV, take rate,
  category growth, advertising penetration, transaction losses, operating
  margin, reinvestment, and diluted shares. Value the mature marketplace and
  any recommerce or category investments separately from speculative future
  growth.
- **Forensic lens:** the main presentation risks are treating GMV as revenue,
  take-rate expansion as pure pricing power, advertising growth as incremental
  demand, investment-portfolio gains as operating earnings, adjusted profit as
  owner cash, and buybacks as value creation without charging SBC and platform
  renewal.

## Valuation framing

The September 11, 2026 market snapshot was approximately $49.026B of equity
value at $107.75 per share. Against the FY2025 continuing owner-cash screen of
$1.276B, the equity value is about 38 times that cash measure. The market is
therefore pricing more than a stable mature marketplace; it requires continued
GMV growth, advertising monetization, category strength, and improved cash
conversion.

A normalized range of $1.0B / $1.3B / $1.6B at 18x / 22x / 26x implies
approximately $18B / $28.6B / $41.6B of equity value. The bull case requires
the 2026 growth acceleration to persist after Depop integration and requires
transaction losses, seller incentives, AI investment, and stock compensation
to remain controlled. The current market value is above this simple bull case,
so the price demands either higher normalized cash or a longer period of
above-normal growth.

## Evidence grades and thesis breakers

| Question | Current grade | What would upgrade or weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketplace control point | B+ | Durable buyer/seller retention and category-specific supply depth |
| GMV and organic growth | B+ | Growth remains after acquisition, promotions, and macro recovery effects |
| Take-rate quality | B | Higher monetization without seller or buyer deterioration |
| Advertising economics | B- | Incremental seller return, conversion, and profit after ad investment |
| Cash conversion | B- | 2025 decline explained and reversed without investment gains or tax timing |
| Trust and transaction losses | B- | Losses per GMV and disputes remain controlled |
| Capital allocation and dilution | C+ | Buybacks exceed SBC at prices supported by normalized owner cash |

Thesis breakers:

1. GMV growth slows after favorable consumer or category effects fade.
2. Take-rate increases reduce seller retention, listing quality, buyer
   conversion, or category competitiveness.
3. Advertising revenue grows mainly by charging sellers for traffic that was
   previously organic rather than creating incremental sales.
4. Transaction losses, refunds, cancellations, or fraud rise faster than GMV.
5. Buyer and seller activity migrates to AI agents, social platforms, brand
   sites, Amazon, or competing resale marketplaces.
6. Operating cash remains below the 2023–24 level while management continues
   large buybacks or acquisitions.
7. Capitalized platform development, stock compensation, and transaction costs
   consume a growing share of the cash claimed as free cash flow.
