### Q2 2026 primary cross-check: growth still carries acquisition and contract claims

The Q2 2026 SEC release reported record quarterly revenue of $5.15B, organic
growth of 19.6% after adjusting for acquisition contribution and the UK sale,
and remaining performance obligations of $17.14B. It also reported contract
liabilities of approximately $2.61B. These data strengthen the demand read, but
they do not erase the dossier's core contradiction: organic execution can be
strong while acquisition amortization, contract-liability release, claims,
retainage, and working-capital timing determine how much becomes owner cash.

## Bottom line

EMCOR is a diversified specialty-construction and facilities-services platform.
It controls an execution relationship across electrical and mechanical systems,
building automation, data-center fit-outs, industrial services, maintenance,
and complex institutional facilities. Scale gives it a broader demand surface
than Comfort Systems, but it does not remove the core burden: project estimates,
craft labor, subcontractors, claims, retainage, safety, and customer collection.

2025 revenue was $16.99B, operating income $1.713B, net income including
noncontrolling interests $1.273B, and operating cash flow $1.302B. Remaining
performance obligations were $13.25B, up 31.2% from $10.10B in 2024. The
headline result is strong, but operating income included a $144.9M gain on the
sale of United Kingdom operations, and $1.61B of RPO growth came from
acquisitions, including Miller Electric. The clean operating conclusion must
separate organic project economics from acquisition and disposition effects.

Current judgment: EMCOR is a high-quality execution and demand sensor, with
better diversification and substantial customer-funded working capital, but the
owner-cash case is conditional on project-estimate discipline, labor
productivity, claims/retainage realization, acquisition returns, and the
reversal risk embedded in contract liabilities.

## What the company actually controls

EMCOR operates through roughly 100 operating subsidiaries across electrical and
mechanical construction, facilities services, building services, and industrial
services. Its 2025 demand exposure included network and communications,
manufacturing and industrial, high-tech manufacturing, healthcare, institutional,
water and wastewater, transportation, hospitality, and commercial work.

Its control chain is:

`complex facility requirement -> bid / contract -> engineering and procurement -> craft labor and subcontractors -> measured progress -> billing / retainage -> acceptance and service`

The valuable relationship is with customers that need one or more accountable
specialty operators to make a facility functional and compliant. The burden is
carried by EMCOR's operating units and the labor/subcontractor network. A
customer can sign a large contract, but EMCOR must still convert it into
productive hours, material availability, acceptable project margin, and
collected cash.

- Revenue grew 16.0% in 2025, while OCF declined 7.5% and net income grew

## Cash conversion and owner-cash bridge

### 2025 reported bridge

| Item | $ millions |
|---|---:|
| Net cash from operations | 1,302.1 |
| Less: purchases of property and equipment | (112.8) |
| Less: acquisitions, net of cash acquired | (1,022.1) |

The $423.8M screen is not a normalized owner-cash result. It includes the
disposition proceeds and does not include the full economic cost of integrating
Miller, contingent consideration, debt, dividends, repurchases, insurance, or
the working capital required to support growth. Excluding the UK proceeds, the
screen is approximately $167.2M after capex and acquisitions.

OCF itself was supported by customer-funded balances: contract liabilities
increased $155.6M, accounts payable increased $189.3M, and accrued payroll and
other liabilities increased $141.2M. Against that, accounts receivable used
$437.8M, inventories used $27.0M, and contract assets used $42.8M. This is a
normal feature of construction economics, not evidence of fraud, but it means
cash conversion depends on billing and project timing.

### Contract liabilities and retainage

At year-end 2025, current contract assets were $337.7M, while current contract
liabilities were $2.327B. Net contract liabilities were $1.990B, up from
$1.764B in 2024. Within uncompleted construction projects, costs incurred plus
estimated earnings totaled $28.506B while billings totaled $30.428B, producing
$1.922B of net contract liabilities.

The clean read is that EMCOR has negotiated customer advances that fund part of
the working-capital cycle. The skeptical read is that future revenue and cash
conversion are partly dependent on successfully executing work already billed.
The company disclosed $944.5M of billed retainage in receivables and $152.3M of
subcontractor retainage in payables. It expects about 90% of its retainage to be
collected in 2026 and about 89% of subcontractor retainage to be paid. Those
collections and payments are concrete next-filing tests.

## Earnings quality and accounting judgment

### Cost-to-cost revenue recognition

EMCOR generally recognizes construction revenue over time using a cost-to-cost
input method. The filing and audit report state that the process requires
estimating variable consideration from pending change orders, claims, bonuses,
penalties, and costs to complete. The auditor describes the estimates as
especially challenging, subjective, and complex because they rely on project
forecasts, customer history, third parties, and dispute resolution.

This is the core forensic risk. A project can show accounting revenue and
profit before the final cost, claim, or change-order outcome is known. The
appropriate question is not whether EMCOR uses percentage-of-completion—it is
whether its prior estimates systematically prove conservative, stable, or
optimistic when projects finish.

### Claims and unapproved change orders

At December 31, 2025, contract assets included $26.2M of unapproved change
orders. Claims included in accounts receivable were $116.1M, up from $6.9M in
2024. The company also disclosed $158.8M of undisputed billed amounts and
retention related to unapproved change orders and claims. Amounts in claim
status may not be paid until final resolution.

The increase in claims-related receivables is not automatically improper; it may
reflect a larger project portfolio. But it is a direct warning against using
RPO, revenue, or operating income as a substitute for collected cash. Track
claim aging, write-offs, settlements, gross margin on affected contracts, and
whether claims grow faster than revenue.

### Disposition and acquisition accounting

The $144.9M UK-sale gain boosted 2025 operating margin by approximately 85 basis
points. It should be excluded from recurring earnings. At the same time, the
$1.022B acquisition program produced only $24.4M of incremental contribution
net of identifiable-intangible amortization in the reported year. This is not a
failure by itself, since acquisitions can have partial-period contributions and
integration periods, but it creates a high hurdle for future returns.

Goodwill was $1.41B at year-end 2025 versus $1.02B in 2024. The accounting model
does not amortize goodwill; impairment testing depends on reporting-unit fair
values and future expectations. The acquisition thesis therefore needs a
post-close return-on-invested-capital bridge and an impairment sensitivity, not
only pro forma revenue growth.

## Balance-sheet resilience and liquidity

EMCOR ended 2025 with $1.112B of cash and cash equivalents, including $870.5M
in money-market funds. Its cash balance declined from $1.340B in 2024 despite
strong net income because acquisitions, repurchases, dividends, and working
capital consumed cash. The revolving facility and cash generation are expected
to meet liquidity needs, but the credit agreement limits dividends, repurchases,
acquisitions, debt, and capex through customary covenants.

The balance-sheet risks are less about immediate liquidity than about a stacked
claim set: project retainage, insurance liabilities, letters of credit and
surety, acquisition obligations, lease and equipment commitments, goodwill,
and the possibility that customer advances reverse before project cash is
collected. The company also carries $360.1M of estimated insurance liabilities
at year-end across current and non-current portions, partly offset by insurance
recoveries.

## Lyn Alden lens: durability, liquidity, and cycle exposure

EMCOR benefits from multiple durable physical trends: data centers and digital
processing, complex electrical loads, high-tech manufacturing, reshoring,
healthcare, water infrastructure, energy transition, building replacement, and
maintenance. Its diversification makes it a broad real-economy sensor rather
than a single-theme AI bet.

But macro durability does not mean every project is durable. The chain is:

`customer financing and capex -> awards / RPO -> labor and materials -> project completion -> billing / retainage -> collected cash`

Rising liquidity can produce large RPO before labor and project capacity are
available. Tightening credit can impair customer awards, collections, and
subcontractor health with a lag. The most durable part of the portfolio may be
maintenance, building services, controls, healthcare, water, and replacement
work; the highest upside and cyclicality sit in large technology, high-tech
manufacturing, and data-center deployments.

## Damodaran lens: narrative, lifecycle, and reinvestment

The narrative is “scale plus specialty execution captures rising physical
complexity.” To value it, translate the story into:

`signed work -> recognized revenue -> after-tax project margin -> working-capital release -> maintenance capex -> acquisition and labor reinvestment`

Scale can improve procurement, prefabrication, virtual design, automation,
insurance, and customer access. It can also hide a weak project cohort inside a
larger consolidated result. A lifecycle model should separate:

- organic growth from acquired growth;
- recurring building services from new construction;
- project margin improvement from disposition gain;
- customer advances from durable cash generation;
- maintenance capex from expansion capex; and
- per-share growth from repurchases and SBC.

The key incremental-return question is whether the $1.022B of acquisition
capital produces durable after-tax cash above the cost of capital after
integration, amortization, labor investment, and working capital. The correct
multiple is therefore applied to normalized owner cash or segment earnings—not
to 2025 EPS inflated by a sale gain and reduced share count.

## Management incentives and financial engineering

Management incentives naturally reward revenue, operating income, EPS, backlog,
acquisition integration, and shareholder returns. Those objectives can create
several presentation risks:

- RPO can grow through acquisitions and include certain variable consideration,
  so its headline growth is not the same as organic demand;
- cost-to-cost estimates can shift current profit when cost-to-complete changes;
- disposition gains can improve reported operating margin while removing future
  revenue and cash flow;
- repurchases can increase EPS even when total invested capital or acquisition
  debt rises; and
- goodwill and intangible assets can defer recognition of a weak acquisition
  until impairment or margin pressure appears.

The current evidence supports these as tests, not allegations of misconduct.
The strongest check is a cohort-level acquisition and project roll-forward
that ties original estimates to final cash margins, claims, retainage,
contract assets, and impairment outcomes.

## Valuation and reverse-valuation questions

No price target is assigned. The market must prove that:

1. RPO growth converts into revenue and cash without a material margin reset.
2. The $1.990B net contract-liability position remains a productive funding
   advantage rather than a future execution obligation that consumes cash.
3. The United States electrical margin recovers or stabilizes after new-geography
   labor and Miller integration pressures.
4. Miller and other acquisitions earn an adequate return on the cash invested.
5. Maintenance and facilities services provide a durable base through a weaker
   construction or data-center cycle.
6. Claims, retainage, insurance, and customer concentration remain manageable.
7. Share repurchases create per-share value after SBC and acquisition funding,
   rather than merely lowering the denominator.

## Filing-based thesis breakers

The thesis weakens if future filings show:

- RPO growth increasingly comes from acquisitions or unapproved variable
  consideration rather than new profitable awards;
- revenue grows while contract assets, claims, retainage, or unbilled
  receivables grow faster than cash collections;
- project-margin revisions repeatedly reverse prior profits;
- electrical or mechanical margins fall as labor productivity and availability
  worsen in new geographies;
- customer advances and contract liabilities decline without corresponding
  stable margins and cash realization;
- acquisition contribution remains low relative to purchase price, goodwill,
  amortization, and integration costs;
- the UK-sale gain or other dispositions become necessary to support reported
  earnings or capital returns; or
- repurchases and debt rise while diluted shares, total equity value, or owner
  cash do not improve.

## Final judgment

EMCOR controls a valuable relationship at the point where complex facilities
become usable: electrical, mechanical, controls, building-services, and
industrial execution. Its scale and diversification are advantages, and its
2025 results show real operating strength. But it carries more of the physical,
labor, project-estimate, claims, and working-capital burden than a pure brand,
software, or financial intermediary.

The clean thesis is that scarce execution capability and a broad service
platform create durable returns on capital as physical complexity rises. The
skeptical thesis is that RPO, customer advances, acquisition growth, and
cost-to-cost accounting make current visibility look cleaner than eventual
owner cash. The decisive evidence will be project-margin outcomes, cash
collection, acquisition ROIC, and labor productivity after the backlog is
actually worked.
