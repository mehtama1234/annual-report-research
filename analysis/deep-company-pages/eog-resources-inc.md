# EOG Resources — independent upstream scale, reserve renewal, and cash-return policy

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are U.S. GAAP figures in U.S. dollars; dollar
amounts are in millions unless otherwise stated.

## How to read this memo

EOG's free cash flow is a non-GAAP measure. It is useful only after checking
what the company calls total capital expenditures, how working capital moved,
and whether acquisition-related costs or asset sales affect the result. An
upstream producer owns a depleting reserve base: cash left after one year's
drilling is not fully available to owners if the company must keep replacing
declining wells and reserves.

## Investment question

EOG is a U.S.-weighted, non-integrated oil and gas producer. Its control point is
not a refinery or pipeline; it is a portfolio of drilling locations, geological
knowledge, operating execution, gathering access, and capital-allocation
discipline. The question is whether EOG can convert its reserve depth and field
execution into durable owner cash after drilling, reserve renewal, gathering,
environmental, and commodity-price risk.

proved reserves were in the United States.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Oil, NGL, and gas production create different economics. Crude and condensate
  averaged 521.9 thousand barrels per day in 2025, NGLs 288.2 thousand barrels
  per day, and natural gas 2,533 million cubic feet per day. A total BOE figure
  can hide mix and price differences, so the analysis must keep products separate.
- Q4 2025 showed the downside case: lower oil realizations and impairments
  reduced GAAP net income, yet the company still generated $978M of free cash
  flow. Q1 2026 generated $1.493B and Q2 generated $2.799B as oil realizations
  improved. This is operating leverage, not proof of a new permanent cash level.
- The 2025 Form 10-K reported $843M of impairments. The company excluded about
  $657M in its adjusted reconciliation, primarily for Barnett and Woodford
  assets. The exclusion may be appropriate for a normalized operating measure,
  but it is evidence that resource quality and prior carrying assumptions must be
  tested at the field level.
- EOG committed to return at least 70% of annual net operating cash flow before
  certain balance-sheet changes, less total capital expenditures, through
  regular and special dividends and repurchases. The policy is shareholder-
  friendly only if capital spending is sufficient to preserve the reserve base.
- In 2025 the company returned approximately $4.7B of free cash flow through
  $2.2B of regular dividends and $2.5B of repurchases. The next test is whether
  returns remain covered at mid-cycle oil and gas prices without reducing future
  drilling quality.
- EOG is not independent of infrastructure. Gathering, processing,
  transportation, export, and power access determine whether production can be
  sold at the reported realization. Rising gas volumes make these connections
  more important.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Is free cash flow after reserve replacement?

Compare total capital spending with maintenance drilling, development drilling,
exploration, facilities, acquisitions, and reserve additions. The company can
report positive free cash flow while shrinking its future production base if
capital is below the long-run replacement need.

### 2. How much of the cash change is price versus execution?

Split production, realized price, basis and differentials, hedges, royalties,
taxes, unit costs, and working capital. The movement from Q1 to Q2 2026 shows
how much reported improvement can come from oil price alone.

### 3. Are adjusted cash-flow add-backs recurring?

The 2025 reconciliation added $73M of after-tax acquisition-related costs to
adjusted cash flow from operations. Track integration, severance, transaction,
and asset-retirement costs over several years. A cost can be unusual in one
quarter and economically recurring across an acquisition program.

### 4. What do impairments say about capital allocation?

Follow Barnett, Woodford, and any future impairments from the original reserve
estimate through production, price assumptions, carrying value, and disposal.
Impairments are not proof of misconduct; they are evidence that prior expected
cash flows did not survive updated assumptions.

### 5. Does the 70% return policy protect the asset base?

Reconcile dividends and repurchases to mid-cycle cash after drilling and reserve
renewal. Test whether the company borrows, sells assets, or reduces inventory
quality to maintain the payout during a weak commodity period.

### 6. Are per-share returns genuine after stock compensation?

Track $216M of 2025 stock-based compensation, diluted shares, repurchases,
executive scorecards, and production per share. Repurchases should be judged
against intrinsic value and dilution, not only against the number of shares
retired.

## Incentives, depletion, and cohort position

EOG's policy to return at least 70% of annual net operating cash flow before
specified balance-sheet changes, less total capital expenditures, creates a
clear capital-allocation promise. It does not answer whether total capital
expenditure is enough to replace depleted reserves or whether repurchases are
being made at attractive prices. The policy should therefore be measured in
cash per diluted share and production/reserves per share across a full
commodity cycle, after the $216M stock-compensation cost and all reserve-
renewal requirements.

The 2025 impairment record is also an incentive and lifecycle checkpoint. A
company can distribute high current cash while older acreage loses value and
new drilling is required to maintain output. The next annual report should
show whether reserve additions, production, and cash per share justify the
capital retained and returned.

EOG belongs in the [integrated-energy versus contracted-infrastructure
comparison](../cross-sector/integrated-energy-versus-contracted-infrastructure-forensic-comparison-2026-09-13.md)
and the [commodity scarcity and embedded service first-principles essay](../first-principles/commodity-scarcity-embedded-service-and-owner-cash.md).
Compared with integrated majors, EOG has a simpler upstream denominator and
less downstream diversification; compared with royalty and streaming firms,
it carries direct drilling, depletion, infrastructure, and environmental
burden. Its cleaner economics depend on acreage quality and capital discipline,
not on escaping the commodity cycle.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** EOG benefits from domestic energy security and U.S.
  production flexibility, but it remains exposed to oil and gas cycles, rates,
  inflation in drilling inputs, transport constraints, regulation, and the
  liquidity of energy markets. Cash reserves and low complexity help, but they
  do not remove depletion.
- **Damodaran lens:** value each basin by decline, production, price, cost,
  reserve life, drilling inventory, sustaining capital, taxes, and probability-
  weighted exploration. Use mid-cycle prices rather than Q2 2026 realizations.
  The 70% policy is a capital-allocation constraint, not a valuation input by
  itself.
- **Forensic lens:** the risks are treating BOE growth as equivalent product
  growth, treating adjusted cash flow as owner cash, excluding impairments and
  acquisition costs too readily, and treating current free cash flow as
  durable without reserve-replacement evidence.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $77.95B of equity value at
$147.36 per share. Against FY2025 free cash flow of $4.663B, the screen is
about 16.7x. A normalized owner-cash range of $3.5B / $5.0B / $6.5B at 14x /
16x / 18x implies $49B / $80B / $117B. The current price assumes that EOG can
maintain or grow mid-cycle cash while replacing reserves and continuing its
high-return policy.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Oil and gas prices normalize while drilling, gathering, and service costs
   remain high.
2. Production rises but proved reserves, inventory quality, or cash per share
   decline.
3. Impairments recur in acquired or legacy plays, indicating poor capital
   allocation or weaker resource quality.
4. Free cash flow depends on asset sales, working-capital releases, or adjusted
   add-backs rather than operating improvement.
5. The 70% return policy requires debt or underinvestment in reserve renewal.
6. Gathering, processing, transport, regulation, or environmental liabilities
   reduce realized cash below the field-level plan.
