# Equinix: interconnection density versus the capital burden of digital real estate

## Bottom Line

Equinix controls a dense, vendor-neutral interconnection ecosystem rather than
just renting powered floor space. Its 280 data centers in 77 markets, more than
10,500 customers, and more than 500,000 interconnections can make each new
customer more valuable to the network and each network/cloud relationship more
useful to existing customers. That is the real control point.

The FY2025 filing supports durable relationship economics: revenue was $9.217B,
more than 90% was recurring, adjusted EBITDA was $4.530B, and AFFO attributable
to common stockholders was $3.761B. But the same filing prevents a simplistic
“asset-light digital utility” conclusion. Operating cash flow was $3.911B,
recurring capital expenditures were $284M, total property/equipment capex was
$4.311B, real-estate acquisitions were $994M, total indebtedness was $21.4B,
operating lease liabilities were $1.5B, and SBC was $498M.

The current conclusion is **control-with-burden test**. Equinix may own one of
the strongest network-density control points in the AI infrastructure chain,
but value depends on earning adequate returns on new powered capacity while
preserving liquidity, energy access, tenant health, and the interconnection
network.

## How to read the company

`customer IT estate -> colocation and interconnection density -> network/cloud adjacency -> lower latency and higher reliability -> recurring rent, cross-connect, and managed infrastructure revenue -> cash available for the next capacity build`

Equinix provides premium colocation, physical and virtual interconnection,
data-exchange, edge, and remote-support services. The economic control point is
the ecosystem: network providers, clouds, enterprises, and content partners
benefit from locating and connecting where other participants already are.
The resulting density can create switching friction and a local network effect.

Equinix does not control power prices, utility availability, construction
costs, lease renewals, interest rates, customer IT budgets, or the ability of
joint-venture partners to fund expansion. It owns or operates the platform that
coordinates those dependencies, but it must still fund the physical system.

## What the FY2025 numbers mean

| Fiscal year ended December 31 | Revenue | Net income | OCF | Recurring capex | AFFO | Total property/equipment capex |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2023 | $8.188B | $0.969B | $3.217B | $0.219B | $3.019B | $2.781B |
| 2024 | $8.748B | $0.814B | $3.249B | $0.250B | $3.356B | $3.066B |
| 2025 | $9.217B | $1.348B | $3.911B | $0.284B | $3.761B | $4.311B |

The recurring-capital screen is:

`$3.911B OCF - $284M recurring capex = ~$3.627B before growth capex, acquisitions, distributions, debt, leases, and economic SBC`

That is a more appropriate first screen than subtracting all $4.311B of
property capex, because the latter includes capacity expansion that may create
future revenue. It is also not sufficient: a business that must continually
build data centers to preserve growth may have a larger sustainable capital
requirement than management's recurring-capex definition captures.

The total-investment bridge is much heavier:

`$3.911B OCF - $4.311B property/equipment capex - $994M real-estate acquisitions - $251M business acquisitions = approximately $(1.645)B before investment securities and financing`

This negative bridge does not mean the network is unprofitable. It means the
growth model relies on debt, equity, joint ventures, asset recycling, and future
capacity utilization. The owner must decide whether the next dollar of powered
capacity earns more than its capital cost.

## Earnings quality and forensic review

### 1. Recurring revenue is strong, but the physical obligation is recurring too

Equinix reports that recurring revenues comprised more than 90% of total
revenue for the past three years and that more than 90% of monthly recurring
revenue bookings came from existing customers. This is strong retention and
visibility evidence. It does not eliminate the costs of power, rent, labor,
maintenance, security, cooling, network equipment, and uptime obligations.

The correct test is recurring revenue per cabinet, interconnection, megawatt,
and site alongside utility cost, rent, depreciation, sustaining capex, renewal
rent, and customer service. If MRR rises while cash retained per unit of
capacity falls, the network effect is not yet producing adequate owner returns.

### 2. AFFO has a useful discipline and a broad adjustment set

Equinix's FY2025 AFFO was $3.761B, compared with FFO of $2.668B and GAAP net
income attributable to common stockholders of $1.350B. The AFFO reconciliation
adds back $498M of SBC, $568M of non-real-estate depreciation, $200M of
amortization, $68M of impairments, $33M of restructuring, and $18M of
transaction costs, while subtracting $284M of recurring capital expenditures.

The measure is useful for REIT comparison, but the bridge must stay visible:

`GAAP income -> FFO -> AFFO -> recurring capex -> growth capex/acquisitions -> debt/equity issuance -> dividend capacity`

SBC is non-cash at grant but economically dilutive. Adding it back can make
AFFO look more distributable than per-share owner economics if new shares are
required to retain employees. Impairments and transaction costs may be
nonrecurring in a single year, but Equinix recorded $233M of impairment in 2024,
$68M in 2025, and recurring restructuring/transaction charges in both years.

### 3. Growth capex is the central reinvestment question

Equinix spent $4.311B on property, plant, and equipment in 2025 versus $3.066B
in 2024, while also spending $994M on real-estate acquisitions and $251M on
business acquisitions. The filing says 52 major development projects were
underway, expected to deliver 55,000+ retail cabinets and 100+ MW of xScale
capacity through 2028, and land acquisitions supported approximately 1 GW of
future capacity.

This is a meaningful growth runway, not proof of return. Track project-level
capital, power availability, construction timing, lease-up, customer bookings,
incremental adjusted EBITDA, recurring capex, and the time from spend to
stabilized utilization. A backlog or megawatt pipeline that requires repeated
external capital at lower yields is not equivalent to retained owner cash.

### 4. Joint ventures and VIEs can obscure the full burden

Equinix uses xScale and other joint ventures to expand capacity. The filing
states that some VIE joint ventures are not self-sustaining and that control of
the activities most important to economic performance is shared equally with
partners. The company can earn management fees while capital, debt, and
economic ownership are split across entities.

The forensic bridge should therefore capture consolidated and unconsolidated
capex, debt, guarantees, partner contributions, distributions, management fees,
promote economics, and non-controlling interests. “Asset-light” at the parent
can reflect a genuine capital-efficient partnership or a transfer of capital
burden that still belongs in the economic model.

### 5. Power, lease, and service costs can reverse the margin story

In 2025 Americas cost of revenue increased partly from $29M of higher
depreciation and $23M of higher utility costs. EMEA costs included higher rent
and compensation. The filing warns that power procurement, outages, capacity
constraints, IBX lease renewals, and infrastructure failure can materially
affect results.

The next filing should separate pricing and utilization from energy cost,
renewal rent, facilities labor, depreciation, service quality, and outage/
remediation costs. A data center that is full but power-constrained may have
less incremental economic value than a lower-utilization site with cheap,
reliable expansion capacity.

## Alden lens: digital necessity with real-estate liquidity risk

Equinix benefits from data-intensive workloads, hybrid architectures, AI
inference, and low-latency connectivity. Its global density and vendor-neutral
position can remain valuable through technological change because customers
still need interoperability, security, and local access.

But the liquidity structure is real estate, not pure software. As of December
31, 2025, total indebtedness was approximately $21.4B, cash and short-term
investments $3.2B, operating lease liabilities $1.5B, and a $4.0B revolving
facility was available. The company explicitly expects to use additional debt
and capital markets to support growth, acquisitions, distributions, and
refinancing.

The Alden stress test is higher rates plus power scarcity plus construction
inflation plus slower customer lease-up. Equinix can retain strategic value
while the equity becomes more fragile if financing costs rise faster than
pricing, if power cannot be secured, or if the company must issue equity into a
weak capital market to fund capacity.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers and valuation

The valuation should decompose:

`interconnection density -> recurring MRR and pricing -> site utilization -> EBITDA/AFFO -> recurring capex -> growth capex and acquisitions -> debt/equity claims -> per-share value`

At the September 11, 2026 snapshot, EQIX traded at $1,037.72. Using 98.3M
shares outstanding from the 2025 filing gives an approximate equity value of
$102.0B. That is roughly 27x FY2025 AFFO and 28x the recurring-capex screen of
$3.627B. These are screening multiples, not a price target.

The price requires durable MRR growth, high interconnection retention, pricing
power, successful xScale and retail capacity deployment, and access to
financing at acceptable cost. Reverse valuation cases should vary:

- MRR growth, churn, renewal pricing, interconnection additions, and customer mix;
- power price, utility availability, rent, labor, and service-quality costs;
- recurring versus growth capex and stabilized project utilization;
- JV/VIE capital and economics, acquisitions, and non-controlling interests;
- AFFO adjustments for SBC, impairments, restructuring, transaction costs, and
  straight-line items;
- debt maturities, interest, lease obligations, equity issuance, and dividends;
  and
- the value of network density if hyperscalers or customers build more direct
  interconnection alternatives.

## Incentives and possible financial-engineering risks

Management and employees have natural incentives tied to AFFO, adjusted EBITDA,
revenue, MRR, bookings, capacity, development, and shareholder distributions.
Those measures can improve while capital intensity and financing risk rise.

The specific forensic questions are:

- Does AFFO add back SBC and other adjustments that are recurring costs of
  operating the network?
- Is recurring capex understated relative to the capital needed to maintain
  uptime, power density, and customer quality?
- Do JV/VIE structures shift debt or growth capex away from consolidated
  statements while Equinix retains meaningful economic obligations?
- Do impairments, restructurings, transaction costs, or asset sales recur as
  part of the normal development cycle?
- Are dividends and expansion funded by durable cash, or by debt and equity
  issuance that dilute future per-share economics?
- Does AI-related demand increase high-value interconnection economics or
  merely accelerate a low-return power and construction race?

These are accounting and capital-allocation questions, not allegations of
wrongdoing.

## What would prove this wrong

Downgrade the network-density interpretation if future filings show:

1. MRR, interconnections, or bookings grow while churn, pricing, utilization,
   or cash per cabinet deteriorates;
2. recurring capex, power, rent, or maintenance requirements rise faster than
   recurring gross profit;
3. new projects and xScale capacity require debt/equity but fail to reach
   stabilized returns within the promised time;
4. JV/VIE obligations, guarantees, partner funding shortfalls, or NCI claims
   become a material hidden burden;
5. debt refinancing and lease renewals absorb increasing AFFO;
6. SBC and equity issuance offset a meaningful share of AFFO growth per share;
7. outages, cyber incidents, power constraints, or lease loss damage customer
   retention; or
8. hyperscaler/customer alternatives weaken the pricing and switching value of
   Equinix's interconnection ecosystem.

## Evidence grade and unresolved questions

- **Filing fact:** FY2025 recurring revenue, OCF, AFFO, recurring and total
  capex, acquisitions, debt, leases, SBC, impairments, and JV/VIE disclosures are
  anchored to the Form 10-K.
- **Disclosed fact:** MRR, interconnection, xScale, and capacity-development
  claims are company-reported demand and pipeline evidence.
- **Inference:** Equinix controls interconnection density and customer adjacency,
  but the network effect is constrained by power, rent, construction, service,
  and financing requirements.
- **Unresolved:** the next filing must demonstrate incremental cash return per
  cabinet/megawatt after recurring capex, power, rent, SBC, JV obligations, and
  lease-up. Rising AFFO with falling per-share or project returns would weaken the
  control-point thesis.
