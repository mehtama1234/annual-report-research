## How to read this memo

Operating cash flow is the cash the reported business generated during the
year. Capital spending is cash invested in wells, plants, equipment, and other
productive assets. The difference is a screening residual, not automatically
cash available for dividends or buybacks: an upstream company must replace
depleting reserves, and an integrated company must also maintain refineries,
chemical plants, logistics, environmental controls, and safety systems.

## Bottom line

ExxonMobil controls an integrated energy system: upstream reserves and
production, refining and fuels, chemical and specialty products, trading,
technology, and logistics. Integration can smooth one part of the cycle with
another, but it does not eliminate commodity exposure. The owner
still funds depletion, megaprojects, refineries, working capital, environmental
obligations, and the transition while returning large sums to shareholders.

FY2025 produced $52.0B of operating cash flow and $28.4B of PP&E spending, a
$23.6B screening residual. Exxon returned $37.5B through buybacks and common
dividends, approximately $13.9B more than that residual. This is not proof of
an unsafe policy because some capex is growth capital and commodity cash is
cyclical; it is proof that headline distributions require a through-cycle
reserve-replacement, maintenance-capex, and balance-sheet test.

## Q2 2026 operating checkpoint

ExxonMobil reported Q2 2026 GAAP earnings of `$14.5B` and adjusted earnings of
`$14.7B`. Operating cash flow was `$23.6B`, cash capital expenditures were
`$6.8B`, and reported free cash flow was `$17.2B`. The company distributed
`$9.4B` in the quarter through dividends and repurchases. These are strong
numbers, but they should not be carried straight into a normalized valuation:
the company attributes the earnings increase to higher prices and margins,
advantaged volume growth, and cost savings.

The segment split makes the sensitivity visible. Q2 earnings were `$7.927B`
from Upstream, `$5.465B` from Energy Products, `$1.131B` from Chemical Products,
`$956M` from Specialty Products, and a `$(954)M` Corporate and Financing loss.
The upstream bridge identified approximately `$4.650B` of benefit from price,
`$1.140B` from advantaged volume growth, and a `$(1.060)B` impact from Middle
East disruption. The quarter demonstrates operating leverage to market
conditions as well as asset quality; it does not by itself establish the
sustainable cash level.

Production was `4.514M` oil-equivalent barrels per day in Q2, down from
`4.630M` in the prior-year quarter. Permian and Guyana growth mostly offset the
Middle East disruption, and management said the fifth Guyana FPSO was on plan
for startup in Q4 2026. The next test is whether new advantaged production
raises cash after depreciation, development capital, and the cost of replacing
depleting reserves, rather than only raising reported volumes.

The capital-allocation comparison remains demanding. Cash capital expenditures
were `$13.0B` for the first six months of 2026; dividends were `$8.6B` and
repurchases were `$10.0B`. The company reported a second-quarter debt-to-total-
capital ratio of `13.7%` and net debt-to-capital of `10.7%`, so the balance
sheet was not the immediate constraint. The deeper question is whether the
distribution policy remains prudent at mid-cycle prices after sustaining
capital, reserve replacement, environmental obligations, and project overruns.

This checkpoint strengthens the bull case for low-cost assets and integrated
operations while preserving the main caution: a high-cash quarter can result
from price, margin, and timing before it proves a durable owner-cash run rate.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | $344.582B | $36.010B | $55.369B | $21.919B | $33.450B | $31.539B |
| 2024 | $349.585B | $33.680B | $55.022B | $24.306B | $30.716B | $23.029B |
| 2025 | $332.238B | $28.844B | $51.970B | $28.358B | $23.612B | $10.681B |

The direction matters: residual cash declined as capital spending rose while
revenue and earnings declined. That is a normal cyclical/expansion pattern,
but it is not a clean compounding cash stream. The burden is partly growth
capital and partly the recurring cost of keeping a giant physical system
productive.

## What the cash bridge does and does not prove

The $23.6B 2025 residual answers only one question: how much operating cash was
left after the selected PP&E line. It does not answer whether Exxon replaced
the reserves it produced, whether the projects earn more than their financing
cost, or whether environmental and decommissioning obligations are adequately
funded. It also does not include dividends, repurchases, debt repayment,
working-capital needs for the next cycle, or cash spent on acquisitions and
investments.

The three-year pattern is therefore more useful than the 2025 number alone:

- Revenue fell from $349.6B in 2024 to $332.2B in 2025, while PP&E spending
  rose from $24.3B to $28.4B.
- Net income fell from $33.7B to $28.8B and operating cash flow fell from
  $55.0B to $52.0B.
- The residual fell from $30.7B to $23.6B even before considering the full
  cost of acquisitions, working capital, reserve replacement, and shareholder
  distributions.

This is not an accounting accusation. It is a valuation boundary: a buyer who
uses 2025 operating cash must show why the higher capital burden is temporary,
why commodity prices are not above normal, and why the future production base
will not require more cash than the model allows.

| FY2025 segment | Earnings |
|---|---:|
| Upstream | $21.4B |
| Energy Products | $7.4B |
| Chemical Products | $0.8B |
| Specialty Products | $2.9B |

The upstream segment is the largest earnings contributor, while refining, chemicals, and
specialty products provide diversification and customer access. The control
point is the ability to move molecules and products through a large integrated
system and allocate capital toward the highest-return reservoirs, facilities,
and product chains. Scale lowers some costs and improves optionality, but it
also creates a large fixed asset base with decommissioning, environmental,
geopolitical, and transition exposure.

## Earnings quality and forensic questions

The central quality test is whether reported earnings convert into cash after
the capital required to replace declining reserves and maintain refineries and
chemical assets. OCF remained high, but the post-PP&E residual fell from
$33.5B to $23.6B in two years. Accounts receivable rose to $35.7B and
energy-related inventory to $23.0B in 2025. Those balances can reflect scale
and price timing, but they can also delay conversion of earnings into cash.

Questions that could reveal denominator or presentation problems—not evidence
of misconduct—include:

- How much capex is sustaining, reserve replacement, emissions reduction, or
  discretionary growth, and what return does each cohort earn?
- Are production gains coming from durable reservoir quality, acquisitions,
  favorable price, or accelerated depletion?
- Do refining and chemical margins normalize above or below 2025 levels?
- Are inventory and receivable movements reversing, and how much cash is tied
  to trading and customer financing?
- Do buybacks compete with reserve replacement and balance-sheet resilience?

## Lyn Alden durability lens

The durable macro case is energy demand, geopolitical redundancy, the physical
difficulty of replacing hydrocarbons, and ExxonMobil's low-cost resource
positions and integrated infrastructure. The fragile case is that high rates,
commodity volatility, carbon policy, electrification, methane rules, refining
overcapacity, and stranded-asset risk raise the required return on each new
dollar of capital. Integration reduces volatility; it does not remove it.

Cash fell from $31.5B in 2023 to $10.7B in 2025 while distributions stayed
large. A stronger through-cycle test is whether debt remains manageable at
mid-cycle prices after sustaining capex, cleanup, and reserve replacement—not
whether one year of OCF covers the dividend.

## Damodaran narrative-to-numbers lens

The bullish narrative requires upstream production growth from advantaged
assets, refining and chemicals normalization, high returns on strategic growth
projects, and disciplined buybacks. The skeptical narrative says the market
capitalizes peak commodity cash while treating depletion and transition
capital as optional.

For this screen, normalized owner-cash cases are: bear $18B × 12 = $216B,
base $25B × 15 = $375B, and bull $32B × 18 = $576B, versus the current equity
snapshot of approximately $744.6B. The base case is deliberately below the
market; it means the current price requires stronger normalized cash, a higher
multiple, or both. It is not a price target.

## Thesis breakers and next filing tests

The thesis weakens if production growth requires disproportionate capex,
reserve replacement costs rise, refining/chemical margins fail to normalize,
working capital absorbs cash, debt rises while buybacks continue, or transition
and remediation obligations become more material than the valuation credits.
The next filing should capture reserve life and replacement, unit-level capex
returns, production cost, refinery utilization and margins, inventory/AR
reversal, debt maturities, decommissioning, emissions spending, and buyback
price/share-count effects.
