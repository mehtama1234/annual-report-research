# Forrester Research, Inc. — research workflow under AI and contract-value pressure

Date baseline: 2026-09-14  
Primary evidence: [Forrester company packet](../../extracted/technology/research-services/forrester-research-inc/company-packet.md), [Forrester FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1023313/000119312526105114/forr-20251231.htm), and [Forrester source ledger](../../extracted/technology/research-services/forrester-research-inc/source-ledger.md)

## Investment question

Forrester sells research memberships, advisory work, consulting, and events to
organizations making technology, customer-experience, and business decisions.
Its product is interpreted judgment delivered through a client relationship.

The central question is whether the company can turn that judgment into a
workflow product with enough retention and pricing power to offset the lower
cost of generic information and artificial-intelligence tools. The financial
question is more urgent than the thematic question: 2025 revenue fell to
`$396.9M`, contract value fell 6% to about `$292.4M`, operating cash was only
`$21.1M`, and the company recorded `$110.7M` of goodwill impairment.

Forrester is therefore a useful negative or transition case beside Gartner. It
shows that demand for outside interpretation can remain real while the legacy
commercial model loses value.

## What Forrester controls

The operating sequence is:

`institutional uncertainty -> research membership or advisory access -> client decision -> consulting or workflow use -> renewal and expansion -> cash`

Forrester controls a research brand, analyst interpretation, customer access,
and increasingly an AI-enabled interface to its research. It does not control
the client's technology budget, implementation decision, internal expertise,
or willingness to replace a traditional research subscription with lower-cost
tools.

The key products have different economics:

- Research memberships are recurring but exposed to contract-value pressure,
  retention, product migration, and procurement scrutiny.
- Consulting depends on project bookings, staffing, utilization, and client
  budgets. It is more labor-intensive than research memberships.
- Events depend on attendance, sponsors, travel budgets, venues, and execution.
- Forrester AI may improve access and retention, but it can also increase
  delivery cost or reduce the perceived need for a separate research license.

## Definitions

- **Contract value** is the annualized value of contracts in force. It is a
  forward indicator, not revenue and not cash collected.
- **Goodwill impairment** is an accounting reduction when the estimated value of
  an acquired reporting unit falls below its carrying value. It does not by
  itself consume current-period cash, but it records evidence that prior
  acquisition expectations were too high or that future cash expectations fell.
- **Adjusted earnings** exclude items such as stock compensation, restructuring,
  acquisition-related amortization, and impairment. The excluded items still
  matter if they recur or reflect the cost of operating the business.
- **Owner cash** here means operating cash after property spending, before
  repurchases and discretionary investments.

2025 year-end level. AI adoption is strategically important only if it produces
renewal, expansion, better delivery cost, or measurable customer use—not merely
more product activity.

## Three-year cash bridge

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Property and equipment | Reported recurring-capital residual | Main forensic issue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| FY2023 | `$21.7M` | `$5.5M` | `$16.2M` | Cash was positive despite weak contract economics, requiring working-capital review |
| FY2024 | `-$3.9M` | `$3.4M` | `-$7.3M` | Deferred revenue and accrued liabilities reduced cash; repurchases continued |
| FY2025 | `$21.1M` | `$3.0M` | `$18.1M` | Credit-loss expense, goodwill impairment, restructuring, and contract-value decline complicate quality |

The cash bridge is much weaker than the adjusted-earnings narrative typically
used for a research business. In 2025, operating cash included non-cash
goodwill impairment and credit-loss adjustments. It also received a `$4.8M`
benefit from accounts receivable changes, while deferred revenue reduced cash
by `$6.3M`. The right question is whether cash remains positive after contract
renewal, restructuring, AI investment, and required office capital.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering risks

### Goodwill impairment is a business signal

Forrester recorded `$83.9M` of Research reporting-unit goodwill impairment in
Q1 2025 and another `$26.8M` in Q4. The filing says the first charge followed a
sustained share-price and market-capitalization decline and a larger-than-
expected decline in contract bookings. The second charge was primarily related
to the stock price at the impairment date.

The impairment is non-cash in the current period, but it is evidence that the
forecasted value of the research business declined. It should not be removed
from the investment conclusion. Review the original acquisition price, the
goodwill remaining after impairment, reporting-unit cash flows, and whether
future restructuring is an attempt to restore the acquired economics.

### Adjusted results exclude recurring transition costs

Forrester's adjusted measures exclude stock compensation, restructuring,
acquisition-related amortization, goodwill impairment, and other items. Some
exclusions are genuinely nonrecurring. But restructuring, product migration,
AI development, and stock compensation can recur because they are the cost of
trying to keep the company relevant. The analyst should show GAAP operating
income, adjusted operating income, operating cash, stock compensation, and
cash per diluted share together.

### Deferred revenue is not currently expanding

Deferred revenue was `$141.8M` at December 31, 2025 versus `$145.4M` at the end
of 2024. A small decline is not proof of fraud, but it is consistent with the
contract-value decline and argues against assuming that current cash benefits
are building a larger future revenue base. Track billings, renewal timing,
contract duration, cancellation terms, accounts receivable, and deferred
commission additions.

### Repurchases can consume scarce cash

Forrester repurchased `$15.9M` of stock in 2024 while operating cash was
negative and repurchased another `$2.5M` in 2025. The company also paid tax on
net share settlements and issued shares under incentive plans. A buyback is
value-creating only if the business retains enough cash for product investment,
restructuring, debt service, and the transition to AI-enabled delivery.

## Balance-sheet resilience and capital requirements

At December 31, 2025, Forrester had `$63.3M` of cash, `$64.3M` of marketable
investments, and `$35.0M` of debt. The balance sheet was not heavily levered,
but current liabilities included `$141.8M` of deferred revenue and `$62.4M` of
accrued expenses and other current liabilities. The company had a `$114.3M`
available borrowing capacity under its facility and was subject to liquidity,
leverage, interest-coverage, and capital-expenditure covenants.

The company expected approximately `$28M` of 2026 capital spending for office
renovation, partly offset by a `$17.2M` landlord allowance. That is a material
cash item relative to 2025 operating cash. It should be separated from
maintenance spending and included in near-term liquidity analysis.

## Lyn Alden lens

Forrester's demand is tied to institutional uncertainty, but its customers can
defer or reduce research budgets when liquidity tightens. The company is less
capital-intensive than a physical infrastructure business, but it is exposed
to labor costs, client budgets, technology spending, and reputation.

The current cycle creates both demand and pressure. More AI and technology
complexity can increase the need for trusted interpretation. At the same time,
AI can lower the cost of producing summaries and increase a buyer's demand for
clear measurable outcomes. Forrester's resilience depends on becoming part of
the client's decision process, not merely remaining a publisher of reports.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative becomes:

`retained research clients × contract value × renewal and expansion + consulting bookings × utilization × rate + event attendance and sponsorship -> revenue -> analyst labor, consulting labor, sales, AI delivery, restructuring, debt, taxes, and owner cash`

The valuation should separately model:

1. research contract value, retention, and wallet expansion;
2. Forrester AI usage, pricing, delivery cost, and renewal impact;
3. consulting bookings, utilization, and project margin;
4. event contribution and cancellation exposure;
5. restructuring and office-renovation cash requirements;
6. goodwill and acquired-customer economics; and
7. stock compensation, repurchases, debt, and diluted cash per share.

At the September 14, 2026 market snapshot, Forrester's equity value was about
`$229M`. That is approximately 12.6 times FY2025 reported recurring-capital
residual, but the multiple is fragile because the residual is only `$18.1M`,
contract value is declining, and the business is undertaking a transition.
The valuation has little margin for another prolonged contract-value decline or
large cash restructuring program.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Management is incentivized to emphasize contract value, client retention,
Forrester AI adoption, adjusted earnings, adjusted margin, and stabilization.
Pair those measures with GAAP results, billings, deferred revenue, bookings,
cash after office investment, goodwill, stock compensation, and diluted shares.

The thesis breaks if:

- contract value remains below the 2025 base despite AI adoption;
- AI usage does not improve retention, pricing, or delivery cost;
- adjusted earnings improve mainly because recurring transition costs are
  excluded;
- goodwill impairment continues or the Research reporting unit requires another
  reset;
- deferred revenue and billings decline with contract value;
- cash is consumed by office, restructuring, or repurchase spending; or
- research clients shift to internal AI and lower-cost providers.

## Next-filing evidence ladder

| Question | Confirming evidence | Falsifying evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is the research franchise stabilizing? | Contract value, renewal, billings, deferred revenue, and cash improve together | Contract value declines while only adjusted margin or AI usage improves |
| Does Forrester AI create value? | Clients use it, renew, expand, and pay for the workflow | Usage rises without pricing, retention, or contribution-margin improvement |
| Are adjusted earnings credible? | GAAP operating loss narrows and cash remains positive after recurring transition costs | Restructuring, SBC, impairment, and product-transition costs recur each year |
| Is the balance sheet adequate? | Cash and investments fund office work, debt, and product investment without distressed borrowing | Liquidity falls while contract value declines and covenants tighten |
| Did prior acquisitions earn their cost? | Research-unit cash flow and retention recover after impairment | Further impairment or customer loss follows the 2025 reset |

## Bottom line

Forrester proves that trusted institutional judgment remains a real need but is
not automatically a durable business. Its control point is valuable only when
research becomes part of client workflow and produces enough decision value to
survive budget review and cheaper AI alternatives.

The current evidence is a transition case, not a clean compounder. Contract
value is declining, goodwill has been impaired, operating cash is thin, and
adjusted results exclude costs that may recur. The thesis improves only when
Forrester AI adoption shows up in renewal, contract value, billings, cash, and
cash per diluted share.
