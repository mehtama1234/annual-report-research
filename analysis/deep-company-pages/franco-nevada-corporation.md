# Franco-Nevada Corporation — royalty contracts, commodity exposure, and acquisition discipline

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed IFRS figures in U.S. dollars;
dollar amounts are in millions.

## How to read this memo

A royalty is a contractual right to receive a percentage of production or
revenue from an asset without operating the mine or well. A stream is a right
to buy a specified amount of future metal at a contract price. GEO means gold
equivalent ounces, a conversion used to compare different metals. Owner cash
here means operating cash flow after productive-asset spending; it is a screen,
not a complete valuation measure.

## Investment question

Franco-Nevada receives royalty and stream payments from mines, oil and gas
assets, and other natural-resource projects. It does not carry the operator’s
mine labor, energy bill, processing plant, or sustaining capital in the same
way as a mine owner. The question is whether this cleaner cash structure can
compound through disciplined acquisitions while surviving commodity prices,
operator failures, permitting delays, asset concentration, and the risk of
paying too much for future production.

The residual was approximately US$991M in 2023, US$829M in 2024, and US$1.492B

earnings move sharply, but cash must still be reinvested through acquisitions to
maintain growth.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Franco-Nevada sold 519K GEOs in 2025, with 85% of revenue from precious
  metals. The model is diversified, but gold remains the main economic driver.
- The operator funds the mine or well. Franco-Nevada therefore avoids much of
  the direct labor, energy, processing, and sustaining-capital burden carried
  by Freeport, Newmont, or other mine owners. Its main recurring capital burden
  is acquiring new royalties and streams.
- GEO margin rose to $3,110 in 2025 from $2,109 in 2024 while cash cost per GEO
  was only $325. The margin is wide, but it is sensitive to gold prices and the
  mix of assets that deliver during the year.
- The company ended 2025 debt-free with about $3.1B of available capital. This
  creates strategic flexibility, not automatic value. The next deal can add
  future production or destroy returns if the price paid is too high.
- Cobre Panama, operator stockpiles, and other development assets demonstrate
  counterparty and permitting dependence. A royalty can have excellent terms
  and still generate no cash while an operator is unable to produce or sell.
- Q1 2026 showed the same structure at a higher scale: revenue was $650.7M,
  operating cash flow was $520.4M, and available capital was $3.4B. Oil, gas,
  and NGL revenue added diversification, but precious metals remained the
  dominant source of revenue.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Is the royalty cash recurring?

Reconcile profit to operating cash flow and separate commodity-price changes,
asset sales, settlement receipts, tax payments, receivables, and operator timing.
Check whether a large year comes from a durable royalty stream or from a
temporary high-price period.

### 2. Does contract coverage protect returns or transfer counterparty risk?

Read royalty percentages, stream purchase prices, inflation provisions, payment
priority, operator reporting, audit rights, expansion rights, and termination
terms. The contract may protect Franco-Nevada from operating inflation while
leaving it exposed to the operator’s solvency and production decisions.

### 3. Are acquisitions earning their cost?

For each royalty or stream acquisition, track cash paid, expected GEOs, actual
GEOs, start date, operator capital, reserve life, write-downs, and return on
invested capital. A large available-capital balance can encourage growth that
looks strategically attractive but earns less than the company’s cost of capital.

### 4. How much diversification is real?

Rebuild revenue and cash by gold, silver, copper-linked streams, oil, gas, NGL,
iron ore, and other assets. Test whether diversification changes cash behavior
or merely adds more operator and commodity exposures to the same portfolio.

### 5. Does the balance sheet remain resilient under a delayed cycle?

Test cash, available credit, acquisition commitments, dividends, operator
failures, litigation, taxes, and political restrictions under lower gold and oil
prices or delayed projects. Debt-free status is useful, but the company can
still lose capital through a poorly priced acquisition.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** royalty companies can be less exposed to labor, energy,
  and equipment inflation because operators carry those costs. They remain
  exposed to commodity cycles, financing conditions, permitting, and the supply
  of attractive new deals.
- **Damodaran lens:** value existing royalties and streams from current assets,
  development-stage assets, optionality, and undeployed capital separately. Use
  mid-cycle commodity prices and probability-weighted production, not only
  current GEO margins.
- **Forensic lens:** the main category errors are treating GEO growth as organic
  growth, available capital as earned value, operator reserves as guaranteed
  production, adjusted EBITDA as owner cash, and a low direct capex burden as
  proof that acquisition returns are high.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately US$51.28B of equity value at
US$266.00 per share. That is roughly 34x the FY2025 screening residual of
US$1.492B. A normalized range of US$0.9B / US$1.5B / US$2.2B at 25x / 30x /
35x implies US$22.5B / US$45.0B / US$77.0B. The current value requires high
gold prices or a durable normalized cash base, continued operator production,
and successful reinvestment of the available acquisition capital.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Gold or oil prices fall toward mid-cycle levels and cash declines more than
   the valuation assumes.
2. Major operators experience production, permitting, solvency, or political
   problems that interrupt royalty and stream payments.
3. New acquisitions produce less GEOs, start later, or require more capital
   than underwriting assumed.
4. Cobre Panama or other development-stage optionality remains unavailable while
   the market continues to assign it value.
5. Diversification fails to reduce concentration because precious metals remain
   the dominant cash source.
6. The company uses its debt-free balance sheet for acquisitions with returns
   below the cost of capital.
