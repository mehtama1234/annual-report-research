# Franklin Resources: public-market asset management, product migration, and fee pressure

## What the company does

Franklin Resources, expected to change its corporate name to Franklin Templeton
on August 17, 2026, manages mutual funds, institutional accounts, SMAs, ETFs,
multi-asset products, alternatives, private markets, and cash-management
products. It earns investment-management, distribution, and related fees on
client assets while paying investment professionals, distributors, technology,
operations, and compliance costs.

The economic asset is not the client's AUM. It is the retained fee relationship:
the product, performance, distribution access, brand, advice, and channel that
keep assets invested. The central test is whether Franklin's newer products can
replace legacy active-fund outflows and fee compression without making the
company more dependent on acquisitions, market appreciation, or costly
distribution.

## FY2025 and Q3 FY2026 baseline

Franklin's fiscal year ends September 30. The latest three packet quarters are
Q3 FY2026, Q2 FY2026, and Q1 FY2026.

FY2025 ended with AUM approximately `$1.661T`, long-term net outflows of
approximately `$97.4B`, operating revenue approximately `$8.771B`, and an
effective investment-management fee rate of `40.5` basis points versus `41.1`
in FY2024. Franklin paid approximately `$683.7M` in dividends and repurchased
approximately `$240.3M` of stock.

The later quarter chain shows a possible improvement. Q1 FY2026 had AUM
approximately `$1.684T`, net long-term inflows `$28.0B`, revenue `$2.327B`,
adjusted operating margin `not separately normalized in the packet`, adjusted
net income `$378.4M`, and adjusted EPS `$0.70`. Q2 had AUM `$1.682T`, inflows
`$16.9B`, revenue `$2.295B`, adjusted operating margin `not separately stated
here`, adjusted net income `$384.5M`, and EPS `$0.71`. Q3 had AUM `$1.792T`,
inflows `$18.4B`, revenue `$2.358B`, adjusted net income `$386.3M`, and EPS
`$0.72`.

This is not yet a proven turnaround. The analyst must establish whether the
rebound is broad, fee-paying, and durable, or whether it reflects market levels,
product mix, temporary channel demand, or one unusually strong alternatives or
ETF period.

## Where the economics sit

### Legacy active funds

Franklin's legacy active products carry investment talent, distribution,
research, and brand costs. Their economics deteriorate if performance weakens,
investor preferences shift to passive products, intermediaries demand lower fees,
or outflows force operating deleverage.

Track net flows, performance relative to benchmark, fee rate, redemption,
distribution payments, compensation, product closures, and fixed operating cost.
Revenue can remain high for a period while the economic franchise weakens if
market appreciation offsets outflows.

### ETFs, index products, and SMAs

ETFs, index products, and SMAs are Franklin's product-migration response. They
can attract flows and improve distribution relevance, but they usually face
lower fees and strong competition. Canvas and other channels may improve
advisory or model-portfolio attachment.

Separate AUM, flows, fee rates, gross margin, distribution economics, tracking
quality, technology, and client retention by wrapper. A record AUM number in a
low-fee product may produce less profit than a smaller high-fee product, while
the high-fee product may be losing its long-term position.

### Alternatives and private markets

Alternatives AUM reached approximately `$294.2B` in Q3 FY2026, and Q2
alternatives fundraising was approximately `$14.3B`. This can improve fee mix and
growth, but the economics differ from public funds. Private products have longer
lockups, valuation marks, performance income, fundraising cost, distribution
complexity, and potential liquidity or suitability risk.

Track fee-paying alternatives AUM, fee rate, net flows, commitments, deployment,
realizations, marks, incentive income, compensation, and client liquidity terms.
Do not apply alternatives multiples to Franklin's whole AUM or assume private
capital makes the legacy platform durable.

### Western Asset and internal quality dispersion

The packet identifies Western Asset as a continuing drag while the broader
platform improves. This matters because consolidated Franklin results can hide
very different economics inside its businesses.

Break out Western Asset and other major units by AUM, flows, fee rate, revenue,
performance, personnel, and restructuring or remediation costs. A group-level
flow recovery is less valuable if one large platform continues to lose clients
and requires subsidy or management attention.

### International and cash management

Franklin's international, cash-management, and distribution channels add
diversification and client utility. They also create currency, local regulation,
counterparty, liquidity, and operational burdens. Track owned versus joint-
venture economics and the cash actually available to the parent.

## Earnings quality and forensic review

### AUM, flows, fee rate, and revenue

The core bridge is:

`average fee-paying AUM × fee rate + distribution and performance income - investment talent - distribution - technology - operations - compliance - tax = common-owner cash`

Reconcile FY2025 revenue of approximately `$8.771B` and the quarterly revenue
sequence to average AUM, market movement, net flows, fee rates, product mix,
performance income, distribution payments, compensation, and technology.

The effective fee rate fell from `41.1` to `40.5` basis points in FY2025. That
decline is small in percentage terms but large across trillions of dollars. Test
whether new products stabilize revenue per asset or merely grow low-fee AUM.

### Flow rebound quality

Q1-Q3 FY2026 long-term inflows were approximately `$28.0B`, `$16.9B`, and
`$18.4B`. This is strong evidence of current demand after FY2025 outflows. It
must be separated into ETFs, Canvas, alternatives, fixed income, international,
and products that are losing assets.

Track gross flows, net flows, market appreciation, average AUM, fee-paying AUM,
redemptions, intermediary concentration, and fee rate. A turnaround is stronger
when it persists across products and clients, not when one channel offsets
continued attrition elsewhere.

### Adjusted earnings and recurring costs

Franklin reports adjusted net income alongside GAAP operating and net income.
Reconcile compensation, restructuring, acquisition and integration costs,
impairments, legal or remediation expense, stock compensation, taxes, diluted
shares, and operating cash flow.

If Franklin must keep spending on acquisitions, product migration, technology,
or remediation to defend the platform, those costs belong in normalized owner
cash even when management labels them as adjustment items.

### Capital returns and acquisitions

FY2025 dividends were approximately `$683.7M` and repurchases approximately
`$240.3M`. Capital returns can be attractive for a capital-light manager, but
they should be compared with product investment, distribution, technology,
acquisition spending, debt, and per-share cash conversion.

The name change and product repositioning do not themselves create value. Test
whether management is allocating capital toward fee-paying growth at returns
above the cost of capital or using buybacks to support EPS while the legacy
economic franchise contracts.

## Balance-sheet resilience and macro conditions

The Lyn Alden lens focuses on market levels, savings, rates, credit, liquidity,
and investor risk appetite. Franklin benefits from rising financial assets,
retirement savings, ETF adoption, alternatives demand, and wealth distribution.
It is exposed to market declines, outflows, fee compression, active-performance
weakness, private-market marks, China and international restrictions, and fixed
technology and talent cost.

Stress cases should include:

- global equity and bond-market declines;
- active-fund outflows accelerating;
- alternatives fundraising and realizations slowing;
- ETF and SMA fee compression;
- Western Asset deterioration continuing;
- private-market marks and liquidity terms weakening;
- international currency or regulatory restrictions;
- distribution and compensation costs remaining fixed while AUM falls;
- acquisitions or buybacks consuming cash while flows deteriorate.

## Damodaran lens: translate the product-migration narrative

The narrative is that Franklin can use its global brand and distribution to move
from legacy active funds toward ETFs, SMAs, alternatives, private markets,
multi-asset, and cash management. That narrative requires:

1. Net inflows remain positive after separating market appreciation and temporary
   product demand.
2. New products produce durable fee-paying AUM at acceptable fee rates.
3. Alternatives add recurring fee and performance economics after talent,
   distribution, valuation, and liquidity costs.
4. Western Asset and other weak units stop consuming group-level economics.
5. Fee compression is slower than productivity and scale improvements.
6. Capital returns do not starve product, technology, or distribution investment.
7. The flow recovery persists through a market and investor-risk cycle.

Value Franklin from normalized fee-paying AUM, net flows, fee rate, product mix,
operating margin, talent and distribution cost, acquisitions, tax, cash flow,
and share count. Apply different durability to ETF fees, legacy active fees,
alternatives, performance revenue, and international or joint-venture earnings.

## Valuation screen

A market snapshot at 23:15 UTC on 2026-09-14 showed an equity value of
approximately `$17.005B`. These are normalized net-income sensitivities, not a
price target.

| Case | Normalized net income | Illustrative multiple | Implied equity value | What must be true |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Downside | `$0.8B` | `8x` | `$6.4B` | Flows reverse, fee rates compress, and legacy costs remain fixed |
| Base | `$1.3B` | `11x` | `$14.3B` | Product migration stabilizes flows and margins without major dilution |
| Upside | `$1.8B` | `14x` | `$25.2B` | Alternatives, ETFs, SMAs, and international channels compound fee-paying cash |

The screen must be checked against AUM quality, fee rate, flow breadth, Western
Asset performance, alternatives marks, acquisitions, debt, and capital returns.
The current market value is above the illustrative base case and below the
upside case. That comparison is not a margin-of-safety conclusion: the base
requires the flow recovery and product migration to persist while Western Asset,
fee compression, alternatives costs, acquisitions, and recurring technology and
talent spending remain controlled.

## Thesis breakers and filing tests

| Question | Evidence supporting the thesis | Evidence weakening it |
|---|---|---|
| Is the flow recovery broad? | Positive flows across ETFs, Canvas, fixed income, alternatives, and multiple channels | QQQ-like concentration in one product while active or Western Asset outflows persist |
| Are fees durable? | Fee rate stabilizes as fee-paying AUM grows | New AUM is low-fee, distribution-heavy, or offsets continued pricing pressure |
| Are alternatives additive? | Fee-paying AUM and net fees grow after talent and liquidity costs | Marks or fundraising obscure weak realizations and cash conversion |
| Is Western Asset contained? | Flows, performance, and cost stabilize | Continued outflows or remediation require group subsidy |
| Is adjusted profit reliable? | GAAP, adjusted earnings, cash, and recurring cost reconcile | Repeated restructuring, impairment, or integration costs are excluded |
| Are returns disciplined? | Dividends and buybacks follow product investment and intrinsic value | Capital returns support EPS while the economic franchise contracts |

## Bottom line

Franklin controls a global investment-product and distribution platform, but its
economic asset is fee-paying client retention rather than headline AUM. FY2025
showed approximately `$97.4B` of long-term net outflows and fee-rate decline.
Q1-Q3 FY2026 showed positive flows, rising AUM, and improving adjusted results.

The important unanswered question is whether this is a durable product
repositioning or a temporary recovery in market and channel conditions. The next
filings must show product-level flows, fee rates, average fee-paying AUM,
performance, alternatives cash economics, Western Asset repair, recurring
technology and talent cost, and cash per diluted share.

**Evidence grade:** Strong for FY2025 and Q1-Q3 FY2026 reported AUM, flows,
revenue, adjusted earnings, fee rate, alternatives AUM, capital returns, and
impairment or strategy disclosures; medium for product-migration durability and
segment economics; unresolved for through-cycle flows, fee compression,
alternatives cash conversion, and Western Asset repair.
