# Freeport-McMoRan Inc. — copper scarcity, mine disruption, and the cost of replacing reserves

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed GAAP figures unless explicitly identified otherwise; dollar amounts are in millions.

## How to read this memo

Operating cash flow means cash generated by normal operations. Capital
expenditure means cash spent on mines, processing facilities, and other
productive assets. A byproduct is a metal produced alongside the main product;
Freeport’s gold and molybdenum reduce reported copper costs but also make the
cash result depend on three commodity prices. Asset-retirement obligations are
estimated future costs to close, reclaim, and remediate mining assets.

## Investment question

Freeport controls long-life copper resources, mining infrastructure, processing
capacity, and operating rights in several jurisdictions. Copper demand from
power networks, electrification, industrial equipment, and digital infrastructure
may grow for years. The question is whether that demand becomes durable common-
shareholder cash after ore-grade decline, mine disruption, sustaining and growth
capital, environmental obligations, operating-rights risk, and commodity-price
cycles.

## What the filing and IR record reveal

- Freeport’s valuable asset is not merely a copper reserve. It is the combined
  resource, mine plan, processing system, permits, operating relationships,
  technical workforce, and infrastructure needed to turn ore into saleable
  metal.
- The September 2025 mud-rush incident at Grasberg shows the difference between
  geological ownership and reliable production. Q4 production and cash fell,
  while 2026 results depend on a phased restart and ramp-up of the Grasberg
  Block Cave mine.
- FY2025 capital spending included about $2.3B for major mining projects and
  $0.6B for downstream processing facilities. This is not only maintenance; the
  company is spending to preserve, expand, and sequence future production.
- The filing records about $2.0B of environmental obligations and $3.8B of
  asset-retirement obligations. These obligations reduce the amount of future
  mining cash that can be treated as freely distributable.
- Indonesia’s export-proceeds rules required 100% of certain export proceeds to
  remain deposited in Indonesian banks for 12 months, although permitted uses
  included business needs, taxes, dividends, capital expenditures, and debt
  repayment. Cash location and legal access therefore matter alongside the
  consolidated cash balance.
- Management’s Arizona leach and technology projects, Chilean El Abra options,
  and Grasberg development are future production choices, not current output.
  Each requires permits, capital, construction, and acceptable recovery rates.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Is the copper margin a price effect or an operating improvement?

The company reports unit net cash cost of about $1.65 per pound for 2025, but
that measure benefits from gold and molybdenum byproducts and changes with
production volumes. Recalculate cost using conservative byproduct prices and
include sustaining capital, environmental spending, taxes, royalties, and
minority interests.

### 2. Does the Grasberg restart restore cash or merely production?

Track planned versus actual tonnes, recovery, grades, availability, unit costs,
insurance proceeds, restart capital, and shipment timing. A mine can report
production growth while cash remains weak if capital, inventories, or processing
constraints rise faster than sales.

### 3. Is growth capital earning an acceptable return?

Separate sustaining capital from major projects such as underground development,
Kucing Liar, Arizona leach, and downstream processing. For each project, compare
capital invested with incremental pounds, recovery, cost per pound, start date,
taxes, royalties, and operating-rights duration.

### 4. Are reserve and rights assumptions reliable?

The mine plan assumes continued operating rights and future production through
2041 in Indonesia. Test the legal extension, government participation, export
rules, permitting, community obligations, and the possibility that reported
reserves require higher capital or lower recovery than expected.

### 5. Are dividends truly surplus cash?

The $865M dividend was close to the $1.116B residual before other obligations.
Test dividends against environmental spending, reclamation, debt reduction,
working capital, minority interests, and the next mine-development cycle. A
high copper price can support returns for a period without proving a full-cycle
dividend capacity.

## Incentives, operating rights, and cohort position

Freeport's copper-scarcity narrative can encourage investors to capitalize
future pounds before the mine, processing system, and operating rights have
earned them. Management's capital-return decisions should therefore be tested
against cash per diluted share after Grasberg restart spending, sustaining and
growth capital, environmental obligations, debt, minority interests, and the
next reserve-replacement cycle.

The byproduct cost presentation also requires discipline. Gold and molybdenum
credits can make copper unit costs look unusually low when their prices are
strong. That is economically relevant, but it means the company is exposed to
three commodity prices and not to copper alone. A return target that relies on
all three favorable prices is less durable than a copper return that survives
conservative byproduct assumptions.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** copper is a scarce physical input, and replacement costs
  rise with inflation, permitting delays, energy costs, and geopolitical risk.
  The same physical scarcity does not protect a highly capital-intensive miner
  from price cycles, disruption, or funding stress.
- **Damodaran lens:** value existing producing assets, reserve replacement,
  byproduct economics, development projects, and operating-rights options
  separately. Use mid-cycle prices and full sustaining capital rather than a
  peak-price earnings multiple.
- **Forensic lens:** the main category errors are treating copper demand as
  current cash, unit net cash cost as full cost, reserves as production,
  adjusted earnings as cash, and future projects as though their returns were
  already earned.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $102.55B of equity value at
$71.07 per share. That is roughly 92x the $1.116B FY2025 residual after
productive-asset spending. A normalized range of $1.5B / $3.0B / $5.0B at 16x /
20x / 24x implies $24B / $60B / $120B. The market therefore requires a much
 larger normalized cash base than FY2025 alone provides, supported by copper
 prices, Grasberg recovery, project returns, and long-lived operating rights.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Grasberg production or recovery remains below plan, or restart capital rises.
2. Copper prices fall toward mid-cycle levels while growth and environmental
   spending remain high.
3. Ore grades, recoveries, energy costs, or processing constraints raise full
   costs faster than realized prices.
4. Indonesia operating rights, export rules, permits, or community obligations
   restrict production or cash movement.
5. Kucing Liar, El Abra, Arizona, or downstream projects require more capital or
   take longer to earn acceptable returns.
6. Reclamation and environmental liabilities reduce cash available to common
   shareholders.
