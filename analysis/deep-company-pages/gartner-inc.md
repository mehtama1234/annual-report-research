# Gartner, Inc. — institutional decision support, contract value, and owner cash

Date baseline: 2026-09-14  
Primary evidence: [Gartner company packet](../../extracted/technology/research-services/gartner-inc/company-packet.md), [Gartner FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/749251/000074925126000112/it-20251231.htm), and [Gartner source ledger](../../extracted/technology/research-services/gartner-inc/source-ledger.md)

## Investment question

Gartner sells research, advisory access, conferences, and consulting to
organizations making technology and business decisions. The durable part of
the model is the institutional relationship: a client pays for information,
interpretation, benchmarks, and access that help executives choose and defend
a course of action.

The investment question is whether Gartner can keep that relationship valuable
as generic information and artificial-intelligence tools become cheaper. The
financial question is whether contract value, reported earnings, and free cash
flow remain durable after stock compensation, conference delivery, consulting
labor, deferred-revenue timing, debt, and large share repurchases.

The evidence supports a strong institutional position. It also shows a mature
business where a 1% increase in contract value is not enough by itself to
justify assuming a long period of high growth.

## What Gartner controls

The operating sequence is:

`institutional uncertainty -> research, benchmark, advisory, or event engagement -> executive decision -> consulting or implementation -> renewal and broader wallet -> cash`

Gartner controls a trusted interpretation and access layer. Clients retain
their own decision rights and implementation labor. Competitors include
internal strategy and technology teams, consultants, software vendors, AI
tools, analysts, industry communities, and specialized research providers.

The business has three different economic products:

- **Insights** provides recurring research and advisory access. It produced
  about `$5.1B` of 2025 revenue and has the strongest subscription-like
  characteristics.
- **Conferences** produced about `$644.7M` and depends on attendance,
  exhibitors, travel budgets, venue execution, and the value of in-person
  access.
- **Consulting** produced about `$552.5M` and depends on utilization, staffing,
  backlog, project scope, and labor cost.

Treating all three dollars as one recurring software stream would overstate
the stability and margin quality of the business.

## Definitions

- **Contract value** is management's measure of annualized contract revenue and
  related committed spending. It is a forward indicator, not recognized
  revenue or cash collected.
- **Client retention** measures whether clients continue buying. It does not
  show price, product mix, expansion, or the margin of retained work.
- **Wallet retention** measures spending from an existing client base after
  expansion and contraction. It should be reconciled to contract value,
  revenue, and cash.
- **Deferred revenue** is cash or billing recognized as revenue over the
  service period. It can support cash flow, but a decline in deferred revenue
  can reduce current-period operating cash without proving the franchise is
  weaker.
- **Owner cash** here means operating cash after property and leasehold
  investment, before share repurchases and discretionary capital allocation.
  Repurchases are a use of owner cash, not a substitute for measuring it.

FY2025 Insights client retention was 85%, up from 84% in 2024. That is useful
evidence of embedment, but not proof of pricing power. The company also
disclosed that less than half of its 2024 U.S. federal Insights contract value
was retained in 2025. Government exposure is therefore a specific retention
and budget risk rather than a minor detail.

## Three-year cash bridge

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Property, equipment, and leasehold additions | Recurring-capital residual | Important adjustment or timing issue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| FY2023 | `$1,155.7M` | `$103.1M` | `$1,052.6M` | Included `$161.1M` proceeds from sale of a divested operation in investing cash, not recurring FCF |
| FY2024 | `$1,484.9M` | `$101.7M` | `$1,383.2M` | Included `$300.0M` insurance proceeds in operating cash |
| FY2025 | `$1,290.4M` | `$115.1M` | `$1,175.2M` | Cash declined as the insurance benefit disappeared; fees receivable and deferred commissions helped working capital |

The residual is strong, but the three-year series is not a straight-line
growth story. FY2024 benefited from a `$300M` insurance settlement. In FY2025,
fees receivable contributed `$38.0M` to operating cash and deferred commissions
contributed `$25.3M`, while deferred revenues reduced operating cash by `$41.9M`.
The next analysis should normalize those movements instead of simply using the
highest historical cash number.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering risks

### Contract value is not revenue

Contract value grew only 1% FX-neutral in FY2025 while revenue grew more
quickly. That can happen through timing, conference recovery, consulting mix,
price, or prior commitments. It requires a bridge from contract value to
recognized Insights revenue, retention, wallet expansion, billings, deferred
revenue, and cash collection.

### Stock compensation is material

Gartner recorded `$155.9M` of stock-based compensation in 2025, versus
`$154.8M` in 2024 and `$129.8M` in 2023. It also had `$155.5M` of unrecognized
future stock-compensation cost at year-end. The company repurchased 7.0M
shares for about `$2.0B`, but repurchases do not automatically offset the
economic cost of equity awards. Measure cash per diluted share and total shares
after awards, exercises, and repurchases.

### Working-capital cash is part of the business but not all of the thesis

Subscription billing can produce favorable cash timing. That is a real benefit,
not an accounting error. But FY2025 deferred revenue fell as a cash-flow
source, while fees receivable and deferred commissions provided cash. Review
billings, collection days, contract duration, commission additions, commission
amortization, renewal, and cancellation together.

### Conference and consulting revenue carry different burdens

Conferences have venue, cancellation, attendance, exhibitor, travel, and
reputation risks. Consulting has utilization, staffing, project-margin, and
client-budget risks. A stable consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin can conceal
mix changes between a high-margin Insights contract and more labor-intensive
consulting work.

### Divestiture changes the comparison base

Gartner completed the sale of Digital Markets for approximately `$110M` in
February 2026. Future revenue and adjusted EBITDA comparisons need continuing-
operations denominators. A reported improvement after a divestiture can be
partly portfolio cleanup rather than organic improvement.

## Balance-sheet resilience and capital requirements

At December 31, 2025, Gartner had `$1.7B` of cash and about `$3.0B` of total
debt principal. None of the debt was floating-rate. Debt is manageable relative
to cash generation, but it remains a claim that competes with repurchases,
product investment, acquisitions, and resilience during a downturn.

The company had about 57% of cash overseas. Currency translation can therefore
affect the reported cash balance and the flexibility of returning cash to U.S.
shareholders. The business also carries large deferred commission balances and
lease obligations. These are normal parts of the model, but they should be
included when comparing Gartner with asset-light software companies.

## Lyn Alden lens

Gartner's demand is tied to institutional liquidity and uncertainty. When
technology changes quickly, regulation shifts, or executives must justify
large purchases, external research can remain a priority. But research budgets
are still discretionary within corporate and government budgets. A recession,
public-sector funding cut, or procurement delay can reduce new commitments even
if the long-term need for information remains.

The company has a favorable cash profile because recurring Insights revenue
requires limited physical capital. It has a more cyclical layer because
conferences and consulting depend on travel, labor utilization, client project
approval, and event participation. The macro test is whether Gartner's advice
helps clients reduce uncertainty enough to preserve spending during a liquidity
contraction.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative becomes:

`institutional decision points × retained clients × wallet share × price -> Insights revenue; attendance and exhibitors -> conference revenue; billable people × utilization × rate -> consulting revenue -> delivery cost, sales, research, stock compensation, debt, taxes, and owner cash`

The valuation should separately model:

1. Insights retention, wallet retention, price, and contract-value growth;
2. the risk that AI tools reduce the price of generic research;
3. conference attendance, exhibitor demand, cancellation exposure, and margin;
4. consulting backlog, utilization, labor cost, and project margin;
5. government contract retention and budget sensitivity;
6. stock compensation, diluted shares, and repurchase prices; and
7. cash conversion after working-capital normalization and debt service.

At the September 14, 2026 market snapshot, Gartner's equity value was about
`$12.0B`. That is approximately 10 times FY2025 recurring-capital residual.
The valuation does not require extreme growth, but it does require the cash
stream to remain durable after the FY2024 insurance benefit disappears and
after stock compensation and contract-value stagnation are recognized.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Management is incentivized to emphasize contract value, adjusted revenue,
adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EPS, free cash flow, retention, and share repurchases.
Pair each with GAAP operating income, stock compensation, contract-value
growth, billings, consulting utilization, conference margins, diluted shares,
and government retention.

The thesis breaks if:

- contract value remains near flat while revenue growth depends on conferences,
  acquisitions, or timing;
- AI tools reduce renewal, pricing, or wallet retention for Insights;
- client retention remains high but wallet retention and cash collections weaken;
- conference or consulting recovery hides deterioration in the recurring Insights
  franchise;
- stock compensation remains near `$150M` annually while cash per diluted share
  does not grow;
- repurchases are funded by debt or occur at prices that destroy value;
- government budget cuts produce another large retention loss; or
- deferred commissions and receivables support cash flow without corresponding
  renewal and billings evidence.

## Next-filing evidence ladder

| Question | Confirming evidence | Falsifying evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is the core relationship durable? | Contract value, wallet retention, renewal, and Insights margin improve together | Contract value is flat and growth comes from temporary event or consulting mix |
| Does AI increase or reduce value? | Clients renew, expand, and pay for higher-value research and advisory workflows | Research is unbundled, discounted, or replaced by internal AI without wallet growth |
| Is cash repeatable? | Operating cash remains strong after insurance, receivable, commission, and deferred-revenue normalization | Cash depends on timing benefits or new billings while renewal weakens |
| Are repurchases creating value? | Diluted shares and cash per share improve after SBC and buybacks | Buybacks offset issuance temporarily or are financed with additional debt |
| Is the non-Insights business healthy? | Conference attendance and consulting utilization support contribution margin | Event cancellations, labor cost, or project mix reduce margin and cash |
| Is public-sector exposure controlled? | Federal contract value and retention recover or remain diversified | Government cancellations continue and are not offset by private-sector expansion |

## Bottom line

Gartner owns a credible institutional decision-support relationship. Its best
economics come from Insights: recurring client access, high contribution
margin, limited physical capital, and a workflow tied to executive decisions.
Conferences and consulting add reach and monetization, but they carry more
cyclical and labor-related risk.

The main forensic issue is maturity, not fraud. A 1% contract-value increase,
material recurring stock compensation, large repurchases, working-capital
timing, and government-retention weakness make it necessary to distinguish
reported free cash flow from durable cash per diluted share. The thesis is
strongest if client wallet retention, AI-era pricing, normalized cash, and
share count improve together.
