# Glencore — mining plus marketing, inventory financing, and copper growth

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are reported in U.S. dollars; dollar amounts
are in millions unless otherwise stated.

## How to read this memo

Glencore has two linked businesses. Industrial operations mine, process, and
refine commodities. Marketing operations buy, sell, transport, finance, and
manage risk around commodities. Adjusted EBITDA, Marketing Adjusted EBIT, and
Funds from Operations are management measures. They are not the same as cash
available to common owners because working capital, readily marketable
inventories, trading leases, debt, capex, partner claims, and shareholder
returns can absorb cash.

## Investment question

Glencore's possible advantage is the combination of physical assets, logistics,
market knowledge, customer relationships, and the ability to profit when energy,
freight, and metals markets become dislocated. The central question is whether
the marketing arm produces durable through-cycle returns or whether recent
profits mainly reflect favorable volatility, inventory prices, and working-
capital timing while the industrial assets require heavy renewal capital.

The three-year cash record is uneven. Funds from operations were $9.452B in
2023, $10.529B in 2024, and $8.714B in 2025. Net capex was approximately
$5.6B, $6.7B, and $6.9B, leaving FFO-less-capex screens of roughly $3.852B,
$3.829B, and $1.814B. In 2025, a $1.6B release from non-RMI working capital and
$1.0B of net investment inflows, mainly the Viterra disposal, helped keep net
debt flat.

## What the annual and current-period record reveals

- Industrial operations generated $9.9B of adjusted EBITDA in 2025, while
  marketing generated $2.9B of adjusted EBIT. These are different economic
  engines and should not be valued at the same multiple.
- Copper-led growth is a stated strategic objective. Glencore expects more than
  one million tonnes of annualized copper production by the end of 2028 and
  about 1.6 million tonnes by 2035. The returns depend on capital, ownership,
  grades, project timing, and jurisdictional obligations.
- The Viterra disposal simplified the portfolio and supplied about $940M of
  cash. An asset sale can improve leverage, but it is not repeatable operating
  cash and may remove future earnings.
- Net funding reached $39.405B, including readily marketable inventories that
  rose with metals prices. This is the balance-sheet price of the marketing
  model: inventory and prepayment activity can generate profits, but they also
  require financing and can lose value or cash when prices, counterparties, or
  delivery terms change.
- Cobalt output fell 46% in H1 2026 as DRC quotas led Glencore to prioritize
  copper and hold some cobalt for later processing and sale. Production numbers
  therefore reflect policy, inventory, and marketing decisions, not only mine
  geology.
- H1 2026 showed strong conditions: Group Adjusted EBITDA rose to $10.1B,
  Marketing Adjusted EBIT to $3.3B, and net debt fell to $10.2B. This is useful
  evidence of operating leverage, but it is also a test of how much marketing
  profit depends on market dislocation remaining unusually favorable.
- Net income attributable to equity holders was only $363M in FY2025, versus
  $2.3B before significant items. The difference requires a full reconciliation
  of impairments, provisions, financing, tax, and other significant items before
  adjusted EBITDA is used to infer owner earnings.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Is marketing profit durable through the cycle?

Separate volume, price, spreads, freight, financing, counterparty losses,
inventory revaluation, derivatives, and realized cash. Compare Marketing
Adjusted EBIT with the $2.3B-$3.5B stated through-cycle range and test whether
the range includes enough working-capital and lease cost.

### 2. Does net funding hide the capital intensity?

Track readily marketable inventory, non-RMI inventory, receivables, payables,
prepayments, commodity loans, collateral, and marketing leases. Net debt can
remain flat while the company takes on a much larger inventory-financing need.

### 3. Are working-capital inflows repeatable?

The 2025 $1.6B non-RMI working-capital release and $1.0B investment inflow
helped fund capex and returns. Identify the underlying inventory and receivable
movements and test whether they reverse when prices or volumes normalize.

### 4. Does copper growth earn its capital?

For each project, record ownership, original and revised capital, production,
grade, recovery, unit cost, start date, ramp, taxes, closure obligations, and
marketing economics. A target of 1.6 million tonnes is a production plan, not a
cash-return conclusion.

### 5. Are significant items economically recurring?

Reconcile the $363M reported net income attributable to equity holders with the
$2.3B pre-significant-items figure. Impairments, legal matters, restructuring,
provisions, and portfolio changes may be individually unusual but still part of
owning and operating this business.

### 6. Are distributions covered after the full system burden?

Compare $3.466B of distributions and buybacks with FFO, net capex, working-
capital investment, inventory financing, debt maturities, and partner claims.
The marketing business cannot be treated as asset-light if it needs substantial
funding to carry and move commodities.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** Glencore benefits from energy security, supply-chain
  dislocation, strategic metals, and inflation-sensitive physical assets. It is
  also exposed to credit liquidity, inventory prices, trade restrictions, DRC
  policy, freight, and counterparty stress.
- **Damodaran lens:** value industrial assets, marketing earnings, inventory
  funding, and copper projects separately. Use a through-cycle Marketing EBIT,
  mid-cycle commodity prices, full capital and working-capital requirements,
  taxes, debt, and probability-weighted project returns.
- **Forensic lens:** the key risks are treating FFO as owner cash, treating a
  working-capital release or disposal as recurring, treating marketing EBIT as
  fee-like income without financing risk, and treating copper production targets
  as value creation before capital returns are proven.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-10 ADR snapshot was approximately $95.22B of equity value at
$16.11 per ADR. Against the $1.814B FFO-less-net-capex screen, the value is
about 52.5x. A normalized owner-cash range of $2.5B / $4.5B / $7.0B at 15x /
18x / 22x implies $37.5B / $81B / $154B. The current value requires marketing
earnings to remain strong, working-capital needs to stay controlled, and the
copper-growth program to create returns above its capital burden.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Marketing Adjusted EBIT falls below the through-cycle range when freight and
   commodity dislocations normalize.
2. Net funding or readily marketable inventories rise faster than profits and
   cash, increasing financing and mark-to-market risk.
3. Copper growth requires higher capital, produces lower grades, or misses its
   schedule.
4. Working-capital releases, asset sales, or prepayments continue to support
   distributions without recurring operating cash.
5. Significant items, impairments, legal provisions, or partner claims recur.
6. Net debt rises while shareholder returns remain above post-capex cash.
