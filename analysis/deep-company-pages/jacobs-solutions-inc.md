# Jacobs Solutions Inc. — complex infrastructure delivery, PA acquisition, and backlog-to-cash quality

Date baseline: 2026-09-14  
Primary evidence: [Jacobs company packet](../../extracted/industrial-goods/engineering-construction/jacobs-solutions-inc/company-packet.md), [Jacobs FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52988/000162828025053316/jec-20250926.htm), [Jacobs Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52988/000162828026052573/jec-20260626.htm), and [Jacobs source ledger](../../extracted/industrial-goods/engineering-construction/jacobs-solutions-inc/source-ledger.md)

## Investment question

Jacobs designs, manages, and helps deliver complicated facilities and public
infrastructure. Its Infrastructure & Advanced Facilities segment serves data
centers, semiconductors, life sciences, water, energy, and transportation. PA
Consulting adds advisory, digital, and public-sector capability.

The investment question is whether Jacobs controls enough of the customer
relationship and technical workflow to earn durable owner cash, or whether the
reported backlog and adjusted earnings are offset by pass-through revenue,
receivable growth, project risk, PA acquisition financing, employee-related
consideration, stock compensation, and debt.

## What Jacobs controls

The operating sequence is:

`customer capital or public program -> planning, design, permitting, engineering, and program management -> procurement and construction coordination -> facility or system delivery -> operation, compliance, and follow-on work -> billing and collection -> cash`

Jacobs controls technical expertise, program coordination, design standards,
regulatory knowledge, customer access, and the ability to coordinate many
specialists. The customer generally controls the underlying capital budget,
site, financing, project approval, and final asset. Jacobs often earns a fee
for reducing execution complexity rather than owning the asset that remains at
the end.

## Definitions

- **Gross revenue** includes amounts billed or passed through under certain
  project arrangements. **Adjusted net revenue** removes some pass-through
  costs and is often more informative about the economic work Jacobs performs.
- **Backlog** is remaining contracted work expected to be recognized as
  revenue. It is not a guarantee of margin or cash.
- **Remaining performance obligations** are contracted obligations not yet
  recognized as revenue. They still require labor, subcontractors, delivery,
  billing, and collection.
- **Book-to-bill** compares awards or bookings with revenue. It indicates
  demand direction but not the return earned on each award.
- **Recurring-capital residual** here means operating cash flow less property
  and equipment additions. Acquisitions, working capital, taxes, debt,
  employee consideration, dividends, and buybacks remain separate claims.

The difference between gross revenue and adjusted net revenue is central. A
large gross backlog can increase reported scale without producing the same
profit or cash as technical design, program management, or consulting work.
The next filing should keep the two measures visible and reconcile them to
segment margin and owner cash.

## Three-year cash bridge

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Property and equipment additions | Recurring-capital residual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2023 | `$974.8M` | `$137.5M` | `$837.3M` |
| FY2024 | `$1.0547B` | `$121.1M` | `$933.6M` |
| FY2025 | `$686.7M` | `$79.2M` | `$607.5M` |

The decline in FY2025 cash is not automatically evidence of weaker economics.
The filing attributes it in part to higher cash uses for receivables and
income taxes, while FY2024 included discontinued-operation and separation
effects. It does mean the FY2024 cash figure should not be used as a permanent
run rate without normalization.

The forward capital-allocation burden is more important. Jacobs paid about
`£997.6M` in cash for the remaining PA shares in March 2026 and issued about
`2.04M` shares. It also owes an additional `£75M` of consideration in fiscal
2028, payable in cash, shares, or both. The Q3 filing showed `$917.6M` used to
repurchase PA redeemable noncontrolling interests and related costs, `$614.1M`
of common-stock repurchases, and `$123.4M` of dividends over nine months. The
company therefore had strong access to capital, but common-owner cash was
allocated simultaneously to acquisition settlement, buybacks, dividends, and
debt financing.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering risks

### Gross backlog can overstate economic work

Backlog is useful only after separating pass-through revenue, subcontractor
content, reimbursable cost, and fee-bearing work. Track gross backlog, adjusted
net revenue backlog, book-to-bill on both bases, segment margin, and cash
conversion. A higher gross backlog with flat adjusted net revenue can indicate
more activity without more economic control.

### Project estimates can move current earnings

Jacobs recognizes revenue across long projects and professional-service
arrangements. Estimated costs, change orders, claims, customer approvals, and
schedule changes affect current margin. Review unfavorable estimate changes,
contract assets, receivables, provisions, and whether cash collection follows
reported revenue.

### PA acquisition accounting widens the adjusted-to-GAAP gap

Q2 FY2026 produced a `$43M` GAAP net loss while adjusted EBITDA and adjusted
EPS were positive. The filing identifies PA transaction effects, employee
consideration, higher taxes, amortization, and other adjustments. The nine-
month filing included `$237.5M` of charges for certain subsidiary-level
compensation arrangements and `$122.7M` primarily related to consideration
costs for specified PA employees. These may be transaction-specific, but they
are real cash or economic claims and should not disappear from a valuation
without a clear end date and post-acquisition test.

### Receivables and contract assets are a major cash claim

At June 26, 2026, receivables and contract assets were about `$3.761B`, up from
`$2.989B` at FY2025 year-end. Nine-month operating cash included a `$595M`
use from receivables and contract assets, partly offset by a `$518M` source
from accounts payable. Management said a favorable cash timing item would
reverse in the fourth quarter. That is direct evidence that reported cash and
reported earnings need a working-capital normalization.

### Acquisition leverage and dilution must be counted together

Long-term debt rose from about `$2.236B` at FY2025 year-end to `$3.579B` at
June 26, 2026. The increase primarily funded PA transaction cash consideration,
buybacks, dividends, and taxes on vested restricted stock. The company also
issued shares and carries redeemable noncontrolling-interest claims. The
question is not whether PA increases revenue. It is whether PA's incremental
profit and cash exceed the financing cost, employee consideration, integration
cost, and dilution.

### Investment gains and tax effects can distort net income

The FY2025 cash-flow statement included a large loss on investment in equity
securities after a prior-year gain, while the income statement also reflected
tax effects connected with the Amentum investment and other items. Separate
operating project earnings from equity-investment marks, tax normalization,
and discontinued operations before assigning a multiple.

## Balance-sheet resilience and capital requirements

At June 26, 2026, Jacobs reported `$1.173B` of cash, `$3.579B` of long-term
debt, approximately `$4.763B` of goodwill, and about `$640M` of intangible
assets. Operating lease liabilities were about `$475M` in total, and pension
liabilities were about `$258M`.

The company had `$1.5B` of available revolver capacity, but liquidity is not
the same as unencumbered owner cash. Cash was distributed across international
entities, restricted cash remained associated with the PA employee benefit
trust, and contract assets and receivables absorb funding before collection.
Required capital therefore includes working capital, debt service, employee
consideration, pension, lease payments, and project support—not just capex.

## Lyn Alden lens

Jacobs is exposed to several forms of institutional and industrial liquidity.
Public infrastructure, water, transport, and government programs depend on
budgets and policy. Data centers, semiconductors, and life-sciences facilities
depend on corporate capital spending and financing conditions. When money is
cheap and strategic investment is high, Jacobs can benefit from a broad build
cycle. When rates rise or customers defer projects, backlog can remain visible
while starts and collections slow.

PA also changes the financing sensitivity. Debt-funded acquisition growth makes
the equity more dependent on sustained operating cash and successful integration.
Higher rates claim more of project cash before shareholders receive it.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative becomes:

`institutional and industrial complexity × customer capital spending × Jacobs design and program-management share × technical talent and execution capacity -> adjusted net revenue -> labor, subcontractors, project risk, overhead, taxes, debt, and reinvestment -> owner cash`

The valuation should separately model:

1. I&AF growth by data center, semiconductor, water, energy, transportation,
   and life-sciences end market;
2. PA revenue, margin, retention, and integration cost;
3. gross revenue versus adjusted net revenue and pass-through intensity;
4. backlog and RPO conversion, estimate changes, receivables, and contract
   assets;
5. PA acquisition cash consideration, employee claims, share issuance, and
   deferred consideration;
6. debt, interest, pension, leases, and required working capital; and
7. buybacks, dividends, SBC, redeemable noncontrolling interests, and diluted
   cash per share.

At the September 14, 2026 market snapshot, Jacobs equity value was about
`$16.7B`. The valuation register uses FY2025 recurring-capital residual of
`$607.5M`, but normalizes the bear and base cases for the cash decline,
receivable timing, PA financing, acquisition consideration, and dilution. The
central issue is whether the market is valuing technical control or merely
capital-budget visibility.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Management is incentivized to emphasize gross revenue, adjusted net revenue,
backlog, book-to-bill, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EPS, free cash flow, and PA
strategic value. Pair each measure with GAAP cash, pass-through content,
receivables, project estimates, debt, employee consideration, SBC, and diluted
shares.

The thesis breaks if:

- gross backlog grows while adjusted net revenue, margin, or cash does not;
- receivables and contract assets repeatedly consume cash faster than revenue
  grows;
- PA adds revenue but not incremental cash after financing and employee claims;
- debt stays high because buybacks and dividends compete with repayment;
- data-center, semiconductor, water, or transport projects are delayed by
  financing, permits, or customer approvals;
- project estimates, claims, or fixed-price work produce recurring margin
  reversals;
- the company needs repeated adjustments to reconcile GAAP earnings with the
  claimed operating trajectory; or
- cash per diluted share falls while adjusted EPS rises through buybacks,
  investment marks, or acquisition accounting.

## Bottom line

Jacobs is a technical coordination business that earns revenue when customers
need help converting capital budgets into permitted, designed, procured, and
operating systems. It is a useful counterpart to KBR: Jacobs is more exposed
to advanced facilities, water, transportation, and consulting, while KBR has a
larger federal mission-support and process-technology separation question.

The demand story is credible, especially around data centers, semiconductors,
water, and energy. The financial question is harder. FY2025 cash declined,
receivables and contract assets rose, PA required substantial cash and debt,
employee consideration widened the adjustment gap, and buybacks and dividends
continued alongside acquisition financing. Durable owner economics require
adjusted net revenue to convert through working capital, PA to earn its
financing and employee claims, and cash per diluted share to improve without
relying on gross-backlog growth or temporary cash timing.
