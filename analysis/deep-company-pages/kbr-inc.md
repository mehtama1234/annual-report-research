# KBR, Inc. — mission support, sustainable technology, spin-off economics, and backlog conversion

Date baseline: 2026-09-14  
Primary evidence: [KBR company packet](../../extracted/industrial-goods/engineering-construction/kbr-inc/company-packet.md), [KBR FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1357615/000135761526000051/kbr-20260102.htm), [KBR Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1357615/000135761526000172/kbr-20260703.htm), and [KBR source ledger](../../extracted/industrial-goods/engineering-construction/kbr-inc/source-ledger.md)

## Investment question

KBR has two different economic engines. Mission Technology Solutions (MTS)
provides defense, intelligence, readiness, space, and federal-civilian
execution. Sustainable Technology Solutions (STS) provides engineering,
process technology, licensing, and industrial project work.

Management plans to spin off MTS on January 4, 2027. The investment question
is therefore two linked questions: can the existing company convert backlog
into owner cash while paying for debt, working capital, pension, capital
spending, and separation costs; and will the separated businesses carry the
right costs and balance sheets once corporate expenses and financing are
reallocated?

## What KBR controls

The operating sequence is:

`institutional or industrial requirement -> funded contract or project -> engineering, program management, logistics, and specialist labor -> cost and schedule execution -> acceptance and billing -> collection -> follow-on work or license renewal -> cash`

KBR controls specialized program knowledge, technical labor, engineering and
process IP, contract execution, readiness support, and relationships with
government and industrial customers. Customers control funding, scope,
procurement, change orders, and project timing. KBR carries execution risk,
especially when a fixed-price or project obligation is priced before all costs
are known.

## Definitions

- **Backlog** is remaining contractual work expected to be performed. It is
  visibility, not profit or collected cash.
- **Award options** are potential future work that is not the same as funded
  backlog. KBR reported backlog plus options separately.
- **Remaining performance obligations** are the portion of contract revenue
  not yet recognized under the applicable revenue rules. They still require
  performance and collection.
- **Cost-reimbursable work** generally passes eligible costs through to the
  customer with a fee, while **fixed-price work** leaves more cost-overrun
  exposure with KBR. **Time-and-materials** work depends on billable labor,
  rates, and utilization.
- **Recurring-capital residual** here means continuing-operations operating
  cash flow less property, plant, and equipment purchases. Acquisitions,
  working-capital volatility, pension funding, spin-off costs, debt, and
  capital returns are then examined separately.

| FY2025 | Revenue `$7.786B`; backlog `$16.864B`; backlog plus options `$23.211B` | Operating cash `$557M`; capital spending `$42M`; residual about `$515M`; acquisition cash `$14M`; KBR-attributable net income `$415M` |
| Q1 FY2026 | Revenue `$1.923B`; remaining performance obligations about `$13.6B` | Six-month-to-date operating cash was `$110M`; the separation plan began adding cost and financing complexity |

The annual mix matters. FY2025 revenue included about `$3.305B` of
cost-reimbursable work, `$2.311B` of time-and-materials work, and `$2.170B` of
fixed-price work. A large part of the contract base therefore has different
cash and margin behavior from a simple fixed-price backlog model.

## Three-year cash bridge

| Fiscal year | Continuing operating cash flow | PP&E purchases | Recurring-capital residual | Acquisition cash |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2023 | `$301M` | `$62M` | `$239M` | none |
| FY2024 | `$450M` | `$52M` | `$398M` | `$738M` |
| FY2025 | `$557M` | `$42M` | `$515M` | `$14M` |

FY2025 cash conversion was strong on the reported numbers. But the bridge
needs three qualifications. First, KBR's FY2025 operating cash included
working-capital movements, distributions from unconsolidated affiliates, and
contract execution timing. Second, FY2024's acquisition cash was large enough
to exceed the recurring-capital residual, so growth has not always been funded
internally. Third, the HomeSafe discontinued operation had separate cash uses;
those should not be mixed casually with the continuing business.

The planned spin-off creates another cash question. KBR disclosed that MTS is
expected to make a cash distribution to KBR using proceeds from financing
undertaken before separation, with KBR intending to use the distribution to
reduce debt. That may improve KBR's near-term balance sheet, but it can also
place leverage or financing obligations inside the separated MTS business.
The post-spin capital structure must be evaluated directly rather than
assuming that a distribution is operating cash.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering risks

### Backlog quality depends on contract type and customer action

KBR's backlog was stable to higher, but backlog can be cancelled, renegotiated,
rephased, or delayed. Options add visibility but are not the same as funded
work. Compare backlog conversion with revenue, margin, billings, contract
assets, receivables, and cash by MTS and STS.

### Cost-to-cost estimates can move current earnings

Long-term project and service contracts often recognize revenue as work is
performed. Estimated total cost, change orders, claims, and expected losses
can change current profit. Review unfavorable estimate changes, contract-loss
provisions, unapproved change orders, and whether contract assets grow faster
than revenue.

### Joint ventures affect cash access

KBR receives distributions from unconsolidated affiliates and also has
relationships where cash belongs partly to a joint venture or partner. Cash
recognized in operating cash flow is not always immediately available for
general corporate use. Track distributions, capital calls, guarantees,
receivables from affiliates, and the difference between consolidated earnings
and cash actually available to KBR shareholders.

### The spin-off can create recurring cost rather than remove it

The Q2 filing recorded `$31M` of spin-off costs in the quarter and `$46M` for
the first six months, including lease right-of-use asset impairment charges.
Some separation cost is one-time. Other items can reappear as standalone
public-company costs, duplicated systems, new facilities, executive costs, or
stranded corporate expense. The relevant test is post-spin cash cost per
segment, not management's adjusted earnings before separation charges.

### Adjusted EBITDA can conceal contract and capital burdens

KBR emphasizes adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EPS, adjusted operating cash flow,
and operating cash conversion. These measures can help compare periods, but
they should be reconciled to GAAP operating cash after working capital, pension
funding, capital spending, spin-off costs, and debt service. The FY2025 filing
also recorded a `$26M` resolution of a legacy U.S. government contract dispute;
that benefit should not be treated as a normal operating margin improvement.

### Capital returns compete with debt reduction and separation funding

KBR repurchased common stock and paid dividends while carrying more than `$2.5B`
of long-term debt in the latest balance-sheet chain. The Q2 2026 six-month
filing shows `$25M` of share repurchases, `$42M` of dividends, and net debt
repayment activity. Buybacks can improve per-share metrics, but the test is
whether they are funded after pension, project working capital, capex, debt,
and the cost of becoming two public companies.

## Balance-sheet resilience and capital requirements

At the latest packet point, cash fell from about `$500M` at FY2025 year-end to
about `$312M` by Q2 FY2026 while long-term debt remained above `$2.5B`. The
Q2 filing also showed continuing-operations operating cash of `$160M` for the
quarter and `$2M` for the six-month period because project and working-capital
timing was unfavorable.

KBR has operating leases, pension obligations, letters of credit, joint-venture
commitments, and project guarantees in addition to reported debt. Capital
requirements are therefore not limited to PP&E. A sustainable owner-cash
estimate must include working-capital funding, contract support, pension
contributions, lease payments, legal and project obligations, and the cost of
separation.

## Lyn Alden lens

KBR's MTS demand is linked to public-sector spending, geopolitical priorities,
and government readiness. STS demand is linked to industrial capital budgets,
energy and process projects, and the cost of financing large facilities. The
two segments respond to different liquidity conditions.

Government work can remain strategically important while award timing is
delayed by appropriations or procurement. Industrial work can have a strong
long-term need while customers postpone projects when financing costs rise or
project economics weaken. The combined company therefore has diversification,
but not a single stable demand cycle.

Debt raises the sensitivity. If the spin-off requires new financing, more cash
will be claimed by interest before it reaches common owners. If customers
delay payment, working capital can absorb cash even when backlog remains
healthy.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative becomes:

`institutional and industrial complexity × funded program demand × KBR execution share × specialist labor and process IP -> revenue -> labor, subcontractors, project costs, overhead, pension, taxes, debt, and reinvestment -> owner cash`

The valuation should separately model:

1. MTS revenue, margin, funding, and post-spin corporate cost;
2. STS licensing, engineering, project, and backlog economics;
3. cost-reimbursable, time-and-materials, and fixed-price mix;
4. contract assets, receivables, change orders, and joint-venture distributions;
5. spin-off costs and each entity's post-spin capital requirements;
6. debt, interest, pension, guarantees, and refinancing; and
7. stock compensation, repurchases, dividends, and diluted cash per share.

At the September 14, 2026 market snapshot, KBR's equity value was about
`$4.7B`. The valuation register uses FY2025 recurring-capital residual of
`$515M` as the starting measure, but normalizes it down in the bear and base
cases for working-capital variability, spin-off costs, debt, and separate
company overhead. This is an expectations audit, not a financial signoff.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Management is incentivized to emphasize adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EPS,
operating cash conversion, backlog, bookings, options, and the strategic value
of the spin-off. Pair every measure with GAAP cash, contract-type mix, estimate
changes, capital spending, debt, pension, separation cost, and diluted shares.

The thesis breaks if:

- backlog and options remain high but funded conversion, margins, or cash fall;
- fixed-price work produces recurring cost overruns or contract-loss charges;
- cash generation depends on affiliate distributions or favorable working
  capital that cannot be repeated;
- the spin-off creates stranded corporate cost, duplicate infrastructure, or
  more debt than the separated businesses can service;
- STS licensing and project work weaken when industrial customers defer capex;
- MTS revenue declines as contingency or federal work rolls off without enough
  replacement awards;
- buybacks and dividends continue while debt, pension, or separation funding
  require external financing; or
- adjusted EPS improves while GAAP cash per diluted share declines.

## Next-filing evidence ladder

| Question | Confirming evidence | Falsifying evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is backlog high quality? | Funded work converts into revenue, margin, billing, and cash across both segments | Options and backlog rise while funded work, margins, or collections weaken |
| Does contract mix protect returns? | Cost-reimbursable and time-and-materials work supports cash while fixed-price margins remain controlled | Fixed-price estimates, claims, and change orders repeatedly reduce profit |
| Is cash truly available? | Affiliate distributions, contract assets, and working capital normalize without recurring external funding | Reported cash depends on partner distributions, receivable monetization, or payable timing |
| Does the spin-off create value? | Each entity reports clear revenue, margin, cash, debt, and standalone cost with declining separation charges | New public-company cost, debt, and stranded corporate expense consume the expected benefit |
| Is STS durable? | Licensing, engineering, and project backlog convert through customer capex cycles with stable margins | Licensing mix or large projects produce revenue without cash and return on capital |
| Is leverage manageable? | Spin-off proceeds reduce debt and post-spin interest burden without starving MTS | Separation financing shifts debt to a weaker entity or total interest rises |

## Bottom line

KBR is the execution layer between institutional priorities and delivered
programs. MTS connects government budgets to readiness, intelligence,
space, and technical mission support. STS connects industrial capital budgets
to engineering, process technology, and licensing.

FY2025 showed strong reported cash conversion and a substantial backlog. The
main risk is not lack of demand visibility. It is whether the cash belongs to
the continuing common-owner business after contract estimates, joint ventures,
working capital, pension, debt, capital returns, and the planned separation.
The spin-off can make the economics easier to see, but it can also expose
standalone costs and financing burdens that the combined company currently
absorbs.
