# Keysight Technologies: measurement and assurance control with acquisition and electronics-cycle risk

## Bottom line

Keysight sells design, simulation, measurement, test, and network-assurance
tools used to develop and operate communications, aerospace and defense,
automotive, semiconductor, industrial, and electronic systems. Its economic
position comes from helping engineers prove that a complex system meets speed,
signal, reliability, security, and interoperability requirements.

This is a different control point from Teradyne. Teradyne is concentrated in
production and system test. Keysight reaches further across design simulation,
prototype validation, manufacturing, network deployment, and operational
assurance. That can produce recurring technical workflows without owning the
network or data center. It does not eliminate electronics-capex cycles,
customer budget timing, acquisition integration, or the need to keep its
measurement standards current.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $5.375B, operating cash flow was $1.409B, PP&E
spending was $128M, and the simple cash-after-PP&E screen was about $1.281B.
In Q3 fiscal 2026, revenue reached $1.85B and management reported a second
consecutive record quarter with orders above $2B. At the September 14, 2026
market snapshot, equity value was about $56.2B, or approximately 44 times the
fiscal 2025 cash-after-PP&E screen.

The question is whether Keysight owns a durable validation workflow with
software and service attachment, or whether current AI and high-speed-network
demand is a strong electronics-capex cycle being capitalized at a premium.

## How to read the company

`technical standard or system requirement -> design and simulation -> measurement and validation -> production/deployment assurance -> software/service workflow -> cash`

Keysight has two reportable segments:

- **Communications Solutions Group:** commercial communications and aerospace,
  defense, and government markets, including wireless, wireline, enterprise,
  networking, and related test and assurance.
- **Electronic Industrial Solutions Group:** automotive, energy, industrial,
  semiconductor, and general electronics markets.

The company sells instruments, systems, software, and services. The value of a
measurement tool is not only the hardware. It includes accuracy, reference
standards, software integration, data continuity, customer training, and the
engineer's confidence that the result is valid.

guidance was $1.930B–$1.950B.

## Earnings quality and forensic review

### 1. Validation demand is real, but it is not automatically recurring

Keysight benefits when communications links, optical paths, wireless systems,
semiconductor designs, automotive electronics, and defense systems become more
complex. Customers need to validate performance against technical standards and
customer requirements.

The recurring claim must be decomposed. Some demand is software subscription,
service, calibration, support, and repeat use of an installed workflow. Some is
new instrumentation purchased for a design cycle or capacity program. A high
Q3 order number proves current demand and backlog formation; it does not prove
that the next design cycle will require the same spending.

### 2. Cash conversion is strong, but tax and working capital matter

Fiscal 2025 operating cash flow was $1.409B against $850M of net income. The
10-K states that cash flow can fluctuate materially with working-capital needs,
income-tax payments, variable compensation, pension funding, and other items.
The 2025 cash increase reflected changes in derivatives, compensation and
payroll accruals, tax accruals, deferred revenue, and acquisition-related
payments.

This is not evidence of improper accounting. It identifies the normalization
work. Compare receivables, inventory, deferred revenue, income-tax receivables,
variable-pay accruals, pension contributions, and cash taxes before treating
the 2025 cash level as a steady owner-cash run rate.

### 3. Deferred revenue is a useful indicator of workflow commitments

Keysight's contract liabilities are mainly customer deposits received before
products ship or services are provided. The balance rose from $767M at the
beginning of fiscal 2025 to $884M at year-end. During the year, $513M of opening
deferred revenue was recognized, while $549M of current-period revenue was
deferred and $73M came from acquisitions.

The balance supports future revenue visibility, but part of the increase came
from the Spirent acquisition. The test is whether deferred revenue grows from
organic service and software adoption, whether it converts into margin and
cash, and whether cancellations or customer acceptance change the timing.

### 4. Spirent materially changes the acquisition and balance-sheet analysis

Keysight acquired Spirent for $1.564B on October 15, 2025, using existing cash,
and sold selected high-speed Ethernet, network-security, and channel-emulation
business lines to Viavi for $399M to satisfy regulatory conditions. Spirent
contributed only $9M of revenue after acquisition in fiscal 2025 and generated
a $40M net loss attributable to the acquisition period, including a $19M loss
from discontinued operations.

The purchase created $698M of goodwill across the two reportable segments and
$515M of developed-technology and customer-relationship intangible assets. The
company recorded $42M of acquisition and integration costs and $7M of
acquisition-related compensation in 2025.

The strategic rationale may be sound: network test, assurance, and positioning
expand Keysight's communications and data-center reach. The owner test is
harder. Future filings must show organic growth, Spirent revenue and margin,
integration savings, customer retention, amortization, goodwill, and the
returns on the cash spent after the regulatory divestiture.

### 5. R&D and software are required reinvestment

Fiscal 2025 R&D was approximately $1.007B. Keysight competes on measurement
accuracy, standards, software, automation, signal integrity, network assurance,
and technical support. A company can preserve near-term margin by reducing
R&D, but that can weaken future qualification and product leadership.

The cash-after-PP&E screen therefore understates the full reinvestment burden
if a material portion of software, engineering, standards, calibration, and
customer-support work is required to maintain the workflow.

### 6. SBC is meaningful relative to owner cash

Fiscal 2025 stock-based compensation was $176M, compared with about $1.281B of
cash after PP&E. At year-end, $163M of unrecognized stock-compensation cost
remained for non-vested awards, excluding some performance awards whose targets
had not yet been set.

The per-share test is cash after PP&E less the economic cost of ongoing equity
compensation, acquisition cash, debt service, and required R&D. Buybacks should
be compared with shares issued through equity plans, not treated as a separate
proof of owner return.

## Alden lens: technical complexity inside an electronics-liquidity cycle

Keysight benefits from long-term growth in AI networking, optical links,
wireless standards, semiconductor complexity, defense electronics, automotive
systems, and industrial automation. The company does not have to own the
network to benefit when the network becomes difficult to design and validate.

The liquidity risk is that test budgets are still part of customer engineering
and capital programs. Higher rates, weaker venture and enterprise budgets,
slower handset or automotive cycles, defense timing, or a pause in data-center
network upgrades can delay tool purchases.

The Spirent acquisition also makes the macro test more demanding. The company
used existing cash to acquire a large adjacent platform just as the current
AI and high-speed-network cycle accelerated. That can increase long-term
capability, but it also increases integration, debt-equivalent, goodwill, and
execution exposure.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers and valuation

The valuation chain is:

`technical complexity -> design/test workflow adoption -> instrument, software, and service revenue -> gross margin -> R&D and acquisition reinvestment -> owner cash`

Fiscal 2025 cash after PP&E was approximately $1.281B. At a market value of
about $56.2B, the equity value is roughly 44 times that screen. The current
price is demanding but less extreme than Teradyne's valuation because Keysight
has a larger, broader cash base and a more diversified workflow.

Illustrative equity-value cases:

| Normalized owner cash | Multiple | Implied equity value |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0.9B bear | 25x | $22.5B |
| $1.3B base | 35x | $45.5B |
| $1.7B bull | 45x | $76.5B |

The base case is below the current market value, while the bull case requires
successful Spirent integration, sustained AI and network validation growth,
strong software/service attachment, and continued high returns on R&D. The
market is not merely valuing historical test-instrument cash; it is valuing a
larger future validation workflow.

## Possible financial-engineering questions

These are tests, not accusations:

- How much of 2025 and 2026 cash flow came from tax timing, compensation
  accruals, derivatives, or deferred revenue rather than recurring economics?
- Does Spirent's acquisition increase organic revenue and customer retention,
  or mainly increase reported scale through purchase accounting?
- Are deferred-revenue additions organic, and do they convert into gross profit
  and collected cash?
- Does capitalized software or acquisition accounting shift required investment
  outside the simple PP&E free-cash-flow screen?
- Does SBC reduce per-share cash despite buybacks?
- Are high Q3 orders sustained after current AI-network and defense programs
  normalize?
- Are measurement and assurance tools becoming workflow software, or are they
  still mainly cyclical instruments sold around design launches?

## What would prove this wrong

Downgrade the thesis if future filings show that:

1. Communications and AI-network orders reverse after customers complete a
   capacity or standards cycle.
2. Service and software attachment does not grow with the installed measurement
   base.
3. Spirent fails to retain customers, improve margins, or generate returns above
   the acquisition and integration cost.
4. Receivables, inventory, tax assets, or deferred revenue weaken cash
   conversion.
5. R&D and software investment rise faster than incremental gross profit.
6. A shift to virtualized or internal test tools reduces Keysight's pricing and
   workflow control.
7. SBC, debt, and acquisition amortization prevent cash per diluted share from
   growing with reported earnings.
