# Kinross Gold Corporation — multi-asset gold production, reserve renewal, and buybacks

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed IFRS figures in U.S. dollars; dollar
amounts are in millions unless otherwise stated.

## How to read this memo

Attributable production means the gold that belongs economically to Kinross
after partner ownership. Sustaining capital keeps existing mines operating;
non-sustaining capital funds growth or extensions. AISC adds sustaining capital
and related costs to a mine’s direct operating costs. An impairment reversal
raises reported profit when an earlier asset write-down is partly reversed; it
does not prove that the asset has generated new cash.

## Investment question

Kinross is a multi-asset gold producer with mines and projects across several
countries. It is less dependent on one mine than Lundin Gold and less sprawling
than Newmont. The question is whether diversification improves through-cycle
owner cash or merely adds more mines, jurisdictions, development projects,
reserve-replacement needs, taxes, and capital-allocation decisions.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Kinross produced 2.012M attributable gold-equivalent ounces in 2025. Tasiast,
  Paracatu, La Coipa, Fort Knox, Manh Choh, Round Mountain, and Bald Mountain
  create diversification, but each carries its own grade, recovery, cost,
  labor, equipment, and permitting risks.
- The named development queue—Great Bear, Curlew, Round Mountain Phase X,
  Bald Mountain Redbird, and Lobo-Marte—shows that current cash is funding the
  next production base. Project value requires capital, schedule, permits,
  construction, ramp-up, and acceptable returns.
- Kinross returned $752.4M in 2025 and repurchased and cancelled 30.7M shares.
  Buybacks can create per-share value when shares are cheap, but can destroy
  value if management uses peak commodity cash to buy shares while the asset
  base needs capital.
- Cash net of debt reached approximately $1.004B. This improves resilience and
  gives management flexibility, but cash location, taxes, repatriation, and
  project commitments determine what is available to the parent.
- Q1 2026 included about $91M of withholding tax tied to planned repatriation of
  earnings, mainly from Mauritania. Consolidated profit therefore does not
  describe the full cost of moving cash across jurisdictions.
- Q4 2025 included a $116.1M impairment reversal at Lobo-Marte. The reversal is
  a useful accounting warning: changes in valuation assumptions can alter
  reported profit without changing current mine cash.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. How much cash depends on gold price?

Rebuild revenue and cash at mid-cycle gold, silver, and copper prices. Separate
price, ounces, grades, recovery, fuel, labor, local currency, royalties, and
taxes. A rising gold price can increase both revenue and government or employee
claims.

### 2. Is AISC a complete economic cost?

Reconcile AISC to the filed cash-flow statement. Separate sustaining capital,
non-sustaining capital, exploration, deferred stripping, reclamation, closure,
and working-capital changes. AISC is useful for comparing mines, but it does not
capture every cost of maintaining the corporate asset base.

### 3. Does diversification improve returns?

Compare each mine’s capital invested, production, cost, reserve life, taxes,
reclamation, and cash return. A portfolio is better only when the combined
assets produce more resilient returns than a concentrated asset after corporate
overhead and project capital.

### 4. Are reserves and projects being replaced economically?

Track reserve changes after depletion, price assumptions, grades, recovery,
acquisitions, and divestitures. For Great Bear, Curlew, Phase X, Redbird, and
Lobo-Marte, compare approved capital with actual spending, start date, output,
cost, and cash return. An impairment reversal is not project success.

### 5. Are buybacks and dividends covered through the cycle?

Compare returns to shareholders with mid-cycle cash after sustaining capital,
reclamation, debt maturities, taxes, and required project funding. Test whether
share count falls because of genuine value creation or because the company is
using debt or selling assets to preserve the payout.

## Incentives, reserve renewal, and cohort position

Kinross's capital-allocation story combines buybacks, dividends, debt reduction,
and a visible project queue. That creates two separate incentive tests. First,
strong gold-price cash can make current distributions look repeatable even when
reserve replacement and project construction require more capital later. Second,
share repurchases can improve reported per-share results while the company is
still using cash to extend mine lives or develop the next production base.

The correct scorecard is cash per diluted share after sustaining capital,
reclamation, taxes, debt service, and the capital required to replace depleted
ounces. Great Bear, Curlew, Phase X, Redbird, and Lobo-Marte should be judged as
separate investments with their own capital, schedule, production, cost,
reserve, and return records. The Lobo-Marte impairment reversal should not be
allowed to improve the investment case unless subsequent mine cash confirms the
revised assumptions.

Kinross belongs in the [diversified-versus-focused mining comparison](../cross-sector/diversified-miner-versus-focused-miner-forensic-comparison-2026-09-13.md)
and the [commodity scarcity and renewal first-principles essay](../first-principles/commodity-scarcity-embedded-service-and-owner-cash.md).
Relative to Wheaton and Franco-Nevada, Kinross carries direct labor, energy,
equipment, reclamation, and site-level execution. Relative to Newmont, it is a
smaller portfolio with less breadth but the same requirement: diversification
only creates value if the combined mines renew reserves at acceptable returns.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** gold can benefit from monetary and fiscal stress, but a
  miner remains exposed to energy, labor, equipment, currency, and country
  inflation. Multiple jurisdictions diversify risk and also increase it.
- **Damodaran lens:** value producing mines, reserves, development projects,
  and capital returns separately. Use mid-cycle prices, explicit sustaining
  capital, probability-weighted projects, and a required return on new capital.
- **Forensic lens:** the main category errors are treating adjusted operating
  cash flow as common cash, AISC as full cost, impairment reversals as operating
  improvement, reserves as production, and buybacks as value creation without
  a price and reinvestment test.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $35.80B of equity value at
$29.13 per share. Against management’s attributable FY2025 free cash flow of
$2.474B, the equity value is about 14.5x. A normalized range of $1.5B / $2.3B /
$3.2B at 15x / 18x / 22x implies $22.5B / $41.4B / $70.4B. The current value
requires cash well above the 2023–2024 level and assumes strong prices,
continued execution, and economically successful project renewal.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Gold prices normalize while mine costs, taxes, and project spending stay high.
2. Tasiast, Paracatu, Round Mountain, or another major mine misses production,
   grade, recovery, or availability expectations.
3. Great Bear, Curlew, Phase X, Redbird, or Lobo-Marte requires more capital or
   produces less cash than planned.
4. Mauritania, Brazil, Chile, the United States, or Canada changes taxes,
   royalties, permits, repatriation rules, or operating conditions.
5. Reserve replacement falls below depletion and the company becomes dependent
   on acquisitions or higher prices to maintain output.
6. Buybacks and dividends continue above mid-cycle cash after renewal capital.
