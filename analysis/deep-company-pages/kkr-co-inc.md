## Bottom line

KKR is a multi-engine capital platform: alternative-asset management, Global
Atlantic insurance, strategic holdings, private wealth, asset-based finance,
and monetization. Its control point is long-duration capital plus origination,
distribution, and the ability to move assets across funds, insurance, and
wealth vehicles.

The breadth can make earnings more durable than a classic buyout manager, but
it also makes the denominator harder. The packet reports FY2025 FRE of roughly
$3.7B, Total Operating Earnings of roughly $5.0B, ANI of roughly $4.4B, $744B
of AUM, $604B of FPAUM, $129B of capital raised, and $95B invested. The 10-K
reports $19.464B of revenue, $2.370B of GAAP net income, and only $477.8M of
consolidated operating cash flow. Insurance and fund consolidation make that
GAAP cash figure unsuitable as a standalone owner-cash measure.

The core question is whether KKR's insurance, monetization, and wealth
channels create genuinely recurring common-owner value or merely add more
claims, marks, capital requirements, and complexity around a strong fee base.

## Q2 2026 checkpoint: what actually improved

The latest quarter strengthens the fee engine, but it does not remove the
need for separate tests. AUM reached `$796.5B`, up 16% year over year. FPAUM
reached `$638.4B`, up from `$604.1B` at year-end 2025. KKR reported Q2 FRE of
`$1.214B`, Insurance Operating Earnings of `$288M`, and Strategic Holdings
Operating Earnings of `$37M`; together these produced TOE of `$1.539B`, up from
`$1.194B` in Q2 2025. Q2 ANI was `$1.493B`, or `$1.63` per adjusted share.

The quality distinction is important. Management fees were `$1.250B`, up 26%,
while fee-related compensation was `$302M` and other operating expenses were
`$210M`. That leaves FRE after the disclosed operating costs, but FRE is still
a management-defined measure rather than GAAP net income. KKR also moved
`$255M` of fee-related performance revenue into the fee-related section in the
quarter, so the comparison with earlier periods needs the company's
reclassification note.

The performance component was unusually strong: realized performance income
was `$848M` before its `$636M` compensation allocation, leaving `$212M` net;
realized investment income was `$190M` before compensation. This is valuable
cash-generating activity, but it should not be capitalized at the same multiple
as management fees because it depends on exits, financing markets, buyer
demand, and the ability to realize portfolio values.

The forward fee base has several measurable supports: `$143B` of uncalled
commitments, `$72B` of committed capital not yet paying fees, and `$410B` of
carry-eligible AUM, of which `$325B` was above cost and accruing carry. These
are potential future economics, not current owner cash. The next test is how
much becomes fee-paying capital, at what fee rate, after distribution and
compensation costs.

Global Atlantic requires a separate balance-sheet analysis. KKR reported Q2
Insurance Operating Earnings of `$288M` and broader Total Insurance Economics
of `$515M`, which also includes fees paid by the insurance segment and related
capital-markets and sidecar fees. The release explicitly says Total Insurance
Economics is not the profitability of the insurance segment and should not be
used by itself. KKR's asset-management and strategic-holdings cash and
short-term investments were `$5.314B`, investments were `$8.612B`, and debt at
par was `$9.299B`, excluding Global Atlantic from the cash figure. That scope
boundary matters: the insurance subsidiary has its own liabilities, asset
quality, liquidity, reserve, and statutory-capital requirements.

KKR also reported `$10.2B` of gross unrealized carried interest and `$18.2B`
of remaining unrealized gains when balance-sheet gains are included. These
amounts show future performance potential, not money already available to
common shareholders. A proper owner-cash bridge must deduct carried-interest
compensation, taxes, partner and noncontrolling claims, preferred dividends,
debt, and any capital that must remain inside Global Atlantic.

## The control point

`institutional / insurance / wealth capital -> origination and product design
-> asset management, insurance spread, private wealth, and monetization -> fees,
investment income, and performance economics`

KKR controls access to private assets and bespoke capital, but Global Atlantic
also operates an insurance balance sheet. That distinguishes KKR from a pure
fee platform: insurance assets and liabilities, investment spread, statutory
capital, duration, and regulatory permissions become part of the system. KKR's
origination and distribution network can still be valuable even when exits are
slow, but the risk is transferred rather than removed.

## Narrative-to-numbers bridge

The 2025 operating claims require separate tests:

- **FRE:** recurring management fees after operating expenses; test fee rate,
  FPAUM, product mix, compensation, and distribution cost.
- **TOE:** adds monetization and other realized economics; test timing,
  transaction volume, asset sales, and whether monetization can recur.
- **ANI:** adds performance and other income; test marks, realized carry,
  compensation, tax, and per-share dilution.
- **Global Atlantic:** insurance economics can diversify earnings, but the proof
  is spread after credit losses, asset/liability duration, statutory capital,
  reserves, and funding cost.
- **Private wealth and ABF:** new channels can extend the fee runway, but test
  net flows, fee rates, borrower quality, liquidity terms, and product returns.
- **Capital raised and invested:** scale is not value until new capital earns
  acceptable returns after compensation, financing, and risk capital.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering risks

### One platform, several denominators

KKR's FRE, TOE, ANI, GAAP income, insurance income, and consolidated cash are
not interchangeable. FRE is the closest recurring operating measure. TOE adds
monetization. ANI adds performance and other income. GAAP includes marks,
insurance accounting, noncontrolling interests, and consolidation. A single
premium multiple on ANI or revenue can hide a declining recurring fee share.

### Monetization timing

Management described 2025 as a record monetization year. Monetization can be a
high-return recycling mechanism, but it is also dependent on transaction
markets, valuation gaps, financing conditions, and buyer demand. The forensic
test is whether monetization revenue is repeatable after fees, carried interest,
tax, and the opportunity cost of selling assets.

### Insurance balance-sheet and liquidity burden

Global Atlantic's portfolio includes large available-for-sale debt holdings and
long-duration insurance liabilities. The filing shows substantial realized and
unrealized securities gains/losses and insurance premium/benefit flows. Higher
rates may improve reinvestment yield while reducing asset values; credit losses,
duration mismatch, policyholder behavior, and statutory capital can overwhelm a
simple spread narrative.

### Compensation, partner, and dilution claims

Share-based compensation was $722M in 2025. KKR also has carried interest,
employee ownership, noncontrolling interests, preferred stock, and strategic
holding economics. The common shareholder receives residual value only after
those claims. Capital invested and AUM can grow while per-share cash lags if
the platform issues equity or allocates economics to partners and employees.

### Consolidated cash-flow trap

The 10-K's $477.8M consolidated OCF is not a useful direct measure of fee-owner
cash because insurance investment purchases/proceeds and consolidated entities
move through the statements. Conversely, stripping out all insurance and fund
flows would also be wrong if Global Atlantic is a core permanent economic
engine. The correct answer is a component model with insurance capital and
required liquidity shown explicitly.

### Q2 forensic tests

- **Recurring fee test:** track FPAUM, fee rates, net new capital, committed
  capital that has not started paying fees, fee-related compensation, and
  per-share FRE together. AUM growth without fee-paying growth or per-share
  growth is not enough.
- **Carry test:** keep realized performance income, unrealized carry, and
  realized investment income separate from FRE. Reconcile each with the
  related compensation charge and the actual cash received.
- **Insurance test:** reconcile Insurance Operating Earnings to Total
  Insurance Economics, then separately review Global Atlantic's asset quality,
  duration, policyholder funding, reserves, liquidity, and statutory capital.
- **Capital-allocation test:** compare the `$9.299B` asset-management debt and
  `$13.926B` of asset-management cash plus investments with distributions,
  acquisitions, employee equity, and required platform investment. The debt is
  long-dated and mostly fixed, but it remains a claim before common equity.
- **Dilution test:** compare adjusted-share growth with basic shares,
  preferred conversion, time-based equity compensation, performance-based
  compensation, and noncontrolling interests. A higher ANI headline is not
  sufficient if the common claim grows more slowly.

## Alden macro/liquidity lens

KKR benefits when banks retreat, borrowers seek private credit, insurers need
asset management, and institutions seek private infrastructure and real assets.
Higher rates can support insurance reinvestment and private-credit coupons, but
they raise borrower stress, reduce exit multiples, pressure private marks, and
increase funding costs.

The platform is resilient if long-duration capital and insurance liabilities
remain stable while underwriting stays disciplined. It is fragile if liquidity
is assumed rather than earned: a period of weak exits, policyholder pressure,
credit losses, or private-wealth redemptions would test every engine at once.

## Damodaran lens

Value KKR as a component sum:

`normalized FRE + probability-weighted monetization/performance + normalized
insurance spread + wealth/ABF growth - compensation, partner, NCI, preferred,
tax, debt, required insurance capital, and liquidity claims`

The lifecycle question is whether diversification improves incremental returns
or merely expands the story. New wealth and ABF products should be assessed on
fee rates, acquisition cost, retention, risk-adjusted returns, and capital
requirements. Global Atlantic should be valued with insurance economics, not a
plain asset-manager multiple.

## Valuation and thesis breakers

At the September 11, 2026 market snapshot, KKR was approximately $101.08 per
share with an equity value near $95.579B.

| Case | Normalized owner cash / earnings proxy | Multiple | Implied equity value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | $3.5B | 20x | $70.0B |
| Base | $4.5B | 23x | $103.5B |
| Bull | $5.8B | 26x | $150.8B |

The base case is close to the market and depends on FRE compounding while
monetization, insurance, and wealth economics remain constructive.

Thesis breakers:

- FPAUM or fee rates slow while capital-raising and compensation costs rise;
- monetization revenue fades and cannot be replaced by recurring fee growth;
- Global Atlantic suffers credit, duration, reserve, liquidity, or statutory-
  capital stress;
- ABF and private-credit growth produces weak risk-adjusted returns or rising
  nonaccruals;
- partner, NCI, preferred, SBC, and dilution claims absorb per-share growth; or
- insurance and consolidated cash-flow complexity prevents recurring earnings
  from converting into common-owner cash.

These are denominator and capital-allocation questions, not allegations of
misconduct. KKR's breadth may be a moat, but it must earn a return after every
engine's burden is made visible.
