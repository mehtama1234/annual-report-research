# Korn Ferry: talent infrastructure, interim capacity, and the cost of institutional adaptation

Date baseline: 2026-09-14

## Business economics: the company sells capability outside the organization

Korn Ferry helps institutions obtain and organize talent when internal hiring,
leadership development, or recruiting systems are too slow or too narrow. Its
activities include Executive Search, Professional Search & Interim, Consulting,
Digital, and Recruitment Process Outsourcing (RPO).

The operating chain is:

`institutional capability gap -> search, advice, interim labor, or recruiting workflow -> fee revenue -> professional compensation and delivery cost -> collected cash -> reinvestment and capital returns`

This is not one uniform service. Executive Search is a relationship-led,
high-value assignment business. Professional Search & Interim adds more direct
labor capacity and can grow faster while carrying lower margins. Consulting
depends on skilled project teams and utilization. Digital and RPO create more
workflow and contract continuity but require technology, implementation, and
service delivery. The analysis must keep these products separate.

Korn Ferry's strategic control point is the institutional relationship and the
ability to combine leadership advice, talent access, interim capacity, and
recruiting operations. The company does not own the client's workforce or
guarantee the client's business outcome. Its burden is professional labor,
utilization, delivery quality, platform investment, acquisitions, and the need
to retain trusted advisers.

## Revenue quality and cash conversion

Q4 FY2026 fee revenue was approximately `$759.8M`, up `7%`, and FY2026 fee
revenue was approximately `$2.9B`. Q4 net income attributable to Korn Ferry was
approximately `$73.1M`, with a `9.6%` margin. Adjusted EBITDA was approximately
`$129.5M`, with a `17.0%` margin. Remaining fees under existing contracts were
approximately `$1.9B`, up `10%`.

The contract balance is useful evidence of work already awarded, but it is not
cash and does not establish final margin. It must be converted through delivery,
professional compensation, subcontractor or interim labor cost, implementation,
collection, and any obligation to provide future service.

The recent mix matters:

- Executive Search Q4 fee revenue was approximately `$242.0M`, up `7%`.
- Professional Search & Interim was approximately `$149.1M`, up `14%`.
- RPO was approximately `$97.6M`, up `5%`.
- Q3 remaining fees were approximately `$1.9B`, up `11%`.
- Q2 remaining fees were approximately `$1.842B`, up `20%`.

The faster growth in Professional Search & Interim is not automatically better
than Executive Search growth. Management stated that interim work has lower
margins than permanent placement. A larger contract balance can therefore
coexist with lower mix quality if the balance shifts toward labor-intensive
delivery.

The owner-cash bridge should start with operating cash flow and subtract the
cash cost of professional delivery, technology and platform investment,
acquisitions, debt, taxes, dividends, repurchases, and dilution. The analyst
should not treat adjusted EBITDA as owner cash or treat remaining fees as
backlog with a fixed margin.

## Forensic risks, accounting tests, and management incentives

The main earnings-quality risk is not necessarily fabricated revenue. It is
mix and timing: recognizing fees before delivery costs are fully visible,
using contract balances as evidence of durable demand, or presenting adjusted
profit without the recurring cost of platform retirement, integration, or
interim labor.

Test the following in each filing:

1. Reconcile fee revenue to remaining fees, bookings, assignment activity,
   delivery, collection, and contract duration.
2. Separate Executive Search, Professional Search & Interim, Consulting,
   Digital, and RPO revenue and margin.
3. Track professional compensation, utilization, consultant headcount, interim
   labor cost, subcontractor expense, and revenue per professional.
4. Test whether acquisitions add organic client relationships and cash after
   integration cost, earn-outs, debt, and dilution.
5. Reconcile adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS for restructuring, platform
   retirement, acquisition, stock compensation, and other recurring costs.
6. Review receivables, contract assets, deferred revenue, remaining fees, and
   cash collection for reversals or persistent working-capital funding.
7. Test client concentration, renewal, placement completion, consultant
   retention, and the risk that a weak hiring market reduces assignments.
8. Read the proxy for incentives tied to fee revenue, adjusted EBITDA, margin,
   cash flow, bookings, share price, or per-share returns. Volume targets can
   reward lower-quality interim work if margin and collection are not included.

The Trilogy acquisition closed on `2024-11-01` for approximately `$44.4M` and
expanded digital interim talent capability. Salo and ICS add related interim
finance, HR, and IT capacity. The acquisition test is whether those businesses
produce incremental fee cash after delivery labor, integration, and capital
claims, not whether they increase consolidated revenue.

## Lyn Alden and Damodaran lenses

The Lyn Alden lens asks how Korn Ferry behaves when institutions reduce hiring,
delay discretionary consulting, or protect only the roles tied to essential
operations. A diversified solution set can make demand more durable, but
professional compensation and technology costs can remain sticky when clients
slow spending. Higher rates can reduce acquisition capacity and lower the value
of future contract work.

The Damodaran lens requires a separate lifecycle model for each solution. Search
and consulting may have higher relationship value but depend on professional
utilization. Interim and RPO may provide more recurring workflow but carry more
direct labor and delivery obligations. Digital can support retention only if
customers renew and the platform improves client economics after technology
cost.

## Valuation: fee quality after labor and acquisition cost

Value Korn Ferry from normalized fee revenue by solution, client retention,
fee rate, professional utilization, compensation, technology investment,
acquisition returns, taxes, debt, and diluted shares. Remaining fees can inform
near-term revenue visibility, but they should be converted with a probability
for completion and a margin appropriate to the product mix.

Use normal, weak-hiring, and stronger-adaptation cases. The weak case should
reduce assignments and utilization while leaving a meaningful portion of
professional compensation and platform cost in place. The stronger case should
show whether Digital, RPO, and interim growth improve retained margin or merely
increase delivery intensity.

The current valuation should be tested against the cash return after the
company pays the people and systems required to deliver the fee. A high
multiple is not justified by contract visibility if remaining fees shift toward
lower-margin work or if acquisitions are required to prevent organic decline.

### Initial common-owner cash screen

FY2025 operating cash flow was `$364.4M`. After PP&E spending of `$62.5M` and
the `$44.4M` Trilogy acquisition, the residual was about `$257.4M`. After
`$48.0M` of stock compensation, the owner-cash screen is about `$209.5M`.
The comparable after-PP&E-and-acquisition residuals after stock compensation
were about negative `$17.5M` in FY2023, `$188.8M` in FY2024, and `$209.5M` in
FY2025. That history makes utilization, collections, mix, and acquisition
normalization more important than the latest adjusted margin.

At the 2026-09-14 market snapshot, Korn Ferry's equity value was about
`$4.025B`. An initial screen uses bear/base/bull normalized owner cash of
`$150M`/`$220M`/`$300M` at `15x`/`20x`/`25x`, implying approximately
`$2.250B`/`$4.400B`/`$7.500B`. These are scenario inputs, not price targets.
The base case requires the company to sustain cash near or above FY2025 while
converting remaining fees without allowing interim labor, platform spending,
working capital, or acquisitions to consume the economics.

## What to check next

The next filing should show fee revenue and margin by solution, organic growth,
remaining fees, bookings, client retention, consultant utilization,
professional compensation, interim labor cost, RPO renewal, Digital revenue,
receivables, contract assets, acquisitions, integration cost, debt, stock
compensation, repurchases, dividends, and diluted shares.

The thesis strengthens if growth is broad across solutions, remaining fees
convert into collected cash at stable or improving margins, acquired businesses
become self-funded, and cash per diluted share improves. It weakens if growth
depends on acquisitions, interim volume expands while margin falls, remaining
fees fail to convert, utilization drops, or adjusted results exclude recurring
delivery and platform costs.
