# Loews Corporation: patient capital, subsidiary mix, and holding-company value

## What the company does

Loews is a diversified holding company whose major subsidiaries include CNA
Financial, Boardwalk Pipelines, Loews Hotels, and Altium Packaging. The parent
allocates capital among unlike operating businesses, receives dividends or other
cash from subsidiaries, carries parent-level assets and obligations, and decides
when to reinvest, acquire, repurchase shares, or retain liquidity.

The control point is not one customer product. It is the allocation system and
the ability to own businesses with different cycles under one parent. The
potential benefit is resilience and patient capital. The burden is complexity:
subsidiary cash may be trapped, the parent may not control every operating
decision, business results may move in opposite directions, and the market may
apply a holding-company discount.

FY2025 net income attributable to Loews was approximately `$1.7B`, or roughly
`$7.97` per diluted share. Q4 2025 net income was approximately `$402M`, Q1 2026
approximately `$337M`, and Q2 approximately `$444M`. Six-month 2026 net income
was approximately `$781M`, or `$3.79` per share.

The quarter sequence demonstrates the model's central feature: diversification
does not produce smooth earnings. It can reduce dependence on one industry, but
the parent still depends on CNA insurance, pipeline regulation and volumes,
hotel demand, packaging demand, subsidiary capital, and management allocation.

## Where the economics sit

### Parent allocation and ownership

Loews can retain cash, support a subsidiary, repurchase shares, or wait for an
opportunity. This flexibility is valuable only if management allocates capital
at attractive risk-adjusted returns and does not let parent cash become a
permanent low-return asset.

Track parent cash, debt, interest, dividends received, capital contributions,
subsidiary restrictions, share repurchases, acquisitions, and per-share book or
intrinsic value. The parent cannot spend every dollar of consolidated operating
cash if subsidiaries need capital to meet claims, maintain assets, or comply with
regulation.

### CNA insurance

CNA supplies insurance underwriting and investment economics. It carries claims,
reserve uncertainty, catastrophe and reinsurance risk, invested assets, and
regulatory capital. CNA may be the largest or most important subsidiary, so its
results can dominate Loews despite the broader corporate label.

Analyze premiums, combined ratio, current accident-year losses, prior-year
reserve development, investment income, capital, dividends to the parent, and
catastrophe exposure. A holding-company valuation must not count CNA earnings as
fully available until subsidiary capital and regulatory constraints are covered.

### Boardwalk Pipelines

Boardwalk adds energy infrastructure and contracted or regulated pipeline
economics. It carries maintenance capital, operating reliability, volume and
counterparty risk, leverage, regulation, and possible commodity or contract
exposure.

Track throughput, contract terms, customer credit, tariff or regulatory status,
maintenance and growth capital, debt service, outages, and cash remitted to
Loews. Pipeline EBITDA is not parent cash unless sustaining capital and debt are
funded first.

### Loews Hotels

Hotels expose the group to travel demand, occupancy, average daily rate, labor,
food, insurance, renovation, and property capital. Hotel results can diversify
insurance or pipeline cycles, but they are more sensitive to economic activity
and discretionary travel.

Track occupancy, rate, RevPAR, group and business mix, labor cost, property
renovation, insurance, debt, and cash after maintenance capital. A strong revenue
quarter can still produce weak owner cash if renovation or labor costs rise.

### Altium Packaging

Packaging adds manufacturing and industrial demand. It carries resin and input
cost, volume, pricing, labor, plant maintenance, inventory, customer
concentration, and leverage or acquisition risk.

Track price versus volume, input pass-through, gross margin, inventory, receivables,
maintenance capital, capacity utilization, customer retention, debt, and cash
remitted to the parent. Consolidated diversity is useful only if each subsidiary
earns an adequate return after its own capital burden.

## Earnings quality and forensic review

### Consolidated net income is a starting point

Loews reported approximately `$1.7B` of FY2025 net income and approximately
`$781M` of six-month 2026 net income. These figures combine businesses with
different accounting, capital, and cash-flow systems.

Build a subsidiary-level bridge from revenue to operating income, taxes,
minority interests, parent costs, dividends, capital spending, debt, and cash
available to Loews. Separate realized investment gains, insurance reserve
movements, asset sales, impairments, and one-time items.

### CNA and reserve timing

Insurance earnings can rise because underwriting improves, investment yield
improves, or prior reserves develop favorably. Those explanations have different
durability. Read CNA's current accident-year results and reserve development by
line and accident year, then test capital and dividend capacity.

### Subsidiary cash versus parent cash

Consolidated operating cash can overstate the amount available for parent use.
Subsidiaries need capital for insurance claims, pipeline maintenance, hotel
renovation, packaging plants, debt, and working capital. Dividends received by
the parent are the more direct owner-cash input, but even those can be cyclical or
restricted.

Reconcile:

`subsidiary operating cash - subsidiary maintenance capital - debt and claims - required subsidiary capital = cash available to parent`

Then subtract parent interest, overhead, taxes, acquisitions, and repurchases.

### Holding-company discount and per-share value

A diversified structure can trade below the sum of subsidiary values because of
tax, overhead, capital-trapping, governance, and complexity concerns. It can
also trade above book value if the market trusts management's allocation record.

Track subsidiary market values or valuation proxies, debt, parent cash,
noncontrolling interests, taxes on realization, and share count. Test whether
repurchases occur below a conservative estimate of look-through value rather
than merely below accounting book value.

## Balance-sheet resilience and macro conditions

The Lyn Alden lens follows rates, insurance losses, energy infrastructure,
travel, manufacturing demand, debt, and liquidity. Loews benefits from unlike
subsidiary cycles, but it is exposed to a compound stress in which CNA claims,
pipeline cash, hotel demand, and packaging margins weaken together.

Stress cases should include:

- adverse CNA reserve and catastrophe development;
- insurance asset and capital-market losses;
- pipeline volume, customer, regulatory, or outage pressure;
- hotel occupancy, rate, labor, and renovation weakness;
- packaging input-cost, volume, inventory, and customer stress;
- subsidiary dividend restrictions;
- parent debt and liquidity pressure;
- acquisitions at high prices or repurchases during a downturn;
- holding-company discount widening as complexity rises.

Permanent capital is useful when liquidity is scarce. It is not automatically
productive if management retains cash without a high-return use or supports a
subsidiary whose economics are deteriorating.

## Damodaran lens: turn diversification into numbers

The narrative is that Loews can compound through patient ownership, subsidiary
diversification, conservative financing, and disciplined capital allocation. That
narrative requires:

1. CNA produces durable underwriting and investment earnings after reserves and
   required capital.
2. Boardwalk generates cash after maintenance capital, debt, regulation, and
   customer obligations.
3. Hotels and packaging earn acceptable returns through their operating cycles.
4. Subsidiaries can upstream cash without weakening their own resilience.
5. Parent costs, taxes, and capital allocation do not consume the diversification
   benefit.
6. Repurchases occur below look-through value and improve per-share economics.
7. Management avoids overpaying for acquisitions or retaining excessive idle cash.

Value Loews by sum of the parts: CNA normalized earnings and capital, Boardwalk
cash after maintenance and debt, hotel and packaging operating value, parent cash
and debt, taxes, minority claims, and a discount or premium for allocation skill.
Do not apply a single operating-company multiple to consolidated net income.

## Valuation screen

A market snapshot at 23:15 UTC on 2026-09-14 showed an equity value of
approximately `$22.448B`; the local packet does not provide a complete
subsidiary financial bridge. These are normalized earnings sensitivities, not a
price target.

| Case | Normalized net income | Illustrative multiple | Implied equity value | What must be true |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Downside | `$1.2B` | `10x` | `$12.0B` | CNA or operating subsidiaries weaken and the holding discount widens |
| Base | `$1.8B` | `13x` | `$23.4B` | Subsidiaries remain healthy and parent allocation is disciplined |
| Upside | `$2.4B` | `16x` | `$38.4B` | Insurance, pipelines, hotels, packaging, and repurchases compound value |

The final valuation must be sum-of-the-parts based and checked against parent
liquidity, subsidiary capital, debt, taxes, cash remittance, and share count.
The current market value is slightly below the illustrative base case. That
comparison is not a margin-of-safety conclusion: the base requires CNA, Boardwalk,
hotels, and packaging to remain healthy while subsidiary capital, maintenance,
debt, taxes, trapped cash, and parent costs remain funded.

## Thesis breakers and filing tests

| Question | Evidence supporting the thesis | Evidence weakening it |
|---|---|---|
| Is diversification additive? | Subsidiaries produce independent cash and acceptable returns | Weak businesses absorb capital and offset stronger subsidiaries |
| Is CNA healthy? | Underwriting, reserves, investments, and parent dividends remain sound | Reserve deterioration, catastrophe losses, or capital restrictions rise |
| Is subsidiary cash distributable? | Dividends reach the parent after maintenance, debt, and required capital | Cash remains trapped or parent funding needs rise |
| Is allocation disciplined? | Repurchases occur below look-through value and acquisitions earn returns | Overpayment, idle cash, or repurchases at high value destroy per-share returns |
| Is leverage controlled? | Parent and subsidiary debt remain manageable under stress | Refinancing or covenant pressure reduces flexibility |
| Is reporting transparent? | Subsidiary earnings, cash, capital, and parent costs reconcile | Consolidated income hides recurring losses, restrictions, or one-time gains |

## Bottom line

Loews controls a patient-capital holding-company structure across insurance,
pipelines, hotels, and packaging. Its cleaner feature is the ability to allocate
capital across different cycles without needing one subsidiary to be perfect.
Its burden is that diversity creates a harder denominator: subsidiary capital,
maintenance, regulation, debt, taxes, minority claims, and parent liquidity all
stand between reported earnings and common-owner cash.

FY2025 and Q2 2026 show strong net income and continued diversified earnings, but
the quarter variation confirms that resilience is not smoothness. The next proof
is subsidiary-level cash, CNA reserve and capital quality, parent liquidity,
look-through value, and whether repurchases improve value per share.

**Evidence grade:** Strong for FY2025 and Q1-Q2 2026 reported net income and EPS;
medium for subsidiary diversification and parent allocation; unresolved for
subsidiary cash remittance, CNA reserve quality, holding-company discount, and
through-cycle capital allocation.
