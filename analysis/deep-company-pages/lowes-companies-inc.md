# Lowe's Companies, Inc. — home-improvement workflow, Pro relationships, and owner cash

Date baseline: 2026-09-14  
Primary evidence: [Lowe's source ledger](../../extracted/consumer-goods/home-improvement-stores/lowes-companies-inc/source-ledger.md) and [Lowe's company packet](../../extracted/consumer-goods/home-improvement-stores/lowes-companies-inc/company-packet.md)

Current filing checkpoint: [Lowe's Q2 2026 results](https://corporate.lowes.com/newsroom/press-releases/lowes-reports-second-quarter-2026-sales-and-earnings-results-08-19-26) and [Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/60667/000006066726000117/low-20260731.htm)

## Investment question

Lowe's is not only a chain of stores selling home-improvement products. Its
strategy is to increase Pro penetration, online sales, home services, loyalty,
and space productivity. The customer relationship can therefore become a
repeat project workflow involving product selection, contractor supply,
delivery, installation, and service.

The investment question is whether this broader relationship produces durable
owner cash after stores, inventory, labor, delivery, project timing, housing
conditions, acquisitions, and maintenance capital. The archive has to separate
DIY traffic from Pro demand, ordinary merchandise sales from services, and
reported earnings from cash available after the network is maintained.

## What Lowe's controls

The control sequence is:

`home repair or improvement need -> product discovery and advice -> purchase
or project order -> delivery, installation, or service -> repeat household or
Pro relationship -> loyalty and digital data -> owner cash after stores and
inventory investment`

Lowe's controls a national store network, digital ordering, distribution,
product assortment, supplier access, Pro account relationships, loyalty data,
and home-services connections. It does not control the full outcome of a
construction or remodeling project. Contractors, installers, manufacturers,
carriers, and homeowners carry material execution risk.

The value of the platform is higher when the customer needs project advice,
reliable availability, delivery, and repeat supply rather than a one-time
commodity purchase. The burden is also higher because larger projects create
delivery, damage, return, installation, and service obligations.

## Operating evidence

Fiscal 2025 net sales were `$86.3B`, up `3.1%`; comparable sales increased
`0.2%`; net earnings declined `4.4%` to `$6.7B`; and diluted EPS declined
`3.1%` to `$11.85`.

| Period | Sales and demand | Operating mix | Forensic significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 2025 | Sales `$20.8B`; comparable sales `+0.4%`; diluted EPS `$2.88`; adjusted EPS `$3.06` | Online sales `+11.4%`; home services grew double digits; Pro sales grew | Shows mix improvement while project demand remained constrained; adjusted EPS needs reconciliation |
| Q4 2025 | Sales `$20.6B`; comparable sales `+1.3%`; diluted EPS `$1.78` | Pro, online, home services, and holiday demand supported growth | Includes `$149M` of pre-tax FBM and ADG acquisition expenses |
| Q1 2026 | Sales `$23.1B`; comparable sales `+0.6%` | Online sales `+15.5%`; appliances, home services, and Pro were strong | Tests whether spring demand and digital growth persist after seasonal working capital and promotion |

The company operates more than `1,750` home-improvement stores, `540` branches,
and `120` distribution centers according to the Q1 materials. That footprint
supports availability and Pro service, but it creates fixed labor, rent,
maintenance, inventory, and distribution obligations.

## Q2 2026 checkpoint: Pro and services offset weak DIY, but the hurdle fell

Q2 sales were `$26.0B`, comparable sales increased only 0.2%, and online sales
rose 15.7%. Management attributed the positive comparable result to Pro and
home-services sales while persistent pressure remained in discretionary DIY.
That is a useful mix signal, but it does not yet prove that the larger Pro and
service relationship earns more after delivery, credit, labor, installation,
and warranty costs.

The accounting bridge remains material. Reported diluted EPS was `$4.27` and
adjusted diluted EPS was `$4.40`; the adjustment included `$96M` of acquisition
expenses tied to FBM and ADG. Both numbers included an `$0.11` benefit from
IEEPA tariff refunds. The 10-Q separately says Lowe's recognized approximately
`$80M` of tariff refunds in Q2 and that future collections remain uncertain.
Those amounts should not be treated as evidence of durable Pro economics.

For the first six months, operating cash flow was `$7.009B` versus `$7.610B` in
the prior-year period, and capital spending was `$1.063B` versus `$1.013B`.
The company repaid `$2.397B` of debt and spent `$366M` on share repurchases.
Lowe's also lowered its FY2026 outlook to `$92.0B` of sales, flat comparable
sales, 11.2% reported operating margin, and approximately `$11.75` diluted EPS.
The current evidence therefore supports improving Pro and service mix, but the
return on the acquisition and the resilience of owner cash remain unproven.

## Three-year cash bridge and valuation screen

The FY2025 Form 10-K reports operating cash flow of `$9.864B`, `$9.625B`, and
`$8.140B` for fiscal 2025, 2024, and 2023. Capital expenditures were `$2.213B`,
`$1.927B`, and `$1.964B`, producing simple cash after capex of `$7.651B`,
`$7.698B`, and `$6.176B`.

The 2025 number needs a major qualification. Lowe's says merchandise-inventory
changes from inventory optimization were a primary driver of the increase in
operating cash flow. It also spent `$10.088B` on the acquisitions of ADG and
FBM, financed partly with `$6.974B` of new debt. A recurring-retail cash screen
that ignores those acquisitions overstates cash available for capital return;
a screen that treats the full acquisition as ordinary maintenance spending may
understate the potential value of a successful Pro expansion. The correct
answer requires an acquisition-return test in subsequent filings.

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Capital expenditures | Cash after capex | Acquisition cash |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | `$8.140B` | `$1.964B` | `$6.176B` | — |
| 2024 | `$9.625B` | `$1.927B` | `$7.698B` | — |
| 2025 | `$9.864B` | `$2.213B` | `$7.651B` | `$10.088B` |

At the September 14, 2026 market snapshot, Lowe's equity value was about
`$110.2B`. That is approximately `14.4x` the simple FY2025 cash-after-capex
figure, before considering acquisition debt and integration. A skeptical
normalized screen uses bear, base, and bull common-owner cash of `$4.5B`,
`$6.5B`, and `$8.0B` at `12x`, `16x`, and `20x`. The base case is only useful if
ADG and FBM produce incremental cash after interest, integration, branches,
inventory, and service support rather than merely increasing sales.

## Where the economics sit

### DIY customer

DIY demand includes maintenance, repair, seasonal projects, and discretionary
improvement. Essential repair can remain resilient while large remodels are
deferred because of mortgage rates, home prices, labor availability, or
household liquidity.

The analyst should separate transaction count, average ticket, project mix,
promotion, and gross margin. Sales growth caused by price or mix is not the
same as volume growth or higher customer frequency.

### Pro customer

Pro customers can purchase more frequently and require product availability,
credit, delivery, job-site coordination, and account support. A strong Pro
relationship may create repeat demand even when casual DIY traffic is weak.

It also carries customer-credit, contractor-health, delivery, and pricing
risks. Lowe's must demonstrate that Pro growth produces adequate gross profit
after account service, delivery, discounts, receivables, and branch investment.

### Home services and installation

Services can deepen the relationship and improve conversion on appliances or
larger projects. They also add installer availability, warranty, rework,
claims, customer satisfaction, and legal exposure. Reported sales from a
service attachment should be evaluated after the cost of fulfilling the
service, not only at the point of product sale.

### Acquisitions

The packet identifies FBM and ADG acquisition-related pre-tax costs of `$149M`
in Q4 2025. The acquisition thesis needs a return calculation based on
incremental sales, gross margin, customer retention, integration expense,
working capital, branch and distribution investment, and per-share cash. An
acquisition that adds sales but lowers normalized return on capital is not
automatically value creating.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering risks

### Comparable sales are not cash

Comparable sales of `0.2%` for fiscal 2025 and `0.6%` in Q1 2026 show modest
positive demand, but they do not show volume, price, project mix, promotion,
or cash conversion. A higher average ticket can coexist with lower customer
traffic or weaker project frequency.

### Adjusted EPS and acquisition costs

Q3 2025 adjusted diluted EPS was `$3.06` versus reported diluted EPS of `$2.88`.
The difference requires a recurrence test. Acquisition costs may be genuinely
nonrecurring, but integration, retention, rebranding, technology, and branch
investment may continue after the stated charge ends.

### Inventory and supplier terms

Home improvement requires broad inventory and seasonal availability. Operating
cash flow can improve when inventory is reduced or supplier payables increase.
The next filing should compare inventory units and dollars, turns, in-stock
rates, markdowns, accounts payable, supplier terms, and gross margin.

### Capital spending and store productivity

Online, distribution, delivery, and home-services growth can require more
capital even when the company describes the business as asset productive. The
owner-cash bridge must separate maintenance store spending, distribution
capacity, technology, acquisitions, and growth projects. Deferring store
maintenance can raise current cash while lowering future sales and customer
trust.

### Buybacks and per-share economics

Home-improvement retailers often return capital through buybacks. The relevant
test is cash per diluted share after maintenance investment and debt, not the
number of shares repurchased in isolation. Compare stock compensation, debt,
lease obligations, and repurchase price with normalized cash generation.

## Lyn Alden lens

Lowe's sits between essential repair and discretionary housing improvement.
Household liquidity, interest rates, home turnover, housing affordability,
weather, contractor employment, and material inflation affect demand. A
national store and distribution network can support resilience, but it also
requires liquidity through slow project cycles.

Pro relationships may make the business more durable because contractors need
availability and reliable supply. They can also transmit stress quickly when
housing activity, construction credit, or contractor cash flow weakens.

The balance-sheet question is whether Lowe's can maintain inventory and
distribution service while protecting price and availability during a housing
slowdown. Cash returns should be judged after the network's maintenance and
after acquisition integration needs are funded.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers and valuation

The narrative becomes:

`households and Pros × projects or transactions × average ticket × gross margin
-> store, labor, distribution, delivery, service, and technology costs ->
operating income -> tax, working capital, maintenance capex, acquisitions,
debt, leases, dilution -> owner cash per share`

The valuation must separately test:

1. mature store economics and comparable-sales normalization;
2. Pro penetration and repeat account economics;
3. online conversion and fulfillment cost;
4. home-services revenue after installation and warranty claims;
5. acquisition returns after integration and capital needs; and
6. housing-cycle sensitivity and balance-sheet resilience.

The current market expectation is not just that sales recover. It is that Pro,
online, services, and loyalty produce a better mix and higher return on the
existing network without requiring excessive capital. That expectation should
be compared with normalized owner cash, not with adjusted EPS alone.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Management is incentivized to emphasize comparable sales, adjusted EPS, Pro
growth, online sales, services, and strategic acquisitions. Those indicators
should be tested against traffic, volume, margin, inventory, receivables,
acquisition cost, capex, debt, and diluted shares.

The thesis breaks if:

- Pro growth requires discounts, receivables, delivery, or branch investment
  that consume the additional gross profit;
- online growth raises fulfillment, return, damage, or delivery cost faster
  than retained profit;
- home-services growth increases claims, rework, installer cost, or customer
  dissatisfaction;
- acquisition expenses recur through integration, retention, or impairment;
- comparable-sales growth is mostly price or mix while traffic and project
  frequency weaken;
- operating cash improves from inventory liquidation or extended supplier
  terms and later reverses; or
- buybacks raise EPS while owner cash per diluted share and return on capital
  remain flat.

## Next-filing evidence ladder

| Question | Confirming evidence | Falsifying evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is Pro a stronger relationship? | Repeat Pro sales, account retention, availability, delivery contribution, and stable credit losses improve | Pro sales rise through discounts and receivables while contribution or contractor health weakens |
| Is online growth profitable? | Online growth improves conversion and contribution after fulfillment, returns, and delivery | Digital sales require rising subsidies, delivery cost, or inventory complexity |
| Do services add value? | Installation attachment and gross profit rise while claims and rework remain controlled | Service sales grow but warranty, labor, or customer-support costs absorb the benefit |
| Are acquisitions value creating? | Incremental cash earnings exceed integration and capital cost on a per-share basis | Sales grow but acquisition charges, goodwill, dilution, or working capital rise faster |
| Is owner cash durable? | Cash remains strong after inventory normalization, maintenance capex, debt, leases, and buybacks | Cash depends on working-capital release or deferred store and technology investment |

## Bottom line

Lowe's is a home-improvement workflow and distribution relationship, not only a
store chain. Pro, online, home services, and loyalty can improve the economics
of the relationship, but the evidence remains conditional because the network
has substantial inventory, labor, distribution, project, and maintenance
burdens. The next filings must prove that mix improvement becomes durable cash
per share after acquisitions and the housing cycle are treated honestly.
