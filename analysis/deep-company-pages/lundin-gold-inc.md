# Lundin Gold — concentrated high-grade mine, extraordinary cash, and one-asset risk

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed IFRS figures in U.S. dollars; dollar
amounts are in millions unless otherwise stated.

## How to read this memo

Fruta del Norte is Lundin Gold’s main operating mine. Cash operating cost is a
site-cost measure; all-in sustaining cost, or AISC, adds sustaining capital and
other costs needed to keep production operating. Sustaining capital is spending
to maintain the existing mine. Non-sustaining capital funds growth, exploration,
or expansions. Free cash flow is management’s operating-cash measure after
investing cash outflows; it is not automatically safe to distribute if the mine
needs future expansion or closure spending.

## Investment question

Lundin Gold owns a concentrated, high-grade gold operation in Ecuador. A single
high-quality mine can produce cleaner economics than a large portfolio if its
costs remain low and its balance sheet stays strong. The central question is
whether Fruta del Norte can fund dividends, exploration, underground development,
tailings, and mine-life extension while Ecuadorian taxes, employee profit
sharing, royalties, country risk, and single-asset concentration are fully
included.

The free-cash-flow screen was approximately $304M in 2024 and $926M in 2025;
2023 was much lower during the early operating period. The 2025 jump was driven
primarily by a much higher realized gold price, not only by improved operations.
For every $100 per ounce increase in gold price, management estimated that
royalties and statutory profit sharing raise unit costs by about $10 per ounce.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Fruta del Norte produced 498,315 ounces in 2025 and sold 503,330 ounces. The
  operation averaged more than 5,000 tonnes per day through the mill after the
  process-plant expansion. Throughput and recovery must continue to be tested
  against grades and underground access.
- AISC of $1,015 per ounce was far below the $3,594 realized gold price. This
  created exceptional cash, but the margin is not a safe long-term assumption.
  The 2025 guidance used a $2,500 gold-price assumption.
- The company paid $664M to shareholders while free cash flow was $926M. The
  payout leaves less room for FDNS underground development, tailings, district
  exploration, and mine-to-mill expansion if gold prices decline.
- FDNS, FDN East, Sandia, Trancaloma, and Castillo broaden the district story.
  They are options requiring drilling, studies, permits, capital, and execution;
  they are not current reserves that can be valued at full production.
- Ecuador’s corporate tax, government profit sharing, employee profit sharing,
  royalties, withholding tax, local procurement, and community agreements are
  part of the actual owner-cash burden. A rising gold price increases some of
  these payments rather than flowing entirely to shareholders.
- Q1 and Q2 2026 show the cash timing risk. Q1 free cash flow was about $349M
  and quarter-end cash was $704M; after annual tax and profit-sharing payments
  plus distributions, Q2 cash fell to about $507M despite a higher gold price.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. How much of the margin is gold price?

Recalculate cash operating cost, AISC, tax, profit sharing, and free cash flow
at $2,000, $2,500, and $3,000 gold. Separate price, grade, recovery, tonnes,
cost inflation, and government or employee claims. A high current margin is not
evidence of a permanent low-cost business.

### 2. Is free cash flow sufficient to renew the mine?

Separate sustaining capital, non-sustaining capital, exploration, FDNS
development, tailings, closure, and mine-to-mill expansion. Compare the cash
returned with the capital required to preserve production and extend mine life.
Variable dividends can be economically aggressive if growth spending is deferred.

### 3. Does the single asset justify the concentration?

Track monthly production, grades, recovery, mill availability, underground
development, safety, labor, water, tailings, and transport. A diversified miner
can absorb one site’s problem; Lundin Gold cannot do so without a material
company-wide effect.

### 4. Are district projects real options or repeated spending?

For FDNS and each exploration target, record drilling, resource conversion,
capital spent, expected ounces, schedule, probability of development, and
incremental cash return. Do not assign full value to a target before engineering,
permitting, and financing demonstrate a viable project.

### 5. Can Ecuador relations remain stable?

Read tax changes, profit-sharing calculations, government payments, community
agreements, local hiring, security, environmental approvals, and water or
tailings obligations. The company’s operating permission is an economic asset
that must be renewed through performance and trust.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** gold can perform well during monetary and fiscal stress,
  but the mine remains exposed to energy, labor, equipment, currency, and
  country inflation. Ecuador risk matters even when the gold price is strong.
- **Damodaran lens:** value Fruta del Norte’s producing cash separately from
  FDNS, exploration targets, and expansion options. Use mid-cycle gold prices,
  explicit sustaining capital, mine-life assumptions, and probability-weighted
  project value.
- **Forensic lens:** the main category errors are treating AISC as full economic
  cost, current gold prices as permanent, free cash flow as surplus after mine
  renewal, reserves as production, and variable dividends as proof of durable
  owner cash.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 TSX price was approximately C$93 per share. Using about 241M
shares and an approximate CAD/USD conversion, equity value was roughly US$16.3B.
That is about 18x FY2025 free cash flow of $926M. A normalized range of $700M /
$1.0B / $1.3B at 15x / 18x / 22x implies $10.5B / $18.0B / $28.6B. The market
therefore requires sustained high mine margins and credible FDNS or district
growth, not merely another year at the 2025 gold price.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Gold prices decline while royalties, tax, and employee profit-sharing costs
   remain high relative to production.
2. Fruta del Norte production, grade, recovery, or mill availability falls.
3. FDNS or the mine-to-mill expansion requires more capital or takes longer than
   expected.
4. Ecuador changes taxes, royalties, security requirements, or operating terms.
5. Tailings, water, environmental, labor, or community issues interrupt output.
6. Dividends consume cash needed for mine renewal and leave the company exposed
   to a single-asset downturn.
