# LyondellBasell — polyolefin spreads, feedstock advantage, and footprint repair

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed U.S. GAAP figures in U.S. dollars;
dollar amounts are in millions unless otherwise stated.

## How to read this memo

Polyolefins are plastics such as polyethylene and polypropylene made from
petrochemical feedstocks. A feedstock advantage means that the company can buy
key inputs at a lower cost than a competing region. Utilization is production
divided by available capacity. A co-product is a secondary product made in the
same process whose price can change total plant economics. EBITDA excludes
interest, tax, depreciation, and selected items; it is not owner cash. A cash
improvement plan is valuable only after its savings survive the cost of closures,
working-capital changes, and lost production.

## Investment question

LyondellBasell operates a global polyolefin and petrochemical network. Its
potential advantage is North American feedstock, integrated assets, scale,
commercial reach, and the ability to shift activity toward advantaged regions.
The central question is whether that position generates durable owner cash after
spread cycles, European footprint losses, plant renewal, working capital, debt,
dividends, and the Cash Improvement Plan.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- LyondellBasell is primarily a spread and cost-curve business. North American
  feedstock can support margins, but it does not eliminate global oversupply,
  demand weakness, or regional outages.
- The O&P Americas and International businesses should be separated by
  feedstock, geography, utilization, product, and joint-venture contribution.
  A consolidated EBITDA recovery can hide a weaker region or a temporary
  co-product benefit.
- The 2025 base year was difficult: revenue was $30.153B, GAAP loss was $738M,
  and gross debt was not offset by the $3.443B cash balance enough to remove
  refinancing and plant-renewal risk.
- The Cash Improvement Plan delivered $800M in 2025, above the original $600M
  target, with a cumulative target of $1.3B by the end of 2026. The test is cash
  after implementation, site exits, environmental work, and lost volume.
- Four European asset divestitures completed in Q2 2026 may reduce exposure to
  high-cost production. The analysis must include proceeds, taxes, stranded
  overhead, lost cash, and the economics of the remaining footprint.
- Q2 2026 was strong, with $752M operating cash flow and $270M capex in the
  quarter, but geopolitical supply disruption and tighter margins contributed.
  A disrupted quarter should not be treated as the mid-cycle base without a
  normal supply and price test.
- Dividends and repurchases remain claims on a business whose required plant
  capital is large. Capital returns are evidence of discipline only if they
  remain covered through a weak spread period.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. What is the mid-cycle spread?

Rebuild product prices, feedstock, energy, co-product revenue, freight,
utilization, and fixed costs by region. Separate management pricing actions from
market-wide spread movements. Test earnings after global capacity additions and
normal demand.

### 2. Does cash conversion depend on timing?

Bridge adjusted EBITDA to operating cash flow. Track inventory, receivables,
payables, customer advances, joint-venture distributions, tax, interest, and
working-capital releases. Management's 95% cash-conversion statement needs to
be reconciled to the filed cash flow and the capital required to keep plants
available.

### 3. Are footprint exits value creating?

For each European asset, record sale proceeds, lost EBITDA, closure costs,
environmental obligations, tax, stranded corporate expense, and avoided capital.
The remaining North American advantage must be measured after the complete exit
cost, not only after the announced savings.

### 4. Is the Cash Improvement Plan recurring?

Track the source of each dollar: procurement, fixed-cost removal, inventory,
receivable collections, price, volume, asset sale, or working-capital timing.
Label each as recurring, reversible, or claim-bound. A plan that produces cash by
reducing inventory or delaying maintenance is not equivalent to structural
margin improvement.

### 5. Are joint ventures and co-products fully available?

Identify the ownership share, debt, distributions, retained cash, and capital
requirements of each material joint venture. Separate co-product price benefits
and derivative effects from the cash generated by the main polymer business.

### 6. Are dividends and buybacks safe?

Compare shareholder returns with mid-cycle cash after sustaining capital,
environmental work, debt, pension, and project commitments. Test whether
repurchases occur when the stock is cheap relative to normalized owner cash,
not merely when a temporary spread peak produces excess cash.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** LyondellBasell links energy geography, trade, industrial
  demand, and geopolitics. Feedstock advantage can improve resilience, but high
  fixed costs, debt, and global excess capacity make the balance sheet sensitive
  to rates and prolonged weak spreads.
- **Damodaran lens:** value each regional and product business using mid-cycle
  spreads, utilization, feedstock, maintenance capital, and the probability-
  weighted result of footprint changes. Treat the Cash Improvement Plan as an
  explicit operating and investment forecast, not an automatic multiple uplift.
- **Forensic lens:** the main risks are treating adjusted EBITDA as common cash,
  treating working-capital release as recurring, treating asset-sale proceeds as
  operating improvement, and treating a feedstock advantage as permanent
  pricing power.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $20.57B of equity value at
$63.69 per share. Against the FY2025 $384M OCF-less-PP&E screen, the equity
value is about 53.6x. A normalized cash range of $1.0B / $1.75B / $2.5B at 10x /
12x / 15x implies $10B / $21B / $37.5B. The current value requires a sustained
cash recovery and assumes the cost plan and portfolio exits improve the cycle-
normalized denominator.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Global polyolefin oversupply keeps spreads below the level needed to support
   plant renewal.
2. Feedstock advantage is offset by low utilization, outages, freight, or
   regional demand weakness.
3. Cash Improvement Plan savings depend on inventory release, delayed capex, or
   asset-sale proceeds.
4. European exits create stranded costs or remove cash without improving returns.
5. Joint-venture distributions or co-product benefits reverse.
6. Dividends and buybacks continue while post-capex cash remains weak.
