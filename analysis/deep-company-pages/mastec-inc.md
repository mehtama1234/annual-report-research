## Bottom line

MasTec is a diversified infrastructure-execution business spanning communications,
clean energy and infrastructure, power delivery, and pipelines. It is a useful
physical-infrastructure test of the AI/electrification narrative because it converts
customer capex, permits, rights-of-way, equipment, and skilled labor into installed
infrastructure. Its scale and diversification reduce single-lane risk, but they do
not remove contractor risk: fixed-price estimates, customer concentration, claims,
labor, policy timing, and acquisition integration determine whether backlog becomes
owner cash.

The current evidence supports strong demand, not yet clean economics. FY2025
revenue was about $14.3B, net income $422M, adjusted EBITDA about $1.2B, and
18-month backlog $19.0B. Q2 2026 backlog reached $21.391B, while Q2 revenue was
$4.374B and six-month revenue $8.202B. The more important contradiction is cash:
six-month 2026 operating cash flow was only about $120.3M against $188.3M of
capital expenditures, a negative $68.0M pre-acquisition owner-cash bridge. Debt
obligations were $2.753B at June 30, 2026, including $2.574B of long-term debt.

Current judgment: MasTec is a high-relevance physical-capacity case and a stronger
diversification comparison to Sterling, but its backlog must be discounted until
contract assets, receivables, customer advances, project margin, acquisitions, and
cash conversion reconcile over a full cycle.

## What the company controls

The operating chain is:

`customer capex / policy support -> award -> estimate and contract -> crews, fleet, materials and permits -> measured progress -> billing / collection -> repeat work`

MasTec's control point is not merely labor supply. It is the coordination of large
field organizations across communications, grid and power delivery, pipelines, and
renewables. The 2025 annual materials say the company built more than 15,000 miles
of telecommunications networks, laid more than 1,000 miles of oil and gas
pipelines, installed more than 14,000 miles of transmission and distribution lines,
and installed more than 3.6 GW of renewable energy.

That breadth creates a useful portfolio exposure to AI power demand, grid hardening,
telecom, renewable buildout, and pipeline work. It also creates a harder analytical
problem than a single-segment contractor: mix can improve reported margins while
the underlying economics of a weaker segment remain hidden.

## Reported growth and latest-quarter bridge

The 2025 packet reports revenue up 16% to $14.3B and net income up 112% to $422M.
Q2 2026 revenue was $4.374B versus $3.545B in the prior-year quarter; six-month
revenue was $8.202B versus $6.392B. Q2 operating income was $226.2M and net income
attributable to MasTec was $130.1M. Six-month operating income was $368.0M and
attributable net income $191.0M.

The segment table shows six-month 2026 revenue of $1.691B in Communications,
$2.952B in Clean Energy and Infrastructure, $2.292B in Power Delivery, and
$1.325B in Pipeline Infrastructure. Six-month segment EBITDA was $779.3M before
corporate and eliminations, versus $534.3M in the comparable 2025 period. This is
good evidence of operating leverage and demand breadth, but EBITDA is not the same
as distributable cash in a project business.

## Backlog hierarchy and accounting quality

At June 30, 2026, estimated 18-month backlog was $21.391B: Communications $5.461B,
Clean Energy and Infrastructure $7.791B, Power Delivery $6.347B, and Pipeline
Infrastructure $1.792B. The Q2 filing also reports remaining performance obligations
of approximately $16.3B, with about $6.6B, or 40.5%, expected to be satisfied within
six months.

The evidence hierarchy is:

`remaining performance obligations / signed contractual work > 18-month backlog > awards and pipeline > AI or policy narrative`

Backlog is still not guaranteed earnings. The contract can be delayed, repriced,
terminated, or become less profitable as labor, materials, weather, permitting,
subcontractors, and change orders move against the estimate. The filing identifies
accounts receivable and contract assets as a combined working-capital exposure;
contract assets were $2.484B at June 30, 2026, versus $2.002B at December 31, 2025,
while accounts receivable were $1.744B versus $1.540B. Those balances are not an
accusation of manipulation, but they are the exact assets that must convert into
cash for backlog to have owner value.

Contract liabilities were about $760.9M in the latest-quarter evidence. Customer
advances can temporarily support operating cash, while contract assets and
receivables consume it. The next read should separate the two rather than treating
all working-capital movement as execution quality.

## Cash conversion and reinvestment burden

For the six months ended June 30, 2026, operating cash flow was approximately
$120.3M and capex approximately $188.3M. The resulting negative $68.0M bridge came
before acquisitions, earn-outs, equity repurchases, and debt service. This is the
central forensic contradiction: revenue, EBITDA, and backlog expanded while the
cash available after required physical investment was negative.

The appropriate owner-cash test is:

`operating cash flow - maintenance and growth capex - cash acquisitions - earn-outs - required working-capital investment - dilution / repurchases`

MasTec's equipment, facilities, fleet, and field organization make capex and
working capital economically real. The analysis should not automatically label all
capex as maintenance; growth capex may be sensible, but it still requires a return
hurdle. Likewise, adjusted EBITDA should be reconciled to cash after contract
assets, receivables, equipment, interest, taxes, and acquisition integration.

## Acquisition and leverage test

MasTec completed multiple acquisitions and disclosed acquisition-related revenue of
approximately $195.1M in Q2 and $363.7M for the first six months of 2026. A July
2026 Superior acquisition added electrical contracting and data-center capability.
The strategic logic is credible: the company can move closer to mission-critical
power and electrical execution. The forensic question is whether the acquired
revenue earns returns above the cash price, financing cost, integration cost,
amortization, working capital, and earn-out burden.

Debt obligations rose to $2.753B at June 30, 2026 from $2.346B at December 31,
2025. The balance included $475.8M of revolver borrowings, $1.223B of senior notes,
$600M of term debt, and $454.4M of finance leases and other obligations. Cash was
$315.6M, down from $396.0M. This is manageable only if backlog converts, collections
remain timely, and acquisition EBITDA is real rather than a temporary contribution.

Goodwill, customer relationships, and backlog intangibles are not automatically
bad assets. They do, however, raise the return-on-invested-capital hurdle and can
delay recognition of an underperforming acquisition until operating evidence is
already deteriorating.

## Concentration, incentives, and related-party questions

The filing identifies customer concentration, including a major communications
customer, as an economic risk. That matters because a diversified segment label can
still conceal dependence on a few large project owners. Track the ten-largest
customer share, contract renewal, payment terms, termination rights, and the
customer-specific contract assets behind the backlog.

The latest filing also contains related-party and joint-venture disclosures,
including equipment and construction-service arrangements. These may be ordinary
infrastructure relationships, but they deserve a recurring review for pricing,
capital allocation, guarantees, and whether economics sit inside or outside
consolidated MasTec results. Noncontrolling interests also matter: Q2 net income
included $15.6M attributable to noncontrolling interests.

## Lyn Alden lens: durable physical demand versus liquidity

MasTec is close to durable physical constraints: grid capacity, communications
networks, renewable interconnection, pipelines, and data-center electrical work.
That supports the structural side of the thesis. But the cash timing is liquidity
sensitive. Customer capex, project financing, permitting, policy support, and
interest rates determine when awards become crews-on-site and when work becomes
collected cash.

The macro chain is:

`AI / electrification demand -> customer funding -> permits and awards -> labor and materials -> measured progress -> billing -> collection`

MasTec benefits from the first link but carries much of the burden in the middle
links. In a tighter-liquidity regime, backlog can remain high while start dates,
working capital, and collections weaken.

## Damodaran lens: narrative-to-numbers and lifecycle

The narrative is that MasTec is becoming a comprehensive infrastructure business
for the AI and energy transition. To earn a high-growth valuation, that narrative
must become durable organic revenue, stable project margins, positive owner cash,
and high-return reinvestment after acquisitions and fleet investment.

The company currently looks like a growth-through-execution and acquisition case,
not a mature cash-yield case. The model should therefore separate organic segment
growth from acquired growth, normalize project margins, use a conservative backlog
conversion rate, and apply a lower terminal margin if labor or customer concentration
weakens. A high valuation that capitalizes adjusted EBITDA without the negative
cash bridge would embed the most optimistic version of the narrative.

## Valuation framing

The scenario register uses the $285.7M FY2025 operating-cash-less-capex screen
only as a starting denominator. It does not call that amount distributable
because the company still has acquisition, earn-out, contract-asset, debt, and
fleet requirements. The screening cases are $150M, $300M, and $500M of
normalized annual owner cash at 20x, 30x, and 40x. They imply approximately
$3.0B, $9.0B, and $20.0B of equity value against the register's approximately
$19.0B market snapshot.

The range is intentional. The lower case assumes backlog converts slowly and
the capital and working-capital burden remains high. The base case requires
positive cash conversion after the current investment phase and acceptable
returns on acquired work. The higher case requires backlog conversion, stable
project margins, acquisition returns, and controlled dilution without relying
on customer advances or asset sales. These are screening cases, not price
targets.

## Falsifiers and next filing watchlist

The thesis weakens if:

- backlog and remaining obligations rise while contract assets and receivables grow faster than cash collections;
- gross-margin gains are concentrated in mix or acquisition contribution rather than repeatable project execution;
- six-month operating cash flow remains below capex after backlog has begun converting;
- debt rises faster than EBITDA and acquisition returns do not cover financing and integration costs;
- customer concentration or a major communications customer creates an abrupt award, cancellation, or payment shock;
- claims, change orders, retainage, or contract losses appear late in the project cycle; or
- policy support, permits, or customer capex timing account for more of the backlog than the company’s execution capability.

Next filing: reconcile revenue and gross margin by segment to cash flow; roll forward
contract assets, receivables, contract liabilities and aging; disclose cash paid for
acquisitions and acquisition-related revenue; separate organic from acquired growth;
track debt, finance leases, earn-outs, goodwill and intangibles; and compare signed
backlog and remaining obligations with starts, cancellations, collections, and
project estimate changes.

## Evidence boundary

This memo uses the locally preserved 2025 annual report/10-K package and Q2 2026
10-Q HTML plus the local company packet. No standalone earnings-call transcript is
saved locally. The financial conclusions are filing-backed; claims about management
framing, AI relevance, and segment outlook are disclosed-company evidence and should
be tested against the next cash and project-margin bridge.

### Evidence grades and unresolved questions

- **Filed fact:** FY2025 revenue, net income, operating cash flow, capex, debt,
  receivables, contract assets, contract liabilities, and the Q2 2026 backlog are
  anchored to the annual/quarterly filing package.
- **Disclosed fact:** backlog composition, segment outlook, and AI/clean-energy
  demand framing come from company materials and are not independent demand proof.
- **Inference:** the current read is that MasTec controls field execution and
  contract selection, while customers and project owners carry much of the timing,
  permitting, and financing burden; backlog is therefore a visibility asset rather
  than owner cash.
- **Unresolved:** the next filing must reconcile backlog conversion with contract
  assets, receivables, cash conversion, and segment margin. A rising backlog with
  flat or negative operating cash flow, worsening contract assets, or acquisition
  margin slippage would weaken the thesis.
