# Microsoft Corporation — software control with an expanding physical-compute burden

Research date: 2026-09-14  
Evidence window: FY2025 and FY2026  
Ticker: MSFT  
Evidence grade: A for reported financials and company operating metrics; B for the future return on AI capacity, Copilot adoption, and the durability of current cloud demand.

## Bottom line

Microsoft controls several high-value enterprise relationships at the same time: identity, productivity software, collaboration, developer tools, business applications, security, and cloud infrastructure. This control matters because customers do not purchase only a stand-alone program. They place work, permissions, data, applications, and technical skills inside Microsoft-managed systems. That creates renewal strength and distribution for new products, but it does not guarantee that every new product earns a good return.

The company is no longer adequately described as a low-capital software business. In FY2026, revenue was $331.839B, operating income was $155.237B, net income was $133.749B, and operating cash flow was $182.935B. Additions to property and equipment were $115.948B. A simple operating-cash-less-property-spending screen was therefore $66.987B before acquisitions, investments, debt repayment, dividends, repurchases, leases, and stock compensation. Microsoft also recorded $12.405B of stock compensation and $11.047B of net recognized investment gains in the cash-flow reconciliation.

The core question is not whether Microsoft has a strong franchise. It does. The question is whether the incremental economics of Azure AI and Copilot justify the capital, power, equipment, depreciation, leases, model-inference cost, engineering labor, and dilution required to deliver them. FY2026 evidence is favorable on demand: Azure revenue exceeded $100B, Azure and other cloud services grew 41%, Microsoft Cloud revenue reached $214.4B, and commercial remaining performance obligation reached $678B. But demand is not the same as return on invested capital, and remaining performance obligation is not the same as cash already earned.

## What Microsoft controls

The principal economic chain is:

`identity and productivity workflow -> data and permissions -> developer or business application -> cloud consumption -> recurring contract and expansion`

Microsoft controls the software surfaces through which many enterprises authenticate users, create documents, communicate, manage devices, develop applications, run business processes, and consume cloud services. Azure also gives Microsoft a direct relationship with infrastructure demand. The company can therefore sell a new security, analytics, AI, or developer product into an installed base that already has procurement, technical integration, and billing relationships with Microsoft.

The company does not control customer budgets, competing cloud platforms, the cost and supply of advanced chips, power availability, data-center construction, model quality, regulation, or the utilization of every new facility. It also does not disclose a separate Azure income statement. Intelligent Cloud segment profit is useful, but it includes Azure, server products, enterprise services, and other offerings.

## Reported operating baseline

Amounts are $ billions unless noted.

| Measure | FY2025 | FY2026 | Comment |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Revenue | 281.724 | 331.839 | FY2026 growth was 18% |
| Operating income | 128.528 | 155.237 | Operating margin remained very high, but mix shifted toward infrastructure |
| Net income | 101.832 | 133.749 | FY2026 included material investment gains |
| Operating cash flow | 136.162 | 182.935 | Includes non-cash add-backs and working-capital movements |
| Additions to property and equipment | 64.551 | 115.948 | Main visible physical-capacity investment line |
| Operating cash less property additions | 71.611 | 66.987 | Simple screen, not distributable owner cash |
| Stock-based compensation | 11.974 | 12.405 | Ownership cost of employee compensation |
| Repurchases and dividends | 42.502 | 48.716 | Cash returned during the year |

The FY2026 cash screen fell despite higher operating cash because property investment grew much faster. That does not prove poor investment. It proves that current earnings must be evaluated together with the capital required to produce future cloud and AI revenue.

Working capital also matters. Unearned revenue increased operating cash by $9.361B in FY2026, while accounts receivable used $12.737B. Unearned revenue is cash collected before the related service is recognized as revenue. It supports liquidity, but the company still has to provide the service over time. It should not be treated as permanent incremental profit.

## Segment economics and the shift toward infrastructure

FY2026 segment results were:

| Segment | Revenue | Operating income | Operating margin |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Productivity and Business Processes | $139.996B | $83.879B | 59.9% |
| Intelligent Cloud | $137.791B | $56.972B | 41.3% |
| More Personal Computing | $54.052B | $14.386B | 26.6% |

Productivity and Business Processes contains highly profitable software and services such as Microsoft 365, LinkedIn, and Dynamics. Intelligent Cloud contains Azure and other cloud services but also server products and related activities. The lower segment margin is not evidence that Azure is weak; it is evidence that cloud infrastructure has more direct equipment, energy, depreciation, and service-delivery cost than a traditional license or subscription.

Microsoft reported that Azure and other cloud services revenue grew 41% in FY2026, while the Intelligent Cloud segment grew 30%. The difference matters because Azure growth is occurring inside a broader segment with different products and cost structures. The investor should track Azure growth together with Intelligent Cloud margin, depreciation, property additions, capacity utilization, and the cost of delivering AI workloads.

Commercial remaining performance obligation, or RPO, is the amount of contracted commercial revenue that has not yet been recognized. It reached $678B at FY2026 year-end, up 84%. RPO provides evidence of customer commitments, but it includes amounts that will be invoiced and recognized later and can contain consumption-linked contracts. The next question is how quickly RPO becomes revenue and cash, at what margin, and with how much additional capacity spending.

## AI and Copilot: the conversion test

Microsoft 365 Copilot had more than 30 million paid seats at FY2026 year-end. That is a meaningful adoption signal. It is not yet a complete economic answer. The company must show whether paid seats are incremental purchases or substitutions within an existing Microsoft 365 budget, whether users retain the product after the initial deployment, how much inference and support cost is incurred per user, and whether the product raises renewal, seat growth, or workflow depth.

The same test applies to Azure AI consumption. Revenue can rise because customers are experimenting, because Microsoft is selling reserved capacity, or because production workloads are becoming embedded in customer systems. These have different durability and margin characteristics. The filings should be read for usage, contract duration, pricing, capacity constraints, gross-margin movement, and customer concentration rather than for AI revenue growth alone.

Microsoft said in its FY2026 call that Azure demand exceeded available capacity and that Azure margin percentage declined because of sales mix and continued scaling of AI infrastructure, partly offset by efficiency gains. This is a useful disclosure: demand is strong, but meeting it consumes capital and currently pressures the margin mix. The key future evidence is whether efficiency gains and pricing can eventually offset depreciation, energy, equipment, and model-serving costs.

## Cash conversion and earnings quality

### Investment gains must be separated from operating earnings

Other income was $10.697B in FY2026 compared with a $4.901B expense in FY2025. Microsoft’s FY2026 release says investment gains, including the effect of OpenAI investments, increased reported net income by $4.963B. That gain is not cash generated by selling software or delivering cloud services. It may represent real economic value, but it is volatile and should be excluded from a recurring operating cash valuation unless the investment itself is being valued separately.

### Stock compensation is a real owner cost

Stock compensation was $12.405B in FY2026. It is added back in operating cash flow because no cash leaves the company when the award is recorded. Existing owners still bear the cost through shares issued to employees or through repurchases needed to offset dilution. Microsoft repurchased $22.271B of stock and paid $26.445B of dividends in FY2026. The analysis should compare those payments with the change in diluted shares, not assume that a large buyback automatically created value.

### Capital spending is not automatically growth or maintenance

Property additions rose to $115.948B and net property and equipment on the balance sheet rose to $313.076B. The cash-flow statement does not tell the investor how much of the spending is maintenance, replacement, expansion, or AI-specific capacity. Microsoft reports depreciation, amortization, and other of $38.534B in the FY2026 operating-cash reconciliation, but accounting depreciation does not necessarily equal the cash required to keep current service capacity competitive.

The important reconciliation is:

`customer demand -> contracted revenue -> actual cloud usage -> gross profit after serving cost -> cash after new and replacement capacity -> cash per diluted share`

If revenue grows while the last two steps weaken, the business may be expanding activity without improving owner economics.

## Lyn Alden lens: liquidity, durability, and physical constraints

Microsoft has strong liquidity: cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments were $76.843B at FY2026 year-end, against long-term debt of about $31.067B plus current debt. The stronger macro question is not survival. It is the return earned on a rapidly expanding asset base during a period when power, chips, construction capacity, and financing conditions can constrain supply.

Enterprise software subscriptions are more durable than discretionary project revenue, but enterprise customers can delay migrations, reduce seats, or renegotiate consumption when budgets tighten. Azure usage can remain strategically important while its near-term growth rate and pricing weaken. AI infrastructure also creates exposure to technology cycles: a faster and cheaper model can lower the value of existing capacity before the accounting life of the equipment ends.

## Damodaran lens: turn the story into operating variables

The narrative is: Microsoft has a mature, high-return software and enterprise-control franchise; Azure extends that relationship into infrastructure; AI increases product value and cloud demand; the company is investing heavily before the full return is visible.

That narrative must be translated into measurable assumptions:

- Microsoft 365 renewal, seat growth, price realization, and Copilot attach rate.
- Azure growth, usage mix, pricing, capacity utilization, and Intelligent Cloud margin.
- RPO conversion into revenue and cash, including the timing of customer consumption.
- Property investment as a percentage of cloud revenue and the useful economic life of servers and facilities.
- Depreciation, power, networking, model-inference, support, and security costs per unit of AI revenue.
- Stock compensation, repurchases, diluted shares, and cash per share.
- Investment gains and losses excluded from normalized operating earnings.

At a market value of approximately $3.690T on September 14, 2026, the equity value was about 55 times the FY2026 operating-cash-less-property-additions screen of $66.987B. A simple scenario screen is:

| Normalized annual owner-cash screen | Multiple | Implied equity value |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $65B bear case | 35x | $2.275T |
| $95B base case | 40x | $3.800T |
| $125B strong case | 45x | $5.625T |

These are not price targets. They show the burden placed on the future cash-flow path. The base case requires substantial growth in cash after infrastructure spending, not merely continued revenue growth or investment gains.

## Thesis breakers and next evidence

The thesis weakens if commercial RPO grows faster than revenue and cash for several periods; if Azure growth remains high while Intelligent Cloud margin falls without a credible recovery path; if Copilot paid seats rise but renewals, usage, or incremental revenue do not; if capital additions remain near FY2026 levels without corresponding cash returns; if investment gains make reported earnings look stronger than operating performance; if stock compensation stays high while diluted shares do not fall; if a model-cost decline reduces Azure pricing before utilization rises; or if power, chip, construction, security, or regulatory constraints delay capacity and raise cost.

The next annual and quarterly filings should answer: How much FY2026 RPO converted to revenue? Did Azure margin stabilize as new capacity was utilized? What portion of property additions supports AI workloads? What is depreciation relative to new capacity? Are Copilot seats incremental and retained? Did cash after property investment improve? Did diluted shares decline after repurchases? And did management compensation reward durable cash per share rather than only revenue, adjusted earnings, or reported AI adoption?
