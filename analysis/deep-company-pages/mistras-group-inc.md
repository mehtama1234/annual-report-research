# MISTRAS Group — inspection expertise, recurring asset integrity, and weak cash conversion

Date baseline: 2026-09-13

## Bottom line

MISTRAS provides asset-protection services: nondestructive testing, inspection,
condition monitoring, engineering, data management, analytics, and products.
Customers use it to inspect critical assets without shutting down an entire
facility. Its markets include oil and gas, aerospace and defense, power,
petrochemicals, industrial facilities, and infrastructure.

The control point is certified technical judgment connected to asset data. A
customer is not only buying hours in the field; it is buying inspection
evidence, regulatory support, failure prevention, and the ability to keep an
asset operating. Run-and-maintain programs and recurring inspection work can
make revenue more durable than one-off consulting.

The burden is skilled labor, utilization, customer capital-spending cycles,
receivables, software and data investment, accreditation, safety, and debt.
The company must retain certified technicians and engineers while customers can
delay turnarounds or projects.

FY2025 revenue was $724.0M, gross profit was $204.5M, operating income was
$40.6M, net income attributable to MISTRAS was $16.8M, adjusted EBITDA was
$91.1M, operating cash flow was $33.0M, and free cash flow was only $3.8M.
Gross debt was $178.0M against $28.0M of cash. The gap between adjusted EBITDA
and free cash flow is not a technicality; receivables, capital spending, and
technical capability investment consume the cash.

Current judgment: MISTRAS has a real technical and regulatory control point,
and recent aerospace and defense growth may improve mix. But the economics
remain fragile until recurring programs produce cash after labor, working
capital, software, accreditation, and debt. Margin improvement alone is not
enough evidence of a durable franchise.

## What MISTRAS controls

`critical asset -> inspection or monitoring requirement -> certified technician and technical system -> condition data and engineering judgment -> compliance, uptime, and customer renewal`

MISTRAS controls technical procedures, certified personnel, inspection data,
analytics, customer history, and the ability to coordinate field services with
engineering and products. Customers control maintenance schedules, capital
budgets, project timing, vendor selection, and payment.

The control point is stronger when MISTRAS is embedded in a customer’s asset-
integrity program and data history. It is weaker when the work is a discrete
inspection bid with easily comparable labor pricing.

## Recurring work and technical mix

MISTRAS says many services are supplied on a recurring run-and-maintain basis.
Nondestructive testing can be valuable because it allows customers to inspect
mission-critical assets without shutting them down. The durable model has
three parts:

1. recurring inspection and integrity-management work;
2. technical projects and turnarounds tied to customer capital spending; and
3. products, software, data, and analytics that improve the service.

The next filings should separate recurring program revenue from project,
turnaround, product, and one-time work. The customer value should be tested
through renewal, pricing, utilization, rework, safety, and cash per technician.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. Margin improvement did not become owner cash

FY2025 gross margin improved to 28.2% from 26.3%, and adjusted EBITDA reached
$91.1M. Yet free cash flow fell to $3.8M from $27.1M. The most direct
explanation is higher receivables and capital spending, but this also shows
why operating margin cannot stand in for owner economics.

Track operating cash relative to adjusted EBITDA, accounts receivable, days
sales outstanding, contract assets, customer advances, capitalized software,
and technician hiring.

### 2. Receivables can absorb the apparent growth

The company reported weak cash conversion in 2025 and described higher
accounts receivable and working-capital timing as major drivers. A technical
services company may perform work before customer acceptance or billing. A
project can be profitable on paper while cash arrives later or is disputed.

The next filing should show aging, contract assets, unbilled revenue,
allowances, customer concentration, retention, billing terms, and cash
conversion by segment and end market.

### 3. Certified talent is both moat and cost

MISTRAS needs certified technicians, engineers, scientists, and specialized
technical staff. Scarcity can support pricing and customer retention, but
wages, training, travel, safety, accreditation, and utilization determine
profit. If the company cuts staff too aggressively, service quality and
renewal can suffer; if it hires ahead of demand, utilization falls.

Track technicians per program, utilization, billable hours, wage inflation,
turnover, certification cost, subcontractor usage, safety incidents, and
revenue per technical employee.

### 4. Adjusted measures can exclude ongoing portfolio work

The company has pursued cost reductions, voluntary laboratory consolidation,
workflow automation, digital inspection, and higher-complexity aerospace and
defense capability. Restructuring and integration costs may be individually
unusual but can recur when the strategy depends on continual portfolio change.
Include recurring transformation and capability costs in normalized owner cash.

### 5. Debt magnifies working-capital volatility

At year-end 2025 gross debt was $178.0M and net debt was $150.0M. A business
with $3.8M of annual free cash flow has little room for a sustained working-
capital use, customer delay, or acquisition miss. Debt repayment and interest
must be tested against normalized cash, not adjusted EBITDA alone.

## Alden macro and durability lens

MISTRAS is exposed to oil and gas turnarounds, aerospace and defense budgets,
power generation, industrial production, infrastructure spending, customer
maintenance schedules, wages, tariffs, interest rates, and the availability of
certified personnel.

Regulation and asset aging support the need for inspection, but project timing
and customer capital spending remain cyclical. A downturn may defer inspections
that are not legally urgent, while fixed technical staff and debt remain.
Liquidity depends on collection, utilization, working-capital control, debt
access, and capital spending discipline.

## Damodaran narrative-to-numbers lens

The narrative is “MISTRAS is a technical asset-integrity platform that earns
recurring work from the need to inspect and protect critical assets.” The model
must specify:

1. recurring run-and-maintain versus project and turnaround revenue;
2. aerospace, defense, oil and gas, power, industrial, and infrastructure mix;
3. technician utilization, pricing, wages, certification, and subcontracting;
4. renewal, customer concentration, acceptance, billing, and collections;
5. software, data, laboratory, and accreditation investment;
6. capital spending required to preserve and expand capability;
7. debt, interest, acquisition, and restructuring burden; and
8. normalized common cash per technician and share.

MISTRAS is in a capability-repair and mix-upgrade phase. A higher valuation
requires evidence that aerospace and defense growth and recurring programs
raise cash returns, not merely reported adjusted EBITDA.

margin.

## Thesis breakers and next filings

The thesis weakens if:

- revenue and adjusted EBITDA grow while operating cash remains weak;
- accounts receivable or unbilled work grows faster than revenue;
- aerospace and defense growth is project-specific and does not renew;
- technician shortages raise wages, subcontracting, or rework costs;
- digital, laboratory, and accreditation investment does not improve pricing
  or utilization;
- debt remains high relative to free cash flow;
- recurring “special” costs continue through portfolio cleanup; or
- customer capital-spending pauses reduce turnaround and project demand.

The next filings should show recurring-program revenue, renewal, end-market
mix, utilization, certified headcount, wages, subcontractors, receivables,
contract assets, capex, software investment, debt, claims, acquisition and
restructuring costs, and cash per technical employee.

## Evidence grade

- Control point: **B+** — certification, data, and asset-integrity knowledge
  create switching friction.
- Operating economics: **B-** — technical need is durable, but utilization,
  project timing, and talent costs are material.
- Cash conversion: **C+** — FY2025 margin improved while free cash flow fell
  to $3.8M.
- Balance-sheet resilience: **C+** — net debt is large relative to current
  free cash flow.
- Capital allocation: **B-** — capability investment may improve mix, but
  returns and recurring costs require proof.
- Valuation: **C+** — current value assumes a cash recovery not yet visible
  in FY2025.
