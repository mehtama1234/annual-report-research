# Newmont Corporation — gold-major scale, portfolio renewal, and capital returns

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed U.S. GAAP figures; dollar amounts
are in millions.

## How to read this memo

Attributable production means the ounces that belong economically to Newmont
after accounting for partners’ ownership. Sustaining capital is spending needed
to keep existing mines operating. Growth capital funds expansions or new mines.
Asset-retirement obligations are estimated future costs to close, reclaim, and
remediate mine sites. Adjusted EBITDA is management’s earnings measure before
interest, taxes, depreciation, and selected adjustments; it is not shareholder
cash.

## Investment question

Newmont is a large portfolio of mines, processing systems, reserves, projects,
and operating rights. Scale can diversify individual mine problems and support
capital allocation across a large asset base. The question is whether that scale
creates durable owner cash after sustaining and growth capital, reclamation,
project delays, host-country obligations, acquisitions and divestitures, debt,
and the risk that strong gold prices make current results look permanent.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Newmont achieved its 2025 production and cost guidance and produced 5.889M
  attributable gold ounces, 28M ounces of silver, and 135K tonnes of copper.
  The breadth reduces dependence on one mine, but creates more operating and
  jurisdictional variables to monitor.
- The company reduced debt by about $3.4B, ended with approximately $7.6B of
  cash, and reported a net-cash position of about $2.1B. This is a meaningful
  improvement in balance-sheet resilience after the Newcrest acquisition period.
- Ahafo North, Lihir, Cadia, Tanami Expansion 2, and Red Chris show that the
  future cash base still requires construction, permits, operating execution,
  and additional capital. The project pipeline is not equivalent to produced
  ounces.
- Divestitures generated about $4.5B of after-tax proceeds to date from the
  announced non-core program. Selling assets can improve portfolio quality, but
  analysts must test whether the company sold cash-generating assets to fund
  returns or removed weak assets at an acceptable price.
- Q1 and Q2 2026 produced very high cash flow and large shareholder returns.
  These results strengthen the balance sheet and per-share case, but they also
  increase the risk that investors value current gold prices as a permanent
  operating condition.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. How much of the cash result is commodity price?

Recalculate revenue, margins, and owner cash using a mid-cycle gold price and
conservative silver and copper assumptions. Separate volume, grade, recovery,
unit cost, byproduct credit, and realized price. A price increase is valuable,
but it is not the same as management improving the mine system.

### 2. Is sustaining capital complete?

Reconcile management’s free-cash-flow definition to the filed cash-flow
statement. Separate sustaining capital from growth capital, deferred stripping,
capitalized exploration, tailings work, and project construction. Test whether
reclamation, closure, and environmental spending is included in the economic
cost of producing ounces.

### 3. Did portfolio optimization create value?

For every acquisition and divestiture, compare cash paid or received with the
production, reserves, debt, reclamation liabilities, and future capital removed
or added. A smaller portfolio is better only if the remaining assets earn higher
returns after the lost cash flow is considered.

### 4. Are reserves and projects reliable?

Track reserve changes from depletion, price assumptions, grades, recovery,
engineering changes, and acquisitions. For each major project compare approved
capital with actual capital, construction timing, first production, ramp-up,
unit cost, and cash return. A reserve estimate is not a guarantee of an
operating mine.

### 5. Are shareholder returns covered through the cycle?

Compare dividends and repurchases with mid-cycle operating cash less sustaining
capital, closure obligations, debt maturities, and required project funding.
Returning cash during a high-price year is not evidence that the same policy is
safe at lower prices.

## Incentives, portfolio simplification, and cohort position

Newmont's debt reduction and divestiture program can improve resilience, but
they should not be treated as operating improvement. A sale may remove a weak
asset, a strong cash-generating asset, or a future capital obligation; the
economic result depends on the price received and the cash that would otherwise
have been generated. Likewise, a high-price year can support large buybacks
while the remaining portfolio still needs sustaining capital and project
renewal.

The management scorecard should therefore use cash per diluted share after
sustaining capital, closure and reclamation obligations, taxes, debt service,
and project funding. Each project should be judged against its final cost and
cash return, not only the company's production guidance. The $6.8B asset-
retirement obligation is part of the lifecycle denominator even when the cash
payment occurs years later.

compared with Wheaton and Franco-Nevada, it owns and operates the mines and
therefore carries direct depletion, labor, energy, and remediation risk.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** gold can benefit from monetary instability, fiscal stress,
  and demand for scarce physical assets. A gold miner still faces energy,
  labor, equipment, political, and financing inflation, so a higher gold price
  does not automatically expand owner returns.
- **Damodaran lens:** value producing mines, reserves, development projects,
  and portfolio options separately. Use mid-cycle prices, explicit sustaining
  capital, probability-weighted project completion, and a return requirement for
  new capital.
- **Forensic lens:** the main category errors are treating adjusted EBITDA as
  owner cash, current gold prices as permanent, reserves as production, reduced
  debt as operating improvement, divestiture proceeds as recurring cash, and
  shareholder returns as proof of full-cycle coverage.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $135.31B of equity value at
$126.81 per share. That is about 18.5x the FY2025 post-productive-asset cash
screen of $7.299B. A normalized range of $4.0B / $6.0B / $8.0B at 18x / 22x /
26x implies $72B / $132B / $208B. The market is therefore close to the base
case in this register and requires high normalized cash, continued production,
and successful portfolio renewal rather than only one strong gold year.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Gold prices normalize while costs, reclamation, and project capital remain
   elevated.
2. Production declines because grades, recoveries, mine availability, or
   project ramp-ups disappoint.
3. Sustaining capital is higher than management’s current definition implies.
4. Ahafo North, Lihir, Cadia, Tanami Expansion 2, or Red Chris require more
   capital or produce less cash than the approved plans assume.
5. Host governments, environmental authorities, or indigenous communities delay
   or restrict assets.
6. Dividends and repurchases continue above mid-cycle cash after sustaining
   capital and closure obligations.

Next filing checks: realized metal prices; production, grades, recovery, and
unit costs by mine; sustaining versus growth capital; project budgets and
