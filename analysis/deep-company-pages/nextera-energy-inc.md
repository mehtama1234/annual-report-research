## Bottom line

NextEra is a hybrid utility-and-development business. Florida Power & Light
provides regulated rate-base compounding, customer growth, and a formal recovery
mechanism. NextEra Energy Resources adds competitive renewables, storage, gas,
nuclear, transmission, tax-credit monetization, and large-load development. The
combination makes NextEra one of the clearest ways to study how electricity demand
from AI and industrial reshoring becomes generation, transmission, and customer
capital.

The quality of the growth narrative is conditional. In the first six months of
2026, operating cash flow was $7.276B, but capital expenditures, independent-power
investments, and nuclear-fuel purchases were $19.389B. The difference was funded by
$15.566B of long-term debt issuance, $3.431B of commercial-paper and other
short-term debt, asset sales, equity, and tax-credit proceeds. This is normal for a
capital-intensive utility/developer, not a shenanigan by itself; it means valuation
must be built around allowed returns, project returns, financing access, dilution,
and customer affordability rather than adjusted EPS alone.

Current judgment: FPL is the cleaner control point because rate recovery converts
approved investment into a regulated return. NEER is the higher-upside but more
complex development business. The company deserves durability credit for power
infrastructure, but its 8%+ long-term EPS narrative carries a large capital-market,
regulatory, tax-credit, construction, and execution burden.

## What the company controls

The operating chain is:

`customer growth / large-load demand -> regulatory approval or contracted project -> generation, transmission and storage investment -> operating output -> rate recovery, PPA cash flow, tax credits, or asset sale`

FPL controls a regulated electricity-delivery relationship with roughly 12 million
people across Florida. Its control point is not simply generation ownership; it is
the combination of customer franchise, rate base, reliability obligation, storm
recovery, and regulatory process.

NEER controls a development and asset-management business that originates, finances,
constructs, operates, and recycles wind, solar, battery, gas, nuclear, and
transmission assets. Its relationship is with utilities, corporate buyers,
hyperscalers, project partners, tax-credit buyers, and capital providers. That
relationship can be valuable, but it is more exposed to project timing and capital
availability than FPL's formal rate mechanism.

## Latest-quarter operating picture

The Q2 2026 packet reports GAAP EPS of $1.50 and adjusted EPS of $1.15, up from
$1.05 adjusted EPS in Q2 2025. FPL Q2 EPS was $0.67 versus $0.62, while NEER
adjusted EPS was $0.62 versus $0.53. NEER added 3.6 GW of renewables and storage
origination to backlog during the quarter. Q1 2026 added a record 4 GW, including
1.3 GW of battery storage.

The SEC filing supplies a more skeptical bridge. Six-month 2026 revenue was
$14.235B versus $12.947B. FPL net income increased by $283M, primarily because
average rate base grew by approximately $6.6B year over year. NEER results also
benefited from $271M of after-tax new-investment contribution, $451M of favorable
non-qualifying hedge activity, and the absence of a prior-year XPLR impairment;
equity-method effects included a $630M favorable year-over-year change from XPLR
investment gains.

The reported earnings increase is therefore a mixture of rate-base growth, new
projects, hedge valuation, tax benefits, asset/investment effects, and prior-year
comparisons. Only the first two are straightforward evidence of repeatable
operating compounding.

## FPL: the cleaner regulated business

FPL's regulatory capital employed increased from $72.9B in Q2 2025 to $79.7B in
Q2 2026, and it earned approximately 11.70% on retail rate base based on a trailing
thirteen-month average. Six-month 2026 FPL capital investment was $5.932B,
including $2.123B of new generation, $2.346B of transmission and distribution,
and $848M of existing generation.

The regulated model has a visible bargain: customers receive reliable service and
the utility receives recovery of prudently incurred capital plus an allowed return.
The burden is political and physical. Bills, storm recovery, fuel costs, reliability,
environmental requirements, and affordability determine how much capital can be
approved and how quickly it can enter rates.

The 2025 rate agreement is not frictionless. During 2026, parties challenged the
FPSC order approving it, and appeals remained pending at the Florida Supreme Court.
The filing also shows FPL using reserve-surplus amortization: six-month 2026 RSM
amortization was approximately $196M after tax, while the remaining available RSM
reserve was approximately $1.335B after tax at June 30. This is permitted rate-case
accounting, not evidence of fraud, but it affects the quality and timing of reported
earnings. A normalized model should separate underlying earned ROE from reserve
amortization and storm-cost recovery.

## NEER: development business and capital burden

Six-month 2026 NEER investment was $13.449B. It included $6.337B in solar and
solar-plus-storage, $2.347B in wind, $1.604B in other clean energy, $1.112B in
natural-gas and oil customer supply, and $367M in regulated electric and gas
transmission.

As of June 30, 2026, approved or internally authorized estimated capital investment
for the remainder of 2026 through 2030 was $40.020B at NEER and $54.730B at FPL.
NEER's plan included approximately 16,245 MW of new solar, 6,115 MW of battery
storage, 3,637 MW of wind and repowering, and 3,915M of regulated transmission
investment. The filing says actual investment can vary significantly from these
estimates, and approximately $10.1B of related procurement and construction
commitments was already included in the NEER estimate.

The strategic narrative is credible: power demand, storage, transmission, and
large-load customers need a scaled developer. The Damodaran test is harder: each
project must earn an adequate return after construction cost, interconnection,
financing, curtailment, tax-credit dependence, PPA terms, operating cost, and
residual value. Backlog or origination is an opportunity set, not owner cash.

## Earnings quality and financial engineering tests

### Hedge and fair-value volatility

Corporate and Other benefited from approximately $846M of favorable after-tax
non-qualifying hedge activity in the first six months of 2026 versus 2025. NEER's
revenue also included a large favorable change in non-qualifying commodity hedges.
These gains and losses can reverse with energy prices and interest rates. Adjusted
EPS growth that depends materially on hedge marks should receive a lower durability
multiple than rate-base earnings or contracted project contribution.

### Tax-credit monetization

Cash proceeds from clean-energy tax-credit sales were approximately $1.173B in the
first six months of 2026, including $169M at FPL, versus $310M in the prior-year
period. Tax-credit sales are a legitimate financing and monetization tool, but they
are not equivalent to customer-paid recurring revenue. The analysis should track
credit generation, transfer pricing, indemnities, policy changes, and how much EPS
and liquidity depend on continued tax-credit markets.

### Capitalized interest and project timing

NEER capitalized approximately $403M of interest during the first six months of
2026; FPL recorded approximately $140M of AFUDC, including $108M of equity AFUDC.
Capitalized interest is appropriate under accounting rules, but it moves financing
cost into asset basis rather than current expense. The return analysis must include
the full cost of capital and construction-period carry.

### Noncontrolling interests and related parties

At June 30, 2026, approximately $9.738B of NEE noncontrolling interests related to
differential membership interests. Six-month earnings associated with those
interests were approximately $1.047B and appeared as net loss attributable to NCI.
NEER also provided guarantees, indemnifications, letters of credit, and surety
bonds totaling approximately $1.8B primarily for XPLR obligations. Amounts due
from XPLR were approximately $427M in other receivables and $171M in noncurrent
other assets.

These structures can reduce parent funding needs and recycle capital, but they also
mean consolidated EBITDA, asset growth, and headline project economics are not all
available to common shareholders on a one-for-one basis.

Cash was not the only funding source. NEE reported approximately $18.1B of net
available liquidity at June 30, 2026 and explicitly relies on credit and capital
markets for requirements not satisfied by operating cash flows. Maintaining an
investment-grade rating is therefore an operating dependency, not merely a finance
department preference.

The company had approximately $170.452B of net property, plant, and equipment at
June 30, including $32.282B of construction work in progress. A large CWIP balance
can support future rate base and project earnings, but it also exposes shareholders
to construction delays, regulatory disallowance, cost overruns, interest rates,
technology shifts, and demand forecasts that may not materialize.

## Lyn Alden lens: electricity durability versus monetary sensitivity

NextEra benefits from a durable physical need: society cannot run data centers,
manufacturing, transport, or homes without reliable electrons. FPL's customer
franchise and transmission/generation assets are difficult to replicate. NEER's
development business benefits when power demand and policy support are strong.

The liquidity chain is:

`large-load demand -> customer commitment and regulatory approval -> project financing and construction -> generation/transmission output -> rate recovery or PPA cash flow -> debt service and new investment`

The company controls the project machine, but it does not control interest rates,
tax policy, public-service commissions, customer affordability, or every customer
load commitment. In a tighter capital regime, the value of existing regulated assets
may remain durable while the value of unbuilt development backlog compresses.

## Damodaran lens: narrative-to-numbers and valuation

Management's narrative is 8%+ adjusted-EPS compounding through 2032 and beyond,
powered by FPL rate-base growth, Energy Resources backlog, storage, transmission,
and large-load hubs. A valuation supporting that narrative must show:

- allowed FPL rate-base growth that converts to earned ROE without excessive customer resistance;
- NEER project returns above the cost of capital after tax credits, financing, and NCI;
- a stable funding mix that does not require repeated expensive equity or asset sales;
- disciplined project selection when data-center demand, policy, or commodity conditions soften; and
- normalized earnings excluding hedge marks, reserve amortization, one-time gains, impairments, and tax-credit timing.

The cleanest model values FPL as a regulated growth asset, values contracted NEER
projects on project-level cash flows, and gives only probability-weighted value to
origination/backlog. Capital expenditures should not be treated as an incidental
expense: they are the price of the growth narrative.

The scenario register therefore does not use a conventional industrial
owner-cash multiple. Its bear, base, and bull normalized common-earnings cases
are $0, $3.0B, and $6.0B at 20x, 35x, and 45x, implying approximately $0,
$105B, and $270B of equity value against the register's approximately $171.7B
market snapshot. The zero bear case is a stress case for the consolidated
funding gap, not a claim that FPL has no value. The base case requires FPL
recovery, NEER project returns, tax-credit and financing support, and controlled
dilution to offset the negative consolidated cash screen. The bull case requires
those conditions plus successful large-load conversion. These are screening
cases, not price targets.

## Falsifiers and next filing watchlist

The thesis weakens if:

- FPL rate-base growth is challenged, delayed, or disallowed, or customer bills and political resistance limit recovery;
- NEER backlog grows but project returns, starts, or contracted cash flow do not;
- adjusted EPS growth depends increasingly on hedge marks, reserve amortization, tax-credit sales, or asset-sale gains;
- capital spending and debt rise faster than operating cash flow and allowed/project returns;
- NCI, guarantees, related-party receivables, or XPLR obligations absorb more economics than headline EPS suggests;
- large-load customer commitments remain conceptual, are delayed, or require uneconomic bespoke infrastructure; or
- the Dominion transaction adds integration, regulatory, or financing risk without improving per-share returns.

Next filing: reconcile GAAP to adjusted earnings; separate FPL earned ROE and rate-
base growth from reserve/storm effects; disclose NEER organic project contribution;
track cash tax-credit proceeds, capitalized interest, capex commitments, debt,
interest, NCI, guarantees, project cancellations, PPA terms, and large-load starts;
and compare actual returns with the long-term EPS narrative.
