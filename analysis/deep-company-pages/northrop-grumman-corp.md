# Northrop Grumman — defense backlog, program estimates, and pension-adjusted cash

Date baseline: 2026-09-13  
Primary annual filing: [Northrop Grumman 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1133421/000113342126000003/noc-20251231.htm)  
Annual report: [Northrop Grumman 2025 Annual Report](https://cdn.northropgrumman.com/-/media/Project/Northrop-Grumman/ngc/who-we-are/corporate-responsibility/2025-Annual-Report-Northrop-Grumman.pdf)  
Companion packet: [Northrop Grumman company packet](/home/mehtama1/git-repo/annual-report-research/extracted/industrial-goods/aerospace-defense-products-services/northrop-grumman-corp/company-packet.md)

## Bottom line

Northrop Grumman designs, builds, and supports strategic systems, aircraft,
space systems, mission electronics, cyber capabilities, and other defense
platforms. Its control point is a combination of technical qualification,
security clearance, classified know-how, production capacity, and long-lived
government relationships. The customer is mainly the U.S. government and its
allies, which creates demand visibility but also exposes the company to
appropriations, contract terms, program affordability, and political decisions.

FY2025 sales were approximately $42.0B, net earnings were $4.182B, operating
cash flow was $4.757B, free cash flow was $3.307B, and backlog was $95.7B. The
cash result improved, but the filing also recorded a $477M B-21 loss provision
and carried large pension, unbilled-receivable, and production commitments.
Capital expenditures were $1.450B, required pension contributions were $72M,
and cash returned to shareholders was about $2.9B.

Current judgment: Northrop has durable strategic relevance and a real barrier
to entry, but backlog is not equivalent to low-risk profit. Fixed-price or
poorly estimated programs can consume years of margin; a production ramp can
require substantial capital before cash arrives; and pension accounting can
make reported earnings and cash obligations diverge. The investment case must
follow each important program from award to cost estimate, delivery, cash
collection, and final margin.

## What Northrop controls

`national-security requirement -> funded program -> technical qualification -> design and production -> delivery and sustainment -> follow-on work`

Northrop controls engineering expertise, intellectual property, facilities,
security-qualified labor, program-management capability, and access to a
limited supplier base. It does not control congressional appropriations,
customer requirements, contract modification, inflation, schedule, supplier
performance, export rules, or whether a program is restructured.

The control point is strongest where the company is embedded in a strategic
system that has few qualified alternatives. It is weaker where multiple
contractors compete for a commodity-like service or where the government can
renegotiate scope and price.

## Three-year reported cash picture

Dollars in millions.

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Capital expenditures | Free cash flow | Required pension contribution | Pension-adjusted cash screen |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2023 | 3,875 | 1,775 | 2,100 | 68 | 2,032 |
| 2024 | 4,388 | 1,767 | 2,621 | 58 | 2,563 |
| 2025 | 4,757 | 1,450 | 3,307 | 72 | 3,235 |

The pension-adjusted screen is not Northrop’s reported free cash flow. It
subtracts required cash pension contributions to make the owner-cash burden
more visible. It still precedes dividends, repurchases, debt repayment,
strategic investment, working-capital growth, and program-specific obligations.

2025 operating cash flow benefited from lower cash tax payments and improved
trade working capital. Northrop recast its working-capital definition in the
fourth quarter of 2025 to include accounts receivable, unbilled receivables,
inventoried costs, trade accounts payable, and advance payments and billings in
excess of costs incurred. Trend analysis should use the recast definition and
inspect each component rather than accept the aggregate improvement.

## Backlog and program economics

Backlog of $95.7B was more than two years of 2025 sales. Q2 2026 awards of
approximately $20B pushed backlog to about $105B. This provides visibility, but
the value of backlog depends on funded status, contract type, expected cost,
customer options, cancellation rights, schedule, and the share of work that
will require new production investment.

The correct program chain is:

1. award and authorization;
2. design maturity and supplier readiness;
3. estimated total cost and expected margin;
4. production or development progress;
5. customer acceptance and billing;
6. cash collection and working-capital release; and
7. sustainment, follow-on awards, or termination.

Northrop’s B-21 Raider and Sentinel programs demonstrate why the chain matters.
They are strategically important and can support long-term work, but complex
development and production ramps create cost-to-complete, schedule, supplier,
and fixed-price risk.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. B-21 loss provisions are direct evidence of estimate risk

Northrop recorded a $477M B-21 loss provision in 2025, after a $1.559B B-21
loss provision in 2023. The company also recognized significant depreciation,
amortization, stock compensation, and pension-related adjustments in operating
cash flow. A loss provision is not proof of wrongdoing; it is proof that
program estimates can change materially.

The next filing should show remaining exposure, expected future cash cost,
contract modification, customer funding, production rate, supplier recovery,
and whether margin on other programs is compensating for the loss.

### 2. Backlog can be large while unbilled cash is tied up

In 2025, unbilled receivables were a significant use of trade working capital.
Revenue recognized under contract accounting can precede billing or collection.
This is normal for long-term defense work, but it creates a real liquidity
requirement and a claim on future customer acceptance.

Track unbilled receivables, inventoried costs, advance payments, contract
assets, billings, receivable aging, and cash conversion by segment. A backlog
increase paired with rising unbilled balances and weaker collections is lower
quality than backlog accompanied by billing and cash release.

### 3. Government funding is durable but not unconditional

Defense demand benefits from strategic urgency, allied spending, and long-lived
platforms. Programs can still face continuing resolutions, budget reductions,
requirements changes, audits, export restrictions, cost caps, or termination.
The company’s bargaining position is not the same on a sole-source strategic
system as on a competitive service contract.

The model should distinguish funded backlog, unfunded or option work,
international exposure, classified work that limits disclosure, and programs
whose economics depend on future appropriations.

### 4. Pension accounting changes the owner claim

Northrop reports both pension expense and cash contributions. Mark-to-market
changes can move reported earnings without matching current cash, while required
contributions reduce cash available for common shareholders. The company also
receives a CAS pension recovery related to government contracts, which must be
understood separately from ordinary operating profit.

The correct bridge is:

`GAAP earnings -> program margin -> operating cash -> pension contributions and recoveries -> capex and working capital -> debt, dividends, repurchases, and owner cash`

### 5. Capital returns can compete with production investment

Northrop returned approximately $2.9B through dividends and repurchases in
2025. That was below reported free cash flow but close to the pension-adjusted
screen after considering other claims. Capital returns are reasonable only if
the company can fund B-21 and Sentinel production, facilities, supplier support,
technology, pension obligations, and debt without underinvesting or issuing
new capital.

## Alden macro and durability lens

Northrop’s demand is tied to national security, deterrence, allied budgets,
strategic modernization, and government procurement rather than household
spending. That can provide a stronger demand floor during some economic
downturns. It does not remove budget, political, program, labor, supply-chain,
or cost-inflation risk.

Liquidity resilience depends on cash collection, customer advances, working
capital, program funding, pension obligations, capital commitments, debt,
surety, and the ability to scale production without repeated cost overruns. A
large backlog is useful only if it remains funded and profitable.

## Damodaran narrative-to-numbers lens

The narrative is “Northrop is a strategic defense compounder with protected
technology, durable backlog, and multi-decade platform relevance.” The model
must specify:

1. sales and margin by program and segment;
2. funded versus option backlog and conversion timing;
3. development versus production versus sustainment mix;
4. cost-to-complete and loss-provision assumptions;
5. working-capital and unbilled-receivable requirements;
6. R&D, capital investment, supplier capacity, and labor needs;
7. pension contributions and CAS recoveries;
8. contract type, customer funding, and termination exposure; and
9. normalized shareholder returns after required reinvestment.

Northrop is in a production-scaling lifecycle for strategic programs, not a
simple mature cash-harvest phase. The valuation should not capitalize backlog
without charging the capital and margin risk required to deliver it.

## Valuation screen and expectations

The September 11, 2026 market snapshot showed equity value of approximately
$73.901B. The screen below uses free cash flow less required pension
contributions rather than EPS or backlog.

| Case | Pension-adjusted owner-cash screen | Equity cash multiple | Implied equity value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bear | $2.4B | 16x | $38.4B |
| Base | $3.2B | 20x | $64.0B |
| Bull | $4.0B | 24x | $96.0B |

The current value is above the base screen and requires sustained program
execution, B-21 and Sentinel improvement, backlog conversion, pension
discipline, and continued capital returns. This is an expectation test, not an
investment recommendation.

## Incentives, thesis breakers, and next filings

Management has an incentive to emphasize backlog, awards, adjusted or
mark-to-market-adjusted EPS, cash flow, and strategic relevance. Those measures
are useful, but they can understate program loss, unbilled working capital,
pension cash, and production capital.

The thesis weakens if:

- backlog grows while funded status, billings, or customer cash collections weaken;
- new B-21, Sentinel, space, or other strategic programs require repeated loss provisions;
- program margin improves only through optimistic cost-to-complete assumptions;
- unbilled receivables and inventoried costs grow faster than revenue;
- production investment and skilled labor costs rise without delivery and cash;
- pension contributions, CAS recoveries, or debt needs consume capital returns; or
- customer budgets, export rules, or contract changes reduce the usable backlog.

The next filings should disclose program-level margin and loss provisions where
possible, backlog funding and conversion, unbilled receivable aging, advance
payments, customer claims, B-21 and Sentinel production rates, capex, R&D,
pension obligations, debt, dividends, repurchases, and remaining capital
commitments.

## Evidence grade

- Control point: **A-** — strategic qualifications, security, and technical
  barriers create meaningful customer dependence.
- Operating economics: **B** — demand and backlog are strong, but program
  estimates and fixed-price exposure can reverse margin.
- Cash conversion: **B+** — cash flow improved, while working capital and
  pension contributions require a full bridge.
- Balance-sheet resilience: **B+** — strategic demand and cash help, but
  production investment, pension, and program claims remain material.
- Capital allocation: **B** — returns are substantial and need to be compared
  with production capacity and program funding.
- Valuation: **B-** — current value requires durable execution beyond the
  reported backlog.
