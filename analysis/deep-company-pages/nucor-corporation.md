## Bottom line

Nucor is a domestic steel and fabricated-products platform with a real
operating advantage in electric-arc-furnace production, scrap recycling,
customer relationships, downstream fabrication, and value-added capacity. It
is not a clean recurring-service business. Steel prices, volumes, scrap,
energy, trade policy, construction cycles, project execution, and massive
capital spending determine whether the advantage becomes owner cash.

FY2025 makes the burden visible. Operating cash flow was $3.234B against $3.422B
of PP&E spending and $2M of acquisitions, producing approximately $(190)M
before dividends, buybacks, and other claims. Nucor still paid $512M of common
dividends and repurchased $700M of stock. The company ended with $2.260B of cash
and $6.909B of debt and capital-lease obligations. This is a cyclical capital-
allocation system, not a harvest year.

Current judgment: Nucor may be one of the stronger steel operators, but the
investment case requires through-cycle return on the $3.4B capex program and
proof that value-added products, contract sales, and domestic policy reduce
cyclicality without hiding low returns on new assets.

## Q2 2026 operating checkpoint

Nucor reported Q2 2026 net earnings attributable to common stockholders of
`$1.16B`, or `$5.04` per diluted share, on net sales of `$10.40B`. EBITDA was
`$2.02B`. The improvement from Q1 and the prior-year quarter came mainly from
higher steel-mill selling prices and volumes, but the result also included a
`$130M` reduction in steel-mill cost of products sold from cash refunds related
to prior-period raw-material procurement. That benefit is real cash, but it is
not a recurring spread assumption for normalized valuation.

The quarter also included a `$61M` non-cash pretax benefit from the increase in
Nucor’s Helion investment valuation. Management excluded it from adjusted
earnings, which is the correct direction for an operating analysis. The broader
lesson is that both favorable procurement settlements and investment marks need
to remain visible when comparing reported, adjusted, and mid-cycle earnings.

Six-month operating cash was `$2.286B` against `$1.232B` of capital
expenditures, leaving approximately `$1.054B` before investments, financing,
and capital returns. The company estimates full-year 2026 capex of about
`$2.50B`, below 2025’s `$3.42B`, but the major projects remain the West Virginia
sheet mill, two Nucor Towers & Structures locations, and a South Carolina
galvanizing line. Six-month working capital used `$952M` for receivables and
`$560M` for inventories. Accounts receivable reached `$4.045B` and inventory
`$6.020B`, so the strong cash result still contains a meaningful collection and
inventory test.

Nucor held `$2.69B` of cash and short-term investments at the end of Q2 and had
an undrawn `$2.25B` revolver. It repurchased `$475M` of stock and paid `$258M`
of dividends in the first six months; Q2 capital returns were approximately
`$479M`. The balance sheet is resilient, but the correct question is whether
capital returns remain prudent while the company is funding new capacity and
working capital rather than whether liquidity is immediately adequate.

The updated conclusion is conditional: Q2 demonstrates strong operating
leverage and domestic steel positioning, but normalized owner cash must exclude
procurement windfalls, separate investment marks, and earn a return on the new
facilities through a full steel cycle.

## What the company controls

The economic chain is:

`scrap / raw materials -> EAF melt and rolling -> steel products / fabrication -> contract customer / infrastructure project -> cash`

Nucor controls a domestic production and downstream network rather than a
single commodity mill. Steel mills represented about 62% of external sales in
2025; the company recycled approximately 20 million gross tons of scrap. Sheet
steel sales were heavily contract-oriented, which can improve visibility and
customer retention.

The burden is physical and cyclical: furnaces, mills, maintenance, energy,
labor, environmental compliance, scrap availability, freight, inventory,
customer credit, and the risk of commissioning capacity into a weak price
environment. Downstream fabrication may capture more relationship value, but it
does not remove steel input and project-cycle risk.

Nucor's $3.422B capex is not necessarily all maintenance. Major projects can
create future value and domestic capacity, so a mature owner-cash screen that
deducts every dollar may be too conservative. But treating the entire program
as optional would be worse: steel mills require continuous investment to remain
competitive, safe, efficient, and compliant.

The 2025 bridge therefore asks a lifecycle question. Is Nucor temporarily
funding high-return growth during a weak steel year, or is capital intensity
structurally rising faster than returns? The answer requires project-level
start-up timing, utilization, realized margin, and cash return.

## Earnings quality and possible financial engineering

### 1. Contract sales improve visibility, not necessarily margins

Approximately 85% of sheet steel sales were to contract customers in 2025.
Contracts can reduce spot-volume risk, but price formulas, scrap pass-through,
minimum commitments, customer credit, and renegotiation matter. Contracted
revenue should not be treated as contracted economic profit.

### 2. Segment mix can flatter quality

Steel mills are the largest segment, while fabricated steel products and raw
materials add downstream and upstream exposure. A shift toward value-added
products may improve margins and relationships, but it can also require more
working capital, project execution, and acquisition capital. Track margin by
segment and incremental return on each new facility.

### 3. Trade policy is a real driver, not a permanent moat

Management links 2026 results to supportive federal trade policy and reduced
unfairly traded imports. Tariffs and trade remedies can support domestic price
and utilization, but they are policy-sensitive and can provoke customer,
substitute, or recession responses. A valuation that assumes permanent policy
support is a macro bet.

### 4. Buybacks during a capital cycle require discipline

Nucor repurchased $700M and paid $512M of dividends in 2025 while the simple
post-capex residual was negative. The company had cash and financing capacity,
so this is not evidence of misconduct. It is an incentive test: buybacks should
be compared with project returns, balance-sheet resilience, and the price paid
for shares across the steel cycle.

The Q2 2026 result shows why the test must be period-specific. The `$130M`
raw-material refund improved steel-mill cost of products sold, while the `$61M`
Helion investment mark was a non-operating non-cash benefit. Neither should be
used as a recurring measure of steel spread economics. The 10-Q also reported
approximately `$120M` of pre-operating and start-up costs for new facilities;
those costs are expensed, but they are part of the economic price of expanding
capacity and should not be ignored when evaluating project returns.

SBC was $133M and noncontrolling-interest earnings were approximately $294M.
Per-share cash analysis must account for both dilution and the portion of
consolidated economics not belonging to common shareholders.

## Capital structure and Lyn Alden durability lens

Nucor benefits from domestic industrial policy, infrastructure, power,
nonresidential construction, recycling, and the strategic value of North
American steel capacity. Its macro sensitivity is high: rates, construction,
manufacturing, trade, scrap, energy, labor, and inventories all affect cash.

The Alden questions are:

- Can domestic capacity and infrastructure demand keep utilization high enough
  to earn the new capital base?
- Does policy support persist through political and inflation cycles?
- Can Nucor preserve liquidity when steel prices, volumes, and scrap spreads
  fall together?
- Does downstream integration reduce volatility or merely increase capital
  intensity and operational complexity?

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The bullish narrative is that Nucor is the best-in-class domestic steel system:
efficient EAF production, recycling, value-added products, contract customers,
and structural U.S. infrastructure demand.

The skeptical narrative is that a quality steel producer remains a cyclical,
capital-intensive commodity business. The market may capitalize peak margins,
policy support, and new-project EBITDA before proving returns through a full
cycle.

The valuation case requires normalized mid-cycle price and volume, scrap and
energy economics, project utilization, maintenance versus growth capex,
working capital, debt, and the return on downstream investments. A single 2026
rebound quarter cannot establish a terminal cash flow.

## Scenario and valuation expectation

The September 11, 2026 market snapshot recorded approximately $59.28B of
equity value for Nucor. The initial owner-cash screen is intentionally
cycle-aware:

| Case | Normalized owner cash | Equity-cash multiple | Implied equity value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | $0B | 12x | $0B |
| Base | $1.5B | 16x | $24.0B |
| Bull | $3.0B | 20x | $60.0B |

The current equity value is close to the bull screen. That means the market
requires a strong normalized cash recovery, successful project ramp-up, and a
multiple that survives a steel downturn. The scenario is not a price target;
it is a demand for proof.

## Thesis breakers and next filing tests

The thesis weakens if new mills or downstream projects underutilize, contract
pricing fails to protect margins, scrap/energy costs rise faster than selling
prices, working capital absorbs the recovery, trade policy reverses, capex
returns disappoint, debt rises, or capital returns continue through a negative
post-capex cycle.

The next filing should capture shipments, realized price, scrap and energy
spreads, contract coverage, segment margin, inventory and receivables, project
cost and utilization, maintenance versus growth capex, debt maturities,
noncontrolling interests, SBC, and cash after dividends and buybacks.
