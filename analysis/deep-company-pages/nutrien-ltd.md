# Nutrien — integrated crop inputs, potash scale, and retail cash quality

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed IFRS figures in U.S. dollars;
dollar amounts are in millions unless otherwise stated.

## How to read this memo

Potash, nitrogen, and phosphate are different fertilizer nutrients with
different mines, plants, inputs, and market prices. Retail means Nutrien's
farmer-facing stores, crop consultants, agronomy products, financing, and
distribution network. Operating rate measures production relative to available
capacity. Free cash flow here is Nutrien's measure: operating cash flow less
sustaining capital, mine development and pre-stripping capital, and lease
principal repayment. It is more useful than adjusted EBITDA for owner analysis,
but it still requires checks for portfolio sales, working-capital funding,
environmental obligations, and future growth capital.

## Investment question

Nutrien combines large upstream fertilizer assets with a global retail and
agronomy network. The possible advantage is that production scale, farmer
relationships, proprietary products, logistics, and crop advice reinforce one
another. The central question is whether that integration produces better
through-cycle owner cash than a focused producer such as CF Industries, after
mining renewal, plant capital, retail working capital, environmental costs,
portfolio exits, debt, and shareholder returns.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Nutrien's control point is broader than production. It has six potash mines,
  twelve nitrogen facilities, six phosphate facilities, more than 1,800 retail
  locations, more than 4,200 crop consultants, and over 600,000 customer
  accounts. Each layer has a different return and burden.
- Potash is the strongest structural asset in the record. Nutrien reported
  record sales volumes and said 49% of potash ore tonnes were mined using
  automation in 2025. Automation can reduce unit labor cost, but it does not
  remove mine development, sustaining capital, environmental, or price risk.
- Retail may reduce reliance on upstream commodity margins through proprietary
  products, agronomy, financing, and distribution. That claim must be tested by
  retail gross margin, working capital, bad debts, same-store growth, customer
  retention, and cash after store and technology investment.
- The 2026 quarterly pattern is highly seasonal. Q1 net earnings were $139M and
  Q2 net earnings were $1.221B. A single quarter is therefore weak evidence of
  normalized earnings; the annual and multi-year bridge is more important.
- Portfolio simplification is a capital-allocation test. Strategic alternatives
  for phosphate, the Trinidad nitrogen facility, and Brazilian retail could
  improve the remaining mix, but sale proceeds, stranded costs, taxes, and
  lost cash generation must be recorded.
- Supplier financing helped FY2025 operating cash flow. The next filings must
  show whether that funding is a normal commercial feature or a growing source
  of cash that would reverse if suppliers change terms.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Does retail really smooth the commodity cycle?

Build separate revenue, gross margin, operating expense, working capital, and
capital-spending bridges for Retail, Potash, Nitrogen, and Phosphate. Compare
retail results during lower fertilizer prices with upstream results. Do not
assume integration creates a benefit unless the consolidated return exceeds the
return from owning simpler assets.

### 2. How much operating cash came from financing suppliers?

Reconcile the change in supplier financing, payables, inventory, and
receivables. Ask whether the $4.007B OCF can recur without another increase in
supplier-funded working capital. A timing-supported cash inflow should not be
valued like recurring operating profit.

### 3. Are free-cash-flow deductions complete?

Reconcile management FCF to the cash-flow statement. Keep sustaining capital,
mine development, pre-stripping, lease principal, environmental spending,
retail investment, growth projects, acquisitions, and divestiture proceeds
visible. A low capex year can reflect delayed maintenance rather than improved
economics.

### 4. What does portfolio simplification really create?

For every sale or proposed exit, record proceeds, taxes, stranded overhead,
lost EBITDA, working-capital release, debt repayment, and the return on the
remaining assets. A smaller company is better only if the remaining capital
earns more after the costs of separation.

### 5. Can potash scale support a mid-cycle return?

Track realized potash price, tonnes sold, production cost per tonne, automation,
mine development, reserve life, and capacity additions. Test whether record
volume is being achieved through durable cost advantage or through favorable
market conditions and inventory timing.

### 6. Are buybacks and dividends covered?

Compare shareholder returns with mid-cycle FCF after mine and plant renewal,
lease principal, environmental obligations, debt maturities, and retail
working-capital needs. Include the cost of repurchases and the price paid, not
only the lower share count.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** Nutrien sits at the intersection of food security,
  energy, trade, and geopolitics. Potash assets and North American nitrogen
  capacity can be strategically valuable, but affordability, energy costs,
  shipping, and farmer balance sheets determine how much value reaches owners.
- **Damodaran lens:** value Retail, Potash, Nitrogen, and Phosphate separately
  before applying a portfolio conclusion. Use mid-cycle nutrient prices, unit
  costs, utilization, mine life, sustaining capital, lease claims, and explicit
  probability-weighted portfolio projects and exits.
- **Forensic lens:** the key risks are calling supplier-financing cash durable,
  calling adjusted EBITDA structural, treating divestiture proceeds as normal
  FCF, and assuming retail integration automatically improves upstream
  commodity economics.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $37.8B of equity value using
$78.45 per share and 481.962M shares outstanding at year-end 2025. Against
FY2025 management FCF of $1.979B, the equity value is about 19.1x. A normalized
range of $1.5B / $2.2B / $3.0B at 12x / 15x / 18x implies $18B / $33B / $54B.
The current value therefore requires a return toward the stronger cash years,
continued potash and nitrogen strength, and evidence that retail and portfolio
simplification improve—not merely stabilize—the cash denominator.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Potash or nitrogen prices normalize while unit costs, mine renewal, and
   environmental spending remain high.
2. Supplier financing or payables growth reverses and operating cash falls
   faster than reported earnings.
3. Retail margins do not remain resilient after inventory, receivables, credit,
   store, agronomy, and technology costs.
4. Portfolio exits produce weak proceeds, stranded costs, or loss of valuable
   cash generation.
5. Mine development, automation, plant reliability, or environmental work
   requires materially more capital than management's current plan.
6. Dividends and buybacks continue above mid-cycle FCF while debt remains high.
