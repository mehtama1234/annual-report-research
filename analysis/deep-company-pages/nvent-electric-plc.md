## Bottom line

nVent supplies electrical connection, protection, enclosure, cable-management,
power-distribution, control-building, and cooling products for mission-critical
equipment, buildings, and processes. Its control point is not the entire data
center or grid. It is the qualified physical interface that protects, connects,
routes, encloses, and cools electrical systems after the customer has selected
the broader architecture.

FY2025 continuing-operations operating cash flow was `$649M`, capital
expenditures were `$93.3M`, free cash flow was `$561M`, and cash paid for the
Electrical Products Group acquisition was `$979.6M`. The strict residual after
capital expenditures and that acquisition was approximately negative `$424M`.
This is the central forensic point: nVent generated strong operating cash, but
its reported growth and portfolio transformation required a large acquisition.

The company may own a valuable control point because qualification, reliability,
installation, and safety matter in critical electrical environments. The
valuation requires proof that EPG integration, new-product growth, liquid-
cooling expansion, and data-center demand produce cash after acquisition cost,
capacity investment, working capital, tariffs, and debt.

## What nVent controls

`critical electrical design -> connection and protection specification -> enclosure, bus, cable, and cooling installation -> operating reliability -> replacement and expansion demand`

nVent's products sit inside electrical and thermal systems. Customers may buy
them through engineering specifications, distributors, original-equipment
manufacturers, or direct project relationships. Once qualified and installed,
replacement is constrained by fit, safety, reliability, certification, and the
cost of redesigning the surrounding system.

That is different from owning the customer's entire facility. nVent remains
exposed to project timing, customer concentration, competitive bidding,
material costs, manufacturing capacity, and the risk that its products become
standardized or are designed out.

The important product layers are:

- electrical connections and protection;
- enclosures, cabinets, bus systems, and cable management;
- control buildings and power-distribution equipment;
- data-center liquid and air cooling; and
- new products that increase content per critical electrical installation.

The company-defined free-cash-flow bridge includes operating cash, capital

owners when acquisition-funded growth is part of the business model.

The filing reports substantial proceeds from discontinued operations. The
proceeds improve liquidity but are not recurring operating cash and should not
be used to support a permanent valuation multiple.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. Organic growth and acquisition growth must stay separate

FY2025 sales grew about 30%, but acquisitions contributed a material part of
the increase. The Electrical Products Group acquisition cost `$979.6M` and
added products in switchgear, bus systems, and control buildings. Management
also describes new-product vitality and infrastructure expansion.

The model should separate organic sales, acquired sales, price, volume, mix,
integration cost, acquired margin, acquired working capital, and required
capacity. An acquisition can improve nVent's product position while still
destroying value if the purchase price and integration capital consume more
than the acquired cash return.

### 2. Margin expansion has a direct capital test

Adjusted operating income was `$786M` in FY2025, while reported operating
income was `$617M`. The gap includes purchase accounting, amortization, and
other adjustments. Some are non-cash, but the associated acquired assets and
integration work are economic claims.

The company also disclosed more than `$160M` of inflation impact in 2025,
including approximately `$90M` of tariff impact, while price and productivity
offset the pressure. The future test is whether margin remains stable after
tariff benefits or price recovery normalize and after capacity is added for
liquid cooling and data centers.

### 3. Backlog is visibility, not cash

Backlog reached about `$2.6B` in Q1 2026 and infrastructure led growth. Backlog
can improve planning and capacity utilization, but it still requires production,
shipment, acceptance, customer payment, inventory, warranty, and sometimes
project-specific engineering.

Reconcile backlog additions with shipments, cancellations, customer deposits,
receivables, inventory, gross margin, and operating cash. A rising backlog with
weak cash conversion or increasing inventory is a warning, not proof of a
stronger control point.

### 4. Liquid cooling carries execution and warranty risk

Liquid cooling can increase nVent's content in high-density data centers, but
it also introduces manufacturing, installation, leak, service, warranty, and
customer qualification requirements. The company is expanding manufacturing
capacity to meet demand.

The test is not simply sales growth. Track capacity spending, yield, warranty
claims, service cost, customer concentration, qualification time, gross margin,
and cash return on each expansion. New products can have different capital and
liability profiles from established enclosures and connections.

### 5. Discontinued operations must not support the operating valuation

The Thermal Management divestiture generated significant cash proceeds in 2025
and changed the portfolio's reported comparison. The proceeds can reduce debt
and fund acquisitions, but they are not evidence that continuing operations
alone produced the same cash.

The valuation should use continuing-operations sales, operating cash, capital
spending, acquisition cash, and debt. It should not mix divestiture proceeds
with recurring owner cash without labeling the difference.

## Balance sheet and liquidity

nVent ended 2025 with about `$237M` of cash, roughly `$600M` available on its
revolver, and about `$1.6B` of debt according to management's results materials.
Debt declined during the year, helped by the Thermal Management divestiture and
operating cash. Liquidity is therefore adequate on the current evidence, but
the acquisition strategy and capacity expansion can reverse that improvement.

The resilience test is whether nVent can fund research and development,
capacity, liquid-cooling expansion, debt service, dividends, buybacks, and
future acquisitions without relying on another divestiture or increasing
leverage faster than cash generation.

## Alden lens: liquidity and durability

nVent benefits from electrification, digitalization, data-center density, power
utility investment, and the need to protect mission-critical equipment. Its
durability depends on technical qualification, installed fit, reliability,
product breadth, and customer cost of changing the surrounding design.

The macro risks are data-center construction timing, utility and industrial
capital spending, tariffs, metals and materials, labor, customer concentration,
interest rates, and competition. The company is more cyclical than a regulated
utility and more capital-dependent than a software provider, even though the
products may be embedded in long-lived infrastructure.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative is: “nVent controls qualified electrical-protection, enclosure,
connection, and cooling content in a growing data-center and electrification
buildout.” The model must specify:

1. organic price, volume, mix, and acquired growth;
2. data-center, utility, industrial, and other end-market exposure;
3. backlog conversion, cancellation, acceptance, and receivable timing;
4. new-product vitality, qualification, margin, warranty, and service cost;
5. liquid-cooling capacity, capex, utilization, and return;
6. EPG integration, acquired cash flow, purchase price, and amortization;
7. tariffs, inflation, working capital, debt, interest, and liquidity; and
8. stock compensation, share count, dividends, repurchases, and cash per share.

nVent is in a portfolio-transformation and high-growth infrastructure phase.
Growth deserves a premium only if the new electrical and cooling content earns
returns above the full acquisition and capacity cost and if per-share cash
improves after those investments.

## Valuation screen

The September 11, 2026 market snapshot showed equity value of approximately
`$26.6B`. The screen starts with the `$561M` reported continuing-operations
free cash flow, then discounts for the fact that the FY2025 acquisition program
used `$979.6M` and that the current growth rate may normalize.

| Case | Normalized common owner cash | Equity cash multiple | Implied common equity value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | `$400M` | `35x` | `$14.0B` |
| Base | `$650M` | `40x` | `$26.0B` |
| Bull | `$900M` | `45x` | `$40.5B` |

The current market value is near the base case. The market is therefore
requiring nVent to convert strong data-center and liquid-cooling growth into
approximately `$650M` or more of normalized common cash while maintaining the
control point, integrating acquisitions, and funding capacity.

## Thesis breakers and next filings

The thesis weakens if:

- organic growth falls sharply after acquired and launch contributions fade;
- backlog rises while shipments, receivables, inventory, or cash conversion
  deteriorate;
- liquid-cooling capacity requires more capital or warranty cost than expected;
- tariff recovery, price, or productivity benefits reverse without volume or
  mix compensation;
- EPG or other acquisitions fail to earn returns above the purchase and
  integration cost;
- debt rises again because acquisition spending exceeds operating cash; or
- stock compensation and repurchases prevent cash per diluted share from
  improving.

The next filings should provide:

1. organic growth, acquisition contribution, price, volume, and mix;
2. backlog conversion, cancellation, customer deposits, inventory, and AR;
3. liquid-cooling capacity, utilization, warranty, and project returns;
4. EPG revenue, margin, integration cost, and cash conversion;
5. tariff and inflation effects on price and margin;
6. debt, interest, cash, revolver capacity, and acquisition commitments; and
7. cash per diluted share after capacity, acquisitions, and capital returns.

## Evidence grade

- **High confidence:** FY2025 sales, continuing-operations operating cash,
  capital expenditures, free cash flow, EPG acquisition cash, debt, Q4 2025
  through Q2 2026 sales and organic growth, backlog, and guidance.
- **Medium confidence:** normalized owner-cash cases and the allocation of
  maintenance versus growth capital during the current expansion.
- **Open:** long-run liquid-cooling returns, EPG integration returns, backlog
  conversion after the current data-center peak, tariff normalization, and
  per-share value after future acquisitions.
