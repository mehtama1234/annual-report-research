## Bottom line

ONEOK is an integrated hydrocarbon-logistics platform, not a simple pipeline
toll road. Its approximately 60,000-mile network connects gathering, processing,
NGL fractionation, transportation, storage, refined products, crude, and marine
exports. That integration can create a valuable coordination advantage: the
company can route multiple molecules through connected assets and monetize volume,
location, storage, and processing complexity.

The economics are strong but more burdened than the “fee-based midstream” label
implies. FY2025 operating cash flow was $5.599B against $3.152B of capex, leaving
$2.447B before acquisitions, dividends, and financing. Adjusted EBITDA was $8.020B,
but included $516M of adjusted EBITDA from unconsolidated affiliates and $386M of
equity earnings deductions in the reconciliation. Total debt was $33.965B and cash
was only $78M at year-end. The core question is therefore not whether ONEOK has
valuable infrastructure; it is whether acquisition integration and growth capital
earn adequate returns after debt, maintenance, commodity exposure, and cash paid to
partners.

Current judgment: ONEOK is a durable physical-infrastructure compounder with a
credible control point in integrated logistics, but it should be valued as a
levered, acquisition-shaped capital allocator—not as an asset-light annuity.

## What the company controls

The operating chain is:

`producer supply / end-user demand -> gathering and processing -> NGL separation and fractionation -> storage, transport, export, and refined-product routing -> contracted and optimized cash flow`

The valuable relationship is with producers, shippers, refiners, exporters, and
downstream customers that need a connected path rather than a single pipe. ONEOK's
system spans natural gas, NGLs, refined products, and crude. Magellan, EnLink,
Medallion, Northern Border, and newer projects broaden the network but also make
comparability, integration, and segment economics more difficult to analyze.

## Earnings quality: fee-based, but not fee-only

ONEOK's reported scale can encourage an overly simple “stable fee income” reading.
The filing identifies several non-toll components:

- gathering and processing has percent-of-proceeds exposure and hedging requirements;
- NGL results depend on product spreads, location differentials, storage, and optimization;
- refined-products and crude results include blending, marketing, and retained product exposure;
- natural-gas pipelines retain some commodity and location-differential exposure; and
- unconsolidated-affiliate EBITDA is included in adjusted EBITDA even though ONEOK does not control those affiliates' operations or cash flows.

The 2025 adjusted-EBITDA reconciliation reached $8.020B versus $6.784B in 2024.
The bridge included $1.783B of interest expense, $1.514B of depreciation and
amortization, $1.028B of taxes, $516M of affiliate EBITDA, a $386M deduction for
equity earnings, and $103M of noncash compensation and other items. These are not
fraud indicators. They define what must be restored when an investor treats EBITDA
as distributable cash.

Management's own annual discussion says operating cash before working-capital
changes increased by $1.0B in 2025, while changes in operating assets and
liabilities reduced operating cash flow by $380M. Receivables and inventory move
with operating scale, commodity prices, supply, and demand. The forensic test is
whether cash conversion remains strong across commodity and working-capital states,
not whether one strong year produces a favorable EBITDA multiple.

## Capital intensity and lifecycle

2025 capex was $3.152B, up from $2.021B in 2024 and $1.595B in 2023. The 2025
breakdown was $1.314B in natural-gas gathering and processing, $758M in NGLs,
$237M in natural-gas pipelines, $752M in refined products and crude, and $91M in
other. ONEOK expected 2026 total capex of $2.7B to $3.2B.

The lifecycle question is whether this investment moves the company from
acquisition-led growth toward internally compounded cash flow. Growth projects
can earn attractive returns when volume is durable and assets are integrated, but
they also create construction, permitting, regulatory, counterparty, schedule, and
cost-overrun risk. The model should separate maintenance capex from growth capex
and ask whether each major project has contracted volume, a credible utilization
curve, and a return above incremental financing cost.

## Acquisition and balance-sheet burden

The 2024 EnLink and Medallion acquisitions transformed the comparison base and
were a major reason 2025 operating cash and EBITDA increased. The 2025 filing says
2025 cash paid for acquisitions was only $25M, while 2025 EBITDA growth included
$295M from Medallion and EnLink. This is a classic post-acquisition test: current
earnings reflect acquired assets, while the original cash price and financing
burden sit in the balance sheet and historical cash-flow period.

Year-end 2025 total debt was $33.965B, including $820M of short-term borrowings and
$30.755B of long-term debt after current maturities and discounts. Cash was $78M,
down from $733M in 2024. The debt load is not inherently unsafe for a large
midstream platform, but it makes interest rates, refinancing, volume resilience,
asset utilization, and project returns central to valuation.

The company also receives cash from unconsolidated affiliates and records equity
earnings. The filing explicitly notes that affiliate distributions are classified
as operating cash until cumulative distributions exceed ONEOK's proportionate share
of affiliate income; excess distributions become investing cash. That accounting
distinction deserves a recurring reconciliation because equity earnings and cash
distributions need not match in timing or economic quality.

## Latest-quarter signal

The Q2 2026 packet reports attributable net income of $966M, diluted EPS of $1.53,
and adjusted EBITDA of $2.121B versus $1.981B a year earlier. Management highlighted
record NGL raw-feed throughput, higher refined-product volumes, and higher natural-
gas processing. Full-year adjusted-EBITDA guidance was raised to $8.2B-$8.5B while
2026 capex remained approximately $2.7B-$3.2B.

The quarter supports the integrated-system thesis, but record throughput is not
the same as high-return growth. The next quarterly cash-flow statement should be
read for working-capital movement, capex progress, debt issuance or repayment,
affiliate distributions, and any increase in optimization or marketing contribution.

## Lyn Alden lens: hard infrastructure with liquidity sensitivity

ONEOK sits close to durable physical demand: molecules still require gathering,
processing, storage, transport, and export, while NGLs and refined products remain
embedded in industrial, residential, and transportation systems. Network density
and difficult-to-replicate rights-of-way support durability.

But the company is not immune to liquidity or commodity cycles. Producers can slow
drilling, shippers can reduce volumes, spreads can compress, exports can be
disrupted, and high rates can make large growth projects less attractive. The
macro chain is:

`energy demand and supply -> producer volumes -> utilization and basis differentials -> processing / transport / export margin -> cash flow -> debt and growth-capital capacity`

ONEOK captures more optionality than a narrow pipeline, but it also carries more
exposure to the middle of that chain.

## Damodaran lens: narrative-to-numbers and valuation

The narrative is that ONEOK's integrated network, acquisition synergies, and
Permian/NGL growth create a long-duration compounding platform. The numbers needed
to support that narrative are organic volume growth, stable or improving segment
margins, cash after maintenance capex, disciplined growth capex, declining leverage,
and returns on acquired assets above the cost of capital.

Valuation should not capitalize the $8.020B adjusted-EBITDA figure without modeling
$3.152B annual capex, $33.965B debt, affiliate cash-flow timing, dividends,
commodity-linked optimization, and the post-EnLink/Medallion normalization. A
reasonable scenario range should include a strong-volume case, a normalized
fee-and-volume case, and a weaker-spread / slower-growth case with capex commitments
still in place.

## Falsifiers and next filing watchlist

The thesis weakens if:

- EBITDA growth is driven mainly by optimization, basis spreads, or acquisition accounting rather than durable volume and fee growth;
- operating cash flow falls below maintenance capex while dividends and growth capex remain high;
- debt stays near $34B while interest costs rise and project returns do not improve;
- affiliate equity earnings rise without comparable distributions or transparent cash conversion;
- acquired volumes fail to produce the promised synergies after integration costs;
- NGL, refined-product, or crude exposure produces larger volatility than the fee-based framing implies; or
- capex projects enter service late, underutilized, or at returns below the financing hurdle.

Next filing: separate organic from acquired volume and EBITDA; reconcile segment
adjusted EBITDA to GAAP income and cash; disclose maintenance versus growth capex;
track receivables, inventory, payables, affiliate distributions, debt, interest,
and project spend; and compare management's guidance bridge with realized volumes,
spreads, optimization, and cash flow.
