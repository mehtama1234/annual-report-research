# Regeneron — biologic franchises, collaboration economics, and patent replacement

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed GAAP figures unless explicitly
identified otherwise; dollar amounts are in millions.

## How to read this memo

Regeneron discovers, develops, manufactures, and commercializes medicines,
including biologic medicines. A biologic is a medicine made using living cells
or biological processes. A product franchise is a medicine and its approved
uses, formulation, manufacturing process, clinical evidence, and commercial
relationships.

The company records some products as its own net product sales and records
other products through collaboration revenue when a partner records the sale
and Regeneron receives a share of profit. GAAP is the accounting rule set used
in the filed statements. Acquired in-process research and development (IPR&D)
is spending on drug programs that have not yet reached approval; it may be
expensed or capitalized depending on the accounting structure.

## Investment question

Regeneron's control point is scientific and clinical: it combines discovery
capability, biological targets, clinical development, regulatory approval,
manufacturing, physician trust, and rights under collaborations. Its current
cash engine is concentrated in Dupixent and the EYLEA franchise.

The investment question is whether those franchises can fund durable common
cash after research, clinical manufacturing, regulatory work, commercialization
partners, price concessions, product competition, litigation, and the cost of
replacing mature products before concentration becomes a financial weakness.

The residual is a screening number, not a complete free-cash-flow measure. It
does not subtract all R&D because R&D is already an operating expense, but it
must be charged as the cost of maintaining the future product portfolio. It
also does not assume that every intangible payment is recurring or that every
successful program will replace current revenue.

## What Regeneron controls

The company controls several connected assets:

- discovery platforms and biological research;
- patents, licenses, and clinical data;
- approved labels and manufacturing processes;
- physician and payer adoption;
- internal and partner commercialization rights; and
- the ability to expand a medicine into additional indications.

The control point is strongest after a medicine has demonstrated clinical value,
obtained approval, achieved reimbursement, and become part of physician
practice. It is weaker before approval, when trial results, regulatory review,
manufacturing scale, safety, and competing therapies can change the outcome.

Regeneron does not control all commercialization. Sanofi records global net
product sales of Dupixent and Kevzara and Regeneron records its share of
profits. Bayer records EYLEA sales outside the United States and Regeneron
records its share of those profits. The partner relationship provides sales,
distribution, and geographic reach, but it also creates dependence on partner
execution and contract terms.

## Franchise concentration

Regeneron states that it is substantially dependent on EYLEA HD, EYLEA, and
Dupixent. In 2025, U.S. EYLEA HD and EYLEA net product sales together represented
31% of total revenue, down from 42% in 2024. U.S. EYLEA sales declined 42% in
2025 as biosimilar competition, compounded bevacizumab, affordability, price,
and the transition to EYLEA HD affected the franchise.

Dupixent is a major offset. Global Dupixent and Kevzara sales recorded by Sanofi
were $18.381B in 2025, and Regeneron's share of collaboration profits was
$5.242B after $930M of reimbursed development costs. Regeneron's share of
profits was 29% of those global product sales, compared with 27% in 2024 and
26% in 2023.

The useful conclusion is not that Dupixent is fully recurring or that EYLEA is
finished. It is that the current cash engine has two distinct lifecycle
problems: Dupixent must expand indications and defend competition, while EYLEA
must shift to EYLEA HD and absorb biosimilar erosion.

## Research and development is the replacement cost

R&D spending rose from $4.439B in 2023 to $5.850B in 2025. It includes direct
trial costs, employee and facility costs, clinical manufacturing, and costs
Regeneron must reimburse to collaborators. In 2025 it also included a $155M
charge related to using an FDA Rare Pediatric Disease Priority Review Voucher.

R&D should not be treated as a discretionary expense that can be added back to
create a higher normalized profit. Some spending supports current franchises,
some expands labels, and some funds programs that fail. The analyst should
separate:

1. maintenance of existing labels and manufacturing;
2. lifecycle expansion of Dupixent and EYLEA HD;
3. late-stage programs near approval; and
4. early-stage research with uncertain value.

The return measure is not the number of programs. It is approved products,
indication expansion, net price, volume, gross profit, collaboration share,
product life, and cash per diluted share after the failed programs are included.

## Collaboration accounting and partner dependence

Collaboration revenue can be economically high quality if the partner pays for
commercial scale while Regeneron retains a durable share of profit. It is also
different from direct product revenue because the partner records the sale,
sets or negotiates some commercial terms, and may bear some costs before the
profit is shared.

In 2025 Sanofi collaboration revenue was $5.884B, including Regeneron's share
of Dupixent and Kevzara profits and related manufacturing reimbursement. Bayer
collaboration revenue was $1.422B, including $1.283B of profit share and $140M
of manufacturing reimbursement.

The correct analysis asks who controls price, payer negotiations, promotion,
distribution, manufacturing, and development decisions. Sanofi records
Dupixent sales in the United States and leads payer negotiations. Bayer records
EYLEA sales outside the United States. Regeneron retains scientific and
contractual rights, but the economic relationship is not identical to owning
the entire commercial channel.

## Cash conversion and balance sheet

Operating cash flow was $4.979B in 2025. Capital expenditures were $898M,
intangible-asset payments were $315M, and acquisitions were $3M. Cash was
$3.118B at year end and long-term debt was approximately $1.986B.

The balance sheet is resilient relative to current cash generation, but a
balance sheet cannot replace scientific success. Receivables were $5.741B and
inventory was $3.201B. The next filings should connect these balances to
partner settlements, product demand, inventory provisions, commercial supply,
and any price or access changes.

The 2025 operating-cash bridge included a $155M reversal-like effect: a
priority-review voucher previously capitalized as an intangible was charged in
the fourth quarter when management decided to use it. The accounting is
disclosed, but it shows why investment and operating lines must be reconciled
before treating cash flow as a steady measure.

## Management incentives and owner claims

Stock-based compensation was $994M in 2025, up from $983M in 2024 and $885M in
2023. This is non-cash in the current accounting period but transfers value to
employees and can dilute shareholders. Regeneron repurchased about $3.456B of
stock in 2025 and had initiated a dividend, so the owner-return analysis must
compare repurchase price and diluted shares with normalized franchise cash.

The compensation structure can reward revenue, product milestones, share price,
or scientific progress before a program proves its full return. The analyst
should compare incentive measures with long-term sales, trial outcomes,
capitalized IPR&D, product quality, and common cash per share.

## Earnings quality and forensic questions

### 1. Is Dupixent profit share repeatable?

Reconcile partner product sales, Regeneron's profit share, development-cost
reimbursements, manufacturing reimbursement, taxes, and cash settlement. Track
profit share per dollar of partner sales and the effect of new indications,
pricing, competition, and payer policy.

### 2. Can EYLEA HD replace EYLEA?

Track U.S. EYLEA and EYLEA HD volume, price, market share, dosing, patient
affordability, biosimilar launches, and gross profit. Higher EYLEA HD sales are
not sufficient if total franchise profit declines or if price concessions rise.

### 3. Does the pipeline replace mature cash?

Build a product-by-product bridge from R&D and IPR&D to approval, launch,
indication expansion, and cash contribution. Treat failures, delays, and
withdrawals as part of the return calculation.

### 4. Are collaboration economics being mistaken for direct ownership?

Read contract rights, partner obligations, profit-share formulas, development
reimbursements, sales responsibility, manufacturing obligations, and termination
rights. Collaboration revenue may be high margin while still dependent on a
partner's decisions and a contract that can change.

### 5. Are intangible assets and IPR&D fully charged?

Separate acquired programs expensed immediately from capitalized IPR&D,
licenses, milestones, and priority-review rights. Test impairment assumptions,
useful lives, and whether the same scientific asset requires future cash before
it generates revenue.

### 6. Is stock compensation economically neutralized?

Compare $994M of SBC with share issuance, repurchases, diluted shares, and
cash per share. A large buyback can offset dilution, but only if the purchase
price is below intrinsic value and the cash is not needed for pipeline or
manufacturing investment.

## Lyn Alden, Damodaran, and forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** current cash and modest debt provide resilience, but
  biotech durability depends on science, patents, reimbursement, and the
  ability to fund long development cycles. Rate changes affect valuation and
  research funding, while healthcare policy affects access and realized price.
- **Damodaran lens:** translate the narrative into product-level volume, price,
  profit share, remaining exclusivity, pipeline probability, R&D reinvestment,
  and terminal franchise cash. Value Dupixent, EYLEA HD, mature products, and
  pipeline programs separately.
- **Forensic lens:** the main presentation risks are treating current franchise
  cash as permanent, treating collaboration sales as fully controlled revenue,
  adding back R&D or IPR&D, ignoring biosimilar and payer pressure, treating
  impairment as irrelevant, and measuring owner returns before SBC and
  buybacks.

## Valuation framing

The September 11, 2026 market snapshot was approximately $82.838B of equity
value at $781.49 per share. Against the FY2025 residual of $3.763B, the equity
value is about 22 times the screen. That multiple assumes that Dupixent growth,
EYLEA HD transition, and pipeline productivity offset EYLEA erosion.

A normalized range of $3.0B / $4.0B / $5.0B at 16x / 20x / 24x implies
approximately $48B / $80B / $120B of equity value. The base case requires
continued Dupixent profit growth, partial EYLEA replacement, and a pipeline
that maintains long-term cash after R&D. The bull case requires new products or
indications to increase the portfolio rather than only delay decline.

## Evidence grades and thesis breakers

| Question | Current grade | What would upgrade or weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scientific and clinical control | A- | New approvals, label expansion, durable clinical evidence |
| Current cash generation | A- | OCF and residual remain strong after full scientific and manufacturing costs |
| Franchise concentration | B- | Dupixent and EYLEA dependence remains high; pipeline diversification is proven |
| EYLEA replacement | C+ | EYLEA HD profit offsets biosimilar and legacy EYLEA decline |
| Collaboration economics | B | Profit shares remain strong and partner dependence remains favorable |
| Pipeline productivity | B- | R&D converts into approved products with acceptable returns |
| Capital allocation and dilution | B- | Buybacks and dividends do not weaken pipeline funding or cash per share |

Thesis breakers:

1. Dupixent growth slows because of competition, reimbursement, safety, or
   limited label expansion.
2. EYLEA HD does not offset EYLEA and biosimilar erosion through volume, price,
   or gross profit.
3. R&D rises without approvals, commercial launches, or adequate returns.
4. Sanofi or Bayer collaboration economics deteriorate, or partner execution
   reduces Regeneron's retained cash.
5. Receivables, inventory, or collaboration settlements become less collectible.
6. New products require repeated IPR&D, manufacturing, or acquisition spending
   that is not reflected in the normalized cash denominator.
7. SBC and buybacks reduce cash per continuing share despite reported EPS growth.
