# Regions Financial: low-cost deposits, fee attachment, and Southeast credit

## What the company does

Regions Financial is a Southeast relationship bank. It gathers deposits,
provides commercial and consumer loans, operates treasury-management and wealth
services, and earns net-interest income and fees after funding, credit,
compensation, technology, regulation, liquidity, and capital costs.

The control point is the operating relationship with households, businesses, and
institutions. Treasury management and wealth services can make the relationship
more useful and deposits more stable. The bank still carries the full balance
sheet: deposits can reprice or leave, loans can deteriorate, securities can lose
value, and capital must remain available for stress.

charge-offs approximately `42` basis points. Those figures support a disciplined
funding and credit reading. They should be tested against deposit mix,
nonaccruals, criticized assets, reserves, and risk-weighted assets.

## Where the economics sit

### Deposits and funding

Low-cost operating deposits can support a bank's margin and payment relationship.
They are earned through service, convenience, local coverage, and trust; they
are not permanent equity. A deposit customer can move cash or demand higher
rates when alternatives improve or confidence weakens.

Track interest-bearing and noninterest-bearing deposits, deposit beta, rate paid,
uninsured and concentrated balances, brokered funding, transaction activity,
and wholesale borrowing. A low current deposit cost is valuable only if it holds
after competitors raise rates and customers become more rate-sensitive.

### Treasury and wealth management

Treasury-management income and wealth-management income diversify Regions beyond
loan spread. The packet reports new annual records in both categories. The
forensic test is whether the fees arise from durable client relationships and
transaction balances, or from temporary market levels, pricing, or one-time
activity.

Reconcile fee growth to client retention, deposits, payment volume, assets under
management, market appreciation, net flows, fee rates, employee compensation,
technology, fraud, and service cost. Fee diversification reduces concentration;
it does not remove operational or regulatory burden.

### Loans and Southeast exposure

Regions's loan book reflects households, commercial borrowers, and property in
the Southeast. Local knowledge may improve underwriting and retention, but the
region remains exposed to employment, migration, construction, commercial real
estate, consumer leverage, and refinancing conditions.

The required loan bridge is:

`originations - repayments + purchases - sales = ending loans`

Read this with yield, fee income, delinquency, nonaccruals, criticized and
classified loans, charge-offs, allowance coverage, collateral, and capital. A
low current charge-off rate is not enough if risk is building in a growing or
recently modified portfolio.

### Hedging and net-interest income

Regions emphasizes hedging and funding discipline. Net-interest income can be
protected by balance-sheet mix and derivatives, but hedging creates cost,
counterparty, collateral, and basis risk. Separate reported NII into asset
yield, funding cost, deposit mix, hedge effects, securities income, and purchase
accounting or other temporary items.

The normalized question is whether deposits remain affordable and loan yields
remain productive across a full rate cycle, not whether one quarter's margin
looks favorable.

## Earnings quality and forensic review

### Adjusted EPS

Regions reported adjusted EPS of approximately `$2.33` for FY2025 versus GAAP
EPS of approximately `$2.30`, and Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of approximately `$0.68`
versus GAAP `$0.64`. The gap is not large, but each adjustment still needs a
clear economic explanation.

Check whether restructuring, merger, securities, legal, technology, or other
items recur. Reconcile adjusted earnings with net income, operating cash flow,
credit provisions, capital expenditures, stock compensation, diluted shares,
and regulatory capital.

### Credit and reserve timing

The approximately `42` basis-point Q2 annualized net charge-off rate is positive
evidence, not a conclusion. Test it against delinquency migration, nonaccruals,
criticized loans, classified loans, allowance coverage, modifications,
recoveries, and collateral values.

Reserve releases raise current earnings without improving the underlying loan
book. The key question is whether the allowance reflects future loss emergence
by portfolio, geography, borrower, property type, vintage, and macro scenario.

### Capital and liquidity

Regions's CET1 ratio was approximately `10.7%` in Q2 2026. Capital must absorb
credit losses, support risk-weighted assets, satisfy regulators and rating
agencies, and protect depositors. Dividends and repurchases are value-creating
only after those needs are covered.

Track tangible common equity, risk-weighted assets, CET1, unrealized securities
losses, liquidity, deposit runoff, borrowing capacity, share count, repurchase
price, and payout. ROATCE can improve through buybacks while the remaining bank
has less capital flexibility.

### Efficiency

Expense discipline is useful when it comes from scale, process, and technology.
It is not useful if it defers credit staff, fraud monitoring, service quality,
cybersecurity, or regulatory work. Compare expense ratios with employee levels,
technology spending, service incidents, fraud, legal cost, and later credit or
regulatory losses.

## Balance-sheet resilience and macro conditions

The Lyn Alden lens focuses on liquidity, rates, regional growth, deposits, and
credit. Higher rates can support asset yields but increase deposit competition
and borrower stress. Lower rates can reduce deposit costs while compressing
asset yields and changing prepayment and refinancing behavior.

Stress cases should include:

- deposit outflows and higher deposit beta;
- Southeast commercial-real-estate weakness;
- unemployment and consumer delinquencies;
- securities losses and liquidity sales;
- hedge and counterparty losses;
- lower treasury and wealth activity;
- fraud, cyber, and operational failures;
- higher risk-weighted assets and capital requirements;
- capital returns before credit deterioration is visible.

## Damodaran lens: translate the relationship-bank narrative

The narrative is that Regions can earn durable returns through low-cost deposits,
Southeast relationships, treasury and wealth fees, hedging, and disciplined
credit. That narrative requires:

1. Deposits remain stable at an acceptable cost through rate and confidence
   changes.
2. Loans grow from sound demand and underwriting rather than relaxed standards.
3. Net-interest income remains productive after normalized deposit pricing and
   hedge cost.
4. Treasury and wealth fees grow with client retention and flows after their
   staffing, technology, fraud, and compliance burden.
5. Charge-offs and reserves remain consistent with the full credit cycle.
6. Efficiency gains do not defer investment needed to protect the franchise.
7. Dividends and repurchases preserve CET1, liquidity, and future growth capacity.

Value Regions from normalized common earnings or return on tangible common equity,
with separate assumptions for credit losses, deposit beta, securities, fee
quality, capital, and payout. Current ROATCE is not a sufficient valuation input
if it depends on unusually low losses or aggressive share reduction.

## Valuation screen

A market snapshot at 23:15 UTC on 2026-09-14 showed an equity value of
approximately `$25.736B`. These are normalized earnings sensitivities, not a
price target.

| Case | Normalized net income | Illustrative multiple | Implied equity value | What must be true |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Downside | `$1.5B` | `8x` | `$12.0B` | Deposit costs rise, credit losses normalize higher, and fee growth weakens |
| Base | `$2.2B` | `11x` | `$24.2B` | Funding and fee discipline support normalized regional-bank returns |
| Upside | `$2.8B` | `13x` | `$36.4B` | Low-cost deposits, fee growth, and credit quality persist through the cycle |

The screen must be checked against tangible book, CET1, securities duration,
deposit concentration, reserve coverage, and normalized loss assumptions.
The current market value is above the illustrative base case and below the
upside case. That comparison is not a margin-of-safety conclusion: the base
requires funding and fee discipline to survive deposit repricing, a Southeast
credit cycle, and the capital needed to keep the relationship system growing.

## Thesis breakers and filing tests

| Question | Evidence supporting the thesis | Evidence weakening it |
|---|---|---|
| Are deposits durable? | Low deposit cost, stable balances, transaction activity, and diversified funding | Rate-sensitive outflows, high pricing, concentration, or wholesale dependence |
| Are fees durable? | Treasury and wealth records continue with stable clients, flows, and fee rates | Market-driven income, fee compression, outflows, or rising service cost |
| Is credit controlled? | Low losses with stable criticized loans, delinquencies, reserves, and collateral | Losses or nonaccruals rise after loan growth, modification, or CRE stress |
| Is NII normalized? | Margin holds after deposit repricing and hedge effects | Margin depends on temporary rate mismatch or securities/hedge gains |
| Is capital adequate? | CET1 and liquidity remain strong after stress and distributions | Payout weakens capital before the loss cycle turns |
| Are efficiency gains real? | Lower cost per relationship while control and service quality improve | Cost cuts defer technology, fraud, credit, or compliance work |

## Bottom line

Regions controls a Southeast relationship system built around operating deposits,
commercial and consumer credit, treasury management, and wealth services. Its
potentially cleaner economics are low-cost funding and fee attachment. Its
unavoidable burden is the regulated bank balance sheet and the need to preserve
liquidity and capital when borrowers and depositors respond to the same macro
shock.

FY2025 and Q2 2026 show strong earnings, record fee categories, approximately
`1.69%` interest-bearing deposit cost, approximately `42` basis-point annualized
net charge-offs, and approximately `10.7%` CET1. The next proof is whether those
figures remain sound after a normalized rate path, Southeast property stress,
and a complete deposit and credit cycle.

**Evidence grade:** Strong for FY2025 and Q2 2026 reported earnings, EPS,
deposit cost, CET1, charge-offs, and fee-record claims; medium for deposit
durability and treasury/wealth economics; unresolved for through-cycle credit,
liquidity, and capital-return behavior.
