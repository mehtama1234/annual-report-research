## Bottom line

Republic Services is a national environmental-services network with collection
routes, transfer stations, landfills, recycling facilities, special-waste and
hazardous-treatment assets, and sustainability projects. Its control point is
the combination of local route density and permitted disposal or processing
access. A customer is not buying only a truck pickup; it is buying a dependable
regulated service that can remove material and deliver it to an approved outlet.

FY2025 revenue was `$16.591B`, operating cash flow was `$4.296B`, capital
expenditures were about `$1.887B`, and adjusted free cash flow was `$2.43B`.
Republic invested `$1.1B` in value-creating acquisitions and returned `$1.6B`
to shareholders. A strict screen after capital expenditures and that disclosed
acquisition investment is roughly `$1.31B` before other owner claims.

The current judgment is positive but price-sensitive. Republic shows durable
pricing and route economics, but the investment case depends on acquisition
returns, landfill and environmental obligations, sustainability-project output,
and whether cash per diluted share rises after dividends, repurchases, and
growth spending. It is a useful scale peer to Waste Management, not merely a
second generic waste company.

## What Republic controls

`customer route -> collection fleet -> transfer or direct haul -> landfill, recycling, treatment, or resource project -> compliance -> renewal`

As of the FY2025 filing, Republic operated 377 collection operations, 255
transfer stations, 79 recycling centers, 207 active landfills, treatment and
disposal facilities, hazardous-waste facilities, salt-water disposal wells,
deep-injection wells, industrial wastewater facilities, and polymer centers.
The network controls local service density and disposal access, but it remains
exposed to fuel, labor, weather, waste volumes, recycling prices, regulation,
permitting, and customer concentration.

The economic layers differ:

- collection depends on route density, price, yield, volume, labor, fleet
  productivity, and retention;
- disposal depends on landfill capacity, internalization, permits, development,
  closure, post-closure, and environmental compliance;
- recycling and Polymer Centers depend on throughput, contamination,
  commodity prices, and end-market demand;
- sustainability projects depend on project completion, gas or material input,
  operating cost, credits, contracts, and maintenance capital; and
- special-waste and treatment assets carry higher regulatory and liability
  exposure than ordinary municipal collection.

The screen is intentionally stricter than adjusted free cash flow. Republic's
free-cash-flow definition subtracts net capital expenditures, but it does not
represent residual cash available after all discretionary acquisitions. When
acquisitions are a recurring method of adding routes, disposal access, and
sustainability capacity, they are part of the long-run owner-cash requirement.

The 2025 cash flow also requires attention to working capital, capping, closure
and post-closure spending, asset sales, and acquisition-related payments. A
single year's cash conversion is not enough to establish a permanent maintenance
requirement.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. Price and yield are doing more work than volume

The reported model is pricing-led. This can be durable because local waste
service is difficult to replace and disposal capacity is limited. It can also
mask weak customer retention or lower commercial and construction activity.

The next filings should separate core price, yield, volume, acquisition growth,
fuel recovery, churn, bad debt, and route-level labor and fleet costs. Revenue
growth without stable route density is not the same as a strengthening service
network.

### 2. Acquisition returns must be measured after integration

Republic invested about `$1.1B` in acquisitions during 2025 and had already
invested more than `$700M` in acquisitions by Q1 2026. Acquisitions can add
routes, transfer access, disposal capacity, and sustainability assets. They can
also require customer retention, fleet replacement, facility investment,
integration labor, debt, and environmental obligations.

The correct return calculation is:

`purchase cash + integration cost + acquired capital needs + assumed liabilities -> incremental cash after integration`

Annualized revenue or adjusted EBITDA from acquired operations is not enough.
Track acquired revenue, margin, cash flow, customer retention, route density,
capex, environmental obligations, and the time required to reach target returns.

### 3. Sustainability assets add both opportunity and capital risk

Republic operates renewable-natural-gas projects, recycling facilities, and
Polymer Centers. These assets may earn returns from processing, commodity sales,
customer contracts, and policy support. Their economics are different from
ordinary collection routes and should be disclosed by project cohort.

The test is project completion, throughput, realized price, contamination,
operating expense, credit or subsidy dependence, maintenance capital, and cash
return. The sustainability label does not establish an economic return.

### 4. Landfill and environmental obligations are real claims

Landfill closure, post-closure, capping, remediation, leachate, and financial
assurance can require cash long after the current collection revenue is earned.
Treatment, hazardous-waste, injection, and industrial wastewater assets carry
additional regulatory and liability exposure.

Compare reserve changes with actual payments, permitted capacity, remaining
life, closure timing, surety, letters of credit, restricted assets, and capital
spending. EBITDA excludes depreciation and depletion, but the physical assets
still require replacement and the environmental obligations still require cash.

### 5. Adjusted free cash flow is useful but incomplete

Adjusted FCF helps measure liquidity, but the company itself describes it as a
non-GAAP measure. It should be reconciled to operating cash, capital spending,
acquisitions, asset-sale proceeds, capping and closure spending, debt service,
and share-count changes.

If the company simultaneously pays dividends, repurchases shares, buys routes,
and expands sustainability capacity, the analyst must decide which outflows are
required to preserve the current business and which represent optional growth.
That decision should be explicit in valuation rather than hidden inside an
adjusted measure.

## Balance-sheet and liquidity lens

Republic's recurring service receipts and scale support liquidity, but debt,
leases, environmental claims, acquisition commitments, and capital projects
remain ahead of common shareholders. The relevant stress case is a period of
weaker commercial and construction volume combined with higher wages, fuel,
interest rates, and landfill or recycling costs.

The company can remain liquid under that stress while producing lower per-share
returns. Therefore the balance-sheet test is not only survival. It is whether
Republic can keep funding fleet and landfill needs, maintain its dividend,
repurchase shares at sensible prices, and make acquisitions without increasing
debt or diluted shares faster than common cash.

## Alden lens: liquidity and durability

Republic has durable features: recurring local service, route density, permitted
disposal access, a large operating footprint, and compliance requirements that
make replacement providers difficult to assemble. Macro risks are commercial
activity, construction, municipal finances, fuel, labor, interest rates,
weather, recycling commodities, and environmental regulation.

The key durability question is whether national scale improves cash resilience
or merely supports a larger asset and acquisition base. Scale should show up in
route productivity, pricing, retention, internalization, operating cash, and
capital efficiency—not only in customer count or adjusted EBITDA.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative is: “Republic can compound national route density, pricing,
disposal access, sustainability assets, and disciplined acquisitions into
durable cash.” The valuation model must specify:

1. core price, yield, volume, acquisition growth, and customer retention;
2. route productivity, labor cost, fuel, fleet replacement, and transfer costs;
3. landfill utilization, permitted capacity, development, closure, and reserves;
4. recycling and Polymer Center throughput, contamination, and commodity price;
5. RNG and sustainability-project capital, output, support, and return;
6. acquired revenue, margin, integration cost, and capital intensity;
7. debt, interest, leases, surety, environmental claims, and liquidity; and
8. dividends, repurchases, stock compensation, shares, and cash per share.

Republic is a mature platform with continuing acquisition and sustainability
investment. A premium multiple is justified only if the acquired and developed
assets add cash after all associated claims and if the mature route base remains
stable under price increases.

## Valuation screen

The September 11, 2026 market snapshot showed equity value of approximately
`$68.5B`. The screen starts with the `$1.309B` residual after 2025 capital
expenditures and disclosed acquisition investment, then tests higher normalized
cash outcomes if acquisition returns and sustainability projects mature.

| Case | Normalized common owner cash | Equity cash multiple | Implied common equity value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | `$1.3B` | `35x` | `$45.5B` |
| Base | `$2.0B` | `35x` | `$70.0B` |
| Bull | `$2.6B` | `35x` | `$91.0B` |

The market value is close to the base case in this simple screen. The market is
therefore requiring continued pricing, stable route economics, and enough return
from acquisitions and sustainability projects to lift common cash above the
strict 2025 residual.

## Thesis breakers and next filings

The thesis weakens if:

- price and yield rise while volume, retention, or cash per route deteriorate;
- acquisitions increase but acquired margins, cash conversion, or route density
  fail to improve;
- sustainability assets require repeated capital without reaching planned
  throughput or return;
- landfill closure, remediation, environmental, or surety claims rise faster
  than operating cash and reserves;
- adjusted FCF grows while cash after acquisitions and capital remains flat;
- debt, interest, dividends, and repurchases consume cash needed for the network;
  or
- diluted per-share cash fails to improve despite reported EBITDA growth.

The next filings should provide:

1. core price, yield, volume, acquisition growth, and retention;
2. cash after capital expenditures, acquisitions, closure, and integration;
3. acquired route and disposal economics by cohort;
4. recycling, Polymer Center, and RNG project throughput and returns;
5. landfill capacity, closure payments, reserves, and financial assurance;
6. debt, leases, interest, dividends, repurchases, and share count; and
7. cash per diluted share after all recurring claims.

## Evidence grade

- **High confidence:** FY2025 revenue, operating cash flow, capital spending,
  acquisition investment, adjusted FCF, operating footprint, Q1-Q2 2026
  results, and raised 2026 guidance from filed or company-issued materials.
- **Medium confidence:** the normalized cash cases and the split between
  maintenance and growth capital. These require project-cohort and reserve
  disclosures.
- **Open:** long-run acquisition returns, sustainability-project returns,
  route retention under continued pricing, and per-share value after capital
  returns.
