# Restaurant Brands International Inc. Full Forensic Analysis Memo

Date baseline: 2026-09-13  
Primary source: [RBI 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1618756/000161875626000017/qsr-20251231.htm)  
Companion packet: [company-packet.md](/home/mehtama1/git-repo/annual-report-research/extracted/services/restaurants/restaurant-brands-international-inc/company-packet.md)

## Bottom line

RBI is a global franchisor and brand controller, not a conventional restaurant
operator. More than 95% of its restaurants are franchised; the parent owns
brand standards, royalty contracts, advertising architecture, digital systems,
supply-chain interfaces, and selected company restaurants. That makes the
corporate model asset-light relative to the network, but not burden-free.

The 2025 evidence is mixed. System-wide sales were approximately $46.8B and
consolidated revenue reached $9.4B, yet income from operations fell to $2.2B
from $2.4B. Operating cash flow rose to $1.714B, but $265M went to property and
equipment, $152M to acquired franchised restaurants, and $1.108B to common and
Partnership-unit distributions. Long-term debt was $13.25B, with $516M of net
interest expense. The main question is whether RBI owns a durable royalty and
brand-control engine, or whether it is increasingly using corporate capital to
repair, acquire, remodel, and finance a network whose franchisee economics are
under pressure.

This is an investigation memo, not a recommendation. It does not establish
fraud. It identifies where reported corporate quality can diverge from the
health of the restaurant system.

## What the company actually controls

The economic chain is:

`consumer occasion -> franchisee restaurant execution -> system sales -> royalty, property, supply-chain and advertising flows -> RBI cash`

RBI's four brands—Tim Hortons, Burger King, Popeyes, and Firehouse Subs—had
more than 33,000 restaurants in more than 120 countries and territories. 2025
system-wide sales were approximately $46.762B. Consolidated revenue was only
$9.434B because franchisee restaurant sales are not RBI revenue. The parent
therefore controls the monetization layer, while franchisees carry much of the
local labor, food, rent, maintenance, financing, and execution burden.

The denominator matters: system sales are a network-health measure, not an
owner-cash measure. Advertising revenue also should not be treated as ordinary
corporate profit without tracing the corresponding fund obligations and spend.

The simple cash bridge is:

| 2025 item | $ millions |
|---|---:|
| Operating cash flow from continuing operations | 1,714 |
| PP&E purchases | (265) |
| Acquired franchised restaurants, net of cash acquired | (152) |
| Approximate cash after these investments | 1,297 |
| Common-share and Partnership-unit distributions | (1,108) |

The $1.297B residual is not distributable owner earnings. It excludes debt
principal, required remodeling or franchisee incentives embedded in broader
capital spending, working-capital volatility, and the economic claim of
noncontrolling interests. RBI's 2025 net income also included a $126M net loss
from discontinued operations; continuing-operations net income was $1.201B,
of which $299M was attributable to noncontrolling interests. The common
shareholder claim is therefore narrower than consolidated cash flow.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering tests

### 1. System sales can mask franchisee stress

System-wide sales growth can come from new units, price, mix, currency, or
acquired company restaurants. None proves that a typical franchisee earns an
adequate return after labor, food, rent, debt service, remodels, and taxes.
RBI itself identifies franchisee profitability, consumer spending, inflation,
labor costs, commodities, and supply-chain conditions as material risks.

The next filing should be read for traffic versus price/mix, closures,
delinquencies, remodel obligations, development commitments, and incentives.
Royalty growth accompanied by weaker franchisee returns is a burden-transfer
pattern, not clean organic quality.

### 2. The company is adding corporate restaurant exposure

Company restaurant sales rose to $2.348B in 2025 from $1.592B in 2024, while
company restaurant expenses rose to $1.968B from $1.328B. RBI spent $152M to
acquire franchised restaurants in 2025 after $540M in 2024. The Carrols
acquisition and subsequent remodeling/refranchising strategy may create
long-term brand value, but it also makes the supposedly asset-light parent
the shock absorber when franchisees cannot fund the system.

Track acquired-store sales, four-wall margins, remodel spend, refranchising
proceeds, franchisee incentives, and the time between acquisition and sale.
An acquisition followed by refranchising can improve royalty optics while
leaving the parent with execution, capital, and impairment risk in the interim.

### 3. Adjusted operating measures require a cash and capital reconciliation

RBI's segment tables exclude selected amortization, transaction costs,
restructuring/advisory costs, and other adjustments from adjusted operating
income. That may be useful for operating comparison, but it cannot replace
cash available after interest, capex, restaurant acquisitions, and brand
investment. In 2025 the company reported $2.584B of adjusted operating income
at segment level versus $2.202B of consolidated operating income.

Share-based compensation and non-cash incentive compensation were $151M in
the 2025 cash-flow reconciliation, while total share-based compensation
expense was $137M. That is material relative to common net income of $776M.
It should be treated as a recurring economic cost when assessing per-share
compounding, not automatically as free value.

### 4. Advertising funds are a pass-through with timing risk

2025 advertising revenue was $1.217B and advertising expense was $1.358B.
These funds support the system rather than representing ordinary high-margin
corporate earnings. The cash-flow statement also shows a $44M payment of
tenant inducements to franchisees and working-capital movements in receivables,
inventories, payables, and accrued liabilities. Reconcile fund cash, fund
liabilities, advertising spend, franchisee incentives, and timing differences
before assigning a permanent multiple to reported revenue or EBITDA.

## Alden lens: liquidity and durability

RBI has valuable brand and distribution scale, but its durability is a chain,
not a slogan:

`consumer affordability -> franchisee liquidity -> store labor and execution -> comparable sales -> royalty cash -> debt service and reinvestment`

The corporate balance sheet can look resilient while the weakest franchisees
are already underfunded. High interest rates, food and labor inflation,
currency/geopolitical shocks, and weaker discretionary spending can first show
up as slower development, deferred remodels, closures, or demands for
franchise incentives. The corporate system is durable only if it can preserve
unit economics through that cycle without excessive leverage or corporate
store absorption.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to valuation

The proper model is not `system sales x franchisor multiple`. It is:

`system sales -> unit growth and same-store sales -> franchisee cash returns -> royalty/property/advertising flows -> RBI operating cash -> interest, capex, acquisitions, and distributions -> equity value`

At the September 11, 2026 market snapshot, QSR equity value was approximately
$35.4B. Against 2025 operating cash flow, that is roughly 20.7x OCF before
capex, acquisitions, and interest. Against the $1.297B post-PP&E/post-store-
acquisition residual, the equity value is roughly 27x. These are screening
ratios, not price targets, and the second denominator still ignores debt
principal, NCI, required brand investment, and franchisee economics.

The valuation should explicitly vary: comparable sales and traffic, net unit
growth, royalty rates, franchisee failure/remodel support, company-store
margin, interest expense, capital expenditures, acquisition intensity, and
the share of cash economically owed to noncontrolling interests.

## Incentives and thesis breakers

The structure naturally rewards system-wide sales, net restaurant growth,
adjusted operating income/EBITDA, distributions, and total shareholder return.
Those measures can improve through price, acquisitions, refranchising,
adjusted add-backs, leverage, or risk transfer even when the average
franchisee's return is weakening. The forensic task is to connect management's
reported scorecard to store-level cash outcomes.

The thesis is materially weakened if the next filings show:

- system sales growing while traffic, franchisee margins, development, or
  closures deteriorate;
- repeated corporate purchases of troubled franchisees followed by expensive
  remodels or weak refranchising proceeds;
- royalties and advertising revenue rising only because of fee increases or
  incentives that impair franchisee health;
- cash after capex and acquired restaurants failing to cover distributions and
  debt service without new borrowing; or
- adjusted earnings expanding while GAAP earnings, owner cash, or per-share
  value stagnate after SBC and NCI claims.

## Evidence grade and unresolved questions

- **Filing fact:** system sales, segment revenue, operating cash flow, capex,
  acquired restaurants, debt, advertising-fund flows, NCI, and refranchising
  economics are anchored to the 2025 Form 10-K.
- **Disclosed/packet-backed:** Q2 2026 comparable-sales, unit-growth, and brand
  outlook claims are management-reported until the next filing supplies the full
  cash and franchisee bridge.
- **Inference:** RBI controls brand, franchise contracts, and royalty access, but
  franchisees carry most labor, food, rent, remodel, and local financing burden;
  corporate acquisitions and leverage partially reverse that asset-light benefit.
- **Unresolved:** the next filing must reconcile system sales and royalties to
  franchisee health, acquired-store returns, distributions, debt service, and
  NCI. Rising royalties alongside closures, weak development, or recurring
  acquisition cash use would weaken the thesis.
