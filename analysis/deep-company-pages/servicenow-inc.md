# ServiceNow, Inc. — workflow control with recurring software cash and a real dilution burden

Research date: 2026-09-14  
Evidence window: FY2025 and Q2 2026  
Ticker: NOW  
Evidence grade: A for reported financials and operating metrics; B for the long-run economics of AI contract value, acquisitions, and the durability of workflow centrality.

## Bottom line

ServiceNow sells software that records, routes, approves, and measures work inside large organizations. Its control point is not a physical asset. It is the accepted system through which incidents, requests, approvals, security events, employee processes, and other work are assigned and tracked. Once the system is integrated with identity, data, permissions, reporting, and internal controls, replacement becomes expensive and disruptive.

The model is materially lighter than Oracle’s AI-cloud build or Microsoft’s hyperscale infrastructure program. ServiceNow reported FY2025 revenue of $13.278B, operating cash flow of $5.444B, and property-and-equipment purchases of $868M. That produces a simple operating-cash-less-property screen of $4.576B; ServiceNow’s own free-cash-flow presentation differs slightly because it uses its defined capital-expenditure measure. The company still has a meaningful cost that is easy to understate: FY2025 stock-based compensation was $1.955B, and deferred commissions used $758M of cash. A simple cash screen therefore overstates cash available to common owners unless dilution, sales investment, acquisitions, and required product development are considered.

Q2 2026 strengthened the operating thesis. Subscription revenue was $3.877B, current remaining performance obligations were $13.20B, total RPO was $29.0B, and ServiceNow AI exceeded $1B of annual contract value. Those metrics show demand and contract visibility. They do not prove that every AI contract is incremental, that customers will renew at the same price, or that the product’s growing compute and support costs will remain small relative to revenue.

## What ServiceNow controls

The operating chain is:

`organizational complexity -> workflow record -> routing and approval -> measurable execution -> renewal and expansion`

ServiceNow controls the record of work and the rules that determine who acts next. This creates value when customers need fewer manual handoffs, clearer accountability, faster approvals, and auditable AI-assisted decisions. The platform becomes more important as customers add departments, regulations, security requirements, and automated agents.

ServiceNow does not control the customer’s total IT budget, implementation partner quality, employee adoption, competing workflow tools, model providers, or the customer’s ability to replace parts of the platform with internal software. It also does not own the customer’s underlying business process. It must continually prove that centralizing work in its platform reduces cost or risk enough to justify the subscription.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Subscription revenue | 8.680 | 10.646 | 12.883 | Main recurring revenue stream |
| Total revenue | 8.971 | 10.984 | 13.278 | Professional services remained a small, lower-margin component |
| Net income | 1.731 | 1.425 | 1.748 | GAAP result includes stock compensation and other operating costs |
| Operating cash flow | 3.398 | 4.267 | 5.444 | Includes deferred revenue and non-cash compensation |
| Property and equipment purchases | 0.694 | 0.852 | 0.868 | Direct physical-capital burden |
| Operating cash less property purchases | 2.704 | 3.415 | 4.576 | Simple screen before acquisitions and other owner claims |
| Stock-based compensation | 1.604 | 1.746 | 1.955 | Recurring compensation excluded from non-GAAP measures |

The company converts revenue into cash effectively, but the conversion is not costless. In FY2025, deferred revenue supplied $1.179B of operating cash, while deferred commissions used $758M. Deferred revenue reflects customer billing before service delivery. Deferred commissions reflect cash paid to acquire contracts whose revenue will be recognized over time. The two items must be read together.

## Current operating evidence

Q2 2026 total revenue was $3.987B and subscription revenue was $3.877B, approximately 97% of total revenue. Current RPO is the portion expected to be recognized within the next twelve months; it was $13.20B, up 21% year over year. Total RPO was $29.0B, also up 21%.

The company reported 123 transactions over $1M of net new annual contract value in Q2 and 658 customers with more than $5M of ACV. These indicators suggest expansion inside large accounts, but they also increase concentration and implementation risk. A few large customers can support growth while making renewal, pricing, and procurement outcomes more important to the consolidated result.

ServiceNow AI crossed $1B of annual contract value in Q2 2026. Annual contract value is a sales measure of the annualized value of signed contracts; it is not the same as recognized revenue or cash collected. The investor should determine how much AI ACV is new budget, how much replaces existing platform value, whether usage-based components create variable revenue, and whether customer support and cloud-service costs rise with adoption.

## Earnings quality and cash conversion

### Stock compensation is the central owner-cash adjustment

ServiceNow recorded $1.955B of stock compensation in FY2025, equal to about 36% of operating cash flow and about 15% of subscription revenue. This does not imply that the compensation is improper. It does mean that the company’s non-GAAP operating margin and free-cash-flow margin are not the same as cash available to an owner who is not receiving new shares.

The proper reconciliation is:

`operating cash flow - property purchases - acquisitions - recurring dilution cost = owner-cash screen`

The dilution cost should be examined through stock awards, shares issued, repurchases, and diluted shares. A repurchase can offset dilution, but buying back shares at a high valuation can also consume cash that could otherwise fund product development or reduce debt.

### Deferred revenue is visibility plus future service work

Customers often pay before ServiceNow recognizes revenue. This improves near-term cash flow and makes the revenue base more predictable. It also creates a delivery obligation. If customers are paying for multiple years, the company must continue operating the platform, supporting integrations, maintaining security, and adding the promised capabilities.

Current RPO is more useful than a vague bookings claim because it identifies revenue expected within twelve months. It still does not show customer profitability, renewal after the committed term, or the cost of servicing the contract. The next filings should compare RPO growth with subscription gross margin, support headcount, cloud-service cost, deferred commissions, and renewal behavior.

### Low physical capex does not mean low reinvestment

ServiceNow’s property spending was only $868M in FY2025, but the company also spent heavily on research and development, sales and marketing, customer support, implementation resources, cloud services, and acquisitions. Subscription gross margin was 80% in FY2025, while professional services and other revenue had a negative gross margin. That separation matters: a high subscription margin can coexist with lower-quality growth if services labor or customer success expands faster than subscription cash.

The 10-K says infrastructure hardware depreciation and third-party cloud-service costs increased as the company expanded data-center capacity, and it expects subscription gross margin to decrease slightly in 2026 due partly to cloud usage and acquired-intangible amortization. ServiceNow is still lighter than Oracle, but it is not free of infrastructure or delivery costs.

### Acquisitions need a return test

ServiceNow spent $1.084B on business combinations in FY2025 and $1.056B on strategic investments. Acquisitions can broaden the platform and improve retention, but they also create integration work, amortization, goodwill, and potential impairment. The analyst should track acquired revenue, customer overlap, retention, cross-sell, gross margin, incremental R&D, and the cash return relative to the purchase price.

## Lyn Alden lens: durability and liquidity

ServiceNow’s durability comes from workflow centrality and switching cost rather than from physical scarcity. The platform is resilient when customers view workflow records, approvals, and incident history as part of their control environment. In a weak enterprise-spending cycle, customers may delay new modules while retaining the core platform. That supports resilience but does not eliminate pricing pressure or seat and module rationalization.

The balance-sheet risk is lower than Oracle’s because ServiceNow does not need to finance a comparable data-center build. However, the company raised $4B of debt in May 2026, including notes due from 2028 through 2056. This does not create immediate solvency stress, but it adds interest and refinancing claims to a company whose valuation already depends on strong future cash growth.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative is: enterprise organizations need one accepted platform to manage work, security, operations, and AI agents, and ServiceNow can expand from IT workflows into a broader operating layer.

That narrative must become measurable assumptions:

- subscription renewal and price realization;
- large-customer expansion and module attach;
- cRPO conversion into recognized revenue;
- AI ACV conversion into recurring revenue and cash;
- usage-based AI revenue and associated cloud cost;
- subscription gross margin after third-party infrastructure;
- sales efficiency and deferred-commission amortization;
- acquisition returns and integration cost;
- stock compensation, share count, repurchases, and cash per share;
- terminal growth after the platform reaches a mature enterprise penetration level.

At a market value of approximately $137.080B on September 14, 2026, the equity was valued at roughly 30 times FY2025 operating cash less property purchases and roughly 29.8 times FY2025 free cash flow. A simple scenario screen is:

| Normalized annual owner-cash screen | Multiple | Implied equity value |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $4.0B bear case | 25x | $100B |
| $5.5B base case | 30x | $165B |
| $7.5B strong case | 35x | $262.5B |

These are scenario outputs, not price targets. The base case assumes the platform retains pricing power, AI ACV becomes durable subscription revenue, gross margin remains strong, acquisitions earn acceptable returns, and stock compensation does not absorb most of the cash growth.

## Thesis breakers and next evidence

The thesis weakens if cRPO grows while renewal, revenue, or cash per diluted share slows; if AI ACV grows mainly through repackaging existing subscriptions; if AI usage raises cloud and support cost faster than contract value; if subscription gross margin falls materially; if professional-services losses require more labor to make the platform usable; if deferred commissions rise faster than new recurring revenue; if acquisitions become the main source of growth; if stock compensation remains near the current share of operating cash; or if large customers consolidate workflow platforms and reduce modules.

The next filings should answer: Did the $1B AI ACV milestone convert into revenue and cash? Did cRPO growth remain near subscription growth? Did subscription gross margin stabilize after cloud and acquisition costs? Did large-customer expansion improve net retention? Did debt issuance fund buybacks or productive investment? Did diluted shares fall after repurchases? And did free cash flow per share grow after stock compensation and acquisition spending?
