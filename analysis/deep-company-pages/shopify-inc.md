# Shopify — merchant operating system, payment attachment, and the difference between GMV and owner cash

Date baseline: 2026-09-13  
Primary annual filing: [Shopify FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1594805/000159480526000007/shop-20251231.htm)  
FY2025 results: [Shopify FY2025 results](https://www.shopify.com/investors/press-releases)  
Companion packet: [Shopify company packet](/home/mehtama1/git-repo/annual-report-research/extracted/technology/internet-information-providers/shopify-inc/company-packet.md)

## Bottom line

Shopify provides software and services that let merchants create storefronts,
manage products, accept payments, sell across channels, analyze activity,
finance operations, and serve customers. Its subscription solutions anchor the
merchant relationship. Merchant solutions monetize the activity that occurs on
the platform, including payments, transaction services, and other commerce
tools.

The control point is the merchant’s operating workflow. Shopify becomes more
valuable when a merchant uses it for storefront, checkout, payments, inventory,
orders, marketing, fulfillment connections, and data. The control point is not
absolute: merchants still depend on discovery platforms, ad networks,
marketplaces, payment networks, logistics providers, and consumer demand.

FY2025 revenue was $11.556B, subscription solutions revenue was $2.752B,
merchant solutions revenue was $8.804B, gross merchandise volume was $378.4B,
and Shopify Payments penetration was 65.6%. Operating cash flow was $2.033B;
capital expenditures were $26M. Stock-based compensation was $449M. Shopify
also used $579M for purchases and originations of loans, net of repayments and
sales, and ended the year with $1.784B of loans and merchant cash advances on
the balance sheet.

The cleanest interpretation is not “software with incidental payments.” It is
a merchant platform whose economics are shifting toward transaction activity,
payments, and financial services. That broadens revenue but adds lower-margin
processing costs, credit losses, merchant survival risk, regulatory exposure,
and working-capital complexity.

Current judgment: Shopify has a strong relationship and workflow control point,
but valuation requires sustained merchant growth, payment attachment, high
cash conversion, and disciplined credit and equity economics. GMV growth and
recurring subscriptions alone do not establish common-owner returns.

## What Shopify controls

`merchant formation -> storefront and catalog -> checkout and payment -> order and customer data -> repeat sale -> broader platform attachment`

Shopify controls merchant software, storefront presentation, checkout, payment
integration, merchant data, app and partner connections, subscription billing,
transaction services, and selected financing products. Merchants control
product quality, prices, marketing, inventory, and customer relationships.
Consumers control demand. Search, social, marketplace, payment, and logistics
platforms control important external distribution and infrastructure.

The control point is stronger when Shopify is embedded in daily merchant
operations and switching would disrupt products, orders, payments, staff, and
data. It is weaker when a merchant is small, unprofitable, dependent on one
outside traffic source, or able to move storefront and payment functions
without meaningful loss.

securities. That liquidity is meaningful, but a portion supports payment,
working-capital, lending, investment, and strategic flexibility rather than
being automatically distributable.

## Subscription solutions and merchant solutions

Subscription revenue is the recurring software relationship. It is useful
because it can persist even when a merchant’s transaction volume changes. MRR
was $205M at year-end 2025 and rose to $221M in Q2 2026.

Merchant solutions revenue rises with merchant activity and attachment. It
includes payment processing and other services whose costs are often passed to
third parties. That creates a lower gross margin than subscription software,
but it increases Shopify’s participation in merchant success.

Track subscription retention, MRR, merchant cohorts, GMV per merchant,
Payments penetration, payment take rate, transaction costs, chargebacks,
merchant acquisition cost, and gross profit per merchant.

## GMV is activity, not Shopify revenue

GMV is the dollar value of orders facilitated through the platform, net of
refunds and including shipping, taxes, and other amounts defined by the
company. It is a measure of activity. Shopify keeps only a portion of the
economic value generated by that activity.

FY2025 GMV was $378.4B and Shopify Payments processed 65.6% of it. A higher
Payments penetration can improve data and monetization, but it also increases
processing cost, fraud, chargeback, payment-network dependence, and regulatory
responsibility.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. Stock compensation is a large economic cost

Stock-based compensation was $449M in 2025, $430M in 2024, and $615M in 2023.
It is included in operating expenses and added back in operating cash flow.
The economic cost remains because employees receive equity and continuing
shareholders may be diluted.

Track grants, vesting, diluted shares, repurchases, option exercises, tax
withholding, and compensation per employee. Measure cash per continuing share,
not only aggregate free cash flow.

### 2. Merchant lending converts platform data into credit risk

Shopify had $1.784B of loans and merchant cash advances at year-end 2025. The
company used $579M of cash for purchases and originations of loans, net of
repayments and sales during the year.

This can deepen the merchant relationship and support growth, but it means a
merchant slowdown can affect both transaction volume and loan performance.
Review originations, repayments, delinquency, provision for transaction and
loan losses, merchant concentration, funding terms, and the return on capital
after losses.

### 3. Payments attachment adds revenue and cost together

Shopify Payments penetration rose from 65.6% in 2025 to 68% in Q2 2026 in the
packet. The increase shows deeper attachment, but payment processing fees and
chargeback protection costs also rise with volume.

Track gross payment volume, retained take rate, third-party processing cost,
fraud, chargebacks, reserves, dispute rates, and net contribution after all
payment costs. Payments revenue is not subscription revenue.

### 4. Merchant health determines platform durability

Shopify can grow GMV through a small group of large merchants or through many
small merchants. The latter may have higher churn and weaker ability to pay.
The platform’s long-term value depends on merchant survival, repeat sales, and
profitability.

Track merchant cohort retention, GMV concentration, failed merchants, payment
delinquencies, customer acquisition cost, support cost, and merchant gross
profit. Strong platform growth with worsening merchant survival is a warning.

### 5. Growth can be financed by working-capital timing

Subscription revenues are often paid at the beginning of the applicable period,
while Shopify Plus merchants typically pay at the end of the monthly cycle.
Payment processing cash is received daily, but third-party processing fees and
settlements are also paid.

Review deferred revenue, payment liabilities, merchant balances, settlement
timing, receivables, refunds, and chargebacks. Cash received before the service
or settlement obligation is complete is not unrestricted owner cash.

### 6. Investment gains should be separated from platform earnings

Shopify holds marketable securities, equity investments, and equity-method
investments. Net gains and losses on these assets can move net income without
reflecting merchant-platform operating performance.

Track realized and unrealized investment gains, strategic investments,
valuation, liquidity, and any dependence of reported earnings on asset marks.

### 7. AI may strengthen or weaken the control point

Sidekick, Catalog, agentic storefronts, and Universal Commerce Protocol may
help merchants create and operate businesses while positioning Shopify inside
AI-mediated product discovery and purchase workflows.

The test is whether AI increases merchant retention, GMV, payment attachment,
pricing, and gross profit after compute, support, and product-development cost.
AI can also make storefront creation easier to replace or move, and external
AI interfaces may control discovery.

### 8. Founder control and incentives require monitoring

Shopify has a founder-led governance structure and long-term equity incentives.
Long-term ownership can align management with the value of the platform, but
it can also reduce outside shareholders’ ability to influence capital
allocation, acquisitions, investments, or compensation.

Review voting control, executive compensation, option grants, performance goals,
related-party arrangements, acquisition decisions, investment policy, and
buyback prices.

## Alden macro and durability lens

Shopify is exposed to consumer spending, small-business formation, interest
rates, payment losses, advertising costs, freight and supply-chain conditions,
merchant credit, and outside discovery platforms. A recession can reduce GMV,
merchant survival, loan performance, and new-store formation at the same time.

The company’s cash and investment position improves liquidity, but merchant
solutions tie the platform to payment networks, credit, reserves, and customer
settlement. Higher rates can reduce merchant demand and the value of future
platform cash while increasing the cost of merchant financing.

## Damodaran narrative-to-numbers lens

The narrative is “Shopify is the independent operating system for merchants
and the transaction layer for modern commerce.” The model must specify:

1. subscription retention, MRR, merchant cohorts, and pricing;
2. GMV, GMV per merchant, Payments penetration, take rate, and transaction cost;
3. subscription versus merchant-solutions gross margin;
4. merchant survival, customer acquisition, support, and churn;
5. loans, merchant cash advances, delinquency, funding, and losses;
6. external discovery dependence and direct customer ownership;
7. AI adoption, compute, development, and platform attachment;
8. deferred revenue, settlement obligations, refunds, and working capital;
9. stock compensation, founder control, dilution, and buybacks; and
10. normalized common cash after credit, payment, platform, and equity costs.

Shopify is in a high-growth platform phase moving toward a more mature
transaction and financial-services mix. A higher valuation requires durable
merchant attachment without allowing lower-margin payments and credit risk to
consume the benefit of scale.

## Valuation screen

The September 11, 2026 market snapshot showed equity value of approximately
$167.16B. The cases below use normalized common owner cash after platform
investment, stock-compensation dilution, payment risk, and merchant-credit
reserves.

| Case | Normalized common owner cash | Equity cash multiple | Implied common equity value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bear | $2.0B | 35x | $70B |
| Base | $3.0B | 40x | $120B |
| Bull | $4.0B | 45x | $180B |

The current value is near the bull case. The valuation therefore requires
continued growth above the current scale, rising merchant attachment, payment
and AI economics, strong merchant survival, and little deterioration in credit
losses or dilution. The bear case reflects a lower growth rate, weaker GMV,
payment-margin pressure, merchant failures, or higher capital and credit costs.

## Thesis breakers and next filings

The thesis weakens if:

- subscription retention or MRR slows while growth depends on payment volume;
- GMV grows but Shopify’s retained take rate or gross profit falls;
- Payments penetration rises with worsening fraud, chargebacks, or processing cost;
- merchant loans and advances grow faster than repayments and credit quality;
- small-merchant failure and churn increase;
- outside discovery platforms capture more of the customer relationship;
- AI reduces storefront differentiation or forces lower pricing;
- stock compensation and founder control reduce cash per continuing share;
- investment gains or non-operating marks support reported earnings; or
- buybacks continue at high prices while the platform needs more financial-services capital.

The next filings should show merchant cohorts, MRR, GMV, Payments penetration,
take rate, payment cost, chargebacks, loans and advances, delinquency,
provisioning, deferred revenue, settlement obligations, investment gains, AI
usage and cost, diluted shares, and cash per continuing share.

## Evidence grade

- Control point: **A-** — merchant workflow, payments, data, and integrations create strong attachment, but merchants depend on outside demand channels.
- Operating economics: **B+** — platform scale and merchant-solutions growth are strong, while payment and credit mix add burden.
- Cash conversion: **B+** — FY2025 operating cash was $2.033B, but loan originations, investments, and equity compensation require adjustment.
- Balance-sheet resilience: **A-** — cash and marketable securities are substantial, with no comparable leverage burden.
- Capital allocation: **B** — buybacks and investments are possible, but financial-services growth and stock compensation require discipline.
- Valuation: **C** — current value is near the bull case and requires continued high growth with strong payment and credit quality.
