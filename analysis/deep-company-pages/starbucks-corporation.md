# Starbucks Corporation — routine demand, stored value, labor, and owner cash

Date baseline: `2026-09-14`. Primary evidence: [Starbucks source ledger](../../extracted/services/restaurants/starbucks-corporation/source-ledger.md), [Starbucks company packet](../../extracted/services/restaurants/starbucks-corporation/company-packet.md), the FY2025 Form 10-K, and the Q1–Q3 FY2026 SEC filings listed in that ledger.

## Investment question

Starbucks sells beverages and food through company-operated and licensed
coffeehouses. Its economic system also includes a daily routine, a loyalty
account, stored payment value, mobile ordering, and a branded physical place.

The investment question is whether that relationship produces durable owner
cash after store labor, service investment, coffee and product costs, tariffs,
occupancy, closures, remodeling, technology, loyalty rewards, stored-value
liabilities, licensed-store economics, debt, and capital spending.

The FY2025 filing makes the question urgent. Revenue increased `3%` to `$37.2B`,
but operating income fell to `$2.9B` from `$5.4B`, operating margin fell to
`7.9%` from `15.0%`, and diluted EPS fell to `$1.63` from `$3.31`. Management
described a repair program, Back to Starbucks, that invested in coffeehouse
labor and service while also restructuring support operations and closing
stores. The recovery must therefore be judged by transactions, service quality,
store contribution, and cash—not by comparable sales alone.

## What the customer actually buys

The customer buys coffee, beverages, food, convenience, a predictable product,
and access to a familiar place. The customer may also buy the ability to order
through the app, earn Stars, use stored value, and repeat the same purchase
with less effort.

The operating sequence is:

`customer routine -> store visit or mobile order -> beverage and food
production -> labor and service execution -> payment and rewards accounting ->
repeat visit -> store and corporate cash`

Starbucks controls the brand, menu, store standards, loyalty program, digital
ordering, payment relationship, product quality, and company-operated store
execution. Licensed stores extend reach with a different burden split: the
licensee operates more of the location, while Starbucks supplies brand,
products, standards, and support and records a different revenue stream.

## Operating denominator

The proper starting measures are comparable transactions, average ticket,
store count, company-operated versus licensed mix, mobile-order volume,
Rewards activity, stored-value balances, and store-level margin. The investor
must then subtract:

- store labor and service hours;
- coffee, food, packaging, and tariff costs;
- occupancy, utilities, maintenance, and remodels;
- mobile-order and delivery complexity;
- loyalty rewards and payment processing;
- support-organization cost and restructuring;
- new-store and closure costs;
- interest, taxes, leases, and required capital; and
- stock compensation and diluted ownership.

Comparable sales combine traffic and spending per transaction. The two must be
separated because price and mix can raise revenue while customer frequency or
store economics weaken.

The current evidence supports an improving traffic trend, not a completed
turnaround. A durable conclusion requires several quarters of transaction
growth, store-level margin recovery, labor productivity, customer retention,
and cash generation after the repair program.

## Loyalty, stored value, and deferred revenue

Starbucks has a financial relationship beneath the store visit. Customers can
load money onto Starbucks Cards, use the app, and earn Stars. Registered card
holders are enrolled in Starbucks Rewards in key markets. Unredeemed card
balances and unredeemed Stars are recorded as deferred revenue or related
liabilities until the company provides the product or recognizes breakage
under its policy.

This creates both a benefit and a claim:

- stored value can bring cash before a beverage is sold and can increase repeat
  visits;
- Rewards can improve identification, frequency, personalization, and
  ordering convenience;
- unredeemed balances and Stars are obligations, not unrestricted earnings;
- redemption, expiration, breakage, refunds, and program changes affect timing;
  and
- the value of the relationship depends on profitable visits after rewards,
  payment, labor, and product costs.

The accounting test is to reconcile deferred revenue and stored-value changes
to actual redemption, breakage, sales, and cash. A rise in prepaid cash is not
the same as a rise in owner earnings.

## Company-operated and licensed store economics

Company-operated stores give Starbucks more control and more direct revenue,
but the parent pays wages, occupancy, product costs, maintenance, and service
investment. Licensed stores can reduce the parent’s direct store burden, but
they provide less control over execution and depend on licensee health.

The comparison is:

| Model | Parent captures | Other party carries | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Company-operated | Store sales, direct customer relationship, store contribution, data, and loyalty activity | Starbucks funds labor, occupancy, equipment, food, service, and repairs | Traffic recovery may not cover labor and occupancy cost |
| Licensed | Product sales, royalties, brand, standards, and relationship reach | Licensee funds more store labor, local occupancy, and execution | Licensee quality, store economics, and lower direct control |

The correct question is not which model has higher reported margin. It is which
model produces more durable common-owner cash after support, quality control,
loyalty, capital, and partner-health requirements.

## Cash conversion and reinvestment

The owner-cash bridge is:

`transactions × average ticket -> store revenue -> coffee, food, packaging,
labor, occupancy, and service -> company support and loyalty -> maintenance
capex, remodels, closures, and new stores -> working capital, leases, debt,
interest, and dilution -> owner cash`

Back to Starbucks deliberately spends on labor, service, training, and store
execution. That spending can create value if it restores transactions,
retention, throughput, and store margin. It destroys value if it becomes a
permanent expense without enough repeat demand or if management later cuts
service and repeats the traffic decline.

Capital spending must be separated into:

- maintenance and equipment replacement;
- store remodels and service improvements;
- new store growth;
- technology and mobile-order infrastructure; and
- support for closures, restructuring, or the China transaction.

A reported free-cash-flow recovery is only durable if it does not come from
underinvesting in stores or from temporary working-capital movements.

## Earnings-quality and financial-engineering tests

### 1. Comparable sales must be split into traffic and ticket

Q2 FY2026 provides the right structure: transactions rose `3.8%` and average
ticket rose `2.3%`. A future quarter with only ticket growth would be weaker
evidence than one with both transactions and ticket growth. The analysis must
also check product mix, promotions, delivery, and discounts.

### 2. Margin repair must include service quality

Labor cuts can improve short-term margin while making stores slower, less
reliable, or less pleasant. Labor additions can reduce short-term margin while
repairing the customer relationship. The correct test is labor hours and cost
per transaction against service time, customer retention, transactions, and
store contribution.

### 3. Stored value can support cash before earnings

Card loads and deferred revenue can make cash timing look strong. Track new
loads, redemptions, breakage, deferred revenue, refunds, and sales recognition.
Do not treat all prepaid balances as profit or as permanently available cash.

### 4. Licensed-store growth can hide execution differences

Licensed units may expand reported reach with less parent capital. The parent
must still fund product supply, standards, technology, support, and brand
protection. Track licensee openings, closures, comparable sales, quality
events, and licensee financial health.

### 5. China transaction changes distort consolidated comparisons

Q3 FY2026 revenue was down about `1%` while comparable store sales improved.
The reported revenue change reflects the Starbucks China transaction, so the
investor must separate continuing company economics, China exposure, proceeds,
royalties, and any changed control or capital claims.

### 6. Restructuring and closures may recur

Back to Starbucks includes support restructuring and coffeehouse closures.
Restructuring may be economically necessary, but recurring charges should not
be treated as permanently outside owner cash if the company needs repeated
reorganizations to maintain the model.

## Lyn Alden lens: routine demand and cost liquidity

Starbucks has more routine demand than a travel or high-ticket home company.
That helps frequency, but it does not make demand immune to household budgets.
Customers can reduce visits, trade down, make coffee at home, or choose a
cheaper competitor.

The company is also exposed to coffee prices, food inputs, wages, benefits,
tariffs, rent, utilities, and local labor availability. Higher costs can reduce
store margin before pricing fully catches up. The balance sheet and cash
generation may remain resilient while the customer relationship weakens if
management protects revenue with price and the traffic base falls.

The durable asset is the combined brand, location network, routine, Rewards
identity, stored payment, and mobile-order relationship. The durability test
is whether the system can maintain service and store economics through input
and labor cycles without losing customer frequency.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers and lifecycle

The recovery narrative should become:

`transactions × ticket × store base -> sales -> labor, product, occupancy,
loyalty, support, and service cost -> mature-store margin -> maintenance and
growth reinvestment -> owner cash and per-share value`

Starbucks is a mature network undergoing a repair phase. The valuation should
not assume that historical margins return automatically. It should model:

1. transaction recovery and retention;
2. average ticket and price elasticity;
3. company-operated versus licensed mix;
4. labor and service investment;
5. coffee, food, tariff, and occupancy costs;
6. store closures, remodels, and new-unit returns;
7. Rewards and stored-value economics;
8. technology and mobile-order productivity; and
9. China transaction effects and continuing exposure.

Reinvestment is value creating only if each labor, technology, remodel, or new
store dollar produces enough additional store contribution and repeat demand
to exceed its full cost. A recovery to prior earnings is not enough if it
requires more capital, more labor, or weaker customer value than the earlier
period.

### Initial owner-cash screen — 2026-09-14

The FY2025 Form 10-K reports `$4.748B` of operating cash flow and `$2.306B`
of PP&E purchases. The reported residual is therefore about `$2.442B` before
charging the `$318M` stock-compensation expense, leaving roughly `$2.124B`
after that equity cost. This is before deciding whether the current repair
program, store closures, remodels, technology, and China transaction create a
new normal or remain an unusually heavy investment period.

The comparable operating-cash-less-PP&E residuals were approximately `$3.675B`
in FY2023, `$3.318B` in FY2024, and `$2.442B` in FY2025. After stock
compensation, they were approximately `$3.372B`, `$3.010B`, and `$2.124B`.
The decline matters because a mature company-operated network should not be
valued on a historical peak while service and store economics are being
repaired.

The FY2025 filing also shows about `$2.771B` of dividends and `$177M` of net
cash paid for acquisitions. Dividends therefore exceeded the FY2025
SBC-adjusted residual; the distribution was not proof that all maintenance and
repair requirements had been funded without balance-sheet pressure.

A finance quote retrieved at `2026-09-15 00:15 UTC` showed a share price of
`$99.07` and equity value of approximately `$113.316B`. This is a dated market
input, not filing evidence or a price target.

| Screen | Bear | Base | Bull |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Normalized common-owner cash | `$1.200B` | `$2.200B` | `$3.200B` |
| Equity cash multiple | `25x` | `40x` | `50x` |
| Implied equity value | `$30.0B` | `$88.0B` | `$160.0B` |

The base case assumes service investment improves traffic and store economics
without fully restoring the earlier cash peak. The bear case assumes the repair
phase lasts, transactions remain weak, and input, labor, lease, and debt costs
absorb more of the ticket. The bull case requires transaction recovery, stable
service quality, effective store repair, and a lower recurring capital burden.
At the dated market value, the quote is above the base case and requires a
near-bull cash outcome or a multiple above the base assumption. The screen
must be refreshed after the next filing and is not a price target.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Management has reason to emphasize comparable sales, transactions, average
ticket, Rewards members, mobile ordering, licensed-store reach, adjusted
earnings, and Back to Starbucks milestones. Pair each measure with store
margin, labor cost, service time, cash, capex, deferred revenue, loyalty
redemption, licensed-store health, and diluted shares.

The thesis weakens if:

- comparable sales rise mainly through price and average ticket while
  transactions weaken;
- transaction recovery stalls after the initial Back to Starbucks improvement;
- margins recover by reducing service quality or by deferring maintenance;
- coffee, food, tariff, wage, rent, or benefit costs absorb pricing gains;
- stored-value cash rises while redemption, deferred revenue, or loyalty costs
  create a larger future claim;
- licensed-store growth masks closures, weak licensee returns, or quality
  problems;
- restructuring and closure charges recur as a normal cost of operating;
- mobile ordering increases complexity without better throughput or store
  contribution; or
- owner cash and diluted cash per share remain weak while dividends, buybacks,
  or debt continue.

closures and openings, capex by purpose, Rewards and stored-value balances,
redemption and breakage, licensed-store activity, restructuring, debt, leases,
stock compensation, diluted shares, and operating cash after maintenance.
