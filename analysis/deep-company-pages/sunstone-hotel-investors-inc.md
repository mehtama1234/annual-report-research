# Sunstone Hotel Investors — concentrated hotel ownership, renovation, and capital recycling

## The investment question

Sunstone owns a small portfolio of upper-upscale and luxury hotels. Its hotels
participate in travel, resorts, business gatherings, and destination occasions,
but the company earns through property ownership rather than through a broad
brand, loyalty, or reservation platform.

The question is whether strong room demand becomes durable common-owner value
after labor, property operations, renovation, repositioning, interest,
insurance, asset sales, buybacks, and the risk that one property or event
changes the whole portfolio result. Sunstone can create value through property
selection and redevelopment, but those choices make the earnings record more
dependent on timing than a fee-led hotel company.

## What Sunstone controls and what it carries

| Layer | Sunstone's position | Burden that remains with the owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hotel properties | Owns `14` hotels and `6,999` rooms across `7` states at 2025 year-end | Renovation, insurance, labor, property taxes, utilities, and local demand |
| Guest experience | Owns the physical destination and can reposition assets | Brand and loyalty economics usually belong to Marriott, Hyatt, Hilton, or other partners |
| Capital allocation | Recycles assets, funds renovations, and repurchases shares | Sale timing, redevelopment risk, debt, dilution, and the risk of selling future cash |
| Demand exposure | Captures ADR, occupancy, and RevPAR in upper-upscale and luxury markets | Event concentration, resort seasonality, weather, business travel, and high operating costs |
| Common shareholder | Receives distributions and property residual value | Must absorb the full capital and financing burden before that residual is available |

Sunstone is therefore a high-beta owner case. Apple Hospitality shows a broader
and more standardized rooms portfolio. Host Hotels shows a larger scale-heavy
owner. Sunstone adds a concentrated property-recycling and redevelopment model
where property-specific execution can matter as much as broad travel demand.

The sequence supports a qualified conclusion. Sunstone has current evidence of
upper-end travel demand and better property performance. It has not yet proved
that the improvement survives normalized renovation capital, financing cost,
asset-sale effects, and a weaker destination or business-travel cycle.

## How demand becomes owner cash

The operating chain is:

`destination or business travel -> rooms occupied -> ADR and RevPAR -> hotel
revenue -> labor, management, insurance, utilities, and property costs -> hotel
EBITDA -> renovation and repositioning capital -> debt and asset sales ->
common-owner cash`

Sunstone's economics have more moving pieces than the RevPAR number. A hotel
can show strong revenue while a renovation is consuming cash, a new property is
below stabilized margin, or an asset sale is changing the comparison base. The
investor must keep property operations, capital allocation, and financing in
separate columns.

Adjusted EBITDAre and Adjusted FFO are useful REIT measures, but neither should
be treated as industrial free cash flow. The owner-cash test must charge
replacement and renovation capital, interest, debt maturities, preferred or
operating-partnership claims, buybacks, and the dilution or leverage used to
fund acquisitions.

## Earnings quality and forensic questions

### 1. How much of RevPAR growth is normal demand?

Q1 2026 RevPAR rose `14.6%`, and Q2 rose `9.3%`; excluding Andaz Miami Beach,
growth was still `5.7%` and `4.3%`. That is useful evidence against a purely
single-property explanation. It does not settle the question because resort
mix, events, weather, renovation completion, and comparison periods can still
change the result. Follow occupancy, ADR, Total RevPAR, and property EBITDA by
asset rather than accepting the portfolio average.

### 2. Does a new property create normalized cash?

Andaz Miami Beach was a major swing factor in the recent results. A new or
repositioned hotel can lift RevPAR and future property value while consuming
cash during ramp-up. The test is stabilized occupancy, ADR, margin, renovation
spend, management cost, and the return on the capital committed. Exclude the
property temporarily when necessary, but do not exclude it permanently from
the owner-cash burden.

### 3. Do asset sales improve value or improve the reported period?

Sunstone sold Hilton New Orleans St. Charles and later identified Hyatt Regency
San Francisco as held for sale. Sales can be rational recycling, but they can
also remove weak assets from the operating base and create nonrecurring cash.
Record sale price, book value, debt retired, lost EBITDA, expected replacement
yield, and any gain or loss. A sale should not be treated as recurring FFO.

### 4. Is Adjusted FFO supported by property quality?

Adjusted FFO per diluted share rose `7.5%` in FY2025. The measure is relevant
for a REIT, but the reconciliation must be read for gains and losses, interest,
straight-line rent, recurring capital needs, and other adjustments. The core
question is whether per-share cash improves after the hotels are maintained and
the portfolio remains competitive, not merely whether a non-GAAP measure rises.

### 5. Can capital returns coexist with required reinvestment?

Sunstone reported more than `$170M` returned through buybacks and dividends in
2025 while also completing capital investments and pursuing property recycling.
That can be sensible when assets are undervalued and maintenance is funded. It
becomes fragile if buybacks or distributions depend on selling assets, using
debt, deferring renovation, or assuming a permanent high-end travel cycle.

## Balance-sheet resilience

The stress sequence is:

`weaker resort or business demand -> lower occupancy and hotel margin -> fixed
property costs consume more cash -> renovation and debt needs remain -> asset
sales, refinancing, or equity become more important`

Sunstone's concentration creates more property-specific risk than Apple's
diversified rooms base. The company may earn more when a high-quality asset
recovers, but it can also be hurt by one renovation, event, insurance issue,
local market shock, or sale process. Track debt maturities, interest coverage,
fixed versus floating rates, secured debt, property values, renovation
commitments, and liquidity after distributions.

The portfolio's small size also makes per-share analysis important. A hotel
sale, repurchase, or redevelopment can change the denominator materially. A
reduction in shares is valuable only if the company is not selling future cash
or underfunding the asset base to fund the repurchase.

## Lyn Alden lens: rates, travel income, and asset scarcity

Upper-upscale and luxury hotels can benefit from affluent travel, destination
scarcity, and high ADR. They remain exposed to employment, corporate travel,
wealth effects, rates, insurance, labor, and replacement cost. Inflation may
raise the room rate while also raising the cost of the building and the cost of
financing it.

The important macro test is whether demand is broad enough to cover the hard
asset. Sunstone's Q1 and Q2 results are encouraging, especially where RevPAR
grew after excluding part of the Andaz effect. The durability test is a weaker
travel environment with lower occupancy, stable renovation needs, and debt that
still has to be refinanced.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative is “a high-quality, concentrated hotel portfolio that can create
value through renovation, repositioning, recycling, and discounted buybacks.”
The numbers must specify RevPAR by property, stabilized hotel EBITDA, renovation
capital, debt cost, asset-sale gains, share count, FFO, AFFO, and the value of
the property portfolio under multiple cap-rate assumptions.

This dossier does not invent a current equity value or treat Adjusted EBITDAre
as owner cash. The packet preserves operating and FFO evidence but does not
preserve a complete current market snapshot and normalized property-level AFFO
bridge. A defensible valuation requires:

- stabilized EBITDA for each property after ordinary operating costs;
- recurring maintenance and renovation capital;
- debt, interest, preferred or operating-partnership claims, and dilution;
- a property-value range under different capitalization rates; and
- a distribution and repurchase policy that leaves sufficient reinvestment.

The current price must therefore be tested against the claim that RevPAR,
property value, and per-share FFO remain strong after the Andaz ramp, asset
sales, renovation cycle, and a higher-rate environment.

## Management incentives and capital allocation

Read the proxy for whether management is paid for RevPAR, Adjusted EBITDAre,
FFO/AFFO, per-share value, leverage, total shareholder return, acquisitions,
dispositions, or share repurchases. A property-EBITDA target can improve while
renovation is deferred. An asset-value target can reward selling. A per-share
target can reward buybacks even when the portfolio needs capital.

The better test is whether management measures value after normalized property
capital, financing, dilution, and the cost of preserving the guest experience.
For each redevelopment, compare stabilized cash return with capital invested.
For each disposition, compare the cash received with the future operating cash
and the capital that would have been required to repair the asset.

## Thesis breakers and next evidence

The thesis weakens if:

- RevPAR growth falls sharply when Andaz or event effects are removed;
- occupancy or ADR improves while property EBITDA margin deteriorates;
- renovation and redevelopment costs repeatedly exceed expected returns;
- asset sales supply distributions or buybacks more often than normalized cash;
- debt maturities, interest, or property-value declines make new equity likely;
- a concentrated hotel or market produces a disproportionate share of results; or
- Adjusted FFO rises while maintenance capital, diluted shares, or leverage worsen.

The next filings should show RevPAR and EBITDA by property, Andaz stabilization,
held-for-sale assets, renovation spending, debt maturity and interest coverage,
shares repurchased and issued, FFO-to-AFFO adjustments, and the bridge from
property operating cash to common-owner distributable cash.
