# Synchrony Financial: merchant-linked credit, household affordability, and the cost of high yield

## What the company does

Synchrony provides private-label cards, co-branded cards, promotional financing,
installment products, and buy now pay later products through merchants,
providers, and other partners. It serves retail, home, health, auto, digital,
and lifestyle categories. Its control point is the financing decision embedded
at the purchase or service moment: the merchant can use credit to increase
conversion, ticket size, repeat activity, and customer loyalty, while Synchrony
earns interest, fees, and partner economics on the receivable.

This is a balance-sheet business, not a pure payments network. Synchrony funds
receivables, underwrites households, absorbs charge-offs, carries rewards and
promotional-financing cost, pays for servicing and fraud controls, and retains
capital against credit risk. The merchant relationship helps distribution but
does not transfer the credit burden away from Synchrony shareholders.

## FY2025 and Q2 2026 baseline

| Measure | FY2025 | Q2 2026 / latest evidence |
|---|---:|---:|
| Net earnings | $3.552B | $885M quarterly |
| Diluted EPS | $9.28 | $2.59 quarterly |
| Purchase volume | $182.3B | $49.8B, up 8% year over year |
| Loan receivables | $103.8B year end | $102.2B quarter end, up 2% year over year |
| Net interest margin | 15.24% | 15.08% |
| Net charge-off rate | 5.65% | Must be read with vintage and delinquency data |
| Return on assets | 3.0% | 2.9% |
| CET1 ratio | 12.6% year end | 13.2% |
| Deposits | $81.1B | Major funding source; Q2 funding mix must be monitored |

Synchrony added more than 20M new accounts in 2025, engaged nearly 70M existing
customers, and generated more than $182B of partner sales. Those are useful
distribution and activity measures, but they are not owner earnings. Average
active accounts declined during 2025, while purchase volume was supported by
spend per account, partner expansion, and mix.

FY2025 net earnings were $3.552B, compared with $3.499B in 2024 and $2.238B in
2023. The 2025 net charge-off rate fell to 5.65% from 6.31% in 2024, returning
to management's long-term target range of 5.5% to 6.0%. The allowance for
credit losses was approximately $10.4B, or 10.06% of period-end receivables.
The high allowance and charge-off rate show that the high NIM is compensation
for substantial credit and funding risk, not a software-like margin.

Q2 2026 net earnings were $885M. Purchase volume rose 8% to $49.8B, receivables
rose 2% to $102.2B, average active accounts were flat at 68.3M, NIM increased
30 basis points to 15.08%, and ROA fell 30 basis points to 2.9%. Efficiency
also worsened to 35.8%. Record purchase volume therefore did not produce a
proportionate improvement in accounting returns.

## Where the economics sit

### Merchant and provider distribution

Synchrony supplies financing at the point where a consumer decides whether to
buy a product or service. The merchant may value approval speed, promotional
terms, installment options, and repeat-card use more than a generic payment
rail. Synchrony can earn partner access and customer data while the merchant
uses financing as a sales tool.

The control point is not permanent. Partners can switch issuers, negotiate
revenue-sharing terms, change promotional offers, or bring financing in-house.
Track renewal rates, partner concentration, purchase volume per partner, credit
loss sharing, merchant-funded promotions, revenue-sharing payments, and the
cost of replacing a lost relationship.

### Receivables and net interest margin

Synchrony earns interest and fees on receivables and pays interest on deposits,
securitization funding, and unsecured debt. The 15% NIM reflects high card
yields and associated credit risk. It cannot be valued without subtracting
charge-offs, provision expense, funding cost, rewards, promotional financing,
servicing, fraud, and capital.

Receivables can grow because of new accounts, higher spending, lower payment
rates, promotional balances, or weaker household liquidity. Each has a
different implication. Higher balances are not automatically better if payment
rates fall and future charge-offs rise.

### Promotional financing and buy now pay later

Promotional financing can increase merchant conversion but changes timing and
risk. Deferred-interest offers can produce high losses if customers do not pay
before the promotion expires. Installments can reduce revolving exposure but
may have lower yield or different loss behavior. Buy now pay later can increase
volume while introducing frequency, small-balance servicing, fraud, and
underwriting questions.

Future disclosures should separate purchase volume and receivables by product,
promotional type, partner, term, yield, payment rate, delinquency, and loss
vintage. A single consolidated NCO rate can hide deterioration in a fast-growing
product.

### Funding and liquidity

Synchrony Bank deposits were $81.1B at year end 2025 and represented 84% of
total funding. Deposit funding reduces dependence on wholesale markets, but
depositors can move cash when rates or confidence change. The company must
maintain liquidity and capital while funding a portfolio that can deteriorate
quickly in a consumer downturn.

Track deposit rate paid, insured and uninsured mix, maturity, brokered or
noncore funding, securitization dependence, liquidity buffers, and the asset
and liability repricing gap. Funding advantages are valuable only if they remain
available when charge-offs rise and capital markets tighten.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering review

### Purchase volume is a distribution metric, not a profit metric

Purchase volume rose 8% in Q2 2026, but ROA fell to 2.9%. This is a direct
reminder that volume can grow while earnings quality weakens. Reconcile volume
to receivables, payment rates, average balances, yield, partner economics,
losses, rewards, and operating expense.

Management's statement that value-oriented partners and diversified offerings
performed well should be tested against credit score, utilization, delinquency,
and loss behavior. Strong volume from financially stressed customers may be
less valuable than slower volume from higher-quality accounts.

### Reserve accounting can move earnings across periods

The allowance for credit losses is an estimate of lifetime expected losses. A
reserve release increases current earnings without changing current-period
purchase economics; a reserve build lowers earnings before all losses are
realized. Allowance coverage declined to about 10.06% at year-end 2025 from
10.44% in 2024 while charge-offs also declined.

The forensic test is whether the lower coverage is supported by better vintages,
credit mix, payment behavior, and delinquency migration, or whether reserves are
being released as the portfolio still carries elevated consumer risk. Compare
allowance by product, FICO or risk band, vintage, delinquency stage, expected
loss assumptions, and charge-off emergence.

### Partner concentration creates hidden revenue risk

Partners provide distribution and may subsidize promotional economics, but a
lost or renegotiated partner can remove purchase volume, receivables, and
customer acquisition efficiency. A portfolio sale or transfer can produce cash
and capital benefits while reducing future earnings.

Track the largest partners, renewal dates, receivable transfers, portfolio
acquisitions and sales, revenue-sharing terms, concentration by category, and
the expense required to replace volume. Do not assume that aggregate customer
counts protect against a major partner loss.

### Capital returns can be pro-cyclical

Synchrony returned approximately $3.3B to shareholders in 2025, including
$2.9B of repurchases and $427M of common dividends. It announced a new $6.5B
repurchase authorization in Q1 2026 and returned $950M in Q2. Buybacks are
attractive when shares are undervalued and credit is stable, but they reduce
capital before a consumer downturn can increase losses.

Compare repurchases with CET1, stress capital, allowance coverage, receivable
growth, funding liquidity, and the cyclicality of the share price. EPS growth
from a smaller share count is not a substitute for underwriting discipline.

### Management incentives

The proxy should be read for how management is rewarded across purchase volume,
new accounts, active accounts, net earnings, EPS, ROA, credit performance,
partner growth, and capital returns. Incentives focused on volume or accounts
can encourage promotional lending that grows receivables without adequate
lifetime returns. Credit outcomes must remain part of the scorecard through the
full loss cycle, not only during origination.

## Balance-sheet resilience and macro conditions

The Lyn Alden lens is direct for Synchrony. The company is exposed to household
income, employment, inflation, interest rates, deposit liquidity, and consumer
confidence. Higher rates can increase yield but pressure household payment
capacity and deposit pricing. A recession can lower purchase volume and increase
charge-offs at the same time.

Stress cases should include lower payment rates, higher utilization, rising
delinquencies, charge-offs above the 5.5%-6.0% target, deposit outflows,
securitization spread widening, partner losses, and lower recovery rates.
Capital must cover not only expected losses but also operational and funding
stress.

## Damodaran lens: translate the embedded-credit narrative

The narrative is that Synchrony can use merchant relationships, underwriting
technology, multi-product financing, and deposit funding to grow purchase volume
and earn high returns while credit remains within target ranges.

That narrative requires:

1. Partner relationships remain durable and economically favorable after revenue
   sharing and promotional subsidies.
2. Purchase volume grows through customer utility and spend per account rather
   than weaker payment behavior.
3. Receivable yields cover charge-offs, funding, rewards, servicing, fraud,
   technology, and required capital.
4. Reserve coverage remains adequate as products and partners change.
5. New accounts and BNPL or installment products earn acceptable lifetime
   returns after acquisition and servicing cost.
6. Buybacks occur only after stress capital and liquidity needs are covered.

Synchrony is a mature but cyclical consumer-finance company. Growth in volume or
accounts is valuable only when incremental risk-adjusted return on capital
remains above the cost of equity across a full consumer cycle.

## Valuation screen

The September 14, 2026 market feed showed an equity value of approximately
$24.7B. This is a normalized net-income screen rather than industrial owner
cash.

| Case | Normalized net earnings | Earnings multiple | Implied equity value | Required interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bear | $3.0B | 7x | $21.0B | Charge-offs rise, partner economics weaken, funding costs increase |
| Base | $3.8B | 9x | $34.2B | Volume and NIM remain productive with normal credit and capital discipline |
| Bull | $4.5B | 11x | $49.5B | Better credit mix, durable partners, lower losses, and high returns persist |

The current value is above the bear case and below the base case. The price
requires confidence that 2025-26 credit improvement is durable and that the
high-yield spread remains after a normalized loss rate, funding cost, partner
economics, and capital requirement. A low headline P/E is not sufficient when
earnings are highly sensitive to charge-offs.

## Cash conversion and distributable earnings

For Synchrony, owner cash begins with interest and fee income after funding cost, rewards, operating expense and credit losses. It is not the cash collected from cardholders or the receivables balance. The company must also retain capital against the consumer-credit portfolio and fund receivables through the cycle before dividends or repurchases are distributable.

The practical bridge is: purchase volume and receivables -> interest and fee income -> funding, rewards, servicing, charge-offs and provision expense -> capital retained for expected and stressed losses -> dividends and repurchases. Track common shares, tangible capital, reserve coverage, delinquency and charge-off trends, funding cost, and capital return together. Growth that requires more receivables and capital than the spread can support is not clean owner cash.

## Thesis breakers and future filing tests

| Question | Evidence that supports the thesis | Evidence that weakens it |
|---|---|---|
| Are partner relationships durable? | Renewals, diversified volume, stable revenue share, and low replacement cost | A major partner exits, renegotiates economics, or concentration rises |
| Is volume high quality? | Purchase growth with stable payment rates, utilization, delinquencies, and credit mix | Volume grows while payment rates fall or riskier vintages expand |
| Are reserves adequate? | Coverage matches vintage, delinquency, and expected-loss evidence | Coverage falls while loss emergence or macro risk rises |
| Does high NIM create value? | NIM exceeds charge-offs, funding, rewards, servicing, and capital cost through cycle | Spread is consumed by losses, promotions, funding, or regulatory expense |
| Are new products profitable? | BNPL and installments show lifetime return after fraud and servicing | Account growth has weak repeat use, high loss, or high acquisition cost |
| Are buybacks safe? | CET1 and liquidity remain strong after receivable growth and stress tests | Capital returns precede credit deterioration or funding pressure |

## Bottom line

Synchrony controls a useful merchant-linked financing interface. It can help
merchants close sales and can earn high spread income from a broad partner
network. The clean economics are not the 15% NIM or $182B of purchase volume by
themselves. They are the residual after charge-offs, reserves, promotions,
rewards, funding, fraud, servicing, partner payments, capital, and the risk of
losing distribution.

FY2025 and Q2 2026 show strong activity and improved credit relative to 2024,
but Q2's lower ROA despite higher purchase volume keeps the central question
open. Future filings must show that credit improvement is structural, not a
temporary reserve or mix benefit, and that partner-linked growth remains
profitable through a consumer downturn.

**Evidence grade:** Strong for FY2025 and Q2 2026 reported volume, receivables,
funding, earnings, capital, and credit metrics; medium for partner durability
and product-level lifetime economics; unresolved for through-cycle charge-offs,
reserve adequacy, and the safety of aggressive capital returns.
