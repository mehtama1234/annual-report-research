# Target Corporation — retail relationship repair, vendor funding, and owner cash

Date baseline: 2026-09-14  
Primary evidence: [Target source ledger](../../extracted/services/discount-variety-stores/target-corp/source-ledger.md), [Target company packet](../../extracted/services/discount-variety-stores/target-corp/company-packet.md), and [Target FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27419/000002741926000016/tgt-20260131.htm)

Current filing checkpoint: [Target Q2 2026 earnings release](https://corporate.target.com/press/release/2026/08/target-corporation-reports-second-quarter-earnings) and [Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27419/000002741926000042/tgt-20260801.htm)

## Investment question

Target is a national mass retailer whose core merchandise business weakened in
fiscal 2025 while non-merchandise revenue, membership, retail media,
marketplace, and same-day delivery improved. The investment question is
whether the relationship layer can repair the economics of the base retail
business or whether it is being used to make a weak merchandise margin look
more durable.

The key forensic issue is vendor funding. Target records vendor-sponsored
program consideration primarily as a reduction of cost of sales. At January
31, 2026, vendor-income receivable was `$542M` and valuation of that receivable
was identified as a critical audit matter because it depends on forecasted
collections and the period over which the income was earned. This does not
establish an error. It identifies a material estimate that must be reconciled
to cash collections, vendor contracts, inventory, promotions, and advertising.

## What Target controls

The operating sequence is:

`household shopping need -> store or digital discovery -> merchandise sale ->
pickup, delivery, or fulfillment -> supplier relationship and purchase data ->
membership, retail media, marketplace, and convenience services -> owner cash`

Target controls stores, digital traffic, assortment, fulfillment, Target Circle
and Circle 360 relationships, Roundel advertising, Target Plus marketplace
access, and supplier placement. It does not control household budgets,
supplier willingness to fund promotions, carrier economics, or the cost of
maintaining product availability.

Target's economic position differs from Walmart's. It has a smaller operating
base and less global breadth, so the attached services may be more important to
margin repair. That also makes it more exposed if suppliers or customers do
not accept higher monetization while the core basket is weak.

## Operating evidence

| Period | Core retail evidence | Relationship-layer evidence | Forensic significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 2025 | Sales declined `1.5%`; comparable sales declined `2.7%`; adjusted EPS was `$1.78`; GAAP EPS was `$1.51` | Digital comparable sales grew `2.4%`; same-day delivery grew more than `35%`; Roundel grew double digits; Target Plus GMV grew nearly `50%` | The attached layers strengthened before merchandise demand recovered |
| Q4 2025 | Sales were `$30.5B`, down `1.5%`; comparable sales declined `2.5%`; operating income was `$1.4B` | Non-merchandise sales grew more than `25%`; membership revenue more than doubled; Roundel grew double digits; marketplace and same-day delivery grew more than `30%` | Relationship growth and core retail weakness diverged materially |
| FY2025 | Sales declined `1.7%` to `$104.8B`; operating income declined `8.1%` to `$5.1B`; GAAP EPS was `$8.13` versus `$8.86` | Supplier-funded, membership, marketplace, advertising, and convenience layers expanded | The business needs margin repair, not only traffic recovery |
| Q1 2026 | Sales grew `6.7%` to `$25.4B`; comparable sales grew `5.6%`; comparable digital sales grew `8.9%`; operating income was `$1.1B`; GAAP and adjusted EPS were `$1.71` | Non-merchandise sales grew `24.6%`; Roundel, Circle 360, same-day delivery, and marketplace contributed | The recovery is a useful test, but capex also increased `31%` to `$1.0B` |

The Q1 recovery matters only if it persists after seasonality, promotions,
inventory normalization, vendor-income collection, and higher capital spending
are considered.

## Q2 2026 checkpoint: the recovery is real, but the margin proof is not clean

The official [Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript](https://corporate.target.com/investors/events-presentations/event-details-08-19-26)
is preserved in the [management-call control-point memo](../company-first-principles/combined-investment-research-pilot-02-target-q2-management-call-control-point-upgrade-2026-09-15.md).
Its observations are treated as management evidence and tested against the
10-Q, not as independent proof of service-level cash conversion.

Q2 net sales grew 5.3%, comparable sales grew 3.8%, traffic grew 3.6%, store
comparable sales grew 2.7%, and digital comparable sales grew 8.7%. Same-day
delivery grew more than 25%, while non-merchandise sales grew 20.1%. The
operating data show a broader recovery than Q1, including growth across all six
core merchandise categories.

The earnings quality test is unusually clear. Target recognized `$994M` of
IEEPA tariff refunds as a reduction of cost of sales. The refund added `$1.65`
to Q2 EPS and `$752M` to net earnings. Q2 GAAP and adjusted EPS were both
`$4.11`, but excluding the refund the company says EPS growth was about 20%,
not 100%. The refund also added 3.7 percentage points to the quarterly gross
margin rate and 1.9 points to the six-month rate. This is a realized benefit,
not an accounting error, but it must be removed from normalized margin and
cash expectations.

For the first six months, operating cash flow was `$4.519B`, property spending
was `$2.404B`, and cash after property spending was approximately `$2.115B`.
Inventory rose to `$13.2B`. The 10-Q says the operating-cash increase was
primarily due to higher accounts-payable leverage, which more than offset
higher inventory purchases. Accounts payable provided `$612M` of cash in the
period, compared with a `$1.125B` use of cash in the prior-year period. Target
also had `$3.2B` of vendor obligations eligible for early payment through
supplier-finance programs. These figures make working-capital normalization a
central part of the recovery test.

Target's updated full-year outlook calls for sales growth around 5%, but the
EPS range of `$9.90` to `$10.90` includes approximately `$1.65` of Q2 tariff
refund benefit. The company also expects a full-year operating-margin rate
around 6%, including roughly 90 basis points from the refund. The current
evidence supports genuine traffic and category improvement, but it does not
yet establish that the merchandise margin and attached services can produce
durable owner cash without supplier-term support or one-time benefits.

## Three-year cash bridge

Target's FY2025 Form 10-K reports operating cash flow of `$6.562B`, `$7.367B`,
and `$8.621B` for fiscal 2025, 2024, and 2023. Property and equipment spending
was `$3.727B`, `$2.891B`, and `$4.806B`, leaving simple cash after capex of
`$2.835B`, `$4.476B`, and `$3.815B`.

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Property and equipment | Cash after capex |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | `$8.621B` | `$4.806B` | `$3.815B` |
| 2024 | `$7.367B` | `$2.891B` | `$4.476B` |
| 2025 | `$6.562B` | `$3.727B` | `$2.835B` |

Operating cash flow declined as net earnings fell and accounts-payable leverage
and inventory effects became less favorable. Target expects approximately `$5B`
of fiscal 2026 capital expenditures for stores, remodels, supply chain,
technology, and new stores. The owner-cash denominator is therefore exposed to
both merchandise recovery and the cost of rebuilding the physical and digital
system.

The [H1 owner-cash denominator control](../company-first-principles/combined-investment-research-pilot-02-target-h1-owner-cash-denominator-control-2026-09-15.md)
places the `$2.115B` cash-after-property screen beside `$1.070B` of debt
reduction, `$1.034B` of dividends, `$154M` of share-based compensation, and
`$3.2B` of supplier-finance-eligible vendor obligations. It improves the
denominator boundary, but current-period maintenance capital and normalized
owner cash remain unresolved.

## Vendor income: the central earnings-quality test

Vendor income can include payments for promotions, placement, volume, and other
supplier programs. Target generally records the consideration as a reduction
of cost of sales when earned. That accounting can be appropriate when the
contractual performance is complete and the amount is collectible.

The risk is timing and estimation:

- Is the vendor obligation supported by a signed contract and completed retail
  activity?
- Is the amount recorded when Target has earned it, or before the promotion or
  placement occurs?
- Does the vendor-income receivable convert to cash on the expected schedule?
- Are disputed, delayed, or cancelled programs reserved correctly?
- Does vendor funding grow because the supplier received incremental sales, or
  because Target is shifting more of its merchandise margin burden to vendors?
- Does the receivable move with inventory, markdowns, advertising, and trade
  spending in a way that is economically consistent?

The critical audit matter is an evidence flag, not an accusation. The next
filings should disclose collection trends, receivable aging or roll-forward
information, vendor-program changes, and whether the relationship layer is
creating cash rather than only reducing reported merchandise cost.

## Where the economics sit

### Merchandise and stores

Stores create local availability and fulfillment options, but they require
labor, rent, maintenance, shrink control, inventory, and capital. Target's
merchandise margin is vulnerable to promotions, markdowns, freight, shrink,
tariffs, and consumer trade-down.

### Roundel advertising

Roundel monetizes shopper attention and purchase data. The cleaner revenue is
incremental supplier demand tied to measurable conversion. The weaker case is
when suppliers pay for placement that was previously organic or when ad load
reduces customer experience.

### Circle 360 membership and same-day delivery

Membership can improve frequency and retention, but the economics are:

`membership fee + incremental basket margin - delivery, discount, fulfillment,
customer-support, and acquisition cost`

Same-day delivery adds convenience and can raise order frequency. It also adds
carrier and store-picking cost. Target must show contribution after the entire
fulfillment chain.

### Target Plus marketplace

Marketplace growth expands assortment without Target owning all inventory. It
also adds seller quality, returns, counterfeit, customer-protection, data,
payment, and regulatory obligations. Target Plus GMV is not Target revenue or
owner cash.

## Possible financial engineering and incentive risks

- Vendor income may be recognized before collection or before the related
  performance is economically complete.
- Non-merchandise growth may improve reported mix while the merchandise basket,
  traffic, and gross margin remain weak.
- Adjusted EPS may exclude costs described as transformation, restructuring,
  shrink, or supply-chain investment that recur as the operating model changes.
- Inventory reduction can improve cash temporarily while lowering availability
  or shifting future replenishment cost.
- Digital and same-day delivery growth can be reported without showing the
  complete labor, carrier, return, damage, and fulfillment burden.
- Membership growth can be purchased through discounts and benefits that are
  not visible in membership revenue alone.
- Buybacks can make per-share earnings look better while maintenance capex,
  debt, leases, and stock compensation claims remain high.

### Q2 forensic tests

- **Tariff-normalized margin:** remove the `$994M` refund from gross margin,
  operating income, EPS, ROIC, and full-year guidance before judging the
  underlying merchandise recovery.
- **Working-capital test:** compare accounts payable, supplier-finance
  obligations, inventory, vendor-income receivables, and cash collections. A
  payable increase can fund the quarter but cannot be treated as recurring
  profit.
- **Attached-service test:** reconcile Roundel, Circle 360, Target Plus, and
  same-day delivery growth with retained fees, delivery cost, seller support,
  returns, and customer-service spending.
- **Capital-return test:** compare the `$2.404B` first-half property spending
  with store remodels, new stores, supply-chain investment, and technology. A
  recovery funded by underinvestment is not durable owner cash.

## Lyn Alden lens

Target serves a mix of essential and discretionary household demand. Its
identity and convenience positioning can support customer loyalty, but
household liquidity and affordability remain important. A weaker consumer can
trade down to value items while reducing discretionary categories that carry
higher margin.

The balance-sheet test is whether Target can fund inventory, stores, remodels,
supply-chain technology, and delivery while preserving liquidity through a
margin downturn. Vendor funding can support cash, but dependence on supplier
receivables and trade terms increases counterparty and timing risk.

The durable relationship is the combination of stores, digital convenience,
membership, and first-party transaction data. It is durable only if Target
keeps prices, assortment, service, and fulfillment competitive without asking
suppliers or customers to absorb an unsustainable share of the cost.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative becomes:

`traffic and digital users × transactions × basket × merchandise margin +
vendor income + advertising + membership + marketplace and fulfillment fees -
stores, labor, inventory, delivery, shrink, technology, and support -> cash
after capex, working capital, debt, leases, and dilution`

The valuation must separate:

1. normalized merchandise sales and gross margin;
2. vendor-income contribution and collection quality;
3. Roundel advertising after incremental support cost;
4. Circle 360 contribution after benefits and delivery;
5. Target Plus retained fees after seller and customer obligations; and
6. maintenance versus growth capital for stores, supply chain, and technology.

At the September 14, 2026 market snapshot, Target's equity value was about
`$71.2B`. That is roughly `25x` FY2025 cash after capex before adjusting for
the weaker earnings trend, the expected `$5B` FY2026 capex, and any recurring
cost of rebuilding the retail system. The scenario register uses bear, base,
and bull normalized owner cash of `$2.0B`, `$3.0B`, and `$4.0B` at `18x`, `24x`,
and `30x`. This is an expectations test: the base case requires the
relationship layer to become durable cash while the merchandise business
recovers.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Management is incentivized to emphasize non-merchandise sales, membership,
Roundel growth, marketplace GMV, same-day delivery, adjusted EPS, and
comparable sales. Each should be paired with collection, contribution, and
cash measures.

The thesis breaks if:

- vendor-income receivables rise faster than cash collections or are repeatedly
  revised;
- Roundel growth is not incremental to supplier sales or weakens shopper
  experience;
- Circle 360 and same-day delivery grow while contribution after delivery and
  store-picking cost declines;
- Target Plus GMV rises while returns, seller-quality issues, and customer
  support costs rise faster than retained fees;
- comparable sales improve through price and promotion while volume and gross
  margin weaken;
- inventory or accounts payable create a temporary cash benefit that reverses;
- capex rises but store, supply-chain, and digital returns do not improve; or
- dividends and repurchases continue while normalized cash after maintenance
  capital and debt service weakens.

## Next-filing evidence ladder

| Question | Confirming evidence | Falsifying evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is merchandise recovery real? | Volume, traffic, gross margin, inventory availability, and comparable sales improve together | Sales rise mainly through price and promotion while volume and margin weaken |
| Is vendor income high quality? | Receivable converts to cash, contract activity is completed, and vendor income tracks incremental sales | Receivable grows, collection slows, or recognition precedes completed supplier programs |
| Is Roundel incremental? | Supplier conversion and retention improve after advertising cost | Suppliers pay more without incremental sales or customer experience deteriorates |
| Is Circle 360 profitable? | Renewal, frequency, basket, and contribution after benefits and delivery improve | Membership grows through discounts while fulfillment cost consumes the benefit |
| Is cash durable? | OCF and cash after capex recover after working capital and normalized maintenance spending | Payable/inventory timing or deferred investment drives the improvement |

## Bottom line

Target shows how a retailer can improve its relationship layer while the core
merchandise business is under pressure. The opportunity is real: advertising,
membership, marketplace, and same-day delivery can improve the economics of
existing traffic. The forensic risk is also real: vendor income, working
capital, fulfillment cost, and adjusted measures can make the improvement look
cleaner than the common-owner cash actually is.

The current evidence supports a recovery case, not a completed turnaround. The
next filings must prove vendor-income collection, contribution after
convenience costs, merchandise margin recovery, and cash after the planned
capital program.
