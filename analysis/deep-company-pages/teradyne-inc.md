# Teradyne: semiconductor test and validation control with robotics and cycle risk

## Bottom line

Teradyne provides automated test equipment used to validate semiconductors,
memory, electronic systems, defense and aerospace products, and industrial
automation applications. Its semiconductor-test position sits after wafer
fabrication and packaging but before customers can deploy advanced chips and
systems at scale.

The company adds a missing layer to the semiconductor control-point cohort:
validation. AI increases chip, memory, high-speed interconnect, and system
complexity, which can increase the value of testing. But Teradyne is not a
pure semiconductor-test company. It also has Product Test and Robotics, and
its cash flow remains cyclical and acquisition-sensitive.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $3.190B, net income was $554M, operating cash flow was
$674M, and property-and-equipment spending was $224M. Teradyne's non-GAAP free
cash flow was approximately $450M after property spending. In Q2 2026, revenue
reached $1.329B, including $1.122B from Semiconductor Test, $107M from Product
Test, and $100M from Robotics. At the September 14, 2026 market snapshot,
equity value was about $53.8B. That is roughly 120 times fiscal 2025 free cash
flow.

The investment question is whether AI-driven test intensity can become a
durable, reinvestment-adjusted cash stream, or whether the current results are
a high point in a concentrated semiconductor and memory spending cycle.

## How to read the company

`chip or system complexity -> test requirement -> Teradyne equipment/software -> qualification and yield -> customer deployment -> support and replacement -> cash`

Teradyne reports three segments:

- **Semiconductor Test:** systems and products used to test semiconductors,
  including compute, memory, and system-on-chip applications.
- **Product Test:** test systems for electronic products, defense and
  aerospace, storage, and related applications.
- **Robotics:** collaborative robots, autonomous mobile robots, and industrial
  automation products.

The semiconductor-test business has the strongest connection to AI compute and
memory. Product Test can benefit from complex electronic systems and defense
spending. Robotics has a different adoption cycle, margin profile, labor model,
and competitive set. Company-wide growth should therefore not be treated as
one homogeneous control point.

## Operating evidence

| Year ended December 31 | Revenue | Net income | Operating cash flow | PP&E spending | OCF less PP&E |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2023 | $2.676B | $449M | $585M | $160M | $426M |
| 2024 | $2.820B | $542M | $672M | $198M | $474M |
| 2025 | $3.190B | $554M | $674M | $224M | $450M |

Fiscal 2025 free cash flow was not materially above 2024 despite higher
revenue. The company also spent $144M on business acquisitions and $26M on an
investment in a business. Those amounts are separate from maintenance PP&E and
matter when assessing cash available to common owners.

Q2 2026 revenue was up 104% from Q2 2025 and GAAP net income was $374.5M. The
Semiconductor Test increase was driven primarily by compute and memory related
to AI applications. That is strong current evidence, but it is also exactly
the type of comparison that requires a normalized-cycle test.

## Earnings quality and forensic review

### 1. Test is necessary, but test spending is still tied to customer capex

Semiconductor test is required because advanced devices cannot be sold at
scale without measuring performance, reliability, defects, and yield. More
complex chips and memory can require more test coverage, higher data rates, and
more specialized equipment.

The customer still controls the timing. A semiconductor manufacturer can delay
a new product, adjust capacity, reduce inventory, or reuse existing test
capacity. The test is whether revenue growth comes from more test intensity per
chip, more units shipped, higher system content, and recurring service, rather
than only from a temporary memory or compute-capex surge.

### 2. The Q2 2026 mix shows concentration in Semiconductor Test

Semiconductor Test represented approximately 84% of Q2 2026 revenue. Product
Test and Robotics were each about 8%. This is useful because it shows where the
AI growth is actually occurring, but it also means a semiconductor test
downturn can dominate consolidated results.

The company should be analyzed by segment revenue, margin, bookings or orders
where disclosed, inventory, customer concentration, and R&D. Robotics cannot
be used as a stabilizer unless it reaches sustainable margins and cash flow.

### 3. Cash conversion is positive but not expanding with 2025 earnings

Fiscal 2025 net income was $554M and operating cash flow was $674M. The cash
conversion was helped by depreciation and stock compensation, while working
capital included a $306M accounts-receivable use, a $28M inventory use, and a
$53M deferred-revenue and customer-advances source.

The receivables use matters. Strong reported revenue without collections can
be legitimate timing, but it increases the need to compare billings, customer
acceptance, receivable aging, allowance levels, deferred revenue, and cash in
the following quarters.

### 4. Acquisitions and affiliates complicate the owner-cash denominator

Teradyne spent $144M on acquisitions in 2025 and $26M on an investment in a
business. It also recognized $20M of equity in net earnings of an affiliate in
2025. These amounts are not necessarily problems, but they mean consolidated
earnings include capital allocated outside the core operating cash flow.

The owner test is:

`OCF - PP&E - acquisitions - strategic investments - economic SBC -> common-owner cash`

The company must show that acquired technology, robotics capability, or
adjacent test products produce returns above the cost of capital and do not
create recurring amortization or integration claims.

### 5. Stock compensation is material relative to free cash flow

Fiscal 2025 stock-based compensation was $64M, compared with approximately
$450M of free cash flow after PP&E. SBC is not the largest burden, but it is
large enough to affect per-share economics. Repurchases and employee-plan
issuance should be evaluated together, not separately.

The company also borrowed $250M during 2025 under its credit agreement, with
$200M outstanding at year-end. The reason for borrowing, the timing of
capital returns, interest cost, and future acquisition needs should be
considered in the cash-allocation analysis.

### 6. Deferred revenue and customer advances are useful but not permanent

Teradyne recognized $81.6M in 2025 revenue that had been included in opening
deferred-revenue and customer-advance balances. The 2025 cash flow included a
$52.6M source from deferred revenue and customer advances.

Advance payments can improve liquidity and reflect real customer commitment.
They also represent remaining performance obligations. The review should test
whether deferred revenue grows because service relationships and support are
expanding, or because shipment and acceptance are delayed.

## Alden lens: AI demand inside a concentrated validation cycle

Teradyne benefits from the physical complexity of AI systems. Compute,
high-bandwidth memory, networking, and high-speed systems need testing before
deployment, and failure is expensive once systems are installed.

The macro risk is that customers can defer testing equipment when they defer
fab construction, product launches, memory capacity, or data-center hardware.
Teradyne's exposure is more concentrated than a diversified industrial test
company because Semiconductor Test dominates the current growth signal.

The stress case is a memory correction, slower AI accelerator deployment,
customer inventory digestion, export restrictions, and weaker robotics
adoption at the same time. The company can have a strong technical position
and still produce below-normal cash for several years.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers and valuation

The valuation chain is:

`device complexity -> test content and equipment placements -> utilization and replacement -> service/software -> R&D and inventory -> owner cash`

Fiscal 2025 cash after PP&E was approximately $450M. After acquisitions and
the strategic investment, the broad investment-adjusted cash screen was lower.
At a market value of about $53.8B, the equity value is roughly 120 times the
reported free-cash-flow screen.

Illustrative equity-value cases:

| Normalized owner cash | Multiple | Implied equity value |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $350M bear | 30x | $10.5B |
| $500M base | 40x | $20.0B |
| $700M bull | 50x | $35.0B |

These cases are deliberately below the current market value. To justify the
price, Teradyne must produce substantially more cash than its 2025 level and
maintain a high multiple despite semiconductor cyclicality, customer
concentration, robotics uncertainty, acquisitions, and SBC.

## Possible financial-engineering questions

These are tests, not accusations:

- Are receivables growing faster than collected revenue during AI-driven
  quarters?
- Does deferred revenue support future service cash or mainly reflect delayed
  acceptance and shipment timing?
- Are Semiconductor Test results being generalized to Robotics and Product
  Test despite different economics?
- Are acquisitions and affiliate earnings being treated as organic recurring
  growth?
- Does stock compensation reduce the benefit of repurchases on a per-share
  basis?
- Are customer advances funding current cash while future support obligations
  and warranty claims remain understated?
- Does the current valuation assume peak memory and compute-test demand?

## What would prove this wrong

Downgrade the thesis if future filings show that:

1. Semiconductor Test revenue falls with a normal memory or compute cycle and
   test intensity does not offset the decline.
2. Product Test and Robotics remain too small or unprofitable to diversify the
   consolidated cash stream.
3. Receivables, inventory, or deferred revenue rise faster than gross profit
   and collections weaken.
4. Acquisitions and affiliate investments fail to earn acceptable returns.
5. R&D, test-system inventory, and customer support consume most incremental
   gross profit.
6. A customer develops internal test capability or shifts to a competing
   platform without a meaningful yield or reliability penalty.
7. SBC and debt-funded capital returns weaken cash per share.
