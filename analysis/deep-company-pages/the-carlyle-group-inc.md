# The Carlyle Group: fee-bearing capital, carry timing, and the test of durable alternatives earnings

## What the company does

Carlyle manages private equity, private credit, and AlpInvest strategies for
institutional and other clients. It raises capital, selects investments,
manages portfolios, earns management fees, and receives performance-related
income when funds generate gains and satisfy the relevant distribution terms.

The control point is the combination of fundraising relationships, investment
judgment, distribution, and access to private-market opportunities. The model
has two different earnings engines:

1. Fee-related earnings from managing fee-paying capital.
2. Performance income and carried interest from investment outcomes and
   realizations.

The first can be more recurring. The second is more dependent on marks, exits,
fund performance, timing, clawbacks, and partner economics. Carlyle also carries
personnel, fundraising, compliance, technology, valuation, balance-sheet, and
reputation burdens even when clients own the underlying funds.

FY2025 ended with approximately `$1.2B` of FRE, `$1.7B` of distributable
earnings, `$357M` of realized net performance revenue, `$477B` of total AUM,
`$337B` of fee-earning AUM, `$88B` of available capital, `$53.7B` of inflows,
and `$54.5B` of deployment.

The figures are not interchangeable. Total AUM is not fee-earning AUM. Available
capital is not invested capital. Realized performance revenue is not recurring
fee revenue. Distributable earnings are not automatically common cash after
taxes, compensation, partner allocations, preferred claims, and reinvestment.

Q2 2026 was a strong reported quarter: GAAP net income attributable to common
was approximately `$137.1M`, diluted EPS approximately `$0.37`, FRE
approximately `$358M`, DE approximately `$472M`, and realized net performance
revenue approximately `$115M`. Q1 2026 shows the other side: GAAP net loss
attributable to common was approximately `$132.2M`, while FRE was approximately
`$300M` and DE approximately `$327M`, with realized performance revenue only
approximately `$21M`. Q4 2025 produced GAAP net income approximately `$358.1M`,
FRE approximately `$290M`, DE approximately `$436M`, and realized performance
revenue approximately `$123M`.

The fee engine was positive through the quarter variation. Performance and GAAP
earnings were much more volatile. That supports a component valuation, not one
multiple applied to all reported earnings.

## Where the economics sit

### Fundraising and client relationships

Carlyle earns the right to manage capital through institutional trust,
investment results, distribution, product design, and access to opportunities.
Fundraising is evidence of relationship strength but not immediate owner cash.
New capital can require placement fees, investment staff, legal work, deployment,
and long periods before fee or performance economics mature.

Track inflows, redemptions, fee-paying capital, fee rates, fund life, client
concentration, fundraising cost, retention, product mix, and capital deployed.
Separate new money from market appreciation and fund transfers.

### Fee-related earnings

FRE is intended to capture recurring management and advisory economics after the
operating costs needed to produce them. Test its definition each period. Include
compensation, distribution, technology, compliance, fundraising, and recurring
transformation costs required to preserve the platform.

FRE can grow through fee-earning AUM even when investment performance is weak;
that does not make it immune to future outflows, fee concessions, or fundraising
failure. The cleanest owner cash starts with FRE after taxes, partner claims,
stock compensation, corporate cost, and reinvestment.

### Performance income and carry

Realized net performance revenue depends on investment gains, fund terms,
realizations, distributions, hurdle rates, clawbacks, and the timing of exits.
The approximate `$2.9B` of net accrued performance revenues at year-end 2025 is
not the same as cash received or current distributable earnings.

Track accrued versus realized performance income, carrying values, exit proceeds,
holding periods, net-of-fee and net-of-carry returns, clawbacks, escrow,
partner splits, and tax. A realization validates only the asset or fund sold;
it does not prove that the remaining portfolio carries the same value.

### Global Credit and AlpInvest

Global Credit and Carlyle AlpInvest diversify Carlyle beyond traditional buyout
fundraising. They may support longer-duration or steadier fee streams, but they
have distinct risks. Credit depends on underwriting, defaults, recoveries,
financing, and liquidity. AlpInvest depends on manager selection, institutional
allocation, fund access, fees, and client retention.

Compare each segment's fee-earning AUM, fee rate, net flows, deployment,
realizations, performance, compensation, and capital requirements. Diversified
segments are useful only if their returns and fees are additive after their own
operating and risk burdens.

## Earnings quality and forensic review

### FRE, DE, and GAAP reconciliation

The quarter chain shows why the measures need reconciliation. Q1 2026 had
approximately `$300M` of FRE and a GAAP net loss of approximately `$132.2M`.
Q2 had approximately `$358M` of FRE and GAAP net income of approximately
`$137.1M`. The difference can reflect marks, performance allocations, taxes,
compensation, partner interests, and other items.

Build a period-by-period bridge from GAAP net income to FRE and DE. Identify
which adjustments are truly nonrecurring, which move with markets, and which
represent cash claims by employees, partners, funds, or the tax authority.

### AUM and fee-rate quality

Total AUM reached approximately `$485B` in Q2 2026 while fee-earning AUM was
approximately `$334B`. The difference is economically important. AUM outside
the fee-paying base may generate future value, but it cannot support the same
current fee multiple.

Reconcile average fee-earning AUM to fee revenue, fee rate, net flows, market
movement, product mix, fee waivers, and performance. If AUM grows while fee
rates fall or fee-paying capital stagnates, headline scale overstates economic
growth.

### Marks, realizations, and private-market valuation

Private-market marks affect performance income, book value, and investor
confidence before assets are sold. Test discount rates, comparable-company
multiples, leverage, operating forecasts, exit assumptions, valuation committees,
and later realization prices.

The possible financial-engineering risk is not necessarily misconduct. It is
capitalizing modeled value as if it were cash, using adjusted earnings that omit
recurring platform cost, or treating accrued carry as durable income before
clawback and realization risk are resolved.

### Compensation, partner economics, and dilution

Alternative managers rely on investment professionals and distribution talent.
Compensation and carried-interest sharing are economic costs, even if adjusted
measures present them differently. Track cash compensation, stock compensation,
employee ownership, partner allocations, carried interest, diluted shares,
dividends, and repurchases.

Per-share value rises only when common cash grows after the people who generate
the platform's returns are paid and after partner and tax claims are funded.

## Balance-sheet resilience and macro conditions

The Lyn Alden lens focuses on rates, credit, liquidity, refinancing, and exit
markets. Carlyle can benefit from institutional demand for private credit,
infrastructure, retirement assets, and nonpublic financing when traditional
banks or public markets are constrained. It is exposed when rates increase,
leveraged borrowers weaken, exits slow, or clients reduce commitments.

Stress cases should include:

- slower fundraising and lower fee-earning AUM;
- lower exit activity and delayed carry realization;
- marks declining across private equity and credit;
- defaults and weaker recoveries in Global Credit;
- higher financing and portfolio-company interest expense;
- client outflows or lower commitment pacing;
- compensation and compliance cost remaining fixed while revenue falls;
- clawbacks and partner claims after prior accrued performance income;
- liquidity and corporate debt pressure.

## Damodaran lens: translate the alternatives narrative into numbers

The narrative is that Carlyle can compound through a diversified platform of
private equity, credit, AlpInvest, fundraising, and performance income. That
narrative requires:

1. Fee-earning AUM grows through durable inflows and retention rather than only
   market movement or fee concessions.
2. FRE remains positive after full recurring compensation, fundraising,
   technology, compliance, and corporate costs.
3. Realized performance income is supported by actual exits, not only marks or
   accrued carry.
4. Credit deployment earns adequate returns after defaults, recoveries, funding,
   and liquidity costs.
5. AlpInvest and other diversified products add fee-paying capital without
   disproportionate talent or distribution cost.
6. Partner, employee, tax, NCI, and clawback claims leave an attractive common
   shareholder residual.
7. Capital returns and balance-sheet investment do not weaken liquidity during a
   period of slower realizations.

Value Carlyle by component: FRE after all recurring costs, probability-weighted
performance income, normalized credit economics, corporate expenses, taxes,
partner and employee claims, debt, and retained capital. FRE deserves a higher
durability multiple than realized carry; carry requires an explicit cycle and
realization haircut.

## Valuation screen

A market snapshot at 00:15 UTC on 2026-09-15 showed an equity value of
approximately `$15.361B`. These are normalized earnings sensitivities, not a
price target.

| Case | Normalized FRE | Illustrative multiple | Implied equity value | What must be true |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Downside | `$0.9B` | `10x` | `$9.0B` | Fee-paying capital slows, fee rates compress, and carry remains weak |
| Base | `$1.3B` | `14x` | `$18.2B` | FRE remains durable and diversified strategies retain clients |
| Upside | `$1.7B` | `18x` | `$30.6B` | Fundraising, credit, AlpInvest, and realizations all improve without major dilution |

These values exclude a full value for performance income. Add carry only after
probability-weighting realizations, marks, clawbacks, partner economics, taxes,
and the timing of cash receipt.
The current market value is below the illustrative base case and above the
downside case. That comparison is not a margin-of-safety conclusion: the base
requires durable fee-earning capital and diversified strategies, while realized
carry, credit outcomes, partner claims, compensation, taxes, and dilution remain
separate claims on common value.

## Thesis breakers and filing tests

| Question | Evidence supporting the thesis | Evidence weakening it |
|---|---|---|
| Is FRE durable? | Fee-earning AUM, flows, fee rates, and retention hold after full recurring costs | AUM rises while fee rates fall, outflows grow, or recurring costs are excluded |
| Is carry real? | Realized gains exceed marks after fees, tax, partner claims, and clawback risk | Accrued carry rises without exits or later realizations fall below marks |
| Are segments additive? | Credit and AlpInvest add fee-paying capital and returns after their own costs | Diversification increases staffing, risk, or capital without common cash |
| Is credit controlled? | Defaults, recoveries, leverage, and net returns remain acceptable | Portfolio losses rise as financing and liquidity tighten |
| Is capital allocation sound? | Distributions and reinvestment improve common per-share value | Capital returns or acquisitions increase debt and reduce flexibility |
| Is accounting transparent? | GAAP, FRE, DE, realized carry, and cash reconcile clearly | Adjustments recur, partner claims are obscured, or common earnings lag DE |

## Bottom line

Carlyle controls institutional fundraising, private-market investment judgment,
distribution, and access to credit and buyout opportunities. Its cleanest
economics are fee-related earnings from durable fee-paying capital. Its main
burdens are performance timing, private-asset marks, credit losses, talent cost,
partner claims, leverage, liquidity, and the need to prove that accrued value
becomes cash.

FY2025 and Q2 2026 show meaningful scale, record or near-record FRE, strong
fundraising, and improved performance income. Q1 2026 shows why the model remains
cyclical: FRE held while GAAP income and realized performance revenue fell
sharply. The next filings must show fee-rate and flow quality, realized versus
accrued carry, credit outcomes, partner economics, dilution, and common cash
conversion.

**Evidence grade:** Strong for FY2025 and Q1-Q2 2026 reported AUM, fee-earning
AUM, FRE, DE, performance revenue, inflows, deployment, and GAAP earnings;
medium for fee durability and segment economics; unresolved for marks,
realizations, credit-cycle performance, partner claims, and common-owner cash.
