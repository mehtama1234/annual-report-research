# The Mosaic Company — phosphate conversion, sulfur exposure, and Brazil execution

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed U.S. GAAP figures in U.S. dollars;
dollar amounts are in millions unless otherwise stated.

## How to read this memo

Phosphate fertilizer requires phosphate rock, sulfur, ammonia, processing
plants, and logistics. Potash is a mined nutrient with different costs and
market conditions. A curtailment is a deliberate reduction or shutdown of
production when input costs or selling prices do not support economic operation.
Adjusted EBITDA is management's measure before interest, tax, depreciation, and
selected items; it is not common-owner cash. Mark-to-market gains or losses
change reported profit as contract values move, without necessarily creating or
using cash in the same period.

## Investment question

Mosaic combines phosphate mining and processing, potash mining, and a large
Brazilian fertilizer distribution business. Its possible advantage is control
over scarce crop nutrients and the ability to move products across regions. The
central question is whether that system earns a satisfactory return after
sulfur and ammonia costs, inventory, plant utilization, Brazil working capital,
turnarounds, environmental obligations, and capital spending.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Mosaic operates across Phosphate, Potash, and Mosaic Fertilizantes. FY2025
  external sales were approximately $3.88B, $2.66B, and $4.85B respectively.
  These businesses should not receive one common margin or valuation assumption.
- Phosphate production depends on sulfur and ammonia as well as phosphate rock.
  A high fertilizer selling price does not guarantee a high conversion margin if
  sulfur becomes scarce or expensive.
- Potash was the stronger operating pillar in the recent quarters. That does
  not eliminate the risk of mine capital, price changes, or lower utilization.
- Brazil is a major operating and financing variable. Distribution, inventory,
  currency, local demand, and asset quality can change consolidated cash even
  when North American production is stable.
- FY2025 operating cash flow fell to $825M as finished-product inventories rose
  by about $428M and phosphate-rock volumes and sulfur prices created another
  working-capital burden. Q4 operating cash flow was negative $56M.
- The Q1 and Q2 2026 results show the conversion problem continuing. Adjusted
  EBITDA remained positive, but reported losses, sulfur affordability, partial
  idling, and Brazil-related charges made the accounting result and cash result
  much weaker than the product-demand narrative.
- Management reduced 2026 capital guidance to $1.2B. That may preserve cash, but
  deferred spending can return later as maintenance, reliability, or
  environmental work.
- Mosaic Biosciences reached $68M of 2025 sales, but it remains too small to
  change the enterprise's main phosphate, potash, and Brazil risk profile.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Did EBITDA become cash?

Reconcile segment adjusted EBITDA to GAAP operating income, operating cash flow,
inventory, receivables, payables, taxes, interest, and capital spending. Treat
the $2.4B adjusted EBITDA figure as an operating starting point, not as cash
available to owners.

### 2. How much margin was lost to raw materials?

Build a product-level bridge for selling price, tonnes, sulfur, ammonia,
phosphate rock, energy, freight, and utilization. Test whether price increases
were retained or passed through to suppliers. Track sulfur contracts and unit
costs in future filings.

### 3. Are inventories economic or defensive?

Separate finished product, raw material, and work-in-process inventory. Compare
tonnes and carrying value with realized prices, customer demand, and subsequent
sales. An inventory build can support future shipments, but it can also indicate
that production was not matched to demand and can reverse cash later.

### 4. Are curtailments evidence of discipline or asset weakness?

Compare the cash saved by reduced production with fixed costs, restart costs,
customer service, and lost volume. Track Bartow and other facilities by rate,
maintenance, input availability, and return on capital. Idling a plant can be
the correct decision while still showing that the asset has weak economics.

### 5. Does Brazil improve the system?

Separate Brazil revenue, gross margin, inventory, receivables, currency, debt,
impairments, and restructuring. Require evidence that distribution scale and
customer access produce cash after local financing and asset-maintenance needs.

### 6. Are reported losses one-time?

Maintain a schedule of impairments, held-for-sale charges, foreign exchange,
mark-to-market changes, restructuring, and other adjustments. A charge can be
non-cash in the current period and still reveal that earlier capital was
misallocated or that future cash returns are lower.

## Incentives, inventory, and cohort position

Mosaic's adjusted EBITDA and capital-guidance narrative can make a recovery
look closer than it is if inventory, input costs, curtailment cash, and
maintenance capital are treated as temporary. The appropriate management
scorecard is cash per diluted share after sulfur, ammonia, energy, inventory,
Brazil working capital, sustaining capital, environmental work, and the cost of
restarting or replacing idled capacity.

The company should also be judged by tonnes sold and cash conversion, not only
by tonnes produced or adjusted margin. Inventory that has not been sold is not
cash, and a lower capital budget can improve one year’s cash while deferring
work needed to preserve reliability or environmental compliance. Any recovery
claim must therefore show price, volume, cost, inventory liquidation, and
capital effects separately.

Mosaic belongs in the [agricultural-inputs and crop-nutrients comparison](../cross-sector/crop-processing-versus-nitrogen-inputs-forensic-comparison-2026-09-13.md).
Compared with CF Industries, Mosaic carries phosphate rock, sulfur, potash,
and Brazil distribution complexity; compared with Nutrien, it has less retail
and agronomy integration; compared with FMC, its product need is more tied to
nutrient cycles and input costs than patent replacement. Food-system importance
does not remove the need for a full-cycle cash and capital test.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** fertilizer is tied to food security, energy, shipping,
  trade, and geopolitical supply. Strategic importance does not protect owners
  from input inflation, leverage, or regional execution risk.
- **Damodaran lens:** value Phosphate, Potash, Mosaic Fertilizantes, and
  Biosciences separately. Use mid-cycle nutrient prices, sulfur and ammonia
  costs, utilization, sustaining capital, mine life, working capital, and
  probability-weighted projects and restructuring outcomes.
- **Forensic lens:** the primary risks are treating adjusted EBITDA as cash,
  treating inventory growth as demand, treating impairments as isolated events,
  and using end-market necessity to overlook negative post-capex cash.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $8.01B of equity value at
$25.19 per share. Against the FY2025 OCF-less-PP&E screen of negative $535M,
the current earnings multiple is not meaningful. A normalized cash range of
$500M / $1.0B / $1.5B at 12x / 15x / 18x implies $6B / $15B / $27B. The
current value therefore requires a recovery in cash conversion, not merely
positive adjusted EBITDA.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Sulfur, ammonia, or energy costs remain high while fertilizer prices fall.
2. Inventory and receivables continue to grow faster than tonnes sold and gross
   profit.
3. Curtailments become recurring or restart costs exceed the cash saved.
4. Brazil requires additional restructuring, impairments, or working capital.
5. Maintenance and environmental capital remains above the current guidance.
6. Adjusted EBITDA stays positive while operating cash and post-capex cash stay
   weak.
