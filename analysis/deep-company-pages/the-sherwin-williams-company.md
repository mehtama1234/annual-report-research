# The Sherwin-Williams Company Full Forensic Analysis Memo

Date baseline: 2026-09-13  
Primary source: [Sherwin-Williams 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/89800/000008980026000008/shw-20251231.htm)  
Companion packet: [Sherwin-Williams company packet](/home/mehtama1/git-repo/annual-report-research/extracted/basic-materials/specialty-chemicals/the-sherwin-williams-company/company-packet.md)

## Bottom line

Sherwin-Williams is not best understood as a generic coatings producer. Its
control point is a professional paint and coatings workflow: branch density,
trusted brands, contractor access, color and technical support, product
availability, and recurring repaint/maintenance demand. That creates stronger
customer embedment than a commodity chemical producer, but it does not remove
the capital burden.

FY2025 revenue was $23.574B, net income was $2.569B, and operating cash flow
was $3.452B. Capital expenditures were $797.6M and acquisitions net of cash
acquired were $1.211B, leaving approximately **$1.443B after those listed
uses**. The business returned $2.446B through dividends and equity repurchases,
which exceeded that residual and therefore requires debt, cash balances, or
other financing context rather than being read as unconstrained owner cash.

## Q2 2026 operating checkpoint

Q2 2026 net sales increased `7.5%` to `$6.789B`, and net income increased
`11.8%` to `$843.6M`. Paint Stores Group same-store sales rose `4.2%`, but
management said the increase included mid-single-digit pricing and only
low-single-digit volume growth. That is evidence of continued professional
channel control, not proof that underlying demand has fully recovered.

The margin detail is important. Consolidated gross margin was `49.2%`, slightly
below the prior-year quarter’s `49.4%`, because Suvinil diluted the mix and raw
material costs rose. Paint Stores Group’s reported margin was `24.6%`, down
from `24.8%`. Protective and marine, commercial, and residential repaint all
grew, but the quarter still required price action to offset input inflation.
Sherwin-Williams can protect revenue and gross profit through price,
availability, and contractor relationships, but price growth is doing part of
the work while volume remains modest.

The six-month cash statement produced `$1.487B` of net operating cash against
`$246.7M` of capital expenditures and `$64.9M` of acquisitions, a screening
residual of approximately `$1.175B`. During the same period, the company paid
`$394.6M` of dividends and repurchased `$1.837B` of stock. This is the same
capital-allocation tension visible in FY2025: buybacks are a choice, not proof
that the operating business generated enough normalized cash to fund all
returns without additional financing.

Working capital increased the burden. Accounts receivable rose to `$3.571B`
from `$2.791B` at year-end, inventory rose to `$2.530B` from `$2.318B`, and
current contract assets rose to `$111.4M` from `$73.3M`. Total debt reached
`$12.072B`, including `$2.246B` of short-term borrowings, against only `$293.5M`
of cash. The balance sheet remains financeable, but its margin of safety is
lower than the gross-margin percentage suggests.

Suvinil contributed materially to Consumer Brands growth: that segment’s Q2
sales rose `21.5%`, and the filing states the acquisition was a primary cause.
The next test is to separate Suvinil’s acquired revenue, standalone margin,
working capital, foreign exchange, integration costs, and interest burden from
organic Paint Stores and Performance Coatings execution. The company raised
full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to `$11.80–$12.20`, but the valuation
should not treat the adjustment as owner cash until recurring amortization,
restructuring, debt, and acquisition returns are included.

## The control point

`professional customer -> local branch / technical advice / product
availability -> coating specification and application -> repaint, maintenance,
and repeat purchase`

The Paint Stores Group and professional channels matter because coatings are
often specified and applied by contractors, industrial customers, and property
owners rather than selected as a one-time consumer purchase. The moat is the
combination of availability, advice, relationship, brand, color system,
formulation, and switching cost at the job site. A customer may be able to
change brands, but failure on a coating job is costly, so reliability and
technical support have economic value.

## Three-year reported picture

Amounts are $ billions except percentages.

| Fiscal year | Revenue | Net income | Operating cash flow | Capex | Acquisitions, net | Residual after capex/acquisitions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 23.052 | 2.389 | 3.522 | 0.888 | 0.265 | 2.369 |
| 2024 | 23.099 | 2.681 | 3.153 | 1.070 | 0.079 | 2.004 |
| 2025 | 23.574 | 2.569 | 3.452 | 0.798 | 1.211 | 1.443 |

The three-year record shows a durable cash-generating operation, but not a
straight-line earnings compounder. Revenue grew only 2.1% in 2025. The quality
question is whether controlled distribution and pricing can protect gross
profit through soft housing and industrial demand, or whether the margin is
being supported by temporary mix, cost actions, or raw-material timing.

## Narrative-to-numbers translation

Management's resilience narrative requires several separate tests:

- **Pricing power:** compare price, volume, mix, raw-material cost, and gross
  margin by segment; a price increase without volume or share retention is not
  durable pricing power.
- **Professional share gains:** compare Paint Stores same-store sales, branch
  productivity, new-account wins, contractor retention, and sales per store;
  growth from acquired or newly opened locations is different from share gains.
- **Protective and Marine strength:** determine how much growth reflects project
  timing, backlog, infrastructure spending, or a durable specification moat.
- **Repaint resilience:** distinguish maintenance demand from new construction;
  repaint can be defensive, but contractors and property owners still face
  labor, financing, and occupancy constraints.
- **Suvinil acquisition:** measure acquired revenue, integration cost, local
  currency, working capital, margin, and return on the $1.15B purchase rather
  than treating the deal as immediate organic growth.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering risks

### Adjusted earnings are not the same as owner earnings

Sherwin-Williams reports EBITDA of $4.480B and adjusted EBITDA of $4.609B for
2025. The $129M difference requires a recurring-item test. Restructuring,
acquisition integration, impairment, and other adjustments can be economically
real even when management labels them nonrecurring. The GAAP-to-cash bridge is
therefore more informative than adjusted EPS alone.

### Acquisition accounting and goodwill

Goodwill increased to $8.0366B and net intangible assets excluding goodwill to
$3.9661B at year-end. The Suvinil transaction increases the need to track
acquired margin, local-market execution, foreign exchange, amortization, and
impairment—not because the accounting is improper, but because a premium
coatings franchise can destroy value if integration returns lag the cost of
capital.

### Working capital and supplier financing

Accounts receivable increased from $2.389B to $2.791B while revenue increased
only modestly. Inventory was $2.318B, broadly stable. Net working capital
remained negative, improving from a $(1.408)B deficit to $(913)M. Negative
working capital can be a legitimate distribution advantage, but it also means
accounts payable and short-term borrowings help finance the operating system.
The next filings should test collection, supplier terms, inventory aging, and
whether the improvement is operational or timing-supported.

The Q2 2026 balances make this test active rather than theoretical. Receivables
grew faster than sales from year-end, current contract assets increased, and
short-term borrowings rose. The company also disclosed that six-month net cash
from operations was `$1.487B` while share repurchases were `$1.837B`. That
combination requires the analyst to distinguish cash generated by the paint
workflow from cash released through borrowing and balance-sheet timing.

### Capital returns and leverage

The company paid $789.8M of dividends and repurchased $1.656B of equity in
2025. Those returns exceeded the $1.443B residual after capex and acquisitions.
Total debt was approximately $10.871B against $207M of cash, and management
reported net debt at 2.4 times EBITDA. Buybacks are not automatically
financial engineering, but their value depends on whether the balance sheet
can absorb raw-material inflation, housing weakness, acquisition integration,
and higher interest costs without sacrificing the control point.

## Alden macro/liquidity lens

The durable demand base is maintenance, repaint, commercial upkeep,
infrastructure, and protective coatings. That is more resilient than new-home
construction alone. The cyclical exposure remains meaningful: housing
turnover, remodeling affordability, industrial production, commercial real
estate, contractor employment, and customer financing affect volumes.

Higher rates can delay new construction and large projects while making the
company's debt and acquisition hurdle more expensive. Inflation in resins,
solvents, pigments, packaging, energy, and labor tests whether pricing follows
costs with a lag or permanently loses volume. Sherwin-Williams' liquidity is
stronger than a highly leveraged commodity producer because it generates cash
through the network, but net debt and negative working capital make a demand
shock worth monitoring.

## Damodaran lens

The valuation model should separate three reinvestment pools:

`maintenance capex for plants/branches/IT -> growth capex and store/network
investment -> acquisitions such as Suvinil -> incremental margin and cash
return -> debt, dilution, dividends, and repurchases`

The narrative is attractive if controlled distribution creates pricing power,
stable repaint demand, and high returns on incremental branch and formulation
investment. The narrative is weaker if growth requires buying brands, adding
stores, or increasing debt while organic volume remains flat. The critical
input is return on incremental invested capital after working capital,
maintenance capex, integration, and financing—not the 48.8% gross margin by
itself.

## Valuation and thesis breakers

At the September 11, 2026 market snapshot, SHW was approximately $323.31 per
share with an equity value near $79.534B. The market is pricing a premium
professional-distribution franchise, not merely the 2025 residual.

| Case | Normalized owner cash | Multiple | Implied equity value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | $1.2B | 25x | $30.0B |
| Base | $1.8B | 32x | $57.6B |
| Bull | $2.5B | 38x | $95.0B |

The current value sits above the base case and below the bull case. It requires
the company to preserve professional-channel pricing and convert Suvinil and
future growth into higher cash without a prolonged housing or industrial
downturn.

Thesis breakers:

- same-store professional sales and gross margin weaken while price increases
  fail to hold volume or share;
- accounts receivable grows faster than revenue or inventory aging worsens;
- acquired businesses add revenue but not incremental margin and cash;
- capex, branch investment, or maintenance needs rise while adjusted earnings
  keep excluding recurring costs;
- debt-funded repurchases continue despite weaker cash conversion; or
- Suvinil growth remains acquisition-led while its gross margin and cash return
  do not cover the acquisition and financing burden; or
- raw-material, labor, tariff, environmental, or regulatory costs permanently
  reduce the return on the distribution network.

These are presentation and denominator risks, not a finding of misconduct.
Sherwin-Williams may own a durable workflow, but the investment case still has
to earn the multiple after the full capital and balance-sheet burden.
