# Thermo Fisher Scientific — scientific workflow, recurring consumables, and acquisition discipline

Date baseline: 2026-09-13

## The investment question

Thermo Fisher Scientific supplies instruments, analytical systems, laboratory
products, diagnostics, consumables, and biopharma services. It sits upstream of
many clinical and production decisions: research laboratories, drug makers,
diagnostic providers, hospitals, and manufacturers need its products to test,
measure, develop, and produce.

The investment question is whether this installed scientific workflow produces
durable owner cash after research and biopharma spending cycles, product
replacement, inventory, quality, acquisitions, debt, stock compensation, and
the capital required to expand capacity.

This is a middle-layer healthcare company. Its recurring demand is more
institutional than consumer-facing, and its economic quality depends on how
much of the installed workflow repeats through consumables and services rather
than requiring a new instrument or acquisition for each period of growth.

## What Thermo Fisher actually controls

The operating chain is:

`scientific or production need -> instrument or workflow adoption -> recurring consumables and services -> validated result or usable product -> institutional budget -> collected cash`

Thermo Fisher controls product design, application support, distribution,
installed instruments, consumables, laboratory workflow, and biopharma
production services. It does not control research budgets, drug-development
success, government grants, hospital capital budgets, customer utilization, or
the timing of biopharma production.

The 2025 business was organized into Life Sciences Solutions, Analytical
Instruments, Specialty Diagnostics, and Laboratory Products and Biopharma
Services. The packet reports approximate revenue mix of 52% Analytical
Instruments, 22% Life Sciences Solutions, 16% Laboratory Products and
Biopharma Services, and 10% Specialty Diagnostics. This breadth reduces
dependence on one end market, but it also means the group combines different
replacement cycles, margins, capital needs, and acquisition histories.

## Three-year reported bridge

Amounts are $ billions. Thermo Fisher defines free cash flow as operating cash
flow less net capital expenditures. The final column subtracts acquisitions as
well, because acquisitions are a recurring part of management's capital model
and are not free to common owners.

| Fiscal year | Revenue | GAAP diluted EPS | Operating cash flow | PP&E capex | Reported FCF | Acquisitions | Residual after acquisitions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 42.857 | 15.53 | 8.406 | 1.479 | 6.927 | 3.660 | 3.267 |
| 2024 | 42.879 | 16.20 | 8.667 | 1.400 | 7.324 | 3.132 | 4.192 |
| 2025 | 44.564 | 17.74 | 7.818 | 1.525 | 6.337 | 4.037 | 2.300 |

The 2025 $6.337B reported FCF is useful, but it is not the same as cash after
the company's recurring growth strategy. Thermo Fisher spent $4.037B on
acquisitions in 2025, including approximately $4B for Solventum's purification
and filtration business. It also issued $7.759B of debt, repaid $2.412B, paid
$636M of dividends, and repurchased $3.0B of stock.

The acquisition-adjusted residual fell to approximately $2.3B in 2025 from
$4.2B in 2024. That does not mean the Solventum transaction will destroy value.
It means the return on the acquired earnings and the financing cost must be
proved before the platform receives a fully recurring-growth valuation.

Operating cash also absorbed $430M from higher accounts receivable, $380M from
contract assets and liabilities, and $1.31B from other assets and liabilities,
primarily tax-payment timing. These movements are ordinary business effects,
but they show why one year's free cash flow should not be projected without a
working-capital normalization.

## Where the economics sit

### Instruments create the entry point; consumables and services create repeat use

An instrument can establish a laboratory or production workflow. The durable
economics then depend on reagent, consumable, service, software-adjacent,
replacement, and biopharma-support revenue attached to that workflow. Customer
training, validation, regulatory requirements, and accumulated data can raise
switching costs, but those costs are not permanent if a competing system has
better performance, price, or regulatory acceptance.

The key unit is not the number of instruments shipped. It is the lifetime gross
profit and cash produced by the installed workflow relative to acquisition,
support, R&D, quality, and replacement cost.

### Biopharma services add capacity but add customer and execution risk

Laboratory Products and Biopharma Services benefits from outsourcing and
biologic-production complexity. The Sanofi sterile fill-finish site and Clario
clinical-trial data transaction expand that exposure. More outsourced work can
increase utilization and recurring service revenue, but customers can delay
programs, change suppliers, fail clinical trials, or demand capacity at terms
that do not cover the fixed asset and labor burden.

### AI is an operating claim until productivity and cash are visible

Thermo Fisher describes AI work across products and operations. The relevant
questions are whether AI reduces labor or cycle time, improves instrument or
assay performance, increases consumables attachment, or creates a separately
paid product. AI language alone does not establish a new moat or justify a
higher multiple.

## Earnings quality and forensic questions

### 1. Acquisition amortization is a recurring economic cost

Thermo Fisher excludes acquisition-related intangible amortization from
adjusted results because acquired purchase prices are allocated to long-lived
intangibles. The accounting presentation is understandable for comparing
operating trends, but the acquired customer relationships and technologies
must eventually be replaced, defended, or supplemented with new acquisitions.

The test is to compare adjusted operating income and EPS with GAAP results,
cash acquisition spending, intangible balances, replacement R&D, and actual
organic growth. If adjusted growth requires continuous acquisition spending,
the excluded amortization is not the only cost of the strategy.

### 2. Solventum and Clario make the forward denominator demanding

Thermo Fisher completed the approximately $4B Solventum purification and
filtration acquisition in September 2025. It also agreed to acquire Clario for
approximately $8.875B cash, plus deferred and contingent consideration, with
closing expected in 2026 subject to approvals. These are strategically related
to biopharma and clinical workflow, but the purchase prices are material
relative to annual residual cash.

For each deal, track acquired revenue, organic growth after ownership,
operating margin, customer retention, integration spending, debt, interest,
working capital, and return on invested capital. A larger revenue base is not
proof that the purchase earned more than its funding cost.

### 3. Working capital can make the cycle look better or worse

In 2025, receivables and contract-account changes consumed cash while payables
provided $420M. The direction can reverse with billing, project timing,
inventory, customer deposits, or tax payments. Track days sales outstanding,
inventory turns, contract liabilities, supplier terms, and cash conversion by
segment.

### 4. Capital spending has both sustaining and capacity components

The company spent $1.525B on PP&E in 2025 and expects $1.8B–$2.0B net PP&E
spending in 2026. Some spending maintains laboratory and manufacturing
capacity; some adds capability for new products, biopharma, or filtration.
The valuation should not capitalize all of it as growth or dismiss all of it as
maintenance. The required evidence is added revenue, utilization, margin, and
cash return from the new capacity.

### 5. Debt financed part of the portfolio expansion

Total debt rose to $39.384B at year-end 2025 from $31.275B. Approximately half
of cash and operating cash flow is generated outside the United States, which
adds cash-transfer and currency considerations even though management reports
adequate liquidity.

The investor should test interest coverage, debt maturities, currency, cash
location, acquisition funding, and whether share repurchases continue while
debt remains elevated. A buyback can increase per-share metrics while reducing
financial flexibility needed for integration.

### 6. Stock compensation must be included in per-share economics

Stock compensation is a non-cash income-statement expense, but dilution and the
need to repurchase shares are real owner claims. Compare stock compensation,
weighted-average shares, gross repurchases, net share change, and acquisition
consideration paid in shares. A business that repurchases stock mainly to offset
employee issuance is not returning the same cash as a business reducing its
share count after fully funding compensation.

## Lyn Alden lens

Thermo Fisher participates in a long-term increase in scientific complexity,
biologics production, diagnostics, and outsourced research. That supports
durability, but customer budgets remain cyclical. Higher rates can delay
biotech funding and laboratory capital purchases. Public research budgets,
pharma pipelines, and hospital investment can weaken at different times.

The balance sheet matters because Thermo Fisher has used debt and acquisitions
to build scale. The most resilient version of the thesis is a workflow platform
whose consumables and services grow through a downturn while debt declines from
internal cash. The less resilient version is a serial acquirer whose organic
demand slows while debt, integration work, and replacement capex rise.

## Damodaran lens and valuation

The narrative is “the scaled serving-science platform owns recurring scientific,
diagnostic, and biopharma workflow and can keep compounding through disciplined
acquisitions.” Translate it into:

`installed workflow -> consumables and services -> organic growth -> margin -> working capital and sustaining capex -> acquisition reinvestment -> debt and dilution -> common cash per share`

At the September 11, 2026 market snapshot, Thermo Fisher's equity value was
approximately $226.2B. A residual-cash screen is:

| Normalized annual residual cash after acquisitions | Multiple | Implied equity value |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $4.0B bear case | 35x | $140B |
| $6.0B base case | 40x | $240B |
| $8.0B strong case | 45x | $360B |

These are scenario outputs, not price targets. The base case assumes organic
growth recovers, Solventum and Clario earn acceptable returns, working capital
normalizes, debt does not absorb the cash stream, and recurring consumables and
services support the premium multiple.

The current value therefore requires more than a return to reported FCF. It
requires evidence that acquisition spending is an optional source of growth,
not a recurring payment required to preserve the headline growth rate.

## Incentives and thesis breakers

Management incentives emphasize adjusted EPS, revenue growth, operating margin,
free cash flow, organic growth, strategic execution, and capital deployment.
These metrics are useful, but adjusted EPS and FCF can improve through
acquisition accounting, working-capital timing, debt-funded buybacks, and
exclusions of recurring amortization or integration costs.

The thesis weakens if:

- organic growth remains low while reported growth depends on acquisitions;
- Solventum or Clario revenue grows without acceptable incremental margin and
  cash return;
- acquisition amortization, integration, restructuring, or quality costs recur
  at a level that makes adjusted earnings misleading;
- consumables and services fail to grow with the installed instrument base;
- working-capital releases reverse and FCF falls toward acquisition-adjusted
  cash levels below the valuation requirement;
- debt rises faster than operating cash and interest consumes the residual; or
- AI investment raises cost without measurable productivity, utilization,
  customer retention, or separately paid revenue.

## Evidence grades and next filing questions

- **Filed fact:** 2025 revenue, GAAP EPS, operating cash flow, PP&E spending,
  acquisitions, debt, working-capital movements, share repurchases, and
  dividends are anchored to the 2025 Form 10-K.
- **Current operating evidence:** Q1 and Q2 2026 revenue, organic growth,
  adjusted margin, and acquisition updates come from the preserved packet and
  quarterly filings; they require later cash and return confirmation.
- **Inference:** Thermo Fisher controls an important upstream scientific
  workflow, with potentially cleaner economics than hospitals or distributors,
  but its return quality is materially dependent on acquisition discipline.
- **Unresolved:** Solventum and Clario returns, organic growth, consumables
  attachment, debt reduction, working-capital normalization, sustaining capex,
  and the economic effect of AI spending.

The next filings should answer: Did organic growth improve without another major
deal? Did installed instruments produce more consumables and service cash? Did
Solventum and Clario earn above funding cost? Did debt fall? Did working capital
normalize? Did adjusted EPS converge with cash per share after amortization,
stock compensation, integration, and acquisition spending?
