# Trane Technologies — cooling, controls, service, and the cost of backlog

Date baseline: 2026-09-13  
Primary annual filing: [Trane 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1466258/000162828026005731/tt-20251231.htm)  
Annual report: [Trane 2025 Annual Report](https://s2.q4cdn.com/950394465/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/Trane-technologies-annual-report-web-ready-final.pdf)  
Companion packet: [Trane company packet](/home/mehtama1/git-repo/annual-report-research/extracted/industrial-goods/industrial-equipment-components/trane-technologies-plc/company-packet.md)

## Bottom line

Trane Technologies designs, manufactures, installs, and services HVAC,
transport refrigeration, building controls, energy services, and thermal
systems. Its strongest control point is a qualified cooling and controls system
inside a building, factory, cold-chain network, or data center. The customer
depends on uptime, efficiency, safety, regulatory compliance, and technician
support; replacement therefore has cost and operational risk.

FY2025 reported revenue was approximately $21.3B, organic revenue growth was
6%, adjusted continuing EPS was $13.06, adjusted EBITDA margin was 20.1%, and
free-cash-flow conversion was 98%. Continuing operating cash flow was $3.220B,
capital expenditures were $383M, and cash acquisitions were $276M. Backlog was
approximately $7.8B after bookings of $22.6B and a 106% book-to-bill ratio.

Current judgment: Trane is a cleaner physical-control case than a contractor.
It converts installed equipment, service, controls, and thermal expertise into
high cash conversion, while its customers carry the building and project
financing burden. But the valuation already assumes durable cooling demand,
strong backlog conversion, technician capacity, pricing, and successful
acquisitions. The risks are not eliminated; they move into customer capex,
project execution, refrigerant transitions, labor, inventory, and the cost of
servicing a larger installed base.

## What Trane controls

`building or process need -> HVAC and controls specification -> equipment and installation -> service, retrofit, optimization, and replacement`

Trane controls product design, engineering standards, brand qualification,
distribution, service relationships, controls integration, and energy-
efficiency expertise. It does not control commercial construction schedules,
data-center financing, building occupancy, electricity prices, regulation,
refrigerant policy, or the customer’s decision to defer a replacement.

The company’s climate and sustainability language has a concrete operating
translation: equipment, controls, digital energy management, liquid cooling,
retrofit, and service. The investment test is whether those offerings produce
recurring cash after technician labor, warranty, inventory, R&D, capacity, and
acquisition costs.

## Three-year cash picture

| Fiscal year | Continuing OCF | Capital expenditures | Acquisitions, net | OCF less capex and acquisitions |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2023 | 2,426.8 | 300.7 | 862.8 | 1,263.3 |
| 2024 | 3,177.7 | 370.6 | 180.3 | 2,626.8 |
| 2025 | 3,220.4 | 383.0 | 276.0 | 2,561.4 |

The 2025 residual is positive after capex and acquisitions, but it is not a
universal free-cash-flow measure. It precedes dividends, share repurchases,
debt service, restructuring, legal payments, pension obligations, and the
investment required to support higher bookings.

Trane’s reported free cash flow was $2.887B in 2025 after the company’s defined
adjustments. Those adjustments included restructuring costs, a legacy legal
liability, M&A transaction costs, proceeds from a corporate-asset sale, and a
special outperformance incentive-program adjustment. The management measure is
useful, but each adjustment must be tracked for recurrence and cash timing.

## Backlog and bookings

FY2025 bookings of $22.6B, book-to-bill of 106%, and $7.8B of backlog indicate
strong demand. Q1 2026 backlog reached $10.7B, and Q2 reached $12.1B as organic
bookings rose 37%. Americas Commercial HVAC bookings rose 50% in Q2.

Backlog becomes value only after:

1. customer funding and permits remain available;
2. equipment and component capacity is sufficient;
3. skilled technicians and installation partners are available;
4. project pricing covers materials, labor, warranty, and delay risk;
5. customer acceptance permits billing and collection; and
6. the installed system produces service and replacement demand later.

The next filing should separate product orders, project work, service,
controls, and data-center demand. It should also show cancellation, deferral,
lead time, customer concentration, backlog gross margin, and working-capital
requirements. A high book-to-bill can reflect customer ordering ahead of a
capacity constraint rather than a permanent increase in normalized demand.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. Strong cash conversion still needs a working-capital bridge

Continuing OCF increased from $2.427B in 2023 to $3.178B in 2024 and $3.220B in
2025. The annual report shows that inventory, receivables, payables, customer
advances, and other liabilities can materially move cash between years. A
98%-of-adjusted-earnings free-cash-flow conversion is strong evidence, but it
should be tested against inventory turns, receivable aging, customer deposits,
contract assets, and warranty accruals.

The key question is whether cash conversion remains strong when backlog is
delivered and inventory is rebuilt, not only when customer deposits or supplier
terms provide temporary support.

### 2. Service and software quality must be visible

The installed-base thesis assumes that equipment placement leads to service,
parts, controls, energy optimization, and replacement. The company should
report or allow inference of service revenue, recurring contract renewal,
technician utilization, attachment, gross margin, and customer retention.

If equipment sales rise but service revenue and renewal do not, the system is
more cyclical than the installed-base narrative suggests. If service grows but
field labor, warranty, and inventory rise faster, recurrence may not create
clean owner cash.

### 3. Data-center demand can be real and still be concentrated

Data centers require cooling, controls, and thermal management, and Trane has
expanded into those offerings. This is a credible demand channel. It can also
create customer concentration, project timing, capacity commitments, and
pricing risk. Hyperscalers or large developers can delay projects, specify
multiple vendors, or negotiate aggressively once capacity expands.

Track data-center orders separately from broader commercial HVAC, project
milestones, customer concentration, margin, receivables, and capex. Do not
capitalize all data-center bookings at the same multiple as recurring service.

### 4. Acquisitions and integration affect the clean story

Trane paid $862.8M for acquisitions in 2023, $180.3M in 2024, and $276M in
2025. Acquisitions can add controls, energy services, liquid cooling, and local
technician capability. They also add goodwill, acquired intangibles,
integration costs, and businesses with lower margins during the transition.

The proper cohort test is revenue retained, recurring service attached,
incremental margin, working capital, capex, and return on the purchase price.
Adjusted EPS growth alone cannot show whether the acquisition earned above the
cost of capital.

### 5. Adjusted measures include real future payments

Trane’s free-cash-flow definition adjusted for a three-year outperformance
incentive program whose cash payment could occur in March 2027 if targets were
met. That does not imply improper reporting; it shows why incentive accruals
and payment timing belong in the owner-cash bridge. “Adjusted” cash should be
reconciled to GAAP OCF, cash paid, and future obligations each year.

## Alden macro and durability lens

Trane is exposed to commercial construction, building replacement, data-center
capacity, energy prices, refrigerant rules, efficiency regulation, cold-chain
activity, rates, and technician availability. Cooling and reliability can be
necessary expenditures, but customers still have budgets and can defer projects.

The balance-sheet test is whether the company can fund capacity, service
technicians, inventory, R&D, cybersecurity, acquisitions, and shareholder
returns during a weaker construction cycle. Liquidity is stronger when the
company has low working-capital stress, a manageable debt ladder, and flexible
capacity commitments.

Durability is strongest in qualified service and retrofit relationships. It is
weaker in new-build projects dependent on a small number of large customers or
on a short data-center construction cycle.

## Damodaran narrative-to-numbers lens

The narrative is “Trane is a high-quality climate and thermal-infrastructure
company with secular cooling demand, pricing power, service recurrence, and
operating leverage.” The model must specify:

1. organic growth by commercial, residential, transport, and service activity;
2. equipment versus recurring service and controls revenue;
3. backlog conversion, cancellations, and project margin;
4. technician capacity and labor cost;
5. data-center exposure and customer concentration;
6. capex, R&D, warranty, and working-capital requirements;
7. acquisition returns and integration costs; and
8. the normalized reinvestment rate after the current capacity cycle.

Trane is a mature high-quality platform in a current expansion phase. The
valuation should not assume that 2026 bookings growth continues indefinitely or
that every project dollar has the economics of a recurring service contract.

investment recommendation.

## Incentives, thesis breakers, and next filings

Management has an incentive to emphasize bookings, backlog, adjusted EPS,
free-cash-flow conversion, sustainability, and data-center opportunity. Those
are relevant, but the owner test is cash after working capital, service labor,
warranty, capex, incentives, acquisitions, and debt.

The thesis weakens if:

- bookings and backlog grow while project margin, delivery, or cash collection falls;
- service and controls fail to attach to the installed base;
- data-center orders become concentrated and customers delay or renegotiate;
- inventory, receivables, warranty, or technician costs rise faster than revenue;
- acquisitions add revenue but reduce returns or require repeated adjustments;
- free-cash-flow conversion depends on customer advances, supplier terms, or
  exclusions that recur every year; or
- refrigerant, energy-efficiency, labor, or regulatory transitions require more
  capital than pricing can recover.

The next filings should show service and controls revenue, backlog composition,
orders, cancellation and deferral, gross margin, inventory and receivables,
warranty, technician headcount and utilization, data-center concentration,
acquisition cohorts, capex, R&D, debt, and incentive cash payments.

## Evidence grade

- Control point: **A-** — qualified HVAC, controls, and service relationships
  create real switching and uptime value.
- Operating economics: **A-** — high margins and strong cash conversion are
  supported, but project mix and customer budgets remain relevant.
- Cash conversion: **B+** — continuing OCF and FCF are strong, while working
  capital and adjusted-cash definitions require ongoing testing.
- Balance-sheet resilience: **B+** — liquidity is healthy, but expansion,
  acquisitions, and customer-cycle exposure can increase claims.
- Capital allocation: **B+** — capex and acquisitions support capability, but
  purchase-price and integration returns need cohort evidence.
- Valuation: **B-** — current value requires a high-quality continuation of
  growth and service economics.
