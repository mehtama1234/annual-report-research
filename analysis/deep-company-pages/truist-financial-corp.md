# Truist Financial: merger repair, regional scale, and the proof of durable returns

## What the company does

Truist is a large regional bank serving consumers, small businesses, commercial
customers, wealth clients, and institutions. It combines deposits, consumer and
commercial lending, payments, treasury services, wealth, capital markets,
investment banking, mortgage, and insurance-related capabilities.

The company was built through the combination of BB&T and SunTrust. Its core
investment question is therefore not only whether the regional franchise is
valuable. It is whether a large merger platform can finish reducing duplicate
systems and expenses while retaining customers, strengthening controls, and
earning an adequate return on the enlarged common equity base.

## FY2025 and Q2 2026 baseline

| Measure | FY2025 | Q2 2026 / latest evidence |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total revenue | $20.319B | $5.27B quarterly; $5.31B taxable-equivalent |
| Net income available to common shareholders | $4.974B | $1.519B quarterly |
| Diluted EPS | $3.82 | $1.23 quarterly |
| Net interest income | $14.423B | $3.62B quarterly |
| Noninterest income | $5.896B | $1.64B quarterly |
| ROTCE | Not the cleanest FY2025 headline measure | 15.4% |
| CET1 ratio | 10.8% | Approximately 10.9% in Q2 packet |

FY2025 common earnings increased from 2024, but the comparison is not clean.
The 2025 results included $156M of severance, a $130M legal settlement
accrual, and $19M of securities losses. 2024 included unusual effects from the
insurance divestiture and other changes. Truist reported $20.319B of revenue,
of which $14.423B was net interest income and $5.896B was noninterest income.

Q2 2026 net income available to common shareholders was $1.519B, up 29% year
over year, with total revenue up 5.5% on a taxable-equivalent basis. NII grew
only 0.9%, while noninterest income rose 17%, driven by investment banking,
trading, and wealth-management income. That mix produced a strong quarter but
also makes the current return sensitive to capital-markets conditions.

## Where the economics sit

### Deposits and regional relationships

Truist's deposits provide funding and access to consumer, small-business, and
commercial relationships. Premier clients, digital acquisition, and deeper
relationships are management priorities. Deposit value depends on operating
activity, rate paid, noninterest-bearing balances, retention, and cross-selling
rather than branch count alone.

The bank must show whether deposits remain stable when customers can move cash
to money-market funds or higher-yielding products. Track deposit beta,
uninsured balances, wholesale funding, liquidity, and product attachment. A
regional bank can appear stable while losing its lowest-cost deposits.

### Lending and credit risk

Lending remains the largest burden. Commercial real estate, office exposure,
construction, commercial and industrial credit, consumer lending, and mortgage
all have different loss behavior and capital requirements.

The test is risk-adjusted return after funding, expected loss, capital, and
servicing cost. Track criticized loans, nonaccruals, delinquencies,
charge-offs, allowance coverage, reserve assumptions, and concentrations by
property type and geography. Loan growth should not be credited as value if it
only expands the denominator of future losses.

### Wealth, payments, and fee businesses

Wealth management, payments, investment banking, trading, and mortgage diversify
Truist beyond net interest income. Wealth can generate recurring fees and deepen
relationships, but asset-based fees rise with markets and net flows. Investment
banking and trading are cyclical, and mortgage depends on rates and housing
turnover.

Q2 2026's 17% noninterest-income growth must be decomposed into client flows,
market levels, underwriting and advisory volume, trading conditions, mortgage
activity, and any one-time items. Also include producer compensation,
technology, compliance, and capital costs. A fee line is not automatically
cleaner owner cash.

## Earnings quality and financial-engineering review

### Merger cleanup is a recurring economic question

Truist's merger created a large platform and a large integration burden. In
2025, severance and legal costs remained material. Even when a specific charge
does not recur, system replacement, customer migration, branch decisions,
employee retention, cyber controls, and regulatory work are necessary costs of
maintaining the combined bank.

Adjusted earnings should therefore be reconciled to GAAP earnings over several
years. Repeated integration, remediation, restructuring, legal, and technology
charges should not all be removed from the earnings base.

### Capital-markets growth needs normalization

The Q2 improvement came disproportionately from noninterest income, including
investment banking and trading. Those businesses can be durable platforms, but
their revenue varies with issuance, advisory activity, volatility, spreads, and
client risk appetite.

Use a multi-year through-cycle average after compensation and risk capital. A
high quarter should not be capitalized as a permanent margin improvement.

### Lower tax rate may not be operational earnings

The Q2 2026 effective tax rate was 14.4%, below the prior-year 18.0%, partly
because of renewable-energy tax-credit investing activity. That can be a real
capital-allocation benefit, but it should be separated from recurring operating
improvement in valuation.

### Provision expense and reserves can move the cycle

Credit provision reflects expected losses, reserve changes, portfolio mix, and
economic assumptions. A reserve release raises current earnings without making
the loans safer; a reserve build can depress earnings before realized losses.

Compare provisions with charge-offs, delinquency migration, criticized assets,
nonaccruals, allowance coverage, and loan growth. Management's risk-discipline
narrative must be tested against the vintages added during the post-merger
period.

### Capital returns can hide a shrinking buffer

Truist returned significant capital while its CET1 ratio was 10.8% at year-end
2025, down 70 basis points from 2024 because capital returns and risk-weighted
assets outpaced capital generation. Q2 2026 included $1.8B of dividends and
repurchases.

The relevant measure is common equity retained after distributions relative to
stress losses, risk-weighted-asset growth, securities losses, and regulatory
requirements. EPS growth from a lower share count does not by itself establish
value creation.

## Balance-sheet resilience and macro conditions

The Lyn Alden lens focuses on funding, liquidity, rate sensitivity, and loss
absorption. Truist's Southeast and regional footprint can produce relationship
deposits and loan growth, but it also creates commercial property and local
economic exposure. A bank with a diversified fee mix can still be vulnerable if
deposit pricing rises and credit losses arrive together.

Stress should include deposit migration, higher wholesale funding, lower office
and commercial property values, unemployment, borrower refinancing, securities
losses, and operational failures during continued merger integration. Liquidity
and capital must be available at the regulated entities where they are needed,
not just at the consolidated parent.

## Damodaran lens: translate the merger-repair narrative

The narrative is that Truist can complete merger cleanup, improve operating
leverage, deepen client relationships, and convert scale plus wealth and
capital-markets businesses into stronger returns.

That narrative requires:

1. Deposits remain stable at a reasonable cost across the rate cycle.
2. Merger-related expense declines without weaker technology, controls, or
   customer service.
3. Wealth, payments, and other fees grow organically after compensation and
   market normalization.
4. Loan growth earns above expected loss, funding, and capital costs.
5. Commercial credit remains controlled as the portfolio matures.
6. Capital returns do not weaken the balance-sheet buffer.

Truist is a mature financial institution in a post-merger repair phase. The
important growth variable is incremental return on tangible common equity after
all cleanup and capital costs, not the size of the combined platform.

## Valuation screen

The September 14, 2026 market feed showed an equity value of approximately
$62.4B. This is a normalized common-net-income screen.

| Case | Normalized common earnings | Earnings multiple | Implied equity value | Required interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bear | $4.5B | 10x | $45B | Merger costs persist, fee income normalizes lower, credit or funding worsens |
| Base | $5.5B | 12x | $66B | Expense discipline improves and the platform earns near current common earnings |
| Bull | $7.0B | 14x | $98B | Durable wealth and fee growth, clean merger execution, stable credit, stronger ROTCE |

The current value is close to the base case. It requires evidence that Truist
can earn more than its post-merger baseline without relying on favorable trading,
investment banking, tax credits, or repeated cost exclusions.

## Thesis breakers and future filing tests

| Question | Evidence that supports the thesis | Evidence that weakens it |
|---|---|---|
| Is merger repair complete? | Integration and legal costs fall while service and controls improve | “One-time” charges recur or customer, employee, and control metrics worsen |
| Are deposits durable? | Stable low-cost deposits and controlled beta | Deposit migration requires expensive funding or wholesale borrowing |
| Are fees recurring? | Wealth flows, payments, and recurring service fees grow after full cost | Trading, underwriting, asset prices, or tax credits carry growth |
| Is credit controlled? | Reserves, charge-offs, criticized loans, and nonaccruals remain stable | Commercial property or consumer losses outpace reserves and capital |
| Is operating leverage real? | Expense growth remains below revenue after technology and risk investment | Expense cuts defer maintenance or increase remediation and outages |
| Are capital returns safe? | CET1 remains strong after payouts and risk-weighted-asset growth | Distributions weaken buffers or constrain growth and controls |

## Bottom line

Truist has a valuable regional relationship platform, but the central investment
case is execution: can the combined bank finish absorbing merger complexity and
earn durable returns on tangible common equity? FY2025 shows improvement but
still includes severance and legal items. Q2 2026 is strong, yet the mix was
driven by noninterest income, including capital-markets and wealth activity, and
the effective tax rate benefited from renewable-energy tax credits.

The thesis becomes stronger when recurring fee growth, stable deposits, normal
credit, lower full-cost integration spending, and resilient CET1 appear together
over several filings. Until then, the market is paying partly for a repair
outcome that remains to be demonstrated.

**Evidence grade:** Strong for FY2025 and Q2 2026 reported results, revenue mix,
capital, and disclosed unusual items; medium for the post-merger operating
trajectory; unresolved for through-cycle fee earnings, commercial credit, and
the permanence of expense improvement.
