# Vale — Brazilian ore corridor, base-metals growth, and trust-repair burden

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are IFRS figures in U.S. dollars; dollar
amounts are in millions unless otherwise stated.

## How to read this memo

Adjusted EBITDA is management's operating comparison measure. It is not cash
available to owners. Recurring free cash flow is Vale's normalized measure after
working capital, capital expenditure, financing costs, taxes, associates and
joint ventures, legacy-event spending, streaming, and other adjustments. The
important forensic question is which of those deductions are genuinely
non-recurring. Vale's dam, remediation, and Samarco obligations have lasted for
years, so excluding them can make recurring cash look cleaner than the economic
burden actually is.

## Investment question

Vale controls a Brazilian iron-ore mining, processing, rail, port, and customer
system, with copper and nickel operations grouped in Vale Base Metals. Its
potential advantage is low-cost scale, logistics, product quality, and the
ability to fund base-metals growth from iron-ore cash. The central question is
whether improved operating stability and Base Metals growth create durable
owner cash after remediation, environmental, partner, currency, and project
claims.

The 2025 annual report recorded $38.403B of revenue, $15.458B of adjusted
EBITDA, and $2.352B of profit attributable to Vale shareholders. The gap is not
automatically suspicious: impairments, taxes, financing, and legacy obligations
explain part of it. It does mean that adjusted EBITDA cannot be used as a
substitute for common-owner cash.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Iron ore remains the cash anchor. 2025 production reached 336 million tonnes,
  the highest level since 2018, but revenue and cash still depend on realized
  prices, product premiums, freight, the Brazilian real, and China-linked demand.
- The iron-ore system includes mines, beneficiation, pellets, rail, ports,
  shipping, and third-party purchases. A production record is not enough if
  all-in costs, freight, product premiums, or working capital deteriorate.
- Vale Base Metals is becoming economically meaningful. 2025 adjusted EBITDA
  from Vale Base Metals was $3.355B versus $1.453B in 2024, led by copper and
  nickel. The next question is whether this growth remains after project capital,
  operating costs, streaming claims, and partner interests.
- 2025 sustaining investment was $4.371B of total capital expenditure. That
  makes the renewal burden visible: reported mine cash must fund equipment,
  fleet, processing, safety, and mine-life work before it can be distributed.
- The cash bridge contains recurring obligations. Vale paid $874M for Brumadinho
  and $2.298B related to Samarco in 2025, while also carrying $1.911B of
  Brumadinho provisions and $2.613B of Samarco provisions at year-end. Calling
  these items exceptional does not make the liability disappear.
- The 2025 income statement included a $4.599B impairment and disposal charge.
  This is a direct test of earlier capital-allocation assumptions, particularly
  in nickel and other Base Metals assets. An impairment is not itself proof of
  misconduct, but it is evidence that prior carrying values or price assumptions
  were too high.
- The company reported no dam structure at emergency level 3, 63% completion of
  the upstream dam-decharacterization program, and 81% fulfillment of Brumadinho
  remediation obligations. These are useful progress measures, but they are not
  the same as the final cost, schedule, or social-license outcome.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Is recurring free cash flow truly recurring?

Rebuild the $4.762B measure from pro forma EBITDA. Keep working capital,
derivatives, taxes, interest, capex, associates, legacy payments, streaming,
and “other” items visible. Then show a second measure after actual Brumadinho,
Samarco, de-characterization, and railway-concession cash. The difference is an
economic burden even if management labels part of it non-recurring.

### 2. Does iron-ore scale survive mid-cycle prices?

Separate price, volume, grade, product mix, premiums, freight, currency, and
cash cost. In 2025 iron-ore C1 cash cost was $21.3 per tonne, while all-in cost
including sustaining investment was $63.8 per tonne. Use the latter type of
burden when testing dividend and project capacity.

### 3. Is Base Metals growth creating value?

For copper and nickel, record production, realized price, unit cost, sustaining
capital, growth capital, streaming, partner ownership, and project schedule.
The 2025 impairment shows why higher adjusted EBITDA does not by itself prove
that the capital base is earning its cost.

### 4. Are currency and derivatives helping the underlying business?

Vale's cash bridge included $579M of net cash received from derivative
settlements in 2025. Identify the hedged exposure, the period being hedged, and
the cash cost of the hedge. A favorable settlement can protect cash, but it is
not recurring operating improvement.

### 5. Who carries the trust and remediation burden?

Track provisions, cash payments, legal settlements, dam-decharacterization
progress, Samarco obligations, tailings safety, water, and community commitments.
Compare the remaining obligation with annual owner cash and net debt.

### 6. How much cash belongs to common owners?

Separate controlled subsidiaries, associates and joint ventures, non-controlling
interests, streaming transactions, dividends received, debt, leases, and
shareholder debentures. Vale's iron ore and Base Metals EBITDA are not all
available on the same basis to Vale common shareholders.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** Vale is exposed to global industrial demand, China,
  freight, energy, the Brazilian real, trade policy, resource nationalism, and
  institutional trust. Scale and low costs help during weak prices, but net debt
  and remediation obligations reduce flexibility.
- **Damodaran lens:** value the iron-ore corridor, pellets, copper, nickel, and
  project options separately. Use mid-cycle prices, all-in sustaining cost,
  reserve renewal, final project capital, probability-weighted ramp, taxes,
  ownership, legacy claims, and debt.
- **Forensic lens:** the main risks are treating recurring free cash flow as
  fully available cash while excluding long-lived legacy payments, treating
  adjusted EBITDA as owner earnings, treating a derivative settlement as
  operating improvement, and treating higher Base Metals EBITDA as value
  creation despite the impairment.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $65.19B of equity value at
$15.23 per ADR. Against 2025 recurring free cash flow of $4.762B, the screen is
about 13.7x. A normalized recurring-owner-cash range of $3B / $5B / $7B at
10x / 12x / 15x implies $30B / $60B / $105B. The current price therefore
requires the market to believe that 2025's lower capital spending and stronger
Base Metals contribution can persist while remediation and debt burdens fall.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Iron-ore prices or premiums decline while freight, currency, or all-in costs
   stay high.
2. Copper or nickel growth requires more capital, produces less cash, or leads to
   further impairments.
3. Recurring free cash flow remains dependent on favorable derivatives,
   working-capital releases, asset sales, or exclusion of legacy cash payments.
4. Brumadinho, Samarco, dam, water, or community obligations rise faster than
   provisions and cash generation.
5. Expanded net debt does not decline after dividends and Base Metals investment.
6. Production rises without a corresponding increase in cash per share after
   partners, streaming claims, taxes, and remediation.
