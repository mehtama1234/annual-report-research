## Bottom line

Veralto controls embedded quality and water workflows: analytics, monitoring,
treatment, coding, marking, inspection, traceability, and product-integrity
systems. It is closer to a measurement-and-control platform than to a project
contractor or commodity chemical producer. The economic relationship is valuable
because customers need reliable proof that water is safe, products are coded,
and essential-goods processes remain within specification.

FY2025 produced $1.077B of operating cash flow against only $63M of PP&E,
leaving roughly $1.014B of cash after PP&E. That is materially cleaner than
Ecolab’s $283M residual after PP&E and acquisition/affiliate cash. The caveat is
that Veralto’s strong conversion does not make it acquisition-free: goodwill
was $2.838B, debt including current maturities was $2.673B, and bolt-on
acquisitions, cost optimization, and capital returns remain part of the model.

## Control point and burden carrier

The economic chain is:

`water / product-quality risk -> measurement, coding, analytics, and control workflow -> trusted output -> recurring replacement, monitoring, and compliance demand`

Water Quality and Product Quality & Innovation extend across industrial water,
reuse, environmental monitoring, food and beverage, coding and marking,
inspection, and traceability. The control point is embedded measurement and
workflow knowledge. The burden carrier is less steel and field labor than
Ecolab, but includes R&D, installed equipment, service support, acquisitions,
customer qualification, and the risk that monitoring becomes a procurement
commodity.

## Earnings quality and forensic questions

The reported cash conversion is strong, but “more than 100% FCF conversion” is
a management metric that needs a bridge. The first tests are working-capital
timing, restructuring and cost-optimization savings, acquisition contribution,
and the treatment of recurring R&D and service investment. Accounts receivable
rose to $897M and inventory to $307M in 2025; these balances are not alarming
by themselves, but they should be read with cash flow and organic growth.

Goodwill of $2.838B and the bolt-on acquisition strategy create the main
financial-engineering risk. Acquisitions can add high-retention instruments,
analytics, and water capabilities, but the company must demonstrate acquired
revenue, margin, retention, cross-sell, integration cost, and cash return. A
strong FCF-conversion percentage can coexist with weak incremental returns if
acquisition spending is excluded from the recurring denominator.

## Why the recurring model still needs a full-cost test

Veralto's low PP&E requirement is a real advantage, but it does not mean that
the business can grow without reinvestment. The company must keep instruments
accurate, develop software and measurement products, train and support users,
qualify equipment inside customer processes, and buy adjacent capabilities.
Those costs can appear in research, selling, service, or acquisition lines
rather than in PP&E.

The cleanest current reading is therefore conditional. Veralto appears to
convert existing revenue into cash more efficiently than project-industrial or
field-service peers, but the investor must subtract the cost of maintaining
the measurement relationship and the cash paid for acquisitions before calling
the result durable owner earnings. The key comparison is not “recurring versus
non-recurring.” It is cash after the full cost of keeping the workflow trusted,
useful, and difficult to replace.

This places Veralto in the [embedded quality, water, and commodity comparison](../cross-sector/embedded-quality-water-versus-hygiene-and-commodity-forensic-comparison-2026-09-13.md)
alongside Ecolab and CECO. Veralto controls measurement and product-quality
proof; Ecolab controls hygiene, water treatment, and site service; CECO controls
larger engineered environmental and process systems. Veralto currently shows
the cleaner reported cash bridge, but its valuation depends on proving that
organic growth, acquisition returns, R&D, and service support remain strong
after the same full-cost test.

## Lyn Alden durability lens

Veralto benefits from water scarcity, public-health and environmental standards,
food safety, traceability, and the rising value of reliable measurement in
industrial systems. These are durable needs across cycles. Liquidity still
matters: customers can defer equipment upgrades, industrial volumes can slow,
and acquisitions become more expensive when rates rise. The balance sheet is
more resilient than a highly leveraged physical builder, but debt maturities and
the pace of bolt-ons remain relevant.

## Management incentives and capital allocation

The proxy statement should establish whether annual and long-term awards reward
revenue, organic growth, adjusted operating profit, free cash flow, cash
conversion, return on invested capital, margin, customer retention, water-
quality outcomes, share price, or per-share cash. Record definitions, weights,
threshold/target/maximum levels, and exclusions for acquisitions,
restructuring, cost optimization, amortization, stock compensation, and
working-capital changes.

The incentive tests are:

- Does an organic-growth metric exclude acquired revenue and distinguish price,
  volume, and mix?
- Does a cash-conversion metric charge inventory, receivables, recurring R&D,
  service labor, customer qualification, and capitalized software?
- Does adjusted operating profit exclude recurring cost-optimization,
  restructuring, acquisition, or integration costs?
- Are bolt-on acquisitions evaluated by retained customers, cross-sell,
  post-integration margin, debt, dilution, and owner cash rather than revenue
  or goodwill growth?
- Are dividends and repurchases evaluated after acquisition spending, debt
  service, and the investment needed to preserve measurement and service
  capability?

A high cash-conversion target can reward working-capital release while the
business is still growing, or can exclude acquisition cash from the recurring
denominator. A margin target can underinvest in R&D and service capacity. The
proxy and subsequent filings must show whether management is accountable for
organic growth, full-cost cash, and return on invested capital.

For each acquisition cohort, compare purchase price with acquired revenue,
margin, retention, cross-sell, integration spending, working capital, debt,
goodwill, diluted shares, and post-integration cash. For each capital return,
compare the cash paid with normalized owner cash after acquisitions, R&D,
service investment, and debt.

## Valuation expectations

The existing mechanical screen uses $700M, $1.000B, and $1.300B of normalized
owner cash at 16x, 20x, and 24x. It should be read as an expectations test:

| Case | Required operating outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Bear | Organic growth slows, acquisition returns weaken, and cash conversion requires working-capital release or cost cuts |
| Base | Measurement and water-quality demand remain durable, organic growth is stable, and bolt-ons earn above their full capital cost |
| Bull | Organic growth improves, high-retention bolt-ons compound, margins expand without underinvestment, and cash per diluted share rises |

The approximate $23.13B equity value therefore requires more than high reported
cash conversion. It requires recurring cash after R&D, service capability,
working capital, acquisition spending, debt, stock compensation, and diluted
shares. The cases are scenario outputs, not price targets.

## Damodaran narrative-to-numbers lens

The bullish narrative is that Veralto owns mission-critical measurement and
quality workflows, compounds through high-retention bolt-ons, expands margins
through operating discipline, and converts nearly all earnings into cash. The
skeptical narrative is that the market capitalizes a Danaher-style operating
system while organic growth remains modest and acquisitions, R&D, and
customer qualification carry the real reinvestment burden.

For a mechanical screen: bear $700M × 16 = $11.2B, base $1.000B × 20 = $20.0B,
and bull $1.300B × 24 = $31.2B, versus a current equity snapshot of
approximately $23.13B. The base case is below the market by roughly 14%; the
bull requires durable conversion plus successful bolt-ons. These are scenario
inputs, not a price target.

## Thesis breakers and next filing tests

The thesis weakens if core growth fades, water-quality demand becomes a
procurement commodity, acquired businesses fail to retain customers, FCF
conversion depends on receivables or inventory releases, cost savings replace
organic productivity, goodwill rises faster than cash earnings, or debt-funded
returns outrun incremental returns. The next filing should capture organic,
price, volume, and acquisition growth; segment margin; working capital;
acquisition cohorts; recurring R&D/service investment; debt maturities; SBC;
and cash after buybacks and dividends.
