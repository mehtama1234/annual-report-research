# WaFd: thrift-to-business-bank transition, deposit funding, and credit proof

## What the company does

WaFd is a branch-based, deposit-funded regional bank that historically carried a
strong savings-and-loan identity. Management is now shifting the business away
from mortgage-lending dependence and toward business banking, commercial
relationships, treasury services, and other solutions for companies.

That transition is the central investment question. The company is not merely
trying to grow loans. It is trying to make the branch and deposit franchise more
useful to business customers while preserving funding discipline. Success would
raise the relevance and returns of the franchise. Failure could leave a smaller
bank with a mixed loan book, higher execution costs, and no clear advantage.

## Where the economics sit

### Deposit franchise and branch relevance

WaFd's valuable asset is the relationship that produces operating deposits and
repeat borrowing. Transaction deposits are usually more useful than high-cost
time deposits because they can support payments and broader service attachment.
They are not permanent: customers can move cash when rates, trust, or service
quality change.

Track transaction and time deposits, rate paid, deposit beta, uninsured and
concentrated balances, borrowings, branch productivity, treasury usage, and
customer retention. The thesis requires the deposit base to remain affordable
while the bank invests in new business capabilities.

### Business-banking transition

Management describes FY2025 as a shift from mortgage lending toward providing
solutions for businesses. This can improve customer relevance and fee attachment,
but it changes the risk profile. Business banking requires relationship managers,
underwriting, treasury systems, credit monitoring, and local expertise. Those
costs may appear before the new revenue stream is mature.

Measure business-loan originations, deposit attachment, treasury fees, client
retention, relationship-manager productivity, credit quality, acquisition cost,
and revenue per customer. A new label for the loan book is not proof of a better
business model.

### Loan book and real-estate exposure

WaFd still has a material loan and real-estate history. The transition can reduce
mortgage dependence, but it does not eliminate commercial real-estate, business,
construction, residential, or consumer risk.

Use the loan bridge:

`originations - repayments + purchases - sales = ending loans`

Then separate loans by type, geography, borrower, collateral, maturity, rate,
and concentration. Read growth with delinquencies, nonperforming assets,
nonaccruals, criticized loans, reserves, charge-offs, and collateral values.

### Margin and efficiency

Q3 FY2026 NIM was approximately `2.81%`, and the efficiency ratio was
approximately `53.69%`. A higher margin can result from asset repricing, lower
deposit cost, mix, or temporary funding conditions. Better efficiency can result
from scale or from deferred investment.

Reconcile NIM into loan yield, securities yield, deposit cost, borrowings,
hedging, and mix. Reconcile efficiency into personnel, branch, technology,
compliance, credit, and restructuring costs. The transition is successful only
if operating improvements remain after the bank fully funds the capabilities
needed for business customers.

## Earnings quality and forensic review

### Fiscal-year comparison and quarter sequence

FY2025 net income of approximately `$226M` and ROATCE of approximately `9.20%`
provide the starting baseline. Q1-Q3 FY2026 EPS increased from `$0.79` to
`$0.84`, while ROATCE increased from `10.6%` to `11.0%`. Test whether the
improvement comes from sustainable revenue, credit normalization, provision
timing, repurchases, lower funding cost, or changes in tangible equity.

Do not conclude that EPS growth proves the transition. Reconcile net income to
operating cash flow, tangible common equity, share count, provisions, securities
gains or losses, and capital returns.

### Credit and reserve timing

Q2 FY2026 nonperforming assets improved to approximately `0.48%` of total assets
from `0.75%` in Q1; delinquencies improved to approximately `0.78%` of loans from
`1.07%`. That is useful evidence, but a single improvement can reflect sales,
charge-offs, restructurings, collateral actions, or portfolio mix.

Track delinquency migration, nonperforming assets, criticized and classified
loans, allowance coverage, charge-offs, recoveries, loan modifications, and
foreclosed assets. Separate business banking from legacy mortgage and real-estate
portfolios. Reserve releases should be checked against actual loss emergence,
not treated as operating improvement.

### Repurchases and tangible capital

WaFd repurchased approximately `2.74M` shares in Q2 FY2026. Buybacks can improve
EPS and ROATCE, but they also reduce the capital buffer while the bank changes
its loan mix and carries regional credit risk.

Compare repurchase price with tangible book value, CET1 and other capital ratios,
loan growth, allowance coverage, securities marks, liquidity, and stress capital.
The test is whether the remaining bank has more value per share after the buyback
without less capacity to absorb losses.

### Taxonomy and strategic presentation

AnnualReports still classifies WaFd as `Savings & Loans`, while the SEC filing
chain presents it under `National Commercial Banks`. Management's business-bank
language is a strategic claim. The mismatch is useful, not a contradiction to
resolve by choosing one label: it shows that the analyst must follow the actual
assets, deposits, loan types, revenue, and operating costs.

## Balance-sheet resilience and macro conditions

The Lyn Alden lens focuses on deposit liquidity, rates, real-estate refinancing,
regional business conditions, and capital. Higher rates can improve loan yields
but increase deposit competition and borrower stress. Lower rates can reduce
deposit cost while compressing loan yields and changing mortgage prepayment.

Stress cases should include:

- transaction-deposit runoff and higher deposit beta;
- commercial-real-estate and construction weakness;
- mortgage and residential credit deterioration;
- higher business defaults and unemployment;
- securities losses and expensive wholesale funding;
- borrowings rising while deposits fall;
- transition costs exceeding new business revenue;
- repurchases before credit risk is visible;
- technology, branch, fraud, or regulatory failures.

The bank's smaller scale increases the importance of concentration, liquidity,
and execution. A higher ROATCE does not establish resilience if it depends on a
small denominator after repurchases or temporarily low losses.

## Damodaran lens: convert the repositioning narrative into numbers

The narrative is that WaFd can preserve a trusted western deposit franchise while
moving from mortgage lending toward higher-value business relationships. That
narrative requires:

1. Transaction deposits remain stable and affordable.
2. Business customers attach deposits, treasury services, and profitable credit.
3. New loan categories earn more than their full underwriting, staffing,
   technology, and capital cost.
4. Legacy mortgage and real-estate exposure declines without adverse loss
   surprises.
5. NIM and efficiency improve for structural reasons, not only rate or provision
   timing.
6. The branch system becomes more productive as the customer mix changes.
7. Repurchases occur below intrinsic value while capital and liquidity remain
   adequate.

Value WaFd from normalized common earnings or ROATCE, tangible book, deposit
quality, credit concentration, capital, and the probability that the strategic
transition succeeds. The right valuation is not a premium for a new business
label; it is a premium only after the new customer and credit economics are
visible in filings.

A market snapshot at 00:15 UTC on 2026-09-15 showed an equity value of
approximately `$2.489B`. These are normalized earnings sensitivities, not a
price target.

| Case | Normalized net income | Illustrative multiple | Implied equity value | What must be true |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Downside | `$150M` | `8x` | `$1.2B` | Transition costs rise, deposits reprice, or legacy credit worsens |
| Base | `$250M` | `11x` | `$2.75B` | Business banking improves mix while credit and funding remain controlled |
| Upside | `$325M` | `13x` | `$4.225B` | New relationships produce durable fees, better efficiency, and stronger ROATCE |

Check these scenarios against tangible book value, share count after buybacks,
capital ratios, deposit stability, and the actual loss experience of the new loan
categories.
The current market value is below the illustrative base case and above the
downside case. That comparison is not a margin-of-safety conclusion: the base
requires business deposits, treasury services, and profitable new lending to
offset transition costs without weakening liquidity or stress capital.

## Thesis breakers and filing tests

| Question | Evidence supporting the thesis | Evidence weakening it |
|---|---|---|
| Is the business transition real? | Business deposits, treasury fees, and relationship lending grow with acceptable returns | Revenue remains mortgage- or rate-dependent while transition costs rise |
| Are deposits durable? | Transaction deposits hold at reasonable cost and support services | Time deposits or borrowings replace operating funding, or outflows accelerate |
| Is credit controlled? | NPAs, delinquencies, reserves, and criticized loans improve by portfolio | Legacy or new business credit deteriorates after growth or modifications |
| Is NIM structural? | Margin holds after deposit repricing, mix, and hedge normalization | Margin depends on temporary rate or funding conditions |
| Are efficiency gains real? | Ratio improves while staffing, technology, controls, and service remain adequate | Cost cuts defer credit, branch, technology, or compliance investment |
| Are buybacks safe? | Tangible value per share rises after stress capital remains sufficient | Repurchases raise EPS while liquidity or capital flexibility weakens |

## Bottom line

WaFd is a test of whether a thrift-style deposit franchise can become a more
valuable business bank. Its control point is the branch and operating-deposit
relationship; its potential cleaner economics are transaction funding, treasury
attachment, and better customer relevance. Its burden remains the regulated bank
balance sheet, regional real-estate exposure, liquidity, capital, and the cost of
building a new capability set.

FY2025 and Q3 FY2026 show higher ROATCE, improving EPS, a `2.81%` NIM, a
`53.69%` efficiency ratio, and lower Q2 credit indicators. The transition is not
proven until the filings show durable business deposits and fees, full-cost
profitability of new loan categories, stable legacy credit, and capital returns
that do not weaken resilience.

**Evidence grade:** Strong for FY2025 and Q1-Q3 FY2026 reported earnings, EPS,
assets, deposits, loans, NIM, efficiency, credit indicators, and repurchase
figures; medium for the strategic business-bank transition; unresolved for
through-cycle credit, deposit, liquidity, and capital-return behavior.
