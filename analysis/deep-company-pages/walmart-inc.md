# Walmart Inc. — retail traffic, attached services, and owner cash

Date baseline: 2026-09-14  
Primary evidence: [Walmart source ledger](../../extracted/services/discount-variety-stores/walmart-inc/source-ledger.md) and [Walmart company packet](../../extracted/services/discount-variety-stores/walmart-inc/company-packet.md)

Current filing checkpoint: [Walmart Q2 FY2027 earnings release](https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-26-000145/earningsreleasefy27q2.htm) and [Q2 FY2027 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000154/wmt-20260731.htm)

## Investment question

Walmart is a low-price, high-frequency retail system that is adding advertising,
membership, marketplace, fulfillment, and financial services. Management's own
description is important: these are presented as mutually reinforcing parts of
the omnichannel model, not as unrelated experiments.

The investment question is whether the attached businesses create durable
common-owner cash after the costs of preserving low prices, store coverage,
labor, inventory, delivery, technology, shrink, supplier support, and credit.
The second question is whether higher-margin growth is genuinely incremental
or partly compensates for pressure in the underlying retail business.

## What Walmart controls

Walmart controls access to a very large household-shopping relationship. That
relationship includes physical stores, digital search and ordering, pickup,
delivery, payment, membership, supplier placement, marketplace access, and
first-party purchase data.

The control sequence is:

`routine household demand -> store or digital visit -> merchandise and
fulfillment -> repeat relationship -> advertising, membership, marketplace,
and financial services -> cash after reinvestment`

The relationship is valuable because the customer returns frequently. It is
also expensive because the customer expects low prices and reliable product
availability. A high-margin advertising dollar should be evaluated together
with the retail margin and capital required to generate the shopper visit.

## Reported operating evidence

The archive's 2025 annual package covers the fiscal year ended January 31,
2026. The packet reports:

| Period | Revenue / operating signal | Cash signal | Attached-business signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 2025 | Total revenue grew `5.8%`; operating income declined `0.2%`; adjusted operating income grew `8.0%` | OCF `$27.5B`; FCF `$8.8B` | Global advertising grew `53%`; U.S. Walmart Connect grew `33%`; U.S. eCommerce grew `28%` |
| Q4 2025 | Total revenue grew `5.6%`; operating income grew `10.8%`; adjusted operating income grew `10.5%` | OCF `$41.6B`; FCF `$14.9B` | Global advertising grew `37%`; U.S. Walmart Connect grew `41%`; membership fee revenue grew `15.1%` |
| Q1 2026 | Total revenue grew `7.3%`; operating income grew `5.0%`; adjusted operating income grew `5.1%` | OCF `$4.7B` | Global advertising grew `37%`; U.S. Walmart Connect grew `44%`; eCommerce grew `26%`; global membership fee income grew `17.4%` |

The pattern supports a real attached-services strategy. It does not establish
that advertising or membership has a higher return on incremental capital than
the retail base. The next filing review must reconcile merchandise margin,
fulfillment cost, store capital, digital delivery, and attached-service
contribution separately.

## Q2 FY2027 checkpoint: attachment is growing, but cash is being reinvested

The quarter confirms that Walmart's customer relationship is becoming more
valuable, while also showing why the attached businesses cannot be analyzed
separately from the retail network. Q2 revenue was `$187.9B`, up 5.9%; Walmart
U.S. comparable sales rose 2.6%; global eCommerce grew 23%; global advertising
grew 38%; and global membership fee revenue grew 17%. The 10-Q says eCommerce
growth was primarily driven by store-fulfilled delivery, so the digital growth
still depends on stores, labor, inventory, and fulfillment capacity.

The quality of the margin improvement needs a one-time-benefit test. Gross
profit rate rose 96 basis points in the quarter, but Walmart says the increase
was primarily affected by tariff refunds, price investments, higher fuel cost,
and the mix benefit from advertising. The company also reports higher
self-insured liability claims, depreciation from capital investment, and
healthcare costs. A higher gross margin is therefore not yet proof that the
underlying retail network has permanently improved its economics.

For the first six months, operating cash flow was `$19.710B`, capital spending
was `$14.181B`, and Walmart-defined free cash flow was `$5.529B`, down from
`$6.943B` in the prior-year period. Capital spending rose `$2.8B` to support
omnichannel growth. Inventory was up 6.7% at quarter-end, while the cash-flow
statement shows a `$2.660B` inventory use of cash and a `$1.648B` accounts
payable source. These are not accusations of poor accounting; they are the
working-capital and reinvestment variables that determine whether the growth
layers become common-owner cash.

Walmart's own 10-Q states that its free-cash-flow definition excludes debt
service, other contractual obligations, and acquisitions. The current quarter
therefore supports a real attached-platform thesis but also raises the required
valuation denominator: the buyer must fund stores, delivery, automation,
technology, inventory, and claims before advertising and membership cash can
reach owners.

## Three-year cash bridge and valuation screen

The FY2026 Form 10-K reports operating cash flow of `$41.565B`, `$36.443B`,
and `$35.726B` for fiscal 2026, 2025, and 2024. It reports property and
equipment spending of `$26.642B`, `$23.783B`, and `$20.606B`, producing the
company-defined free-cash-flow series of `$14.923B`, `$12.660B`, and `$15.120B`.

This is a useful starting denominator, not unrestricted owner cash. Walmart's
own filing says its free-cash-flow definition does not deduct debt service,
other contractual obligations, or business acquisitions. The FY2026 filing
also says the increase in operating cash flow was helped by lower cash taxes
and the timing of certain payments, while the capital increase supported
omnichannel growth. The normalized screen therefore uses the reported series,
then asks whether the attached-services growth and network investment justify
assuming higher future cash.

The official Q2 management call adds a useful mechanism layer: management
describes value pressure, rapid eCommerce and marketplace growth, store-based
last-mile fulfillment, membership, advertising, and a material tariff-refund
timing effect. The call strengthens the control-point narrative, but it does
not prove standalone attached-service cash or normalized owner cash after
allocated fulfillment, labor, capex, tax, and working-capital burdens. See the
[Walmart Q2 management-call control-point memo](../company-first-principles/combined-investment-research-pilot-02-walmart-q2-management-call-control-point-upgrade-2026-09-15.md)
and its [structured claim CSV](../company-first-principles/data/combined-investment-research-pilot-02-walmart-q2-management-call-control-point-upgrade-2026-09-15.csv).

The [H1 owner-cash denominator control](../company-first-principles/combined-investment-research-pilot-02-walmart-h1-owner-cash-denominator-control-2026-09-15.md)
then places capex categories, debt principal, repurchases, and diluted shares
beside the reported cash-after-property screen. It keeps normalized common-
owner cash held until maintenance capital, leases, taxes, supplier finance,
attached-service costs, and seasonality are bounded.

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Property spending | Company-defined FCF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2024 | `$35.726B` | `$20.606B` | `$15.120B` |
| 2025 | `$36.443B` | `$23.783B` | `$12.660B` |
| 2026 | `$41.565B` | `$26.642B` | `$14.923B` |

At the September 14, 2026 market snapshot, Walmart's equity value was about
`$854.8B`. A simple `$14.923B` current-year FCF denominator implies a multiple
above `57x`; that is not a normal mature-retail valuation. The price therefore
requires substantial growth in normalized common-owner cash, a high multiple
for the attached platform layers, or both. The scenario register uses bear,
base, and bull normalized cash of `$13B`, `$18B`, and `$24B` with equity-cash
multiples of `30x`, `38x`, and `45x`. These are expectations tests, not a
valuation signoff; even the base case must prove that advertising, membership,
marketplace, and omnichannel investment generate enough incremental cash to
support the current price.

## Earnings quality and forensic questions

### Advertising is not automatically incremental

Walmart Connect grew rapidly in the packet's quarterly evidence. The relevant
questions are whether suppliers receive incremental conversion, whether
advertising is replacing unpaid organic placement, whether higher ad load
reduces customer experience, and whether supplier spending remains resilient
when retail demand weakens.

### Membership has a cost

Membership fee income grew `15.1%` in Q4 2025 and `17.4%` in Q1 2026. The
economic bridge is membership fee plus incremental basket and frequency less
delivery benefits, discounts, customer service, and acquisition cost. Without
renewal and contribution data, membership growth is a leading indicator, not a
profit conclusion.

### Marketplace expands selection and risk

Marketplace services can add assortment and fees without Walmart owning all
inventory. They also add seller-quality, returns, counterfeit, fulfillment,
customer-protection, and regulatory exposure. Marketplace gross merchandise
volume must not be treated as Walmart revenue or cash.

### Cash-flow timing

The difference between Q4 operating cash flow of `$41.6B` and free cash flow of
`$14.9B` is large enough that capital spending and the definition of free cash
flow matter. The company-level filing bridge must identify maintenance versus
growth spending, inventory timing, supplier payables, leases, acquisitions,
financial-services balances, and stock compensation. Q1 operating cash flow of
`$4.7B` also shows why one quarter cannot establish a normalized annual cash
denominator.

### Low prices create a permanent burden

Walmart's pricing proposition requires labor, inventory, fulfillment, store
maintenance, technology, and supplier coordination. If these costs are
deferred, near-term adjusted profit and free cash flow can improve while the
customer relationship weakens. This is a capital-allocation and earnings-
quality issue even when the accounting is technically compliant.

## Lyn Alden lens

Walmart serves routine household demand and can gain share when consumers
become more price-sensitive. That demand is more durable than discretionary
spending, but the business is exposed to wages, transportation, utilities,
food and merchandise inflation, supplier health, and household liquidity.

The balance-sheet and cash-flow test is whether Walmart can continue investing
in stores, inventory, fulfillment, and technology during a margin or demand
shock. Advertising and membership may improve liquidity, but they depend on
the retail system remaining useful and trusted.

The burden-transfer question is important. Suppliers fund merchandise and
advertising; marketplace sellers fund inventory; delivery providers fund labor
and vehicles; Walmart funds the customer interface, stores, fulfillment,
pricing, technology, and compliance. The cleaner attached economics do not
erase the costs carried by the core company.

## Damodaran lens and valuation expectations

The narrative should be converted into separate operating drivers:

`stores and digital traffic -> sales, volume, price, and basket -> merchandise
margin -> operating costs -> advertising, membership, marketplace, and
financial-service contribution -> capital spending, working capital, leases,
tax, and dilution -> cash per share`

A reasonable valuation must distinguish:

1. mature retail earnings at a lower margin and higher capital burden;
2. advertising and membership earnings if retention and incremental conversion
   are demonstrated;
3. marketplace and fulfillment earnings after returns and service costs; and
4. financial-service earnings after credit losses, funding, and regulation.

The market's implied expectation is not simply a revenue-growth rate. It is an
expectation that attached services will expand the margin and cash generated
by each customer relationship without requiring disproportionate store,
fulfillment, technology, or working-capital investment. If that expectation
is already reflected in the share price, modest advertising growth will not be
enough; per-share normalized cash must compound.

## Management incentives and thesis breakers

Management has incentives to emphasize adjusted operating income, global
advertising growth, eCommerce growth, membership income, and ecosystem scale.
Those measures should be read with comparable-store sales, retail gross margin,
inventory, labor, delivery cost, capex, diluted shares, and cash per share.

The thesis breaks if:

- advertising growth is accompanied by weaker supplier retention, conversion,
  or customer experience;
- membership grows through discounts while renewal and contribution after
  benefits weaken;
- eCommerce growth requires rising delivery subsidies or fulfillment capital;
- marketplace growth increases returns, disputes, counterfeit exposure, or
  customer-support cost faster than retained fees;
- operating cash benefits from inventory liquidation or supplier-payable timing
  reverse in later quarters;
- maintenance spending is deferred to protect near-term free cash flow; or
- attached businesses grow but common-owner cash per share does not grow after
  stock compensation, debt, leases, and capital spending.

## Next-filing evidence ladder

| Question | Confirming evidence | Falsifying evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is the retail base healthy? | Volume, traffic, availability, gross margin, and customer retention remain stable | Price or traffic growth masks volume loss, margin pressure, or lower availability |
| Is advertising incremental? | Supplier retention and conversion improve while ad revenue grows | Suppliers pay more for visibility without incremental sales or customer experience worsens |
| Does membership create value? | Renewal, frequency, basket, and contribution after benefits improve | Membership rises but delivery, discount, or support cost consumes the fee and incremental margin |
| Does marketplace add owner cash? | Retained fees grow after returns, disputes, support, and fulfillment | Volume rises while seller quality and service costs deteriorate |
| Is cash durable? | OCF and FCF remain strong after normalized capex, working capital, leases, and SBC | Cash depends on inventory/payables timing or deferred maintenance |

## Bottom line

Walmart controls one of the largest repeated household-shopping relationships
and is attaching multiple monetization layers to it. The evidence supports
real operating momentum in advertising, eCommerce, and membership. The deeper
investment conclusion remains conditional: the higher-margin layers create
value only if they improve customer economics without weakening the low-price
retail base or hiding the capital needed to maintain it.
