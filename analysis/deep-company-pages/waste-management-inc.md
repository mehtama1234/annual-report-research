## Bottom line

Waste Management operates a national collection and disposal network, recycling
facilities, renewable-natural-gas projects, and healthcare-waste services. Its
valuable relationship is the recurring local service route combined with access
to permitted disposal and processing capacity. The customer pays for reliable
removal, compliance, predictable service, and a provider that can handle the
material after collection.

The company has a stronger control point than a simple hauling contractor, but
the control point requires large continuing capital. Trucks, transfer stations,
landfills, recycling equipment, sustainability projects, healthcare facilities,
labor, permits, closure obligations, and debt all remain claims on the cash
generated by the network.

FY2025 operating cash flow was `$6.043B`, capital expenditures were `$3.227B`,
acquisitions used `$395M`, and company-defined free cash flow was `$2.937B`.
The strict screen after capital expenditures and acquisitions was approximately
`$2.421B` before other owner claims. This is materially stronger than a small
regional operator's residual, but the apparent cash must be read alongside
WM's sustainability investment cycle, Stericycle integration, landfill
obligations, and recurring fleet and facility replacement.

Current judgment: WM has durable local density and disposal access, with a
credible path for pricing and productivity to compound cash. The valuation
requires proof that sustainability projects and Healthcare Solutions generate
cash after their capital and integration requirements, and that price-led
growth does not conceal weak underlying volume or customer retention.

## What WM controls

`customer route -> collection fleet -> transfer or direct haul -> landfill, recycling, RNG, or healthcare treatment -> compliance record -> renewal`

WM controls route density, collection assets, transfer and disposal access,
processing capacity, local operating systems, and the customer relationship.
It does not control waste volume, fuel, wages, weather, recycling commodity
prices, permitting, environmental rules, or the timing of customer construction
and industrial activity.

The company reports its business through Collection and Disposal, Recycling
Processing and Sales, Renewable Energy, and Healthcare Solutions. Those layers
should not be valued as if they have identical economics:

- Collection and Disposal depends on route density, price, yield, volume,
  labor, fleet productivity, and landfill access.
- Recycling depends on throughput, contamination, processing capacity,
  commodity prices, and contracted end markets.
- Renewable Energy depends on landfill-gas availability, plant completion,
  renewable-fuel economics, interconnection, and regulatory credits.
- Healthcare Solutions depends on regulated waste flows, treatment capacity,
  compliance, service density, customer retention, and the success of the
  Stericycle integration.

## Earnings quality and forensic tests

### 1. Price-led growth can hide low volume

WM entered 2026 expecting Collection and Disposal core price of `5.4%` to
`5.8%`, yield of `3.2%` to `3.6%`, and volume of only `0.2%` to `0.6%`. This is
not automatically weak. Route businesses can improve economics through price,
route redesign, fleet productivity, and service mix. It does mean that the
quality of growth must be tested through customer retention, realized price,
volume, bad debt, and route-level costs.

If price rises while volume remains weak and customers leave, reported revenue
can look healthy for a period while the future route base becomes less dense.
The next filings should show whether price realization is translating into
stable customer relationships and cash per route.

### 2. Stericycle integration is a recurring investment question

Healthcare Solutions is not a normal small acquisition. WM acquired Stericycle
and then integrated its operations into WM's field-management and operating
structure. Management reported improving segment SG&A and expects cross-selling
and operating-cost benefits.

The correct test is not whether adjusted EBITDA rises after the acquisition. It
is whether Healthcare Solutions produces incremental operating cash after
integration labor, fleet and facility investment, compliance spending, debt
interest, customer retention work, and any restructuring or remediation. Costs
called integration or synergies should remain in the model while the work is
still required to obtain the claimed earnings.

### 3. Sustainability spending changes the cash bridge

WM completed seven renewable-natural-gas facilities and nine recycling
automation or growth projects during 2025. It expected additional RNG and
recycling projects during 2026. These projects may earn attractive returns,
but they are growth investments, not free maintenance capacity.

The analyst should track each cohort from capital invested to operating output:
project completion, throughput, realized pricing, renewable credits or other
policy support, operating cost, maintenance capital, and cash returned. A lower
capital-spending year can improve free cash flow while the company is still
harvesting prior projects; that does not prove that future projects will have
the same return.

### 4. Landfill accounting is not the same as cash completion

Landfill assets are depreciated and depleted over estimated useful lives and
remaining capacity. Closure, post-closure, remediation, and environmental
obligations depend on site conditions, regulation, timing, and construction
costs. Financial assurance, surety, letters of credit, and restricted assets
can also consume liquidity without appearing as ordinary capital expenditure.

The next review should reconcile landfill development, closure and post-closure
payments, reserve changes, remaining permitted capacity, internalization of
waste, and cash tied to financial assurance. Reported EBITDA excludes much of
this burden because depreciation and depletion are non-cash in the period, but
the assets still require replacement and the obligations still require funding.

### 5. Adjusted measures need a stable bridge

WM reports adjusted operating income, adjusted operating EBITDA, and free cash
flow. These measures are useful when reconciled to GAAP results. They become
misleading if recurring integration, project, environmental, or restructuring
costs are repeatedly excluded.

For every adjustment, ask whether the cost is actually finished, whether it is
required by the operating strategy, whether it recurs across acquisitions or
projects, and whether cash was paid. The relevant shareholder measure is cash
after the spending required to preserve the network and obtain the stated
growth.

## Balance sheet and liquidity

WM's liquidity is supported by recurring collection receipts and disposal
access, but it also carries debt associated with the Stericycle acquisition,
landfill and facility obligations, leases, environmental commitments, and
capital requirements. The current balance sheet should be read with interest
expense, debt maturities, refinancing conditions, acquisition commitments,
surety capacity, and the cash required for closure and post-closure work.

The key resilience question is not whether WM can fund one year of capital
expenditure. It is whether it can continue fleet replacement, landfill and
sustainability investment, healthcare compliance, dividends, repurchases, and
selective acquisitions through a period of weaker volume and higher financing
costs without relying on asset sales or repeated debt expansion.

## Alden lens: liquidity and durability

WM's durability comes from essential collection, local route density, permitted
disposal capacity, and the difficulty of replacing a complete service network.
The macro risks are commercial and construction volume, municipal finances,
fuel, wages, interest rates, weather, environmental regulation, and recycling
commodity prices.

The balance-sheet risk is more important than the label “defensive” suggests.
The business owns and operates trucks, transfer stations, landfills, recycling
plants, RNG facilities, and healthcare assets. Those assets support service
continuity, but they also require cash and create environmental and compliance
claims. Liquidity must be assessed after capital spending and acquisition
requirements, not from EBITDA alone.

## Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative is: “WM can turn national route density, disposal access,
pricing, automation, sustainability projects, and Healthcare Solutions into
durable common cash.” The model must specify:

1. Collection and Disposal price, yield, volume, retention, and route cost;
2. landfill utilization, permitted capacity, internalization, and development;
3. fleet, transfer-station, recycling, RNG, and healthcare capital needs;
4. sustainability-project investment, output, policy support, and cash return;
5. Stericycle revenue, margin, integration cost, cross-selling, and retention;
6. recycling throughput, contamination, commodity prices, and end markets;
7. debt, interest, leases, environmental reserves, surety, and liquidity; and
8. diluted shares, repurchases, stock compensation, and cash per share.

WM is a mature route-and-disposal platform with a recent large acquisition and
an active sustainability investment program. A premium valuation is justified
only if the mature base remains stable while new projects and Healthcare
Solutions add cash after their full capital and integration requirements.

## Valuation screen

The September 11, 2026 market snapshot showed equity value of approximately
`$85.7B`. The screen uses operating cash after capital expenditures and
acquisitions as a starting point, then varies normalized common cash to reflect
maintenance-capital uncertainty, integration, sustainability returns, and
future acquisition intensity.

| Case | Normalized common owner cash | Equity cash multiple | Implied common equity value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | `$2.4B` | `25x` | `$60B` |
| Base | `$3.5B` | `30x` | `$105B` |
| Bull | `$4.6B` | `35x` | `$161B` |

The current market value is above the bear case and below the base case in
this simple screen. That does not make the shares attractive or unattractive
by itself. It says the market requires the mature route base, price realization,
sustainability projects, and Healthcare Solutions to produce cash closer to the
base case while capital and integration burdens remain controlled.

## Thesis breakers and next filings

The thesis weakens if:

- price increases rise while route volume, retention, or cash per route weaken;
- free cash flow improves mainly because sustainability capital falls, without
  evidence that completed projects are producing the promised cash;
- Healthcare Solutions margin improvement depends on repeated exclusions,
  declining service quality, or unproven cross-selling;
- landfill closure, remediation, surety, or environmental obligations rise
  faster than disclosed cash reserves and operating cash;
- debt, interest, and lease claims rise faster than normalized common cash;
- recycling commodity prices, contamination, or renewable-credit support reduce
  project returns; or
- acquisitions and repurchases consume cash while diluted per-share cash fails
  to improve.

The next filings should provide:

1. Collection and Disposal core price, yield, volume, retention, and cost;
2. cash flow after business-support and sustainability capital;
3. project-level output and returns for RNG and recycling investments;
4. Healthcare Solutions revenue, margin, integration cost, and cash conversion;
5. landfill capacity, closure spending, environmental reserves, and surety;
6. debt, interest, lease, share-count, and acquisition changes; and
7. cash per diluted share after all recurring claims.

## Evidence grade

- **High confidence:** FY2025 revenue, operating cash flow, capital spending,
  acquisitions, free cash flow, reported segment structure, Q4 2025 through Q2
  2026 results, and 2026 guidance from filed or company-issued materials.
- **Medium confidence:** normalized owner-cash cases and the allocation of
  future maintenance versus growth capital. These require additional project,
  reserve, and cohort-level disclosures.
- **Open:** long-run return on sustainability projects, full Stericycle cash
  returns, route retention under continued price increases, and the effect of
  future acquisitions on diluted per-share value.
