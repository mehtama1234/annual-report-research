# Westlake — PVC, chlor-alkali, building products, and cost-curve repair

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed U.S. GAAP figures in U.S. dollars;
dollar amounts are in millions unless otherwise stated.

## How to read this memo

PVC is a plastic material used in pipe, fittings, siding, and other products.
Chlor-alkali plants produce chlorine and caustic soda together, so the economics
of one product affect the other. HIP is Housing and Infrastructure Products,
Westlake's downstream pipe, fittings, siding, and related products. PEM is
Performance and Essential Materials, the more commodity-exposed chlorovinyl,
polyethylene, epoxy, and caustic-soda business. A curtailment is a deliberate
reduction in production when selling prices and input costs do not support
economic operation.

## Investment question

Westlake combines downstream building and infrastructure products with a global
chlorovinyl and performance-materials network. Its possible advantage is
integration, North American feedstock, product distribution, and exposure to
water and infrastructure replacement. The central question is whether HIP can
provide a stable base while PEM recovers, after plant closures, litigation,
feedstock and energy costs, capital spending, debt, and the $600M PEM improvement
plan.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- HIP and PEM are different businesses. HIP depends on housing, repair,
  infrastructure, pipe, fittings, siding, and downstream product margins. PEM
  depends on PVC, polyethylene, chlor-alkali, feedstock, energy, exports, and
  global utilization.
- FY2025 HIP generated approximately $603M of operating income while PEM lost
  approximately $724M. The consolidated company therefore hides a strong
  downstream contribution beneath a stressed commodity-materials segment.
- Q4 2025 included approximately $393M of charges for shutting three North
  American chlorovinyl plants and one styrene plant, plus $102M connected with
  the Pernis shutdown. Those charges are not just accounting noise; they show
  that the asset footprint had become uneconomic at prior conditions.
- Q2 2026 showed a sharp rebound in PEM, but higher PVC and polyethylene prices,
  lower North American gas costs, and logistical disruption all contributed.
  The quarter is evidence of operating leverage, not proof of a permanent
  margin reset.
- Management reduced debt by $500M and refinanced its revolving facility. Lower
  debt improves resilience, but it does not remove the need to fund plants,
  environmental work, and product-system reliability.
- The $600M PEM profitability-improvement plan must be split into price,
  utilization, procurement, closures, fixed-cost reduction, and working-capital
  effects. The source of the improvement determines its durability.
- HIP's ACI acquisition may add pipe and fittings volume, but its return should
  be measured after purchase price, integration, inventory, and capital needs.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Does PEM recovery become cash?

Reconcile PEM pricing, volume, feedstock, energy, co-products, utilization,
freight, and fixed costs to segment operating cash. Separate market-wide PVC
price moves from management-created margin.

### 2. Can HIP absorb housing weakness?

Track housing starts, repair and replacement, infrastructure projects, pipe and
fittings volume, price, gross margin, inventories, receivables, and the ACI
acquisition. A downstream label does not prove that the business is independent
of construction cycles.

### 3. Are shutdown savings real?

For each closed site, record closure cash, environmental and remediation cost,
lost volume, stranded overhead, freight, customer qualification, and avoided
capital. Compare actual savings with the announced PEM plan.

### 4. Are legal and environmental costs recurring?

Track the $67M Q1 2026 litigation settlement, shutdown obligations, remediation,
asset-retirement claims, and environmental capital. A charge can be non-recurring
in form while the underlying operating exposure remains.

### 5. Is the balance sheet resilient through a weak cycle?

Map debt maturities, interest, cash, revolver capacity, inventory, receivables,
and dividends against mid-cycle cash after sustaining capital. Refinancing buys
time; it does not improve returns unless the asset base earns more.

### 6. Does the ACI acquisition improve owner cash?

Compare purchase price, acquired sales, margin, working capital, integration
cost, capital spending, and incremental cash return. Do not value added volume
without the capital required to serve it.

## Incentives, lifecycle, and cohort position

The $600M PEM profitability-improvement plan creates a useful test of
management incentives. Price recovery and lower feedstock cost can improve
results without a lasting change in the business. Permanent improvements must
show up as lower unit cost, better utilization, lower fixed cost, or a smaller
capital requirement, and they must survive after closure cash, environmental
work, stranded overhead, and customer-service costs are included.

The same discipline applies to the ACI acquisition and to debt reduction. A
lower debt balance improves liquidity, but it does not prove that the acquired
or retained plants earn an adequate return. The per-share owner test is cash
after sustaining capital, environmental and litigation payments, interest,
working capital, and any dilution—not EBITDA after a favorable spread period.

The cohort repeats one important distinction: integrated plants can lower input
cost and improve reliability, but integration does not remove the need to
renew capacity, close uneconomic assets, fund environmental obligations, and
survive a weak pricing cycle.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** Westlake connects energy, housing, water infrastructure,
  trade, and industrial supply. Integration and domestic feedstock improve
  resilience, but debt, plant closures, environmental requirements, and
  construction affordability remain material.
- **Damodaran lens:** value HIP and PEM separately using mid-cycle housing and
  infrastructure demand, PVC/chlor-alkali spreads, feedstock, utilization,
  sustaining capital, and the probability-weighted result of closures and ACI.
- **Forensic lens:** the primary risks are treating EBITDA recovery as owner
  cash, treating shutdown charges as fully one-time, assuming HIP removes
  commodity exposure, and counting debt refinancing as economic improvement.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately $9.10B of equity value at
$70.81 per share. Against the FY2025 negative $530M post-capex screen, a current
cash multiple is not meaningful. A normalized range of $500M / $1.0B / $1.5B at
10x / 12x / 15x implies $5B / $12B / $22.5B. The current value requires a
substantial PEM recovery, HIP resilience, and proof that shutdown savings and
capital discipline produce cash after the physical asset burden.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. PVC, polyethylene, or chlor-alkali spreads remain weak after the temporary
   2026 rebound.
2. PEM improvement depends on disruption, price timing, or lower gas cost that
   does not persist.
3. Shutdowns create stranded, environmental, litigation, or customer-service
   costs greater than the savings.
4. HIP weakens with housing affordability or ACI integration problems.
5. Capital spending remains high while operating cash stays below renewal needs.
6. Debt, dividends, and environmental claims absorb the recovery cash.
