# Wheaton Precious Metals Corporation — streaming contracts, portfolio growth, and leverage

As of 2026-09-13. FY2025 figures are filed IFRS figures in U.S. dollars;
dollar amounts are in millions.

## How to read this memo

A stream is a contract that gives Wheaton the right to buy a specified amount
of future metal from an operator at a fixed or formula-based price. A royalty
is a right to receive part of production or revenue without operating the mine.
GEO means gold equivalent ounces, a conversion used to compare different
metals. Owner cash here means operating cash flow after all material investing
cash outflows. It is a screen, not a complete valuation measure.

## Investment question

Wheaton receives metal from partner mines while the operators fund mine labor,
energy, equipment, processing, and sustaining capital. This can produce cleaner
cash than direct mine ownership. The question is whether that advantage survives
large upfront stream payments, operator dependence, high gold and silver prices,
and the debt taken on for the Antamina transaction.

## Antamina: the strongest named cash-return test

Antamina is the clearest example of how a streaming acquisition should be
underwritten. Wheaton paid $4.300B for the BHP precious-metals stream. The
available Q2 2026 filing evidence shows the following first-half results for
the Antamina stream:

| Measure | H1 2026 | What it proves | What it does not prove |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Revenue | $277.563M | The acquired stream produced reported revenue. | It does not separately show delivered ounces, invoices, or collections. |
| Cost excluding depletion | $55.340M | Cash-cost information is visible. | It does not include tax or financing allocated to Antamina. |
| Depletion | $51.322M | The accounting cost of consuming the stream asset is visible. | It does not establish reserve life or future delivery. |
| Profit | $170.901M | A pre-tax, pre-interest accounting profit proxy is visible. | It is not an after-tax financed return. |
| Operating cash flow | $222.223M | A stream-level cash-margin proxy is visible. | It is not debt-service cash or free cash after future stream purchases. |
| Asset carrying amount | $4.708B | A balance-sheet denominator is visible. | It is not fair value, NPV, or the acquisition price. |

The cash margin before depletion was approximately $222.223M, or 80.1% of
revenue. Dividing first-half stream operating cash flow by the $4.300B payment
gives a 5.17% half-year screen, or 10.34% if mechanically annualized. This is
not an IRR, NPV, payback period, or after-tax return. It is a bounded test that
the named asset is producing cash after its stated purchase payment.

The contract evidence adds important limits: the BHP interest raised
Wheaton's attributable payable production share from 33.75% to 67.5% effective
April 1, 2026; the payable factor is 90%; and the ongoing payment is 20% of the
spot price or price received. Wheaton reported 2.3M attributable Antamina
silver ounces in Q2 2026 and said the increase was primarily driven by the new
BHP stream. Production is not the same as delivered ounces, invoiced sales, or
collected cash. Public deal materials provide reserve-life context through
2036, but not a source-backed delivery curve sufficient for IRR or NPV.

The financing evidence shows the balance-sheet change: Wheaton moved from
year-end 2025 debt-free status to approximately $2.0B of debt after funding
Antamina. Company-level term-loan, revolver, tax, and finance-cost data are
visible; Antamina-specific tax allocation, interest allocation, lender
waterfall, and repayment schedule are not. The correct conclusion is therefore
that Antamina is the strongest named cash-return proxy in this archive, not a
proven full-return investment.

## What the annual and quarterly record reveals

- Wheaton sold 689.9K GEOs in 2025 across 23 operating-mine agreements and 25
  development and other projects. Diversification helps, but output remains
  dependent on partner operators.
- About 85% of attributable production came from assets in the lowest half of
  their cost curves. This supports resilience at lower metal prices, but it is
  based on operator data and changes as mines mature.
- The model avoids much direct site-level inflation. Its main capital burden is
  the upfront price paid for new streams and the ongoing purchase payments.
- Wheaton entered 2026 debt-free, then used cash, a term loan, and its revolver
  to fund the $4.3B Antamina transaction. By Q2 2026 cash was about $100M and
  debt was about $2.0B. A capital-light business can become financially
  sensitive when it uses leverage for a large growth transaction.
- Q1 and Q2 2026 produced record revenue and operating cash flow, but that
  growth reflects higher metal prices and Antamina contribution. It does not
  yet prove a full-cycle return on the acquisition.

## Forensic earnings-quality questions

### 1. Is the stream cash recurring?

Reconcile profit to operating cash flow and then to total investing cash flow.
Separate metal-price changes, operator timing, taxes, receivables, stream
purchase payments, and asset sales. A high operating-cash number is not
available to owners if it must be reinvested to preserve future GEOs.

### 2. Do contract terms protect returns or transfer counterparty risk?

Read the stream purchase price, percentage of production, royalty rate, metal
types, delivery priority, inflation clauses, operator reporting, audit rights,
expansion rights, and termination provisions. These contracts reduce direct
operating-cost risk while leaving Wheaton dependent on operators producing and
paying.

### 3. Are acquisitions earning their cost?

For each deal, compare cash paid with actual GEOs, start date, metal mix,
operator capital, reserve life, payment obligations, impairments, and cash
returns. Test whether Antamina, Hemlo, Spring Valley, and other additions earn
more than the cost of capital after debt service.

### 4. How much diversification is real?

Rebuild GEOs and cash by gold, silver, copper-linked, cobalt, palladium, and
other interests. Test whether diversification reduces volatility or simply adds
more operator, permitting, and commodity exposures.

### 5. Does leverage remain safe after Antamina?

Test debt maturities, interest, liquidity, dividend commitments, operator
delays, lower metal prices, and the timing of Antamina cash flow. The key test
is whether the portfolio can reduce debt without buying new assets at poor
prices or weakening existing stream commitments.

## Lyn Alden / Damodaran / forensic synthesis

- **Lyn Alden lens:** the royalty model reduces exposure to operator labor,
  energy, and equipment inflation. It remains exposed to metal prices, credit
  conditions, mine supply, permitting, and attractive-deal availability.
- **Damodaran lens:** value producing streams, development-stage assets,
  optionality, and undeployed capital separately. Model acquisition
  reinvestment explicitly instead of treating low physical capex as zero
  reinvestment.
- **Forensic lens:** the main category errors are treating GEO growth as
  organic growth, operating cash as owner cash, operator reserves as guaranteed
  output, available capital as earned value, and Antamina growth as a proven
  return before a full operating record exists.

## Valuation framing

The 2026-09-11 market snapshot was approximately US$69.2B of equity value at
US$108.00 per share. Against the FY2025 conservative residual of $626M, the
market value is about 111x. A normalized range of $1.2B / $2.0B / $3.0B at 25x
/ 30x / 35x implies $30B / $60B / $105B. The market therefore requires a much
higher cash base than the post-investment 2025 screen, supported by high metal
prices, Antamina growth, and disciplined future dealmaking.

## Thesis breakers and next checks

1. Gold and silver prices fall toward mid-cycle levels and cash declines faster
   than the valuation assumes.
2. Antamina or other major operators deliver less metal or later than expected.
3. New acquisitions produce fewer GEOs, start later, or require larger payments.
4. Debt service and dividends consume cash needed to reduce leverage.
5. Development-stage optionality remains unavailable while the market assigns it
   full value.
6. Write-downs show that acquisition prices exceeded the economic value of the
   acquired production.

Next filing checks: GEOs by asset and metal; operator production and payment
status; stream purchase payments; acquisition-cohort returns; Antamina cash
flow; debt, interest, and liquidity; development-stage assets; impairments; and
dividends after portfolio investment.
