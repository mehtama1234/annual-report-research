# Zscaler: Access Policy, Deferred Revenue, and the Cost of Trust

Date baseline: `2026-09-14`

Evidence base: fiscal `2025` Form 10-K, fiscal `2026` Q1 through Q3 filed
materials, company releases, shareholder letters, and earnings-call materials
preserved in the Zscaler packet. The dated scenario register preserves a
September 14, 2026 equity-value input; it is an expectations screen, not a
live quote or price target.

## Bottom line

Zscaler provides cloud-delivered access, identity, data-security, and policy
enforcement. Its service decides which user, device, workload, or AI agent can
reach an application or dataset under defined conditions.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was `$2.6731B`, gross profit was `$2.0549B`, operating cash
flow was `$972.5M`, and capital spending was `$164.3M`. At fiscal year-end,
cash and cash equivalents were `$2.389B`, short-term investments were `$1.183B`,
and deferred revenue was `$2.468B`. Fiscal 2026 Q3 revenue was about `$850.5M`,
ARR was `$3.525B`, RPO was about `$6.459B`, operating cash flow was about
`$198M`, and free cash flow was about `$136M`.

The control point is unusually clear: Zscaler sits in the policy path between
users, machines, applications, data, and increasingly AI agents. The forensic
question is whether the company can keep expanding that policy role while
turning customer prepayments into durable owner cash after cloud processing,
research, sales, acquisitions, and SBC.

## 1. What Zscaler controls

Zscaler controls access and policy decisions rather than the application or
data itself. Its Zero Trust model requires a user or machine to be evaluated
before access is granted, instead of assuming that a connection is safe merely
because it comes from an internal network.

The customer dependency can include:

- identity and access policies;
- traffic inspection and logging;
- data-loss and browser controls;
- public-sector and sovereignty requirements;
- security operations and incident evidence; and
- procedures for unmanaged devices and non-human identities.

Replacing the platform can require rebuilding policy, connecting identity and
application systems, moving logs, retraining staff, and proving that access
controls still work. That creates switching cost, but a major outage, poor
performance, weak detection, or a cheaper integrated platform can still weaken
renewal.

### Three-year owner-cash diagnostic

The filed annual cash-flow statements support a residual after property and
equipment, capitalized internal-use software, and cash acquisitions:

| Fiscal year | Operating cash flow | Property/equipment and capitalized software | Cash acquisitions | Diagnostic residual |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2023 | `$462M` | `$129M` | `$16M` | `$318M` |
| 2024 | `$780M` | `$195M` | `$375M` | `$210M` |
| 2025 | `$972M` | `$246M` | `$1M` | `$726M` |

The 2024 decline in this diagnostic was driven mainly by acquisition cash and
the 2025 recovery benefited from much lower acquisition spending. It should not
be read as a permanent margin change. Deferred revenue and stock compensation
also remain important: customer prepayment supports liquidity but creates
future service obligations, while SBC reduces cash value per diluted share.

ARR is the annualized value of active recurring contracts at a point in time.
Deferred revenue is the amount billed or collected before all promised service
has been delivered. RPO is the contracted value of future performance
obligations. These measures support visibility but do not equal current-period
profit.

The cash sequence also matters. Q1 operating cash flow and free cash flow were
far above Q2 and Q3. That does not prove manipulation; it shows that contract
billing, collections, annual renewal timing, and working capital can materially
change quarterly cash. The annual bridge is more reliable than one quarter.

## 3. Earnings quality and capital burden

### Deferred revenue is both strength and obligation

Deferred revenue shows that customers pre-fund service. This is valuable because
it supports liquidity and reduces the need for external financing. It is not
free cash in the economic sense: Zscaler must continue providing access control,
inspection, availability, support, and updates during the contract period.

The test is whether deferred revenue converts into revenue, gross profit, and
cash without rising cancellations, concessions, service credits, or processing
cost.

### Cloud and inspection cost

Zscaler inspects and governs traffic at scale. More users, applications, data,
and AI-agent activity can increase the amount of data processed and stored.
That creates a variable cost that a simple subscription multiple may miss.

Gross margin should therefore be followed alongside:

- transaction and data volume;
- cloud and data-center cost;
- storage and logging cost;
- AI-security processing cost; and
- customer support and service-availability cost.

### Acquisition expansion

The packet records the SPLX acquisition, SquareX acquisition, Red Canary
integration, and planned Symmetry Systems acquisition. These expand AI asset
discovery, browser security, managed detection and response, and AI-agent
communication governance.

Acquisitions can strengthen the control point, but they also create purchase
price, integration, product-overlap, and dilution risk. The proof is not the
number of products added. It is whether customers adopt more of the platform,
retain at higher rates, and produce more owner cash after integration cost.

### GAAP loss and adjusted profit

Zscaler reported GAAP operating losses in the quarter sequence while reporting
strong non-GAAP operating income. That gap requires a full reconciliation of
SBC, amortization, acquisition costs, and other exclusions.

Non-GAAP profit can help compare operating decisions, but it cannot replace
GAAP results or owner-cash analysis. The per-share question is whether the cash
generated after SBC and acquisitions benefits each diluted share.

## 4. Alden lens: durable demand and liquidity

Zscaler benefits from durable demand for secure access, remote and distributed
work, data sovereignty, cloud migration, and AI-agent governance. It also has a
relatively light physical burden compared with telecom carriers, data-center
landlords, and appliance-heavy security companies.

The main liquidity risks are:

- customers delaying expansion while retaining only core access services;
- outage or performance problems that create credits and renewal risk;
- data-processing cost rising faster than contract value;
- acquisition cash reducing flexibility; and
- high SBC reducing cash value per diluted share.

The durability test is whether Zscaler remains a required policy layer when
customers consolidate security budgets and demand measurable cost savings.

## 5. Damodaran lens: narrative to numbers

The narrative is that Zero Trust and AI security create a large policy-control
platform. The required numbers are:

1. ARR growth after separating acquired ARR;
2. gross retention and net retention;
3. growth in `$100K+` and `$1M+` customer cohorts;
4. deferred revenue and RPO conversion;
5. gross margin after cloud, storage, and inspection cost;
6. sales efficiency and customer-payback period;
7. acquisition returns and integration cost;
8. SBC and diluted shares; and
9. normalized owner cash per diluted share relative to market value.

The lifecycle question is whether Zscaler remains early in a broad policy
transition or whether the market is moving toward larger security bundles that
compress price and reduce product differentiation.

## 6. Control point versus burden carrier

| Layer | Zscaler captures | Zscaler carries |
|---|---|---|
| Access policy | Embedded rules, identity connections, and renewal friction | Availability, latency, policy errors, and integration work |
| Data security | Governance and compliance value | Storage, inspection, privacy, and processing cost |
| AI security | New demand for non-human identity and agent control | Rapid product development and uncertain usage economics |
| Recurring contracts | Customer prepayment and visibility | Future service obligation and renewal risk |
| Cloud delivery | Lower customer hardware burden | Dependence on cloud and data-center suppliers |
| Acquisitions | Faster product coverage | Purchase price, integration, overlap, and dilution |

Zscaler captures a clean policy layer, but the cost is not zero. It is carried
through cloud processing, service availability, security research, specialized
labor, customer support, acquisitions, and SBC.

## 7. Valuation readiness

The dated scenario register preserves a September 14, 2026 equity-value input
and a normalized owner-cash screen. This is an expectations screen, not a live
quote or a price target. The valuation bridge should be:

`equity value -> normalized owner cash -> required cash yield -> ARR growth, margin, and dilution required`

Bear, base, and bull cases should vary ARR growth, retention, gross margin,
cloud-processing cost, acquisition spending, SBC, diluted shares, and the cash
multiple. A case based only on ARR or deferred revenue would treat future
service obligations as shareholder cash.

### Dated valuation screen

The register records approximately `$26.448B` of equity value against bear,
base, and strong normalized owner-cash cases of `$0.500B`, `$0.800B`, and
`$1.200B`, valued at `30x`, `40x`, and `50x`. The corresponding implied equity
values are `$15.0B`, `$32.0B`, and `$60.0B`.

The recorded value sits between the bear and strong cases. The base case still
requires ARR and RPO to convert into revenue and cash after cloud processing,
support, acquisitions, SBC, and dilution. Refresh the screen with the next
filing and a synchronized market snapshot.

## 8. Next-filing watchlist and thesis breakers

1. Does ARR continue to grow after acquired ARR is separated?
2. Do `$100K+` and `$1M+` customer cohorts expand without rising discounts?
3. Does deferred revenue convert into revenue and cash without service credits?
4. Does gross margin hold as traffic, storage, and AI-security processing rise?
5. Do acquisitions produce cross-sell, retention, and cash returns?
6. Does the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap narrow or remain dependent on recurring SBC?
7. Does diluted share count remain controlled after employee awards and deals?
8. Does owner cash per diluted share rise faster than reported free cash flow?
9. Does Zscaler remain a required policy layer when security budgets consolidate?

The thesis breaks if ARR growth becomes acquisition-dependent, deferred revenue
stops converting cleanly, processing costs compress gross margin, outages harm
renewal, or SBC absorbs the apparent per-share benefit of cash generation.

## Conclusion

Zscaler is one of the clearest examples of a software policy control point.
Customers pay it to govern access, identity, data movement, and increasingly
AI-agent behavior across systems they do not own as a single integrated stack.

The quality of the business depends on more than recurring contracts. It depends
on reliable service, controlled processing cost, disciplined acquisitions, and
cash generation after dilution. The next filings should therefore be read for
renewal, gross margin, deferred-revenue conversion, and owner cash per diluted
share—not ARR alone.
