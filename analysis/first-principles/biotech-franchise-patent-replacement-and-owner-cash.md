# Biotechnology: clinical evidence, franchise concentration, and owner cash

## Business economics: the first principle

A biotechnology company creates value when scientific research becomes an
approved treatment that physicians prescribe, payers cover, manufacturers can
produce, and patients continue to use. The economic life of a medicine is
limited by clinical competition, patents, regulatory rules, reimbursement, and
manufacturing capacity.

The chain is:

`scientific discovery -> clinical evidence -> regulatory approval -> reimbursement -> prescribing -> manufacturing -> profit -> pipeline replacement -> owner cash`

Regeneron is a useful case because it has strong current cash generation but
concentrated product economics. Dupixent is growing through collaboration with
Sanofi. EYLEA is being shifted toward EYLEA HD while biosimilars pressure the
older product. The company must fund the next product before the current
franchises decline.

## Scientific control is not the same as commercial control

The company may control a target, molecule, patent, trial, or manufacturing
process while a partner controls sales, distribution, payer negotiations, or
geographic commercialization. Regeneron records some direct product sales, but
Sanofi records global Dupixent and Kevzara sales and Bayer records EYLEA sales
outside the United States.

In 2025, Sanofi-recorded Dupixent and Kevzara product sales were $18.381B and
Regeneron's share of profits was $5.242B after $930M of development-cost
reimbursement. Bayer collaboration revenue was $1.422B, including $1.283B of
profit share from EYLEA 8 mg and EYLEA outside the United States.

The analyst must identify which party controls price, market access, promotion,
manufacturing, payer negotiation, and clinical-development decisions. A profit
share can be economically attractive while still being less controllable than
direct product revenue.

## Product concentration creates a replacement schedule

Current revenue is not the same as permanent revenue. A medicine can lose
economic value through:

- patent or regulatory exclusivity expiration;
- biosimilar or therapeutic competition;
- lower net price and payer restrictions;
- safety findings or label restrictions;
- a better treatment or delivery method;
- manufacturing constraints; or
- a shift in physician or patient preference.

Regeneron says it is substantially dependent on EYLEA HD, EYLEA, and Dupixent.
U.S. EYLEA HD and EYLEA sales represented 31% of total 2025 revenue, down from
42% in 2024. U.S. EYLEA sales declined 42% in 2025, while EYLEA HD had to
replace part of the legacy franchise. This is a clear example of lifecycle
management: a new version can protect the franchise, but it must earn the
cost of development, approval, manufacturing, and commercial transition.

## R&D is not an optional add-back

Regeneron's R&D expense rose from $4.439B in 2023 to $5.850B in 2025. It
includes trials, scientific staff, facilities, clinical manufacturing, and
payments that the company must reimburse to collaborators. It also included a
$155M priority-review-voucher charge in 2025.

R&D can be divided into:

1. Maintaining current labels and manufacturing capability.
2. Expanding a current product into new indications.
3. Advancing late-stage programs toward approval.
4. Funding early research with uncertain commercial value.

Each category has a different probability and timing of return. A pipeline
list is not a valuation. The return must be shown through approvals, launch
sales, net price, gross profit, remaining exclusivity, and cash after failed
programs.

## Cash bridge

Regeneron's FY2025 operating cash flow was $4.979B. Capital expenditures were
$898M, intangible-asset payments were $315M, and acquisitions were $3M. A
simple residual was approximately $3.763B. This is strong current cash, but it
does not mean R&D can be removed from the economic burden; R&D is already an
operating expense and is the spending that preserves future product value.

Stock-based compensation was $994M. Repurchases were approximately $3.456B and
the company initiated a dividend. Cash per common share should therefore be
calculated after treating equity compensation as an owner cost and after
checking whether repurchases reduce the capital available for pipeline and
manufacturing.

## Who carries the burden?

| Participant | Value controlled | Burden carried |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Biotech company | Discovery, development, IP, evidence, manufacturing | Trial failure, R&D, regulatory, manufacturing, litigation, dilution |
| Commercial partner | Sales, distribution, payer access, geographic reach | Sales execution, market access, partner obligations |
| Payer / government | Coverage and reimbursement | Budget, policy, utilization, price pressure |
| Physician | Prescribing and clinical adoption | Evidence, patient outcomes, workflow, liability |
| Patient | Treatment demand and adherence | Affordability, access, safety, outcomes |
| Shareholder | Development and commercial capital | Franchise decline, pipeline failure, buyback timing, dilution |

The company with the strongest scientific asset does not automatically capture
the cleanest economics. Profit sharing, royalty arrangements, reimbursement of
development expenses, manufacturing obligations, and partner control determine
how much value reaches common shareholders.

## Three analytical lenses

### Durability and liquidity

Cash and modest debt let Regeneron continue trials and manufacturing through a
weak product period. They do not prevent clinical failure, reimbursement
pressure, or competition. Durability depends on a funded pipeline and enough
time to replace current cash before exclusivity or demand declines.

### Narrative translated into numbers

“Franchise depth” becomes sales, units, net price, indications, market share,
and profit share. “Pipeline strength” becomes probability-adjusted cash flow,
R&D spending, approval timing, and launch economics. “Lifecycle management”
becomes EYLEA versus EYLEA HD volume, price, gross profit, and remaining life.
“Collaboration leverage” becomes partner sales, reimbursement, retained profit,
and control rights.

### Forensic earnings quality

Separate direct product revenue from collaboration revenue, operating cash from
investment gains, current R&D from acquired IPR&D, and non-cash amortization
from the cash acquisition that created the asset. Review impairment, litigation,
inventory, receivables, partner settlement, SBC, dilution, and buybacks.

## Cohort comparison

| Model | Control point | Main risk | Cash question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concentrated biologic franchise | Clinical evidence, IP, label, physician adoption | Product and partner concentration | Can the current franchise fund replacement? |
| Diversified medicines and MedTech | Portfolio, pipeline, procedures, clinical trust | Multiple lifecycles, litigation, acquisition | Does breadth reduce risk without hiding capital burden? |
| Crop-protection chemistry | Product registrations and farmer channel | Patent loss, input cycle, inventory, regulation | Does new chemistry replace mature products after working capital? |
| Medical-device platform | Procedure workflow, physician adoption, hospital approval | Quality, R&D, hospital budget, acquisitions | Does recurring procedure use cover platform renewal? |

Regeneron belongs in the concentrated-biologic category. Its current cash is
cleaner than an acquisition-heavy device company, but its revenue has fewer
independent sources. A high-quality current franchise can therefore have a
lower durability margin than a less profitable but more diversified portfolio.

## Valuation discipline

Value current products by remaining life, price, volume, competition, and
probability of replacement. Value pipeline programs by probability-adjusted
cash flow and the investment still required. Value collaborations by retained
profit and control rights, not partner product sales alone.

Regeneron's FY2025 residual of $3.763B supports a strong current cash case. A
$3.0B / $4.0B / $5.0B normalized cash range at 16x / 20x / 24x requires
different assumptions about Dupixent durability, EYLEA HD replacement, and
pipeline output. The market price is justified only if the current franchises
remain strong while R&D produces the next durable cash engine.

## Filing-based falsifiers

- Dupixent growth slows because of competition, reimbursement, or safety.
- EYLEA HD fails to offset legacy EYLEA and biosimilar erosion.
- R&D spending rises without approvals or commercially meaningful launches.
- Collaboration profit share declines faster than partner product sales grow.
- Manufacturing, inventory, or receivables absorb more cash as products scale.
- IPR&D and milestone payments recur without a clear return path.
- SBC and buybacks reduce cash per share while management reports higher EPS.
