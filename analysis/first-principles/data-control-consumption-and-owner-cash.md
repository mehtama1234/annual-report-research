# Data control, consumption, and owner cash

Consumption-based software has a different cash-risk profile from both seat-based subscriptions and physical cloud infrastructure. The customer can expand usage rapidly, but can also optimize usage without leaving the platform. That makes the revenue model responsive to actual activity and more exposed to customer behavior.

**Consumption revenue** is based on the amount of storage, processing, transfer,
or other usage a customer buys. **Net revenue retention** measures how much the
same customer group spends later compared with an earlier period, after expansion,
contraction, and cancellations. **Owner cash** is cash left for common
shareholders after cloud capacity, people, product development, acquisitions,
and dilution are funded.

## What is being sold

Snowflake sells access to a governed data platform. The customer pays for the resources used to store, process, transfer, and apply data. The control point is the permissioned data context that applications, analysts, and AI systems use.

The economic asset is not only the database. It is the combination of data location, permissions, integrations, developer tools, governance, performance, and organizational knowledge. The asset is durable only if moving the data and controls elsewhere costs more than continuing to pay Snowflake.

## The consumption bridge

The relevant sequence is:

`customer data growth -> workload usage -> product revenue -> cloud and GPU cost -> gross profit -> R&D and sales reinvestment -> dilution-adjusted owner cash`

Each link can change independently. Data growth can rise while query efficiency lowers usage. Revenue can rise while cloud cost rises faster. Operating cash can rise because customers prepay. Free cash flow can rise while stock compensation transfers substantial value to employees.

## Comparison with other control layers

ServiceNow sells the governed movement of work. Snowflake sells governed data context. Microsoft sells a broad set of workflow, identity, developer, and cloud services. Oracle combines database and application control with a much heavier infrastructure and financing burden.

The cleaner economics are not determined by the highest growth rate. They depend on who owns the relationship, who supplies the scarce input, and who pays the recurring cost of delivery. Snowflake leaves most physical infrastructure with hyperscalers, which reduces balance-sheet burden but gives cloud providers a claim on product gross profit.

## Who controls the relationship and who carries the burden?

| Participant | Value captured | Burden carried |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data-platform vendor | Governance, permissions, integrations, usage, and customer workflow | Product development, support, security, cloud cost, and customer retention |
| Public-cloud provider | Compute, storage, networking, and availability | Data-center, power, server, financing, and utilization risk |
| Enterprise customer | Governed data access and application or AI output | Usage cost, migration work, security, and vendor dependence |
| Application or model provider | Analytics, software, or AI value built on data | Performance, model cost, integration, and data quality |
| Common shareholder | Residual software economics | Cloud-cost inflation, customer optimization, SBC, and product-transition risk |

Snowflake captures the governance and usage relationship but pays a variable
charge to the cloud provider. Oracle captures more of the software and physical
capacity relationship, but it also carries more servers, depreciation, power,
leases, debt, and financing risk. The control point and the burden carrier are
not necessarily the same company.

## What the investor must not confuse

- Net revenue retention is not permanent demand; it measures a past customer cohort over a defined period.
- RPO is not current revenue; it is future contracted performance.
- Prepaid capacity is not completed profit; it creates a service obligation.
- Free cash flow is not cash per common share when stock compensation is large.
- A public-cloud model is not costless infrastructure; it replaces owned capex with usage-based cloud expense.
- AI product adoption is not AI contribution margin; inference, support, and data-transfer cost must be measured.

## The practical test

For data-control companies, track product revenue per customer cohort, retention, RPO conversion, product gross margin, cloud and GPU cost, capitalized software, deferred commissions, stock compensation, and diluted shares. Then ask whether each additional dollar of customer usage leaves more or less cash for the common owner.

The strongest conclusion is reached only when customer expansion, margin, cash conversion, and cash per share improve together. If only usage or RPO rises, the evidence remains incomplete.

## Earnings-quality and financial-engineering questions

1. Reconcile product revenue to usage, price, customer cohorts, net retention,
   RPO, deferred revenue, and collections.
2. Separate customer prepayment from completed profit. Prepaid capacity improves
   cash now but creates future service and cloud-cost obligations.
3. Track product gross margin after public-cloud, GPU, inference, bandwidth,
   support, availability, and security expense.
4. Include capitalized internal-use software, deferred commissions, and other
   assets in the reinvestment analysis.
5. Compare stock compensation with repurchases and diluted shares. Positive
   free cash flow is not the same as cash per common share when a large portion
   of compensation is paid in equity.
6. Test whether net retention comes from higher usage, price, acquisitions,
   temporary workload, or customers being locked into unused commitments.
7. Read the proxy for incentives tied to product revenue, net retention, RPO,
   adjusted margin, GAAP profit, cash per share, and return on invested capital.
   Growth targets can encourage discounting or prepayment before durable margins
   are known.

## Lyn Alden lens: usage cycles and infrastructure liquidity

Consumption software avoids much of the physical capital burden of a cloud
provider, but its revenue can slow quickly when customers optimize usage or
delay workloads. The public-cloud supplier may raise prices or face capacity
constraints, compressing the data-platform vendor’s margin even when customer
demand remains strong.

The stress case is enterprise budget pressure, lower usage, slower prepayment,
higher cloud and GPU costs, and continued investment in security and product
development. Liquidity should be measured after stock compensation, capitalized
software, deferred commissions, debt, and acquisitions rather than from operating
cash flow alone.

## Damodaran lens: translate data growth into owner economics

The narrative is that a governed data layer becomes more important as enterprises
use applications and AI. The valuation must specify:

- customer growth, retention, usage, price, and product mix;
- RPO and deferred-revenue conversion;
- gross margin after cloud, GPU, support, and security cost;
- capitalized software and replacement investment;
- sales efficiency, deferred commissions, and customer-success cost;
- stock compensation, diluted shares, buybacks, and debt;
- acquisition returns and strategic investments; and
- terminal margin after the current AI and data-adoption cycle.

Value recurring contribution after the cost of serving usage. A high retention
rate does not justify a high valuation if each additional unit of usage has low
or declining contribution margin.

## Cohort comparison

| Business type | Cleaner economic feature | Main uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seat-based software | More predictable contracted revenue | Seat reductions, renewal, and sales cost |
| Consumption data platform | Expansion with data and workload growth | Usage optimization, cloud cost, and variable revenue |
| Cloud infrastructure | Owns capacity and captures usage revenue | Servers, power, depreciation, debt, and utilization |
| Application or model software | Higher-level workflow and output value | Compute cost, competition, data access, and retention |

The cleanest economics belong to the layer that remains important to the customer
while keeping variable delivery cost and reinvestment below the value of the
relationship.

## Filing-based falsifiers

The conclusion should be revised if retention falls, usage growth is mainly
price or temporary workload, RPO grows without revenue and collection, prepayment
grows while margin falls, cloud or GPU cost grows faster than revenue, large
customers gain pricing power, capitalized software and deferred commissions rise
faster than revenue, stock compensation remains a large share of cash, or
diluted shares do not decline as the business matures.

The next filing should show customer cohorts, usage, pricing, RPO conversion,
cloud cost, gross margin, capitalized software, deferred commissions, stock
compensation, diluted shares, and cash per share.
