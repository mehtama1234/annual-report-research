# Defense economics: backlog, program risk, and pension-adjusted cash

## The first principle

A defense prime converts a national-security requirement into a qualified
system, production capability, delivery, and long-term sustainment.

`security requirement -> funded program -> qualification -> design and production -> acceptance and billing -> sustainment and follow-on work`

The prime may control scarce technical knowledge and production access, but the
government controls funding, requirements, contract terms, and procurement
timing.

## Backlog is not a fixed margin stream

Backlog is valuable only when the work is funded, the scope is defined, the
customer will accept delivery, and the cost estimate remains credible. A large
backlog can contain options, development work, production work, sustainment,
international work, and contracts whose economics change with modifications.

The key evidence is cost-to-complete, funded status, contract type, billing,
unbilled receivables, cash collection, loss provisions, and final margin—not
the total award number.

## Program accounting and working capital

Long-term defense work often recognizes revenue as progress occurs. That puts
estimates of cost, schedule, claims, supplier performance, and customer
acceptance into current earnings. Unbilled receivables and inventoried costs can
rise before cash arrives. Loss provisions can arrive late relative to the
period in which the underlying cost problem developed.

The forensic sequence is:

1. compare original and current program margin;
2. inspect cost-to-complete revisions and loss provisions;
3. track unbilled receivables, advance payments, and billing;
4. separate development, production, and sustainment;
5. charge capex, R&D, labor, supplier support, and pension cash; and
6. compare cash returns with the capital needed to deliver the backlog.

## Who carries the burden?

The defense prime carries engineering, classified labor, facilities, supplier,
quality, schedule, warranty, cybersecurity, and fixed-price execution risk.
The government carries mission requirements and program funding but can change
scope or delay authorization. Pension and health obligations can remain with
the company even when contract revenue is funded.

Strategic importance creates barriers to entry and demand durability. It does
not guarantee that every program earns an acceptable return.

## Alden and Damodaran conclusions

From a durability and liquidity perspective, strategic defense demand can be
less tied to consumer and ordinary industrial cycles. It is still exposed to
appropriations, policy, inflation, skilled labor, suppliers, and program
execution. A strong balance sheet must fund production scaling and pension
obligations through award and delivery timing.

From a narrative-to-numbers perspective, “national-security backlog” must
become funded sales, program margin, cash collection, capital investment,
pension-adjusted owner cash, and a realistic terminal reinvestment rate.

## Valuation: backlog must become risk-adjusted owner cash

Backlog is an input to a valuation model, not a valuation denominator. The
model should convert each major program into expected revenue, cost, billing,
collection, capital spending, pension funding, taxes, and the timing of cash
returns. Development and fixed-price work deserve wider downside ranges than
funded sustainment with a documented margin history.

The valuation should separate three questions. First, how much of the backlog
is funded and likely to become revenue? Second, what margin remains after
labor, suppliers, quality, working capital, capex, R&D, and pension cash? Third,
what reinvestment is required to preserve the qualification, facilities, and
technical workforce that make the relationship valuable? A higher backlog
multiple is not justified if the company must spend more capital or accept
lower margins to deliver it.

The useful output is risk-adjusted owner cash per share under a base case, a
cost-overrun and delay case, and a stronger production or sustainment case.
Compare those cases with the market price and the assumptions implied by the
current valuation. If the price requires uninterrupted awards, stable program
margins, low pension cash, and rapid backlog conversion at the same time, the
margin of safety is smaller than the backlog headline suggests.

## Cohort conclusion

Northrop, Caterpillar, Honeywell, and other defense or aerospace companies can
have protected technology and long customer relationships, but their burdens
differ. Northrop has strategic-program and pension exposure; Caterpillar has
dealer and equipment-cycle exposure; Honeywell’s separation changes the
historical denominator; contractors such as Primoris carry field execution and
contract-estimate risk.

The cleanest defense economics belong to a program with high qualification
barriers, funded demand, credible cost control, and sustainment revenue. The
most dangerous interpretation is to treat every backlog dollar as recurring,
high-margin, low-capital cash.

## Next evidence

Track funded backlog, program margin, loss provisions, unbilled receivables,
customer advances, production rates, supplier recovery, capex, R&D, pension
contributions, contract modifications, and capital returns.

## Definitions for the defense cash analysis

**Backlog** is contracted work that has not yet been recognized as revenue. It
can include funded production, development, sustainment, options,
international work, and work dependent on future appropriations or customer
authorization.

**Cost-to-complete** is management's current estimate of the remaining cost to
finish a contract. When that estimate changes, current revenue or profit can
change even though the customer has not paid more. This makes cost estimation a
current-period earnings input.

**Unbilled receivables** are amounts recognized as revenue or contract assets
before the company has the right to issue an invoice under the contract. They
can be normal, but they consume working capital and depend on future billing,
acceptance, and collection.

**Pension-adjusted owner cash** is a conservative screen rather than a reported
metric:

Start with operating cash flow less cash capital expenditure. Locate pension contributions and production-related working-capital movements in the reported cash-flow reconciliation. To the extent they are already included in operating cash flow, they must not be deducted a second time. Pension expense and cash contributions are different measures, not two independent cash payments.

For a normalized forecast, state the assumed pension contribution and explain how it differs from the amount already paid in the historical period. Use that difference as the adjustment. Model future program payments and capital investment in the periods when they are expected to occur, preserving a separate view of historical cash and future obligations.

It should then be tested against debt repayment, dividends, repurchases,
strategic investment, and program-specific commitments. Pension expense in
GAAP earnings and cash pension contributions are different quantities; both
belong in the analysis.

## The contract-to-cash sequence

| Stage | Evidence to inspect | Main burden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requirement and award | Funding, customer, scope, contract type | Appropriations and scope can change |
| Design and qualification | Technical maturity, testing, supplier readiness | Development delay and rework |
| Production | Units, labor, materials, quality, rate | Fixed-price cost overrun and capacity |
| Progress recognition | Cost incurred, estimated margin, claims | Current earnings depend on estimates |
| Billing and acceptance | Contract assets, unbilled receivables, advances | Cash can lag revenue |
| Delivery and sustainment | Acceptance, warranty, support, follow-on work | Defects, labor, and performance obligations |
| Pension and closure | Contributions, CAS recovery, remediation, termination | Cash claims can outlast the program |

This sequence prevents a backlog balance from being treated as a fixed-margin
cash stream. Each program must be followed from award through final margin and
cash collection.

## Contract type changes who carries risk

Cost-plus contracts generally pass more allowable cost through to the customer,
subject to audits and contract terms. Fixed-price contracts place more cost,
schedule, supplier, and execution risk on the contractor. Time-and-materials
contracts depend on labor availability, billing rates, and customer demand.

The annual report should therefore separate backlog by contract type and
program stage. A high backlog made mainly of cost-reimbursable sustainment is
not economically equivalent to a high backlog made mainly of fixed-price
development and production.

## Earnings-quality and possible financial-engineering tests

The main forensic risk is not necessarily improper revenue recognition. It is
the use of optimistic assumptions about cost, schedule, claims, supplier
recovery, and customer acceptance to recognize margin before the final cash
result is known. The required tests are:

- Compare original contract margin with current margin and every material
  estimate revision.
- Reconcile cost incurred, estimated cost-to-complete, loss provisions,
  change orders, claims, incentives, and customer funding.
- Track unbilled receivables, inventoried costs, advances, contract assets,
  billing, collections, and receivable aging.
- Separate funded backlog, options, international work, classified work, and
  work dependent on future appropriations.
- Compare development, production, and sustainment margins and capital needs.
- Test whether supplier delays, labor shortages, quality issues, and production
  ramps are recognized promptly.
- Reconcile pension expense, cash contributions, government or CAS recoveries,
  funded status, and long-term obligations.
- Compare dividends and repurchases with cash after program investment,
  pension, working capital, and debt.

These are evidence-based tests of estimate quality, not accusations of
misconduct.

## Management incentives and capital allocation

The proxy should identify whether compensation rewards revenue, operating
income, free cash flow, backlog, cash conversion, return on invested capital,
stock price, safety, delivery, quality, or program performance. Record metric
definitions, weights, threshold/target/maximum levels, and adjustments for
loss provisions, pension items, restructuring, acquisitions, or contract
settlements.

Backlog and revenue targets can reward award capture and progress recognition
before the program's final return is known. Adjusted earnings can remove
recurring pension, restructuring, or program items. Free cash flow can improve
temporarily when customer advances rise or inventory is released. A stronger
capital-allocation test compares incentive outcomes with final program margin,
cash collection, pension-adjusted owner cash, safety, quality, and diluted
shares.

For each major program, compare executive emphasis with the actual economic
burden: did production scale without cost overruns, did unbilled balances
convert, did pension cash remain funded, and did common-owner cash per share
improve after capital returns?

## Lyn Alden and Damodaran application

The Alden lens asks whether government demand, strategic competition, allied
spending, inflation, labor availability, and liquidity support a stable order
environment. Defense demand can be less sensitive to ordinary consumer cycles,
but appropriations, political priorities, rates, supplier health, and labor
costs still affect delivery and cash.

The Damodaran lens requires separate assumptions for development, production,
sustainment, international work, and pension obligations. The model should
specify funded backlog conversion, contract type, program margin, cost-to-
complete risk, working capital, capex, R&D, pension contributions, debt, taxes,
and the terminal reinvestment required to maintain technical capability.

Strategic importance can justify a durable relationship, but it does not
justify capitalizing backlog before its cost and cash conversion are known.

## Cohort comparison

Northrop controls specialized technical knowledge, clearances, classified
capability, qualified facilities, and long-lived program relationships. The
government controls requirements, funding, contract terms, appropriations, and
acceptance. Suppliers and skilled labor can control schedule and cost at
critical points. Pension and health obligations remain with the contractor even
when program revenue is funded.

Compared with Caterpillar, a defense prime has more government and program-
estimate exposure and less dealer and equipment-utilization exposure. Compared
with Honeywell, a defense-heavy program business has more contract and
development risk; diversified aerospace or industrial service revenue may have
different billing and capital characteristics. Compared with field contractors,
the defense prime has greater technical qualification barriers but more
classified, fixed-price, and long-cycle cost-to-complete risk.

The cleaner economics belong to funded sustainment or production with credible
cost control and follow-on demand. The heavier burden belongs to development,
fixed-price production ramps, pension obligations, and programs requiring
capital before acceptance and billing.

## Filing-based falsifiers

The defense backlog thesis weakens if backlog grows while funded status,
billings, or cash collections weaken; program margin improves only through
optimistic cost-to-complete assumptions; loss provisions arrive after working
capital has accumulated; unbilled receivables and inventoried costs grow faster
than revenue; supplier, labor, quality, or schedule problems require repeated
revisions; pension contributions, CAS recoveries, or debt consume capital
returns; or customer budgets and contract changes reduce usable backlog.

The next filings should include backlog by type and funding, program margin
revisions, cost-to-complete, loss provisions, unbilled receivables, advances,
contract assets, cash collections, production rates, supplier recovery, capex,
R&D, pension funded status and contributions, debt, capital returns, and
compensation metrics.
