# Global institutional network versus domestic deposit franchise and bank owner cash

## The first-principles question

JPMorgan, Bank of America, and Citigroup all control large financial networks,
but their earnings are not interchangeable. The analysis should trace:

`client relationship -> deposits, payments, fees, or lending -> credit and market exposure -> capital and control burden -> common-owner earnings`

The valuable relationship is not enough. A bank creates value only when the
return on the required common equity exceeds the cost of that equity through a
full credit and liquidity cycle.

## Three different control points

| Company | Strongest control point | Main earnings mix | Main burden |
|---|---|---|---|
| JPMorgan | Broad domestic deposit, payments, corporate, card, and markets network | Largest and most diversified spread, fee, markets, and investment-banking platform | Credit, markets, compensation, technology, conduct, capital, and scale complexity |
| Bank of America | Domestic consumer and small-business deposits, payments, wealth, and corporate relationships | Deposit-funded consumer, commercial, wealth, and markets earnings | Deposit repricing, consumer credit, securities duration, fraud, technology, and capital |
| Citigroup | Cross-border institutional payments, treasury, trade, securities services, markets, and banking | Services and markets network plus capital-markets fees, wealth, and cards | Global controls, liquidity transfer, sanctions, transformation, divestitures, market risk, and capital |

Citi's network can be harder to reproduce than a single domestic product, but
it is also more expensive to operate. JPMorgan and Bank of America have larger
domestic funding franchises; Citi's investment case relies more heavily on
institutional connectivity and execution improvement.

## The bank owner-cash denominator

Industrial free cash flow is not an appropriate common denominator. Loans,
securities, deposits, liquidity, and regulatory capital are the operating
system. A useful owner-earnings measure is normalized common net income after
funding cost, expected credit losses, employee and technology expense, legal
and control spending, required capital retention, and prudent growth.

Adjusted earnings must be tested for recurring transformation, remediation,
separation, litigation, stock compensation, and credit provisions. A bank that
excludes required controls may look more profitable precisely when it is
underinvesting in resilience.

## What the filings should prove

For JPMorgan, the key question is whether scale and breadth continue to produce
superior returns after normalizing markets and investment banking. For Bank of
America, it is whether deposits, payments, and wealth offset deposit beta and
consumer credit. For Citi, it is whether Services and institutional
connectivity can support durable returns after global controls and the cost of
completing the transformation.

Across all three, track:

- deposit balances, mix, rate paid, deposit beta, and wholesale funding;
- loan growth, delinquencies, charge-offs, reserve coverage, and criticized
  assets;
- net interest income split between balance, rate, and mix;
- Markets and investment-banking revenue normalized over several years;
- technology, compensation, fraud, legal, compliance, and resilience cost;
- CET1, stress capital, liquidity coverage, risk-weighted assets, and payouts;
- tangible book value, RoTCE, diluted shares, and the gap between reported and
  adjusted earnings.

## Who captures the economics and who carries the burden

The bank owns the customer and institutional relationship, but employees,
depositors, shareholders, regulators, counterparties, and the bank's liquidity
buffer jointly carry the system's burden. The customer relationship captures
fees and funding advantages. Common shareholders absorb the residual credit,
market, legal, operational, and capital risk after other claims are paid.

Citi's case adds a specific burden-transfer question: can the firm capture the
value of cross-border flows while retaining enough local infrastructure and
capital to meet each jurisdiction's rules? If not, geographic breadth can
increase reported activity without increasing common-owner returns.

## Falsifiers

The network thesis weakens if deposit costs rise faster than asset yields,
credit losses outpace reserves, markets revenue carries the valuation, control
costs recur as “one-time” items, or payouts reduce resilience. Citi specifically
needs to show that divestitures reduce expense and capital without merely
moving costs into All Other or creating stranded infrastructure.

The cleanest confirming evidence is stable low-cost funding, risk-adjusted loan
growth, recurring fee income, lower full-cost servicing, strong stress capital,
and returns on tangible common equity that persist after all required controls.

## Definitions that prevent unlike economics from being combined

**A financial network** is a set of customer relationships, information,
infrastructure, contracts, and controls that allows money, risk, securities,
or institutional decisions to move. It may produce fees without owning a large
loan book, or it may combine fees with deposits, lending, and market positions.

**Net interest income** is interest received on loans and securities minus
interest paid on deposits and other funding. **Fee income** comes from
payments, custody, asset management, cards, underwriting, advisory, markets,
or other services. Fee income is not automatically low risk: it can require
technology, employees, fraud control, liquidity, legal oversight, and capital.

**Tangible common equity** is common equity after removing goodwill and certain
intangible assets. **Return on tangible common equity** compares common profit
with that tangible capital base. It is useful for banks because capital is a
required operating resource, not excess cash.

**Owner earnings** for a bank are normalized common earnings after funding
cost, credit losses, compensation, technology, control, taxes, and the capital
that must be retained for prudent growth and stress resilience:

`normalized common earnings = recurring spread and fee income - full operating and risk cost - taxes - required capital retention`

This is the appropriate starting point for comparing a bank with a fee-based
institutional network. Industrial free cash flow is not a common denominator.

## The relationship-to-owner-return sequence

| Stage | Evidence to inspect | Main burden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client relationship | Deposits, custody, payments, trading, data, advisory | Retention and service cost |
| Activity or funding | Volume, balances, loan demand, market levels | Rate, cycle, and liquidity sensitivity |
| Revenue | Spread, fee, commission, management, or trading result | Revenue may be cyclical or acquired |
| Risk absorption | Credit, market, counterparty, fraud, conduct, legal | Losses can arrive after revenue |
| Control system | Technology, cyber, compliance, sanctions, resilience | Required spending may be recurring |
| Capital allocation | Common capital, dividends, buybacks, acquisitions | Payout can weaken stress capacity |
| Owner result | Normalized earnings and tangible book per share | Dilution and retained capital matter |

The customer relationship creates value only after the bank or network pays all
the costs required to keep it reliable and compliant.

## Institutional networks versus deposit franchises

Ratings, indexes, data, exchange, custody, and clearing businesses can have
high incremental margins because a new customer or transaction often uses
existing infrastructure. Their risks include regulation, reputation, market
activity, customer concentration, pricing pressure, cyber failure, and the
cost of maintaining trusted records.

Domestic deposit franchises have a different benefit: stable deposits can fund
loans and securities at a favorable cost. They also carry direct exposure to
deposit repricing, withdrawals, credit losses, securities duration, liquidity,
capital, branches, fraud, and balance-sheet growth.

Citigroup combines cross-border institutional services, payments, treasury,
securities services, markets, and banking. It may control difficult-to-replace
cross-border workflows, but each jurisdiction adds licensing, sanctions,
liquidity, technology, and control cost. JPMorgan and Bank of America have
broader domestic funding and consumer relationships, but their scale adds
credit, card, branch, technology, conduct, and capital complexity.

## Earnings-quality and possible financial-engineering tests

The main forensic risk is treating a favorable market, rate, acquisition, or
adjusted-efficiency period as structural network economics. Test:

- net interest income against loan and securities balances, yields, deposit
  beta, wholesale funding, duration, and rate sensitivity;
- fee revenue against organic transaction volume, client balances, market
  levels, flows, acquired revenue, and employee and technology cost;
- deposits against rate paid, uninsured concentration, brokered funding,
  liquidity, and stress outflows;
- loans against underwriting, criticized assets, nonaccruals, delinquencies,
  charge-offs, collateral, and allowance coverage;
- trading and investment-banking revenue against a multi-year normalized
  market cycle rather than one strong quarter;
- transformation, remediation, legal, technology, cyber, and control spending
  to determine whether excluded costs are actually recurring;
- acquisitions and divestitures against retained clients, revenue, stranded
  costs, capital released, integration cost, goodwill, and dilution; and
- dividends and repurchases against stress capital, tangible book value,
  diluted shares, and through-cycle earnings.

These are tests of reported economic quality, not allegations of misconduct.

## Management incentives and capital allocation

The proxy should identify whether management is rewarded for EPS, revenue,
return on tangible common equity, efficiency, deposits, loan growth, fee
growth, market share, stock price, risk outcomes, customer retention,
regulatory results, or capital ratios. Record definitions, weights,
threshold/target/maximum levels, and adjustments for acquisitions,
transformation, litigation, credit provisions, and unusual market results.

An EPS target can reward repurchases or acquisition accounting. An efficiency
target can improve through underinvestment in controls. A deposit or loan
growth target can expand funding and credit risk. A market-revenue target can
reward a strong cycle rather than durable client economics. A through-cycle
return on tangible common equity that includes full control cost and capital is
more informative, but the proxy must establish whether it is used.

For each acquisition, compare the purchase price and dilution with retained
client balances, fee flows, funding benefit, credit risk, integration cost,
capital consumed, and post-acquisition tangible return. For each divestiture,
test whether costs truly disappear or remain in shared infrastructure.

## Lyn Alden and Damodaran application

The Alden lens asks how liquidity, rates, credit conditions, unemployment,
commercial-property values, market activity, geopolitical risk, and confidence
affect both fee networks and deposit franchises. A trusted network can remain
strategically important while transaction volumes, lending, or asset values
fall. Deposit funding can be valuable until it reprices or leaves.

The Damodaran lens separates recurring institutional fees, spread income,
markets, investment banking, cards, wealth, and one-time gains. A bank forecast
must include funding cost, normalized credit losses, required capital, control
spending, taxes, and dilution. A fee-network forecast must include volume,
pricing, retention, technology, regulatory, cyber, and capital costs.

The valuation question is whether the current price assumes market revenue,
low credit losses, low deposit beta, successful transformation, and high
capital returns at the same time. Each assumption should be tested separately.

## Cohort comparison

JPMorgan's breadth creates multiple sources of client activity and funding, but
also a large control, compensation, technology, conduct, and capital burden.
Bank of America has strong domestic consumer, small-business, wealth, and
payments relationships, with deposit repricing, consumer credit, securities
duration, and fraud exposure. Citigroup's cross-border institutional network
can be difficult to reproduce, but its geographic breadth increases local
control, sanctions, liquidity, transformation, and stranded-cost risk.

Compared with S&P Global or CME, the banks own liabilities and assets that can
produce credit and funding losses. Compared with global payment and clearing
networks, the banks carry more direct balance-sheet and regulatory-capital
exposure. The institutional network may control the workflow, while common
shareholders fund the people, technology, liquidity, credit, and capital
needed to make the workflow trustworthy.

## Filing-based falsifiers

The network thesis weakens if deposits reprice or leave faster than assets
reprice; loan growth outpaces underwriting and reserves; fee growth comes
mainly from market levels or acquisitions; transformation costs recur while
being labeled temporary; control incidents increase; divestitures leave
stranded costs; capital returns reduce stress resilience; tangible book per
share falls through avoidable dilution; or returns on tangible common equity
depend on unusually low credit losses and unusually strong markets.

The next filings should provide deposit beta and mix, liquidity and stress
capital, loan and securities duration, credit quality, fee revenue by source,
market normalization, technology and control spending, acquisition retention,
divestiture stranded cost, goodwill, incentive metrics, tangible book,
diluted shares, and repurchase prices.
