# Levered Agency MBS carry: spread income, hedges, and book-value risk

## Business economics: the first-principles question

A mortgage REIT does not primarily sell housing or originate ordinary bank loans.
It buys mortgage securities, finances them, hedges rates and duration, and pays
out the residual spread and capital-market result to shareholders.

`Agency MBS yield + dollar-roll income - repo and other funding cost - hedge cost - operating expense - losses and book-value changes = common-owner return`

Agency guarantees reduce borrower credit risk, but they do not remove interest-
rate, prepayment, spread, funding, leverage, liquidity, or dilution risk.

| Participant | Controls or receives | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| Home borrower | Mortgage financing and prepayment choice | Interest and housing obligations |
| Agency / housing-finance system | Guarantee and market standardization | Program and policy risk |
| Mortgage REIT | Portfolio, leverage, hedge, repo, and distribution decisions | Duration, convexity, spread, funding, liquidity, and book-value risk |
| Repo lender and hedge counterparty | Financing and risk-transfer contracts | Counterparty and collateral exposure |
| Common shareholder | Residual spread, dividend, and book-value change | Leverage, dilution, market, and capital-loss risk |

## Spread income is not owner cash

Net spread and dollar-roll income can remain positive while comprehensive income
and tangible book value fall. A quarter can show cash-like coupon income and a
negative economic return because rates, spreads, or prepayments reduce the value
of the financed portfolio.

Read three outputs together:

1. Net spread and dollar-roll income.
2. Net income and comprehensive income.
3. Tangible net book value per share and total shareholder return.

The first describes current carry. The second includes accounting and market
movements. The third shows what happened to shareholder capital after dividends.

## Leverage and funding

Leverage increases the spread earned on equity and magnifies small changes in
asset values. Repo financing can be short-term even when the mortgage assets are
long-duration. The risk is not only the interest rate paid; it is the availability,
haircut, margin, maturity, and counterparty behavior of the financing.

Track at-risk leverage, repo maturity, weighted funding cost, haircuts,
unencumbered assets, liquidity, collateral calls, secured and unsecured debt,
and the ability to reduce risk without selling at a loss.

## Hedges and prepayments

Hedges reduce rate exposure but have their own duration, basis, convexity,
counterparty, and collateral risks. Mortgage borrowers can refinance when rates
fall, shortening asset duration; they can remain in place when rates rise,
lengthening duration. This makes the portfolio's risk change as the market moves.

Test hedge coverage, swap and Treasury positions, hedge cost, duration gap,
prepayment speed, premium amortization, TBA positions, and mortgage-spread
changes. A stable leverage ratio does not mean stable risk if duration or
prepayment behavior changes.

## AGNC and Annaly comparison

AGNC's FY2025 portfolio was approximately `$94.8B`, about `95%` in 30-year
fixed-rate Agency RMBS and TBAs, with at-risk leverage approximately `7.2x` and
net spread and dollar-roll income approximately `$1.535B`. Q1 2026 produced a
negative economic return of approximately `-1.6%` while spread income remained
approximately `$475M`; Q2 recovered to an economic return of approximately
`6.7%`, portfolio approximately `$97.2B`, and leverage approximately `7.4x`.

Annaly's existing dossier supplies the peer case. The comparison should use
portfolio composition, leverage, hedge ratio, funding, prepayment, spread income,
book-value movement, dividend coverage, and total shareholder return. Do not
rank the two on dividend yield alone.

Annaly's Q2 2026 filing makes the denominator discipline concrete. Economic
return was `5.5%`, book value per common share was `$20.15`, and EAD was `$0.79`
per average common share against a `$0.75` dividend. Economic leverage was
`5.6x`; repurchase agreements were `$86.9B` with a weighted average maturity of
`33` days and a weighted average rate of `3.85%`. Net interest income was
`$488.2M`, but net derivative gains were `$552.4M` and net gains on investments
and other were negative `$318.5M`. The quarter was economically strong, but the
return cannot be reduced to a recurring coupon spread.

Annaly also raised approximately `$447M` through ATM common-equity sales in the
quarter, and shares outstanding rose from `707.0M` at December 31, 2025 to
`750.6M` at June 30, 2026. Equity can be accretive when issued above book value
and deployed at attractive returns, but per-share compounding must be tested
after issuance. The current evidence therefore supports dividend coverage and
portfolio scale, while keeping funding rollover, book-value protection, and
dilution as the central falsification tests.

## Alden and Damodaran lenses

The Alden lens follows rates, liquidity, housing finance, repo funding, and the
availability of collateral. A tightening can increase funding cost and haircuts
while weakening book value. A falling-rate environment can improve financing
conditions but increase prepayment and reinvestment risk.

The Damodaran lens values normalized spread income and book value separately. The
correct question is whether the portfolio earns an adequate return on tangible
equity after funding, hedging, operating expense, expected book-value volatility,
and dilution. A high current dividend can be a return of capital if it exceeds
sustainable earnings or is followed by equity issuance.

## Valuation: carry and book value are separate inputs

An Agency-MBS mortgage REIT should not be valued from dividend yield alone. A useful scenario starts with tangible book value per share, then asks what price-to-book relationship is justified by expected spread income, leverage, hedge quality, funding access, dividend coverage, and the probability of book-value loss. A company trading above book value must earn a durable return on tangible equity; a discount to book value may reflect real funding, duration, dilution, or asset-mark risk rather than a simple bargain.

The valuation bridge is: normalized spread after funding and hedges + expected book-value change - operating costs and dilution = sustainable return to common owners. Test bear, base, and strong cases across rate and mortgage-spread paths. Do not capitalize one quarter of carry or one year of favorable marks as a permanent earnings stream.

## Forensic filing tests

- Reconcile spread income to average portfolio, asset yield, funding cost,
  leverage, dollar-roll income, and hedge cost.
- Reconcile net income to comprehensive income and tangible book value per share.
- Track dividend per share against spread income, economic return, and book value.
- Test share issuance and at-the-market programs for dilution and book-value
  transfer between existing and new shareholders.
- Separate realized gains, unrealized marks, premium amortization, TBA income,
  and derivative results.
- Read repo maturities, collateral, haircuts, unencumbered assets, and liquidity.
- Track prepayment, duration, hedge ratio, mortgage spreads, and leverage through
  different rate paths.

## Filing-based falsifiers and what to check next

The carry thesis weakens if spread income falls below funding and hedge costs,
book value declines repeatedly, leverage rises to maintain dividends, the hedge
fails in a rate move, repo access tightens, dividends exceed sustainable income,
or share issuance transfers value away from existing holders.

At each quarterly filing, record portfolio yield, repo cost and maturity,
hedge notional and duration, prepayment assumptions, at-risk leverage, tangible
book value per share, dividend coverage, shares outstanding, and unencumbered
liquidity. Compare those figures with the prior quarter and with the rate and
spread environment; the change in the bridge matters more than the headline
dividend.
