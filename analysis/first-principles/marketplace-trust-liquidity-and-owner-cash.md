# Marketplaces: trust, liquidity, take rate, and owner cash

## The first principle

A marketplace creates value by helping a buyer find a seller, decide to trust
the transaction, pay, and receive the item. The platform does not need to own
the merchandise to control an important relationship, but it must keep both
sides participating.

The chain is:

`seller supply -> buyer discovery -> trust and payment -> completed transaction -> repeat participation -> monetization -> platform renewal -> owner cash`

eBay is a useful case because its supply is often unique, used, collectible,
or enthusiast-driven. Shopify helps merchants operate their own stores. The
Trade Desk routes advertising budgets. Amazon combines marketplace access with
owned retail, fulfillment, and membership. These are not interchangeable
platforms.

## GMV is activity, not revenue

GMV is the value of transactions facilitated by the platform. Revenue is the
portion retained through transaction fees, payments, advertising, shipping, or
other services. Take rate is revenue divided by GMV.

For eBay in 2025, GMV was $79.609B, revenue was $11.100B, and take rate was
13.94%. GMV increased 7%, revenue increased 8%, and take rate rose from 13.77%
in 2024. The first-principles question is what caused the increase: better
category mix, advertising, shipping, payment attachment, pricing, or a cost
that could weaken future participation.

Higher take rate is not automatically better. If sellers pay more but list less,
raise prices, or move to other channels, the platform may gain current revenue
while losing future supply. If buyers see lower selection or higher total cost,
repeat purchases can weaken.

## Liquidity is a two-sided operating condition

Marketplace liquidity means a buyer can find a suitable item and a seller can
reach a buyer at acceptable cost and time. It is not the same as website
traffic. The analyst should track:

- active buyers and sellers;
- listings, sold items, and conversion;
- repeat purchase and seller retention;
- time to sale and shipping reliability;
- returns, cancellations, fraud, and payment disputes;
- category-specific supply and pricing; and
- marketing cost per incremental transaction.

eBay's focus categories—collectibles, motors parts and accessories, luxury,
refurbished goods, apparel, and sneakers—can have stronger supply identity and
buyer intent than standardized products. That can support repeat behavior, but
it can also create exposure to narrow tastes, authenticity disputes, tariffs,
and changes in discretionary spending.

## Trust is both an asset and a cost

Trust systems include identity, seller history, ratings, payment controls,
buyer protection, returns, fraud detection, dispute resolution, and shipping
information. They reduce the chance that a transaction fails. They also
require employees, technology, reserves, customer support, payment processing,
and sometimes direct loss absorption.

eBay recorded transaction losses of $396M in 2025, $353M in 2024, and $360M in
2023. The relevant measure is losses as a percentage of GMV and their effect on
repeat behavior, not just the absolute dollar amount. A marketplace with rising
GMV and rising loss per transaction may be growing by accepting lower-quality
activity.

## Advertising can deepen or weaken the marketplace

Promoted Listings let sellers pay for higher visibility. The platform has useful
information about the item, the buyer's search, the category, and the eventual
sale. This can make advertising more measurable than broad brand advertising.

The economic test is whether the promotion creates an incremental sale. If a
seller must pay to receive exposure that was previously free, advertising
revenue may rise without increasing total marketplace demand. Track seller
return on ad spend, advertising penetration, organic conversion, take rate,
listing quality, and seller retention together.

AI agents and chatbots may change product discovery. If buyers search outside
the marketplace, eBay may lose traffic, transaction data, and the context that
makes its advertising valuable. AI can reduce search cost while also weakening
the platform's direct relationship with the buyer.

## The cash and capital burden

eBay's 2025 continuing operating cash flow was $2.009B. Property and equipment
used $525M, acquisitions used $208M, and the simple residual was about $1.276B.
The 2023 and 2024 residuals were about $1.937B and $1.943B. The 2025 decline
occurred despite higher GMV and revenue.

The platform also capitalized $134M of product-development costs in 2025. The
capitalized software is amortized over roughly one to three years, meaning the
company must continually spend to keep the product current. Stock-based
compensation was $607M, and the company repurchased $2.5B of stock. Cash per
continuing share should therefore be measured after platform renewal and
economic dilution, not after adding back every non-cash cost.

## Who controls and who pays?

| Participant | What it contributes | What it bears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seller | Inventory, listing effort, fulfillment, customer service | Unsold goods, fees, returns, advertising, disputes, and platform dependence |
| Buyer | Demand, payment, feedback, repeat activity | Price, authenticity, delivery, privacy, and transaction risk |
| Marketplace | Discovery, trust, payment, data, advertising, support | Technology, fraud, marketing, regulation, staff, and reputation |
| Carrier / payment partner | Delivery or transaction completion | Service failure, cost inflation, and counterparty risk |
| Shareholder | Capital and tolerance for cycle risk | Dilution, buyback timing, debt, and declining cash conversion |

The platform can appear asset-light because sellers own inventory and carriers
deliver goods. That does not mean the marketplace has no operating burden. It
must fund the trust, software, marketing, payment, customer-support, and
reputation systems that make the seller-funded supply usable.

## Cohort comparison

| Platform | Primary control point | Main burden | Core falsifier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| eBay | Category supply, buyer discovery, trust, seller advertising | Transaction losses, marketing, software, SBC, competition | GMV grows while trust, seller retention, or cash conversion weakens |
| Shopify | Merchant workflow, storefront, checkout, payments | Payment cost, merchant credit, chargebacks, AI, dilution | Merchant attachment does not produce cash after payment and credit burden |
| The Trade Desk | Buyer-side ad decisioning and measurement | Partner access, receivables, data, hosting, SBC | Decisioning loses value as discovery and take rates change |
| Amazon marketplace | Buyer demand, seller access, fulfillment, membership | Fulfillment, capex, labor, seller dependence, competition | Marketplace growth fails to cover physical infrastructure and service burden |
| Uber | Mobility, delivery, membership, matching, and payment relationship | Driver and courier supply, insurance, incentives, regulation, support, and autonomous-vehicle transition | Gross bookings and membership grow while contribution per user, provider health, or owner cash weakens |
| Airbnb | Guest discovery, host supply, trust, identity, payment, and trip workflow | Trust and safety, support, regulation, taxes, payment settlement, SBC, and provider quality | GBV and bookings grow while take rate, repeat contribution, supply availability, or per-share cash weakens |

eBay has less fulfillment ownership than Amazon and less merchant operating
workflow control than Shopify. Its advantage is concentrated supply and
category expertise. That advantage is durable only if buyers and sellers keep
using eBay when other channels offer lower fees, better discovery, faster
shipping, or stronger identity and trust.

Uber is a different marketplace because the transaction includes time-sensitive
physical service rather than a shipped item. Its liquidity test is whether a
consumer can obtain a ride or delivery at an acceptable price and wait time,
while a driver, courier, or merchant can earn enough to remain available. The
company can coordinate both sides through one membership and payment
relationship, but it also faces more direct service, insurance, labor, and
regulatory exposure than a marketplace that only provides discovery and
payment. Uber's approximately `$193B` of FY2025 gross bookings and about
`$9.8B` of reported free cash flow therefore require a separate reconciliation
of provider payments, incentives, settlements, stock compensation, capital
spending, and legal obligations.

Airbnb is a different marketplace again. Hosts provide most of the physical
asset and service, while Airbnb provides discovery, trust, identity, payment,
and support. This creates lower physical capital needs but higher dependence on
local regulation, host quality, safety systems, and guest confidence. FY2025
operating cash flow was `$4.646B`, stock-based compensation was `$1.592B`, and
share repurchases were `$3.789B`; owner-cash analysis must therefore subtract
economic dilution and separate platform cash from interest income on short-term
investments.

Amazon's Q2 2026 filing makes the burden visible in the current period. Q2
revenue was `$200.606B`, operating income was about `$27.5B`, and AWS revenue
was `$42.232B`, up 37% year over year. For the first six months, operating cash
flow was `$71.419B` while cash capital expenditures were `$96.3B`. Amazon also
spent `$39.8B` on acquisitions and other investments, including `$28.7B` for
OpenAI preferred stock. The trailing twelve-month measure showed `$161.403B`
of operating cash flow against `$169.007B` of net property spending, producing
negative `$7.604B` of Amazon-defined free cash flow before acquisition and
investment cash.

This does not establish that Amazon's AI or cloud spending is uneconomic. It
establishes the required question: how much of the present cash generation is
being reinvested in capacity, and what return must AWS, advertising, seller
services, subscriptions, and retail earn before common owners receive durable
cash? Amazon's marketplace is therefore not an asset-light comparison with
eBay or Shopify; it is a relationship platform carrying a large physical and
computing balance sheet.

eBay's Q2 2026 filing adds a different current-period lesson. Revenue and GMV
both grew 15%, but first-half transaction losses increased to `$271M` from
`$167M`; management attributed much of the increase to buyer and seller fraud
and recovery rates. Active buyers rose only 2% to 136M while Q2 GMV rose 15%.
This separates three questions that are often merged: whether the marketplace
has more activity, whether each buyer is generating more volume, and whether
the additional volume is safe and profitable after claims and chargebacks.

First-half operating cash flow was `$1.519B`, but the year-over-year increase
was helped by `$794M` less cash tax and improved working capital. Property
spending was `$295M`, stock compensation was `$338M`, and acquisitions were
`$39M` before the `$1.4B` cash Depop purchase completed after quarter end. The
company's reported free cash flow is therefore not the same as cash available
to common owners. A proper eBay analysis must charge transaction losses,
capitalized software, stock compensation, acquisition spending, and the
maintenance of buyer and seller trust against the cash produced by GMV.

DoorDash shows why a marketplace with physical fulfillment requires a separate
burden test. In Q2 2026, Marketplace GOV rose 36% to `$33.078B`, orders rose
27% to 970M, and revenue rose 36% to `$4.454B`, while the net revenue margin
remained 13.5%. The activity and retained-revenue relationship improved, but
GAAP net income fell to `$200M` from `$285M`. For the first half, DoorDash
reported `$1.538B` of operating cash flow and `$1.162B` of company-defined free
cash flow after `$118M` of property spending and `$258M` of capitalized
software.

The owner-cash question is sharper after the exclusions. First-half stock
compensation was `$580M`, unrecognized restricted-stock-unit expense was
`$2.8B`, and the company repurchased `$1.049B` of stock through August 5. A
rough residual after first-half SBC and `$30M` of acquisitions is about
`$552M` before repurchases, debt service, and future platform spending. The
company also says its Q2 growth excluding Deliveroo was 23% for Marketplace
GOV rather than the reported 36%. A marketplace can therefore show excellent
GOV growth while the common-owner result depends on delivery labor, merchant
subsidies, acquisition integration, capitalized software, stock issuance, and
regulatory obligations.

## Three analytical lenses

### Durability and liquidity

Consumer liquidity, shipping costs, tariffs, credit availability, and seller
income affect marketplace activity. A cash balance helps the company continue
investing during stress, but it does not protect the platform from users moving
elsewhere or from rising trust losses.

### Narrative translated into numbers

“Recommerce” becomes used-GMV growth, seller supply, repeat purchase, and
returns. “Focus Categories” becomes category GMV, conversion, pricing, and
margin. “Advertising growth” becomes incremental seller sales and contribution
after ad technology. “AI discovery” becomes conversion, support cost, traffic
ownership, and retention.

### Forensic earnings quality

Separate GMV from revenue, investment gains from operating cash, continuing
from discontinued operations, and adjusted income from the full platform cost.
Charge transaction losses, capitalized software, stock compensation, marketing,
buybacks, and acquisition spending against the owner-cash calculation.

## Valuation discipline

Value a marketplace on normalized cash after trust, marketing, software,
working capital, acquisitions, and dilution. eBay's $1.276B 2025 simple
residual is below its 2023–24 levels. A valuation that assumes $1.0B, $1.3B, or
$1.6B of normalized owner cash at 18x, 22x, or 26x must explain why cash
conversion improves, why advertising remains incremental, and why category
liquidity persists.

The market price should be read as an expectation about future buyers, sellers,
GMV, take rate, advertising, and transaction losses. It is not validated by
GMV growth alone.

## What future filings must prove

- GMV growth persists after consumer, tariff, and category effects normalize.
- Take-rate increases do not damage seller retention, buyer conversion, or
  listing quality.
- Advertising produces incremental seller demand and remains competitive with
  search engines, AI agents, and social platforms.
- Transaction losses, returns, cancellations, and disputes remain controlled as
  categories and cross-border activity grow.
- Capitalized software and product-development expense remain sufficient to
  protect the marketplace without hiding maintenance cost.
- Buybacks exceed SBC at prices supported by normalized owner cash.
- Depop and other category investments add retained cash, not only GMV and
  adjusted earnings.
