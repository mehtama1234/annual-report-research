# Open-internet ad decisioning: why high-margin software still needs a full owner-cash test

Date baseline: 2026-09-13

## The first-principles chain

`advertiser budget -> audience and inventory data -> bid decision -> ad delivery -> measurement -> budget renewal`

An advertising decisioning platform helps a buyer choose where to place ads,
how much to bid, which audience to target, and how to measure the result. It
is paid because making those decisions repeatedly can improve the use of a
large advertising budget.

The platform does not automatically own the advertiser, publisher, viewer, or
data source. Its durability depends on being embedded in the buyer’s workflow
and producing results that are difficult to reproduce elsewhere.

## Gross spend is not revenue

Gross platform spend is the amount of advertising transacted through the
system. Revenue is the portion retained by the platform after the economics of
inventory, data, and other partners are considered.

The analysis must follow both numbers. A rising transaction total can coexist
with a falling take rate, higher supply costs, or lower profit per dollar of
spend. Revenue growth is therefore not enough; measure the retained economics
of each channel.

## Data and measurement are part of the product

Advertisers need to identify useful audiences, buy suitable inventory, avoid
fraud, and estimate whether a campaign worked. Privacy restrictions make old
identifiers less reliable. Identity tools and measurement can create workflow
stickiness, but they depend on consent, partner access, data quality, and
regulation.

Track match rates, opt-outs, measurement accuracy, partner concentration,
incremental advertiser return, and the cost of obtaining and processing data.

## AI changes both productivity and pricing power

AI can automate planning, bidding, optimization, and reporting. That can raise
employee productivity and improve campaign results. It can also make the
workflow easier for competitors, agencies, cloud providers, or advertisers to
build.

The test is incremental cash economics: additional revenue, customer
retention, compute cost, hosting cost, employee count, pricing, and gross
profit. A feature is economically valuable only if customers pay for it or it
defends the platform’s existing economics.

## Working capital and equity compensation matter

Advertising platforms may pay publishers and data providers before collecting
from customers. Receivables can therefore rise during growth. Payables can
also provide temporary cash support when settlement timing changes.

Stock-based compensation is added back to operating cash flow, but employees
still provide real work and shareholders can be diluted. The owner-cash test
must include dilution or the cash cost of offsetting it.

## Alden and Damodaran applied

Lyn Alden’s lens asks how advertising budgets, liquidity, corporate confidence,
privacy rules, and platform access change across the cycle. A cash-rich balance
sheet helps, but advertiser demand and partner control remain external risks.

Aswath Damodaran’s lens converts “independent advertising infrastructure” into
gross spend, take rate, retention, wallet share, channel mix, supply costs,
working capital, AI investment, equity compensation, dilution, and normalized
cash per share.

## Research conclusion

The Trade Desk shows why a valuable workflow position can coexist with
substantial economic questions. The platform may gain importance as media
fragments, but investors must still measure take rate, partner dependence,
receivables, AI cost, stock compensation, dilution, and the growth rate that
the current valuation requires.

## Definitions for the advertising owner-cash analysis

**Gross platform spend** is the value of advertising transactions routed
through the platform. **Revenue** is the amount retained by the company under
its accounting presentation after the economics of publishers, data providers,
and other partners. Gross spend is therefore not company revenue.

**Take rate** is retained revenue divided by the relevant transaction value.
It can fall when growth shifts toward channels with more partner payments or
when the platform lowers pricing to win budget. A high take rate is useful only
if customers receive enough value to keep spending and the platform does not
incur hidden supply, data, fraud, or support costs.

**Incremental advertiser return** is the additional sales, attention, or other
business result the advertiser attributes to a campaign. It is not the same as
impressions, clicks, or platform spend.

For this framework:

Start with operating cash flow and subtract capitalized technology, equipment and other investing payments that are outside that figure. Operating receivable movements and ordinary cash payments for partners, support and compliance are already included when classified as operating activities. Do not deduct those payments twice. Instead, explain any adjustment needed to estimate a representative future year—for example, reversing a temporary benefit from delayed supplier payments. Show share compensation and dilution using one consistent valuation treatment rather than combining overlapping deductions.

This is an analytical screen. It is necessary because an advertising platform
can report high gross margins while paying people and partners in advance,
funding receivables, building identity and measurement tools, and issuing
substantial equity compensation.

## The advertiser-budget-to-cash sequence

| Stage | Evidence to inspect | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget allocation | Advertiser and agency retention, wallet share, spend | Budgets can move to closed platforms or agencies |
| Audience and data | Match rate, consent, quality, partner terms | Privacy, data loss, and poor targeting |
| Bid decision | Win rate, price, optimization, fraud controls | Automation can become easier to reproduce |
| Ad delivery | Inventory, supply path, CTV access, viewability | Publisher and platform dependence |
| Measurement | Attribution, incrementality, reporting | Customer distrust or regulatory limits |
| Billing and settlement | Gross spend, revenue, receivables, payables | Financing and partner-timing burden |
| Renewal | Results, retention, pricing, take rate | Customer may demand lower fees |

The platform creates value only when the advertiser's result, publisher access,
and retained economics persist after data, identity, hosting, employee,
fraud, and compliance costs.

## Revenue, take rate, and working capital

The model should separately show gross platform spend, billings, recognized
revenue, payments to supply and data partners, accounts receivable, accounts
payable, cash collections, and cash settlement. Receivables may rise because
advertiser or agency terms are longer than supplier settlement terms. Payables
may temporarily support operating cash when partners are paid later.

For each channel—open web, connected television, audio, mobile, retail media,
or other formats—track spend, revenue, take rate, partner share, data cost,
fraud cost, hosting, support, and customer retention. A lower take rate can be
acceptable if it creates durable customer control and high incremental profit;
it is weaker when it requires more working capital and partner cost without
better retention.

## Identity, measurement, and AI economics

Identity and measurement are part of the product because advertisers need to
decide which audience they reached and whether the campaign produced a result.
Privacy rules, consent, opt-outs, browser changes, platform restrictions, and
data quality can change the available signal.

AI can automate planning, bidding, optimization, and reporting. The analysis
must test whether AI:

- increases advertiser results and retention;
- raises revenue or merely lowers labor cost;
- increases compute, hosting, data, and support cost;
- reduces pricing because the function becomes easier to reproduce;
- improves take rate without worsening publisher or advertiser economics; and
- creates a durable workflow or only a feature that agencies and platforms can
  copy.

Feature adoption is not proof of economic value until customer retention,
pricing, gross profit, and owner cash improve.

## Earnings-quality and possible financial-engineering tests

The main forensic risk is allowing gross spend growth, adjusted cash, low
capital expenditure, or AI claims to obscure retained economics and dilution.
Test:

- gross spend against revenue, take rate, partner payments, and channel mix;
- revenue against cash collections, receivable aging, customer concentration,
  disputed invoices, and payment terms;
- payables against contractual settlement terms, post-period payments, and
  any change in timing;
- data, identity, measurement, fraud, hosting, and support cost against
  incremental gross profit;
- CTV and retail-media spend against inventory access, partner dependence,
  measurement, and retained margin;
- customer retention and advertiser return against discounts and pricing;
- stock compensation, diluted shares, repurchases, and cash per continuing
  share; and
- non-GAAP exclusions, investment gains, acquisitions, restructuring, and
  recurring platform-development cost.

These are testable economic questions, not accusations of misconduct.

## Management incentives and capital allocation

The proxy should identify whether management is rewarded for gross spend,
revenue, take rate, adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, customer retention,
market share, stock price, advertiser results, or cash per diluted share.
Record metric definitions, weights, threshold/target/maximum levels, and
adjustments for stock compensation, acquisitions, restructuring, and unusual
working-capital effects.

A gross-spend target can reward low-retention or low-take-rate volume. An
adjusted-cash target can benefit from payable timing or exclude equity
compensation. A revenue target can reward pricing concessions that weaken
retained profit. A customer-return and cash-per-share measure is more complete
only if it charges data, partner, fraud, hosting, compliance, and dilution
cost.

For each major product or partnership, compare adoption with incremental
revenue, take rate, partner share, advertiser results, retention, cost, and
cash per share. For each repurchase, compare price with normalized owner cash
and the value of retaining cash for product and measurement investment.

## Lyn Alden and Damodaran application

The Alden lens asks how advertiser liquidity, corporate confidence, rates,
privacy policy, consumer attention, and platform access affect spending. An
open-internet platform can benefit when media fragments, but budgets can
contract quickly and large platforms can redirect inventory or data.

The Damodaran lens separates buyer-side decisioning, data and identity,
measurement, CTV, retail media, and any hardware or content exposure. The
forecast should specify gross spend, take rate, partner and data cost, customer
retention, advertiser return, receivables, payables, AI and hosting cost,
stock compensation, dilution, and normalized cash per share.

The valuation question is whether the platform controls a durable budget-
allocation workflow or merely receives a temporary share of a market whose
prices and access are controlled by advertisers, agencies, publishers, and
closed platforms.

## Cohort comparison and filing-based falsifiers

The Trade Desk controls buyer-side decisioning and measurement but depends on
publishers, agencies, data providers, identity partners, and open-internet
inventory. AppLovin controls a performance-allocation workflow inside mobile
applications but depends heavily on platform access and attribution. DoubleVerify
controls verification and measurement trust but depends on data quality and
customer budgets. Magnite controls sell-side inventory access but carries
publisher, supply-cost, and working-capital risk. Roku controls a consumer
interface but carries content, hardware, and advertising-cycle burdens.

The thesis weakens if gross spend grows while take rate, revenue, or cash per
share falls; receivables grow faster than collections; payables timing supports
cash; identity match and measurement quality deteriorate; CTV or retail-media
growth depends on concentrated partners; AI lowers pricing or differentiation;
customer retention weakens; stock compensation and repurchases fail to improve
cash per diluted share; or closed platforms take more budget and inventory.

The next filings should provide spend by channel, take rate, partner payments,
receivable aging, payable timing, customer concentration, retention, advertiser
return, identity and measurement quality, AI and hosting cost, fraud and
compliance spending, incentive metrics, diluted shares, and repurchase prices.
