# First Principles: Security Control, Recurring Risk, and Owner Cash

Date: `2026-09-14`

## The question

Cybersecurity is often discussed as a software-growth category. The more useful
question is narrower: what exactly does the customer keep paying for, what
costs are required to keep that service reliable, and who bears the cost when
the security promise fails?

The cohort includes CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Zscaler, Fortinet, and
F5. They do not have identical products. CrowdStrike emphasizes endpoint,
identity, cloud, data, and AI-security telemetry and response. Palo Alto
Networks combines network, cloud, security-operations, and platform products.
Zscaler controls cloud-delivered access and policy enforcement. Fortinet has a
larger hardware and appliance component. F5 combines application delivery,
security, and services.

## 1. Define the control point

A security control point is the software or equipment used to observe activity,
decide whether it is permitted, and record or act on a security event.

Its value comes from five measurable forms of dependency:

1. deployment across many users, devices, applications, or locations;
2. stored data and detection history that would be costly to recreate;
3. rules and workflows used by security teams during an incident;
4. regulatory or customer evidence that the control is operating; and
5. staff training and operating procedures built around the platform.

These dependencies can support renewal, but the customer still has a reason to
replace the platform when coverage is inadequate, price becomes excessive, or
trust is damaged.

## 2. The revenue-to-cash chain

The security business should be analyzed through this chain:

`security need -> contract -> deployment -> telemetry and policy use -> renewal -> collected cash`

Each step has a separate test.

- A security need does not guarantee a purchase; budgets can be delayed.
- A contract does not guarantee full revenue; it may be discounted or changed.
- Deployment does not guarantee usage; inactive products may not renew.
- Usage does not guarantee margin; data collection and AI processing cost money.
- Renewal does not guarantee owner cash; SBC, support, and research absorb cash.

This is why ARR is useful but incomplete. ARR measures active contracted value
at a point in time. It does not show the cost of supporting that value or the
cash received after customer concessions and working-capital changes.

## 3. What recurring revenue means in this cohort

Recurring revenue is revenue expected to repeat because the customer continues
to use a contract, subscription, or support service. It is higher quality when:

- customers renew without unusually large discounts;
- customers expand because the product is used more deeply;
- gross margin remains stable after cloud and data costs;
- the company spends less to acquire each dollar of new contract value; and
- owner cash per diluted share rises with the recurring base.

It is lower quality when reported growth depends on prepayments, one-time
incident work, acquisitions, hardware refreshes, or a rising stock award bill.

## 4. The burden stack

Security companies do not carry the same burden as telecom or data-center
owners, but they carry a distinct set of obligations:

| Burden | Why it matters | Filing evidence to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud and data processing | More telemetry and AI usage can raise cost with revenue | Hosting cost, gross margin, usage disclosures |
| Specialized labor | Detection, research, support, and incident response require scarce staff | R&D and support expense, headcount, productivity |
| Product quality | A defect can create remediation, legal, insurance, and renewal costs | Incident charges, reserves, claims, customer credits |
| Trust and compliance | Public-sector and regulated customers require evidence and certifications | Authorizations, audit costs, customer concentration |
| SBC and dilution | Employees may be paid through shares, reducing value per existing share | SBC, diluted shares, repurchases |
| Deferred revenue | Prepayment creates future service obligations | Deferred revenue, billings, cash conversion |
| Platform expansion | More modules can increase sales and integration cost | Net retention, module penetration, sales efficiency |

The cleanest company is not the one with the lowest capex. It is the one that
converts a trusted control point into cash after these obligations are counted.

## 5. CrowdStrike and Palo Alto: two forms of consolidation

CrowdStrike's evidence shows fiscal 2026 revenue of `$4.81B`, subscription
revenue of `$4.56B`, operating cash flow of `$1.61B`, and free cash flow of
`$1.24B`. Its platform argument is broadening from endpoint protection to
identity, cloud, data, and AI-security control.

Palo Alto's packet shows fiscal 2025 revenue of `$9.2215B`, operating cash flow
of `$3.716B`, and non-GAAP free cash flow of `$3.4698B`. Its platform argument
is consolidation across network, cloud, security operations, identity, and
data-security products.

The comparison is not a simple growth ranking. The key questions are:

- Which platform retains customers after a trust event?
- Which platform adds modules without making deployment and support harder?
- Which platform converts contracted value into GAAP profit and owner cash?
- Which company carries more hardware, services, acquisition, and integration
  burden?

## 6. Zscaler and Fortinet: different delivery and capital profiles

Zscaler delivers cloud-based access and policy control. Its packet records
deferred revenue of `$2.351B` in FY2026 Q1, `$2.355B` in Q2, and `$2.477B` in
Q3, while ARR increased from `$3.204B` to `$3.525B`. The correct interpretation is
that customers are pre-funding a recurring access service. The next test is
whether that prepayment converts into durable gross profit and owner cash.

Fortinet has a more hardware-linked model. Hardware shipments, support, and
security subscriptions should be separated because equipment revenue can create
different margins, replacement timing, inventory risk, and working-capital
needs from software subscriptions.

The delivery model changes the burden. Cloud delivery reduces customer hardware
deployment but increases dependence on data-center, bandwidth, and processing
costs. Appliance delivery creates inventory, channel, warranty, and refresh
risk. Neither model is automatically superior.

## 7. The multi-year cash test

The company dossiers support a comparable diagnostic: operating cash less
property and equipment, capitalized internal-use software, and cash
acquisitions. It is not distributable owner cash because it still excludes SBC,
required research and support, debt claims, and some strategic investments. It
is useful because it exposes acquisition timing and the difference between
reported cash generation and cash left after expansion.

| Company | Earlier year | Middle year | Latest annual year | What the pattern says |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Palo Alto Networks | `$2.427B` | `$2.490B` | `$2.415B` | Operating cash remained strong, but FY2025 acquisitions absorbed most of the growth in operating cash. |
| CrowdStrike | `$690M` | `$758M` | `$859M` | The residual improved, while SBC rose from `$649M` to `$1.097B`; per-share economics require a dilution test. |
| Zscaler | `$318M` | `$210M` | `$726M` | Acquisition spending depressed 2024; the 2025 recovery should not be treated as a permanent margin change. |
| F5 | `$564M` | `$729M` | `$735M` | Strong residual cash coexists with systems, services, acquisition, cyber-remediation, and SBC obligations. |

This table changes the research question. The issue is not whether recurring
security revenue exists. It is whether the recurring control point produces
cash after the company pays to expand it, integrates acquisitions, maintains
trust, and compensates the specialized workforce.

## 8. The forensic test

For each company, build the same bridge:

`reported operating cash -> recurring support and research needs -> maintenance capital -> acquisitions -> SBC and dilution -> owner cash per diluted share`

Then inspect four relationships:

1. revenue growth versus receivables and deferred revenue;
2. ARR or billings growth versus gross margin and sales efficiency;
3. adjusted earnings growth versus GAAP earnings and recurring exclusions; and
4. platform expansion versus customer support, cloud, and research cost.

The warning pattern is growth in contracts without corresponding growth in
collected cash, gross profit, or owner cash per diluted share.

## 9. First-principles valuation

Valuation begins with the cash that a common shareholder can receive after the
business remains competitive. The minimum cases should vary:

- renewal and net expansion;
- pricing and discounting;
- gross margin after cloud and AI costs;
- sales and research investment;
- incident and remediation cost;
- SBC and diluted share count; and
- the cash multiple or cash yield applied to the normalized result.

A high recurring-revenue multiple is justified only if recurring revenue stays
valuable after support, processing, research, dilution, and trust-recovery
costs. A high ARR growth rate by itself is not enough.

## 10. Filing-based falsifiers

The security-control thesis weakens if:

- retention declines after a security incident;
- module expansion requires rising discounts or sales cost;
- gross margin declines as telemetry and AI usage increase;
- deferred revenue rises while revenue recognition and collections weaken;
- receivables grow faster than billings;
- adjusted exclusions recur every year;
- SBC and dilution absorb the increase in operating cash; or
- customers use the platform but do not renew or expand it.

The most important future evidence is not another statement that security is
important. It is proof that customers continue to renew, use, expand, and pay
for the control point after all reliability and capital costs are included.

## Conclusion

Security software is a control business. The customer pays for visibility,
policy enforcement, response, and evidence that systems can be trusted. The
economic advantage can be durable because the platform becomes part of the
customer's operating procedures and security history.

The burden is also durable. The provider must maintain detection quality,
support customers during failures, fund research, process growing data volumes,
and compensate specialized employees. The right comparison therefore ranks
security companies by cash per diluted share after trust and platform costs,
not by ARR or adjusted margin alone.
