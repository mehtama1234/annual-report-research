# Software control and AI capital conversion

Software companies are often described as asset-light because a new customer can be added without constructing a new store or factory. That description becomes incomplete when the software company also owns the cloud capacity needed to run the product. Microsoft shows the full transition clearly: enterprise workflow control can produce high-margin recurring revenue, while AI demand turns part of the model into a capital-intensive infrastructure business.

## The first-principles chain

The economic chain has six separate questions:

1. Does the company control a recurring customer relationship?
2. Does the customer pay for a durable workflow rather than a temporary feature?
3. Does the company own or control the infrastructure required to deliver the service?
4. What is the cost of serving one more unit of usage?
5. What additional capital is needed to support growth and replace old capacity?
6. After stock compensation and dilution, how much cash belongs to each owner?

Traditional subscription software often has a favorable answer to questions one through four and a modest answer to question five. AI cloud changes question five. The company may need servers, networking, data centers, power contracts, specialized chips, engineering labor, and model-serving capacity before customer demand becomes recurring cash.

## Why reported growth can mislead

Revenue growth can be genuine while owner economics weaken. A cloud company can sell more usage while spending even more on capacity. A software company can report higher cash flow because customers prepay, while the company still owes future service. Net income can rise because an investment increased in value, while the operating business did not generate that gain.

The required bridge is:

`contracted demand -> recognized revenue -> cash collection -> serving cost -> replacement and growth capital -> dilution -> cash per share`

Every step needs evidence. RPO, deferred revenue, annual recurring revenue, paid seats, and bookings are useful measures of demand visibility. None is a substitute for renewal, gross margin after serving cost, capital intensity, or cash per diluted share.

## Control points versus infrastructure burdens

Microsoft, Adobe, and similar companies can control workflow, identity, file formats, data, distribution, and procurement. Those control points support pricing, retention, and cross-selling. Equinix, Digital Realty, Vertiv, and other infrastructure companies carry more direct exposure to power, equipment, construction, leases, and financing. Microsoft increasingly carries both kinds of exposure.

This distinction helps compare companies across sectors. A software platform may earn a higher return on revenue because it owns the customer relationship and distributes software efficiently. A cloud provider may earn a lower margin on incremental revenue because it supplies physical capacity. A semiconductor or equipment supplier may capture value at a technical bottleneck without funding the entire data-center build. The investor should ask which company controls the scarce input and which company must finance the capacity expansion.

## The practical reading rule

For every AI-era software company, read the income statement and cash-flow statement together. Then reconcile:

- recurring revenue to renewal and usage;
- RPO or bookings to actual revenue;
- operating cash flow to working-capital timing;
- depreciation to replacement spending;
- capital additions to future revenue and utilization;
- stock compensation to diluted shares and repurchases;
- investment gains to recurring operating income.

The durable winner is not simply the company with the fastest AI revenue growth. It is the company that converts control of a valuable workflow into rising cash per share after the full cost of serving the workload and maintaining the capacity.

## Questions that can falsify the thesis

- Does the customer pay more, or is existing software being repackaged?
- Does AI usage raise gross profit after inference and support cost?
- Does new capacity generate enough cash to justify its construction and replacement cost?
- Does contracted backlog convert on schedule and at expected margin?
- Are stock awards creating durable productivity or merely transferring value from existing owners?
- Does the company retain pricing power when models become cheaper and more widely available?

These questions keep the analysis technical without requiring specialist language. They also prevent a high-growth software narrative from hiding the capital and dilution required to deliver it.

## Definitions that separate software economics from infrastructure economics

**Recurring revenue** is revenue expected to repeat under a contract or through
continued usage. It is not automatically recurring owner cash: the company
must still pay for support, cloud capacity, energy, security, and replacement
equipment.

**Remaining performance obligation, or RPO,** is contracted revenue that has
not yet been recognized. It gives information about customer commitments, but
some RPO may depend on future usage, capacity delivery, renewal, or customer
acceptance. The investor must test how much becomes revenue and cash, and at
what margin.

**Owner cash** is the cash available to common owners after maintaining the
existing service, funding unavoidable working capital, replacing productive
capacity, and meeting debt, lease, and contractual obligations. A practical
screen for an AI cloud business is:

Start with reported operating cash flow and subtract cash maintenance, replacement and capacity investment outside that measure. Operating working-capital movements and ordinary support costs already included in operating cash flow must not be subtracted again. Reconcile lease and debt payments to the statement of cash flows, and model future capacity commitments by payment date rather than deducting every contractual amount from one historical year. Show dilution separately using one consistent share-count or cash-cost treatment.

The screen is not a company-reported metric. It is a way to prevent operating
cash flow, customer prepayments, or adjusted free cash flow from being treated
as distributable without considering the obligations attached to them.

## The complete software-to-cash chain

| Stage | What to establish | Risk that remains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer adoption | A customer pays for a workflow, not only a trial or feature | Churn, substitution, or budget reprioritization |
| Contract | Term, price, renewal, usage, and cancellation rights are understood | Reported RPO may not be fixed cash revenue |
| Service delivery | Product performs at the promised reliability and security | Support labor, outages, and compliance cost |
| Usage expansion | More usage increases gross profit after inference and serving cost | Usage can grow faster than contribution margin |
| Capacity build | Servers, facilities, power, and networks are added at an acceptable return | Capital can be committed before demand is proven |
| Collection | Invoices and prepayments convert into cash | Receivables, concessions, and future service obligations |
| Renewal | Customer stays and expands at acceptable economics | Lower prices or cheaper competing models |
| Replacement | Capacity is renewed without destroying returns | Depreciation may understate economic replacement cost |

This chain explains why a software company can retain strong customer control
while its incremental economics become more capital-intensive. The customer
relationship and the delivery infrastructure should be analyzed separately.

## Cash conversion and possible presentation distortions

Three adjustments deserve particular attention:

1. **Customer prepayments.** Prepayment increases current operating cash, but
   the company owes future service. It is a financing benefit and a service
   obligation, not current-period profit.
2. **Capitalized capacity.** Property additions are cash paid today for future
   service. Depreciation is an accounting allocation and may not equal the cash
   needed to replace servers, facilities, power systems, and networking at
   current prices.
3. **Stock compensation and investment gains.** Stock compensation is
   non-cash when recorded but dilutes owners or requires repurchases. Investment
   gains may be economically real but are not recurring cash generated by the
   software or cloud operation.

The reconciliation should therefore show reported profit, operating cash,
working-capital changes, customer prepayments, capital additions,
depreciation, acquisitions, investment gains, stock compensation, debt,
leases, repurchases, and diluted shares. A high adjusted cash number is weak
evidence if the adjustment excludes a growing capital or financing exposure.

## Management incentives and capital allocation

The proxy statement should identify whether management is rewarded for revenue,
RPO, cloud growth, adjusted operating income, adjusted free cash flow, return
on invested capital, market value, or cash per diluted share. The metric's
definition matters as much as its name.

For every performance measure, record its weights, threshold/target/maximum,
peer adjustments, and exclusions for acquisitions, restructuring, stock
compensation, customer prepayments, and infrastructure investment. Then test
whether the measure rewards a durable improvement in common-owner cash or only
the expansion of contracted demand and installed capacity.

An incentive based on RPO can encourage large contracts before margin and
capacity returns are visible. An incentive based on adjusted free cash flow
can reward exclusions for capital or working-capital investment. An incentive
based on cash per diluted share and return on invested capital gives more
weight to collections, capacity utilization, replacement spending, and
dilution. The actual filings must determine which pattern applies.

## Lyn Alden and Damodaran application

The Alden lens asks whether the AI buildout can be funded through a full
liquidity cycle. Strong enterprise software subscriptions can support funding,
but cloud capacity adds exposure to rates, power, chips, construction,
equipment obsolescence, and customer capital budgets. The company may have
excellent liquidity and still earn a poor return on new capacity.

The Damodaran lens requires separate narratives and numbers for mature
subscription software, cloud infrastructure, AI applications, professional
services, and financing. For each, specify retention, price, usage, gross
margin, support cost, capital intensity, useful life, tax, debt, leases, and
diluted shares. Do not apply a software multiple to infrastructure cash flows
without modeling the infrastructure reinvestment required to keep the service
competitive.

The valuation bridge is:

`customer control -> contracted revenue -> recognized revenue -> serving margin -> capacity investment -> replacement cash -> owner cash per diluted share`

The current value is justified only if the future owner-cash path—not merely
AI adoption, RPO, or revenue growth—supports it.

## Cohort comparison

### Three software models and three cash tests

ServiceNow, Cloudflare, and Snowflake all sell software that can become part of
a customer's operating control, but they do not have the same cost structure.
ServiceNow records and routes organizational work. Cloudflare handles traffic,
access, application protection, and edge execution. Snowflake governs data
storage, processing, sharing, and use across clouds and applications.

Their FY2025/FY2026 evidence shows why the denominator must stay specific:

| Company | Reported evidence | Main owner-cash question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ServiceNow | FY2025 operating cash flow `$5.444B`, property purchases `$868M`, stock compensation `$1.955B` | Do workflow renewal, AI ACV, and module expansion remain attractive after cloud services, deferred commissions, acquisitions, and dilution? |
| Cloudflare | FY2025 operating cash flow `$603M`, free cash flow `$261M`, stock compensation `$451M`, and GAAP operating loss `$207M` | Can distributed-network control produce GAAP leverage after bandwidth, servers, technical labor, restructuring, and equity compensation? |
| Snowflake | FY2026 product revenue `$4.472B`, operating cash flow `$1.222B`, free cash flow `$1.120B`, stock compensation `$1.600B` | Does consumption and prepaid capacity produce durable margin after third-party cloud, GPU, inference, support, capitalized software, and dilution? |

ServiceNow currently has the lightest direct infrastructure burden of the three,
but its sales commissions, acquisitions, and stock compensation remain real
claims. Cloudflare controls a broad edge interface, but it must operate a
distributed network even when growth slows. Snowflake avoids owning the full
data-center balance sheet, but public-cloud providers claim part of gross profit
and customers can optimize usage. The comparison is useful only when each
business is charged for the costs it actually funds.

Microsoft and Oracle control enterprise identity, data, applications, and
procurement relationships, which can support retention and cross-selling.
Adobe controls important creative workflows but generally carries less direct
physical-capacity burden. Amazon, Microsoft, and Oracle own more cloud
capacity, so they capture infrastructure revenue but fund more equipment,
power, facilities, and depreciation. Equinix and Digital Realty monetize
powered space and interconnection with large construction and financing needs.
Vertiv and other equipment suppliers receive revenue sooner but carry
manufacturing, inventory, warranty, and project-execution burdens.

The cleaner economics are in software that retains the workflow while serving
additional usage at high incremental margin. The heavier burden is in the
capacity layer when growth requires large up-front capital, debt, customer
prepayments, or replacement spending before utilization is established.

Samsara adds a physical-operations version of the software-control model. It
connects fleets, equipment, sites, and frontline workers, then turns telemetry
into safety, maintenance, dispatch, compliance, and utilization workflows. The
company reported FY2026 revenue of `$1.619B`, ending ARR of `$1.890B`, operating
cash flow of `$236.2M`, and free cash flow of `$207.4M`. Its burden is lighter
than an asset owner but higher than pure digital software because connected
devices, cellular service, cloud processing, implementation, warranty, and
customer ROI remain part of the delivery chain. Operational AI should be
valued only after the customer productivity and incremental gross-profit test
is visible.

## Filing-based falsifiers

The software-control thesis weakens if RPO grows faster than revenue and cash
for several periods; customer prepayments rise while future service costs and
capacity obligations rise faster; cloud growth remains high while margin and
cash after capital decline; depreciation and replacement capital exceed the
assumed economic return; Copilot or AI usage expands without retention or
incremental gross profit; stock compensation and repurchases fail to improve
cash per diluted share; or cheaper models reduce pricing before utilization
improves.

The next evidence set should include RPO conversion, renewal and usage data,
cloud gross-margin detail, capital additions by use, depreciation and useful
lives, power and lease commitments, customer prepayment terms, debt and
interest cost, investment gains, stock compensation, incentive metrics, and
diluted-share change.

## Risks, accounting tests, and what to check next

Test RPO and deferred revenue against delivered revenue, renewal, usage,
implementation work, support cost, and cash. Separate customer prepayments
from profit and ask whether the related capacity, power, and replacement
obligations are funded. Reconcile adjusted free cash flow for capitalized
software, acquisitions, stock compensation, and dilution. The next filing
should show whether AI usage improves retention, price, gross profit, and cash
per diluted share after cloud, infrastructure, sales, and service costs.
