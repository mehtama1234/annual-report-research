# Software, process control, and physical compute: three kinds of technology durability

## The first principle

Technology companies do not all benefit from digitization in the same way. Some own a software workflow, some own the process-control tool that protects a customer’s yield, some own the network or facility where computation occurs, and some sell the power and cooling equipment that makes the facility usable.

The common chain is:

`customer problem -> technical control point -> switching cost or qualification -> recurring revenue -> R&D / support / capex -> owner cash`

The investment task is to identify what the customer cannot easily remove, what the company must continuously reinvest to preserve, and whether the apparent software or infrastructure margin survives stock compensation, acquisitions, working capital, and obsolescence.

## Three forms of technology control

### 1. Workflow and network control: Adobe, F5, Motorola Solutions, and S&P Global

Adobe adds the subscription-workflow case. Its creative, document, and
customer-experience products are embedded in customer work, but annual recurring
revenue is still a management metric rather than proof of permanent demand. FY2025
operating cash flow was about $10.03B and headline free cash flow about $9.85B,
but stock compensation was about $1.94B. The decisive test is cash per diluted
share after buybacks, not only ARR growth. AI-first ARR and RPO can show customer
interest while leaving open the cost of inference, support, storage, and product
development.

F5 controls application delivery, security, traffic management, and associated service workflows. Motorola controls mission-critical public-safety communication, video, evidence, and managed workflows. S&P Global controls trusted reference points—ratings, benchmarks, indices, and market data—that customers and regulators use to make decisions.

These businesses can be durable because the cost of failure and switching is high. But the control points differ. F5 must retain security trust and support talent while customers consolidate vendors. Motorola must integrate acquisitions and preserve public-sector relationships. S&P Global depends on benchmark centrality, issuance activity, subscriptions, and regulatory legitimacy.

Their recurring claims must be tested against actual cash. Adobe’s ARR rose from
$25.20B in Q4 FY2025 to $27.10B in Q2 FY2026, but the model must reconcile that
growth to renewal, usage, gross margin after AI costs, R&D, SBC, and diluted shares.
F5’s FY2025 operating cash was about $950M, but after capex and acquisitions its screen was approximately $735M, before economic SBC and security reinvestment. Motorola’s FY2025 OCF less PP&E was about $2.572B, but large acquisitions and $293M of SBC make mature cash different from platform-expansion cash. S&P Global’s OCF less capex/acquisitions was about $3.433B, but With Intelligence integration, restructuring, ratings cycles, and asset-linked fees complicate the recurring denominator.

### 2. Process-control and yield protection: KLA

KLA controls a narrow but powerful interface in semiconductor manufacturing: inspection, metrology, and process control that reduce defects and protect yield. Customers do not remove a qualified tool casually because the cost is not just purchase price; it is lost yield, process qualification, and production risk.

The moat is therefore technical qualification and customer dependence, not generic AI demand. KLA’s FY2026 operating cash was about $4.143B against $376M of capex, leaving $3.767B before R&D, service infrastructure, SBC, buybacks, and cyclical normalization. The company is exposed to customer fab budgets, export controls, customer concentration, and semiconductor-capex cycles. “Hard to cut” does not mean immune to a downcycle.

### 3. Materials-engineering equipment: Applied Materials

Applied Materials controls a broader set of qualified manufacturing steps:
deposition, etch, thermal processing, planarization, packaging, and related
equipment. Its value depends on the tool being accepted into a customer's
process and producing the required yield and throughput. That creates a
technical control point, but the purchase is still part of a fab-capital cycle.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $28.368B, operating cash flow was $7.958B, and capex
was $2.260B. In the first nine months of fiscal 2026, Semiconductor Systems
revenue grew 10% and Applied Global Services revenue grew 18%, while contract
liabilities increased by $698M and capex was $1.988B. The first-principles
test is therefore not only whether the equipment is necessary. It is whether
the installed-base service layer, customer advances, and process qualification
produce durable owner cash after R&D, facility investment, inventory, China
restrictions, customer concentration, and technology transitions.

### 4. Lithography control: ASML

ASML controls the lithography system layer used to print increasingly complex
patterns on semiconductor wafers. The control point is narrower than generic
semiconductor equipment because the system must combine optics, light source,
motion, software, contamination control, and process qualification. The
customer relationship is therefore technical and operational, not only
commercial.

ASML's 2025 revenue was €32.667B, US-GAAP operating cash flow was €12.659B,
and cash after property, plant, equipment, and intangible purchases was about
€11.027B. Its backlog was €38.8B and installed-base-management sales were
€8.193B. The burden is that the largest customer represented 23.9% of sales,
the two largest represented 38.0%, China represented 29.1%, and R&D was
€4.699B. The test is whether lithography scarcity, upgrades, and service cash
remain durable after fab-capex cycles, export controls, customer delays, and
the investment required to preserve the technology lead.

### 5. Deposition and etch control: Lam Research

Lam Research controls important deposition, etch, clean, packaging, and
customer-support steps. Its value rises when memory and logic structures become
more complex and require more process steps, but it remains tied to customer
fab budgets and technology transitions.

Fiscal 2026 revenue was $23.233B, operating cash flow was $5.858B, and
capital/investing cash use was about $922M. Customer-support revenue was
$8.347B, or nearly 36% of sales. The burden was visible in the cash bridge:
operating cash declined from $6.173B in fiscal 2025, deferred revenue declined
to $2.43B, China was 34% of revenue, and four customers represented 16%, 15%,
12%, and 12%. The first-principles test is whether process intensity and
installed-base support produce durable owner cash after acceptance timing,
working capital, R&D, facilities, China restrictions, and customer
concentration.

### 6. Post-fabrication validation: Teradyne

Teradyne controls a later step in the semiconductor chain: testing whether
chips, memory, boards, and systems meet performance and reliability
requirements before deployment. AI increases the number of high-value devices
and the complexity of compute, memory, and high-speed interconnect, which can
increase test content.

The economics are less clean than a pure lithography or process-control
position because Teradyne also owns Product Test and Robotics. Fiscal 2025
revenue was $3.190B, operating cash flow was $674M, PP&E spending was $224M,
and company-defined free cash flow was about $450M. Q2 2026 Semiconductor Test
revenue was $1.122B of $1.329B total revenue. The test is whether validation
intensity creates durable cash after semiconductor cycles, receivables,
customer advances, acquisitions, R&D, robotics losses, SBC, and valuation.

### 7. Measurement and assurance workflows: Keysight

Keysight controls a broader validation workflow across design simulation,
communications, networking, aerospace and defense, automotive, semiconductor,
and industrial electronics. The customer pays to establish that signals,
systems, and networks meet performance and reliability requirements before
deployment and during operation.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $5.375B, operating cash flow was $1.409B, and PP&E
spending was $128M. The simple cash-after-PP&E screen was about $1.281B, but
the company also spent $1.564B to acquire Spirent, recorded $176M of SBC, and
carried $884M of deferred revenue. The test is whether software, service,
standards, and installed workflows make measurement spending durable after
electronics cycles, acquisition integration, R&D, and customer budget timing.

### 8. Physical compute capacity: Equinix, Digital Realty, and Vertiv

Equinix controls interconnection density, network adjacency, and customer IT-estate placement. Digital Realty controls powered land, data-center capacity, leasing, and hyperscale development. Vertiv controls the integrated power-and-cooling architecture inside high-density compute facilities.

These are valuable physical control points because power, latency, connectivity, and deployment time matter. But physical capacity requires continuous capital. Equinix’s FY2025 OCF less recurring capex was approximately $3.627B, while total property/equipment capex plus acquisitions produced a negative broad investment bridge. Digital Realty’s economics depend on development capex, partner contributions, lease-up, financing, and retained AFFO. Vertiv’s OCF less capex/acquisitions was about $709M, with customer concentration, warranty, working capital, and acquisition integration still unresolved.

The conceptual error is to value all three as software companies. They may have recurring contracts, but they also own or depend on buildings, power, cooling, construction, leases, utilities, debt, and customer-specific capacity.

## Marginal economics versus reinvestment economics

Software and data infrastructure can appear to scale at high margins, but the relevant question is what must be added for the next dollar of revenue:

- F5: engineers, support, security remediation, cloud hosting, and product development.
- Motorola: acquisition capital, public-sector procurement, field deployment, cybersecurity, and SBC.
- KLA: R&D, service organization, inventory, customer qualification, and export-control compliance.
- Applied Materials: R&D, process qualification, service spares, inventory, facility capex, contract liabilities, customer concentration, and export-control compliance.
- ASML: EUV/DUV productivity, customer qualification, backlog conversion, installed-base upgrades, supplier concentration, R&D, export controls, and customer concentration.
- Lam Research: deposition/etch qualification, support revenue, chamber utilization, deferred revenue, Japan-held inventory, customer concentration, China exposure, R&D, and facility investment.
- Teradyne: semiconductor-test intensity, compute/memory mix, receivables, customer advances, Product Test and Robotics diversification, acquisitions, R&D, SBC, and diluted shares.
- Keysight: measurement workflow adoption, CSG/EISG mix, deferred revenue, Spirent integration, R&D, capitalized software, SBC, and customer design-cycle timing.
- Equinix/Digital Realty: power, land, construction, leasing, debt, and recurring capex.
- Vertiv: factory capacity, components, installation, warranty, inventory, and integration.

The best technology economics arise where customer dependence increases faster than required reinvestment. A high gross margin is less important than incremental return on the full system required to preserve the control point.

## The forensic bridge

Every technology and physical-compute company should be tested through:

`customer adoption or order -> deployment / qualification -> recurring use -> billing -> collection -> R&D, support, capex, SBC, and replacement -> owner cash`

Company-specific weak links:

- Adobe: ARR, renewal, AI monetization, gross margin after compute, SBC, buybacks, and diluted shares.
- F5: security trust, renewal, support burden, SBC, and acquisition payback.
- Motorola: organic software/services growth versus acquired revenue, backlog conversion, integration, SBC, and public-sector budgets.
- S&P Global: recurring subscriptions versus ratings issuance and asset-linked fees, integration, restructuring, and debt.
- KLA: semiconductor capex, customer concentration, export controls, deferred revenue, service mix, and R&D.
- Applied Materials: semiconductor capex, customer concentration, export controls, contract-liability conversion, inventory, service mix, facility capex, and R&D.
- ASML: semiconductor capex, customer concentration, export controls, bookings and backlog conversion, service mix, R&D, supplier capacity, and system acceptance.
- Lam Research: semiconductor capex, customer concentration, export controls, systems/support mix, deferred revenue, acceptance, inventory, R&D, and capacity investment.
- Teradyne: semiconductor and memory cycles, test-system placements, receivables, deferred revenue, acquisitions, robotics margins, R&D, PP&E, SBC, and owner cash per share.
- Keysight: communications and network cycles, software/service attachment, tax and working-capital timing, Spirent returns, R&D, capitalized software, SBC, and cash per share.
- Equinix/Digital Realty: recurring versus growth capex, powered capacity, lease-up, JV/VIE economics, debt, dilution, and energy cost.
- Vertiv: backlog quality, customer advances, inventory, warranty, tariffs, capacity, and acquisition accounting.

The common financial-engineering risk is category confusion: treating deferred revenue as permanent demand, backlog as cash, SBC as free, acquisitions as organic growth, adjusted EBITDA as post-capex cash, or real-estate operating cash as unrestricted corporate cash.

## Lyn Alden lens

Digitization is a long-duration demand force, but technology infrastructure is also a liquidity cycle. Higher rates reduce the present value of long-lived software and real-estate cash flows, slow customer capex, and raise the cost of building capacity. Export controls, energy availability, semiconductor cycles, and public-sector budgets can reshape demand even when the secular trend remains intact.

The durable control points are those where customers cannot tolerate failure and where the company owns a trusted standard, qualified process, dense network, or hard-to-replicate capacity. The fragile parts are those dependent on a short capex surge, perpetual acquisition, or pricing that customers can renegotiate when capacity expands.

## Damodaran lens

Value by lifecycle:

- Software/workflow: recurring revenue, net retention, support cost, SBC, R&D, and competitive displacement.
- Process control: semiconductor cycle, installed-base service, customer concentration, technology obsolescence, and export risk.
- Data-center property: recurring rent, development yield, lease-up, recurring capex, debt, JV economics, and dilution.
- Hardware integration: backlog conversion, gross margin, working capital, warranty, capacity, and acquisition returns.

The narrative must explain reinvestment. If the company grows by buying customers, building facilities, or issuing stock, those uses belong in the valuation bridge even when accounting rules classify them outside operating expense.

## Cohort conclusion

Software workflow, semiconductor process control, and physical compute capacity can all be durable, but they earn durability through different mechanisms. Network and workflow companies rely on trust and switching costs. KLA relies on technical qualification and yield economics. Data-center companies rely on location, power, interconnection, and deployment capacity.

The cleanest economics belong to the control point that remains indispensable through a capex slowdown while requiring limited incremental capital. The most dangerous thesis is “AI growth” without a defined customer control point, cash conversion path, and reinvestment hurdle. Future filings should be read for what converts technical importance into per-share owner cash.
