# Workflow control versus cloud capacity

Enterprise AI creates at least two different software businesses. One sells the system that governs work, records decisions, and routes actions. The other supplies the computing capacity where models and applications run. Both may report recurring contracts and strong growth, but their capital burdens are different.

**Workflow control** means software that records work, assigns responsibility,
controls approval, or preserves an audit trail. **Cloud capacity** means the
servers, storage, networking, facilities, and power used to run applications.
**Owner cash** means cash left for common shareholders after the people,
infrastructure, acquisitions, debt, and dilution required to deliver the service.

## Workflow control

ServiceNow’s value comes from making work observable and routable. The platform records an incident, request, approval, or security action; assigns responsibility; preserves the history; and connects the result to management reporting or compliance. The customer pays for control over an operating process.

The primary reinvestment is product development, security, integration, customer support, sales, and implementation. Physical capital is smaller. This can produce high cash conversion if customers renew and the company does not need proportional labor for every new account.

## Cloud capacity

Oracle and Microsoft add a different layer. They also control enterprise software and data, but they own or finance large amounts of computing capacity. The customer pays for software and infrastructure together or in linked contracts. The provider must fund servers, networking, facilities, power, maintenance, replacement, and technical operations.

The relationship can be more valuable because the provider controls more of the stack. The burden can also be greater because the provider must recover a larger investment before the contract becomes attractive to common owners.

## Who controls the relationship and who carries the burden?

| Participant | Value captured | Main burden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow vendor | Records, approvals, routing, and reporting | R&D, integrations, implementation, support, and equity compensation |
| Cloud provider | Compute, storage, networking, and availability | Servers, power, facilities, depreciation, leases, debt, and utilization |
| Enterprise customer | Better execution and access to computing | Subscription, usage, migration, security, and vendor dependence |
| Chip and equipment supplier | Tools and components required for capacity | Product transitions, manufacturing, supply, and customer-cycle risk |
| Common shareholder | Residual cash from the relationship | Failure, competition, financing, capital spending, and dilution |

Owning more of the stack can increase control and cross-selling, but it also
places more fixed cost on the company. A lighter workflow vendor may have less
physical control while producing more cash per dollar of revenue.

## The comparison that matters

| Question | Workflow-control platform | Cloud-capacity platform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer value | Accepted record and routing of work | Software continuity plus available computing capacity |
| Main recurring metric | Subscription revenue, cRPO, renewal, ACV | Cloud revenue, usage, RPO, capacity utilization |
| Main reinvestment | R&D, security, sales, support, integrations | Servers, data centers, power, networking, R&D, support |
| Main accounting risk | Stock compensation, deferred commissions, RPO conversion, acquisitions | Prepayments, RPO conversion, depreciation, debt, leases, customer-supplied equipment |
| Main thesis breaker | Workflow becomes optional or AI ACV fails to renew | Capacity earns low returns or requires continuing external financing |

The relevant comparison is not which company has higher growth. It is which company converts customer dependence into rising cash per diluted share after the full cost of delivering the service.

## AI monetization requires a second bridge

AI contract value can be reported before the economics are visible. For each company, separate:

`AI contract value -> recognized revenue -> usage and renewal -> serving cost -> incremental product and infrastructure investment -> cash per share`

For ServiceNow, the important questions are whether AI ACV is new budget, whether AI usage raises cloud cost, and whether the platform becomes more central to the customer’s processes. For Oracle and Microsoft, add capacity utilization, depreciation, power, financing, and equipment ownership.

## Earnings-quality and forensic questions

1. Reconcile subscription revenue, ACV, RPO, deferred revenue, and cash
   collection. Contracted value is not completed profit.
2. For workflow software, include implementation, support, integrations, sales,
   deferred commissions, R&D, acquisitions, and stock compensation.
3. For cloud providers, include servers, power, facilities, depreciation, leases,
   maintenance, model-serving cost, financing, and equipment replacement.
4. Separate customer prepayments from cash earned. Prepayment improves liquidity
   but creates future service obligations.
5. Test whether AI revenue is incremental budget, a shift from existing products,
   or usage with low contribution after compute cost.
6. Compare stock compensation with repurchases, diluted shares, and cash per
   share. Adjusted earnings can rise while the common-owner claim does not.
7. Read the proxy for incentives tied to ACV, RPO, revenue, adjusted margin,
   GAAP profit, capacity, usage, cash per share, and return on invested capital.
   Contract growth or capacity growth alone can reward uneconomic expansion.

## Lyn Alden lens: software resilience versus physical liquidity

Workflow software may remain embedded when customers reduce discretionary
projects, but new modules and implementations can slow. Cloud providers benefit
from essential computing demand but must fund capacity before utilization and
pricing are fully known. Higher rates, power shortages, chip constraints,
construction delays, and slower enterprise budgets can pressure cloud returns.

The stress case is slower growth, lower usage, higher power and equipment cost,
customer price pressure, and continued security and product investment. The
question is whether the company can fund the obligations without repeated debt or
equity issuance.

## Damodaran lens: translate the AI narrative

The narrative is that AI makes workflow coordination and cloud capacity more
valuable. The model must specify retention, price, usage, RPO conversion, gross
margin after delivery cost, R&D, implementation, capacity utilization,
depreciation, replacement capital, debt, dilution, and terminal margin.

Value workflow software from recurring contribution after people and delivery
cost. Value cloud capacity from cash after maintenance and growth investment,
financing, and utilization risk. A single “AI revenue” multiple hides these
different reinvestment requirements.

## Cohort comparison

| Business type | Cleaner economic feature | Main uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow-control software | Embedded records and approval processes | Integration, implementation, retention, and dilution |
| Cloud-capacity provider | Direct participation in computing demand | Servers, power, depreciation, debt, and utilization |
| Database/application provider | Installed enterprise data and processes | Migration, cloud transition, and infrastructure expansion |
| Chip or system supplier | Design-in to the capacity buildout | Customer concentration, product cycles, and supply commitments |

The cleanest economics are not determined by the highest growth rate. They belong
to the layer that retains customer value while requiring the least incremental
capital and delivery cost.

## Filing-based falsifiers

The conclusion should be revised if workflow ACV or RPO fails to renew, cloud
capacity grows without utilization and pricing, customer prepayments mask weak
margin, AI usage increases third-party cost faster than revenue, depreciation and
interest rise faster than operating profit, capital spending remains above
operating cash without a credible return, stock compensation absorbs cash per
share, or a product or security failure weakens retention.

The next filings should show renewal, usage, RPO conversion, gross margin,
implementation cost, cloud cost, capacity utilization, capex, depreciation,
debt, stock compensation, diluted shares, and owner cash.

## What can be learned across the cohort

ServiceNow shows how AI can strengthen a light-asset workflow control point. Oracle shows how database and application control can pull a company into a heavy financing cycle. Microsoft sits between them: a broad software control platform with a very large cloud infrastructure program.

The control point may be durable in all three cases. The owner economics are not interchangeable. A valuation that works for a workflow platform with modest physical reinvestment may fail for a cloud provider whose capital expenditure remains above operating cash flow. Conversely, a cloud provider may justify the burden if utilization and pricing produce returns above the cost of capital.
