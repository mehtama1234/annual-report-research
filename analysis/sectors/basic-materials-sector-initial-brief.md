## Why this first tranche makes sense

The four-name starter set is designed to pressure-test the biggest gap identified in the archive:

- commodity whiplash
- physical-input repricing
- industrial-policy spillovers
- energy and materials linkage to infrastructure demand

The roles are intentionally different:

- `Exxon Mobil` gives the integrated oil and gas system read.
- `Freeport-McMoRan` gives the copper and critical-inputs read.
- `Nucor` gives the steel and industrial-construction demand read.
- `Nutrien` gives the fertilizer and agricultural-inputs read.
- `Dow` gives the chemicals, plastics, coatings, and materials-science conversion layer.

That is enough to start reading the sector as a set of linked input systems rather than as one commodity price trade.

## Early working hypothesis

The likely sector read is that `Basic Materials` in the `2025` annuals and late-`2025` / `2026` quarterlies will not behave like one uniform cyclical bucket.

The more useful frame will probably be:

- oil and gas as supply discipline plus capital-return infrastructure
- copper and mined inputs as electrification and grid-build bottlenecks
- steel as industrial, construction, and manufacturing-throughput exposure
- fertilizer and crop inputs as food-system and farm-economics exposure
- diversified chemicals as the application and conversion layer between feedstocks and finished goods

That would make Basic Materials one of the clearest places where:

- commodity volatility
- tariff and policy effects
- capex cycles
- infrastructure programs
- global supply chain politics

all show up directly in earnings quality.
