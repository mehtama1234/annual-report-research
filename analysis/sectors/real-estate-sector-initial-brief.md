## What is happening

- Demand: the real-estate set is not behaving like one rate-sensitive property basket. Prologis shows durable logistics demand and embedded rent growth, Equinix and Digital Realty show AI-cloud-network demand pulling through digital-infrastructure bookings and backlog, AvalonBay shows steadier housing demand and multifamily NOI, and CBRE plus JLL show a recovering real-estate-services and capital-markets layer tied to leasing, project work, facilities operations, and selective office improvement.
- Cash-flow engines: the six names reveal different business systems. Prologis is a rent-and-development logistics platform. Equinix and Digital Realty are recurring digital-infrastructure landlords with power and interconnection constraints. AvalonBay is a housing and development operator. CBRE and JLL are service and transaction intermediaries whose earnings depend on leasing, sales, mortgage origination, project management, workplace operations, and occupier outsourcing.
- AI and power: this is one of the cleanest physical-world AI reads in the archive. Equinix and Digital Realty tie AI demand directly to bookings, powered capacity, interconnection, and hyperscale deployment. Prologis links the same capital wave to land, logistics adjacency, and data-center power pipeline opportunity. CBRE and JLL show AI demand flowing into services, project work, cooling, telecom, and critical-infrastructure advisory rather than only into rent.
- Office and services: the set does not support a blanket “office is dead” reading. CBRE and JLL show office leasing and capital-markets activity improving in higher-quality and gateway-market settings, but they also show that the service layer around real estate is becoming more important than generic office exposure alone.
- Capital structure: balance-sheet quality, partner capital, and strategic-capital vehicles are central operating variables. Prologis, Digital Realty, Equinix, and CBRE all show that access to capital and capital-partner structures now directly affect how much infrastructure demand can actually be converted into earnings.
- Taxonomy drift: AnnualReports labels are useful for discovery, but the archive already shows they lag reality. Digital Realty is still bucketed there as office real estate even though the operating evidence is clearly data-center infrastructure, and CBRE still carries legacy naming.

## Strongest early signals

1. Real estate is fragmenting by function, not just by asset class.
   Logistics, digital infrastructure, housing, and services are now distinct earnings systems, not small variations of one property cycle.

2. AI is now a physical real-estate force.
   The archive shows AI demand allocating capital into powered sites, interconnection, cooling, project work, and infrastructure-adjacent land, not just into software and semiconductors.

3. The service layer is becoming a first-order real-estate signal.
   CBRE and JLL make it clear that workplace operations, project management, leasing recovery, and capital-markets normalization are part of the core sector read, not background noise.

4. Office recovery is selective, not universal.
   The better evidence points to quality-tier and gateway-market improvement rather than a broad-based office reset.

5. Capital access and private capital matter more than old property shorthand.
   Strong capital structures and partner ecosystems are helping the better operators turn infrastructure demand into actual growth.

## Recurring themes

- Logistics occupancy durability and embedded rent growth
- Digital-infrastructure demand tied to AI, cloud, and network density
- Powered-land scarcity, interconnection, and backlog visibility
- Housing demand resilience and multifamily NOI discipline
- Leasing recovery and capital-markets normalization
- Workplace management, facilities operations, and project execution
- Strategic capital and private-capital flexibility
- Office recovery concentrated in higher-quality assets
- Real-estate taxonomy lag versus business reality
- Infrastructure-rich and service-heavy assets taking share from generic square footage

## Important cross-company contrasts

- Prologis is the cleanest logistics and industrial-property read.
- Equinix and Digital Realty are the clearest digital-infrastructure reads, but Equinix leans harder into interconnection density while Digital Realty shows more hyperscale and private-capital development framing.
- AvalonBay is the cleanest housing-demand and multifamily-development counterweight to the infrastructure-heavy names.
- CBRE and JLL are the clearest proof that real-estate earnings can come from service throughput and transaction recovery as much as from owned-property rent streams.
- Digital Realty also exposes a taxonomy problem in the archive because the operating model is digital infrastructure while the aggregator classification still says office real estate.

## What this likely means

- Real Estate in `2025` and `2026` should be framed as a split sector where logistics, data centers, housing, and the service layer are increasingly distinct.
- The better-performing pockets are tied to scarce infrastructure, power availability, network density, and strategic capital flexibility rather than to generic property beta.
- Real-estate analysis should track who owns the land or buildings, who controls the service and project layer around them, and who has the balance-sheet capacity to fund next-wave infrastructure demand.

## Gaps

- Need a second residential or self-storage name if we want to test whether AvalonBay is representative of housing-linked real estate more broadly.
- Need a second or third pure digital-infrastructure comparison only if we want to pressure-test whether the AI-linked property thesis is concentrated in the current two REIT anchors.
