# CLI 5 Software Control Versus Physical Chokepoints

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

Use this memo to separate the major profit-capture layers now visible inside `CLI 5`.

## Theme identity

- Name: CLI 5 is separating into software-control tolls, software-led infrastructure allocators, physical chokepoints, and burdened access utilities
- Type: technology / industrial / infrastructure
- Related lane: `CLI 5: Connectivity, Telecom, and Technical Infrastructure`
- Date window:
  - annual: `2025`
  - quarter window: latest three reported quarters as of `2026-08-10`

## Core claim

- The bigger-picture pattern is no longer just that “infrastructure matters.” The stronger claim is that the lane is splitting into four different economic classes. Access utilities such as AT&T, Verizon, Comcast, Charter, and even the stronger T-Mobile still carry the heaviest physical and customer-acquisition burden. Software-control names such as ServiceNow, Palo Alto, and CrowdStrike monetize governance, trust, and workflow standardization with lighter capital burden. Software-led infrastructure allocators such as Oracle still monetize deep enterprise control, but they increasingly do so while taking on much larger datacenter and financing burdens. Physical chokepoint names such as Arista, Keysight, KLA, Lam, Applied Materials, and Amphenol monetize the technical bottlenecks that must work before AI-era systems can scale. The best economics increasingly sit in whichever layer customers cannot bypass when complexity, risk, and uptime expectations rise.
- State the bigger-picture pattern in plain language, not just filing language: society is normalizing always-on digital life and enterprise AI at the same time, and that means customers increasingly pay for governability, validation, and precision rather than only for raw connectivity.

## Why this theme matters

- what changed in the real world: AI, cloud, security exposure, and always-on participation are turning digital systems into core social and institutional infrastructure rather than optional productivity upgrades
- what management teams are reacting to: telecom operators keep reacting to capital discipline, churn control, fiber or mobile execution, and household relationship defense, while software-control and chokepoint names react more to rising technical complexity, platform consolidation, workflow governance, process precision, and backlog depth
- why this is not just a one-company issue: the pattern is repeated across telecom operators, enterprise-control vendors, AI-network architecture vendors, validation vendors, and semiconductor-equipment providers
- why this matters for future packet selection: it gives CLI 5 a clearer map for adding the next names by function, not just by market cap, including second software-governance names, second security names, storage or component chokepoints, and physical infrastructure owners such as towers or data centers
- whether the signal looks structural, cyclical, or still provisional: the broad split between burdened access and higher-value control points looks structural; the exact ranking between software-control and physical chokepoints is strong but still needs more testing across additional names

## Repeated operating pattern

- pricing: software-control and narrow physical chokepoints have more room for value-based pricing than the broad access utility layer
- volume / participation / utilization: the access layer carries the broadest usage volume, but the best profit capture often sits with the control or bottleneck layer that does not need to monetize every end user directly
- customer mix: telecom and cable face broad household or general enterprise exposure, while software-control, software-led allocator, and process-chokepoint names often sell to concentrated buyers facing higher failure costs
- distribution: software-control names expand through installed workflows and standardization, while chokepoint hardware, semicap names, and interconnect platforms expand through technical necessity rather than mass consumer reach
- labor / service model: access utilities need field operations, national footprints, and heavier service infrastructure; software-control layers scale through product depth, telemetry depth, and integration; physical chokepoints scale through engineering precision and installed-base service
- capex / balance-sheet consequences: access owners carry the heaviest recurring physical burden, software-control names carry the lightest direct asset burden, software-led infrastructure allocators now sit in a middle zone where software economics are still present but datacenter and financing burden are accelerating, and physical chokepoint names rely on precision engineering, commercialization depth, embedded design wins, and installed-base service rather than national network ownership

## Consumer, cultural, and societal layer

- consumer behavior signal: households increasingly treat connectivity as a baseline utility, while enterprises increasingly treat AI, security, and workflow coordination as baseline operating expectations
- identity / status / lifestyle signal: end users notice the application or experience surface, but the monetization often migrates to hidden control systems that make the experience reliable, fast, governable, and safe
- household-budget or affordability signal: access utilities stay closest to affordability pressure and churn risk because they live nearer the household bill than most control-point and chokepoint vendors
- participation / experience / community signal: always-on digital participation keeps expanding the total system requirement, but not the profit quality of every layer in that system
- franchise / IP / brand monetization signal: software-control names benefit when enterprises standardize around fewer trusted platforms, software-led infrastructure allocators benefit when application lock-in pulls cloud capacity with it, while telecom mostly monetizes necessity and semicap and interconnect names monetize technical indispensability rather than consumer identity

## Industrial and operating pressure layer

- cost pressure: telecom and cable still absorb the broadest maintenance, spectrum, buildout, and customer-service burdens
- supply-chain or procurement pressure: physical chokepoints benefit because customers cannot easily compromise on performance, yield, validation, or architecture quality
- commercialization pressure: Applied adds evidence that value also concentrates where suppliers can shorten the path from research to volume production through co-development and packaging leadership, not just where they own one narrow inspection or etch step
- integration pressure: Amphenol adds evidence that value can also concentrate where a supplier keeps absorbing adjacent interconnect and signal-integrity capabilities fast enough to stay embedded in the next system architecture
- capacity and customer-risk-sharing pressure: Corning adds evidence that some physical chokepoints can keep cleaner economics when customers are willing to underwrite or de-risk capacity growth because the optical and materials layer has become mission-critical to AI and network expansion
- leverage and landlord-burden pressure: American Tower adds evidence that durable physical tolls can still earn strong cash flows and strategic relevance, but infrastructure landlords keep much more financing, policy, and asset-intensity burden than the lighter software or component chokepoints
- tower-pure landlord pressure: SBA adds evidence that the tower-rent layer remains durable even without data-center adjacency, but the price of that purity is more obvious exposure to carrier concentration, land-control duration, refinancing discipline, and the narrow economics of densification-driven site demand
- simplification pressure inside landlord models: Crown Castle adds evidence that communications landlords may improve earnings quality and investor appeal not only by riding demand, but by narrowing the asset mix, cutting operating complexity, and isolating the cleaner tower-rent layer from noisier fiber and small-cell exposure
- mission-critical workflow pressure: Motorola adds evidence that some communications vendors avoid consumer-access commoditization by owning the institutional control layer around public-safety networks, command software, video, and long-duration service relationships where failure costs are high
- optical and transport intensity pressure: Ciena adds evidence that AI-era demand is also enriching the high-speed transport and network-automation layer where cloud providers and service providers need coherent optics, routing, switching, and software control before traffic can be served at scale
- persistent data-retention and storage-density pressure: Western Digital and Seagate add evidence that AI and always-on digital systems do not only increase transport and compute demand; they also enlarge the need for lower-cost persistent storage, archive density, and physical retention capacity after the data is created
- validation and deployment-readiness pressure: Teradyne adds evidence that AI demand also increases the value of test, memory validation, and high-speed equipment assurance after fabrication but before chips, boards, and AI-data-center systems can be deployed reliably
- platform-scale silicon-and-software control pressure: Broadcom adds evidence that AI demand is also concentrating value in the scaled component and software layers that supply custom accelerators, AI networking, storage connectivity, and infrastructure-software ballast across the deployed system
- interconnect-density and infrastructure-chip pressure: Marvell adds evidence that AI demand is also concentrating value in narrower optical, switching, interconnect, and custom-infrastructure-chip layers that hyperscalers need before the broader stack can scale cleanly
- regulation / reimbursement / compliance pressure: software-control names increasingly monetize compliance and governance burden, software-led infrastructure allocators increasingly inherit more security, sovereignty, and funding scrutiny, while telecom remains more directly burdened by infrastructure and consumer-policy oversight
- governed data-context pressure: Snowflake adds evidence that enterprises will also pay for a lighter software layer that turns fragmented data, open formats, permissions, application surfaces, and AI-agent attachment into a governed control plane before higher-level workflows can scale safely
- edge-protection and runtime-placement pressure: Akamai adds evidence that enterprises will also pay for hidden internet-edge layers that keep applications protected, performant, and increasingly executable across more distributed environments as AI and always-on digital workloads expand
- Zscaler adds evidence that a lighter access-and-policy layer can monetize the same trust burden with less platform breadth and less physical burden than the broader security consolidators
- technology or platform dependency: the lane is becoming more dependent on interoperability across software governance, network fabrics, security posture, testing, and semiconductor precision rather than on any one standalone layer
- execution bottleneck: the bottleneck keeps moving toward the least substitutable point in the system, whether that is AI workflow governance, security standardization, network-fabric design, measurement precision, or wafer-process control
- incident and trust pressure: CrowdStrike adds evidence that even the cleanest software-control models can carry sharp remediation and reputational risk after a trust failure, but the recurring economics can still remain strong if the platform retains control of the broader workflow
- labor-capacity and physical-workflow pressure: Samsara adds evidence that a lighter software-control layer can also monetize labor scarcity, compliance burden, utilization pressure, and workflow fragmentation inside physical industries without owning the full infrastructure or industrial asset base

## Investment interpretation

- CLI 5 should now be read as a hierarchy of burden stacks and monetization stacks rather than as one broad technology-and-telecom bucket.
- Software-control names appear to own the cleanest direct economics because they monetize fear of fragmentation, governance failure, and uncontrolled complexity without funding the whole physical base layer.
- CrowdStrike strengthens that claim by showing a particularly clean AI-security and trust-governance model: cash generation stays strong while the product scope expands across endpoint, cloud, identity, data, and AI workflows.
- Zscaler strengthens it further by isolating a narrower and lighter Zero Trust-native toll: the company monetizes trusted access, policy enforcement, AI-agent governance, and data control without needing Oracle-like capacity ownership or the full endpoint breadth of CrowdStrike and Palo Alto.
- Oracle adds a crucial middle category: a software company can still be a control point while becoming more balance-sheet-heavy and infrastructure-heavy if AI demand and enterprise gravity pull it deeper into capacity ownership.
- Snowflake adds a governed-data-control category inside the software layer: it captures value not by owning the raw infrastructure, but by becoming the trusted context, interoperability, and policy surface that enterprise AI applications and agents increasingly need.
- Akamai adds an edge-protection and distributed-runtime category between software control and physical network ownership: it captures value where applications, traffic, APIs, and increasingly cloud workloads need protection and placement before they reach the end user.
- Cloudflare adds a newer edge-native connectivity-cloud category inside that same bridge layer: it captures value where security, routing, developer runtime, agent mediation, and protected internet-facing execution start to converge on one platform.
- Fastly adds a narrower programmable-edge category inside that same bridge layer: it captures value where delivery, security, compute, and observability still live close together, but where the model remains more exposed to usage volatility and customer concentration than the broadest control-plane winners.
- F5 adds an enterprise app/API control category inside that same bridge layer: it captures value where hybrid multicloud applications need delivery, runtime governance, and security standardization, but still carries more systems-and-services ballast than the lightest pure software-control names.
- NetApp adds a governed data-persistence and data-mobility category between software control and physical infrastructure: it captures value where customers need data to remain portable, protected, AI-ready, and usable across on-prem, cloud, and edge environments rather than merely stored cheaply.
- Elastic adds a governed-context and retrieval category between stored data and trusted AI action: it captures value where customers need unstructured data, logs, traces, and security signals to become searchable, observable, and usable enough for AI systems and human operators to act on them.
- Physical chokepoint names can rival or exceed software-control quality when the customer cannot proceed without architecture, validation, inspection, deposition, etch precision, or packaging commercialization speed.
- Amphenol adds a later-stage physical chokepoint category: once the chips and switches exist, dense interconnect, fiber, cable, connector reliability, and signal integrity can also function as high-value tolls.
- Corning adds a broader materials-and-optics category: before dense interconnect can scale economically at the system level, customers still need trusted fiber, cable, optical-connectivity platforms, advanced materials, and manufacturable capacity expansions.
- American Tower adds the landlord-rent category: once those systems are deployed, towers and interconnection-linked sites can still collect a durable toll, but with meaningfully heavier leverage, capex, and policy burden than the cleaner chokepoints upstream.
- SBA sharpens that landlord-rent category by showing a more tower-pure version of the same economics: less adjacency protection than American Tower, but a clearer read on how much spectrum layering, carrier densification, and always-on wireless participation can support AFFO inside a concentrated, leverage-heavy site portfolio.
- Crown Castle adds a portfolio-reset version of the landlord-rent category: management is effectively conceding that the cleaner toll may sit in simpler tower rent rather than in a broader bundle of towers, fiber, and small cells, which is an important clue about where burden and value are really settling inside physical infrastructure.
- Motorola adds a communications-control category between telecom and pure software: it captures value by owning trusted mission-critical workflows, installed-base upgrades, and safety-system interoperability rather than by billing the broad access relationship directly.
- Ciena adds an optical-network-control category between telecom access and pure software: it captures value when AI and cloud force operators to upgrade the transport, photonics, switching, and automation layers that carry and orchestrate the traffic surge.
- Western Digital adds a storage-persistence category inside the physical layer: it captures value after data moves through the network, because AI, surveillance, enterprise logging, and cloud-scale archives all require lower-cost retained capacity rather than only premium compute or transport.
- Seagate strengthens that storage-persistence category by showing that exabyte economics, cloud demand, and areal-density improvements can also create very strong margins and cash generation inside mass-capacity storage, not just a survival case for legacy HDD infrastructure.
- Teradyne adds a test-and-validation category inside the physical layer: it captures value when AI-driven compute, memory, and interconnect complexity make deployment-readiness, memory test, and system assurance more valuable before infrastructure can be trusted at scale.
- Broadcom adds a scaled silicon-and-software control category inside the physical layer: it captures value not only through hardware complexity but through owning the custom accelerator, Ethernet AI switching, connectivity, and infrastructure-software positions that define what the running system is built on.
- Marvell adds a narrower AI-interconnect bridge category inside the physical layer: it captures value where optical density, custom infrastructure silicon, scale-out switching, and scale-up or scale-out interconnect become gating functions for the datacenter itself.
- Samsara adds a physical-operations workflow-control category between generic enterprise SaaS and physical infrastructure ownership: it captures value by turning connected devices, operational data, AI-driven visibility, and automatable workflows into a recurring control layer for fleets, sites, and frontline operations.
- Access utilities remain necessary, and T-Mobile shows the category is not hopeless, but the burden stack remains heavier and the economics remain less clean than in the best control-point layers.
- who bears the burden versus who captures the economics: telecom and cable still bear the broadest physical and relationship-maintenance burden; software-control and narrow chokepoint vendors disproportionately capture the value created by higher system complexity.

## Where the signal appears strongest

- CrowdStrike, where enterprises are visibly paying for AI security, telemetry depth, and trusted platform consolidation rather than for raw capacity
- Zscaler, where enterprises are visibly paying for Zero Trust access control, AI-policy enforcement, and sovereignty-ready trust governance rather than for raw capacity

## Where the signal breaks or weakens

- T-Mobile shows that the access layer can still outperform when execution and customer-relationship control are unusually strong
- Oracle shows that not every software-control winner stays light; some will absorb much heavier capex and financing burden to hold the next profit pool
- Snowflake shows that even a cleaner data-control layer still depends on consumption behavior, customer optimization, hyperscaler relationships, open-format adoption, and sustained willingness from enterprises to keep paying for a middle-layer governance surface
- Akamai shows that even a stronger edge-control layer still depends on outrunning structural delivery pressure, proving newer cloud infrastructure demand, and holding security relevance while carrying more infrastructure reality than the cleanest pure software-control names
- Cloudflare shows that even a newer edge-native platform still depends on balancing broad platform ambition against lower gross margins, restructuring pressure, and the real infrastructure burden of running a large networked runtime layer
- Fastly shows that even a stronger quarter chain and better security mix do not eliminate the risks of usage-linked revenue, delivery exposure, and meaningful top-customer concentration inside a narrower edge platform
- F5 shows that even a strong enterprise-control layer still carries systems revenue, services ballast, cyber and litigation noise, and more infrastructure reality than the cleanest recurring software-governance models
- NetApp shows that even a strong governed-data layer still carries hardware, supplier, and infrastructure reality, and therefore may remain less clean than the very lightest workflow-governance, trust-governance, or observability software names
- Elastic shows that even a lighter governed-context layer still depends on platform consolidation, cloud usage durability, and proving that mid-teen revenue growth can eventually convert into cleaner GAAP operating leverage
- CrowdStrike shows that even a very high-quality trust-control model can still carry incident-remediation and reputational-risk volatility
- Zscaler shows that even a cleaner policy-control model still depends on sustained large-enterprise budget willingness, integration discipline, and durable monetization of AI-security demand beyond near-term enthusiasm
- Applied Materials shows that even a broad physical chokepoint still carries China, export-control, tariff, and cycle-timing exposure
- Amphenol shows that even a strong hidden physical-control platform can depend heavily on acquisition integration, tariff dynamics, and datacom-cycle strength
- Corning shows that even a broader materials-and-optics toll can still depend on trade conditions, customer concentration, and the durability of hyperscaler buildout schedules
- American Tower shows that even a durable communications-infrastructure rent model still depends on leverage discipline, international FX, carrier health, and ongoing policy and siting conditions
- SBA shows that an even purer tower-rent model still depends on leverage discipline, customer concentration, land-control duration, and the durability of carrier spend after major churn events such as EchoStar
- Crown Castle shows that even a simplification story inside physical infrastructure can still depend on restructuring execution, asset-sale timing, customer churn from DISH and Sprint, and whether the market ultimately rewards a narrower asset mix
- Motorola shows that even a stronger communications-control model still depends on public-sector budgets, large-contract timing, acquisition integration, and regulatory scrutiny around surveillance, cybersecurity, AI-enabled safety systems, and government procurement
- Ciena shows that even a stronger optical and transport-control model still depends on cloud and carrier capex timing, supply execution, product-cycle competition, and the durability of AI-networking demand
- Western Digital shows that even an AI-linked storage beneficiary still depends on hardware-cycle discipline, manufacturing execution, pricing stability, customer mix, and the strategic success of the HDD and Flash separation
- Seagate shows that even a stronger mass-capacity storage winner still depends on cloud-customer concentration, roadmap execution, supply discipline, pricing stability, and the durability of current storage economics once the cycle cools
- Teradyne shows that even a stronger test-and-validation winner still depends on concentrated semiconductor and memory demand, AI-cycle durability, and the risk that capital-equipment intensity remains more cyclical than the cleanest software-control layers
- Broadcom shows that even a very strong semiconductor-and-software control platform still depends on hyperscaler concentration, acquisition integration, platform breadth, and the risk that investors misread a hybrid model as cleaner or simpler than it really is
- Marvell shows that even a strong interconnect and infrastructure-chip bridge still depends heavily on hyperscaler architecture choices, design-win durability, and staying aligned with the next optical and switching transition without the same software ballast as the broader hybrid platforms
- Samsara shows that even a lighter physical-operations workflow-control model still depends on implementation success, hardware attachment, industry-specific ROI proof, and sustained enterprise willingness to standardize on one operating layer for messy real-world systems
- Cisco shows that broad incumbents can blur the line between software-control and physical infrastructure because they monetize both installed software layers and hardware architecture
- semicap and networking chokepoints remain more exposed to concentrated customer capex pauses than the cleanest recurring software-governance names

## Counter-signals

- access utilities can still generate very large cash flows and may remain more defensive in some downturns than faster-growing control points
- software-control names still face platform competition and enterprise budget scrutiny even if their burden stack is lighter
- physical chokepoints can look cleaner during strong technology transitions than they do during a deeper capex downcycle
