# Immigration Squeeze Initial Theme Memo

Date baseline: 2026-08-09

Scope:

- BrightView Holdings, Inc.
- ABM Industries, Inc.
- Waste Management, Inc.
- United Parcel Service, Inc.

Collection window:

- `2025` annual reports
- latest three reported quarters in scope as of `2026-08-09`

## Theme

- Name: Immigration friction is increasingly showing up as a seasonal-labor, field-services, and physical-upkeep constraint rather than only as a macro-policy debate
- Type: cross-sector / labor / immigration / physical services

## Core claim

The archive now has enough evidence to map `the-immigration-squeeze` explicitly. BrightView is the direct proof point because its `2025` `10-K` says the company has historically used, and expects to continue using, the U.S. `H-2B` temporary non-immigrant visa program to satisfy part of its seasonal labor needs in certain markets. It employed approximately `1,500` seasonal workers through the program in `2025` and `2,000` in `2024`. That is stronger evidence than the archive previously had from broader labor-intensive operators whose filings showed staffing pressure without an explicit visa-dependent labor disclosure.

## What is happening

- BrightView turns immigration exposure from inference into disclosure.
  The key step here is that the archive no longer has to guess which operators may be immigration-sensitive. BrightView says directly that part of its seasonal labor needs are satisfied through `H-2B` workers.

- Seasonal labor is an operating input, not background politics.
  The BrightView disclosure makes clear that immigration policy touches revenue-producing field capacity in routine commercial maintenance and enhancement work.

- The force shows up most clearly in recurring physical services.
  This is not mainly a software or high-skill office-labor question. It shows up in the ordinary upkeep of campuses, properties, hotels, hospitals, retail sites, and municipal grounds.

- Immigration pressure and labor pressure overlap but are not identical.
  Comfort Systems already mapped the broader labor squeeze through skilled-trades scarcity. BrightView adds a different labor subtype: seasonal and immigration-linked workforce dependence.

## Cross-company read

- BrightView is the archive's strongest direct `the-immigration-squeeze` anchor because the filing discloses real visa-program reliance and worker counts.
- ABM, Waste Management, and UPS remain partial adjacency names rather than primary immigration anchors.
- The new evidence suggests that the immigration theme is most visible where recurring field-service work depends on large seasonal or hard-to-replace labor pools.

## What this appears to mean

- The archive can now map `the-immigration-squeeze` directly rather than leaving it as the one uncovered force.
- Immigration should be analyzed as an operating-capacity input inside recurring physical-service businesses, not only as a policy narrative.
- The distinction between generic labor pressure and immigration-linked labor dependence matters. BrightView is useful because it proves the second category directly.

## Counter-signals

- BrightView is still only one strong direct operator in the archive for this force.
- Weather, fuel, self-insurance, and development-project mix all affect BrightView's earnings, so not every quarterly fluctuation should be attributed to immigration-linked labor dynamics.
- The archive is still light on agriculture, homebuilding, food processing, and other sectors where immigration dependence may show up differently.

## Follow-up questions

- Should the next direct immigration-sensitive operator come from homebuilding, food processing, or agriculture-adjacent services?
- Does another landscape, staffing, or field-services name disclose `H-2B`, migrant-labor, or employment-verification pressure even more explicitly?
- How much of BrightView's current margin volatility is labor-availability related versus weather, project mix, fuel, and self-insurance noise?
