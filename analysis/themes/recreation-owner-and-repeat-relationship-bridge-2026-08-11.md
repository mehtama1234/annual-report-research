## The question

Was recreation still missing a real owner comparison set and a real specialty / repeat-relationship cluster?

The short answer is:

- less than the checklist implied
- the hotel-owner side is now a real three-model comparison
- the specialty and repeat-relationship side is also wider than one `Ulta` packet

That does not mean the lane is perfect.

It means the remaining gap is narrower and more specific than "owner set missing" or "specialty retail still absent."

## Claim 1: the hospitality owner side is now a real comparison set, not one example plus a wish list

Host, Apple Hospitality, and Sunstone now make that visible.

### Concrete evidence

| Company | Exact evidence | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Host | Full-year `2025` comparable hotel RevPAR increased `3.8%`; Adjusted EBITDAre was `$1,757M`; `Q2 2026` comparable hotel RevPAR was `$251.53`, up `7.0%`. | Host is the scale-heavy upper-upscale and luxury owner model. |
| Apple Hospitality | `Q2 2026` Comparable Hotels RevPAR was `$136.17`, up `5.3%`; occupancy rose to `80.1%`. | Apple gives the broader, more standardized select-service and rooms-focused hotel-owner version. |
| Apple Hospitality | Full-year `2025` RevPAR fell `1.6%`, and management highlighted policy uncertainty plus weaker government travel. | Apple shows that the ownership layer can be more exposed to ordinary travel softness than the asset-light brand controllers above it. |
| Sunstone | Full-year `2025` Total Portfolio RevPAR increased `3.8%` to `$225.12`; Adjusted EBITDAre rose `3.0%` to `$236.6M`; Adjusted FFO per diluted share rose `7.5%` to `$0.86`. | Sunstone adds the concentrated higher-end and redevelopment-heavy owner model rather than another generic hotel REIT. |
| Sunstone | `Q2 2026` RevPAR rose `9.3%` to `$263.61`; Adjusted EBITDAre was `$76.7M`; management kept tying the story to capital realization, not just demand celebration. | Sunstone shows the high-beta owner case where event demand, renovations, recycling, and buybacks all matter at once. |

### Why this supports the bigger conclusion

The owner side is now concrete enough to separate three different hospitality burdens:

- scale-heavy and diversified owner exposure through `Host`
- broader branded-hotel room economics through `Apple Hospitality`
- concentrated upper-upscale redevelopment and capital-recycling exposure through `Sunstone`

That is enough to say the ownership comparison is real, even if another owner could still deepen it.

## Claim 2: specialty and repeat-relationship spending is now a real cluster, not one beauty name standing alone

Ulta, Chewy, and Bath & Body Works are the useful trio.

### Concrete evidence

| Company | Exact evidence | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Ulta | Full-year `2025` net sales increased `9.7%` to `$12.4B`; diluted EPS reached `$25.64`; the packet says the model links stores, salons, e-commerce, loyalty-member data, marketplace assortment, and `UB Media`. | Ulta is the strongest discovery-and-loyalty specialty relationship in the cluster. |
| Ulta | `Q1 2026` net sales rose `11.1%` to `$3.2B`; operating income rose `11.6%` to `$448.3M`. | The model is still widening, not just defending a legacy store base. |
| Chewy | Full-year `2025` net sales reached `$12.60B`; adjusted EBITDA reached `$719.2M`; year-end active customers were `21.3M`; record free cash flow was `$562M`. | Chewy makes the lane more explicit around emotionally durable household routines, replenishment, prescriptions, and care continuity. |
| Chewy | `Q3 2025` Autoship customer sales were about `84%` of net sales; `Q1 2026` adjusted EBITDA margin reached `7.5%`. | This is a repeat-relationship and replenishment system, not just a digital storefront. |
| Bath & Body Works | Full-year `2025` net sales were `$7.291B`; operating income was `$1.126B`; the company had `40 million` active loyalty members. | Bath & Body Works adds the weaker, repair-oriented specialty case rather than another obvious winner. |
| Bath & Body Works | `Q1 2026` sales were down `3%`, and much of the earnings help came from an interchange-fee settlement benefit while management kept centering the `Consumer First Formula`. | This is useful because it shows that specialty retail can have loyalty and scale but still need product and cultural repair. |

### Why this supports the bigger conclusion

The specialty and relationship side is now much clearer:

- `Ulta` shows the strong discovery, loyalty, media, and marketplace version
- `Chewy` shows the emotionally sticky replenishment and care-management version
- `Bath & Body Works` shows the weaker but still meaningful legacy-brand repair version

That is enough to say the lane now has a specialty / repeat-relationship cluster rather than a single beauty packet.

## Claim 3: the real recreation split is increasingly between relationship owners, owner-burden carriers, and repeat household organizers

This is the useful lane-level upgrade.

### Concrete evidence

| Layer | Best proof names | What the packets show |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship owner in travel and leisure | Marriott, Caesars, Hilton, Choice | The cleaner economics often sit with the loyalty, booking, brand, or wallet layer. |
| Hard-asset owner in hospitality | Host, Apple Hospitality, Sunstone | Travel demand can be real while owner economics still vary sharply with property mix, renovation timing, concentration, and capital burden. |
| Repeat household and specialty relationship organizer | Ulta, Chewy, Bath & Body Works | Lifestyle demand also gets monetized through discovery, care routines, loyalty, replenishment, and emotional attachment outside classic travel participation. |

### Why this supports the bigger conclusion

The recreation lane no longer has to be summarized as:

- hotels
- gaming
- some retail

It can be summarized more precisely as:

- relationship-owned leisure systems
- burden-heavy property ownership systems
- repeat household and identity systems

That is a more complete lane map.

## What this note proves

This note proves several narrower but useful things:

1. Hospitality ownership is now a real three-model comparison set through `Host`, `Apple Hospitality`, and `Sunstone`.
2. Specialty and repeat-relationship demand is now more explicit through `Ulta`, `Chewy`, and `Bath & Body Works`.
3. The remaining recreation gap is narrower than the old checklist implied.

## What this note does not prove

- It does not prove the recreation lane is fully closed.
- It does not prove every specialty-retail packet belongs in the same quality tier.
- It does not prove another hotel-owner or participation operator would add no value.

It proves something more practical:

the recreation lane is no longer missing basic owner comparison or basic specialty / repeat-relationship breadth.

The remaining work is now mostly about depth and refinement, not absence.

## Exact next targets to deepen this bridge

1. Another participation-heavy or specialty operator only if the goal is to widen the household and identity side beyond beauty, pet care, gifting, and home.
2. Another hotel owner only if the goal is to turn a strong three-model owner comparison into a deeper owner subcluster.
3. A more explicit restaurant or experiential-service relationship owner if the lane later wants a stronger everyday participation counterpart to travel and gifting.
