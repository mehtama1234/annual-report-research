# Valuation-aware cross-sector research queue

Date: 2026-09-13  
Status: research prioritization, not an investment recommendation

## Purpose

The broader [cross-sector forensic watchlist](../cross-sector/cross-sector-economic-control-and-forensic-watchlist-2026-09-13.md)
identifies where the operating economics deserve investigation. This queue
adds the valuation layer: which cases should receive the next primary-filing
pass because the current scenario register leaves a meaningful gap between the
market snapshot and the base case, which cases require an immediate
expectations audit, and which apparent bargains remain too dependent on
unproven normalization.

The numbers below are mechanical outputs of the
[scenario register](deep-dossier-scenario-inputs-2026-09-13.csv), not price
targets. Each scenario uses a company-specific owner-cash denominator and
multiple; these denominators are not interchangeable across sectors. A large
base-case gap is therefore a reason to investigate the bridge, not evidence of
mispricing.

## Queue rules

| Queue | Mechanical signal | Research action |
|---|---|---|
| A — verify possible underwrite | Base implied equity value is at least 10% above the market snapshot, but the bear case remains materially below it | Rebuild the GAAP-to-cash bridge from the next filing and test the named falsifier before spending valuation confidence |
| B — expectations audit | Base case is within ±10% of the market snapshot | Focus on what the price already assumes; require clean evidence of durability, reinvestment returns, and incentive alignment |
| C — burden/overpayment audit | Base case is at least 10% below the market snapshot, or the bull case still fails to clear it | Do not let control-point quality substitute for price discipline; identify the precise operating outcome required to close the gap |

The C queue contains both excellent businesses and possible cycle/normalization
cases. Its purpose is to prevent a strong narrative—AI scarcity, procedure
control, skilled labor, or industrial density—from becoming a valuation
shortcut. A name should move out of C only when the next filing changes the
cash denominator or the required return, not merely when management repeats
the demand story.

## Cross-sector conclusions from the queue

1. The cleanest control points are not automatically the best current
   opportunities. Intuitive, KLA, Fastenal, and Vertiv illustrate how a real
   moat can coexist with a price that requires unusually favorable durability.
2. Apparent value is least trustworthy where the base case depends on a large
   normalization. Core & Main, Stryker, Baxter, T. Rowe Price, and the drug
   distributors require direct filing reconciliation before the gap is treated
   as investable.
3. Physical execution deserves a higher discount than headline backlog. The
   contractor and infrastructure group has some of the widest gaps between
   visibility and owner cash, especially Comfort Systems, EMCOR, MasTec, and
   Sterling.
4. The next research dollar should go to evidence that changes the cash
   denominator: working-capital unwind, maintenance versus growth capex,
   acquisition returns, franchisee or customer health, debt service, and
   dilution. More market-share or backlog data alone will not resolve the
   central uncertainty.

## Reproducibility and update protocol

The queue is derived from the 56-row scenario register. To update it:

1. Refresh the company source ledger and deep dossier from the next primary
   filing.
2. Change the scenario inputs only with an explicit normalization note and
   source link.
3. Recompute implied values with
   `python3 scripts/verify-deep-dossier-scenario-register.py`.
4. Reclassify the company only after the filing-based falsifier is addressed;
   do not promote a name solely because its share price moved.

This queue is deliberately incomplete as a portfolio-construction tool. It is
the next-action layer for the forensic research system, preserving the
distinction between economic quality, evidence quality, and valuation.
