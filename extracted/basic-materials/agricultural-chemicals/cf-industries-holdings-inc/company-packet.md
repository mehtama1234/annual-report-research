# CF Industries Holdings, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-09

## Snapshot

- Company: CF Industries Holdings, Inc.
- Sector: Basic Materials
- Industry: Agricultural Chemicals
- Ticker: CF
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- CF is the cleaner upstream fertilizer and nitrogen anchor that the archive still needed. Nutrien already gave the sector an integrated crop-input and agronomy read. CF adds the purer nitrogen-production and ammonia-utilization read.
- The annual materials make the key analytical point obvious: this is a business driven by nitrogen pricing, natural-gas input costs, plant reliability, logistics, and geopolitical supply disruption rather than by diversified retail or downstream distribution.
- `2025` was a strong base year. Full-year net earnings were `$1.46 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.89 billion`, net cash from operations was `$2.75 billion`, and free cash flow was `$1.79 billion`.
- The company also returned a large amount of capital in `2025`, repurchasing `16.6 million` shares for `$1.34 billion`, which reduced the outstanding share count by about `10%` versus year-end `2024`.
- Operationally, CF exited `2025` still looking like a high-utilization North American nitrogen platform even with the late-`2025` Yazoo City incident. Full-year gross ammonia production was about `10.1 million` tons, but management also reset `2026` gross ammonia expectations to about `9.5 million` tons because of the ongoing Yazoo outage.
- The annual frame also adds a second theme beyond pure fertilizer cyclicality. Blue Point, the low-carbon ammonia joint venture with JERA and Mitsui, means CF is becoming part commodity producer, part industrial decarbonization infrastructure bet.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net earnings were `$727 million`, diluted EPS was `$4.73`, EBITDA was `$1.17 billion`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$1.19 billion`.
- Operations: first-half `2026` gross ammonia production was about `4.9 million` tons, second-quarter production was about `2.4 million` tons, and the business ran at `98%` of available ammonia capacity year-to-date.
- Capital and projects: CF repurchased `2.0 million` shares for `$230 million` in the quarter, raised the quarterly dividend to `$0.60`, and received permits in July `2026` to begin Blue Point construction in August.
- Structural themes: this is a strong quarter because it shows how much earnings power the company still has even while Yazoo remains partly offline. High utilization, strong benchmark pricing, and shareholder returns all remained intact.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net earnings were `$615 million`, diluted EPS was `$3.98`, EBITDA was `$1.01 billion`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$983 million`. Reported results included an approximately `$170 million` litigation-settlement gain.
- Operations and market framing: management emphasized strong global nitrogen demand, tight global nitrogen supply, and the way Iran-related disruption exposed how fragile the nitrogen supply chain remains.
- Commercial angle: CF launched a low-carbon UAN collaboration with PepsiCo tied to Frito-Lay's U.S. potato supply chain.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` broadens the CF story beyond commodity spot pricing. It shows the company trying to monetize lower-carbon nitrogen products while still benefiting from supply tightness in conventional fertilizer markets.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: full-year `2025` net earnings were `$1.46 billion`, diluted EPS was `$8.97`, EBITDA was `$2.78 billion`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$2.89 billion`; fourth-quarter net earnings were `$404 million`, diluted EPS was `$2.59`, EBITDA was `$731 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$821 million`.
- Operations and costs: full-year gross ammonia production was about `10.1 million` tons. Net sales rose because average selling prices were higher across all segments, but realized natural-gas costs also moved up.
- Capital allocation and strategy: CF returned `$1.7 billion` to shareholders in `2025`, kept cash and cash equivalents near `$2.0 billion` at year-end, and continued funding Blue Point while stepping away from the Donaldsonville electrolyzer project in favor of the higher-return carbon-capture-led low-carbon ammonia path.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` is the clearest statement of what makes CF useful in the archive. It combines commodity whiplash, North American manufacturing advantage, energy-input sensitivity, clean-ammonia optionality, and aggressive capital return in one package.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: CF sharpens Basic Materials because it gives the archive a direct nitrogen and ammonia producer rather than only a broader agricultural-input platform.
- Consumer: the chain is indirect but important. CF sits upstream of food production, acreage decisions, crop yields, and farm economics rather than normal discretionary demand.
- Cultural: this is evidence that physical production systems still drive the modern economy. Fertilizer security, shipping reliability, and natural-gas access remain central to food-system resilience.
- Geopolitics: CF is one of the clearest archive names where war, sanctions, natural-gas availability, and global trade disruption move earnings directly.
- Energy linkage: the business is a useful bridge between agriculture and energy because nitrogen pricing power and natural-gas input costs are tightly linked.
- Climate and industrial policy: Blue Point, low-carbon ammonia, carbon capture, and the PepsiCo low-carbon UAN relationship show how decarbonization themes are now entering a traditional bulk-commodity producer.
- Capital allocation: this is a highly explicit buyback and dividend story. The company is using strong cash generation to repurchase stock, raise dividends, and still fund selective clean-energy expansion.

## Working interpretation

- CF materially improves the archive because it gives the force map a much cleaner `commodity-whiplash` anchor than we had before.
- Nutrien says agricultural inputs matter through an integrated grower and retail system. CF says the same theme can also be read through a focused nitrogen producer where pricing, gas costs, outages, and geopolitical supply shocks hit almost directly.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, CF is strong evidence that commodity-facing earnings remained constructive well beyond metals and energy. Nitrogen markets stayed tight, utilization stayed high, cash generation stayed strong, and management still had room to buy back stock, raise the dividend, and fund Blue Point.
