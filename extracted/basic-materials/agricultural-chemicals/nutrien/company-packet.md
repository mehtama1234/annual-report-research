## Annual report takeaways

- Nutrien gives the Basic Materials archive the agricultural-input and food-system layer that the other starter names do not cover. This is not just a fertilizer price trade. It is an integrated agriculture platform that combines upstream production with a large retail and agronomy network.
- The annual materials show real scale. Nutrien frames itself as the `#1` global ag retailer, the `#1` global potash producer, and the `#2` North American nitrogen producer. It operates more than `1,800` retail locations, more than `4,200` crop consultants, more than `600,000` customer accounts, `6` potash mines, `12` nitrogen facilities, and `6` phosphate facilities.
- The higher-quality part of the story is the mix of assets and relationships, not just tonnage. Nutrien repeatedly emphasizes proprietary products, agronomy, financing, logistics, and farmer relationships as the mechanisms that make the retail business more resilient and higher quality.
- Potash remains the most important structural upstream asset. The annual and quarterly materials point to strong potash affordability, tight global fundamentals, record sales volumes, and continuing mine automation. Nutrien also disclosed that `49%` of potash ore tonnes were mined using automation in `2025`.
- `2025` was a strong base year. Full-year net earnings were `$2.30 billion` and adjusted EBITDA was `$6.05 billion`, helped by higher fertilizer selling prices, record upstream fertilizer sales volumes, and higher retail earnings.
- Portfolio simplification matters. Management keeps presenting the company as a business being narrowed toward stronger, lower-risk, structural free-cash-flow generation. Strategic alternatives for the phosphate business, the Trinidad nitrogen facility, and the Brazilian retail business are part of that effort.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net earnings were `$1.22 billion`, diluted EPS was `$2.53`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.43 billion`, and adjusted net earnings per share was `$2.61`.
- Segment read: in the first half of `2026`, Retail adjusted EBITDA was `$1.24 billion`, Potash adjusted EBITDA was `$1.24 billion`, and Nitrogen adjusted EBITDA was `$1.12 billion`.
- Cash and capital return: Nutrien returned `$848 million` to shareholders in the first half of `2026`, increased the pace of repurchases in the third quarter, and reported approximately `$82 million` of third-quarter repurchases through August 4, 2026.
- Management message: management emphasized record first-half potash sales volumes, stronger proprietary products margins, better nitrogen reliability and cost position, raised potash sales volume guidance, and lower capital expenditure guidance.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is the clearest evidence that Nutrien can produce very strong earnings when potash volumes, nitrogen margins, and retail execution line up at the same time.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net earnings were `$139 million`, diluted EPS was `$0.27`, adjusted EBITDA was `$1.11 billion`, and adjusted net earnings per share was `$0.51`.
- Segment read: Retail adjusted EBITDA was `$108 million`, Potash adjusted EBITDA was `$578 million`, and Nitrogen adjusted EBITDA was `$482 million`.
- Cash and capital return: Nutrien returned `$409 million` to shareholders in the quarter through dividends and share repurchases.
- Management message: management emphasized record potash sales volumes, higher global fertilizer benchmarks, stronger retail proprietary-product margins, and unchanged strategic priorities and capital allocation.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` shows that Nutrien’s operating engine can still look good even when bottom-line earnings are relatively muted. Potash and nitrogen were already strong, and the quarter helps explain the much larger jump that followed in `Q2 2026`.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net earnings were `$0.58 billion`, diluted EPS was `$1.18`, adjusted EBITDA was `$1.28 billion`, and adjusted net earnings per share was `$0.83`.
- Segment read: full-year `2025` Retail adjusted EBITDA was `$1.74 billion`, Potash adjusted EBITDA was `$2.25 billion`, Nitrogen adjusted EBITDA was `$2.15 billion`, and Phosphate adjusted EBITDA was `$382 million`.
- Cash and capital return: Nutrien highlighted strong free cash flow in `2025`, about `$900 million` of gross divestiture proceeds since the fourth quarter of `2024`, lower adjusted net debt, and a `30%` increase in total cash returns to shareholders.
- Management message: management framed `2025` as a defining year marked by cost and capital discipline, portfolio optimization, stronger balance sheet positioning, and structural free-cash-flow growth.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` and the full-year wrap-up show the core Nutrien frame clearly: stronger potash and nitrogen, a better retail margin profile, ongoing cost work, and a more intentional capital-allocation model.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Nutrien is strong evidence that Basic Materials includes agricultural systems and food-production infrastructure, not just mined and energy commodities.
- Consumer: direct end-consumer demand is far downstream, but the chain runs through food, feed, biofuels, acreage decisions, and grower economics. That makes the company a useful read on real-economy demand that is not consumer-discretionary in the normal sense.
- Cultural: the bigger cultural signal is that basic production systems still matter. Fertilizer, agronomy, and distribution networks remain central to food security and agricultural productivity.
- Regulatory: trade flows, geopolitics, natural-gas costs, environmental standards, and cross-border agricultural policy all matter here. This is one of the clearest places where geopolitics and physical supply chains enter earnings directly.
- Labor: field execution, agronomy support, mining reliability, plant turnarounds, and logistics all matter. This is not a capital-light software story.
- Technology: Nutrien’s technology signal is operational rather than digital-native. Mine automation, proprietary products, digital retail capabilities, and logistics optimization all show up as competitive advantages.
- Capital allocation: Nutrien is balancing dividends and buybacks with capex discipline and divestitures. The key question is whether that combination genuinely improves earnings quality and free-cash-flow durability.
