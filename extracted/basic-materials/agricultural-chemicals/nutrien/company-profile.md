## What changed

- Portfolio shape: Nutrien is broader than a fertilizer producer. The annual materials frame it as an integrated agriculture-input system with upstream potash, nitrogen, and phosphate assets tied to a downstream retail network of more than `1,800` locations, over `4,200` crop consultants, and more than `600,000` customer accounts.
- Operating footprint: the annual report presents Nutrien as the `#1` global ag retailer, the `#1` global potash producer, and the `#2` North American nitrogen producer, with `6` potash mines, `12` nitrogen facilities, and `6` phosphate facilities.
- 2025 base year: full-year `2025` net earnings were `$2.30 billion` and adjusted EBITDA was `$6.05 billion`. Retail adjusted EBITDA rose to `$1.74 billion`, Potash adjusted EBITDA rose to `$2.25 billion`, and Nitrogen adjusted EBITDA rose to `$2.15 billion`.
- Potash and nitrogen strength: annual and quarterly materials point to strong potash fundamentals, record upstream fertilizer sales volumes in `2025`, higher fertilizer benchmark prices, improved nitrogen reliability, and continuing automation at the potash mines. Nutrien also noted that `49%` of potash ore tonnes were mined using automation in `2025`.
- Retail quality: the more durable signal is not just fertilizer price leverage but the strength of the retail and proprietary-products engine. `Q1 2026` and `Q2 2026` both highlighted stronger proprietary products margins and disciplined execution across the retail system.
- 2026 quarter pattern: `Q1 2026` was much weaker in bottom-line earnings than `Q2 2026`, but the business still showed strong potash and nitrogen momentum in `Q1`. `Q2 2026` then showed a sharp profit step-up to `$1.22 billion` of net earnings and `$2.43 billion` of adjusted EBITDA.
- Capital allocation: Nutrien is pairing operating improvement with a balance-sheet and capital-return story. It returned `$409 million` to shareholders in `Q1 2026` and `$848 million` in the first half of `2026`, while also using divestitures and portfolio simplification to support free-cash-flow quality.
- Strategic repositioning: management is still simplifying the portfolio. `Q1 2026` and `Q2 2026` materials both referenced strategic alternatives for the phosphate business, the Trinidad nitrogen facility, and the Brazilian retail business.
- Market exposure: this is a crop-input and farm-economics story as much as a commodity story. Results are shaped by crop acreage, grain prices, weather, fertilizer affordability, grower cash margins, natural-gas inputs, and geopolitics affecting fertilizer trade flows.

## Open questions

- How much of the `Q2 2026` earnings surge is sustainable once fertilizer benchmarks normalize?
- Does Nutrien’s portfolio simplification end with a stronger, more cash-generative core business, or does it mainly reduce complexity without materially improving through-cycle returns?
