## Annual report takeaways

- Mosaic adds the fertilizer-processing and crop-input layer that sits between mining, chemicals, logistics, and food-system dependence. `CF Industries` already gave the archive the cleaner nitrogen-and-natural-gas view. `Nutrien` gave it the integrated farm-input and retail network. Mosaic adds the phosphate-potash-conversion and Brazil-distribution version of the same system.
- The `2025` annual filing shows why the company matters structurally. Mosaic serves customers in approximately `40` countries, accounts for about `10%` of estimated global annual phosphate production, accounts for about `12%` of estimated global annual potash production, and describes itself as a leading fertilizer production and distribution company in Brazil.
- This is not a pure upstream extraction story. The segment structure makes that clear. `2025` external net sales were about `$3.88 billion` in Phosphate, `$2.66 billion` in Potash, `$4.85 billion` in Mosaic Fertilizantes, and `$669 million` in Corporate and Other, for total external net sales of about `$12.05 billion`.
- `2025` was profitable but not clean. Gross margin was about `$1.90 billion`, operating earnings were about `$822 million`, full-year adjusted EBITDA was `$2.4 billion`, and full-year net income was `$541 million`. Those figures are good enough to show scale, but the year already carried real pressure from working capital, Brazil restructuring, and raw-material costs.
- The annual filing and year-end materials make the operating mechanism visible: Mosaic’s earnings are shaped not only by phosphate and potash pricing, but also by sulfur availability, phosphate-rock costs, inventory swings, Brazil channel health, and whether production plans can stay economic.
- The annual read also adds a small but important adjacency. Mosaic Biosciences contributed positively in `2025`, with annual net sales more than doubling to `$68 million`. That does not change the bulk-commodity nature of the enterprise, but it shows management trying to add a higher-value biological-input layer on top of a cyclical fertilizer base.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` revenue was `$2.8 billion`, operating loss was `$36 million`, net loss was `$273 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$407 million`.
- Segment and volume read: second-quarter sales volumes totaled `1.4 million` tonnes in Phosphates, `2.0 million` tonnes in Potash, and `1.5 million` tonnes in Mosaic Fertilizantes. Segment adjusted EBITDA was `$128 million` in Phosphate, `$278 million` in Potash, `$60 million` in Mosaic Fertilizantes, and `$(59) million` in Corporate and Other.
- Cost and operational pressure: management said sulfur availability and affordability remained the main issue, third-quarter sulfur contracts were settled at `$705` per long ton, and second-quarter raw-material costs averaged `$522` per long ton for sulfur and `$621` per tonne for ammonia. Bartow was running at only `40%` of its targeted annual operating rate.
- Capital and market message: Mosaic reduced `2026` capital-expenditure guidance again to `$1.2 billion` and said potash market conditions should remain constructive through the remainder of `2026`, with Brazil continuing to replenish inventories.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is the cleanest evidence that Mosaic is a food-system input and conversion-economics packet, not just a commodity ticker. Potash remained resilient, but sulfur affordability, partial idling, Brazil resets, and mark-to-market noise kept reported profitability weak.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` operating loss was `$373 million`, net loss was `$258 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$416 million`.
- Segment and volume read: first-quarter sales volumes totaled `1.9 million` tonnes in Phosphate, `2.2 million` tonnes in Potash, and `1.6 million` tonnes in Mosaic Fertilizantes. Segment adjusted EBITDA was `$115 million` in Phosphate, `$275 million` in Potash, `$42 million` in Mosaic Fertilizantes, and `$(16) million` in Corporate and Other.
- Operating response: Riverview, Bartow, and Faustina phosphoric-acid production rates were at target in the first quarter, and the major New Wales turnaround was completed in March, but management said phosphate production in the U.S. and Brazil was under review because of raw-material constraints, with partial curtailments beginning in May.
- Capital discipline: management reduced `2026` capital-expenditure expectations to `$1.25 billion`, explicitly reflecting deferred less-time-sensitive spending.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` shows the awkward middle state of the Mosaic model. Core assets can still produce solid adjusted EBITDA, but profitability can be overwhelmed by impairments, raw-material constraints, and Brazil-related actions faster than at a cleaner nitrogen producer.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: full-year `2025` net income was `$541 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.4 billion`, operating earnings were `$822 million`, and net sales were about `$12.1 billion`. `Q4 2025` net sales were `$2.974 billion`, operating loss was `$100 million`, net loss was `$519 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$505 million`.
- Production and segment read: full-year potash production totaled `8.8 million` tonnes, with MOP output at its highest level since `2019`. In `Q4 2025`, segment adjusted EBITDA was `$144 million` in Phosphate, `$336 million` in Potash, `$45 million` in Mosaic Fertilizantes, and `$(20) million` in Corporate and Other.
- Working-capital drag: full-year `2025` cash flow from operations fell to `$825 million` from `$1.3 billion` in `2024`, primarily because finished-product inventories increased by `$428 million` and phosphate-rock volumes and sulfur prices drove another `$346 million` working-capital hit. `Q4 2025` operating cash flow was `$(56) million`.
- Notable items: the fourth-quarter net loss was distorted by a large stack of impairments and other charges, including the Carlsbad held-for-sale impairment, Brazil goodwill and fixed-asset impairments, and other FX and mark-to-market items.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` is the clearest annual explanation of the Mosaic model. The company can produce real EBITDA at scale, but that cash conversion is heavily shaped by inventories, sulfur, rock costs, Brazil asset quality, and whether weak demand forces product to sit on the balance sheet.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Mosaic strengthens the agricultural-chemicals lane by adding the phosphate-potash-conversion and Brazil-distribution system rather than another nitrogen or retail-heavy input model.
- Consumer: the demand signal is indirect but essential. Mosaic sits upstream of food production, planting economics, acreage decisions, and fertilizer affordability rather than normal discretionary consumption.
- Cultural and societal: this packet reinforces that food security and crop productivity remain hard physical systems. The real cultural signal is not branding or consumer habit but dependence on nutrient flows, global trade corridors, and reliable farm-input availability.
- Industrial: Mosaic is one of the clearest names in the archive where inventory timing, sulfur contracts, ammonia costs, partial idling, and distribution geography can matter as much as nominal end demand.
- Geographic exposure: Brazil matters disproportionately. Mosaic Fertilizantes is not just an add-on segment; it is a major earnings and working-capital variable.
- Commodity exposure: this is not one clean price beta. Mosaic is simultaneously exposed to phosphate and potash pricing, sulfur and ammonia input costs, rock economics, and mark-to-market noise in ways that make reported earnings much messier than the headline crop-demand story suggests.
- Capital intensity: this is a heavy fixed-asset and turnaround-driven business. The cuts from `$1.25 billion` to `$1.2 billion` of `2026` capex guidance show that management is actively trying to preserve flexibility when conversion economics are under pressure.
- Comparative read: `CF Industries` is the cleaner nitrogen-and-gas system, `Nutrien` is the integrated agronomy and retail system, and `Mosaic` is the phosphate-potash, sulfur-sensitive, Brazil-dependent crop-input conversion system.

## Working interpretation

- Mosaic materially improves the branch because it widens heavy inputs beyond hydrocarbons, steel, copper, and precious metals into the crop-nutrient conversion layer.
- It also sharpens the internal split inside agricultural chemicals. `CF Industries` is mostly about nitrogen and natural-gas economics. `Nutrien` is mostly about integrated retail, agronomy, and potash scale. Mosaic is about phosphate and potash conversion, sulfur affordability, Brazil inventory cycles, and the way working capital can absorb a large share of the system’s cash generation.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Mosaic is useful evidence that food-system inputs can remain strategically essential even when reported earnings are messy. The market may need the product, but that does not mean conversion margins, inventories, or regional asset quality cooperate in a straight line.
