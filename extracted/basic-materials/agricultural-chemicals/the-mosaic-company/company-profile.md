## What changed

- Business mix: Mosaic is not just a mine. The `2025` annual filing frames it as a phosphate, potash, and Brazil-distribution system serving customers in approximately `40` countries.
- Category fit: the company sits between mining and processing. AnnualReports calls it `Specialty Chemicals`, but the SEC classifies it as `Agricultural Chemicals`, which better matches its crop-nutrient role and its comparison set in this branch.
- `2025` base year: total external net sales were `$12.05 billion`, gross margin was `$1.90 billion`, operating earnings were about `$822 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$2.4 billion`.
- Segment shape: `2025` external net sales were about `$3.88 billion` in Phosphate, `$2.66 billion` in Potash, `$4.85 billion` in Mosaic Fertilizantes, and `$669 million` in Corporate and Other.
- Food-system linkage: Mosaic says it accounts for approximately `10%` of estimated global annual phosphate production and approximately `12%` of estimated global annual potash production, which makes it a useful food-security and crop-input infrastructure packet rather than a normal chemicals name.
- Brazil importance: the annual filing explicitly describes Mosaic as a leading fertilizer production and distribution company in Brazil, and the quarterly materials show how much Brazil inventory cycles, impairments, and operating resets matter.
- `Q4 2025` pattern: the year ended with weak U.S. demand, higher sulfur and phosphate-rock costs, an accelerated inventory build, and negative fourth-quarter operating cash flow of `$(56) million` even though full-year potash production reached `8.8 million` tonnes.
- `Q1 2026` pattern: Mosaic kept adjusted EBITDA positive at `$416 million`, but the quarter showed how quickly raw-material constraints and Brazil-related charges can distort reported profitability. Management cut `2026` capital-expenditure expectations to `$1.25 billion` and said phosphate production in the U.S. and Brazil was under review.
- `Q2 2026` pattern: headline earnings stayed weak, but the structure of the weakness changed. Sulfur availability and affordability remained central, second-quarter sales volumes were `1.4 million` tonnes in Phosphates, `2.0 million` tonnes in Potash, and `1.5 million` tonnes in Mosaic Fertilizantes, and management reduced `2026` capex guidance again to `$1.2 billion`.
- Operational asymmetry: Potash remained the cleaner earnings pillar, while Phosphate and Mosaic Fertilizantes carried more of the raw-material, impairment, and idling stress.

## Open questions

- How much of the current weakness is cyclical sulfur and inventory pressure versus a more persistent structural drag in the phosphate and Brazil system?
- Can Mosaic convert its Biosciences and Brazil-distribution adjacency into a higher-quality earnings mix, or will the company remain primarily a volatile crop-input conversion story?
