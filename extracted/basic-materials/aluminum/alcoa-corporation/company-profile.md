## What changed

- Portfolio shape: Alcoa adds the missing upstream aluminum chain to Basic Materials. The `2025` annual filing describes a vertically integrated system spanning bauxite mining, alumina refining, aluminum smelting and casting, and energy generation.
- Operating footprint: Alcoa reported direct and indirect ownership of `25` operating locations across `8` countries on `5` continents. As of `2025-12-31`, it had approximately `14,900` employees in `16` countries.
- Segment frame: the company is simpler than several other names in the sector sample. It has two reportable segments, `Alumina` and `Aluminum`, which makes the operating story easier to read through price, volume, and restart dynamics.
- 2025 base year: full-year `2025` revenue was `$12.8 billion`, net income attributable to Alcoa was `$1.2 billion`, adjusted EBITDA excluding special items was `$2.0 billion`, cash from operations was `$1.2 billion`, total debt was `$2.4 billion`, and adjusted net debt was `$1.5 billion`.
- Asset actions matter: the annual materials show that Alcoa is actively reshaping the portfolio rather than just taking aluminum prices as given. In `2025`, it sold its `25.1%` Ma'aden joint-venture stake, announced the permanent closure of the Kwinana refinery in Australia, and advanced the San Ciprian complex restart through a joint venture structure.
- Operational read: Alcoa reported annual production records at `5` aluminum smelters and `1` alumina refinery in `2025`. The annual filing also said the San Ciprian smelter was operating at approximately `65%` of annual capacity at `2025-12-31`, with full restart then expected by mid-`2026`.
- 2026 quarter pattern: `Q1 2026` showed solid profitability but meaningful disruption from the Middle East conflict and Cyclone Narelle, while `Q2 2026` stepped up sharply to a quarterly revenue record as the San Ciprian restart was completed and aluminum shipments rose.
- Geopolitics and tariffs: Alcoa is a clean example of how policy and supply disruption now run directly through earnings. The `Q1 2026` and `Q2 2026` filings reference Middle East conflict effects, Section 232 tariffs on Canadian aluminum imports into the U.S., and premium changes such as the Midwest and Rotterdam premiums.
- Strategic expansion: `Q2 2026` added a new layer beyond recovery and restart execution. Alcoa announced the AliGroup transaction with South32's interests in bauxite, alumina, and aluminum assets, which would increase scale but also add financing and integration risk.

## Open questions

- How much of Alcoa's current strength is durable operating improvement versus favorable aluminum pricing and premium conditions?
- Does the AliGroup transaction create a stronger upstream system, or does it reintroduce leverage and integration risk at the wrong point in the cycle?
