# Air Products and Chemicals, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Air Products and Chemicals, Inc.
- Sector: Basic Materials
- Industry: Chemicals - Major Diversified
- Ticker: APD
- Fiscal year-end: September
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: FY2026 Q3
  - quarter 2: FY2026 Q2
  - quarter 3: FY2026 Q1

## Annual report takeaways

- Air Products adds the industrial-gases and hydrogen-infrastructure layer that this branch still lacked. Despite the industry label, this is not mainly a spread-driven commodity-chemicals packet. It is a long-duration industrial-gas, pipeline, on-site, merchant, helium, electronics, and large-project platform.
- The `2025` annual report described the company as a world-leading industrial-gases business that also develops, engineers, builds, owns, and operates some of the world's largest clean-hydrogen projects. That dual identity is the key to the packet.
- The adjusted base business remained strong in `2025` even while GAAP results were heavily distorted. Sales were about `$12.037 billion`, adjusted operating income was about `$2.858 billion`, adjusted operating margin was `23.7%`, and adjusted EPS was `$12.03`.
- The GAAP picture looked very different. Air Products reported a GAAP operating loss of about `($877 million)` and GAAP EPS of `($1.74)` because of large business and asset actions. That makes the company a strong example of why this archive needs to separate reported headline damage from the underlying operating franchise.
- Geographic and asset breadth matter. The annual report described operations in roughly `50` countries or regions outside the United States and kept the segment view centered on `Americas`, `Asia`, `Europe`, and `Middle East and India`, plus `Corporate and other`.
- Capital intensity is unusually important here. Total debt rose to about `$17.7 billion` at `2025-09-30` from about `$14.2 billion` a year earlier, with the increase tied partly to senior-note issuance and partly to incremental NEOM project-financing borrowings that were described as non-recourse to Air Products.
- The annual read is therefore not a standard “chemicals demand softened” story. It is a capital-allocation and infrastructure packet where stable industrial-gases earnings coexist with very large hydrogen, ammonia, and energy-transition project exposure.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `FY2026 Q3` sales were about `$3.2 billion`, up `5%`. GAAP loss per share was `($6.47)` and GAAP operating loss was about `($2.1 billion)`, but adjusted EPS was `$3.47` and adjusted operating income was about `$810 million`.
- Operating read: the sales increase reflected about `3%` higher volumes, `1%` higher pricing, and `1%` favorable currency. Segment-level growth remained positive across the system, including `Americas`, `Asia`, and `Europe`, while `Middle East and India` equity affiliates' income rose to about `$101 million`.
- Project reset: this was the quarter where management formally exited or stopped several clean-energy projects. Air Products said it would not proceed with the Louisiana Clean Energy Complex and would discontinue the Arizona or Casa Grande zero-carbon liquid-hydrogen facility and other smaller distribution projects.
- Capital read: management lowered FY2026 capex guidance to about `$3.5 billion`, down from the roughly `$4.0 billion` level used earlier in the year. That matters because the packet is as much about capital discipline as about quarterly volume growth.
- Structural themes: `FY2026 Q3` is the cleanest proof that Air Products needs to be read as two systems at once:
  - a relatively steady industrial-gases franchise
  - a much riskier megaproject and energy-transition investment portfolio

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `FY2026 Q2` sales were about `$3.2 billion`, up `9%`. GAAP EPS was `$3.19`, adjusted EPS was `$3.20`, and adjusted operating income was about `$753 million`.
- Growth drivers: sales reflected `4%` higher volumes, `4%` favorable currency, and `2%` higher energy pass-through, partly offset by `1%` lower pricing.
- End-market read: the quarter showed why Air Products is not simply a cyclical chemicals company. Management highlighted Samsung fab-related specialty-gas wins in South Korea, liquid hydrogen and helium support for NASA Artemis II, and broader helium supply resilience actions.
- Capital and balance sheet: management still guided to about `$4.0 billion` of FY2026 capex. Cash and cash items were about `$951 million` at `2026-03-31`.
- Structural themes: `FY2026 Q2` looked like a healthy industrial platform quarter, where electronics, aerospace, and supply-system scale were more visible than the project-writeoff risk that would dominate the next quarter's GAAP result.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `FY2026 Q1` GAAP EPS was `$3.04`, GAAP operating income was about `$735 million`, adjusted EPS was `$3.16`, and adjusted operating income was about `$757 million`.
- Operating read: `Americas` sales were about `$1.3 billion`, up `4%`, helped by higher energy pass-through and pricing; `Asia` sales were about `$832 million`, up `2%`; `Europe` sales were about `$782 million`, up `12%`; and `Middle East and India` equity affiliates' income was about `$85 million`.
- Strategic updates: management highlighted advanced negotiations with Yara on low-emission ammonia projects in the U.S. and Saudi Arabia, a quarterly dividend increase to `$1.81` marking the `44th` consecutive year of increases, and NASA contracts totaling more than `$140 million` for liquid hydrogen.
- Balance-sheet read: total debt decreased to about `$17.5 billion` at `2025-12-31` from about `$17.7 billion` at fiscal year-end, while cash and cash items fell to about `$1.026 billion`.
- Structural themes: `FY2026 Q1` showed the cleaner pre-reset version of the Air Products thesis, where the base industrial-gases business, dividend record, and infrastructure relevance still looked solid even as the capital burden from major projects remained high.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Air Products expands the branch beyond hydrocarbons, fertilizers, mining, and ordinary chemicals into industrial gases, hydrogen, helium, and process-infrastructure systems.
- Consumer: the company is mostly upstream of end demand, but it still touches broad social systems through manufacturing, semiconductors, food, healthcare, aerospace, and energy infrastructure.
- Cultural and societal: the packet shows that parts of the energy-transition story are now less about simple demand growth and more about which projects are actually financeable, executable, and worth keeping.
- Industrial: this is one of the strongest heavy-input packets for understanding long-duration supply systems. Pricing, volumes, energy pass-through, pipeline and on-site contracts, electronics demand, helium positioning, and hydrogen infrastructure all matter at once.
- Capital intensity: Air Products is a strong proof that not all “chemicals” names should be analyzed the same way. The capex load, debt stack, and project-finance complexity are closer to infrastructure and energy-development systems than to a lighter industrial formulation business.
- Comparative read: `FMC` is an agricultural-science and post-patent-reset packet; `Dow` is more exposed to spread and commodity-chemical pressure; Air Products is more utility-like in its base gas franchise but carries a separate layer of megaproject and energy-transition risk.
- Infrastructure dependence: the packet improves the archive's ability to explain physical-system dependence. Semiconductors, space launch, heavy manufacturing, and hydrogen all require supply reliability, not just favorable commodity prices.

## Working interpretation

- Air Products materially improves the heavy-input branch because it adds a system that sits between chemicals, industrial infrastructure, and energy-transition capital deployment.
- The packet is most useful if the archive keeps the split explicit:
  - the base industrial-gases franchise is relatively steady, contract-backed, and exposed to resilient industrial and technology demand
  - the hydrogen, ammonia, and other megaproject bets can create very large accounting charges, capital-allocation mistakes, and financing complexity
- That distinction strengthens the overall branch thesis. Heavy inputs are not only mines, wells, fertilizers, refineries, mills, or pipelines. They also include invisible process and gas networks that keep semiconductors, aerospace, manufacturing, and parts of the energy system functioning, but which can still be destabilized by oversized project ambition.
