## What changed

- Air Products adds a different kind of chemicals packet. This is an industrial-gases, hydrogen, helium, electronics, and project-execution system rather than a spread-driven commodity-chemicals story.
- The `2025` annual report showed that the core operating base was still strong on an adjusted basis even while GAAP results were heavily distorted. Sales were about `$12.0 billion`, adjusted operating income was about `$2.86 billion`, and adjusted EPS was `$12.03`, but GAAP operating loss and GAAP EPS were pulled down by large business and asset actions.
- The quarter chain makes the split even clearer. `FY2026 Q1` and `FY2026 Q2` showed rising adjusted earnings and stable-to-improving industrial demand in regional gases, while `FY2026 Q3` showed how quickly hydrogen and clean-energy project resets can swamp the reported GAAP picture.
- Debt and capex are central to the packet. Total debt was about `$17.7 billion` at `2025-09-30` and about `$17.5 billion` at `2025-12-31`, while management was still describing a multibillion-dollar annual capex program tied to long-duration projects.
- The NEOM and clean-hydrogen story matters, but it should not be confused with the full company. The local filing chain shows a steadier base business in on-site, merchant, and pipeline gases than the headline project-noise might imply.

## Open questions

- Can Air Products preserve the attractive industrial-gases base business while imposing tighter discipline on megaproject selection and clean-energy capital deployment?
- How much of the investment case should be attributed to semiconductor, helium, aerospace, and traditional industrial-gases demand versus hydrogen, ammonia, and energy-transition optionality?
- Does the `FY2026 Q3` project reset mark the end of the worst capital-allocation overreach, or is more portfolio pruning still likely?
