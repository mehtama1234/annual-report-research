# Celanese Corp. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Celanese Corp.
- Sector: Basic Materials
- Industry: Chemicals - Major Diversified
- Ticker: CE
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Celanese adds a more specialized chemicals-and-materials-science packet than the branch had before. This is not just another commodity-chemicals spread story. It is a hybrid of specialty engineered materials, acetyl chain chemicals, nylon repositioning, high debt, divestitures, and manufacturing-footprint simplification.
- The annual `2025` financials were weak on a GAAP basis. Net sales were `$9.544 billion`, down from `$10.268 billion` in `2024`. Operating loss was `($786 million)` and net loss attributable to Celanese was `($1.165 billion)`.
- Management's own operating frame was much better than the GAAP result implied. In the `Q4 2025` prepared remarks, Celanese said full-year `2025` adjusted EPS was `$3.98`, operating EBITDA was `$1.9 billion`, and free cash flow was `$773 million`.
- The company spent `2025` trying to convert a difficult portfolio into a more cash-generative and less fragile one. Management said it refinanced approximately `$4 billion` of debt, reduced maturities due in `2026` and `2027` from `$4.8 billion` to `$2.1 billion`, realized more than `$120 million` of cost reductions, and continued footprint optimization.
- The segment split matters. `Engineered Materials` is tied to autos, medical, electronics, nylon, mix quality, and value-based pricing. `Acetyl Chain` is more exposed to pricing formulas, raw-material pass-through, industrial utilization, and the value of a reliable global supply network.
- This makes Celanese a good contrast to `Dow`. `Dow` reads more like a large commodity and materials-science spread-cycle name. Celanese reads more like a leveraged portfolio-repair and specialty-chemicals repositioning story with segment-level operating asymmetry.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net sales were `$2.752 billion`, operating profit was `$276 million`, adjusted EPS was `$2.45`, and free cash flow was `$140 million`.
- Business read: management described the quarter as driven by strong commercial execution, pricing actions, favorable mix, and reliable supply, even while underlying demand stayed softer than normal. In management's framing, both businesses performed strongly, with operating EBITDA up more than `20%` year over year.
- Segment read: `Engineered Materials` generated adjusted EBIT of `$234 million` and operating EBITDA of `$335 million`. Medical and electronics each posted double-digit year-over-year net-sales growth, offsetting still-soft automotive demand.
- Restructuring and footprint read: Celanese said it had completed the Ulsan, South Korea compounding closure, completed the previously announced nylon `6,6` manufacturing optimization ahead of schedule, and remained on track to close the Lanaken, Belgium acetate-tow facility in the third quarter.
- Balance-sheet read: Celanese said it repaid the `$508 million` bond due `July 19, 2026` and the `$400 million` bond due `August 5, 2026`, leaving no other bond maturities in `2026`. The `10-Q` showed cash and cash equivalents of `$1.364 billion` and total debt of `$12.007 billion` at `2026-06-30`.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net sales were `$2.337 billion`, operating profit was `$214 million`, adjusted EPS was `$0.85`, and free cash flow was `$3 million`.
- Business read: this was still a repair quarter, but a visibly better one than the `2025` backdrop. Management said results landed at the upper end of expectations and reflected deliberate execution across both businesses.
- Segment read: `Engineered Materials` generated adjusted EBIT of `$220 million` and operating EBITDA of `$324 million`, with net sales up `4%` sequentially. Management highlighted pipeline quality, diversification, mix improvement, and value-based pricing, but still called out weak end markets, especially Chinese automotive.
- Operating-system read: Celanese also used the quarter to announce nylon actions, including the planned Sakra, Singapore closure and optimization of its North American nylon `6,6` polymerization footprint, framing them as steps to build a more resilient and competitive platform.
- Balance-sheet read: Celanese held unusually high liquidity ahead of bond repayments. Management said it ended the quarter with `$1.8 billion` of cash and an undrawn `$1.75 billion` revolving facility. The `10-Q` showed cash and cash equivalents of `$1.758 billion` and total debt of `$12.554 billion` at `2026-03-31`.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` adjusted EPS was `$0.67` and fourth-quarter free cash flow was `$160 million`. Full-year `2025` adjusted EPS was `$3.98`, full-year operating EBITDA was `$1.9 billion`, and full-year free cash flow was `$773 million`.
- Business read: management said fourth-quarter volumes declined `7%` sequentially because of seasonality and year-end destocking, but the company still benefited from favorable mix, slightly better-than-expected pricing in `Engineered Materials`, and continuing cost reductions.
- Segment read: `Engineered Materials` generated fourth-quarter adjusted EBIT of `$183 million` and operating EBITDA of `$288 million`. Management said the business maintained adjusted EBIT margins versus the third quarter despite an `8%` sequential net-sales decline, supported by cost reduction, mix enrichment, and value-based pricing.
- Capital and debt read: year-end `2025` is where the deleveraging story became explicit. Management said it had refinanced about `$4 billion` of debt and cut near-term maturities meaningfully, while also completing the Micromax divestiture and multiple footprint actions.
- Working interpretation: `Q4 2025` is the cleanest explanation of what Celanese is in this archive. It is a chemicals platform trying to rebuild earnings quality and balance-sheet flexibility through segment repositioning, cost work, divestitures, and more disciplined capital structure management.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Celanese expands the branch beyond commodity chemicals, industrial gases, fertilizers, metals, and hydrocarbons into a specialty-materials and acetyls system with meaningful restructuring and debt pressure.
- Consumer: this is still an upstream packet, but it reaches into broad end markets through autos, medical devices, electronics, packaging, industrial applications, and consumer-linked materials demand.
- Cultural and societal: the bigger signal is not direct consumer behavior. It is the industrial sorting process underneath modern materials demand: which end markets deserve capital, which product lines can earn pricing, and which manufacturing footprints are too expensive to keep.
- Industrial: this is one of the best branch packets for showing how a chemicals company can split internally between a specialty mix-improvement business and a more classic value-chain and network-reliability business.
- Balance sheet: Celanese is a strong proof that the heavy-input branch cannot just map products. It also has to map leverage, maturities, refinancing, divestitures, and how much cash generation is needed to keep the structure stable.
- Comparative read: `Dow` is closer to broad petrochemical and materials-science cyclicality. `Air Products` is closer to industrial-gases infrastructure and megaproject risk. `FMC` is a crop-protection and patent-reset story. Celanese is the cleaner debt-heavy specialty-materials and acetyl-chain repair story.
- Infrastructure and supply dependence: management's repeated emphasis on reliable global supply, network flexibility, and security-of-supply value during disruption shows that even specialty chemicals can behave like partial infrastructure businesses during stressed markets.

## Working interpretation

- Celanese materially improves the heavy-input branch because it adds a chemicals business where the main story is not only product demand or feedstock spreads. It is also about leverage, maturity management, divestitures, segment quality, and whether a specialty-materials franchise can be rebuilt under real balance-sheet pressure.
- The packet works best when the company is read in two layers:
  - `Engineered Materials` as the specialty mix, nylon, auto, medical, and electronics repositioning business
  - `Acetyl Chain` as the more network, reliability, and value-in-use pricing business
- That split helps the wider archive. It shows that “major diversified chemicals” is too blunt a category on its own. Some names are broad spread-cycle systems, some are infrastructure-like gas networks, some are agricultural science resets, and Celanese is a more leveraged specialty-materials and acetyls repair platform.
