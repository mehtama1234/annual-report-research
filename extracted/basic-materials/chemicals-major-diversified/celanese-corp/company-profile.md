## What changed

- Celanese adds a more differentiated chemicals packet than `Dow`, `FMC`, or `Air Products`. This is a specialty materials, acetyls, nylon, and balance-sheet-repair platform rather than a pure petrochemical spread business, crop-protection reset, or industrial-gases infrastructure system.
- The `2025` annual read showed a weak GAAP income statement but improving cash discipline. Net sales were `$9.544 billion`, operating loss was `($786 million)`, and net loss attributable to Celanese was `($1.165 billion)`, but management still reported full-year adjusted EPS of `$3.98`, operating EBITDA of `$1.9 billion`, and free cash flow of `$773 million`.
- The operating model is split between two different systems:
  - `Engineered Materials`, tied to autos, medical, electronics, nylon, and mix quality
  - `Acetyl Chain`, tied more directly to pricing, value-in-use, industrial utilization, and network reliability
- Balance-sheet repair remains central. Celanese said it refinanced about `$4 billion` of debt during `2025` and reduced maturities due in `2026` and `2027` from `$4.8 billion` to `$2.1 billion`.
- The `2026` quarter chain shows a visible improvement path. `Q1 2026` still looked like a cautious repair quarter, while `Q2 2026` showed a much stronger pricing, mix, and earnings outcome.

## Open questions

- Can the engineered-materials repositioning and nylon-footprint optimization produce durable earnings power even if auto demand remains soft?
- How much of the `Q2 2026` strength reflects temporary supply disruptions and timing benefits versus a more durable uplift in segment economics?
- Does Celanese become a cleaner deleveraging and specialty-materials-repair story from here, or does the debt and restructuring burden remain too heavy for the underlying business quality?
