## Annual report takeaways

- FMC adds the crop-protection and agricultural-science chemistry layer that the branch still lacked. `CF Industries`, `Nutrien`, and `Mosaic` cover fertilizer and crop-input economics, but FMC sits closer to patented chemistry, formulation strategy, regulatory registration, and post-patent erosion.
- The `2025` annual report is not a normal “materials demand held up” story. Revenue fell to `$3.47 billion`, down `18%` year over year. Adjusted EBITDA fell to `$843 million`, down `7%`, and adjusted earnings fell to `$2.96` per diluted share, down `15%`.
- The structural reason matters more than the headline decline. FMC spent `2025` repositioning itself for a much harsher competitive environment, especially around generic pressure and the post-patent transition for `Rynaxypyr`.
- India is central to the packet. Management decided to divest the India commercial business because of intense generic competition, regulatory complexity, and heavy working-capital requirements. The company still intends to participate in India through supply agreements, but the annual read makes clear that the old model was no longer attractive.
- The annual packet also shows a real reset in operating architecture. FMC launched `Project Foundation` to exit higher-cost production sites, improve sourcing, and consolidate operations. That makes this a cost-structure and manufacturing-footprint packet as much as a product packet.
- There is still a growth layer inside the stress. The three newest active ingredients, `fluindapyr`, `Isoflex`, and `Dodhylex`, generated about `$200 million` of `2025` sales, up `54%` from `2024`. That makes FMC useful as an innovation-risk packet: mature chemistry is under pressure while the new platform has to scale fast enough to matter.
- The annual packet also adds a channel-shift theme. FMC expanded direct sales and technical service in Brazil to reach large corn and soybean growers, which means the company is not only defending patents and pricing but also changing its route to market.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` revenue was `$867 million`, revenue excluding India was `$841 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$153 million`, adjusted EPS was `$0.26`, and GAAP cash from operations was `$363 million`.
- Operating read: excluding India, revenue fell `20%`. Volumes declined `10%` because of lower diamide partner orders and weaker demand for core legacy products, especially in North America. Price fell `7%` because of core-product pressure and planned `Rynaxypyr` pricing actions.
- Growth offset: the growth portfolio still grew mid-single digits, with new active ingredients and `Cyazypyr` offsetting weaker Plant Health sales. Branded `Rynaxypyr` sales were essentially flat year over year excluding India.
- Balance-sheet and strategy update: management said the India sale, Newark property sale, and Tessenderlo investment should together generate about `$1 billion` of proceeds for debt paydown. The company also said the strategic-review process announced in February had concluded.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is the clearest current proof that FMC is not waiting for a clean cyclical rebound. It is actively using asset sales, licensing, cost reduction, and route-to-market changes to survive a weaker pricing and demand environment.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` revenue was `$758.6 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$72.1 million`, and adjusted earnings were `$(28.9) million`, or `$(0.23)` per diluted share.
- Distortion and pressure: the quarter absorbed large restructuring and tax noise. `Project Foundation` alone drove about `$90.1 million` of charges inside the restructuring line, while India held-for-sale effects and tax adjustments made reported results much worse than the underlying crop-season demand read.
- Balance-sheet read: total debt rose to about `$4.53 billion` at `2026-03-31`, up from about `$4.07 billion` at year-end `2025`, while cash and cash equivalents fell to about `$390.9 million`.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` is the messiest quarter in the chain, but that is exactly why it matters. It shows how quickly a product- and channel-reset can collide with debt, tax, and restructuring pressures when the core portfolio is still under competitive attack.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` revenue was `$1.083 billion` and full-year `2025` revenue was `$3.467 billion`. Full-year adjusted earnings from continuing operations were `$372.0 million`, and `Q4 2025` adjusted earnings were `$150.5 million`. Management said fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA was about `$280 million`.
- Strategic reset: `Q4 2025` was the moment FMC made the new playbook explicit. Management launched a formal strategic review, targeted more than `$1 billion` of debt paydown through asset sales and licensing, and framed `2026` around balance-sheet repair, core-cost reduction, post-patent `Rynaxypyr` management, and new-active-ingredient growth.
- Impairment and India effects: the company wrote off its entire goodwill balance by about `$1.356 billion` in the quarter and disclosed total India-held-for-sale adjustments of about `$522 million` for `2025`, with the India asset base marked down to about `$450 million`.
- Core portfolio economics: management said `2025` sales of core products excluding `Rynaxypyr` were about `$2.2 billion`, with nearly `$1 billion` of those sales coming from products made in high-cost facilities. That is why manufacturing-footprint reform is central rather than optional.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` is the cleanest explanation of FMC's current role in the archive. This is a heavy-input chemistry system where patents, formulations, registrations, manufacturing cost, working capital, and grower economics all matter at once.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: FMC expands the branch beyond hydrocarbons, fertilizers, metals, and paper into crop-protection chemistry and agricultural-science platforms.
- Consumer: the end demand is still indirect. FMC sits upstream of crop yields, planted acreage, and grower profitability rather than normal consumer spending.
- Cultural and societal: the bigger signal is food-system resilience under harder economics. Growers still need productivity and pest control, but they are demanding lower prices and are more willing to switch as patents expire and generic supply expands.
- Industrial: FMC is one of the clearest packets in the archive where manufacturing footprint, route-to-market design, licensing, and restructuring matter as much as nominal demand.
- Innovation risk: this is a strong proof that not all science platforms are smooth compounders. Older chemistry can erode fast, so newer active ingredients have to replace both pricing power and category relevance.
- Geographic exposure: Brazil and India matter disproportionately. Brazil is the growth and channel-rebuild opportunity; India is the restructuring and divestiture problem that changed the balance-sheet story.
- Capital and balance sheet: this is a leveraged reset packet. Debt remained above `$4 billion` through the window, so asset sales, upfront licensing payments, and working-capital release all matter directly.
- Comparative read: `CF Industries` is upstream nitrogen, `Nutrien` is integrated farm-input retail, `Mosaic` is phosphate-potash conversion, and `FMC` is crop-protection chemistry under post-patent and channel-transition stress.

## Working interpretation

- FMC materially improves the heavy-input branch because it adds a new economic mechanism. This is not extraction, transport, refining, or nutrient conversion. It is a crop-protection chemistry platform where intellectual property, registration timing, post-patent pricing, formulation strategy, and manufacturing cost determine whether the system holds together.
- It also sharpens the internal split inside agricultural inputs. `CF Industries`, `Nutrien`, and `Mosaic` are all tied mainly to fertilizer and nutrient economics. FMC is tied more directly to chemistry innovation, generic erosion, biological and synthetic crop-protection demand, and the operational difficulty of defending margins after patents expire.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, FMC is useful evidence that not all heavy-input businesses are simple commodity betas. Some are closer to stressed industrial science platforms, where financial repair, product refresh, route-to-market redesign, and asset sales are as important as the crop cycle itself.
