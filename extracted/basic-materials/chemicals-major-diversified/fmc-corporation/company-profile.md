## What changed

- FMC adds a different kind of heavy-input story than fertilizer, metals, or hydrocarbons. This is agricultural science and crop-protection chemistry shaped by patent cliffs, generic entry, crop economics, and channel positioning.
- The annual report makes the reset explicit. `2025` revenue fell to `$3.47 billion`, adjusted EBITDA fell to `$843 million`, and adjusted EPS fell to `$2.96`, while management described `2025` as a strategic repositioning year rather than a normal chemicals cycle.
- India is not a side issue. FMC moved to divest its India commercial business, took approximately `$522 million` of related charges and write-downs in `2025`, and now treats the business as held for sale while planning to stay in the market through supply agreements.
- `Rynaxypyr` is the center of gravity for the operating story. The company is managing the post-patent transition with lower-priced basic formulations, newer mixtures and formulations, and a larger push into new active ingredients.
- The balance sheet matters more here than in cleaner materials packets. Total debt moved from about `$4.07 billion` at year-end `2025` to `$4.53 billion` at `Q1 2026`, then improved to about `$4.28 billion` at `Q2 2026`, while management repeatedly framed debt reduction as a core priority.

## Open questions

- Can FMC stabilize the core portfolio quickly enough against generic diamide and broader crop-protection pricing pressure?
- How much of the `2026` earnings-repair story depends on one-time asset sales, licensing proceeds, and working-capital release rather than durable operating improvement?
- Does the Brazil direct-sales buildout become a true structural advantage, or is it only a partial offset to pricing pressure in the legacy portfolio?
