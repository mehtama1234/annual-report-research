## Annual report takeaways

- Freeport gives the Basic Materials archive a clean copper-and-mining-infrastructure read. The annual materials show a company built around long-life copper assets, with gold and molybdenum as meaningful byproduct contributors to earnings quality and cost support.
- AnnualReports now has a saved local company-page artifact for this branch, and it confirms the `Basic Materials` / `Copper` taxonomy while still lagging at the `2024 Annual Report and Form 10-K` as of `2026-08-10`. The authoritative `2025` chain remains the official annual report and SEC filing set.
- The full-year `2025` package shows a business that remained strongly cash generative even through disruption: revenues of `$25.9 billion`, net income attributable to common stock of `$2.2 billion`, operating cash flow of `$5.6 billion`, and capital expenditures of `$4.5 billion`.
- The biggest structural issue in the packet is Grasberg. The September `2025` mud-rush incident hurt `Q4 2025`, but the `2026` quarterlies show management treating the phased restart and ramp-up of Grasberg Block Cave as the central bridge back to stronger production and cash flow.
- Freeport is especially useful for cross-sector work because it links directly into electrification, grid buildout, industrial investment, AI-adjacent power infrastructure, and broader hard-asset supply chain questions. Copper is the core product, but the bigger story is physical-system dependence.
- Management is also clearly leaning into an organic growth narrative rather than just waiting for price. The packet repeatedly points to leach and technology initiatives in Arizona, brownfield growth options, and the potential major El Abra expansion in Chile.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income attributable to common stock was `$984 million`, adjusted net income was `$1.1 billion`, operating cash flow was `$2.0 billion`, and capital expenditures were `$1.1 billion`.
- Operations: consolidated production totaled `786` million pounds of copper, `192` thousand ounces of gold, and `23` million pounds of molybdenum. Consolidated sales totaled `710` million pounds of copper, `123` thousand ounces of gold, and `25` million pounds of molybdenum.
- Pricing and costs: average realized prices were `$6.17` per pound for copper, `$4,520` per ounce for gold, and `$28.75` per pound for molybdenum. Unit net cash costs were `$1.97` per pound of copper.
- Management message: the message was strong margins, strong cash flow, operational execution, and a still-positive market outlook, with Grasberg ramping on schedule and the copper growth pipeline intact.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` shows what Freeport looks like when the copper market is supportive and Grasberg is healing. The business becomes a direct lever on copper scarcity, industrial electrification, and large-project mining execution.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income attributable to common stock was `$881 million`, while adjusted net income was `$830 million` because reported earnings included net credits tied mainly to an insurance settlement. Operating cash flow was `$1.5 billion` and capital expenditures were `$1.0 billion`.
- Operations: consolidated production totaled `662` million pounds of copper, `97` thousand ounces of gold, and `22` million pounds of molybdenum. Consolidated sales totaled `657` million pounds of copper, `121` thousand ounces of gold, and `24` million pounds of molybdenum.
- Pricing and costs: average realized prices were `$5.78` per pound for copper, `$4,889` per ounce for gold, and `$25.21` per pound for molybdenum. Unit net cash costs were `$1.91` per pound of copper.
- Management message: management emphasized the start of the Grasberg Block Cave ramp-up, the Indonesia operating-rights memorandum, and copper growth projects in Chile and Arizona.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` is the transition quarter. It shows a miner trying to convert a disrupted asset base back into growth while still benefiting from stronger metals pricing and keeping large project spending moving.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net income attributable to common stock was `$406 million`, adjusted net income was `$688 million`, operating cash flow was `$0.7 billion`, and capital expenditures were `$1.0 billion`. Full-year `2025` operating cash flow was `$5.6 billion`.
- Operations: consolidated `Q4 2025` production totaled `640` million pounds of copper, `65` thousand ounces of gold, and `25` million pounds of molybdenum. Full-year `2025` production totaled `3.383` billion pounds of copper, `1.0` million ounces of gold, and `92` million pounds of molybdenum.
- Pricing and costs: average realized prices were `$5.33` per pound for copper, `$4,078` per ounce for gold, and `$23.77` per pound for molybdenum in `Q4 2025`. Unit net cash costs were `$2.22` per pound of copper in the quarter and `$1.65` for the year.
- Management message: management framed the quarter around resilience, restart planning at Grasberg, and confidence in long-term copper demand despite near-term disruption.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` is the stressed quarter in the packet. It shows how sensitive a mining company can be to asset-specific disruption even when commodity prices and longer-term demand remain supportive.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Freeport is a strong reminder that Basic Materials is not a generic inflation trade. The core drivers are ore quality, mine reliability, project execution, byproduct mix, realized-price leverage, and geopolitical operating rights.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is not the main read. The better lens is copper demand from power systems, transmission, electrification, industrial equipment, autos, and construction.
- Cultural: the broader signal is that the modern economy still depends on hard-mined materials at scale. Copper remains a quiet but unavoidable foundation input for the physical buildout behind digital and energy-transition narratives.
- Regulatory: Indonesia operating rights, permitting, environmental approvals, and mine safety are first-order variables. The El Abra and Arizona growth themes depend heavily on regulatory and social-license execution.
- Labor: this is a labor- and operations-heavy business even when labor is not the headline. Ramp-ups, underground mine recovery, maintenance, and project execution all depend on operational discipline and specialized capability.
- Technology: Freeport is not a software beneficiary, but it is exposed to technology through mining methods, leach innovation, data analytics, and the simple fact that copper demand is tied to power and digital infrastructure buildout.
- Capital allocation: Freeport is a clear case where shareholder returns stay secondary to major project investment and operational recovery. The business has to keep funding its mines and downstream infrastructure to sustain the copper thesis.
