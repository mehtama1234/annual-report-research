## What changed

- Revenue and earnings: full-year `2025` revenues were `$25.9 billion` and net income attributable to common stock was `$2.2 billion`. `Q4 2025` net income fell to `$406 million` after disruption and impairment-related charges, then improved to `$881 million` in `Q1 2026` and `$984 million` in `Q2 2026`.
- Cash flow: Freeport generated `$5.6 billion` of operating cash flow in full-year `2025`, with `$0.7 billion` in `Q4 2025`, `$1.5 billion` in `Q1 2026`, and `$2.0 billion` in `Q2 2026`.
- Capital spending: capital expenditures were `$4.5 billion` in `2025`, including about `$2.3 billion` for major mining projects. Capital expenditures were `$1.0 billion` in `Q4 2025`, `$1.0 billion` in `Q1 2026`, and `$1.1 billion` in `Q2 2026`, showing that Freeport is still investing aggressively through the copper cycle.
- Production and sales: full-year `2025` production totaled `3.383` billion pounds of copper, `1.0` million ounces of gold, and `92` million pounds of molybdenum, with sales of `3.574` billion pounds of copper, `1.1` million ounces of gold, and `83` million pounds of molybdenum. `Q1 2026` copper production was `662` million pounds and `Q2 2026` rose to `786` million pounds as Grasberg recovered.
- Price realization and costs: average realized copper prices rose from `$4.75` per pound in full-year `2025` to `$5.78` in `Q1 2026` and `$6.17` in `Q2 2026`. Unit net cash costs were `$1.65` per pound in `2025`, but higher quarterly variability persisted at `$2.22` in `Q4 2025`, `$1.91` in `Q1 2026`, and `$1.97` in `Q2 2026`.
- Balance sheet: at year-end `2025`, Freeport had `$3.8 billion` of cash and `$9.4 billion` of total debt. By `2026-06-30`, cash had improved to `$4.1 billion` while total debt remained roughly `$9.4 billion`, keeping net debt moderate for a capital-heavy miner.
- Operational issues: the September `2025` external mud rush incident at PT Freeport Indonesia clearly shaped the whole period. `Q4 2025` reflects the disruption most directly, while `Q1 2026` and `Q2 2026` increasingly reflect phased restart and ramp-up execution at Grasberg Block Cave.
- Growth agenda: management kept emphasizing organic copper growth through leach and technology initiatives in Arizona, brownfield expansion options, and the potential major El Abra expansion in Chile.
- Management tone: the company’s framing is that Freeport is not just a cyclical metal producer. It is presenting itself as a large-scale copper supplier to a structurally growing market, with gold and molybdenum byproducts helping support margins and cash costs.

## Open questions

- How quickly can Grasberg normalize enough for Freeport to consistently convert higher copper prices into cleaner earnings and cash flow?
- How much of the current margin improvement is durable operating performance versus unusually strong copper, gold, and molybdenum price support?
