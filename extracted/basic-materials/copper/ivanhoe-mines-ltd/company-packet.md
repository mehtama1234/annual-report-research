# Ivanhoe Mines Ltd. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Company: Ivanhoe Mines Ltd.
- Sector: Basic Materials
- Industry: Copper
- Ticker: IVN
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target reported-period window:
  - latest period: `Q2 2026`
  - period minus 1: `Q1 2026`
  - period minus 2: `Q4 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- Ivanhoe belongs in this archive as the African copper-and-development buildout packet, not just another copper miner. The current earnings engine still runs through `Kamoa-Kakula`, but the company-level story also depends on `Kipushi`, `Platreef`, project finance, smelter ramp-up, and corridor logistics.
- AnnualReports was not useful here in the normal way. The saved `Companies?a=I` page shows `Ivanhoe Electric Inc.` but not `Ivanhoe Mines Ltd.`, and Ivanhoe's own annual-reports tab still showed only a `2024 Annual Report` when collected on `Monday, August 10, 2026`.
- The authoritative `2025` annual package therefore had to be assembled from the audited annual financial statements, the annual `MD&A`, the `2025 Annual Information Form`, and the `Q4 2025` and annual-results page.
- The `2025` annual read shows a much more capital-intensive and structurally unusual copper platform than most of the branch. Ivanhoe ended `2025` with total assets of about `$7.626 billion`, cash and short-term deposits of about `$885 million`, total liabilities of about `$1.901 billion`, and a consolidated working-capital surplus of about `$626 million`.
- The balance sheet was reshaped materially by financing activity. The company issued `$750 million` of `7.875%` senior notes due `2030` in January `2025`, completed a large private placement later in `2025`, and kept spending heavily on project development, especially at `Platreef`.
- One of the most important annual distinctions is accounting structure. Ivanhoe recognized revenue and profit directly from its consolidated assets, but the core economic engine of `Kamoa-Kakula` comes through its `49.5%` share of profit from the `Kamoa Holding` joint venture. That means reported company earnings can diverge from asset-level operating strength.
- Bigger-picture annual theme: Ivanhoe is a multi-asset African mining platform whose value is driven as much by financing architecture, smelter integration, and logistics optionality as by ore grades or copper prices alone.

## Reported-period takeaways

### Latest period

- The latest reported period by `Monday, August 10, 2026` is `Q2 2026`, reported on `2026-07-29`.
- Profit attributable to owners was `$49.7 million`, compared with `$44.1 million` in `Q2 2025`. Company-level adjusted EBITDA was about `$179 million`, compared with about `$132 million` in the prior-year quarter.
- The quarter also shows why this packet is not just a copper-price story. Kamoa-Kakula's copper inventory remained above `40,000` tonnes, while Kipushi's unsold zinc concentrate inventory increased by about `14,000` tonnes because of timing around sales and exports.
- As of `June 30, 2026`, cash and cash equivalents were about `$635 million`, total assets were about `$7.803 billion`, and total liabilities were about `$2.003 billion`.
- Development spending remained heavy. `Platreef` capital expenditure was about `$83 million` in the quarter and about `$150 million` in the first half, while the company also kept investing at `Kamoa-Kakula` and `Kipushi`.
- Structural theme: `Q2 2026` shows Ivanhoe as a throughput-and-buildout system where inventories, export timing, smelter economics, and development capital can matter as much as the headline metal price.

### Period minus 1

- `Q1 2026`, reported on `2026-05-06`, is the clearest quarter for showing the mismatch between operational progress and reported consolidated earnings.
- Ivanhoe recorded a loss of about `$2 million` for the quarter, compared with a profit of about `$122 million` in `Q1 2025`. The main reason was its share of loss from `Kamoa-Kakula`, which reflected a `$183 million` tax adjustment after settlement of tax claims.
- Even with that accounting hit, company-level adjusted EBITDA was about `$191 million`, compared with about `$226 million` in `Q1 2025`.
- Liquidity remained substantial. Ivanhoe had about `$754 million` of cash and short-term deposits as of `March 31, 2026`.
- This quarter is also where the operating-system story becomes more distinctive. Kamoa-Kakula's on-site smelter had ramped to about `60%` of capacity, sulphuric acid by-product credits were offsetting diesel inflation, and logistics charges dropped sharply because the operation was exporting `99.7%`-pure copper anodes instead of lower-contained-copper concentrate.
- The quarter also highlighted logistical fragility. The first anode shipment moved via the `Lobito Railway Corridor`, but flooding in Angola temporarily damaged the corridor and interrupted shipments until repairs were completed.
- Structural theme: `Q1 2026` shows why Ivanhoe should not be read as a simple earnings packet. Tax disputes, JV accounting, smelter ramp, sulphuric acid credits, and corridor reliability all change the economics at once.

### Period minus 2

- `Q4 2025`, reported on `2026-02-18`, is the correct base period because it anchors both the full-year `2025` read and the pre-`2026` smelter-ramp and inventory-destocking setup.
- Ivanhoe's profit attributable to owners in `Q4 2025` was about `$46 million`, and the company had about `$885 million` in cash and short-term deposits at year-end.
- The year-end read is especially useful for explaining how this company differs from the other copper packets. Kamoa-Kakula sold `351,674` tonnes of payable copper in `2025`, realizing about `$3.281 billion` of revenue at the joint-venture level, while Ivanhoe's own consolidated earnings reflected only its attributable share after financing, development, and ownership structure effects.
- Management explicitly framed `2026` as the year when `Kamoa-Kakula` would destock unsold copper concentrate as the on-site smelter became operational and margins improved through lower logistics costs and sulphuric acid sales.
- Year-end capital framing also matters. `Platreef` spent about `$252 million` in `2025`, and its `2026` capital guidance stayed heavy because the mine is moving through its next expansion stage rather than simply harvesting cash.
- Structural theme: `Q4 2025` makes Ivanhoe legible as a company where project pipeline, financing depth, and downstream integration are inseparable from the copper story itself.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Ivanhoe adds the African copper-and-development platform lane, where copper is the center of gravity but zinc, PGMs, nickel, and large future mine builds all affect the valuation and risk profile.
- Consumer: there is no direct consumer demand read, but the packet sits behind electrification, grid buildout, infrastructure, chemicals supply, and industrial metals demand.
- Cultural: there is no retail-facing angle. The institutional signal is that resource nationalism, tax settlement risk, and infrastructure dependence still heavily shape how critical-minerals value is created in emerging mining jurisdictions.
- Societal: this is one of the clearest packets in the branch for local-infrastructure dependence. Corridor reliability, grid power consistency, regional flood damage, labour and community stability, and host-country fiscal terms are all directly earnings-relevant.
- Regulatory: the packet matters not only because of mining permits but because tax claims, export routes, and financing agreements can shift consolidated outcomes materially.
- Capital allocation: Ivanhoe is a strong packet for development-stage capital intensity. Senior notes, project-finance facilities, equity funding into joint ventures, and sustained spend at `Platreef` and `Kamoa-Kakula` all sit inside one growth architecture.
- Industrial: the packet shows a different kind of copper leverage. Smelter integration, sulphuric acid by-product sales, anode exports, zinc inventory timing, and rail-corridor access can move results as much as mine output.
- Comparative read: `Freeport-McMoRan` is the giant-asset and operating-rights copper packet, `Southern Copper` is the Peru/Mexico and by-product-supported packet, `First Quantum` is the restart-risk and political-permission packet, `Hudbay` is the mid-tier copper-plus-gold diversification packet, `Lundin` is the simplified Chile-and-Brazil platform, and `Ivanhoe` adds the African JV-heavy buildout-and-smelter-integration packet.
