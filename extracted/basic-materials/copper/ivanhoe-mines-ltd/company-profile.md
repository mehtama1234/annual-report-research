## What changed

- Ivanhoe adds a copper packet that behaves differently from `Freeport`, `Southern Copper`, `First Quantum`, `Hudbay`, or `Lundin`. This is a Southern Africa development-and-ramp platform where Kamoa-Kakula dominates the current economics, but the company read also depends materially on `Kipushi`, `Platreef`, project-finance capacity, smelter ramp execution, and corridor logistics.
- The annual package is not a single glossy `2025 annual report`. Instead, the authoritative `2025` chain is the audited annual financial statements, the annual `MD&A`, the `AIF`, and the year-end results page. That distinction matters because it reflects how this branch should handle foreign issuers cleanly when AnnualReports is absent or unhelpful.
- Ivanhoe is also structurally different because much of the economic engine sits inside the `Kamoa Holding` joint venture rather than only in consolidated revenue. That means headline profit can swing sharply with joint-venture profit share, tax adjustments, inventory movements, and ramp conditions even when the operating assets themselves remain strategically strong.

## Open questions

- Can Ivanhoe translate Kamoa-Kakula's smelter and logistics advantages into more stable consolidated earnings, or will joint-venture accounting and periodic tax or inventory distortions keep masking the operational improvement?
- Does `Platreef` deepen the long-cycle optionality enough to justify the capital intensity, or does it mainly add another financing and execution burden on top of an already complex African mining platform?
