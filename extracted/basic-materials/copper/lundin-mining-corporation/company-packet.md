# Lundin Mining Corporation Company Packet

- Target reported-period window:
  - latest period: `Q2 2026`
  - period minus 1: `Q1 2026`
  - period minus 2: `Q4 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- Lundin belongs in this archive as the simplified multi-asset copper system built around Chile and Brazil rather than a giant diversified-miner model or a single-country restart story. The `2025` materials make clear that the company is now centered on `Caserones`, `Candelaria`, and `Chapada`, with `Vicuña` as the long-cycle growth engine.
- The saved AnnualReports page confirms the `Copper` taxonomy, but the authoritative `2025` annual chain had to come from Lundin Mining's own reporting hub. The financial-reports page lists both the `2025 Swedish Annual Report` and the `2025 Annual MD&A and Financial Statements`.
- Full-year `2025` revenue from continuing operations was `$4,053.2 million`, adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was `$1,917.1 million`, free cash flow from continuing operations was `$538.9 million`, and net cash at year-end was `$77.4 million`.
- The annual read is also a portfolio-shaping story. Lundin had already sold `Neves-Corvo` and `Zinkgruvan` in `April 2025`, then agreed in `December 2025` to sell `Eagle`, which closed in `January 2026`. That makes `2025` the inflection year in which the company stopped reading like a broader base-metals operator and started reading like a more focused copper platform.
- Mine-level concentration is now much easier to see. In `2025`, continuing-operations revenue was about `$1,769.0 million` from `Candelaria`, `$1,618.8 million` from `Caserones`, and `$665.4 million` from `Chapada`. The system is still diversified across assets, but it is no longer diversified across as many operating regions or metal complexes.
- Bigger-picture annual theme: Lundin is using divestitures, balance-sheet repair, and targeted copper-heavy reinvestment to turn a broad mining portfolio into a more explicit electrification-and-infrastructure metals platform.

## Reported-period takeaways

### Latest period

- The latest reported period by `Monday, August 10, 2026` is `Q2 2026`, prepared as of `2026-08-05`.
- Revenue was `$1,212.7 million`, net earnings from continuing operations were `$370.7 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$658.0 million`, free cash flow from operations was `$360.2 million`, and free cash flow was `$265.0 million`.
- Copper production was `76,877` tonnes and gold production was `33,427` ounces. Revenue by mine was `$517.9 million` from `Caserones`, `$476.2 million` from `Candelaria`, and `$218.6 million` from `Chapada`.
- Realized prices were unusually supportive: copper reached `$6.51` per pound, gold reached `$4,385` per ounce, and molybdenum reached `$38.33` per pound.
- Balance-sheet flexibility remained intact even after growth moves. Lundin ended the quarter with total debt of `$186.8 million`, cash of `$265.8 million`, and net cash of `$79.0 million` after spending `$215.0 million` to acquire an additional `5%` interest in `Caserones` and a `30.9%` interest in `Los Helados`.
- The most useful structural detail is the operating and infrastructure exposure after quarter-end. Severe winter storms in Chile caused regional flooding and snowfall, temporarily suspending `Caserones` for `13` days and pushing management to expect `Caserones` production toward the lower half of the annual guidance range.
- Structural theme: `Q2 2026` shows that a stronger copper tape and good mine execution can still be filtered through weather, site access, power reliability, and capital deployment into adjacent district growth.

### Period minus 1

- `Q1 2026`, prepared as of `2026-05-06`, is the cleanest quarter for seeing what the simplified portfolio can do in a strong pricing environment before the `Q2` acquisitions and storm effects complicate the read.
- Revenue reached `$1,158.8 million`, net earnings were `$387.0 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$626.7 million`, free cash flow from operations was `$379.7 million`, and free cash flow was `$313.6 million`.
- Cash and cash equivalents reached `$565.4 million`, while net cash rose to `$249.4 million`.
- Revenue by mine was `$506.3 million` from `Caserones`, `$453.4 million` from `Candelaria`, and `$199.1 million` from `Chapada`. Revenue by metal was still predominantly copper at `$971.4 million`, with gold contributing `$123.1 million`.
- Realized prices reached `$5.70` per pound for copper and `$5,123` per ounce for gold. That matters because `Q1 2026` combines higher prices with a much cleaner capital structure than the company had a year earlier.
- The quarter also made the long-cycle growth story more explicit. Management spent `$52.2 million` of capital at `Vicuña` during the quarter, continued targeting a sanctioning decision as early as the end of `2026`, and described the district as a staged project intended to become a globally significant copper operation.
- Structural theme: `Q1 2026` is the quarter that proves Lundin is no longer only a current-production miner. It is a current-cash-flow copper platform that is actively trying to turn that cash flow into district-scale future optionality.

### Period minus 2

- `Q4 2025`, prepared as of `2026-02-19`, is the right base period because it anchors both the full-year `2025` result and the pre-`2026` acquisition structure.
- Revenue from continuing operations was `$1,301.5 million`, gross profit was `$496.8 million`, net earnings from continuing operations were `$912.3 million`, adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was `$686.4 million`, and free cash flow from continuing operations was `$388.3 million`.
- Copper revenue was `$1,128.1 million` in the quarter and full-year copper revenue was `$3,436.2 million`, which shows how copper-heavy the business had already become before the extra `Caserones` and `Los Helados` exposure was added.
- Full-year continuing-operations revenue was `$4,053.2 million`, full-year adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was `$1,917.1 million`, and full-year net earnings from continuing operations were `$1,417.7 million`.
- One important nuance is that not all of the earnings quality was purely operating. `Q4 2025` net earnings benefited from a deferred tax recovery of `$517.0 million` at `Caserones`, while `Chapada` also took a non-cash long-term ore-stockpile write-down of `$99.9 million`.
- Still, the quarter makes the operating identity legible. `Caserones` generated `$598.4 million` of revenue in `Q4`, `Candelaria` generated `$518.5 million`, and `Chapada` generated `$184.6 million`, showing a more balanced three-asset copper system than a single-mine packet would.
- Structural theme: `Q4 2025` shows the combination that now defines Lundin: concentrated copper exposure, cleaner balance sheet, continued by-product support from gold and molybdenum, and an increasingly visible need to execute on future district growth without losing financial discipline.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Lundin adds the focused multi-asset copper lane that sits between giant diversified miners and more fragile restart- or jurisdiction-dominated copper names.
- Consumer: there is no direct consumer-demand read, but the packet is a useful proxy for physical electrification exposure because copper demand only becomes investable when actual mines, tailings facilities, power systems, access roads, and desalination or pipeline infrastructure all work together.
- Cultural: there is no retail-facing culture angle, but there is a corporate-positioning signal. Management is explicitly reframing the company around strategic copper growth, top-ten producer ambitions, and district-scale relevance rather than simple base-metals diversification.
- Societal: this is a useful cross-border resource-development packet because it sits across Chile, Brazil, and Argentina. Community relationships, permitting, grid access, infrastructure agreements, and provincial or national fiscal regimes are central to value realization.
- Regulatory: `Vicuña`'s `RIGI` approval in Argentina, the pending San Juan provincial arrangements, and ongoing Chile operating and infrastructure permissions make regulation a growth enabler rather than only a constraint.
- Capital allocation: Lundin is one of the cleaner examples in the branch of portfolio simplification followed by targeted recommitment. It sold non-core mines, rebuilt the balance sheet, then used cash to increase `Caserones` ownership, add `Los Helados`, and keep advancing `Vicuña`.
- Industrial: the packet shows that copper exposure is not just a price call. Weather shocks, power availability, freight and site-access issues, diesel and FX pressure, and district infrastructure planning can all reshape earnings and guidance.
- Comparative read: `Freeport-McMoRan` is the giant-asset and operating-rights copper packet, `Southern Copper` is the Peru/Mexico and by-product-supported packet, `First Quantum` is the restart-risk and political-permission packet, `Hudbay` is the mid-tier copper-plus-gold diversification packet, and `Lundin` adds the simplified Chile/Brazil operating platform plus `Vicuña` growth-optionality packet.
