## What changed

- Lundin adds a cleaner pure-copper system than the diversified miners but a less fragile one than restart-risk names. The company is now a simplified Chile-and-Brazil operating platform built around `Caserones`, `Candelaria`, and `Chapada`, with `Vicuña` and `Los Helados` supplying the longer-cycle growth lane.
- The annual `2025` package makes the portfolio reshaping legible. Lundin exited `Neves-Corvo`, `Zinkgruvan`, and then `Eagle`, leaving a more focused copper-heavy system and ending `2025` with net cash of `$77.4 million` versus net debt of `$1,332.4 million` a year earlier.
- `Q1 2026` shows the benefit of that simplification. Revenue reached `$1,158.8 million`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$626.7 million`, free cash flow reached `$313.6 million`, and net cash rose to `$249.4 million` as strong copper and gold prices met a tighter asset base.
- `Q2 2026` shows why this is still an infrastructure-and-operations packet, not just a metal-price winner. Revenue rose again to `$1,212.7 million`, adjusted EBITDA to `$658.0 million`, and free cash flow to `$265.0 million`, but the company also had to absorb higher diesel costs, FX pressure, and then severe winter storms in Chile after quarter-end that temporarily suspended Caserones for `13` days.
- Growth optionality matters more here than in a simple mid-tier miner read. `Vicuña` received `RIGI` approval in Argentina, management continued to target a sanctioning decision as early as year-end `2026`, and the integrated study still points to a potential top-five global copper, gold, and silver complex. Lundin also used `Q2 2026` to close the acquisition of an additional `5%` interest in `Caserones` and a `30.9%` interest in `Los Helados` for `$215.0 million`.
- Compared with the rest of the branch, Lundin is a useful bridge name:
  - less globally diversified than `Rio Tinto`, `BHP`, or `Glencore`
  - less by-product-extreme than `Hudbay`
  - less restart- and politics-dominated than `First Quantum`
  - more explicitly tied to the copper-growth pipeline than `Teck`

## Open questions

- Can Lundin preserve its net-cash flexibility if `Vicuña`, `Los Helados`, and `Chapada` expansion spending all accelerate into the same window?
- Does the simplified portfolio make Lundin structurally more investable, or does it simply concentrate weather, Chilean operating, and copper-price exposure into fewer assets?
