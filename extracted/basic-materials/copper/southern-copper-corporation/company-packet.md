## Annual report takeaways

- Southern Copper adds a second copper archetype to the lane, but it behaves differently from `Freeport-McMoRan`. `Freeport` is more about giant-asset reliability and global optionality. Southern Copper is more concentrated in Peru and Mexico, with earnings shaped by copper price strength, by-product leverage, ore grades, and the political and social durability of a smaller set of core mining systems.
- AnnualReports remained useful for taxonomy confirmation, but as of `Monday, August 10, 2026` it still only exposed Southern Copper's `2024` annual package. The authoritative `2025` annual chain therefore had to come from the company-hosted annual filing PDF and SEC `10-K`.
- The `2025` annual base year was exceptionally strong. Net sales reached a record `$13,420.0 million`, net income reached `$4,334.9 million`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$7,822.4 million`, and cash flow from operating activities reached `$4,752.1 million`.
- This was not only a copper-price story. Management said the `2025` result was also driven by higher by-product production and improved prices across copper, molybdenum, zinc, and silver. That matters because Southern Copper is not a pure single-metal exposure. Its earnings quality is heavily supported by the by-product basket.
- `2025` copper production totaled `954,270` tons and management described it as in line with the annual plan. The annual discussion also described Southern Copper as one of the world's largest copper mining companies, with principal operations in Peru and Mexico and exploration programs in Chile and Argentina.
- The balance sheet at `2025-12-31` gives the company real scale: total assets of `$21,381.4 million`, cash and cash equivalents of `$4,304.6 million`, and long-term debt of `$6,750.7 million`.
- The strategic read is that Southern Copper is a hard-asset platform sitting at the center of electrification, power buildout, and industrial metals demand, but its economics still depend on mine physics and jurisdiction. Lower grades, recoveries, social friction, permitting, and long-cycle expansion sequencing matter as much as the copper tape.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net sales reached a quarterly record of `$4,289.0 million`, net income hit a record `$1,670.0 million`, adjusted EBITDA hit a record `$2,856.0 million`, and first-half operating cash flow reached `$3,683.0 million`.
- Price and mix: the release said growth was primarily driven by better price variances for copper, molybdenum, zinc, and silver. This is a good reminder that Southern Copper is not just a volume story. Price realization across the whole by-product basket can swing results materially.
- Operating read: copper production fell `3.5%` quarter on quarter to `230,662` tonnes. Mexican operations improved `3.2%` sequentially, but Peruvian production fell `12.0%`, mainly because of lower ore grades.
- By-products: mined silver production fell `3.8%`, zinc production fell `14.5%`, and molybdenum production fell `11.0%` year over year in the quarter. Production weakness at Toquepala, Cuajone, Buenavista, and other mines shows how exposed the model is to mine-level variability even during a period of extreme profitability.
- Balance sheet and financing: by `2026-06-30`, cash and cash equivalents had increased to `$5,665.0 million`, while long-term debt had increased to `$7,994.4 million`. This reflects a company generating huge cash but still running a capital- and financing-intensive buildout cycle.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` shows Southern Copper at its strongest when copper-system scarcity and by-product pricing line up together. It also shows why the archive needs to separate copper demand optimism from actual mine delivery risk.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net sales were `$4,251.4 million`, net income attributable to SCC was `$1,576.9 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2,712.8 million`, and cash flow from operating activities was `$1,694.5 million`.
- Cost structure: Southern Copper reported `Q1 2026` operating cash cost per pound of copper, net of by-product credits, of `-$0.11`, versus `$0.77` in `Q1 2025`. That is a powerful sign of how much by-product economics can distort the apparent cost position of a copper producer.
- Production: copper production fell `4.0%` versus `Q1 2025`, driven mainly by lower Peruvian production because of lower ore grades and recoveries, partially offset by stronger output at La Caridad.
- By-products and capex: mined silver production rose `11.1%`, zinc production rose `2.0%`, molybdenum production fell `2.2%`, and capital investments rose to `$441.9 million`.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` is a strong example of why mining packets need more than income-statement numbers. Southern Copper delivered record earnings while still showing operating fragility beneath the surface: grade pressure in Peru, by-product dependence, and a capital program that remains heavy even during a windfall period.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net sales were `$3,869.8 million`, net income was `$1,307.9 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2,310.5 million`, and the quarter ended a record full year for revenue, EBITDA, and earnings.
- Full-year frame: full-year `2025` net sales were `$13,420.0 million`, net income was `$4,334.9 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$7,822.4 million`, cash flow from operations was `$4,752.1 million`, and copper production was `954,270` tons.
- Production read: `Q4 2025` copper production increased `1.4%` quarter on quarter, helped by La Caridad, Toquepala, Cuajone, and IMMSA, partially offset by weaker Buenavista production.
- By-products: management highlighted higher molybdenum, silver, and zinc production in the quarter and across the full year, especially from the Buenavista zinc concentrator and higher mined-silver output at multiple mines.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` is the cleanest anchor quarter for the packet. It shows Southern Copper entering `2026` with extraordinary price leverage and by-product support, but still as a mine-sequencing and grade-management story rather than a simple “copper up, profits up” line.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Southern Copper strengthens the copper lane by adding a more concentrated Peru/Mexico system. This matters because copper is not one commodity story. It is a set of geologically and politically specific operating systems.
- Consumer: the company is upstream, but the demand map is broad. Southern Copper sits behind electrification, grid buildout, construction, industrial machinery, electronics, and transport.
- Cultural: this is another reminder that the digital and energy-transition economy still depends on difficult physical extraction systems. Copper availability is not abstract.
- Societal: Southern Copper brings more visible community, permitting, and regional-development exposure than some U.S.-centered industrial names. The economic value is large, but so is the social footprint.
- Regulatory: mining law, environmental controls, water permits, consultation processes, tax and royalty regimes, and debt-market access all matter directly to the investment case.
- Capital allocation: the company combines very strong cash generation with a large capital investment program and a meaningful dividend posture. That creates a real tension between harvesting current prices and funding long-cycle growth.
- Industrial: the current filing set shows why copper should be treated as an infrastructure bottleneck. The bottleneck is not just demand. It is whether grades, recoveries, labor, permitting, and project sequencing allow metal to actually reach market.
- Comparative read: `Freeport-McMoRan` shows the giant-asset and global-optionality version of copper; `Southern Copper` shows the two-country, by-product-rich, lower-cost, grade-sensitive, long-cycle mine-development version.
