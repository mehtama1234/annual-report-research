## What changed

- Copper platform: Southern Copper adds a high-quality copper-mining system rather than a metals-conversion or distribution system. The operating base is concentrated in Peru and Mexico, so earnings are driven by ore grades, mine sequencing, metallurgy, community and permitting exposure, and the cadence of large mine and infrastructure projects.
- Annual base year: `2025` was a record year. Net sales hit `$13,420.0 million`, net income reached `$4,334.9 million`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$7,822.4 million`, and operating cash flow reached `$4,752.1 million`.
- Balance sheet: at `2025-12-31`, Southern Copper had total assets of `$21,381.4 million`, cash and cash equivalents of `$4,304.6 million`, and long-term debt of `$6,750.7 million`.
- Production frame: `2025` copper production totaled `954,270` tons and management described it as in line with the annual plan, while by-product production and prices provided major support to the earnings profile.
- Geographic and operating footprint: Southern Copper explicitly describes itself as one of the world's largest copper mining companies, with principal operations in Peru and Mexico and exploration programs in Chile and Argentina.
- Q1 2026 surge: the company opened `2026` with net sales of `$4,251.4 million`, record net income of `$1,576.9 million`, adjusted EBITDA of `$2,712.8 million`, operating cash flow of `$1,694.5 million`, and capital investments of `$441.9 million`.
- Q2 2026 surge: `Q2 2026` then extended that step-up with record net sales of `$4,289.0 million`, record net income of `$1,670.0 million`, record adjusted EBITDA of `$2,856.0 million`, and first-half operating cash flow of `$3,683.0 million`.
- Peru versus Mexico: the filings and releases make the current operating split very clear. Mexican operations improved sequentially in `Q2 2026`, but lower ore grades at the Peruvian mines pulled down consolidated copper production and several by-products.
- Financing and capex: by `2026-06-30`, long-term debt had risen to `$7,994.4 million`, reflecting a more active financing posture alongside a large capital program and still-rising cash balances of `$5,665.0 million`.

## Open questions

- How much of the current earnings strength is sustainable if copper and silver prices normalize while Peruvian grades remain weak?
- Does the project and reserve pipeline translate into durable production growth, or does Southern Copper remain more dependent on price and by-product support than the headline margins imply?
