## What changed

- CEMEX is trying to convert a cyclical cement identity into a broader infrastructure, urbanization, and operating-efficiency story. The `2025` annual and the 2026 quarter chain keep returning to cost discipline, pricing, portfolio simplification, and structurally leaner operations rather than simple volume expansion.
- Mexico has become the clearest incremental-demand engine in the packet. Government-backed social programs, Rural Roads, and scaled social housing activity show up repeatedly as concrete demand drivers rather than background macro commentary.
- The United States business is more mixed. Infrastructure and industrial demand remain supportive, including IIJA-linked work and AI-related industrial construction, but residential affordability pressure, freight costs, and weather disruptions keep limiting margin flow-through.
- Europe has become a clearer pricing and regulation story. Management repeatedly frames the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism and the phase-down of free EU ETS allowances as supportive for cement pricing.
- The portfolio is also being simplified. The annual statements show divestitures in Panama and the Dominican Republic and a greater focus on businesses and regions with better operating density or pricing support.

## Open questions

- Can Project Cutting Edge keep delivering structural savings after the easiest cost actions are exhausted?
- How durable is Mexico’s public-program and social-housing support if fiscal priorities shift or project execution slows?
- Does U.S. infrastructure and AI-related industrial demand offset residential affordability pressure strongly enough to stabilize U.S. margins?
- How much of Europe’s pricing support from carbon regulation becomes durable margin versus a temporary pass-through benefit?
