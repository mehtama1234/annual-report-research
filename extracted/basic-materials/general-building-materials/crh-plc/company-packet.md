# CRH Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: CRH plc
- Sector: Basic Materials
- Industry: General Building Materials
- Ticker: `CRH`
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Why this name matters

- CRH is one of the clearest ways to connect basic materials to the physical layer behind software, AI, water systems, transport rebuilds, reshoring, and utility expansion. It does not just sell heavy materials. It increasingly packages aggregates, roads, water, infrastructure products, and building solutions into a connected operating platform.
- That makes it a useful flagship comparison against the narrower materials names already in this batch. Martin Marietta and Vulcan are stronger scarcity-and-aggregates reads; CRH is the broader allocator that tries to turn scale, acquisitions, and portfolio design into recurring infrastructure cash generation.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual chain shows a company that wants to be read less as a cyclical commodity producer and more as a capital-compounding infrastructure platform. The `Q4/FY 2025` release said full-year revenue reached `$37.4B`, net income reached `$3.8B`, and adjusted EBITDA reached `$7.7B`, with adjusted EBITDA margin rising to `20.5%`.
- Management explicitly tied that result to favorable end-market demand, disciplined commercial execution, contributions from acquisitions, continued pricing momentum, and disciplined cost control rather than to any single one-off boom.
- The most important strategic signal in the annual read is capital allocation. Management said CRH deployed `$5.8B` in value-accretive growth investments across its connected portfolio in `2025` while also returning `$2.2B` to shareholders through dividends and share buybacks.
- The filings also make the business mix more important than the headline sector label suggests. CRH now reports across `Americas Materials Solutions`, `Americas Building Solutions`, and `International Solutions`, and the `10-Q` describes business lines spanning essential materials, road solutions, building and infrastructure solutions, and outdoor living solutions.
- This matters because the company is using acquisitions and divestitures to move deeper into higher-value infrastructure categories. The packet is not just about cement and aggregates demand. It is about the ownership of a wider construction-input and installed-infrastructure network.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Revenue increased `6%` to `$10.8B`, net income increased `13%` to `$1.5B`, adjusted EBITDA increased `7%` to `$2.6B`, net income margin reached `14.0%`, and adjusted EBITDA margin reached `24.4%`.
- The quality signal was pricing and mix. Management said positive pricing momentum, good underlying demand, disciplined commercial execution, performance-improvement initiatives, and acquisition contributions drove the quarter.
- The latest quarter also sharpened the strategic direction. Management said it had invested `$1.4B` in `17` value-accretive acquisitions year-to-date and agreed to acquire Arcosa for `$8.5B`, describing the deal as reinforcing CRH’s position as the leading aggregates and critical-infrastructure player in North America.
- The segment detail from the `10-Q` is especially useful. `Americas Materials Solutions` revenue increased `10%`, supported by positive pricing momentum and acquisitions. `Americas Building Solutions` revenue declined `2%` as strong data-center and utility-infrastructure demand was offset by divestitures. `International Solutions` revenue increased `5%` on pricing, activity gains in some markets, and acquisitions.
- Pressure points did not disappear. The `10-Q` says subdued residential demand, cost inflation, higher freight and input costs, and the drag from divestitures still mattered, especially in `Americas Building Solutions`.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Revenue increased `9%` to `$7.4B`, adjusted EBITDA increased `18%` to `$0.6B`, and adjusted EBITDA margin improved to `8.0%`, but the company posted a seasonal net loss of `($0.2B)`, worse than the prior year because of higher depreciation, impairment charges, and interest expense.
- The first-quarter message was that early-season activity and acquisition carryover were strong enough to offset macro anxiety. Management highlighted good momentum from early-season project activity, disciplined commercial execution, and positive acquisition contributions.
- This quarter also makes the platform story more concrete. Management said it was reallocating capital into higher-growth, more connected businesses and specifically called out `Axius Water` as part of the acquisition set.
- The cultural and industrial read here is important: water infrastructure is not a side pocket. It is one of the ways CRH is trying to capture recurring replacement demand tied to aging utility networks and municipal underinvestment.
- The first-quarter release reaffirmed full-year `2026` guidance for net income of `$3.9B-$4.1B` and adjusted EBITDA of `$8.1B-$8.5B`, signaling that management viewed the demand environment as durable enough to absorb seasonal noise and macro uncertainty.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Full-year `2025` revenue reached `$37.4B`, net income reached `$3.8B`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$7.7B`, net income margin reached `10.1%`, and adjusted EBITDA margin reached `20.5%`.
- The strongest year-end pattern is continued margin expansion. Management said `2025` delivered double-digit adjusted EBITDA growth and a `12th` consecutive year of margin expansion, reinforcing its self-description as the leading capital compounder in the industry.
- Capital allocation is central to the interpretation. The company said it invested `$4.1B` in `38` value-accretive acquisitions during the year-end release and declared a quarterly dividend of `$0.39` per share alongside a new `$0.3B` quarterly buyback.
- Guidance for `2026` also matters because it links infrastructure optimism to specific financial expectations. Management said it expected `2026` net income of `$3.9B-$4.1B` and adjusted EBITDA of `$8.1B-$8.5B`.
- This is also the point where CRH’s identity becomes clearer: it is trying to be paid for scale, local market power, disciplined pricing, and portfolio evolution rather than just for raw exposure to cement demand.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- CRH is not a consumer brand, but it sits under a set of social demands that have become more visible and more politically charged: housing affordability, aging water systems, road repair backlogs, grid resilience, data-center power demand, and industrial reshoring.
- The company also reflects a broader cultural shift in infrastructure spending. Publics and private developers increasingly want faster build cycles, lower failure risk, and fewer supply-chain surprises. That favors scaled platforms that can bundle materials, products, logistics, and project support rather than single-line commodity suppliers.
- Water exposure matters more than it first appears. The company’s emphasis on Axius Water, utility infrastructure, and connected infrastructure solutions is a direct read on how invisible civil systems are becoming investable themes because of deferred maintenance and climate stress.
- There is also a geographic and political concentration pattern here. The strongest narratives are North American and especially U.S.-centric: Sun Belt migration, public works, utility upgrades, data-center load growth, and reindustrialization all channel more spending toward local heavy-input networks.
- CRH also captures a social asymmetry that repeats across the archive: end users experience infrastructure through reliability and convenience, while the monetization happens through hidden layers of aggregates, paving, water fittings, and utility products that few people think about until systems fail.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Pricing remains essential. The `Q2 2026` `10-Q` says `Americas Materials Solutions` benefited from positive pricing momentum, with aggregates pricing up `5%` and asphalt pricing up `6%` in road solutions. That pricing power is the core protection against cost inflation.
- Residential softness is still a real drag. The filings repeatedly note subdued residential demand, especially in outdoor living and parts of building solutions, even while public and nonresidential categories stay firmer.
- Portfolio churn creates both opportunity and noise. Acquisitions support growth, but divestitures can distort revenue comparisons, pressure segment results in the near term, and complicate simple volume reads.
- Balance-sheet risk is manageable but rising. The `Q2 2026` `10-Q` says net debt was `$15.4B` at `2026-06-30`, cash and cash equivalents plus restricted cash were `$3.1B`, gross indebtedness was about `$18.4B`, and the company expects to add about `$8.75B` of debt in connection with the Arcosa acquisition.
- Working capital and financing costs still matter. The first-quarter release explicitly tied the net-loss deterioration to higher interest expense, while the `Q2 2026` filing notes higher tax payments and divestiture timing effects on operating cash flow.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Infrastructure demand is broadening from roads and buildings into water, utility, and data-adjacent systems.` CRH’s mix shows how the physical layer behind AI and electrification increasingly monetizes through old-economy networks.
- `The winning model is not just ownership of hard assets.` It is the ability to combine local scarcity, logistics reach, pricing systems, and acquisition discipline into a higher-margin connected portfolio.
- `Residential weakness does not mean total construction weakness.` CRH keeps showing that public works, utility infrastructure, industrial projects, and data-center demand can offset housing softness in important parts of the portfolio.
- `Acquisition discipline is becoming part of the moat.` The company’s strategy depends on redeploying capital into connected, higher-growth niches rather than just sitting on a static materials base.
- `Scale does not remove cyclicality, but it changes where the risk sits.` For CRH, the risk is less a single-market collapse and more the combination of leverage, execution on large deals, residential softness, and the need to keep pricing ahead of cost inflation.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Martin Marietta Materials, Inc.`: Martin is the cleaner aggregates scarcity and reserve-depth read; CRH is the more diversified infrastructure allocator using acquisitions and connected-product breadth to widen monetization beyond quarry economics.
- Against `Vulcan Materials Company`: Vulcan is the pure heavy-materials pricing discipline comparison, while CRH adds roads, water, utility products, and building solutions that make the portfolio more downstream and more mixed.
- Against `Core & Main, Inc.`: Core & Main captures the distribution layer for aging-water replacement; CRH captures more of the upstream materials and manufactured-infrastructure-product layer that can feed the same replacement cycle.
- Against `AECOM`: AECOM monetizes design, advisory, and planning complexity; CRH monetizes the physical materials, products, and installed-systems layers that convert those plans into concrete infrastructure throughput.
