# Knife River Corporation Packet

  - quarter 1: Q2 2026

## Why this name matters

- Knife River is the best follow-on to Vulcan in this archive because it adds a second heavy-materials and infrastructure-buildout read, but with a more explicit mix of aggregates, downstream materials, and contracting pull-through.
- That matters for the user’s bigger-picture lens. Vulcan is a cleaner local-scarcity and pricing-discipline story. Knife River adds the same physical-substrate layer plus more direct exposure to public-works backlog, seasonal construction cadence, contracting mix, and acquisition-led market expansion.

## Annual report takeaways

- The annual filing describes Knife River as an aggregates-based construction materials and contracting services provider with `1.3 billion` tons of aggregate reserves. That reserve base underpins the whole thesis.
- The `10-K` says about `35%` of `2025` aggregate volume was used internally to support higher-value downstream products such as ready-mix concrete and asphalt, as well as contracting services. That makes the business more than a quarry operator. It is a vertically integrated physical-delivery model.
- Full-year `2025` revenue was `$3.146B`, net income was `$157.1M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$496.5M`. Those numbers matter, but the more important signal is demand quality. Backlog ended `2025` at `$1.0B`, with about `89%` tied to publicly funded projects.
- Management’s `Competitive EDGE` framework is worth taking seriously. The filing defines the acronym as EBITDA Margin Improvement, Discipline, Growth, and Excellence. That is effectively the company’s explicit playbook for converting reserve depth and public-infrastructure exposure into sustained margin expansion rather than just cyclical volume.
- The annual materials also frame the geographic strategy clearly: Knife River wants mid-size, higher-growth markets where local reserve position, plant footprint, and transportation advantage support stronger pricing and better pull-through.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Revenue was `$938.6M`, net income was `$43.9M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$139.7M`.
- Management said contracting services revenue rose `20%`, aggregate pricing improved `8%` on a product-mix-adjusted basis, and backlog increased sequentially to `$1.2B`.
- The quarter is useful because it shows both strength and friction at the same time. Volumes and pull-through were strong, but EBITDA and margins were pressured by energy costs, delayed impact projects, and the type and timing of contracting work.
- Capital allocation also sharpened the story. Knife River spent `$244.5M` on growth initiatives in the first half, including `$184.4M` on acquisitions and `$60.1M` on aggregate expansions and greenfield projects. Net leverage reached `3.2x` at June `30`, which is manageable but makes execution and acquisition quality more important.
- Management raised guidance for revenue and aggregate volumes, which suggests the company saw enough demand depth and pricing strength to absorb the quarter’s margin noise.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Revenue was `$410.1M`, net loss was `($79.2M)`, and adjusted EBITDA was `($31.8M)`.
- This quarter is the clearest reminder that even strong construction-materials franchises remain seasonal and weather-sensitive. Knife River’s first quarter still carried winter absorption pressure even as revenue grew `16%` year over year.
- The more constructive read is that the loss profile improved from the prior year on an adjusted EBITDA basis, which suggests the company’s pricing, footprint, and efficiency efforts were still moving in the right direction despite the seasonal trough.
- This quarter matters thematically because it keeps the packet honest. Public-funding exposure and strong reserves do not remove timing volatility. They just make the business more resilient across the full cycle.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Full-year `2025` revenue was `$3.146B`, net income was `$157.1M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$496.5M`.
- Fourth-quarter revenue was `$755.1M` and fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA was `$119.4M`, capping what management described as a record quarter and a strong second-half finish.
- Management highlighted five aggregates-based acquisitions completed in `2025`, high-single-digit aggregate pricing for the year, and a record year-end backlog of `$1.0B`.
- Initial `2026` guidance called for revenue of `$3.3B` to `$3.5B` and adjusted EBITDA of `$520M` to `$560M`, with aggregate volumes and pricing both expected to increase mid-single digits.
- This is the right anchor quarter because it shows Knife River entering `2026` with pricing momentum, acquisition expansion, and a heavily public-funded backlog rather than depending only on a hot private-cycle rebound.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Knife River is far from the end consumer, but it is tightly linked to social and political priorities. Housing formation, suburban expansion, logistics facilities, road maintenance, bridge repair, and state DOT spending all eventually show up in aggregate demand and contracting backlog.
- This is one of the archive’s better reads on how public money becomes physical output. Roughly `89%` of year-end `2025` backlog was tied to publicly funded work, which turns the company into a useful gauge of infrastructure policy translating into actual field activity.
- There is also a geographic and demographic layer. The company’s focus on mid-size, higher-growth markets lines up with Sun Belt and interior-market population movement, local road expansion, utility adjacency, and ongoing warehouse and industrial-site development.
- Culturally, Knife River reinforces the same blind spot visible in Vulcan: markets can become obsessed with digital narratives while ignoring that real economic growth still requires rock, asphalt, concrete, grading, hauling, and public-works execution.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Knife River’s margin structure depends on more than aggregate pricing. It also depends on contracting mix, project timing, energy costs, freight, seasonal weather, and how effectively acquisitions are integrated into the operating footprint.
- Public funding is a stabilizer, but it also introduces timing and award friction. A strong backlog does not mean perfect quarterly conversion.
- Leverage bears watching. The company is using acquisitions and organic growth aggressively, and `3.2x` net leverage at June `30, 2026` means the quality of deal execution matters more if the cycle gets choppier.
- The vertically integrated model is a strength, but it also raises execution complexity. Knife River has to manage reserve depth, plant efficiency, pricing, contracting services, and liquid asphalt inputs rather than just one clean product line.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Public funding improves demand quality but not timing smoothness.` Knife River’s backlog is heavily public-funded, yet quarterly conversion still depends on weather, project timing, and contracting mix.
- `Vertical integration creates margin opportunity.` The company’s reserve depth matters more because it can pull aggregates through ready-mix, asphalt, and contracting work instead of stopping at raw material sales.
- `Physical infrastructure still compounds through local density.` Reserves, plants, trucking, and local network reach are the real operating moats, not abstract scale alone.
- `Acquisition-led growth in basic materials is really footprint strategy.` Knife River’s M&A is not just revenue buying. It is density building, pull-through enhancement, and access expansion in specific markets.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Vulcan Materials Company`: Vulcan is the cleaner pure-play local-scarcity and aggregates-pricing comparison, while Knife River adds more visible contracting and downstream pull-through.
- Against `Core & Main`: Core & Main distributes water infrastructure inputs; Knife River supplies and installs core physical substrate into roads, sites, and public-works projects.
- Against `MasTec`: MasTec is the project-execution layer for power, telecom, and energy infrastructure; Knife River is the local materials-and-contracting layer for roads, sites, and civil buildout.
