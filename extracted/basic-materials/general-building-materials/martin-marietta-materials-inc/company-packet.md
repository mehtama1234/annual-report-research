# Martin Marietta Materials, Inc. Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Martin Marietta Materials, Inc.
- Sector: Basic Materials
- Industry: General Building Materials
- Ticker: `MLM`
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Why this name matters

- Martin Marietta is the highest-value follow-on after Vulcan and Knife River because it adds a megascale aggregates-led operator that is deliberately reshaping itself into a cleaner, higher-margin physical-infrastructure franchise.
- The company is not just a commodity rock story. It is a strong read on pricing power, freight-limited local scarcity, portfolio optimization, infrastructure demand quality, and how public works, warehouses, energy, and data centers translate into real volume and margin.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` `10-K` describes Martin Marietta as a natural resource-based building materials company supplying aggregates through approximately `400` quarries, mines, and distribution yards in `28 states`, `Canada`, and `The Bahamas`.
- Aggregates generated `88%` of the company’s total reportable segment gross profit in `2025`, which makes clear where the economic center of gravity sits.
- Net earnings from continuing operations attributable to Martin Marietta were `$990M` in `2025`.
- The filing shows management still working its `SOAR` framework, but the more important issue is what that framework means in practice: portfolio pruning, bolt-on acquisitions, pricing discipline, and a more concentrated aggregates-led mix.
- The `2025` filing repeatedly frames demand around infrastructure, nonresidential construction, logistics, energy, and data centers, while keeping a more cautious tone on residential construction.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Official results showed revenues increased `21%` to a record `$1.947B`.
- Adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations rose `13%` to `$638M`, while net earnings from continuing operations attributable to Martin Marietta declined `12%` to `$256M`.
- Aggregates shipments increased `17%` to `61.6M` tons, but average selling price per ton decreased `2%` to `$22.74` because of acquisition mix and purchase-accounting noise. Organic mix-adjusted ASP was still positive.
- Aggregates revenues rose `16%` to `$1.533B`, but gross profit per ton was pressured by a `$52M` purchase-accounting charge tied to selling acquired inventory after markup to fair value.
- Management raised full-year `2026` revenue guidance to `$7.2B` to `$7.4B` and reaffirmed adjusted EBITDA guidance of `$2.36B` to `$2.5B`.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Revenues increased `17%` to `$1.362B`.
- First-quarter aggregates shipments increased `12.4%` to `43.9M` tons, driven by organic growth plus partial-quarter contributions from the `QUIKRETE` exchange.
- Average selling price was `$23.70` per ton and was effectively flat year over year because geographic and acquisition mix offset the underlying pricing trend.
- Net earnings from continuing operations attributable to Martin Marietta were `$79M` in the quarter.
- The quarter also mattered strategically: Martin Marietta closed the `QUIKRETE` asset exchange, received `$450M` in cash as part of that transaction, and signed the New Frontier acquisition for another roughly `8M` tons of annual aggregates capacity.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Fourth-quarter revenues were `$1.534B`.
- Adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was `$515M`.
- Full-year adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations reached `$2.065B`, while full-year consolidated adjusted EBITDA reached `$2.320B`.
- The fourth-quarter and year-end release captured the company at the transition point into a more aggregates-led shape, with the pending `QUIKRETE` exchange and the earlier `Premier Magnesia` acquisition both affecting the quality of the future earnings mix.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Martin Marietta sits far from the consumer, but it is a clean indicator of where society is still choosing to build. Roads, warehouses, subdivisions, manufacturing sites, utilities, airports, and data centers all need rock, asphalt, and site work long before they become visible as finished demand.
- The filings make clear that infrastructure and heavy nonresidential remain stronger than residential. That matters because it signals where current U.S. capital formation is actually landing: public works, industrial reshoring, logistics, electrification, and compute-heavy physical buildout.
- Data-center demand is not just a software story. Martin Marietta’s annual filing explicitly ties attractive markets to data-center siting, which is a useful reminder that AI and cloud expansion run through quarries, trucking, aggregates pricing, and local permitting constraints.
- There is also a demographic layer. Sun Belt and high-growth market migration continue to support the exact kinds of physical projects that drive aggregates intensity: roads, site development, utility corridors, and low-rise expansion.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Martin Marietta’s moat is local and physical. Quarry position, hauling distance, reserves, and distribution reach matter more than abstract scale.
- Freight intensity and permitting still shape supply. These are not frictionless products, and local scarcity is part of the pricing engine.
- The portfolio is cleaner after the `QUIKRETE` exchange, but the reported numbers now carry acquisition mix noise and purchase-accounting distortion, especially in near-term gross profit per ton.
- Capital intensity remains central. The company had `$1.2B` of unused borrowing capacity at `2025-12-31` and `2026-03-31`, but that fell to `$742M` at `2026-06-30` as acquisitions and deal preparation moved forward.
- Residential demand was still relatively soft, so near-term resilience depended more on infrastructure and heavy nonresidential than on a housing rebound.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Aggregates are one of the clearest local-scarcity businesses in the archive.` Trucking distance, reserves, and permitting constraints convert physical position into pricing power.
- `The AI and cloud buildout still runs through the physical layer.` Martin Marietta is direct evidence that data-center narratives eventually become quarry demand, site work, and heavy materials volume.
- `Portfolio optimization in heavy materials is really mix engineering.` The `QUIKRETE` exchange and New Frontier acquisition are not random deals. They push Martin Marietta toward a more aggregates-led, higher-margin, more durable earnings base.
- `Public and industrial construction are carrying more of the load than the household cycle.` Infrastructure, manufacturing, logistics, and energy matter more to the current volume story than a broad residential rebound.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Vulcan Materials Company`: Vulcan is the cleaner aggregates benchmark; Martin Marietta adds more explicit portfolio reshaping and specialties exposure.
- Against `Knife River Corporation`: Knife River has more visible contracting and downstream exposure, while Martin Marietta is the larger, purer scarcity-and-pricing franchise.
- Against `MasTec, Inc.`: MasTec is the execution contractor layer; Martin Marietta is the materials substrate feeding those same infrastructure and data-center projects.
