## What changed

- Operating identity: Martin Marietta is an aggregates-led building-materials platform with additional ready-mix, asphalt, paving, and specialties exposure rather than just a quarry operator.
- Footprint: the `2025` `10-K` says the company supplied aggregates through approximately `400` quarries, mines, and distribution yards in `28 states`, `Canada`, and `The Bahamas`; after the `QUIKRETE` asset exchange, the `Q1 2026` `10-Q` described approximately `480` sites.
- Portfolio shape: management exchanged cement and Texas ready-mix assets for roughly `20M` tons of acquired aggregates capacity plus cash, making the company more explicitly aggregates-led.
- Demand mix: filings repeatedly tie the demand base to infrastructure, heavy nonresidential, logistics, manufacturing, energy, and data-center construction, with residential still softer.
- Capital posture: the company kept meaningful liquidity, with `$1.2B` of unused borrowing capacity at both `2025-12-31` and `2026-03-31`, before that buffer tightened to `$742M` at `2026-06-30`.

## Open questions

- How much of the current growth profile is durable organic pricing power versus acquisition and mix effects tied to the `QUIKRETE` exchange and New Frontier?
- Does the shift toward a more purely aggregates-led portfolio improve cycle resilience enough to offset lower near-term reported gross profit per ton from acquisition accounting noise?
- How much upside remains from infrastructure and data-center-linked construction if residential recovery stays slow?
