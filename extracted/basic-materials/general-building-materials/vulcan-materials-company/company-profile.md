## What changed

- Physical layer role: Vulcan gives the archive a direct physical-inputs operator where roads, bridges, water systems, homes, industrial sites, data centers, and public works all begin with stone, sand, gravel, asphalt, concrete, and cement-linked demand.
- Scale and network: the `2025` annual report describes Vulcan as the nation’s largest supplier of construction aggregates, with `425` active aggregates facilities, `71` asphalt facilities, and `76` concrete facilities.
- Pricing and scarcity: this is not a pure volume story. Management keeps emphasizing commercial discipline, local-market positioning, and cash gross profit per ton rather than just headline shipment growth.
- 2025 base year: full-year `2025` net earnings attributable to Vulcan rose to about `$1.077B`, adjusted EBITDA rose to about `$2.324B`, and operating cash flow increased `29%` to about `$1.8B` even though demand stayed muted relative to a more expansionary construction backdrop.
- 2026 quarter pattern: `Q1 2026` and `Q2 2026` show a consistent pattern of price-led profit expansion, resilient public and large-project activity, and enough cost control to keep unit profitability climbing even when weather and diesel costs turned into headwinds.
- Cultural and social signal: population growth, repair backlog, housing need, logistics buildout, and public-infrastructure spending remain durable real-economy forces, even when the market narrative is more focused on software or semiconductors.
