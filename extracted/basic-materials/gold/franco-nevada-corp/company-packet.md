## Annual report takeaways

- Franco-Nevada belongs in this archive as the second flagship royalty-and-streaming precious-metals allocator, but it should not be collapsed into the same read as `Wheaton Precious Metals`. This packet matters because Franco-Nevada keeps a clearer diversified and energy-linked revenue branch inside the royalty model.
- AnnualReports could not supply a usable company page on `2026-08-10`, so the authoritative `2025` annual chain here comes from Franco-Nevada's official investor materials and SEC `40-F` / year-end `6-K` filings.
- The `2025` year-end materials show a business that remained strongly gold-led while still monetizing diversification. `2025` actual GEO sales were `519K`, with `440K` from precious metals and `79K` from diversified assets.
- Revenue concentration stayed high where it mattered. Official year-end materials show `85%` of `2025` revenue came from precious metals, which keeps Franco-Nevada anchored in the gold lane even though it also earns from oil, gas, NGL, iron ore, and other diversified sources.
- The profitability structure remained unusually strong for a mining-adjacent business. The year-end presentation shows annual GEO margin rising to `$3,110` in `2025` from `$2,109` in `2024`, while annual cash cost per GEO was only `$325`.
- Balance-sheet optionality remained one of the main strategic advantages. Franco-Nevada ended `2025` debt-free and presented `$3.1 billion` of available capital as of `December 31, 2025`, which is central to how the company compounds through new royalty and stream transactions instead of direct mine ownership.
- The long-cycle optionality is explicit rather than abstract. Official materials point to more than `230` additional assets that could potentially contribute about `222,000` GEOs of annual production beyond the five-year outlook, with additional Cobre Panama upside if the mine returns to full capacity.
- This packet matters because it gives the archive a harder-edged version of capital-light commodity exposure. Franco-Nevada is not simply a gold-price beneficiary. It is a contract-and-optionality business that can hold gold upside, energy upside, and acquisition capacity in the same structure while staying less exposed to direct operating-cost inflation than a conventional miner.

## Reported-period takeaways

### Latest period

- The latest reported period by `Monday, August 10, 2026` is `Q1 2026`.
- Franco-Nevada reported record `Q1 2026` revenue of `$650.7 million`, record operating cash flow of `$520.4 million`, record net income of `$468.6 million`, and adjusted net income of `$458.3 million`.
- GEOs sold were `136,353`, up `8%`, while net GEOs sold were `126,020`, up `11%`.
- Available capital reached `$3.4 billion` as of `March 31, 2026`, including `$714.7 million` of cash and cash equivalents plus a large equity-investment book and unused credit capacity.
- The quarter shows why Franco-Nevada is not just another precious-metals royalty name. Diversified assets contributed meaningful revenue, including `oil` revenue of `$33.5 million`, `gas` revenue of `$20.6 million`, and `NGL` revenue of `$5.3 million`.
- Management also made the energy angle explicit. The company stated that higher oil prices should help `Q2 2026` revenue while the royalty and streaming model remains largely insulated from oil-driven cost inflation.
- Cobre Panama remained a live option rather than dead paper. Management expected First Quantum's stockpiled-ore processing to translate into approximately `23,100` gold ounces and `265,000` silver ounces of future stream deliveries, with most deliveries expected in `2027`.
- Structural theme: `Q1 2026` shows Franco-Nevada as the royalty-and-streaming model with the broadest embedded optionality in this branch. It combines gold upside, diversified-resource cash flow, energy sensitivity, and acquisition capacity without taking on the fixed-cost burden of a conventional operator.

### Period minus 1

- `Q4 2025` is the right base period because it closes the full `2025` year and shows the company before the next wave of `2026` acquisitions and quarter-specific energy-price effects.
- Official year-end materials show `2025` actual GEO sales of `519K`, including `440K` from precious metals and `79K` from diversified assets.
- The year-end quarter and annual framing also show how much operating leverage the model captured during the stronger commodity tape:
  - annual total revenue increased `64%`
  - annual GEOs sold increased `12%`
  - annual adjusted EBITDA increased `74%`
  - annual adjusted net income increased `74%`
- The quarterly panel showed the same effect at the quarter level:
  - `Q4 2025` total revenue increased `86%`
  - `Q4 2025` GEOs sold increased `18%`
  - `Q4 2025` adjusted EBITDA increased `94%`
  - `Q4 2025` adjusted net income increased `95%`
- Franco-Nevada also entered `2026` with the balance sheet still clean. The year-end presentation explicitly described the company as debt-free with `$3.1 billion` of available capital as of `December 31, 2025`.
- Structural theme: `Q4 2025` is the clearest annual anchor for the packet because it shows the Franco-Nevada model in its preferred state: high commodity prices, very wide GEO margins, no net debt pressure, and enough available capital to keep buying future optionality.

### Period minus 2

- `Q3 2025` shows the same model before the year-end close and confirms that the diversification branch was already materially affecting reported results.
- Revenue was a record `$487.7 million`, GEOs sold were `138,772`, operating cash flow was `$348.0 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$427.3 million`, net income was `$287.5 million`, and adjusted net income was `$275.0 million`.
- The quarter also carried an unusually clear internal split: `85%` of revenue came from precious metals, but the company also benefited from strong operations, new acquisitions, and the sale of Cobre Panama copper-concentrate stockpiles.
- Management highlighted that after drawing on the revolver to fund the Arthur Gold royalty acquisition in July, Franco-Nevada was once again debt-free by the time of the `Q3 2025` report.
- Guidance discipline also mattered. After the strong quarter, the company narrowed `2025` total GEO sales guidance toward the higher end of the original range.
- Structural theme: `Q3 2025` shows the Franco-Nevada model before the full year closes. The company is already behaving like a gold-focused royalty platform, but one with a broader commodity and financing toolkit than a simpler precious-metals streamer.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Franco-Nevada deepens the gold lane by adding a second royalty-and-streaming flagship. `Newmont` is the mega-major reserve-and-allocation model, `Kinross` is the multi-asset senior miner, `Lundin Gold` is the concentrated single-asset cash engine, `Wheaton` is the cleaner precious-metals streaming allocator, and `Franco-Nevada` is the broader royalty-and-streaming platform with a more visible diversified and energy branch.
- Commodity exposure: this is still gold-centered exposure, but the route to earnings is different from both a miner and a cleaner streamer. Franco-Nevada captures gold upside, silver upside, diversified mining exposure, and oil-and-gas upside through contracts rather than direct operations.
- Capital intensity: Franco-Nevada reinforces that capital-light does not mean low consequence. The recurring burden is not mine capex at owned sites but acquisition discipline, credit capacity, equity-investment management, and deciding when to spend the next tranche of available capital.
- Energy linkage: this packet matters because the company can benefit from rising oil prices on the revenue side while staying less exposed to oil-driven mining cost inflation. That makes it a distinct industrial read from both miners and pure midstream names.
- Counterparty dependence: the model remains highly dependent on operators. Franco-Nevada's economics still depend on mines running, stockpiles being processed, projects moving through permitting and financing, and partners honoring the underlying contracts.
- Growth model: this is an acquisition-and-optionality business. New transactions such as `Casa Berardi`, `i-80 Gold`, `Bullabulling`, and the Victoria Gold royalty portfolio show that future growth often comes through dealmaking rather than through directly building mines.
- Comparative read: compared with `Wheaton`, Franco-Nevada has a broader commodity and revenue mix and a more visible energy branch. Compared with miners like `Newmont` or `Kinross`, it has less direct exposure to site-level labor, sustaining capital, and operating-cost inflation.
- Bigger-picture theme: this packet makes the extraction layer more complete because it shows that not all gold exposure should be read as mining exposure. Some of the most durable ways to monetize commodity systems sit above the mine in royalty contracts, with optionality that can stretch across gold, silver, copper-linked streams, iron ore, and hydrocarbons.
