## Annual report takeaways

- Kinross belongs in this archive as the multi-asset senior gold miner packet rather than the mega-major gold allocator or the concentrated single-asset gold engine. The company sits between `Newmont` and `Lundin Gold` in a way that makes the internal structure of the gold lane much clearer.
- The saved AnnualReports page confirms the `Basic Materials` / `Gold` taxonomy and, unusually for this branch, was already current to the `2025 Annual Report` on `2026-08-10`. The local annual-report PDF was therefore usable through AnnualReports even though the direct Kinross IR host was blocked by Cloudflare challenges.
- The annual and year-end `2025` filing chain shows a business converting high gold prices into both cash returns and development spending. Kinross reported net earnings attributable to common shareholders of `$2.390 billion`, adjusted net earnings of `$2.244 billion`, attributable adjusted operating cash flow of `$3.605 billion`, and attributable free cash flow of `$2.474 billion`.
- Production scale was strong but still legible. Kinross reported `2.070 million` total gold equivalent ounces produced in `2025` and `2.012 million` attributable gold equivalent ounces, which is large enough to matter globally without becoming as sprawling or institutionally abstract as a supermajor.
- The capital-return message was explicit. In `2025`, Kinross returned `$752.4 million` of capital, including `$600.3 million` of share repurchases and `$152.1 million` of dividends, while repurchasing and cancelling `30.7 million` common shares under its NCIB.
- Balance-sheet strength also improved meaningfully. Total cash and cash equivalents increased by `$1.131 billion` in `2025`, and cash net of debt reached `$1.004 billion` at year-end.
- The annual materials also make the reinvestment queue visible. `Great Bear`, `Curlew`, `Round Mountain Phase X`, `Bald Mountain Redbird`, and `Lobo-Marte` all show up as meaningful project or mine-life-extension layers, which is central to understanding why strong current cash generation does not simply translate into maximum immediate payout.
- Reserve depth remains material but not static. Proven and probable gold reserves were approximately `20.9 million` ounces at `2025-12-31`, down from the prior year, which reinforces that a mid-to-large gold miner still has to keep replacing and extending the asset base rather than merely harvesting price upside.
- This packet matters because it shows a version of gold exposure where the key question is not just whether the company benefits from higher prices, but how effectively it converts those prices into a mix of repurchases, dividends, mine-life extension, and next-project funding across multiple jurisdictions.

## Reported-period takeaways

### Latest period

- The latest reported period by `Monday, August 10, 2026` is `Q2 2026`.
- Kinross produced `501,341` total gold equivalent ounces and `492,326` attributable gold equivalent ounces.
- Metal sales were `$2.238 billion`, net earnings attributable to common shareholders were `$844.2 million` or `$0.71` per share, adjusted net earnings were `$847.8 million`, and attributable free cash flow was `$726.8 million`.
- Attributable adjusted operating cash flow was `$1.112 billion`, net cash flow provided from operating activities was `$1.146 billion`, and capital expenditures were `$411.0 million`.
- The cost side stayed visible even in a strong price environment. Average realized gold price was `$4,483` per ounce, attributable production cost of sales per equivalent ounce sold was `$1,336`, and attributable AISC per equivalent ounce sold was `$1,821`.
- Balance-sheet and capital-return signals remained strong. Cash increased to `$2.7 billion`, and total return of capital in the quarter was `$277.6 million`, including `$230.0 million` of share repurchases and `$47.6 million` of dividends.
- Structural theme: `Q2 2026` shows the full Kinross model. High gold prices are generating major cash, but that cash is not flowing into one bucket. It is being split between shareholder returns, development spending, and the continued funding of a multi-project pipeline.

### Period minus 1

- `Q1 2026` is the cleanest quarter for reading Kinross before second-quarter capex ramps further.
- The company produced `500,941` total gold equivalent ounces and `492,563` attributable gold equivalent ounces.
- Metal sales were `$1.498 billion`, net earnings attributable to common shareholders were `$843.0 million` or `$0.70` per share, adjusted net earnings were `$854.1 million`, and attributable free cash flow was `$837.5 million`.
- Attributable adjusted operating cash flow was `$1.129 billion`, net cash flow provided from operating activities was `$1.140 billion`, and capital expenditures were `$283.2 million`.
- Attributable production cost of sales per equivalent ounce sold was `$1,380`, and attributable AISC per equivalent ounce sold was `$1,732`.
- The quarter also made capital allocation concrete. Kinross returned `$298.0 million`, including `$250.1 million` of repurchases and `$47.9 million` of dividends, and repurchased `7.7 million` shares.
- A useful friction point appeared in tax. Income tax expense included `$91.0 million` of withholding tax tied to planned repatriation of earnings, mainly from Mauritania, which highlights how jurisdiction and cash movement can affect what looks like a simple gold-cash-flow story.
- Structural theme: `Q1 2026` shows Kinross as a multi-jurisdiction gold cash engine where local tax regimes, mine sequencing, and development timing matter almost as much as the gold price itself.

### Period minus 2

- `Q4 2025` is the correct base period because it closes the full-year `2025` story and shows how much of the annual cash surge had already become visible in quarter-end results.
- Fourth-quarter metal sales were `$2.023 billion`, net earnings attributable to common shareholders were `$906.5 million` or `$0.75` per share, and net cash flow provided from operating activities was `$1.147 billion`.
- The quarter sold `487,972` total gold equivalent ounces at an average realized gold price of `$4,144` per ounce.
- Full-year `2025` net earnings attributable to common shareholders were `$2.390 billion`, attributable adjusted operating cash flow was `$3.605 billion`, attributable free cash flow was `$2.474 billion`, and total return of capital was `$752.4 million`.
- The quarter also carried notable accounting and project signals. Kinross recorded a `$116.1 million` impairment reversal related to property, plant and equipment at `Lobo-Marte`, while still emphasizing `Great Bear`, `Curlew`, `Phase X`, and `Redbird` as key pieces of the longer-cycle development map.
- Structural theme: `Q4 2025` closes the loop on why Kinross is a useful gold packet. It is not merely a high-price beneficiary; it is a company actively deciding how much of the cash surge to distribute and how much to reinvest into the next decade of mine life and project optionality.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Kinross adds the third clean gold archetype in this branch. `Newmont` is the scale major and portfolio allocator, `Lundin Gold` is the concentrated high-grade single-asset engine, and `Kinross` is the multi-asset senior miner with high cash conversion, heavy buybacks, and a visible development queue.
- Commodity exposure: this is direct gold exposure, but the economic read is not one-dimensional. Royalties, mine sequencing, fuel, local currency, withholding taxes, and sustaining versus non-sustaining capital all shape how much of the gold-price move reaches per-share value.
- Capital intensity: Kinross is a strong reminder that a miner can generate exceptional free cash flow and still remain capital hungry. `Great Bear`, `Curlew`, `Round Mountain Phase X`, `Bald Mountain Redbird`, and other mine-life-extension work keep the platform tied to long-cycle investment decisions.
- Geographic risk: the operating and development base spans Mauritania, Brazil, Chile, the United States, and Canada. That creates diversification, but it also makes the packet more dependent on fiscal stability, permitting, repatriation rules, community relationships, and site-specific execution than a simpler domestic miner would be.
- Operating structure: this is a physically heavy system with open pits, haul fleets, leach pads, mills, tailings structures, and development headings. Even when the gold tape is favorable, the business remains constrained by physical mine plans and asset-level sequencing.
- Shareholder returns: Kinross is a useful comparison point because it shows a senior miner trying to formalize capital returns without presenting itself as a pure yield vehicle. The payout and buyback stance is strong, but it sits alongside a real reinvestment queue rather than replacing it.
- Comparative read: unlike `Lundin Gold`, Kinross is not dependent on one flagship orebody for nearly the entire franchise. Unlike `Newmont`, it remains small enough that the key operating mines and named projects are still directly legible in a single packet.
- Bigger-picture theme: this packet deepens the archive's extraction layer by showing that a strong gold cycle is being monetized in multiple ways at once. High prices are supporting dividends and buybacks, but they are also underwriting development pipelines, reserve extension work, and more durable future operating capacity.
