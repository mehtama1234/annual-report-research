## What changed

- Business mix: Kinross is not a single-mine story. The `2025` annual and quarter chain show a multi-asset senior gold miner with operating exposure across `Tasiast`, `Paracatu`, `La Coipa`, `Fort Knox` with `Manh Choh`, `Round Mountain`, and `Bald Mountain`, plus a visible development queue at `Great Bear`, `Curlew`, `Phase X`, and `Redbird`.
- Archive fit: this is a strong gold packet because AnnualReports was current to `2025`, the SEC chain is complete, and the company sits neatly between the mega-major profile of `Newmont` and the concentrated single-asset profile of `Lundin Gold`.
- `2025` base year: Kinross reported net earnings attributable to common shareholders of `$2.390 billion`, adjusted net earnings of `$2.244 billion`, attributable adjusted operating cash flow of `$3.605 billion`, attributable free cash flow of `$2.474 billion`, and attributable gold equivalent production of `2.012 million` ounces.
- Capital returns: management returned `$752.4 million` in `2025`, including `$600.3 million` of share repurchases and `$152.1 million` of dividends, and repurchased `30.7 million` common shares under the NCIB.
- Balance-sheet improvement: total cash and cash equivalents increased by `$1.131 billion` in `2025`, and cash net of debt reached `$1.004 billion` at year-end.
- Reserve and development read: proven and probable gold reserves were approximately `20.9 million` ounces at `2025-12-31`, while the annual chain kept emphasizing `Great Bear`, `Curlew`, `Round Mountain Phase X`, `Bald Mountain Redbird`, and `Lobo-Marte` as the next layer of franchise extension.
- `Q4 2025` pattern: the year ended with exceptionally strong gold-price leverage, `Q4` metal sales of `$2.023 billion`, `Q4` net earnings attributable to common shareholders of `$906.5 million`, and a visible impairment reversal of `$116.1 million` at `Lobo-Marte`.
- `Q1 2026` pattern: metal sales were `$1.498 billion`, attributable free cash flow was `$837.5 million`, and the company used the cash surge to keep buying back stock while stepping up development activity at `Great Bear`, `Curlew`, `Phase X`, and `Redbird`; the quarter also exposed Mauritania-linked tax friction through a `$91.0 million` withholding-tax charge tied to planned repatriation.
- `Q2 2026` pattern: metal sales climbed to `$2.238 billion`, attributable free cash flow remained high at `$726.8 million`, and cash rose to `$2.7 billion`, but higher AISC and higher capital expenditures showed that stronger gold prices were funding both capital returns and heavier project spending at the same time.
- Operating structure: this packet is useful because it shows that gold miners are not just price beta. Mine sequencing, royalties, local currency costs, fuel exposure, sustaining versus non-sustaining capital, and the timing of development projects all materially shape how much of the gold-price windfall reaches shareholders.

## Open questions

- Can Kinross preserve this strong cash-return profile if gold stays elevated but development spending at `Great Bear`, `Curlew`, `Phase X`, and `Redbird` keeps rising?
- Does the current reserve and project pipeline extend the franchise cleanly enough to narrow the strategic gap between a multi-asset senior miner like Kinross and the much larger reserve-depth platforms led by the gold majors?
