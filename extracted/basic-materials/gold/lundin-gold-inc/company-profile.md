## What changed

- Asset concentration: Lundin Gold remains a one-core-mine company built around `Fruta del Norte`, but the packet shows a meaningful shift toward district optionality through `FDNS`, `FDN East`, and nearby copper-gold targets.
- Cash generation: the company moved from record full-year `2025` free cash flow of `$926 million` to first-half `2026` free cash flow of `$445 million`, still while paying out unusually large dividends.
- Balance sheet: Lundin Gold ended `2025` with `$630 million` of cash and no debt, rose to `$704 million` at `Q1 2026`, then moved down to `$507 million` by `Q2 2026` after annual tax and profit-sharing payments plus larger distributions.
- Capital return model: the company elevated the fixed dividend and linked the variable dividend to normalized free cash flow, then used that framework aggressively through `2025` and `2026`.
- Growth pipeline: the period makes the mine-to-mill expansion, `FDNS` development, and district drilling much more central to the story than a simple mature single-mine cash-cow read would suggest.
- Comparative value: this is a useful contrast inside gold. `Newmont` is the giant diversified gold allocator, while `Lundin Gold` is a concentrated high-grade gold engine whose main questions are jurisdiction, mine life, and how much cash to return versus reinvest.

## Open questions

- Can Lundin Gold preserve its current margin profile if gold prices cool while royalties and statutory profit-sharing remain structurally tied to price?
- How much of the current equity appeal should be attributed to near-term cash distributions versus long-term district growth through `FDNS`, `FDN East`, and the copper-gold porphyry corridor?
- Does the market ultimately reward Lundin Gold more as a dividend-heavy single-asset gold cash engine or as a broader Ecuadorian district-development platform?
