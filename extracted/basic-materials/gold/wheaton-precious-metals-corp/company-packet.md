## Annual report takeaways

- Wheaton belongs in this archive as the streaming-and-royalty precious-metals allocator rather than as another mine operator. That distinction matters because it adds a different kind of gold exposure than `Newmont`, `Kinross`, or `Lundin Gold`.
- The saved AnnualReports page confirms the `Basic Materials` / `Gold` taxonomy, but as of `2026-08-10` it still lagged at the `2024 Annual Report`. The authoritative `2025` annual chain therefore comes from Wheaton's official company-hosted annual PDFs and the SEC year-end exhibit package.
- Full-year `2025` results were record-setting. Wheaton reported attributable production of `689,900` gold equivalent ounces, revenue of `$2.3 billion`, net earnings of `$1.5 billion`, adjusted net earnings of `$1.4 billion`, and operating cash flow of `$1.9 billion`.
- The portfolio scale is large, but the model is still much lighter than a miner's. At year-end `2025`, Wheaton had streaming and royalty agreements on `23` operating mines and `25` development and other projects, with `85%` of attributable production coming from assets in the lowest half of their cost curves.
- The annual chain shows why this model deserves its own lane. Wheaton does not need to own and operate the mines directly to gain exposure to their production. Instead, its economics are shaped by contract terms, fixed or formula-based ongoing payments, partner asset quality, and the ability to keep adding new streams and royalties.
- `2025` was also a meaningful portfolio-expansion year. Wheaton added the `Hemlo` and `Spring Valley` gold streams and then, after year-end, announced the BHP `Antamina` transaction that management described as the largest precious-metals streaming transaction ever.
- The annual materials make the growth path unusually explicit. `2026` guidance was `860,000` to `940,000` GEOs, and long-term output was projected to rise by roughly `50%` to `1.2 million` GEOs by `2030`, supported by `Antamina`, `Blackwater`, `Aljustrel`, `Marmato`, `Hemlo`, `Goose`, and several development-stage projects.
- This packet matters because it shows a very different route to precious-metals upside. The question is not simply whether a miner can hit grade, throughput, and cost targets at its own sites. The question is whether Wheaton can allocate capital into the right agreements, preserve balance-sheet flexibility, and keep compounding cash flow across a diversified partner network.

## Reported-period takeaways

### Latest period

- The latest reported period by `Monday, August 10, 2026` is `Q2 2026`.
- Wheaton reported record `Q2 2026` revenue of `$929 million`, net earnings of `$543 million`, and operating cash flow of `$650 million`.
- First-half `2026` revenue reached a record `$1.8 billion`, first-half net earnings reached a record `$1.1 billion`, and first-half operating cash flow reached a record `$1.4 billion`.
- Attributable production in `Q2 2026` was `202,200` GEOs, up `6%` year over year, driven mainly by the additional `Antamina` silver stream plus growth contributions from `Hemlo`, `Fenix`, `Platreef`, and `Goose`.
- The balance-sheet profile changed materially after the BHP `Antamina` deal. Cash fell to `$100 million`, debt outstanding rose to `$2.0 billion`, net debt rose to `$1.9 billion`, and available liquidity still remained large at `$2.6 billion` after the revolving-facility upsizing.
- The quarter also widened the optionality map. Wheaton added the `Jervois` stream in Australia, the `Spanish Mountain` royalty, and the `Cipango` royalty in Japan, showing how the model keeps growing through deal origination rather than direct mine construction ownership.
- Structural theme: `Q2 2026` shows the streaming model under pressure and strength at the same time. Wheaton took on meaningful debt to fund a major transaction, but the same portfolio then immediately produced record revenue and cash flow, reinforcing how a capital-light model can still use leverage aggressively when the next stream is large enough.

### Period minus 1

- `Q1 2026` is the cleanest quarter for seeing Wheaton just after the BHP `Antamina` deal was announced and before the full second-quarter debt and integration effects became visible.
- Revenue reached a record `$901 million`, net earnings reached a record `$582 million`, adjusted net earnings reached `$583 million`, and operating cash flow reached a record `$766 million`.
- Attributable production reached `212,000` GEOs, up `22%` year over year, helped by stronger production from `Peñasquito`, `Antamina`, and `Blackwater`, along with the resumption of production at `Aljustrel`.
- The balance sheet still looked unusually strong before the full financing step-up. Cash balance was `$2.2 billion`.
- The quarter also made the transaction logic visible. The BHP `Antamina` PMPA closed on `April 1, 2026`, and management highlighted how the deal broadened the company's counterparty base and deepened future growth, while also introducing a much larger financing package.
- Structural theme: `Q1 2026` shows why streaming companies trade differently from miners. Strong quarter results matter, but the bigger read is strategic. A streaming business can radically reshape its future production profile through one very large contract, even before the underlying mine owner changes anything operationally at the asset.

### Period minus 2

- `Q4 2025` is the right base period because it closes the record `2025` year and shows the company just before the BHP `Antamina` balance-sheet transformation.
- Fourth-quarter revenue was a record `$865 million`, net earnings were a record `$558 million`, adjusted net earnings were `$555 million`, and operating cash flow was a record `$746 million`.
- Fourth-quarter attributable production was `205,000` GEOs, up `8%`, helped by stronger production at `Salobo`, `Antamina`, and the start of production at `Blackwater`.
- Full-year `2025` revenue reached `$2.3 billion`, net earnings reached `$1.5 billion`, adjusted net earnings reached `$1.4 billion`, operating cash flow reached `$1.9 billion`, and annual dividends reached `$0.66` per share.
- The quarter also made the financing pivot explicit. Management outlined the funding plan for the `$4.3 billion` BHP `Antamina` transaction using cash on hand, a new `$1.5 billion` term loan, and a draw on the revolving credit facility, with expected net debt at closing of about `$2.4 billion`.
- Structural theme: `Q4 2025` shows Wheaton as a cash-rich streaming platform just before a deliberate leverage step-up. The packet is useful because it captures the moment when a capital-light business chooses to use that lighter structure to absorb a very large acquisition without becoming a conventional operator.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Wheaton adds the missing royalty-and-streaming archetype to the gold lane. `Newmont` is the mega-major reserve-and-allocation model, `Kinross` is the multi-asset senior miner, `Lundin Gold` is the concentrated single-asset cash engine, and `Wheaton` is the capital-light streaming allocator layered on top of many partner mines.
- Commodity exposure: this is still precious-metals exposure, but it reaches shareholders through a different mechanism. Wheaton has major upside to stronger gold and silver prices, yet its site-level exposure to labor, sustaining capital, and direct mine operating cost inflation is lower than at a conventional miner.
- Capital intensity: the business is lighter on recurring site capex, but not low-consequence. Capital intensity shows up at the portfolio-allocation level through very large upfront payments, facility sizing, and the need to preserve deal capacity for the next stream or royalty.
- Counterparty dependence: Wheaton's diversification across assets helps, but the model depends on counterparties operating well, honoring agreements, and delivering expected volumes. That makes contract durability and partner quality core risk variables.
- Growth model: this company grows through transactions, not just through orebody development it directly controls. `Hemlo`, `Spring Valley`, `Antamina`, `Jervois`, `Spanish Mountain`, and `Cipango` all reinforce that the growth engine is sourcing, underwriting, and financing agreements rather than building and running mines.
- Balance-sheet read: the BHP `Antamina` transaction shows how even a capital-light model can use leverage aggressively when a large stream is available. The key question becomes whether the added production and cash flow justify the temporary compression in balance-sheet flexibility.
- Comparative read: unlike `Newmont`, `Kinross`, or `Lundin Gold`, Wheaton is not primarily a mine-plan and capex-execution packet. It is a contract architecture and capital-allocation packet whose margins depend on streaming terms, metal prices, and portfolio construction.
- Bigger-picture theme: this packet deepens the archive's extraction layer by showing that precious-metals exposure should not be treated as one operator template. Some of the strongest ways to monetize gold and silver are not to own the mines at all, but to sit above them with the right stream, the right counterparties, and enough financing flexibility to keep compounding the portfolio.
