## What changed

- Taxonomy versus reality: AnnualReports preserved the archive entry, but its category labels are not reliable here. The company description and SEC materials make clear that EOG belongs in the independent-producer lane.
- Production mix: `2025` average production reached `1,232.2 MBoed`, and the latest three-quarter set shows continued scaling to `1,383.8 MBoed` in `Q1 2026` and `1,410.4 MBoed` in `Q2 2026`.
- Cash generation: despite a weak reported `Q4 2025`, EOG still produced meaningful free cash flow. By `Q2 2026`, free cash flow had climbed to `$2.799 billion`, showing how quickly upstream cash engines can inflect when price and volume cooperate.
- Commodity leverage: the move from `Q1 2026` to `Q2 2026` is the clearest proof point. Composite crude realizations moved from `$72.47` to `$98.15` per barrel, and earnings stepped up sharply.
- Capital allocation: the `10-K` hardens the shareholder-return story into policy. EOG says it intends to return at least `70%` of annual operating cash flow before certain balance-sheet changes, less total capital expenditures, to stockholders.
- Asset quality and discipline: the year-end impairments tied mainly to Barnett Shale and Woodford Oil Window assets show that EOG is willing to high-grade its portfolio and let weaker play economics show up in reported results.
- Geographic concentration: with about `99%` of proved reserves in the United States, this is a strong packet for domestic supply depth, shale productivity, and the U.S. extraction base rather than diversified international optionality.

## Open questions

- How much of the `Q2 2026` earnings step-up is repeatable if oil realizations retreat while gas realizations stay softer?
- Does EOG's strong U.S. weighting increase resilience through operational familiarity, or increase risk through basin concentration and domestic transport dependence?
- Can the company keep pairing production growth with a high cash-return commitment without sacrificing inventory quality or future optionality?
