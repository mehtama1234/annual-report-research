## What changed

- Anglo American is not just another diversified miner. It is one of the clearest transition packets in the entire lane because management is actively shrinking away from steelmaking coal, nickel, and eventually De Beers while leaning harder into copper, premium iron ore, and crop nutrients.
- Unlike `Rio Tinto`, `Vale`, and `BHP`, this packet is not primarily about a stable bulk-materials platform. It is about portfolio simplification, merger logic with `Teck`, and the attempt to become a narrower critical-minerals champion.
- The annual-reporting page makes the demand framing explicit. Anglo says its products feed smartphones, electric vehicles, solar panels, wind turbines, and data centres, while also warning that future commodity demand is being reshaped by battery chemistry shifts, AI-driven infrastructure growth, rising capital intensity, longer lead times, and stricter social scrutiny.
- `FY2025` continuing-operations revenue was `$18.546 billion`, underlying EBITDA was `$6.417 billion`, attributable free cash flow was `$790 million`, and closing net debt fell to `$8.571 billion`.
- `H1 2026` accelerated materially, with revenue up to `$9.926 billion`, underlying EBITDA up to `$4.002 billion`, attributable free cash flow up to `$803 million`, and net debt down again to `$8.234 billion`.
- The production chain shows that copper is the center of gravity. `Q4 2025` copper production was `169,500` tonnes, `Q1 2026` rose to `170,400`, and `Q2 2026` stayed strong at `173,200`, while unit-cost guidance improved despite fuel and consumables pressure.

## Open questions

- Does the Anglo simplification story create a structurally cleaner and higher-quality miner, or just a narrower business with more copper and permitting concentration?
- If the Teck merger closes in the expected `September 2026` to `March 2027` window, how much of the value will come from operational synergies versus simply removing weaker portfolio pieces first?
