## What changed

- BHP adds a cleaner third point to the diversified-miner comparison already built with `Rio Tinto` and `Vale`. It is not just another iron ore exporter. It is a bulk-materials and copper-growth allocator where Australian iron ore scale, Chilean copper quality, and potash optionality all compete for capital.
- AnnualReports is useful but not enough on its own. The saved AnnualReports page confirms the taxonomy and shows a current `2025` report entry, but the real annual and current-period chain still depends on BHP's own annual-report, half-year-results, and operational-review materials.
- `FY2025` annual-report framing is strong enough to stand on its own. BHP highlighted record annual copper production of `2 Mt`, total economic contribution of `$46.8 billion`, profit from operations of `$19.5 billion`, shareholder cash dividends of `110 USc` per share, and payments to governments of `$10.4 billion`.
- The fiscal-calendar difference matters. As of `Monday, August 10, 2026`, the correct latest three-period window is not `Q2 2026`, `Q1 2026`, and `Q4 2025` in a domestic `10-Q` sense. The honest mapping is `FY26` year-end operational review, `FY26` nine months operational review, and `FY26` half-year results.
- Copper is now the center of gravity. In the `FY26` half-year results, copper provided `51%` of Underlying EBITDA, and management raised full-year group copper guidance to `1.9 - 2.0 Mt`. That makes BHP a stronger copper-and-bulk-materials comparator than a simple iron ore read would suggest.
- Cost control is part of the thesis, not a background detail. The `FY26` year-end operational review said every asset was expected to remain within unit-cost guidance despite inflation, higher diesel prices, and global supply-chain disruption.

## Open questions

- Does BHP become a more copper-led business over the next cycle, or does iron ore remain too dominant as the cash engine?
- How much of the long-duration upside really depends on potash and future copper development options rather than near-term commodity prices?
