# Glencore plc Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Glencore plc
- Sector: Basic Materials
- Industry: Industrial Metals & Minerals
- Ticker: GLNCY / GLEN
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target reported-period window:
  - latest period: `H1 2026` results plus `H1 2026` production report
  - period minus 1: `Q1 2026` production report
  - period minus 2: `Q4 2025` / `FY2025` preliminary results plus full-year production report

## Annual report takeaways

- Glencore belongs in this archive as the trading-and-logistics-enhanced version of diversified mining. `Rio Tinto`, `Vale`, `BHP`, and `Anglo American` are mainly interpreted through assets, ore bodies, logistics systems, and portfolio structure. Glencore has those too, but it also has a large marketing engine that can swing earnings meaningfully when freight, energy, and commodity markets are dislocated.
- The saved AnnualReports page confirms taxonomy, but it lagged on current archive depth. The real source chain had to come from Glencore's own publications hub, annual report, annual results, and production reports.
- The `2025` annual report frames the business as two global operations working together: industrial and marketing. The industrial side produced adjusted EBITDA of `$9.9 billion` in `2025`, while the marketing side produced adjusted EBIT of `$2.9 billion`. That split is one of the most important facts in the packet because it makes Glencore structurally different from the other miners already in the lane.
- `FY2025` Group Adjusted EBITDA was `$13.511 billion`, down `6%` year over year, with Funds from operations of `$8.714 billion` and net debt unchanged at roughly `$11.2 billion`.
- The annual framing says `2025` was a year of significant progress with strong operational performance, continued portfolio optimisation, and clear momentum for Glencore's copper-led growth strategy. Management highlighted the path to more than `1 million` tonnes annualised by end-`2028` and around `1.6 million` tonnes by `2035`.
- Another critical annual signal is that the business was simplified again after the `Viterra` sale to `Bunge` in `July 2025`. That matters because it shows Glencore is not only a trader or only a miner; it is constantly pruning and reallocating around where it sees the best trading and industrial economics.

## Reported-period takeaways

### Latest period

- The most recent reporting set by `Monday, August 10, 2026` is the combination of the `2026 Half-Year Production Report` released on `2026-07-29` and the `2026 Half-Year Results` released on `2026-08-05`.
- `H1 2026` Group Adjusted EBITDA rose to `$10.1 billion`, up `86%`, while Industrial Adjusted EBITDA rose to `$6.5 billion`, up `72%`, and Marketing Adjusted EBIT rose to `$3.3 billion`, up `142%`.
- Funds from operations reached `$8.1 billion`, net debt fell to `$10.2 billion`, and management still announced roughly `$1.5 billion` of additional shareholder returns. This is a useful reminder that Glencore can convert commodity upswings and trading disruption into cash very quickly.
- The period also shows how the two-engine model works in practice. Management said marketing benefited from materially disrupted energy, freight, and other markets, while the industrial segment benefited from higher commodity prices and solid operations. The business model is diversified not only by commodity but also by function.
- `H1 2026` own sourced copper production rose `15%` to `397,000` tonnes, supported by higher mining rates and improved grades at African Copper and higher grades at Antamina.
- Cobalt fell `46%` because the DRC government's quota regime caused Glencore to prioritise copper over cobalt, with some cobalt effectively held back for later processing and sale. That is one of the strongest examples in the entire branch of how upstream economics can be actively managed around quotas, inventories, and marketing realities rather than just mined tonnage.
- Structural theme: the latest period shows that Glencore is one of the best packets for understanding why commodity systems are also trading, inventory, transport, and optionality systems.

### Period minus 1

- `Q1 2026` is the clearest short-period operating read before the half-year jump.
- Own sourced copper production increased `19%` to `199,600` tonnes, mainly due to improved grades at African copper and stronger throughput and grades at Antamina.
- Cobalt dropped `39%`, zinc dropped `17%`, and steelmaking coal dropped `22%`. This reinforces that Glencore's portfolio cannot be reduced to a single clean metals-up story; several large commodity streams were moving in different directions at the same time.
- The CEO explicitly linked the quarter to Middle East conflict effects, especially around crude, refined products, and sulphuric acid. He also said stronger commodity prices and marketing performance should more than offset rising diesel, acid, and weaker-USD cost pressures.
- Structural theme: `Q1 2026` shows why Glencore belongs in a separate sub-bucket from the other miners. When supply chains get messy, its marketing business and metallurgical footprint can partly offset the same market disruptions that pressure inputs for many peers.

### Period minus 2

- `Q4 2025` and the `FY2025` preliminary results are the right base period for this packet.
- `FY2025` Group Adjusted EBITDA was `$13.5 billion`, Industrial Adjusted EBITDA was `$9.9 billion`, Marketing Adjusted EBIT was `$2.9 billion`, and Funds from operations were `$8.7 billion`.
- Full-year own sourced copper production was `851,600` tonnes, down `11%`, but `H2 2025` copper production was almost `50%` above `H1 2025`, which management described as evidence of momentum inside the copper-led growth strategy.
- Full-year cobalt production fell `5%` as Glencore prioritized copper under DRC export restrictions, while zinc production rose `7%`.
- The annual results note that `Viterra` disposal proceeds, working-capital inflows, and tighter portfolio management helped hold net debt at around `$11.2 billion` even after `$3.5 billion` of shareholder returns and heavy capital spending.
- Structural theme: `Q4 2025` is the period that makes Glencore's identity legible. This is not a pure miner and not a pure trader. It is a company that tries to use commodity-market volatility, transport complexity, working capital, and marketing reach to supplement returns from its industrial base.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Glencore adds the marketer-miner hybrid layer that was still missing from the archive even after Rio, Vale, BHP, and Anglo.
- Consumer: Glencore is far upstream, but its marketing and logistics reach make it one of the best packets for showing how disruptions in energy, freight, metals concentrates, and inventories can propagate into downstream industrial and consumer systems.
- Cultural: there is no direct consumer-culture angle, but this packet says something about the current global economic environment: geopolitical stress, freight dislocation, and commodity volatility are not just risks. For a business structured like Glencore, they can also become profit opportunities.
- Societal: this is one of the strongest packets for commodity-security and supply-chain realism. The company sits across mining, smelting, logistics, distribution, and risk management, making it unusually embedded in the plumbing behind energy and metals flows.
- Regulatory: DRC cobalt quotas, local asset sales, and commodity-market regulation matter directly. This packet is a good reminder that policy can re-route production priorities even before it changes mine ownership or long-term asset value.
- Capital allocation: Glencore behaves like an allocator across industrial assets, working capital, trading books, and portfolio simplification transactions. It is not enough to follow capex alone; the balance sheet, inventories, and marketing engine also matter.
- Industrial: the packet strengthens the split inside diversified mining by adding a group where marketing profits, transport disruptions, and risk-management capability are central to the investment case.
- Comparative read: `Glencore` is the sharpest contrast to `Anglo American`. Anglo is simplifying toward a cleaner copper/premium-iron-ore/crop-nutrients story. Glencore is still explicitly defending a broader portfolio plus a powerful marketing arm that monetizes volatility rather than simply trying to escape it.
