## What changed

- Glencore adds a different model than the other diversified miners in this branch. This is not only a mining portfolio. It is a combined industrial-and-marketing system where trading, logistics, inventory positioning, and risk management can materially alter earnings even when production volumes are not the whole story.
- AnnualReports was useful only for taxonomy confirmation. The authoritative annual and current-period chain came from Glencore's own publications page and official reporting PDFs.
- `FY2025` Group Adjusted EBITDA was `$13.511 billion`, Funds from operations were `$8.714 billion`, and net debt finished at `$11.2 billion`, with Marketing Adjusted EBIT contributing `$2.9 billion`.
- The annual report and preliminary results both frame the company around a copper-led growth strategy. Management said Glencore expects to exceed `1 million` tonnes of annualised copper production by the end of `2028` and is targeting around `1.6 million` tonnes by `2035`.
- `H1 2026` accelerated sharply. Group Adjusted EBITDA rose to `$10.1 billion`, Industrial Adjusted EBITDA rose to `$6.5 billion`, Marketing Adjusted EBIT rose to `$3.3 billion`, and net debt fell to `$10.194 billion`.
- Production and commodity nuance matter together here. `H1 2026` own sourced copper rose `15%`, while cobalt fell `46%` because the DRC quota system drove prioritisation of copper over cobalt. That is one of the clearest examples in the whole lane of how policy, quotas, by-product management, and trading logic can reshape reported production economics.

## Open questions

- How durable is Glencore's marketing windfall if freight, energy, and geopolitical dislocations normalize?
- Does the company’s copper-led growth strategy ultimately make it look more like a mining champion with a trading arm, or does the marketing business remain the real differentiator through the cycle?
