# Hudbay Minerals Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Company: Hudbay Minerals Inc.
- Sector: Basic Materials
- Industry: Industrial Metals & Minerals
- Ticker: HBM
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target reported-period window:
  - latest period: `Q2 2026`
  - period minus 1: `Q1 2026`
  - period minus 2: `Q4 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- Hudbay belongs in this archive as the mid-tier Americas copper-and-gold system with meaningful by-product support. This is not a pure copper miner in the narrow sense. Gold exposure is large enough to change cash costs, margin shape, and capital-allocation flexibility in a way the more single-metal packets do not capture.
- The saved AnnualReports page confirms taxonomy, but it still lagged at `2024` when collected on `2026-08-10`. The authoritative `2025` chain had to come from Hudbay's own annual-report materials and the SEC `40-F`.
- The annual-report-highlights framing is unusually clear. Hudbay described `2025` as record-breaking financially, with about `$2.2 billion` of annual revenue, about `$1.1 billion` of annual adjusted EBITDA, and a `$600 million` Mitsubishi joint-venture transaction tied to Copper World.
- The annual framing also highlights operating consistency rather than only growth promises: the company said it achieved annual consolidated copper production guidance for the `11th` consecutive year and annual consolidated gold guidance for the `5th` consecutive year.
- Bigger-picture annual theme: Hudbay is trying to position itself as a premier Americas-focused copper company, but the actual operating model is still a diversified copper-plus-gold system spread across Peru, Manitoba, and British Columbia, with longer-cycle Arizona growth options layered on top.

## Reported-period takeaways

### Latest period

- The latest reported period by `Monday, August 10, 2026` is `Q2 2026`, reported on `2026-07-29`.
- Revenue was `$631.3 million`, net earnings attributable to owners were `$137.4 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$321.2 million`, and free cash flow was `$101.8 million`.
- Consolidated copper production was `28,267` tonnes and gold production was `51,234` ounces. Consolidated cash cost, net of by-product credits, was `($0.40)` per pound and sustaining cash cost was `$1.39` per pound.
- Peru operations produced `19,446` tonnes of copper and `5,282` ounces of gold, while Manitoba produced `40,344` ounces of gold and British Columbia produced `6,455` tonnes of copper. That operating mix matters because it shows why gold credits and geographic diversification are central to the packet.
- The most useful structural detail in the latest period is not a mine failure but a logistics interruption. Management said lower second-quarter sales volumes were caused by a temporary build-up of concentrate inventory at the port in Peru after ocean swells temporarily closed the port and delayed about `10,000` dry metric tonnes of copper concentrate shipments into July.
- Hudbay still improved full-year consolidated cash-cost guidance to `($0.45)` to `($0.25)` per pound from `($0.30)` to `($0.10)`, arguing that strong gold by-product credits and continued operating efficiencies were more than offsetting higher fuel and consumables costs.
- Structural theme: `Q2 2026` shows that even a miner with strong margins can have earnings shaped by export logistics and shipment timing, not just mine output or commodity prices.

### Period minus 1

- `Q1 2026`, reported on `2026-05-01`, is the cleanest quarter for seeing Hudbay's margin structure and balance-sheet reset.
- Revenue reached a record `$757.3 million`, adjusted EBITDA reached a record `$421.9 million`, and adjusted earnings attributable to owners reached `$159.1 million`.
- Consolidated copper production was `27,929` tonnes and gold production was `61,700` ounces. Consolidated cash cost was `($1.80)` per pound and sustaining cash cost was `$0.00` per pound, both unusually strong because by-product credits and metal prices were doing a large amount of work.
- Management said gold revenue was `39%` of gross revenue in the quarter. That is one of the most important facts in the packet because it explains why Hudbay cannot be read as a simple copper beta story.
- The balance sheet improved sharply. Cash and cash equivalents reached `$1,003.8 million`, total liquidity reached `$1,429.0 million`, and net debt fell to just `$5.6 million`, largely because the initial Mitsubishi cash contribution from the Copper World JV closed in January 2026.
- The quarter also moved the growth story forward: Copper World remained on track for a `2026` sanctioning decision, the Arizona Sonoran acquisition was announced, and Hudbay framed itself more explicitly as a premier Americas-focused copper company with a pipeline of long-life, low-cost assets in tier-one jurisdictions.
- Structural theme: `Q1 2026` is the quarter that shows how a mid-tier miner can use strong commodity conditions, by-product support, and a well-timed project partnership to move from manageable leverage to near-net-cash very quickly.

### Period minus 2

- `Q4 2025`, reported on `2026-02-20`, is the right base period because it anchors both the full-year `2025` operating result and the pre-JV balance-sheet state.
- Q4 revenue was `$732.9 million`, net earnings attributable to owners were `$128.0 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$385.9 million`, and free cash flow was `$228.2 million`.
- Full-year `2025` revenue was `$2,211.0 million`, net earnings attributable to owners were `$568.5 million`, and full-year adjusted EBITDA was `$1,060.9 million`.
- Full-year consolidated copper production was `118,188` tonnes and full-year gold production was `267,934` ounces. Full-year cash cost was `($0.22)` per pound, sustaining cash cost was `$1.30` per pound, and all-in sustaining cash cost was `$1.74` per pound.
- The quarter and year-end read also highlight the transition risk embedded in the operating system. Peru was still benefiting from strong Pampacancha grades in Q4, but management also made clear that `2026` would face lower Peru grades after Pampacancha depletion, partly offset by British Columbia throughput ramp-up toward `50,000` tonnes per day in the second half of `2026`.
- Net debt at year-end was still `$439.7 million`, which matters because it makes the Q1 2026 balance-sheet improvement legible. Hudbay did not begin the year in a pristine position; it improved rapidly because project monetization and cash generation aligned.
- Structural theme: `Q4 2025` makes Hudbay's identity legible. This is a miner where grades, by-product credits, freight timing, and project-financing strategy all matter at once.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Hudbay adds the mid-tier copper-and-gold diversification layer that sits between giant diversified miners and more concentrated single-asset or restart-risk names.
- Consumer: there is no direct consumer-demand read, but the packet is exposed to copper demand tied to electrification and power buildout while also showing how gold can operate as a major economic offset inside an ostensibly copper-led business.
- Cultural: there is no direct consumer-culture angle. The broader signal is institutional: critical-minerals positioning now increasingly depends on how management frames jurisdiction quality, domestic supply relevance, and strategic project pipelines in North and South America.
- Societal: Hudbay is a useful social-license and regional-economy packet because it spans Peru, Manitoba, British Columbia, and Arizona. Community, environmental, and permitting relationships are not background issues here; they directly shape mine life, mill expansion, and project sanction timing.
- Regulatory: this is a lighter regulatory-risk packet than First Quantum, but permitting still matters materially through Copper World, New Ingerbelle, and the Arizona Sonoran/Cactus growth path.
- Capital allocation: Hudbay is one of the clearer packets for project monetization and balance-sheet engineering done from strength rather than distress. The Mitsubishi JV, Arizona Sonoran acquisition, debt retirement, and rising U.S. growth spend all sit inside one capital-allocation story.
- Industrial: the packet shows that copper systems with strong by-product support can behave very differently from more direct copper miners. Gold credits can turn cash costs negative, while port closures and grade depletion can still reshape quarterly earnings.
- Comparative read: `First Quantum` is the Zambia-and-Panama restart and leverage packet, `Southern Copper` is the Peru/Mexico by-product-supported copper packet, `Freeport-McMoRan` is the giant-asset and operating-rights packet, and `Hudbay` adds the mid-tier Americas copper-plus-gold and project-partnership packet where balance-sheet flexibility and growth staging matter as much as mine scale.
