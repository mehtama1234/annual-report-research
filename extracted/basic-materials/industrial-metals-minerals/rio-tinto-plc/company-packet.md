## Annual report takeaways

- Rio Tinto fills a different heavy-input role than the other mining packets already in the lane. `Freeport-McMoRan` and `Southern Copper` are copper-led systems. `Newmont` and `Pan American Silver` are precious-metals systems. `Teck` is a more mixed copper-zinc-processing-and-coal platform. Rio is the large-scale bulk-materials allocator where iron ore still drives the cash machine, but aluminium, copper, and lithium increasingly change the growth mix.
- AnnualReports was useful only for taxonomy and lag confirmation. The saved AnnualReports company page correctly places Rio in `Industrial Metals & Minerals`, but it still surfaced the `2024 Annual Report` when collected on `Monday, August 10, 2026`. The authoritative `2025` annual chain therefore comes from Rio’s own annual-report page, annual report PDF, annual results release, and SEC `20-F`.
- `2025` was a strong base year. Consolidated sales revenue was `$57.638 billion`, underlying EBITDA was `$25.363 billion`, underlying earnings were `$10.868 billion`, and profit after tax attributable to owners was `$9.966 billion`.
- Cash generation remained central. Net cash generated from operating activities reached `$16.8 billion`, and Rio held the ordinary dividend at `$6.5 billion`, a `60%` payout ratio. This remains a high-cash-return mining platform, not just a long-duration growth story.
- The annual messaging makes the portfolio shift explicit. Management said results were supported by operational excellence, cost discipline, and rising contributions from copper and aluminium. That matters because the company is trying to evolve from an iron-ore-dominant cash engine into a broader critical-materials platform without giving up cash flow.
- Capital intensity is still the governing logic. The `2025` release highlights Oyu Tolgoi completion, Simandou first ore shipment, Western Range start-up, and additional lithium construction progress. Rio’s earnings are inseparable from whether giant, multi-year projects stay on schedule.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Reporting cadence note: Rio does not provide a standard stand-alone U.S.-style `Q2 2026` earnings package. The closest authoritative set by `2026-08-10` is the `H1 2026` results release plus the `Q2 2026` operations review. This packet therefore treats `Q2 2026` as a combined half-year financial and second-quarter operating read.
- Financial base: for the six months ended `2026-06-30`, consolidated sales revenue was `$31.028 billion`, underlying EBITDA was `$14.826 billion`, underlying earnings were `$6.851 billion`, profit after tax attributable to owners was `$6.664 billion`, and net cash generated from operating activities was `$9.173 billion`.
- Capital returns and growth balance: Rio declared an interim ordinary dividend of `$3.4 billion`, up `43%`, while still funding more than `$5.0 billion` of capital investment in the half. This is a useful example of how a giant miner tries to keep both yield investors and long-cycle growth projects satisfied.
- Operating momentum: `H1 2026` CuEq production rose `3%`, and management said Copper, Aluminium and Lithium contributed more than `50%` of underlying EBITDA. That is one of the clearest signs in the packet that the portfolio is diversifying away from pure iron ore dependence even while iron ore remains essential.
- `Q2 2026` operations review: Oyu Tolgoi stayed on track and delivered `31%` YoY growth in first-half production. Q2 global iron ore sales were `88.8 Mt`, up `5%` YoY, and Q2 Pilbara sales were up `7%` YoY. SimFer mine construction and port infrastructure were both more than three quarters complete.
- Lithium and cost signals: lithium production rose `20%` YoY in `Q2`, helped by the ramp at Rincon and first production at Sal de Vida and Fenix `1B`, while copper C1 net unit cost guidance was cut to `US 30 - 50c/lb` from `US 65 - 75c/lb`. That combination matters because it points to portfolio growth that is becoming more operationally real rather than just conceptual.
- Structural theme: the latest quarter is the best proof that Rio should be read as a logistics-and-project-execution machine as much as a commodity producer. Pilbara productivity, Simandou buildout, Oyu Tolgoi ramp quality, and lithium commissioning now matter as much as the spot price tape.

### Quarter minus 1

- `Q1 2026` was a pure operations read rather than a full quarterly earnings package. The key signal was `9%` YoY growth in copper equivalent production.
- Copper and growth options: production rose `9%` YoY, supported by the continued Oyu Tolgoi ramp-up. Rio also said drilling at Resolution was underway after the land exchange completed in March, reinforcing that the company is still trying to secure the next wave of copper optionality beyond current output.
- Iron ore and logistics: Rio reported the second-highest `Q1` Pilbara production since `2018`, up `13%` YoY, with sales up `2%` YoY. But two tropical cyclones reduced Pilbara shipments by roughly `8 Mt`, with around half expected to be recovered. That is a good reminder that transport, port, and weather exposure are central earnings variables in this business.
- Simandou signal: the first full SimFer shipment of high-grade Simandou product was delivered to China, with first sales realized in April. This is strategically important because Simandou is not just another project; it is a future iron ore and logistics platform with geopolitical and supply-chain implications.
- Lithium and aluminium: management said the integrated aluminium business again delivered a strong performance, while Fenix `1B` and Sal de Vida reached mechanical completion with first production targeted for `H2 2026`.
- Structural theme: `Q1 2026` shows how Rio’s system can remain resilient even when physical disruptions hit the transport layer. The business absorbed cyclone pressure because scale, geography, and portfolio breadth matter as much as any single mine.

### Quarter minus 2

- `Q4 2025` and the full-year `2025` results are the right base period for Rio because annual results and annual report disclosures carry more financial detail than a simple quarter-only release.
- Full-year financial picture: consolidated sales revenue was `$57.638 billion`, underlying EBITDA was `$25.363 billion`, underlying earnings were `$10.868 billion`, and profit after tax attributable to owners was `$9.966 billion`.
- Cash flow and payout: operating cash flow reached `$16.8 billion`, and the ordinary dividend was `$6.5 billion`, sustaining a `60%` payout ratio. Rio is still fundamentally a conversion engine from mined volumes into shareholder cash.
- Production and mix: management highlighted `8%` growth in CuEq production, record Pilbara iron ore production since April, and rising copper and aluminium contributions. The important nuance is that the growth narrative came from portfolio mix change, not from abandoning iron ore.
- Project sequence: the `2025` materials highlight the Oyu Tolgoi underground development project completion, Simandou first ore shipment in December, Western Range opening on time and on budget, and continuing lithium construction progress. This is what capital intensity looks like in practice: the investment case is inseparable from a multi-asset build queue.
- Structural theme: `Q4 2025` is the bridge quarter showing that Rio entered `2026` with both high cash generation and an unusually broad set of long-cycle growth options. That combination is why it belongs in the archive as more than just another iron ore name.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Rio adds the global diversified-miner and bulk-materials layer that was still missing from the archive after the earlier copper, steel, aluminium, uranium, and precious-metals packets.
- Consumer: Rio is far upstream from end consumers, but affordability still matters indirectly through construction, autos, power systems, and industrial demand. When metals costs, freight friction, or project delays rise here, downstream systems eventually feel it.
- Cultural: there is no direct cultural consumer pattern, but there is a societal and strategic-systems pattern. Decarbonization and electrification narratives depend on whether companies like Rio can actually deliver copper, aluminium, lithium, and iron ore at scale.
- Societal: Rio is a useful packet for energy security and industrial policy. Simandou, Oyu Tolgoi, Resolution, and the lithium projects all show how commodity systems are also geopolitical systems involving host-country relationships, rail and port buildout, and long permitting arcs.
- Regulatory: the company is highly exposed to permitting, environmental oversight, trade policy, and geopolitical risk, but the filings also show a quieter operational truth: weather, shipping, and infrastructure execution can matter just as much as formal regulation.
- Capital allocation: Rio behaves like a portfolio allocator more than a single-asset miner. Cash from iron ore and legacy assets is being redeployed into copper, lithium, and replacement or expansion projects intended to reshape the mix over time.
- Industrial: this packet shows why industrial metals should be split into subtypes. Rio is not a pure copper scarcity name, not a steel converter, not a distributor, and not a gold major. It is a scale logistics-and-mining system where iron ore cash flow funds diversification into other strategic materials.
- Comparative read: Rio is one of the best comparison packets for `Teck`. Teck is a copper-growth and zinc-processing portfolio with merger-driven industry-structure questions. Rio is a larger and more globally embedded system where transport, ports, mega-projects, and commodity mix rotation are the central strategic variables.
