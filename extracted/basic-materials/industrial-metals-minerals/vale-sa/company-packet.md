## Annual report takeaways

- Vale is the right next packet after `Rio Tinto` because it adds a second giant ore-and-logistics system rather than another copper-only or precious-metals case. Both are global miners, but Vale is more concentrated around Brazilian iron ore corridors, pellets, and a more visibly separate Base Metals growth narrative in copper and nickel.
- AnnualReports was useful only for taxonomy and lag confirmation. The saved AnnualReports company page correctly places Vale in `Industrial Metals & Minerals`, but it still pointed to the `2024 Annual Report` when collected on `Monday, August 10, 2026`. The authoritative `2025` annual chain therefore comes from Vale’s annual-report release page, official reports index, and SEC `20-F`.
- Vale’s official annual-report release page gives a clear annual base. It says `2025` net revenue was `$38.4 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$15.5 billion`, recurring free cash flow was `$4.8 billion`, and iron ore production reached `336 million tons`, the highest level since `2018`.
- The `20-F` reinforces the same direction with slightly more formal filing language: Vale recorded net income of about `$2.0 billion` in `2025`, while adjusted EBITDA rose to `$15.458 billion` from `$14.840 billion` in `2024`, helped by higher iron ore, copper, and nickel sales volumes and the depreciation of the Brazilian real.
- Operational stability and capital discipline are core themes in the annual framing. Vale explicitly ties `2025` performance to stronger operating stability, cost discipline, and capital allocation, which matters because this company still carries a trust-and-risk overhang from dams and remediation.
- Trust and license-to-operate remain a real part of the packet. The annual release highlights GISTM implementation across all dams, no structures at emergency level `3`, `63%` completion of the upstream dam decharacterization program, and `81%` fulfillment of Brumadinho remediation obligations. That makes Vale more than a commodity story; it is also a systems-trust and operational-governance story.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- `Q2 2026` results were strong but not frictionless. Net revenues were `$7.888 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$3.676 billion`, proforma EBITDA was `$4.066 billion`, and recurring free cash flow was `$1.505 billion`.
- Sales improved across all major lines. Iron ore, copper, and nickel sales increased `3%`, `10%`, and `7%` year over year, respectively. That matters because it shows Vale’s quarter was volume-backed rather than purely price-driven.
- Pricing also helped, especially in base metals. Realized iron ore fines prices averaged `$95.0/t`, down `1%` sequentially but up `12%` year over year. Realized copper prices rose to `$14,062/t`, up `7%` quarter over quarter and `57%` year over year, while realized nickel prices reached `$18,061/t`, up `6%` sequentially and `14%` year over year.
- Costs were the main offset. Vale said iron ore `C1` cash cost was `$24.1/t`, up `9%` year over year, mainly because of BRL appreciation, and all-in costs reached `$61.6/t`, pressured further by higher freight and bunker prices.
- The strategic read is broader than cost and price. Management said iron ore reached its highest `Q2` production since `2018`, copper had its best `Q2` in nine years, Serra Sul `+20` was moving toward start-up, and the Bacaba copper project was advancing ahead of schedule. This is a useful packet for seeing how a giant miner tries to improve current cash generation while still seeding the next decade of ore and base-metals growth.
- Structural theme: Vale’s latest quarter shows that bulk-materials businesses are not just about ore volume. FX, freight, logistics, and project ramp sequencing can all alter the conversion of strong production into margins and cash flow.

### Quarter minus 1

- `Q1 2026` net revenues were `$6.875 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$3.830 billion`, proforma EBITDA was `$3.895 billion`, and recurring free cash flow was `$813 million`.
- Sales performance improved across all business segments. Iron ore, copper, and nickel sales increased `4%`, `11%`, and `15%` year over year, respectively.
- Realized base-metals pricing was materially stronger. Vale said realized iron ore fines prices were `$95.8/t`, up `0.4%` sequentially and `5.5%` year over year. Realized copper prices rose `19%` sequentially and `48%` year over year to `$13,143/t`, while realized nickel prices increased `13%` sequentially and `6%` year over year to `$17,015/t`.
- Cost pressure remained visible in iron ore. `Q1 2026` iron ore `C1` cash cost totaled `$23.6/t`, up `12%` year over year, mainly because of BRL appreciation, while all-in costs reached `$55.4/t`.
- The production-and-sales report adds the physical-system angle. Vale said iron ore sales reached their highest first-quarter level since `2018`, while copper posted its best first-quarter output since `2017` and nickel its best since `2020`. That matters because the company is trying to prove that Base Metals growth is becoming operationally tangible rather than only strategic messaging.
- Structural theme: `Q1 2026` is one of the clearer proofs that Vale’s earnings quality depends on a full chain: mine and processing execution, rail and port flow, pricing, and currency dynamics all interact at once.

### Quarter minus 2

- `Q4 2025` and full-year `2025` together provide the strongest bridge into `2026`. Vale reported `Q4 2025` adjusted EBITDA of `$4.588 billion`, proforma EBITDA of `$4.834 billion`, and recurring free cash flow of `$1.688 billion`.
- For full-year `2025`, the quarter materials show adjusted EBITDA of `$15.458 billion`, proforma EBITDA of `$15.869 billion`, and recurring free cash flow of `$4.762 billion`.
- Vale said all `2025` guidances were achieved, which is strategically important because this company has to rebuild confidence not only through volumes but through predictability.
- Sales were robust across the main mined products. In `Q4 2025`, iron ore, copper, and nickel sales increased `5%`, `8%`, and `5%` year over year, respectively. Across full-year `2025`, sales increased `3%`, `12%`, and `11%`, respectively.
- Pricing and cost detail sharpen the business-model read. Average realized iron ore fines price was `$95.4/t` in `Q4 2025`, up `1%` sequentially and `3%` year over year. Iron ore `C1` cash cost for `2025` was `$21.3/t`, down `2%` year over year, while `Q4 2025` all-in cost was `$54.3/t`.
- Structural theme: `Q4 2025` shows Vale entering `2026` with three things at once: stronger ore volumes, a clearer Base Metals growth story, and continued evidence that risk management and remediation progress are still part of how the market will judge the company.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Vale deepens the industrial-metals lane by adding a second giant global bulk-materials platform beside `Rio Tinto`, which is more analytically useful than adding another narrow commodity exposure.
- Consumer: Vale is far upstream from consumers, but affordability still transmits through steel, autos, construction, energy infrastructure, and electrification inputs. What looks like an iron ore or copper issue here eventually hits downstream capex and end-market costs.
- Cultural: there is no direct consumer culture story, but there is a social-trust and institutional-legitimacy story. Vale’s filings still have to prove that a large miner can regain operational and public trust after a severe tailings-dam legacy.
- Societal: this packet is one of the cleanest examples of how commodity systems are also social-license systems. Dam safety, remediation, environmental restoration, and community obligations are inseparable from the investment case.
- Regulatory: Vale has more visible remediation, dam-governance, and environmental oversight pressure than many peers. That makes it a strong comparator for miners whose risk profile is shaped not just by geology and logistics but by institutional trust.
- Capital allocation: the company is trying to balance ore-system cash generation with a Base Metals growth push in copper and nickel. That is a different portfolio-design problem than Rio’s iron ore plus aluminium plus copper plus lithium mix, but the broader theme is similar: legacy cash engines funding strategic diversification.
- Industrial: Vale shows why iron ore leaders should not be grouped too casually. This is not only a volume-and-price story. Rail, ports, pellet mix, cash-cost discipline, FX exposure, freight, and remediation liabilities all shape returns.
- Comparative read: Vale is a strong pair with `Rio Tinto`. Rio reads like a global mining-and-logistics allocator with iron ore funding copper, aluminium, and lithium diversification. Vale reads like a Brazilian ore-and-logistics system that is trying to convert stronger operational stability into higher trust, stronger cash returns, and a more meaningful copper-and-nickel growth lane.
