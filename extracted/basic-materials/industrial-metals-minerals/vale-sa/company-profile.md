## What changed

- Second giant ore-and-logistics comparator: Vale gives the archive a second global bulk-materials platform next to `Rio Tinto`, but with a different operating center of gravity: Brazilian iron ore systems, dam-risk legacy, and a more explicit Base Metals carve-out narrative.
- AnnualReports lag is explicit: when collected on `2026-08-10`, AnnualReports still showed the `2024 Annual Report`, so the authoritative `2025` annual chain had to come from Vale’s annual-report release page and SEC `20-F`.
- Annual base year: the official annual-report release page says `2025` net revenue was `$38.4 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$15.5 billion`, recurring free cash flow was `$4.8 billion`, and iron ore production reached `336 million tons`, the highest since `2018`.
- Portfolio mix matters: Vale remains heavily tied to iron ore and logistics, but the annual release and the `Q1` and `Q2 2026` materials repeatedly stress Base Metals growth in copper and nickel as a strategic energy-transition lane rather than a side business.
- Risk and trust remain part of the story: the annual release emphasizes completion of GISTM implementation at all dams, no structures at emergency level `3`, and continued upstream dam decharacterization and Brumadinho remediation progress. That makes Vale a more explicit trust-and-license-to-operate packet than Rio.
- `2026` quarter shape: Vale’s `Q1` and `Q2` results show better sales and stronger realized copper and nickel pricing, but they also show how BRL strength, freight costs, and bunker prices can compress some iron ore cost optics even in a solid operating environment.

## Open questions

- How much of Vale’s upside depends on ore and logistics execution versus whether Base Metals becomes a meaningfully larger share of the mix?
- Does the company’s trust and remediation burden remain a discount on the equity story even if operational stability continues to improve?
