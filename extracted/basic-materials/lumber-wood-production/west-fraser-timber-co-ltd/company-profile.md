## What changed

- Sector role: West Fraser adds the wood-products and housing-input layer that the current Basic Materials archive was missing. The company spans lumber, OSB, LVL, MDF, plywood, particleboard, pulp, paper, wood chips, and residuals.
- Taxonomy and listing note: AnnualReports still classifies West Fraser as `Basic Materials / Lumber, Wood Production`, but the company page used the legacy TSX ticker `WFT` and still lagged at the `2024` annual package as of `2026-08-09`.
- Annual base year: `2025` sales were `$5.462 billion`, earnings were `$(937) million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$56 million`, with the year hurt by a difficult lumber and panel environment plus large restructuring and impairment charges.
- Portfolio shape: West Fraser framed itself as a leading lumber and engineered wood producer. In `2025`, `54%` of lumber capacity was in the U.S. South, `28%` in Alberta, and `18%` in British Columbia.
- Capital strategy: the company invested `$411 million` back into the business in `2025`, paid `$101 million` in dividends, repurchased `$124 million` of stock, and ended the year with more than `$1.2 billion` of available liquidity.
- Operating repositioning: `2025` and the trailing quarterlies repeatedly emphasized portfolio high-grading, the Henderson, Texas lumber-mill commissioning, the Allendale, South Carolina OSB ramp-up, and the closure or wind-down of weaker assets such as High Level, Alberta OSB.
- Quarter pattern: `Q4 2025` was the weakest quarter in the current window, `Q1 2026` was still loss-making due partly to a large duty adjustment, and `Q2 2026` improved meaningfully as pricing and segment performance stabilized and all core segments turned positive on an adjusted EBITDA basis.
- Demand exposure: this is a direct read on housing affordability, mortgage rates, repair and remodeling, industrial wood demand, and the long-term housing-supply deficit, not just on commodity lumber pricing alone.
- Policy exposure: softwood duties, Section 232 tariffs, and the newly announced Section 338 tariffs all show up as direct earnings and planning variables.

## Open questions

- Does the portfolio high-grading and U.S. South shift materially reduce cycle volatility, or does it mainly lower the cost base inside the same highly cyclical end-market exposure?
