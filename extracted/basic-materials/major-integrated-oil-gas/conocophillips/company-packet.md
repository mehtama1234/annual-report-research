## Annual report takeaways

- ConocoPhillips is useful to the new energy-inputs buildout because it behaves less like a refining-and-marketing system and more like a global upstream cash machine with a strong capital-allocation overlay.
- The packet also shows why energy cannot be read only through top-line sector labels. AnnualReports classifies ConocoPhillips as `Major Integrated Oil & Gas`, but the actual evidence chain is much closer to a scale upstream portfolio built around production depth, low-cost supply, LNG optionality, and shareholder returns.
- Full-year `2025` earnings were `$8.0 billion`, adjusted earnings were `$7.7 billion`, cash provided by operating activities was `$19.8 billion`, and CFO was `$19.9 billion`.
- In `2025`, the company distributed `$9.0 billion` to shareholders, including `$5.0 billion` of buybacks and `$4.0 billion` of ordinary dividends, while funding `$12.6 billion` of capital expenditures and investments.
- Full-year production was `2,375 MBOED`, with Lower 48 at `1,484 MBOED`, reinforcing that the investment case is still anchored in the scale and durability of the shale and broader upstream base.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` EPS was `$3.23`, adjusted EPS was `$3.24`, cash provided by operating activities was `$7.4 billion`, CFO was `$7.2 billion`, and total production was `2,248 MBOED`.
- Guidance: management kept the company on track for a `45%` return of CFO in `2026` and maintained the larger free-cash-flow inflection story toward `2029`.
- Management message: management emphasized exceptional operating execution, record Permian production, doubled share repurchases, hitting the `$5 billion` asset-disposition target early, and increasing LNG offtake.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is the clearest proof that the company is trying to turn an upstream portfolio into a disciplined capital-return platform. The important read is not only higher EPS; it is that portfolio high-grading, low-cost barrels, LNG positioning, and aggressive buybacks are being presented as one integrated capital-allocation system.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` EPS was `$1.78`, adjusted EPS was `$1.89`, cash provided by operating activities was `$4.3 billion`, CFO was `$5.4 billion`, and production was `2,309 MBOED`.
- Guidance: the company excluded Qatar from second-quarter production guidance because of Middle East uncertainty and guided second-quarter production to `2.185` to `2.215 MMBOED`.
- Management message: management kept the same shareholder-return tone but had to acknowledge geopolitical fragility more directly, especially through the Qatar production caveat.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` is useful because it shows how exposed an upstream-led model remains to geopolitics and transport or market disruption. The business still throws off cash, but the quarter reminds you that the cash engine depends on global physical reliability, not just on management rhetoric about discipline.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` EPS was `$1.17`, adjusted EPS was `$1.02`, fourth-quarter cash provided by operating activities and CFO were each `$4.3 billion`, and full-year `2025` earnings were `$8.0 billion`.
- Guidance: management guided to roughly `$12 billion` of `2026` capital expenditures, `$10.2 billion` of adjusted operating costs, and `2026` production of `2.33` to `2.36 MMBOED`.
- Management message: management framed `2025` as another year of strong execution, top-quartile dividend growth, and consistent delivery against the returns-focused value proposition despite weaker commodity conditions.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` closes the year with the cleanest statement of the company’s strategy. This is a large-scale upstream operator trying to make itself legible as a returns compounder: sustain the base dividend, keep buying back stock, high-grade the asset base, push LNG and low-cost supply options, and use dispositions to stay flexible.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: ConocoPhillips strengthens the archive’s energy read by showing an upstream-first system that is distinct from integrated majors, pipelines, and refiners even when taxonomy labels blur those boundaries.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is not the main read. The more relevant demand variables are global crude and gas pricing, industrial energy need, LNG offtake, and the reliability of international supply chains.
- Cultural: the broader signal is that energy security still has real pricing power and political weight. A company like ConocoPhillips benefits when reliability, scale, and low-cost resource depth matter more than transition narrative alone.
- Regulatory: this packet is sensitive to geopolitics, Middle East conflict, permitting, climate policy, and trade or sanctions risk. The Qatar production-guidance caveat in `Q1 2026` is the cleanest example.
- Labor: labor is not the headline signal in the quarterlies collected so far, but major project execution, shale productivity, maintenance, and field reliability remain labor- and capability-dependent.
- Technology: technology matters here through drilling efficiency, project design, LNG commercialization, and production optimization rather than through a software-style AI angle.
- Capital allocation: ConocoPhillips is one of the stronger examples in the archive of an upstream company trying to be judged on CFO conversion, return of capital, capital intensity discipline, and disposal-backed portfolio management rather than only on barrel growth.
