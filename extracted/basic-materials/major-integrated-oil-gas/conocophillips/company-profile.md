## What changed

- Revenue and earnings: `Q4 2025` and full-year `2025` earnings fell from the prior year because lower prices outweighed volume gains. `Q1 2026` stayed profitable but was still a more moderate quarter. `Q2 2026` rebounded sharply, with EPS stepping up to `$3.23`.
- Cash flow: full-year `2025` cash provided by operating activities was `$19.8 billion` and CFO was `$19.9 billion`. `Q1 2026` operating cash flow was `$4.3 billion` and CFO was `$5.4 billion`. `Q2 2026` operating cash flow rose to `$7.4 billion` and CFO was `$7.2 billion`.
- Capital allocation: management kept a disciplined return-of-capital frame intact. In `2025`, ConocoPhillips distributed `$9.0 billion` to shareholders. By `Q2 2026`, the company had doubled quarterly share repurchases and was still targeting a `45%` return of CFO in `2026`.
- Capital spending: `2025` capital expenditures and investments were `$12.6 billion`. `Q1 2026` funding for capital expenditures and investments was `$2.9 billion`, and `Q2 2026` was `$3.0 billion`, keeping the company broadly aligned with its roughly `$12 billion` full-year capital plan.
- Production: full-year `2025` production was `2,375 MBOED`. `Q1 2026` production was `2,309 MBOED`, then `Q2 2026` was `2,248 MBOED`. Lower 48 remains the engine, with Delaware, Midland, Eagle Ford, and Bakken volumes doing most of the work.
- Operational issues: `Q1 2026` guidance excluded Qatar from second-quarter production guidance because of uncertainty tied to Middle East conflict. That makes the packet useful for understanding how geopolitics can hit a nominally diversified upstream operator.
- Portfolio direction: management kept emphasizing dispositions, LNG offtake growth, low-cost-of-supply project inventory, and the post-Marathon Oil integration. This is a cleaner upstream portfolio and capital-allocation story than a downstream or chemicals story.
- Management tone: the tone is disciplined, returns-focused, and scale-aware. The company is trying to show that upstream concentration does not mean fragile economics if it pairs low-cost barrels, disposal discipline, LNG optionality, and aggressive shareholder returns.

## Open questions

- How much of the `Q2 2026` earnings rebound reflects sustainable operating leverage versus a temporary improvement in commodity realizations?
- Can ConocoPhillips keep balancing large shareholder returns with project needs such as Willow and LNG-linked growth without becoming more exposed to cycle whiplash?
