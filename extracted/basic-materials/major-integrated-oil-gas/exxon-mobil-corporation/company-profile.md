## What changed

- Revenue and earnings: full-year `2025` earnings were `$28.8 billion`, down from `$33.7 billion` in `2024`, but still industry-leading in ExxonMobil’s framing. `Q1 2026` reported earnings fell to `$4.2 billion` because of Middle East disruptions and large unfavorable timing effects, while `Q2 2026` rebounded sharply to `$14.5 billion`.
- Cash flow: ExxonMobil generated `$52.0 billion` of operating cash flow and `$26.1 billion` of free cash flow in full-year `2025`. In `Q1 2026`, operating cash flow was `$8.7 billion` and free cash flow was `$2.7 billion`. In `Q2 2026`, operating cash flow rose to `$23.6 billion` and free cash flow to `$17.2 billion`.
- Capital allocation: shareholder distributions stayed central. ExxonMobil distributed `$37.2 billion` in `2025`, including `$17.2 billion` of dividends and `$20.0 billion` of buybacks. Shareholder distributions were `$9.2 billion` in `Q1 2026` and `$9.4 billion` in `Q2 2026`.
- Capital spending: cash capital expenditures were `$29.0 billion` in `2025`. `Q1 2026` cash capital expenditures were `$6.2 billion`, and year-to-date through `Q2 2026` they reached about `$13.0 billion`, still consistent with the company’s full-year plan.
- Segment mix: the company is no longer best read as only an upstream oil price story. In `2025`, earnings by business were `$21.4 billion` for Upstream, `$7.4 billion` for Energy Products, `$0.8 billion` for Chemical Products, and `$2.9 billion` for Specialty Products, with Corporate and Financing negative. `Q2 2026` was especially strong in Upstream and Energy Products.
- Production: full-year `2025` oil-equivalent production averaged `4,736` koebd, the highest annual Upstream production in more than `40` years in the company’s own framing. `Q1 2026` production was `4,594` koebd, while `Q2 2026` production was `4,514` koebd, with record Permian output above `1.8` Moebd and strong Guyana growth offset by Middle East disruption.
- Operational issues: `Q1 2026` was heavily affected by Middle East supply disruption and timing effects from derivatives versus physical settlements. `Q2 2026` still carried Middle East impacts but showed how much portfolio integration, reliability, and trading or logistics flexibility can stabilize results.
- Technology and infrastructure linkage: ExxonMobil is one of the clearest energy-system enablers in the archive. Its read touches LNG, Guyana, Permian growth, refining and diesel output, high-value products, and a broader “reliable energy when it matters most” message that matters for industrial and power-system themes.
- Management tone: management remains disciplined and expansive at the same time. The tone is that ExxonMobil’s advantage comes from portfolio quality, integration, structural cost savings, and capital-allocation capacity rather than only from commodity price direction.
