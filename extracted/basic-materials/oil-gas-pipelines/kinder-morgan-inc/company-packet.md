# Kinder Morgan Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Kinder Morgan, Inc.
- Sector: Basic Materials
- Industry: Oil & Gas Pipelines
- Ticker: KMI
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Kinder Morgan is the clearest pipeline and transport-infrastructure name for this batch because the business is built around fee-based midstream cash flows, long-term contracts, and physical bottleneck control rather than direct commodity ownership.
- The annual-report chain is especially useful for the bigger-picture energy thesis because it shows how energy security and power demand become monetizable through pipes, storage, and terminals rather than only through drilling or refining.
- Full-year `2025` net income attributable to KMI was a record, up `17%` versus `2024`, while adjusted EPS rose `13%` and adjusted EBITDA rose `6%`.
- Fourth-quarter `2025` net income attributable to KMI was `$996 million`, adjusted net income attributable to KMI was `$866 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$2.271 billion`.
- By the end of `2025`, backlog stood at `$10.0 billion`, with roughly `90%` tied to natural-gas projects and nearly `60%` associated with power generation support.
- The company framed itself as a key infrastructure beneficiary of U.S. LNG export growth, saying it delivered more than `40%` of the natural-gas feedstock to U.S. LNG facilities and had long-term contracts to move `8 Bcf/d` to LNG facilities, projected to rise to `12 Bcf/d` by the end of `2028`.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income attributable to KMI was `$867 million`, adjusted net income attributable to KMI was `$821 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.199 billion`, EPS was `$0.39`, and adjusted EPS was `$0.37`.
- Cash flow and balance sheet: cash flow from operations was about `$2.0 billion`, free cash flow after capital expenditures was about `$1.0 billion`, and net debt-to-adjusted EBITDA ended the quarter at `3.6x`.
- Guidance: management said KMI now expected to be more than `5%` favorable to the `2026` adjusted EBITDA budget and more than `12%` favorable to the `2026` adjusted EPS budget, while still expecting year-end leverage of about `3.6x`.
- Management message: management emphasized that stable fee-based cash flows were funding almost all of the project backlog internally while supporting dividend growth and a strong balance sheet.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is the clearest proof that pipeline systems are effectively toll roads on rising LNG, power, and industrial gas demand. The important read is not only earnings growth, but the way utilization, connectivity, and contract structure turn transport infrastructure into durable cash generation.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income attributable to KMI was `$976 million`, adjusted net income attributable to KMI was `$1.063 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.539 billion`, EPS was `$0.44`, and adjusted EPS was `$0.48`.
- Cash flow and balance sheet: cash flow from operations was about `$1.5 billion`, free cash flow was about `$687 million`, and net debt-to-adjusted EBITDA ended the quarter at `3.6x`.
- Guidance: management said the company was already trending more than `3%` favorable to the `2026` adjusted EBITDA budget because of first-quarter outperformance, excluding Monument Pipeline acquisition contributions.
- Management message: management stressed that geopolitical turmoil mattered less to Kinder Morgan than to upstream producers because its midstream model is anchored by long-term, take-or-pay, fee-based contracts with creditworthy customers.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` shows why pipelines matter in the energy system. Winter weather, LNG pull, storage position, and network utilization drove outsized results without requiring Kinder Morgan to be the commodity owner.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net income attributable to KMI was `$996 million`, adjusted net income attributable to KMI was `$866 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.271 billion`, EPS was `$0.45`, and adjusted EPS was `$0.39`.
- Cash flow and balance sheet: fourth-quarter `2025` cash flow from operations was about `$1.7 billion`, free cash flow after capital expenditures was about `$0.9 billion`, and net debt-to-adjusted EBITDA ended the quarter at `3.8x`.
- Guidance: the `2026` budget called for net income attributable to KMI of about `$3.1 billion`, adjusted EPS of `$1.36`, adjusted EBITDA of `$8.6 billion`, and dividends of `$1.19` per share.
- Management message: management highlighted record Natural Gas Pipelines performance, increasing LNG feedgas exposure, power-demand growth, and the backlog mix skewing toward natural-gas infrastructure.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` closes the year with the strongest case for energy transport as an infrastructure business. Kinder Morgan is not simply exposed to commodity cycles; it is exposed to the need for molecules to move reliably from basin to demand center.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Kinder Morgan adds the transport and bottleneck layer that neither upstream producers nor refiners can supply.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is indirect here. The real demand inputs are LNG exports, utility and data-center power demand, industrial activity, and the continued need to move gas, NGLs, crude, and products through fixed networks.
- Cultural: the broader social signal is that energy security and electrification rhetoric still rely on old-economy infrastructure. Pipelines and storage remain physically embedded in the economy even when the public conversation focuses elsewhere.
- Regulatory: the packet is highly exposed to permitting, safety regulation, environmental litigation, interstate pipeline policy, and the pace at which U.S. gas infrastructure can expand.
- Labor: labor is not the headline in the quarterly message set, but project execution, system reliability, maintenance, and storage or terminal operations are still labor- and contractor-dependent.
- Technology: technology matters through asset optimization, capacity expansions, compression, storage, and system utilization rather than through a software-led narrative.
- Capital allocation: Kinder Morgan is a good example of a company using budgetable, contract-backed cash generation to fund dividends, growth capital, and internal backlog execution without taking as much direct commodity-price risk as producers.
- Infrastructure dependence: this is one of the strongest packets for the archive’s transport-bottleneck theme because it ties LNG feedgas growth, power-demand growth, storage, and pipeline conversion projects into one coherent infrastructure story.
