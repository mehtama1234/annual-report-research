## Annual report takeaways

- Teck adds a different heavy-input archetype than the names already in the lane. `Freeport-McMoRan` and `Southern Copper` are concentrated copper systems. `Newmont` and `Pan American Silver` are precious-metals systems. The steel packets are domestic conversion systems. `Teck` is a more mixed industrial-metals platform where copper growth, zinc and smelting support, and steelmaking-coal cash flow all coexist inside one capital-allocation frame.
- The attempted AnnualReports capture did not return usable company-specific content in this pass, so it was not strong enough for taxonomy or archive confirmation by itself. The authoritative annual chain therefore comes from Teck’s own annual report PDF and the SEC `40-F`, with company-hosted quarterly PDFs and SEC `6-K` wrappers carrying the quarter evidence.
- The annual base year was already strong before the bigger `2026` quarter acceleration. `2025` revenue was `$10.756 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$4.3 billion`, profit from continuing operations before taxes was `$1.7 billion`, adjusted profit from continuing operations attributable to shareholders was `$1.5 billion`, and profit from continuing operations attributable to shareholders was `$1.4 billion`.
- Teck ended `2025` in a net cash position, supported by `$1.5 billion` of cash flow from operations, with liquidity of `$9.1 billion` including `$5.0 billion` of cash. That balance-sheet posture matters because the company is trying to fund copper growth and strategic optionality from a position of strength rather than from a distressed or overlevered base.
- The strategic story is explicit. Teck describes itself as a leading Canadian mining company focused on metals essential to global economic growth and the energy transition, with ownership in world-class copper and zinc operations, a large metallurgical complex, and an industry-leading copper growth pipeline. This is a portfolio-level bet on copper scarcity and processing relevance, not just a one-mine exposure.
- The merger overlay also matters. The `2025` annual report and subsequent quarters are partly framed around the planned merger of equals with Anglo American to form `Anglo Teck`. That gives this packet a distinctive industry-structure angle: copper growth scarcity is valuable enough to drive large-scale portfolio combination and synergy claims rather than only incremental brownfield spending.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` revenue was `$3.605 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.193 billion`, profit before taxes was `$1.459 billion`, profit attributable to shareholders was `$854 million`, and adjusted profit attributable to shareholders was `$948 million`.
- Cash generation and balance-sheet read: `Q2 2026` cash flow from operations was `$1.7 billion`. Liquidity at `2026-06-30` was `$10.3 billion`, including `$6.1 billion` of cash, and management said the net cash position increased by `$756 million` during the quarter.
- Copper engine: the copper segment generated gross profit before depreciation and amortization of `$1.8 billion`, compared with `$673 million` in `Q2 2025`, helped by record copper prices averaging `US$6.05` per pound and significantly higher production. Copper production volumes of `135,900` tonnes were up `25%` year over year.
- QB and operating consistency: management said QB delivered strong production for the third consecutive quarter, reinforcing the idea that the asset is moving from ramp risk toward a more reliable operating base. That matters because Teck’s equity story is much stronger when QB is seen as stable rather than perpetually troubleshooting.
- Zinc and processing support: the zinc segment generated gross profit before depreciation and amortization of `$353 million`, with `$202 million` of gross profit from Trail Operations. Teck is not just a copper beta vehicle; the processing layer still matters.
- Strategic theme: Teck, Canada Growth Fund Inc., and Natural Resources Canada’s accelerator announced an agreement supporting possible expansion of germanium, gallium, and antimony production at Trail. That ties the packet to critical-minerals refining and Western processing security, not just mine output.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` revenue was `$3.943 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.088 billion`, profit before taxes was `$1.336 billion`, profit attributable to shareholders was `$819 million`, and adjusted profit attributable to shareholders was `$858 million`.
- Cash generation: `Q1 2026` cash flow from operations was `$1.0 billion`, increasing the net cash position by `$338 million` at `2026-03-31`. Liquidity at `2026-04-22` was `$9.8 billion`, including `$5.7 billion` of cash.
- Copper sales and pricing: Teck highlighted record quarterly copper sales and copper prices averaging `US$5.83` per pound. The copper segment generated gross profit before depreciation and amortization of `$1.8 billion`, compared with `$704 million` in `Q1 2025`.
- QB detail: QB copper sales volumes of `70,300` tonnes were a quarterly record and materially exceeded production as inventory was drawn down. Management also said QB delivered strong production consistent with `Q4 2025`, even with a planned maintenance shutdown and a shorter February, which is a meaningful operating-quality signal.
- Zinc support: the zinc segment generated gross profit before depreciation and amortization of `$387 million`, with `$257 million` from Trail Operations. Trail’s optimized feed strategy helps explain why Teck should not be treated as a pure mine-only story.
- Strategic theme: `Q1 2026` is the clearest proof that Teck’s portfolio is working as a copper-growth system rather than only a commodity-price windfall. QB stability, by-product leverage, and zinc-processing support all contributed to the earnings quality.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` revenue was `$3.058 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$1.513 billion`, profit from continuing operations before taxes was `$792 million`, profit from continuing operations attributable to shareholders was `$544 million`, and adjusted profit from continuing operations attributable to shareholders was `$671 million`.
- Cash generation and liquidity: Teck generated `$1.3 billion` of cash flow from operations in `Q4 2025`. Liquidity at `2026-02-18` was `$9.3 billion`, including `$5.2 billion` of cash.
- Copper contribution: the copper segment generated gross profit before depreciation and amortization of `$1.1 billion`, with gross profit of `$747 million`, driven by higher copper prices averaging `US$5.03` per pound and lower smelter processing charges.
- Zinc contribution: the zinc segment generated gross profit before depreciation and amortization of `$305 million`, with gross profit of `$243 million`. Lower Red Dog shipment timing was largely offset by stronger Trail profitability.
- Operating and strategic context: `Q4 2025` is the bridge quarter showing that Teck entered `2026` with both pricing tailwinds and better operating footing. Progress on the QB action plan continued, including tailings management development and operational improvement, while the Anglo American merger advanced through shareholder approval and Canadian regulatory approval.
- Structural theme: Teck is one of the cleaner examples of how “industrial metals” should not be treated as a simple copper line item. The real story is a portfolio of copper growth, zinc and smelting resilience, steelmaking-coal cash flow, and strategic consolidation pressure.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Teck expands the heavy-input lane into a more diversified industrial-metals system rather than another pure copper, pure precious-metals, or pure steel name.
- Consumer: the company is far upstream from consumers, but it is still indirectly tied to affordability and growth through construction, electrification, industrial production, and the cost of capital for large energy-transition and mining projects.
- Cultural: there is no direct cultural lens here, but there is a societal and strategic-systems lens. The energy transition narrative is only real if copper, zinc, and refining capacity can actually be financed, operated, and expanded at scale.
- Societal: this packet adds resource security and processing security as themes. Trail Operations and the possible expansion into germanium, gallium, and antimony capacity matter because the West is trying to rebuild more of the refining and critical-minerals chain domestically or within allied systems.
- Regulatory: Teck is unusually exposed to regulatory and political structure because the Anglo American merger requires multi-jurisdiction approvals and because copper growth projects depend on permitting, social license, and host-government stability.
- Capital allocation: the company is trying to translate a strong balance sheet and a net cash position into copper growth and strategic optionality. The market is rewarding copper leverage, but the packet shows that portfolio shape and merger strategy matter almost as much as near-term spot prices.
- Industrial: Teck shows that diversified mining should be split more carefully. This is not the same as a giant iron-ore house, not the same as a pure copper miner, and not the same as a precious-metals operator. It is a copper-growth platform with zinc-processing depth and steelmaking-coal cash support.
- Comparative read: Teck is one of the best packets for showing how copper scarcity changes corporate behavior. It encourages large-scale merger logic, makes ramp reliability at QB disproportionately important, and raises the value of associated refining and by-product systems around zinc and specialty metals.
