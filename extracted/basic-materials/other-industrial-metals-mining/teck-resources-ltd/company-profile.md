## What changed

- Portfolio shape: Teck is no longer just a coal-and-zinc story. The 2025 annual report frames it as a metals-for-the-energy-transition company built around copper growth, zinc and Trail smelting support, and a remaining steelmaking-coal cash engine.
- Annual base year: `2025` revenue was `$10.756 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$4.3 billion`, adjusted profit from continuing operations attributable to shareholders was `$1.5 billion`, profit from continuing operations attributable to shareholders was `$1.4 billion`, and cash flow from operations was `$1.5 billion`.
- Balance-sheet quality: Teck ended `2025` in a net cash position with liquidity of `$9.1 billion`, including `$5.0 billion` of cash. That gives the company much more strategic flexibility than a comparably cyclical miner carrying heavy net debt into a major growth phase.
- Copper weighting: the annual report positions Teck as a top-10 copper producer in the Americas with an industry-leading copper growth pipeline. QB is the center of gravity, but the portfolio also includes Highland Valley Copper, Antamina, and Carmen de Andacollo.
- Zinc and processing support: Teck remains the largest net zinc miner globally and owns Trail Operations, one of the world’s largest integrated zinc and lead smelting and refining complexes. That matters because the company is not only exposed to mine output; it also sits in the processing and refined-metals layer.
- Fiscal 2026 acceleration: the `Q4 2025` through `Q2 2026` reporting run shows copper becoming a much stronger earnings engine as QB stabilized, copper prices stayed unusually strong, and by-product revenue amplified margins.
- Strategic overlay: the annual and quarterly materials are deeply shaped by the planned Anglo American merger. That makes Teck a useful packet for understanding how scarce copper growth options can drive industry consolidation, capital allocation, and portfolio redesign.

## Open questions

- How much of the current earnings strength remains if copper prices retreat while QB continues to scale?
- Does the Anglo American merger path strengthen Teck’s copper-growth strategy enough to outweigh the complexity and integration risk that now sit over the standalone investment case?
