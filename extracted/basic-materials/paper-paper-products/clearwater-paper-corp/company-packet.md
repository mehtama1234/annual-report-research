## Annual report takeaways

- Clearwater extends the Basic Materials archive into paperboard and packaging. That matters because it connects the sector more directly to food service, household packaged goods, folding-carton demand, and the material substrate of everyday consumer products.
- The current Clearwater story is much more focused than the historic company description suggests. After the tissue divestiture, the `2025` annual filing and `2026` quarterlies read as a paperboard packaging company centered on SBS production, converter relationships, and packaging-market economics.
- The `2025` base year was operationally better than the GAAP headline. Net sales from continuing operations were `$1.6 billion`, adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was `$107 million`, and net loss from continuing operations was `$53 million`, with the loss driven mainly by a `$48 million` non-cash goodwill impairment.
- The strongest annual message is cost and execution. Management highlighted more than `$50 million` of fixed-cost reductions in `2025`, including `$16 million` of SG&A savings, plus all three major maintenance outages completed on target at about `$50 million` of direct cost.
- Clearwater also used the year to finish the strategic reset: integrating Augusta, separating the tissue business, improving the operating footprint, and pushing SG&A down from `8.4%` to `6.5%` of sales.
- The market backdrop was still weak. Full-year volumes rose to `1,236,114` tons, but average net selling price fell to `$1,167` per ton from `$1,210`, which is the core reason the annual read is “better operator in a still-bad market,” not “cycle fully recovered.”
- The annual strategic read is that Clearwater is trying to become a more focused, lower-cost paperboard supplier positioned for eventual recovery in SBS operating rates, rather than a diversified paper-and-tissue company.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net sales were `$375 million`, net loss from continuing operations was `$21 million`, diluted EPS was `$(1.33)`, and adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was `($8) million`.
- Operating drivers: the quarter was hurt by lower market pricing and the timing of the planned Lewiston, Idaho outage, which was completed on time and on target at a total direct cost of `$22 million`.
- Volume and pricing: sales volumes rose to `328,722` tons, up `8%` year over year, but paperboard average net selling price fell `9%` to `$1,077` per ton.
- Cash and balance sheet: liquidity was `$445 million`, net leverage was `3.5x`, and net debt fell by `$59 million` in the quarter through lower working capital, a tax refund, and insurance recovery.
- Management message: Clearwater said it was seeing early signs of improvement in SBS conditions through volume growth, lower imports, and higher operating rates, but pricing was still below acceptable-return levels.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is the clearest evidence that pricing and maintenance timing still dominate near-term earnings even when volumes are improving and management is cutting costs.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net sales were `$360 million`, net loss from continuing operations was `$13 million`, diluted EPS was `$(0.80)`, and adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was `$2 million`.
- Operating drivers: lower pricing and severe weather at Augusta and Cypress Bend were the main reasons adjusted EBITDA fell from `$30 million` in `Q1 2025` to roughly breakeven in `Q1 2026`.
- Volume and pricing: sales volumes rose `5%` to `302,918` tons, but average net selling price fell `7%` to `$1,101` per ton.
- Restructuring and product mix: the quarter included the Velora lightweight folding-carton launch and the April `2026` Cypress Bend restructuring, with expected annual savings of `$8 million` to `$12 million`.
- Balance sheet: liquidity remained solid at `$407 million` and net leverage was `2.9x`.
- Guidance frame: management guided `Q2 2026` adjusted EBITDA to `$0` to `-$10 million`, maintained `2026` revenue guidance of `$1.4 billion` to `$1.5 billion`, and kept capex guidance at `$65 million` to `$75 million`.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net sales were `$386 million`, net income from continuing operations was `$3 million`, diluted EPS from continuing operations was positive, and adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was `$20 million`.
- Full-year frame: full-year `2025` net sales from continuing operations were `$1.6 billion`, adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was `$107 million`, and net loss from continuing operations was `$53 million`.
- Volume and pricing: `Q4 2025` volumes rose to `317,715` tons, up `4%`, while average net selling price fell `3%` to `$1,139` per ton; full-year average price was `$1,167` per ton.
- Execution and capital allocation: Clearwater ended `2025` with `$439 million` of available liquidity, `2.5x` net leverage, and had repurchased roughly `$17 million` to `$21 million` of stock under the buyback authorization launched in November `2024`.
- Market frame: management emphasized an oversupplied SBS industry, `83%` operating rates in `Q4 2025`, `500K` tons of new North American capacity added in `2025`, and the need for demand growth plus supply rationalization to restore healthy margins.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` is the best “reset quarter” read. The company had materially improved operations and cost structure, but pricing and industry capacity still capped earnings quality.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Clearwater proves that Basic Materials reaches into fiber, paperboard, and packaging, not only metals, chemicals, energy, lumber, and fertilizers.
- Consumer: this is one of the most direct consumer-adjacent names in the current sector pilot because the end markets include food service, folding cartons, and everyday packaged goods.
- Cultural: Clearwater adds a useful “everyday materials” layer. A lot of modern consumption depends on commodity-like inputs that are invisible to consumers until packaging costs, supply reliability, or sustainability claims become a problem.
- Sustainability: the company repeatedly frames paperboard as a sustainable packaging substrate, and the `2026` product launches show how fiber-based packaging is being positioned against other packaging materials.
- Labor and operations: weather, outages, maintenance execution, facility restructuring, and cost actions all show up directly in quarterly earnings quality. This is a useful reminder that paperboard is an operating-discipline business, not just a pricing business.
- Industrial policy and trade: Clearwater is not as tariff-explicit as steel or lumber, but imports, domestic capacity additions, and industry operating rates are core to its earnings math.
- Capital allocation: Clearwater is trying to balance buybacks, liquidity protection, debt discipline, and outage-heavy capex inside an oversupplied market. That is a different form of capital discipline than the one seen in the metals and energy names, but it is still central.
