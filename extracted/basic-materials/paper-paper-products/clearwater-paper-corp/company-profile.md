## What changed

- Sector role: Clearwater adds the paperboard and fiber-packaging layer that the Basic Materials pilot did not yet have. It is the cleanest way to move from metals, energy, chemicals, fertilizers, and wood products into everyday packaging demand.
- Business shape: the current Clearwater story is no longer a mixed tissue and paperboard story. After the tissue divestiture, the `2025` and `2026` materials read as a focused paperboard packaging supplier built around SBS grades, food service, folding carton, and converter relationships.
- Annual base year: `2025` net sales from continuing operations were `$1.6 billion`, adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was `$107 million`, and net loss from continuing operations was `$53 million`, with the loss driven mainly by a non-cash goodwill impairment.
- Operating reset: management repeatedly highlighted more than `$50 million` of fixed-cost reduction in `2025`, including `$16 million` of SG&A savings, plus better-than-expected execution of the Augusta integration and tissue separation.
- Pricing versus volume: the key tension is clear. Volume improved through `2025` and into the `2026` quarter window, but selling prices remained under pressure because the SBS market stayed oversupplied.
- Quarter pattern: `Q4 2025` was a visibly better quarter than the prior year, `Q1 2026` was hit by weather and lower pricing, and `Q2 2026` was the trough quarter in the current window because the Lewiston outage and lower pricing overwhelmed the cost actions.
- Balance sheet and cash discipline: Clearwater still emphasized liquidity, leverage, working-capital improvement, and opportunistic repurchases while navigating the downturn. Liquidity was `$439 million` at year-end `2025`, `$407 million` at `Q1 2026`, and `$445 million` at `Q2 2026`.
- Product and market development: the `2026` materials also show Clearwater trying to widen the paperboard portfolio, including the Velora launch in `Q1 2026`, while the Cypress Bend restructuring suggests the company is willing to reduce capacity and labor to defend operating rates and cash flow.

## Open questions
