## Annual report takeaways

- Pan American is the cleanest precious-metals extension for this batch because the `2025` annual chain shows a miner whose value is not just generic gold exposure. The company is explicitly trying to become a larger silver-weighted cash-flow platform with gold helping fund the buildout.
- AnnualReports confirms the `Basic Materials` / `Silver` taxonomy, but as of `2026-08-10` it still lagged at the `2024 Annual Report`. The authoritative `2025` annual chain therefore comes from the company IR `2025 Annual Report` PDF and the SEC `40-F`.
- The `2025` package shows a business that reached a stronger scale after the MAG Silver acquisition. Full-year `2025` revenue was `$3.6 billion`, attributable revenue was `$3.8 billion`, net earnings were `$980 million`, adjusted earnings were `$959 million`, and operating cash flow totaled `$1.3 billion`.
- At `2025-12-31`, Pan American had `$1.3 billion` in cash and short-term investments and `$2.1 billion` of total available liquidity, which matters because this is a capital-intensive mining business trying to fund growth while also increasing shareholder returns.
- The most important structural change in the annual chain is the acquisition of MAG Silver and the resulting `44%` stake in the Juanicipio mine. Management explicitly frames Juanicipio as a high-quality silver asset that should raise attributable silver production and reduce consolidated silver all-in sustaining costs.
- The annual materials also show management trying to balance three things at once: silver leverage, gold-generated cash flow, and project optionality. The filings repeatedly point to Juanicipio, La Colorada Skarn, and Jacobina optimization as the bridge from current cash generation to future production depth.
- This is useful archive coverage because it gives a different commodity-system read than hydrocarbons. The business depends on mine quality, reserve and development visibility, metals pricing, jurisdictional risk, and multi-year project execution rather than refining margins, basin transport bottlenecks, or service intensity.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent reported quarter

- Results: `Q1 2026` revenue was `$1.154 billion`, net earnings were `$456 million`, and adjusted earnings were `$459 million`.
- Cash flow and balance sheet: cash flow from operations was `$505 million`, attributable cash flow from operations was `$582 million`, attributable free cash flow was `$488 million`, and cash and short-term investments reached a record `$1.8 billion`, including `$199 million` attributable to the company's interest in Juanicipio.
- Operations: attributable silver production was `6.44 million` ounces and attributable gold production was `169.2 thousand` ounces.
- Capital allocation: management approved an updated capital allocation framework targeting up to `$1 billion` in returns in `2026`, with excess attributable free cash flow beyond the dividend directed toward share repurchases through the NCIB.
- Management message: the company tied the quarter to strong mine operating earnings, a record cash balance, and a framework that explicitly links shareholder returns to free cash flow while still funding growth.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` shows the model at full strength. Juanicipio is no longer just an acquisition story; it is feeding low-cost silver exposure, balance-sheet expansion, and the confidence to formalize shareholder returns while still advancing La Colorada.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q4 2025` revenue was `$1.2 billion`, attributable revenue was `$1.3 billion`, net earnings were `$452 million`, and adjusted earnings were `$470 million`.
- Cash flow and capital allocation: cash flow from operations was a record `$554 million`, the dividend was increased by `29%` to `$0.18` per common share for `Q4 2025`, and total shareholder returns in `2025` reached `$221 million` through dividends and share repurchases.
- Operations: attributable silver production was `7.0 million` ounces in `Q4 2025` and `22.8 million` ounces for full-year `2025`; attributable gold production was `197.8 thousand` ounces in `Q4 2025` and `742.2 thousand` ounces for full-year `2025`.
- Balance sheet and guidance: the company ended the year with `$1.3 billion` of cash and short-term investments, excluding `$127 million` of its share of cash at Juanicipio, and guided `2026` attributable silver production to `25` to `27` million ounces.
- Management message: management framed `Q4 2025` as a record quarter and full year, with another step-up in silver production and lower silver-segment costs expected because of Juanicipio.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` is where the silver thesis becomes legible. The company is still a multi-metal miner, but the filings show a deliberate shift toward higher-quality silver exposure supported by gold cash flow and a stronger balance sheet.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q3 2025` attributable revenue was a record `$884.4 million`, while net earnings were `$169.2 million`, or `$0.45` per share.
- Cash flow and capital allocation: attributable cash flow from operations was a record `$323.6 million`, attributable free cash flow was a record `$251.7 million`, and the dividend was increased to `$0.14` per common share.
- Operations: attributable silver production was `5.5 million` ounces and attributable gold production was `183.5 thousand` ounces.
- Balance sheet: cash and short-term investments reached `$910.8 million`, plus an additional `$85.8 million` of cash at Juanicipio attributable to Pan American's `44%` interest.
- Management message: management emphasized that the MAG Silver acquisition closed on `September 4, 2025`, and that even a one-month contribution from Juanicipio was already materially improving the silver-segment profile and free-cash-flow generation.
- Structural themes: `Q3 2025` is the transition quarter. It captures the moment when Pan American stopped being just a diversified precious-metals miner and started becoming a more explicitly silver-centered platform with a better growth and cash-flow narrative.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Pan American adds the precious-metals and mining-development layer that the hydrocarbons-heavy batch did not have.
- Commodity exposure: this is a direct packet for silver and gold price leverage, but the more important nuance is mix quality. Silver is the strategic identity; gold remains a meaningful cash engine that supports growth and returns.
- Capital intensity: the company makes clear that mining cash flow cannot be read in isolation from development spending. La Colorada Skarn, Jacobina optimization, and mine-life extension work all compete with dividends and buybacks for capital.
- Industrial: unlike oil and gas, the production base here is physically embedded in ore bodies, mine sequencing, metallurgy, development ramps, and jurisdictional execution. Resource quality and project timing matter as much as commodity prices.
- Infrastructure dependence: even a precious-metals miner is really an infrastructure business underneath. Cash generation depends on processing plants, underground access, concentrate shipments, and operational continuity across multiple countries.
- Geographic and regulatory risk: the portfolio spans the Americas, which diversifies the asset base but also creates exposure to permitting, local politics, consultation processes, and community or jurisdiction-specific execution risk.
- Scarcity and consolidation: the MAG Silver acquisition is a reminder that the investable primary-silver universe is relatively small. Scale silver exposure increasingly comes through consolidation, joint-venture interests, and portfolio reshaping rather than greenfield abundance.
- Shareholder returns: `Q1 2026` matters because it turns the company from a miner that can occasionally pay more into a miner trying to formalize a free-cash-flow-linked return system.
- Bigger-picture theme: this packet adds an important contrast to energy. Hydrocarbon systems are dominated by reserve replacement, transport, and downstream margin capture, while precious-metals systems are dominated by ore quality, mine life, production ramp quality, and direct commodity-price leverage with much longer development lead times.
