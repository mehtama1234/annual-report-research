## Annual report takeaways

- Sherwin-Williams is a high-value archive addition because it extends the home-improvement and built-environment read upstream from store operators into coatings, contractor workflow, repaint cycles, and controlled distribution.
- AnnualReports.com and Sherwin-Williams investor relations were aligned as of `2026-08-09`, each exposing the `2025` annual package, so this is a relatively clean annual-report capture compared with some lagging AnnualReports entries elsewhere in the archive.
- The fiscal `2025` annual package showed a business still growing in a soft demand backdrop. Net sales increased `2.1%` to `$23.6 billion`, gross profit increased `2.9%` to `$11.5 billion`, gross margin increased to `48.8%`, GAAP diluted EPS declined `2.7%` to `$10.26`, and adjusted diluted EPS increased `0.9%` to `$11.43`.
- The annual materials matter because they show where demand is actually holding up. Sherwin-Williams said it delivered record sales and adjusted diluted net income per share despite weak new and existing home sales and cautious consumer sentiment, with Paint Stores Group sales up `3.2%` to `$13.6 billion` and growth led by Protective & Marine and Residential Repaint.
- This is not just a chemicals packet. The company ended `2025` with `4,853` Paint Stores Group stores, generated `$3.5 billion` of net operating cash, and returned approximately `$2.4 billion` to shareholders, which makes the packet useful as a read on pricing power, branch density, contractor access, and maintenance demand.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` net sales increased `7.5%` to `$6.79 billion`; same-store sales in Paint Stores Group increased `4.2%`; diluted EPS increased to `$3.43`; adjusted diluted EPS increased to `$3.70`; net income increased to `$843.6 million`; EBITDA increased to `$1.43 billion`.
- Operating posture: management said the company outperformed despite ongoing global uncertainty and no meaningful improvement in demand, with growth investments, new account wins, share-of-wallet gains, and targeted pricing offsetting raw-material inflation.
- Structural themes: this quarter is especially useful because it shows the strength of the professional side of the model. Management said all pro segments grew, Protective & Marine rose by a mid-teens percentage, commercial increased at a high-single-digit rate, residential repaint and property maintenance both increased at a mid-single-digit rate, and new residential remained very challenging. The company also announced an `8%` price increase effective `2026-09-01`.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` net sales increased `6.8%` to `$5.67 billion`; same-store sales in Paint Stores Group increased `2.4%`; diluted EPS increased to `$2.15`; adjusted diluted EPS increased to `$2.35`; net income increased to `$534.7 million`; EBITDA increased to `$998.2 million`.
- Operating posture: management said the quarter was characterized by heightened global uncertainty and continued demand softness in most end markets, but growth investments and new-account wins drove sales above guidance across all three reportable segments.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows the business still winning share in a difficult backdrop. Management highlighted double-digit Protective & Marine growth, mid-single-digit commercial growth, a return to mid-single-digit residential repaint growth, and continued weakness in new residential, while the Suvinil acquisition remained a visible but manageable mix factor.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` and full-year `2025` net sales increased `2.1%` in the year to `$23.57 billion`; same-store sales in Paint Stores Group increased `1.7%` in the year; full-year diluted EPS was `$10.26`; full-year adjusted diluted EPS was `$11.43`; Q4 diluted EPS was `$1.92`; Q4 adjusted diluted EPS was `$2.23`.
- Operating posture: management said the fourth quarter was driven by solid core performance amid continued demand choppiness and included the first full quarter of the Suvinil acquisition.
- Structural themes: this quarter closes the year and makes the mix explicit. Sherwin-Williams described high-single-digit Protective & Marine growth, solid residential repaint demand, low-single-digit commercial growth, flattish new residential demand in a down market, and cautious consumer sentiment that still gave little support for a broad-based recovery.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Sherwin-Williams enters the archive through `Basic Materials`, but the lived economics are much closer to a contractor-access and built-environment operating system than to a pure commodity producer.
- Consumer: this is an indirect but important consumer read because repaint, maintenance, affordability pressure, and cautious sentiment all affect demand, even though the stronger near-term mix is coming from professional channels.
- Cultural: coatings and repaint work sit between upkeep and aspiration. Sherwin-Williams therefore reads on maintenance behavior, physical environment quality, housing durability, and the ability of contractors and property owners to keep spending despite weak housing turnover.
- Labor and industrial overlap: the packet sits close to field execution and skilled-trades workflow because Paint Stores Group, commercial accounts, property maintenance, and Protective & Marine all depend on professional customer throughput rather than casual retail traffic.
- Business model: the key business-model signal is controlled distribution. Sherwin-Williams keeps using branch density, targeted pricing, new-account wins, share of wallet, and store-network adjustments to defend margins in a soft macro backdrop.
- Cross-company positioning: Sherwin-Williams is especially useful next to Lowe's and Home Depot because it turns the existing home-improvement track into a more complete chain from materials and coatings upstream to retailer and trade-customer demand downstream.

## Working interpretation

- Sherwin-Williams materially improves the archive because it adds the coatings and contractor-access layer to the housing and maintenance story. That lets the built-environment track stop depending only on retailer filings.
- The important evidence is not just that sales and earnings rose. It is that management keeps describing resilience through pro demand, commercial share gains, residential repaint, controlled distribution, and pricing discipline even while DIY and new residential remain softer.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Sherwin-Williams is a strong archive addition because it bridges commodity-sensitive raw-material inflation, controlled-distribution execution, and downstream maintenance and contractor demand in one evidence set.
