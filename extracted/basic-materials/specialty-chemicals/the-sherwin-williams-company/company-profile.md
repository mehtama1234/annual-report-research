## What changed

- Sherwin-Williams gives the archive a direct coatings and specialty-chemicals operator that still reads on the same housing, maintenance, and contractor activity already visible in Lowe's and Home Depot.
- The annual package showed a resilient but not euphoric business. Net sales increased `2.1%` to `$23.6 billion`, gross margin improved to `48.8%`, GAAP diluted EPS declined to `$10.26`, and adjusted diluted EPS increased to `$11.43`.
- The structural point is distribution and professional demand, not just chemicals volume. Sherwin-Williams ended `2025` with `4,853` Paint Stores Group stores and repeatedly described growth through new accounts, share of wallet, controlled distribution, and pro end markets.
- The quarter set shows why that matters. Q1 and Q2 `2026` both show faster growth than the full-year base, while management kept calling out strong Protective & Marine, commercial, and residential repaint trends against very challenging new-residential demand.
- The packet also sharpens the built-environment lane in a different way than Lowe's and Home Depot. Sherwin-Williams sits upstream and makes pricing actions, raw-material inflation, branch density, and contractor workflow visible rather than just store traffic or basket mix.

## Open questions

- How durable is the current pro and repaint resilience if new residential and DIY stay weak for longer?
- Does Sherwin-Williams remain primarily a controlled-distribution and branch-density story, or does the upstream coatings and raw-material angle become more important if inflation re-accelerates?
- How much of the current outperformance is share gain versus underlying end-market recovery?
