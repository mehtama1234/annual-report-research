## What changed

- Westlake adds a more useful downstream-heavy physical-systems packet than the `Specialty Chemicals` label suggests. This is a PVC, chlor-alkali, polyethylene, building-products, pipe, fittings, and infrastructure company whose earnings sit between chemical spreads, housing affordability, and public-works demand.
- The annual `2025` read was weak on a reported basis. In the full-year `2025` results release, Westlake said net sales were `$11.2 billion`, net loss was `($116 million)`, and loss from operations was `($186 million)`. Management tied the deterioration to lower PEM pricing, lower sales volume, and higher feedstock and energy costs.
- The business split matters:
  - `Housing and Infrastructure Products (HIP)` ties Westlake to construction, pipe, fittings, siding, and downstream building systems
  - `Performance and Essential Materials (PEM)` ties Westlake to chlor-alkali, PVC resin, polyethylene, epoxy, caustic soda, global cost curves, and feedstock advantages
- `Q4 2025` reads like the trough and reset quarter. Large shutdown and restructuring charges, weaker PEM spreads, and slower housing made the platform look broken on a GAAP basis.
- `Q1 2026` still looked transitional, but `Q2 2026` showed what the earnings structure can do when PEM pricing and margins recover and HIP gets help from infrastructure demand and seasonality.
- Balance-sheet management is part of the operating story. By `Q2 2026`, Westlake had reduced debt by `$500 million`, redeemed the remaining `3.60%` notes due `2026`, and replaced its revolver with a new `$1.5 billion` facility maturing in `2031`.

## Open questions

- How much of the `Q2 2026` PEM rebound is structural versus tied to temporary logistics dislocation and Middle East volatility?
- Can HIP keep offsetting residential affordability pressure through pipe, fittings, and infrastructure demand if homebuilding remains soft?
- Does Westlake become a cleaner construction-and-infrastructure chemicals packet from here, or do feedstock, export, and PVC-chain swings keep the earnings path too volatile?
