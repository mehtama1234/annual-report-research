## What changed

- Operating identity: Westlake is no longer just a cyclical basic chemical producer. The current packet needs to treat it as a conversion-materials and building-products company spanning housing, infrastructure, packaging, healthcare, automotive, and consumer goods.
- Structural split: the company’s two-segment design matters. `Housing and Infrastructure Products` gives Westlake a steadier pipe, fittings, compounds, roofing, siding, and building-products layer, while `Performance and Essential Materials` carries the more volatile chlorovinyl, polyethylene, epoxy, chlorine, and caustic exposure.
- 2025 reset year: full-year `2025` was defined by weak pricing, lower volume, higher feedstock and energy costs, asset shutdowns, and a major goodwill impairment tied to the North American chlorovinyls reporting unit.
- 2026 rebound shape: `Q1 2026` still looked weak, but `Q2 2026` showed how quickly margins can recover when PVC and polyethylene pricing move up and the footprint optimization program starts to flow through.
- Infrastructure and housing bridge: the HIP side gives the archive a direct bridge from chemicals into physical construction products and water infrastructure, which is a more interesting economic role than a plain commodity-chemical label suggests.

## Open questions

- How much of the `Q2 2026` PEM rebound depends on oil-price-linked pricing that could reverse quickly?
- Can HIP keep offsetting soft residential new-build activity through repair, remodel, and infrastructure pipe demand?
- Do the PEM plant closures and cost actions create a structurally better earnings base, or mostly reduce the damage from a still-weak chlorovinyl cycle?
