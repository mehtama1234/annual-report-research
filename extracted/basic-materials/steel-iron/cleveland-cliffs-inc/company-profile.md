## What changed

- Cyclical position: `2025` shows the downside of an auto-heavy steel cycle, while `Q1` and especially `Q2 2026` show partial recovery through higher revenues, improved EBITDA, and steadier throughput.
- Margin mechanics: the packet makes clear that Cleveland-Cliffs is not just “long steel.” Fixed-price annual contracts, automotive exposure, and legacy slab commitments delayed the earnings recovery relative to market improvement.
- Asset intensity: this is a vertically integrated steelmaker with mining, DRI, scrap, primary steel, and downstream finishing all inside the system, which raises control but also keeps fixed costs high.
- End-market shape: direct automotive remained roughly `29%` of steelmaking revenue in both `Q1` and `Q2 2026`, reinforcing how closely the company is tied to North American vehicle production and manufacturing activity.
- Operating friction: extreme weather and energy costs were material enough to call out explicitly in `Q1 2026`, which is a useful reminder that hard-asset industrial systems are exposed to more than just price charts.
- Comparative value: Cleveland-Cliffs is a useful counterweight to both the energy names and the precious-metals names because it shows a heavy-input business where throughput, contracts, and manufacturing demand matter more than reserve life or direct spot-price torque.

## Open questions

- How quickly can contract resets and product-mix improvements convert a better steel market into normalized profitability?
- Does North American automotive production recover enough to unlock materially better utilization and pricing for Cleveland-Cliffs?
- How much advantage does vertical integration provide in a weak cycle once energy, labor, and legacy contract friction are included?
