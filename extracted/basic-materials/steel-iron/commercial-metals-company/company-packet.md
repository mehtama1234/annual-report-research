## Annual report takeaways

- Commercial Metals adds a different steel archetype than the names already in the lane. `Cleveland-Cliffs` is the integrated and auto-linked transformation story. `Nucor` is the scale electric-arc-furnace and fabricated-products story. `Reliance` is the distribution and service-center layer. `Commercial Metals` is the rebar, fabrication, recycling, and construction-project steel system.
- AnnualReports remained useful for taxonomy confirmation, but as of `Monday, August 10, 2026` it was still lagging at the `2024` annual package. The authoritative `2025` annual chain therefore had to come from the official investor-relations route and the SEC `10-K`.
- The annual base year itself was not yet a recovery year. Fiscal `2025` revenue was `$7.798 billion`, net earnings were only `$84.7 million`, total assets were `$7.172 billion`, and total debt plus finance-lease obligations were `$1.354 billion`. That weak base matters because the stronger `2026` quarter run is a rebound off a subdued year, not a continuation of already-peak profitability.
- The `10-K` describes CMC as a vertically integrated company with recycling facilities, steel mills, and fabrication operations serving the construction sector. It also says the company manufactures products from approximately `98% recycled material`, which is a major thematic distinction inside steel. This is a scrap-intensive, lower-ore-exposure system tied to recycling flows and fabrication demand rather than a traditional blast-furnace model.
- The annual segment framing is useful: `North America Steel Group`, `Emerging Businesses Group`, and `Europe Steel Group`. By fiscal `Q1 2026`, management had renamed the emerging-businesses segment to `Construction Solutions Group`, which is a better description of what CMC is trying to become after the precast acquisitions.
- The strategic read is that CMC sits at a useful junction in the heavy-input economy. It is exposed to steel margins and scrap spreads, but it is also more directly tied to public infrastructure, construction starts, backlog conversion, and the physical delivery layer behind rebar, fabrication, and precast systems.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q3 2026` net sales were `$2.483 billion`, net earnings were `$173.0 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.55`, adjusted earnings were `$193.0 million`, and core EBITDA was `$353.6 million`.
- Margin and earnings rebound: compared with `Q2 2026`, net sales rose `16.5%`, net earnings rose `86.0%`, and core EBITDA rose `18.9%`. Compared with `Q3 2025`, net earnings were up `108.1%` and core EBITDA was up `78.6%`.
- North America Steel Group: adjusted EBITDA was `$253.5 million`, with adjusted EBITDA margin of `14.2%` versus `11.5%` a year earlier. Management said metal margins improved by `$111` per ton year over year as average selling prices increased by `$130` per ton while scrap increased only `$19` per ton.
- Construction and infrastructure read: shipment volumes were down `1.7%` year over year because of planned downtime, heavy rainfall, and commercial discipline, but the company said project pipeline and backlog were being supported by public infrastructure, data centers, semiconductors, and energy buildouts. That is a much more project-linked demand picture than simple hot-rolled-coil pricing.
- Construction Solutions Group: precast acquisitions contributed `$52.9 million`, reinforcing that a growing portion of the story is no longer just steel conversion. CMC is trying to own more of the embedded construction system.
- Balance sheet and financing: by `2026-05-31`, total assets had reached `$9.796 billion`, cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments were `$559.8 million`, and total debt plus finance-lease obligations were `$3.400 billion`. Management said it had reduced net leverage and had visibility to below `2x` well ahead of the mid-`2027` goal.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q2 2026` net sales were `$2.132 billion`, net earnings were `$93.0 million`, diluted EPS was `$0.83`, adjusted earnings were `$130.1 million`, and core EBITDA was `$297.5 million`.
- North America Steel Group: adjusted EBITDA was `$269.7 million`, up `96.9%` year over year, with adjusted EBITDA margin of `16.8%`. Average steel product selling prices increased `$35` per ton sequentially while scrap costs increased `$33` per ton.
- Construction Solutions Group: net sales jumped to `$314.4 million`, up `97.9%`, and adjusted EBITDA rose to `$53.4 million`, up `127.1%`. Management said the recently acquired precast platform alone generated `$33.6 million` of adjusted EBITDA, or `$40.3 million` excluding a purchase-accounting charge.
- Europe Steel Group: adjusted EBITDA slipped to a loss of `$1.4 million`, with management pointing to European import pressure and the overhang from the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism environment. That makes Europe a weaker and more policy-sensitive leg of the system.
- Balance sheet read: at `2026-02-28`, total assets were `$9.562 billion`, cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments were `$495.0 million`, and total debt plus finance-lease obligations were `$3.363 billion`.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is where the packet starts to look distinct from other steel names. This is not only a sheet or bar-margin rebound. It is also a bet that construction backlog, precast integration, and public-infrastructure demand can offset the normal cyclicality of steel spreads.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q1 2026` net sales were `$2.120 billion`, net earnings were `$177.3 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.58`, adjusted earnings were `$206.2 million`, and core EBITDA was `$316.9 million`.
- North America Steel Group: adjusted EBITDA was `$293.9 million`, up `57.9%`, with adjusted EBITDA margin of `17.7%`. Steel product margins improved by `$53` per ton sequentially as selling prices rose and scrap stayed stable.
- Construction Solutions Group: net sales were `$198.3 million`, up `17.0%`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$39.6 million`, up `74.7%`. Management emphasized the December closings of the CP&P and Foley acquisitions and said it had deployed more than `$2.5 billion` of capital into the precast concrete growth platform.
- Naming change and strategic shift: management used `Q1 2026` to rename `Emerging Businesses Group` as `Construction Solutions Group`. That matters because it signals a deliberate attempt to frame CMC as more than a steel producer. It is trying to become a construction-input and project-delivery system.
- Balance sheet read: at `2025-11-30`, total assets were `$9.243 billion`, cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments were `$1.023 billion`, and total debt plus finance-lease obligations were `$3.352 billion`.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` is the clearest proof that the story is now part steel-margin recovery and part portfolio transformation. The company still lives inside commodity spreads, but it is using acquisitions and backlog-driven construction exposure to make the model less purely dependent on spot steel conditions.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: CMC expands the steel lane into the rebar, fabrication, and construction-linked side of heavy inputs rather than another integrated mill, generic EAF producer, or metals distributor.
- Consumer: the company is upstream from end consumers, but it is still sensitive to affordability through construction activity, public budgets, and commercial project timing.
- Cultural: there is no direct cultural lens here, but the company is part of the physical delivery layer behind the built environment. Data centers, power networks, and infrastructure rhetoric only matter if rebar, fabricated steel, and precast systems actually arrive on schedule.
- Societal: public infrastructure and energy-buildout spending show up directly in backlog support. This makes the story partially dependent on state capacity, permitting velocity, and the execution quality of large civil projects.
- Regulatory: trade policy, import pressure, CBAM-related European effects, and public-infrastructure funding all shape the economics. Steel is not just a supply-demand story; it is also a policy-shaped market.
- Capital allocation: the recent acquisitions made the balance sheet much larger and more levered. The bet is that precast and construction solutions will justify the extra asset intensity and leverage by giving CMC a better mix and more embedded project exposure.
- Industrial: this packet makes clear that heavy-input coverage needs a distinct construction steel category. `Cleveland-Cliffs` is tied more to automotive sheet and integrated steel transformation, `Nucor` to broad EAF and fabricated-products scale, `Reliance` to local processing and distribution density, and `Commercial Metals` to rebar, fabrication, recycling, and construction backlog conversion.
- Comparative read: `Commercial Metals` is one of the better proofs that infrastructure dependence is not just about producers of oil, gas, or copper. It also runs through the lower-profile steel and concrete systems that sit inside bridges, buildings, industrial facilities, and grid expansion.
