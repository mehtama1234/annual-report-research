## What changed

- Earnings and sales: `Q4 2025` was softer, with net earnings attributable to Nucor stockholders of `$378 million` on net sales of `$7.69 billion`. The business then improved sharply in `Q1 2026` to `$743 million` of earnings on `$9.50 billion` of sales, and again in `Q2 2026` to `$1.16 billion` of earnings on `$10.40 billion` of sales.
- Segment mix: Nucor remains best read through its three operating segments: steel mills, steel products, and raw materials. In `Q1 2026`, segment earnings before taxes and noncontrolling interests were `$1.128 billion` in steel mills, `$285 million` in steel products, and `$45 million` in raw materials. In `Q2 2026`, those rose to `$1.556 billion`, `$353 million`, and `$146 million`, respectively.
- Steel-cycle dynamics: the key quarter-to-quarter driver was not just volume but price plus mix. `Q4 2025` suffered from lower volumes and margin compression, especially in sheet. `Q1 2026` improved on higher volumes and selling prices. `Q2 2026` improved further on higher realized selling prices, higher volumes, and stronger raw-material pricing.
- Cash and balance sheet: Nucor ended `Q4 2025` with `$2.70 billion` of cash and short-term investments, ended `Q1 2026` with `$2.48 billion`, and ended `Q2 2026` with `$2.69 billion`, while keeping its `$2.25 billion` revolving credit facility undrawn.
- Capital allocation: Nucor continued meaningful shareholder returns while keeping heavy investment behind growth. It returned about `$250 million` to stockholders in `Q1 2026`, about `$479 million` in `Q2 2026`, and roughly `$1.2 billion` during full-year `2025`.
- Capital spending: cash used for capital expenditures increased to `$3.42 billion` in `2025` from `$3.17 billion` in `2024`, and management estimated approximately `$2.50 billion` of capital expenditures for `2026`.
- Operating footprint: Nucor remains a scale domestic steel system, not a single-product steel name. In `2025`, it recycled approximately `20 million` gross tons of scrap steel, sold about `19.848 million` tons to outside customers from the steel mills segment, and ran a business mix where sheet contracts accounted for about `85%` of sheet steel sales.
- Strategic positioning: the company is leaning into a value-added, less-volatile product mix through new projects and expansions, including the West Virginia sheet mill, galvanizing capacity, towers and structures, and other fabricated-steel capabilities tied to data centers, energy, and industrial buildout.
- Management tone: Nucor is framing itself as a domestic industrial winner from supportive U.S. trade policy, strong backlogs, capital investment, and a North America-centered manufacturing base.

## Open questions

- How much of `Q2 2026` strength reflects sustainable through-cycle earnings improvement versus a strong mid-cycle steel-price and policy tailwind?
- Can Nucor’s recent capex wave actually reduce volatility over time by shifting mix toward more value-added and contract-oriented products?
