## Annual report takeaways

- Reliance adds a missing layer to the current Basic Materials starter set: downstream metals processing, service-center logistics, and quick-turn distribution. That matters because the sector is not only about mining, smelting, or steelmaking. It also includes the infrastructure that moves, cuts, processes, and supplies metal into end markets.
- AnnualReports now has a saved local company-page artifact for this branch, and it confirms the `Basic Materials` / `Steel & Iron` taxonomy while still lagging at the `2024 Annual Report and Form 10-K` as of `2026-08-10`. The authoritative `2025` chain remains the official annual report, the IR quarter releases, and the SEC filing set.
- The annual materials describe the largest metals service center company in North America, operating through about `310` locations in `41` U.S. states and `10` countries outside the U.S. The scale is operationally important because Reliance wins not just on commodity exposure, but on availability, mill relationships, processing capacity, and speed.
- The `2025` operating model is unusually clear. Average order size was only `$3,120`, about `49%` of orders included value-added processing, and about `40%` of orders were delivered within `24` hours. This is not a pure “ship tons of steel” story. It is a fragmented, high-service industrial supply-chain model.
- The annual base year was solid. `2025` net sales were `$14.3 billion`, up `3.3%`, while tons sold reached a record `6.4 million`, up `6.2%`. Reliance also said it held about `17%` of the U.S. metals service-center market in `2025`, up from about `15%` in `2024`.
- Capital allocation and reinvestment matter here too. Reliance returned nearly `$850 million` to stockholders in `2025`, including `$594.1 million` of repurchases, while still spending `$328.9 million` on capital expenditures. This is another Basic Materials name returning cash while still investing behind operating capability.
- The strategic read is that Reliance sits downstream from mills but upstream from a wide set of industrial end markets. That makes it a useful bridge between metals pricing and actual physical activity across infrastructure, energy, aerospace, defense, manufacturing, and semiconductors.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net sales were `$4.63 billion`, pretax income was `$429.8 million`, diluted EPS was `$6.29`, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$6.27`.
- Volume and pricing: tons sold rose `7.0%` sequentially and `10.8%` year over year, while average selling price per ton sold increased `7.8%` sequentially. Management said Reliance outperformed the industry-wide shipment increase reported by MSCI by more than `5` percentage points.
- Margin and cash: gross profit margin was `28.1%`, while non-GAAP FIFO gross profit margin improved to `30.5%` from `30.1%` in `Q1 2026`. Cash flow from operations was `$162.2 million`, cash was `$235.4 million`, and total debt was `$1.7 billion`.
- Management message: the company emphasized constructive conditions, extended mill lead times, strong pricing, record quarterly tons sold, and stronger activity across infrastructure, semiconductor, manufacturing, and aerospace.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is the clearest proof point that Reliance is not just a passive volume reseller. Pricing discipline, share gains, and project execution mattered, especially with the DHS border wall contract contributing about `$0.41` of EPS.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net sales were `$4.0 billion`, pretax income was `$349.5 million`, diluted EPS was `$5.10`, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$5.16`.
- Volume and pricing: tons sold hit a quarterly record and were up `9.4%` sequentially and `2.7%` year over year. Reliance said it outperformed MSCI’s shipment trend of `-5.1%` by nearly `8` percentage points. Average selling price per ton sold increased `5.3%` sequentially.
- Cash and capital return: cash flow from operations was `$151.4 million`, free cash flow was `$87.2 million`, cash was `$249.7 million`, and total debt was `$1.7 billion`. Reliance returned about `$301 million` to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases.
- Management message: management highlighted continued healthy demand in infrastructure, data centers, energy, defense, aerospace, and improving semiconductor activity, while also calling out the new DHS border wall contract.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` shows the service-center model leveraging into a better pricing environment. It also shows how government project awards and trade-policy conditions can flow directly into a downstream metals distributor, not only into producers.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net sales were `$3.5 billion`, pretax income was `$154.8 million`, non-GAAP pretax income was `$166.9 million`, and non-GAAP pretax income FIFO was `$205.6 million`.
- Full-year frame: full-year `2025` net sales were `$14.3 billion`, tons sold were a record `6.4 million`, cash flow from operations was `$831.4 million`, capex was `$328.9 million`, and buybacks were `$594.1 million`.
- End-market read: management described healthy nonresidential construction, infrastructure, energy infrastructure, broader manufacturing, and aerospace demand, with semiconductor demand still under pressure entering `Q1 2026`.
- Balance sheet and returns: year-end cash was `$216.6 million`, total debt was `$1.43 billion`, and revolver borrowings were `$277.0 million`. The company raised the quarterly dividend `4.2%` to `$1.25` and repurchased `$200.1 million` of stock in the quarter.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` is the setup quarter. It shows Reliance entering `2026` with record tons, improving share, healthy industrial end markets, and a service-intensive operating model that can capitalize quickly once price and activity improve.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Reliance is strong evidence that Basic Materials includes downstream industrial distribution and processing infrastructure, not only upstream extraction and production.
- Consumer: direct consumer exposure is limited. The better lens is nonresidential construction, infrastructure, industrial machinery, aerospace, defense, and semiconductor-capex demand flowing through metal consumption and quick-turn supply.
- Cultural: this is another reminder that the physical economy depends on dense operating networks, not just on factories or mines. Reliance’s model is about availability, logistics, cut-to-size processing, and rapid fulfillment.
- Regulatory: tariffs and trade policy matter directly here because they shape price formation, mill lead times, material availability, and project economics. Government contracts also matter, as shown by the border wall contribution.
- Labor: this is an execution-heavy operating system spread across hundreds of locations. Processing throughput, local service quality, and the ability to support both small daily orders and large projects matter even when labor is not the headline.
- Technology: Reliance is not a direct software or semiconductor name, but it is tied to data-center and semiconductor buildout through metals demand. The quarterlies repeatedly connect semiconductor improvement to rising data-center activity.
- Capital allocation: Reliance combines buybacks and dividends with steady capex and working-capital deployment. This is a good example of a capital-return story that still depends heavily on operational density and inventory execution.
