## What changed

- Business role: Reliance broadens the Basic Materials archive beyond upstream production into downstream metals processing and distribution. This is the largest metals service center company in North America, with approximately `310` locations in `41` states and `10` countries outside the U.S., distributing more than `100,000` metal products to over `125,000` customers.
- Operating model: this is a small-order, quick-turn system rather than a pure mill story. In `2025`, the average order size was `$3,120`, about `49%` of orders included value-added processing, and about `40%` of orders were delivered within `24` hours. That makes scale, logistics, and processing density central to the moat.
- Annual base year: `2025` net sales were `$14.3 billion`, up `3.3%`, and tons sold reached a record `6.4 million`, up `6.2%`. Reliance returned nearly `$850 million` to stockholders in `2025`, including `$594.1 million` of buybacks, while still investing `$328.9 million` in capital expenditures.
- Market-share read: the `2025` filing and quarterlies point to a company gaining share inside a fragmented service-center market. Reliance said its domestic market share reached about `17%` in `2025`, up from about `15%` in `2024`.
- Quarter pattern: `Q4 2025` was the handoff quarter, with healthy nonresidential construction, energy infrastructure, data-center, and aerospace demand, but semiconductor demand still soft. `Q1 2026` and `Q2 2026` then accelerated on stronger pricing, higher tons sold, and continued outperformance versus MSCI shipment trends.
- Policy and end-market mix: the evidence shows a business tied directly to infrastructure, aerospace, defense, semiconductor, energy, and manufacturing activity. By `Q2 2026`, management was explicitly pointing to improving semiconductor demand supported by data-center activity and to meaningful volume and earnings contribution from the DHS border wall project.
- Balance sheet and capital return: Reliance remained levered but flexible across the window. Cash was `$216.6 million` at year-end `2025`, `$249.7 million` at `Q1 2026`, and `$235.4 million` at `Q2 2026`, while total debt ran from `$1.43 billion` to `$1.7 billion`. The company still kept returning capital through dividends and opportunistic buybacks.

## Open questions

- How durable is the recent earnings acceleration once border-wall project mix and tariff-driven price support normalize?
- Does Reliance’s heavy value-added processing and logistics density make it structurally less cyclical than upstream steel producers, or does it still mostly amplify the same cycle with a different timing profile?
