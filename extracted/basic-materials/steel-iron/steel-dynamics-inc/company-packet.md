## Annual report takeaways

- Steel Dynamics adds a broader domestic metals-platform archetype than the other steel names already in the lane. `Cleveland-Cliffs` is the integrated and automotive-linked transformation story. `Nucor` is the large-scale EAF and fabricated-products platform. `Reliance` is the service-center and local processing layer. `Commercial Metals` is the rebar, fabrication, recycling, and construction-project system. `Steel Dynamics` sits between them as a large EAF steelmaker with real recycling depth, meaningful downstream fabrication, and an increasingly material aluminum expansion.
- AnnualReports remained useful for taxonomy and archive confirmation, and unlike several other packets it already displayed the `2025 Annual Report and Form 10K` package as of `Monday, August 10, 2026`. But the saved view kept the report locked, and the official IR annual-reports route was mostly a B2i shell. The authoritative annual evidence therefore still had to come from the SEC `10-K`.
- The annual base year was solid but not yet at the level implied by the stronger `2026` quarter run. Fiscal `2025` net sales were `$18.2 billion`, net income was `$1.186 billion`, operating cash flow was `$1.450 billion`, total assets were `$16.420 billion`, and total debt was about `$4.211 billion`.
- The `10-K` and annual release describe Steel Dynamics as a leading industrial metals solutions company using a circular manufacturing model with recycled scrap as the primary input. That matters because this is not just a generic hot-rolled-coil price story. It is a scrap-based, conversion-heavy, domestic industrial system that sits inside manufacturing, construction, and infrastructure.
- Segment mix shows why the packet matters. At `2025-12-31`, segment assets were `$9.234 billion` in steel operations, `$1.463 billion` in metals recycling, `$667.0 million` in steel fabrication, and `$3.906 billion` in aluminum operations. The aluminum buildout is already too large to ignore and makes this packet more than another steel-cycle read.
- The strategic read is that Steel Dynamics sits directly inside domestic reindustrialization. Management repeatedly tied demand to energy, non-residential construction, manufacturing, automotive, warehouses, healthcare, onshoring, infrastructure investment, and data centers. That makes the company a useful proof that the heavy-input economy is being pulled not only by commodity demand but also by the physical buildout behind power, AI, logistics, and industrial relocation.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net sales were `$6.1 billion`, net income was `$534 million`, diluted EPS was `$3.69`, first-half net income was `$938 million`, and first-half net sales were `$11.3 billion`.
- Steel operations strength: operating income was `$721 million`, up `30%` sequentially, supported by record shipments and wider metal spreads. Average external steel selling price rose to `$1,298` per ton, up `$105` sequentially, while average ferrous scrap cost rose only `$7` to `$404` per ton.
- Downstream demand read: steel fabrication operating income was `$85 million`, roughly in line with `Q1`, while backlog was nearly `45%` higher than a year earlier and extended into `Q1 2027`. Management highlighted commercial construction, data centers, manufacturing, warehousing, and healthcare as the main demand drivers.
- Recycling and portfolio breadth: first-half metals recycling operating income improved to `$95 million` from `$47 million` a year earlier. That matters because Steel Dynamics is not dependent on one earnings engine; recycling and fabrication help support the broader platform when conditions are favorable.
- Capital and project nuance: `Q2 2026` EPS included a non-cash impairment tied to relocating the second satellite aluminum recycled slab center from Arizona to Columbus, Mississippi. The growth plan is still active, but execution is not frictionless.
- Balance sheet and cash flow: by `2026-06-30`, total assets were `$17.113 billion`, cash plus restricted cash was `$573.0 million`, total debt was about `$4.182 billion`, and first-half operating cash flow was `$576.3 million`.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net sales were `$5.2 billion`, net income was `$403 million`, diluted EPS was `$2.78`, and operating cash flow was `$148.3 million`.
- Spread expansion: steel operations operating income rose to `$557 million`, up `73%` sequentially, as average external steel selling price climbed to `$1,193` per ton, up `$86`, while average ferrous scrap cost rose `$22` to `$396` per ton.
- End-market mix: management pointed to demand from energy, non-residential construction, automotive, and industrial markets. That gives Steel Dynamics a broader industrial exposure than more narrowly construction-linked or auto-linked steel names.
- Fabrication visibility: steel fabrication operating income was `$90 million`, and backlog was more than `38%` above the prior year, extending through `Q3` and into `October 2026`. This supports the idea that a meaningful portion of earnings is tied to booked project work rather than only spot steel conditions.
- Strategic additions: management noted that results now include New Process Steel beginning `December 1, 2025`, reinforcing the company's push deeper into downstream processing and metals handling rather than staying only in mill output.
- Balance-sheet read: at `2026-03-31`, total assets were `$16.720 billion`, cash plus restricted cash was `$561.8 million`, and total debt was about `$4.201 billion`.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net sales were `$4.4 billion`, net income was `$266 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.82`, and adjusted EBITDA for full-year `2025` was `$2.2 billion`.
- Annual operating context: full-year `2025` net sales were `$18.2 billion`, operating income was `$1.5 billion`, net income was `$1.2 billion`, diluted EPS was `$7.99`, operating cash flow was `$1.4 billion`, and liquidity was `$2.2 billion` at year-end.
- Shipment and cost structure: the company reported record full-year steel shipments of `13.7 million tons`. In `Q4 2025`, steel operations operating income was `$322 million`, average external steel selling price was `$1,107` per ton, down `$12` sequentially, and average ferrous scrap cost was `$374` per ton, down `$7`.
- Multi-leg platform: `Q4 2025` metals recycling operating income was `$19 million`, and steel fabrication operating income was `$91 million`. Even before the `2026` earnings acceleration, the packet already showed multiple operating legs beyond core melt-shop economics.
- Bigger-picture read: management highlighted backlog through the first half of `2026`, driven by commercial, data center, manufacturing, warehouse, and healthcare projects, while also emphasizing onshoring, domestic infrastructure support, biocarbon development, and lower-carbon steel and aluminum positioning.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` is the bridge quarter that makes the later 2026 strength interpretable. It shows a company entering the year with healthy liquidity, heavy capex, a circular scrap-based model, and visible downstream demand rather than simply being rescued by a late price spike.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Steel Dynamics deepens the steel lane into a broader industrial-metals platform that spans EAF steel, scrap recycling, fabrication, and aluminum flat-rolled expansion rather than just one conversion node.
- Consumer: the company is several steps upstream from end consumers, but it is still indirectly sensitive to affordability through vehicle demand, warehouse and commercial construction timing, and the financing environment for large built-environment projects.
- Cultural: there is no direct cultural lens, but there is a real societal buildout lens. Data centers, reshoring, and energy security narratives only matter if domestic steel, scrap, fabrication, and eventually aluminum systems can actually supply the physical project stack.
- Societal: this packet ties the archive more directly to reindustrialization and infrastructure dependence. The filings repeatedly connect earnings to onshoring, domestic manufacturing expansion, and project categories like data centers and healthcare facilities.
- Regulatory: steel economics are shaped by trade actions, domestic industrial policy, and the regulatory durability of large capital projects. This is still a commodity business, but not a policy-neutral one.
- Capital allocation: the company is funding large organic growth projects while holding debt broadly steady. The aluminum buildout, recycling investments, and downstream expansion make this a capital-intensive compounder bet rather than a cash-out steel harvest story.
- Industrial: Steel Dynamics is a useful midpoint inside steel. `Cleveland-Cliffs` shows the fixed-cost integrated and auto-sheet model, `Nucor` the broad EAF and fabricated-products system, `Commercial Metals` the construction-rebar and precast system, `Reliance` the distribution layer, and `Steel Dynamics` the domestic EAF platform with stronger internal recycling, fabrication, and aluminum diversification.
- Comparative read: one of the clearest cross-company lessons is that the heavy-input economy cannot be mapped by commodity labels alone. Steel Dynamics shows how scrap sourcing, downstream backlog, aluminum adjacency, and domestic manufacturing exposure can matter as much as the headline steel price.
