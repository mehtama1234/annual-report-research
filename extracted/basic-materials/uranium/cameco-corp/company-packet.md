## Annual report takeaways

- Cameco adds a distinctly different heavy-input system than the metals and hydrocarbons packets already in the lane. It is not a generic miner and not a pure uranium-price proxy. It is a nuclear-fuel platform combining uranium mining, procurement, inventory management, fuel-services conversion, long-term contracting, and a strategic stake in Westinghouse.
- The attempted AnnualReports capture did not return usable Cameco-specific content in this pass, so it was not strong enough for substantive archive confirmation. The authoritative annual chain therefore comes from Cameco’s own `2025` annual page and annual report PDF plus the SEC `40-F`.
- The annual base year was strong. `2025` annual revenue was `$3.482 billion`, gross profit was `$970 million`, uranium revenue was `$2.874 billion`, and full-year adjusted EBITDA increased by approximately `$398 million` to about `$1.9 billion`.
- Balance-sheet quality is a major part of the story. As of `2025-12-31`, Cameco had `$1.2 billion` in cash and cash equivalents plus short-term investments, with `$1.0 billion` in total debt. It repaid the remaining `US$200 million` on its U.S. term loan during the year.
- The operating read is more complex than a spot-commodity narrative. The annual page says `2025` uranium production was `21.0 million` pounds on Cameco’s share basis, fuel-services production was `14.0 million kgU`, uranium deliveries were `33.0 million` pounds, and uranium inventory ended the year at `9.7 million` pounds with an average inventory cost of `$61.85` per pound.
- Contracting quality matters heavily. After meeting `2025` deliveries, Cameco said it had long-term commitments to deliver about `230 million` pounds of uranium, with average annual deliveries of about `28 million` pounds over the next five years. This is a contract book and inventory orchestration business as much as a mining business.
- Westinghouse makes the packet more structurally important. The annual and quarter materials repeatedly tie earnings and strategic optionality to Westinghouse distributions, reactor-construction participation, and broader nuclear-power deployment rather than only to mined uranium.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` included net earnings of `$25 million`, adjusted net earnings of `$77 million`, and adjusted EBITDA of `$391 million`. First-half `2026` included net earnings of `$156 million`, adjusted net earnings of `$281 million`, and adjusted EBITDA of `$899 million`.
- Main variance driver: management said quarterly and first-half results were lower than `2025` primarily because equity earnings from Westinghouse were lower. This is the clearest proof that Cameco’s earnings are not a simple uranium line; corporate-level results can swing materially with reactor-services economics and project timing.
- Balance sheet: as of `2026-06-30`, Cameco had `$1.1 billion` in cash and cash equivalents, `$1.0 billion` in total debt, and a `$1.0 billion` undrawn revolving credit facility. It also received `US$124 million`, net of withholdings, from JV Inkai in the second quarter.
- Uranium segment: second-quarter uranium earnings before taxes were `$170 million` and adjusted EBITDA was `$252 million`, down from `$281 million` and `$352 million` in `Q2 2025`, mainly due to normal delivery timing and lower planned `2026` sales volumes from contracting discipline.
- Operational and marketing detail: total second-quarter production on Cameco’s share basis was `3.9 million` pounds, deliveries were `7.1 million` pounds, purchases were `2.8 million` pounds at an average unit cost of `$91.40` per pound, and uranium inventory ended `Q2` at `8.7 million` pounds with an average cost of `$58.05` per pound.
- Strategic theme: this is a useful quarter for showing how uranium businesses can look weak on headline earnings even while the long-term nuclear setup remains intact. Cameco is optimizing contracting and delivery timing rather than maximizing short-term spot exposure.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` results were stronger than the prior year, with net earnings of `$131 million`, adjusted net earnings of `$203 million`, and adjusted EBITDA of `$509 million`.
- Balance sheet: as of `2026-03-31`, Cameco had `$1.1 billion` in cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments, `$1.0 billion` in total debt, and a `$1.0 billion` undrawn revolving credit facility.
- Uranium segment: first-quarter uranium earnings before taxes were `$358 million` and adjusted EBITDA was `$423 million`, compared with `$227 million` and `$286 million` in `Q1 2025`. Management said results benefited from higher sales volumes and continued improvement in average realized price under market-related contracts.
- Fuel Services: first-quarter fuel-services earnings before taxes were `$44 million` and adjusted EBITDA was `$54 million`, compared with `$68 million` and `$75 million` in the prior year quarter. This is a reminder that the conversion business has its own pricing cycle rather than moving in lockstep with uranium.
- Marketing and inventory: Cameco produced `6.2 million` pounds on its share basis, purchased `0.2 million` pounds and borrowed `750,000` pounds under product-loan facilities. After delivering `7.8 million` pounds in the quarter, uranium inventory was `9.1 million` pounds at an average cost of `$50.24` per pound.
- Structural theme: `Q1 2026` is the quarter that best shows Cameco’s advantage as a fuel-cycle manager. Production, procurement, inventories, and contract timing all interact with improving long-term pricing rather than just flowing through as mine output.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` and full-year `2025` results were stronger than `2024`. The annual page says net earnings for the quarter and year increased by `$64 million` and `$418 million`, respectively, versus `2024`, while adjusted net earnings increased by `$60 million` and `$335 million`.
- Annual profitability: full-year adjusted EBITDA increased by about `$398 million` to `$1.9 billion`, driven mainly by the uranium segment’s improving pricing environment and higher annual revenue from Westinghouse tied to the Dukovany project.
- Balance-sheet and capital returns: the company ended the year with `$1.2 billion` in cash and short-term investments, `$1.0 billion` in total debt, and an advanced dividend-growth plan that raised the annual dividend to `$0.24` per share in `2025`.
- Uranium operations: Cameco produced `21.0 million` pounds on its share basis in `2025`, exceeding revised consolidated production guidance. It delivered `33.0 million` pounds of uranium and ended the year with `9.7 million` pounds of inventory.
- Fuel-cycle breadth: Fuel Services produced `14.0 million kgU`, including a production record of `11.2 million kgU` of `UF6` at Port Hope. That makes Cameco a better energy-system packet than a pure extraction story because conversion capacity and processing throughput matter directly.
- Structural theme: `Q4 2025` is the bridge that makes the `2026` quarters interpretable. It shows a company benefiting from improving uranium prices, strong contract coverage, a resilient balance sheet, and a rising strategic premium from Westinghouse and broader nuclear demand.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Cameco expands the heavy-input lane into uranium and the nuclear fuel cycle rather than another metal, steel, or hydrocarbon exposure.
- Consumer: the company is far upstream from consumers, but it is tied to energy affordability and grid reliability because nuclear procurement decisions sit inside electricity planning, decarbonization pressure, and industrial power demand.
- Cultural: there is no direct cultural lens, but there is a societal and policy lens. The packet shows that energy security increasingly includes nuclear power, fuel conversion, reactor technology, and secure long-term contracting.
- Societal: Cameco is one of the clearest proofs that the physical economy depends on contract-backed fuel systems that can last for decades. This is a slower, more state-linked, and more security-sensitive demand pattern than most commodity businesses.
- Regulatory: uranium and nuclear fuel are shaped by geopolitics, sanctions, export controls, reactor approvals, and security-of-supply policy more than most other basic-materials names.
- Capital allocation: Cameco’s strong balance sheet and lower leverage allow it to keep playing a long-cycle contracting game rather than forcing short-term production or spot selling.
- Industrial: the archive now has a new distinction inside extraction and energy-linked materials. Hydrocarbons, copper, precious metals, steel, and uranium are all driven by different bottlenecks. Uranium depends much more on contracts, conversion, inventories, reactor demand, and geopolitical fuel security.
- Comparative read: Cameco is one of the strongest packets for showing that “energy transition” is not just copper and grids. It also requires durable nuclear fuel supply, conversion infrastructure, and reactor-service capacity through Westinghouse.
