## What changed

- Nuclear-fuel system: Cameco is not just a uranium miner. The `2025` annual and quarter materials show a broader nuclear-fuel platform spanning uranium production and marketing, fuel-services conversion, inventory management, long-term contracting, and a large equity stake in Westinghouse.
- Annual base year: `2025` annual revenue was `$3.482 billion`, gross profit was `$970 million`, uranium revenue was `$2.874 billion`, and adjusted EBITDA rose to about `$1.9 billion`.
- Balance-sheet quality: as of `2025-12-31`, Cameco had `$1.2 billion` in cash and cash equivalents plus short-term investments, with `$1.0 billion` in total debt. The company also extinguished its remaining U.S. term loan during the year.
- Uranium economics: annual average realized uranium price rose to `$87.00` per pound, compared with `$79.70` in `2024`, and the annual page says improved pricing under base-escalated and market-related contracts continued to support results.
- Operating structure: uranium remains the core segment, but Fuel Services and Westinghouse are material differentiators. Fuel Services adds conversion and fabrication exposure, while Westinghouse adds reactor, services, and nuclear-platform optionality rather than pure commodity beta.
- Fiscal `2026` shape: quarterly results in `2026` became more uneven because the company is driven by delivery timing, contracting discipline, inventory use, and equity earnings from Westinghouse, not just spot uranium prices or production tonnage.
- Strategic role: Cameco is one of the clearest packets in the archive for showing how energy security can depend on fuel-cycle infrastructure, conversion capacity, reactor-services franchises, and long-term contracting behavior rather than only on raw resource ownership.
