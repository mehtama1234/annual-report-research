## What changed

- Direct measurement anchor: Comscore gives the frontier a true audience-measurement and ad-currency operator rather than another adjacent agency, publisher, or broadcaster.
- Fragmented-media economics: the packet shows how measurement vendors are being pulled between legacy syndicated products in decline and newer cross-platform currency products growing from a smaller base.
- Trust as product: management repeatedly frames Comscore as a trusted third-party source, which makes the packet useful for studying how information trust itself becomes monetizable infrastructure in a fractured media environment.
- Local TV as transitional stronghold: the quarter chain shows local TV still functioning as a durable transaction currency even while national TV and syndicated digital products weaken.
- Capital-structure stress: the business is not only a media-fragmentation story; it is also a leverage and recapitalization story, with preferred-dividend burden, term-loan management, and strategic-actions language shaping operating flexibility.
- Retail media and data volatility: the Q3 `2025` disruption tied to one large advertiser's data-strategy shift shows how fragile newer ad-tech and cross-platform revenue can be even when structural demand is favorable.

## Open questions

- Can Comscore turn cross-platform growth into a true replacement engine for declining national TV and syndicated digital products?
- Does third-party measurement gain durable power as trust erodes elsewhere, or does the market keep fragmenting faster than any one measurement framework can stabilize?
- Is Local TV a durable modern-currency franchise or mainly a bridge while the broader audience-measurement market is being rebuilt?
- How much strategic freedom does Comscore really have while capital-structure repair remains part of the operating story?
