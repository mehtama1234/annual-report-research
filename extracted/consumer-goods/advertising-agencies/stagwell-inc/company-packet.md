## Annual report takeaways

- Stagwell is the frontier's strongest current challenger-agency packet because it turns `Advertising Agencies` into a direct fight over whether a digital-first, AI-forward operator can gain share from larger legacy holding companies.
- AnnualReports classifies Stagwell as `Consumer Goods / Advertising Agencies`, which makes it a clean exact-taxonomy addition even though its operating language sounds more like software-and-services infrastructure than a classic ad agency.
- The `2025` annual package matters because management uses it to declare an inflection year built around AI applications, record net new business, accelerating net revenue ex-advocacy, and more than doubled free cash flow.
- The most important structural read is that Stagwell is not pitching itself as a pure creative house. It is pitching a combined system of agencies, self-service marketer tools, data, ID infrastructure, and agentic workflow software such as The Machine and The Marketing Cloud.
- That matters for the archive because it shows the attention-and-influence lane evolving in two directions at once: giant incumbents like `Omnicom` are consolidating scale, while challengers like Stagwell are trying to win by looking more adaptive, AI-native, and less burdened by legacy structure.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$786M`; net revenue was `$632M`; organic revenue growth was `10%`; organic net-revenue growth was `5%`; adjusted EBITDA was `$109M`; adjusted EBITDA margin was `17%`; adjusted EPS was `$0.25`.
- Segment and demand signals: digital-transformation net revenue reached `$107M` with `18%` organic growth, communications posted `12%` organic net-revenue growth as the political cycle started to ramp, and quarterly net new business hit a record `$171M`.
- Management tone: the company explicitly said its software, services, and engineers were being embraced in the AI era and raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to `$1.03-$1.17`.
- Interpretation: Q2 is the clearest proof that Stagwell is not just selling anti-incumbent rhetoric. It is showing that AI-led workflow compression, communications demand, and challenger positioning can translate into faster growth and better incremental economics.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$704M`; net revenue was `$585M`; digital-transformation net revenue was `$97M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$90M`; adjusted EPS was `$0.17`; net new business was `$141M`.
- Management tone: Stagwell highlighted record new wins, first government contracts, and a pivot toward agentic applications for the marketing industry.
- Interpretation: Q1 shows the company's thesis more directly than Q4. The pitch is that agencies can still matter if they behave like adaptive operating partners instead of only as labor-heavy holding-company portfolios.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$807M`; net revenue was `$651M`; Q4 adjusted EBITDA was `$129M`; FY `2025` revenue was `$2.909B`; FY net revenue was `$2.428B`; FY adjusted EBITDA was `$422M`; FY adjusted EPS was `$0.83`; free cash flow was `$187M`.
- Operating and strategic signals: FY net revenue excluding advocacy grew `9%`; digital-transformation net revenue grew `13%`; marketing-services net revenue grew `6%`; the company said The Marketing Cloud delivered `230%` year-over-year net-revenue growth and announced a `$350M` increase to its repurchase authorization.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY2025 is the annual anchor because it shows the company using AI and software language not as side branding but as the core explanation for why it expects to outgrow larger peers in `2026`.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Attention monetization: Stagwell monetizes attention indirectly, but unlike `Omnicom` it tries to do so with a more software-inflected, AI-mediated, and self-service-friendly model.
- Media fragmentation: this packet is useful because management is not asking for a return to simpler media markets. It is arguing that fragmentation creates demand for faster, more modular operating partners that can connect creative, data, commerce, advocacy, and AI tooling.
- Advertising cyclicality: the advocacy adjustment is crucial. It shows how the company tries to separate underlying client demand from political and public-affairs volatility, which makes it a strong packet for understanding event-driven distortions inside agency economics.
- Trust and information distribution: Stagwell matters less as a trust source than as an orchestrator of message delivery across fragmented surfaces. The company is trying to make brands legible inside a noisier, AI-influenced discovery environment.
- Challenger versus incumbent structure: this may be the packet's most important higher-level use. Stagwell openly says larger competitors are distracted by restructurings and mergers, while it is trying to stay focused on AI adoption and new-business capture. That creates a live archive contrast against `Omnicom`'s integration-heavy story.
- Technology pressure: The Machine, Palantir, ID-graph tooling, and The Marketing Cloud show that agency competition increasingly includes workflow software and automation, not only creative talent and media-buying relationships.

## Working interpretation

- Stagwell materially improves the attention lane because it gives the archive a second real agency model instead of another measurement or ad-tech adjacent operator.
- The recurring pattern is that the agency layer itself is bifurcating. `Omnicom` shows consolidation, scale, and integration. Stagwell shows a challenger trying to weaponize speed, AI posture, self-service tooling, and anti-bureaucratic positioning.
- The cultural point is that attention is no longer only bought through bigger media budgets. It is increasingly routed through software-like systems that help brands adapt to AI-driven discovery, fragmented surfaces, and event-sensitive communications cycles.
- Stagwell also sharpens a broader frontier theme: companies can monetize complexity without owning the audience directly, but the way they do it is changing from classic agency coordination toward a hybrid of software, data, and services.
