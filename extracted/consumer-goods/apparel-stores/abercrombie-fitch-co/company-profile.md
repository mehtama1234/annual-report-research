## What changed

- Revenue: full-year `2025` net sales reached about `$5.27B`, up `6%`; Q4 `2025` and Q3 `2025` were stronger growth quarters than Q1 `2026`, but the most recent quarter still extended the growth streak.
- Margin: operating margin was `13.3%` for full-year `2025`, `12.0%` in Q3 `2025`, and `8.0%` in Q1 `2026`, showing a still-strong but moderating near-term earnings profile.
- Guidance and tone: management consistently framed results as above or ahead of outlook, while becoming more explicit in Q1 `2026` about regional softness and geopolitical pressure.
- Capital allocation: the company remained active on buybacks, including about `$100M` in Q3 `2025` and about `$105M` in Q1 `2026`.
- Segment mix: Americas remains the core engine; APAC is the highest-growth region; EMEA turned into the visible weak spot in Q1 `2026`.
- Brand mix: the leadership rotated. Hollister drove Q3 `2025` and the full-year `2025` story, while the Abercrombie brand improved in Q4 and led Q1 `2026`.
- Consumer and cultural signals: this is one of the best examples in the archive of a mall-sensitive apparel company winning because the brands still mean something culturally and the operating execution is good enough to convert that into margins.
- Management tone: confident but more nuanced than the cleaner Q4 read, especially once Q1 `2026` exposed EMEA softness and a flatter Hollister comparison.

## Open questions

- Is the current model genuinely balanced across Abercrombie and Hollister, or does leadership still rotate enough that the company remains more fragile than the headline margins suggest?
- How much of the Q1 `2026` regional pressure in EMEA is temporary, and how much reflects a more persistent problem for Hollister outside the Americas?
- Does Abercrombie belong in the same comparison lane primarily with Levi and lululemon, or is it more useful as the strongest contrast case against Gap and American Eagle?
