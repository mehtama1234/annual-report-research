Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Sector: Consumer Goods

- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026

## Annual report takeaways

- Levi Strauss is a useful archive addition because it gives the consumer-facing work a cleaner brand-led apparel and direct-relationship operator, not just another weak department-store or premium wellness case.
- AnnualReports.com was only partially useful here. On Monday, August 10, 2026, it still showed `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, even though Levi Strauss's official investor-relations stack already exposed the `2025` annual report.
- The `2025` annual and year-end materials show that Levi Strauss is not just a wholesale jeans supplier. The operating story now runs through the Levi's brand, direct-to-consumer expansion, broader lifestyle-category reach, international growth, and the Beyond Yoga acquisition while Dockers remains part of the portfolio but is no longer the main growth lens.
- Full-year `2025` reported net revenues reached `$6.3B`, organic growth was `7%`, direct-to-consumer represented `49%` of total net revenues, the company described four consecutive quarters of high-single-digit organic revenue growth, and the retail footprint was roughly `3,300` stores and shop-in-shops.
- The strategically important point is that Levi Strauss is using a durable heritage brand to widen into a more controlled consumer relationship system through DTC, category expansion, international growth, and premium adjacency rather than relying primarily on wholesale denim replenishment.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` reported net revenues increased `8%`; organic net revenues increased `6%`; gross margin reached `62.7%`; operating margin was `7.8%`; adjusted EBIT margin was `9.0%` versus `8.3%`; and diluted EPS was positive in the reported quarter chain.
- Operating posture: Europe revenue reached about `$420M` versus `$403M`; Asia revenue reached about `$284M` versus `$258M`; Beyond Yoga revenue reached about `$43M` versus `$37M`; and management continued to frame the business around DTC, international growth, and brand-led category expansion.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows Levi Strauss operating like a branded relationship platform with improving margin quality, international breadth, and premium-adjacent growth rather than like a simple wholesale apparel vendor.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` net revenues were about `$1.7B`, up `14%` reported and `9%` organic; Americas rose `9%` reported and `7%` organic; U.S. rose `4%`; Europe rose `24%` reported and `10%` organic; and Asia rose `13%` reported and `12%` organic.
- Operating posture: the company used the quarter to show that the Levi's brand, DTC mix, and international strength were still doing the heavy lifting, with the strongest growth outside the slower U.S. core.
- Structural themes: Q1 is useful because it makes the geography mix visible. Levi Strauss is still a U.S.-rooted denim name, but the better growth and the stronger relationship quality are increasingly tied to Europe, Asia, and a broader direct brand system.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` net revenues were about `$1.766B`; diluted EPS was `$0.41`; operating margin was `11.9%`; adjusted EBIT margin was `12.1%` versus `13.9%`; gross margin was `60.8%` versus `61.8%`; and full-year `2025` net revenues were about `$6.3B`.
- Operating posture: the year-end packet anchored the annual story around four straight quarters of high-single-digit organic growth, a DTC mix at `49%` of net revenues, and better brand control across channels and geographies.
- Structural themes: Q4 is the anchor quarter for the packet because it closes the year with the clearest proof that Levi Strauss is becoming more DTC-led, more internationally balanced, and more relationship-driven than a simple wholesale apparel story would suggest.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Levi Strauss widens `Consumer Goods` with a brand-led apparel and DTC-control case instead of another staples, food, beauty, or value-retail operator.
- Consumer: this is a useful read on a consumer who still responds to heritage brands, fit and identity, and direct brand relationships, especially when the offer extends beyond basic denim into lifestyle and athleisure adjacencies.
- Cultural: the company matters because Levi's remains culturally legible in a way many weaker mall-sensitive brands do not. The archive should treat it as a heritage-brand control system with room for category and geography expansion, not just as a clothing maker.
- Channel and interface: Levi Strauss is a useful bridge case because the relationship now includes DTC stores, eCommerce, global brand control, category expansion, and premium adjacency through Beyond Yoga rather than primarily department-store dependence.
- Business model: the key signal is that brand durability matters more when the company owns more of the consumer interface. The better economics show up through DTC mix, international momentum, gross-margin quality, and a less wholesale-dependent posture.
- Competitive positioning: Levi Strauss is most useful next to `NIKE`, `lululemon`, `Bath & Body Works`, Macy's, and weaker mall-sensitive brands not yet packetized. It is a cleaner mid-premium heritage-brand relationship case than Macy's and a broader, more mass-reachable apparel case than lululemon.

## Working interpretation

- Levi Strauss materially improves the archive because it adds a cleaner heritage-brand and DTC-led apparel control case rather than another plain retail traffic or department-store repair read.
- The important point is not just that recent quarters were positive. It is that the source chain shows where the quality is coming from: DTC mix, international growth, gross-margin discipline, the Levi's brand, and category extension through Beyond Yoga.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Levi Strauss is one of the archive's better proofs that culturally durable apparel brands can still widen their economics by owning more of the consumer relationship rather than by depending mainly on wholesale distribution.
