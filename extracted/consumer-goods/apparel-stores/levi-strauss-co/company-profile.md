## What changed

- Levi Strauss adds a direct brand-led and DTC-control apparel system to `Consumer Goods`, not just another plain retail-format datapoint.
- The annual and quarter materials show the business widening beyond wholesale jeans distribution through the Levi's brand, DTC mix, international growth, broader lifestyle-category penetration, and Beyond Yoga.
- Full-year `2025` net revenues reached about `$6.3B`, organic growth was `7%`, DTC reached `49%` of total net revenues, and the company reported about `3,300` stores and shop-in-shops.
- The quarter trail shows that recent strength is not only a year-end cleanup story: Q4 `2025` closed a stronger year, Q1 `2026` showed broad-based growth with Europe and Asia leading, and Q2 `2026` kept revenue and margin quality moving the right way.
- That makes Levi Strauss a useful bridge case between `NIKE` direct brand control, `lululemon` premium wellness identity, and weaker mall-sensitive operators, because the company combines heritage-brand breadth with a more controlled and more profitable consumer relationship stack.

## Open questions

- How durable is the current growth once the U.S. denim core becomes a smaller share of the story and more of the quality depends on international momentum?
- Can Beyond Yoga and other category extensions become meaningful enough to keep Levi Strauss from being read mainly as a denim brand with better execution?
- Does the archive need a weaker mall-sensitive or lower-price apparel operator next so Levi Strauss can be compared against a less advantaged branded-apparel model?
