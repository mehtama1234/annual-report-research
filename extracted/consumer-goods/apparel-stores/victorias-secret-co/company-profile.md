## What changed

- Revenue: the company moved from a stronger Q3 `2025` inflection to a stronger Q4 holiday close and then into an even stronger Q1 `2026`, with net sales of `$1.472B`, `$2.270B`, and `$1.560B` across the trailing quarter chain.
- Margin: the quarter trail shows a real earnings turn, from adjusted breakeven operating income in Q3 `2025` to `$316M` in Q4 `2025` and `$80M` in Q1 `2026`, supported by higher regular-price selling and fewer promotions.
- Guidance and tone: management progressed from raising fiscal `2025` guidance in Q3 to beating full-year expectations in Q4 and then raising full-year `2026` guidance again after Q1.
- Capital allocation: the company repurchased `2.2 million` shares for `$100M` in Q1 `2026`.
- Brand and category mix: Victoria's Secret, `PINK`, and Beauty were all called out as growth drivers in the most recent quarter, while Adore Me remains the digital-acquisition and all-budgets adjacency rather than the core earnings engine.
- Consumer and cultural signals: this is a direct read on how intimate apparel and beauty demand can recover when fit, product, storytelling, and identity feel current enough to pull in new customers without leaning as hard on promotions.
- Structural nuance: the source set carries a ticker discrepancy as of `2026-08-10`, with `VSCO` still dominant on AnnualReports and most IR artifact titles while the latest official quarter release and SEC submissions JSON showed `VSXY`.

## Open questions

- Is the current acceleration durable enough to make Victoria's Secret a sustained winner, or is it still early enough that the story depends heavily on near-term product and marketing execution?
- How much of the current improvement is coming from true brand relevance and customer-file growth versus a favorable promotional reset?
- Does Adore Me become strategically more important again, or does the investment case remain centered on the core Victoria's Secret, `PINK`, and Beauty system?
