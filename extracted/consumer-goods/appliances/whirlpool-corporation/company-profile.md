## What changed

- Category context: Whirlpool’s `2025` annual report shows a global appliance company increasingly concentrated in the Americas, with roughly `$16B` of annual net sales, about `41,000` employees, and a segment structure centered on `MDA North America`, `MDA Latin America`, and `SDA Global`.
- Portfolio shift: the company deconsolidated Whirlpool India in late `2025`, had already deconsolidated Europe in `2024`, and now presents itself as a more focused Americas-plus-small-appliances operator.
- Operating reality: the Q4 `2025`, Q1 `2026`, and Q2 `2026` chain makes clear that appliances are a replacement-cycle business tied to housing, consumer sentiment, retail promotions, builder demand, and inflation-sensitive household budgets.
- Margin pressure and response: by `2026`, Whirlpool was openly using price hikes, promotional tightening, inventory reduction, structural cost takeout, and recapitalization to defend profitability amid tariffs, raw-material inflation, and weak North American demand.
- Capital structure stress: the company cut and then suspended its common dividend, raised equity, issued secured bonds, and put a large ABL facility in place to extend maturities and preserve flexibility.

## Open questions

- How much of Whirlpool’s eventual margin recovery depends on a real housing and demand rebound versus company-controlled pricing and cost actions?
- Can the company sustain price increases in a promotional category without losing too much volume or share?
- Does the stronger `SDA Global` and direct-to-consumer performance meaningfully offset the cyclicality of the large-appliance core, or mostly cushion it?
