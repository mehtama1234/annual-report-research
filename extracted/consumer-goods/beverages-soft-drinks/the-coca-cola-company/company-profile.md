## What changed

- Routine beverage scale: Coca-Cola adds a direct global routine beverage and trusted refreshment system to Consumer Goods, which was previously represented more indirectly through PepsiCo and more category-intensely through Monster.
- Revenue and earnings path: FY`2025` net revenues were `$47.9B` and comparable EPS was `$3.00`; then Q1 `2026` and Q2 `2026` reported net revenues of `$12.5B` and `$13.4B` with comparable EPS of `$0.86` and `$0.97`.
- Volume quality: full-year `2025` volume was even, Q1 `2026` volume grew `3%`, and Q2 `2026` volume grew `5%`, which shows the system still recruiting consumers rather than relying purely on price.
- Portfolio and pack architecture: the quarter chain repeatedly highlights Coca-Cola Zero Sugar, mini-cans, single-serve packs, local pack formats, affordability architecture, and revenue growth management.
- Cultural distribution layer: the FIFA World Cup `2026` campaign and connected-packaging activity show how Coca-Cola converts brand scale into retail reach, social reach, consumer recruitment, and first-party data capture.
- Cash-flow and accounting distortions: the fairlife contingent-consideration payment and BODYARMOR impairment matter because they complicate a simple read of free cash flow and reported operating income even while the underlying system looks stable to stronger.

## Open questions

- How much of Coca-Cola's current strength is specific to a favorable volume and cultural-marketing window versus a more durable structural upgrade in the system?
- Does connected packaging and first-party data capture meaningfully change the economics of a legacy beverage company, or is it still mostly an execution enhancer around the edges?
- As the archive adds more direct beverage names, will Coca-Cola prove to be the cleanest routine-use beverage interface, or will PepsiCo's broader portfolio still offer the more resilient earnings architecture?
