## Annual report takeaways

- Church & Dwight's `2025` annual and year-end materials show a narrower everyday-use household and personal-care platform than Procter & Gamble, but one that is increasingly shaped by brand incubation, acquisitions, and eCommerce scale rather than by a static legacy-brand portfolio alone.
- Full-year `2025` net sales reached `$6.2032 billion`, reported growth was `1.6%`, organic sales growth was `0.7%`, adjusted EPS reached `$3.53`, and cash from operations was `$1.215 billion`.
- The annual read matters because management explicitly framed the company as balancing value and premium products while pruning weaker or slower assets. During `2025`, Church & Dwight exited the VMS, FLAWLESS, SPINBRUSH, and WATERPIK showerhead businesses and repositioned the portfolio toward faster-growing brands and categories.
- This is one of the cleaner acquisition-led routine-use stories in Consumer Goods. THERABREATH, HERO, TOUCHLAND, and later MISS MOUTH'S show a model where everyday demand is reinforced by newer brands with stronger growth or digital momentum.
- Compared with Kimberly-Clark and Colgate, Church & Dwight reads as more acquisition-active and eCommerce-forward. Compared with P&G, it is much narrower and more selective, but that narrower shape makes it a useful test of whether routine-use resilience can still hold without massive category breadth.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` net sales increased `1.6%` to `$1.530 billion`, organic sales growth accelerated to `5.8%`, adjusted gross margin was `45.4%`, reported EPS was `$0.85`, adjusted EPS was `$0.89`, and first-half cash from operations reached `$461.6 million`.
- Guidance: on July 31, 2026, Church & Dwight raised its `2026` outlook to net sales of flat to `+1%`, organic sales growth of `4%` to `5%`, adjusted gross margin expansion of `100` to `120` basis points, adjusted EPS growth of `6%` to `8%`, and cash from operations of about `$1.175 billion`.
- Management message: management called this the second straight quarter of industry-leading organic sales growth and highlighted broad-based gains across all three divisions.
- Structural themes: eCommerce grew `22.7%` in Q2 and global online sales reached `25.5%` of total consumer sales, which makes Church & Dwight one of the cleaner examples of a staples-like routine-use company still gaining digital share.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` net sales increased `0.2%`, organic sales growth was `5.0%`, adjusted gross margin was `46.4%`, reported EPS was `$0.91`, adjusted EPS was `$0.95`, and cash from operations was `$174.8 million`.
- Guidance: Church & Dwight maintained a `2026` outlook at that time calling for reported net sales of `-1.5%` to `-0.5%`, organic sales growth of `3%` to `4%`, adjusted gross margin expansion of `100` basis points, and cash from operations of about `$1.15 billion`.
- Management message: management stressed strong domestic performance and called out THERABREATH, ARM & HAMMER cat litter, HERO, and OXICLEAN as current growth drivers.
- Structural themes: online sales reached `24%` of total consumer sales, which reinforces that this is not just a shelf-staples operator. The company is increasingly dependent on digital discovery, repeat purchase, and newer brand recruitment.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` net sales increased `3.9%`, organic sales growth was `0.7%`, adjusted gross margin was `45.5%`, reported EPS was `$0.60`, adjusted EPS was `$0.86`, and cash from operations was `$363.4 million`.
- Guidance: the January 30, 2026 release framed `2026` around organic sales growth of `3%` to `4%`, a reported sales decline of about `1.5%` to `0.5%`, and cash from operations of about `$1.15 billion`.
- Management message: management emphasized a balanced value-and-premium portfolio, stronger second-half `2025` organic growth of `2%`, and the benefits of the `2025` portfolio repositioning.
- Structural themes: this quarter is the clearest bridge between the annual packet and `2026` quarter growth. Church & Dwight entered `2026` after pruning slower or less strategic businesses and tightening around faster-growing daily-use brands.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Church & Dwight strengthens the case that household and personal-care resilience does not require P&G-like breadth. A narrower portfolio can still work if routine demand, digital growth, and disciplined portfolio management align.
- Consumer: this is a useful read on how consumers still buy trusted everyday-use products across household cleaning, oral care, over-the-counter wellness, and specialty personal care, while newer premium or digitally recruited brands can still scale.
- Cultural: the signal is less about aspirational branding and more about trusted efficacy, repeat use, wellness adjacency, and online brand discovery.
- Regulatory: tariffs and inflation are visible enough to matter in the margin discussion, but the more important read is still brand mix and portfolio pruning.
- Labor: labor is not the central message. Productivity, digital commerce, and brand mix are more visible than workforce themes.
- Technology: technology mainly appears through eCommerce penetration, digital marketing, and acquisition-led innovation rather than as a standalone product thesis.
- Capital allocation: Church & Dwight is a useful capital-allocation case because it is both a cash-generative staples platform and an active portfolio shaper, using pruning and tuck-in acquisitions to refresh the demand base.
