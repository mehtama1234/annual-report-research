## Annual report takeaways

- Mondelez is the right next consumer-goods packet because it adds the indulgence and affordable-treat snacking layer that sits between PepsiCo's broader convenience portfolio and the more routine beverage systems represented by Coca-Cola and Monster.
- AnnualReports is current here. As of `2026-08-10`, the site exposed a `2025 Annual Report and Form 10K` package for Mondelez, and the official annual-reports page also exposed a current `2025 ANNUAL REPORT (ON FORM 10-K)` link.
- The FY`2025` evidence chain shows a large global snacking platform under real cost pressure rather than a frictionless staples compounder. Full-year net revenues reached `$38.537B`, organic net revenues grew `4.3%`, volume/mix fell `3.7%`, adjusted EPS was `$2.92`, free cash flow was `$3.2B`, and capital returned to shareholders reached `$4.9B`.
- The key annual tension is clear in management's own framing: Mondelez says it delivered solid top-line results and strong cash flow in `2025`, but unprecedented cocoa cost headwinds materially pressured profitability.
- The annual and year-end materials also make the operating response explicit. Management entered `2026` focused on improved volumes, brand investments, structural cost savings, disciplined capital allocation, and stabilizing cocoa costs rather than depending on pure price carry.
- Mondelez matters to the broader archive because it is a clean case where global snack demand is still resilient, but earnings quality depends heavily on commodity exposure, brand strength, route-to-market execution, and whether consumers keep absorbing price while still buying affordable treats.
- The business also broadens the cultural side of Consumer Goods. Mondelez explicitly frames itself as leading the future of snacking across more than `150` countries, with brands such as Oreo, Ritz, LU, Clif Bar, Tate's Bake Shop, Cadbury Dairy Milk, Milka, and Toblerone.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` net revenues were `$9.355B`, organic net revenues grew `2.2%`, volume/mix rose `0.7%`, diluted EPS rose to `$1.20`, and adjusted EPS was `$0.73`.
- Cash flow and capital return: year-to-date cash provided by operating activities was `$1.3B`, free cash flow was `$0.7B`, first-half capital returned to shareholders was `$1.5B`, and the quarterly dividend was raised `4%`.
- Management message: management said the quarter showed robust top-line expansion, volume growth, share improvement, and improved profitability, with continued strength in Emerging Markets, strong growth in North America, and early positive share trends in Europe.
- Guidance: Mondelez raised the top end of the organic view by saying it now expects at least `2%` organic net revenue growth for `2026`, while maintaining adjusted EPS growth of flat to `5%` on a constant-currency basis and free cash flow of about `$3B`.
- Structural read: Q2 is the clearest recent signal that Mondelez is not only managing price. The company is trying to restore a healthier mix of pricing, volume, and share while still carrying elevated raw-material and geopolitical uncertainty.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` net revenues were `$10.080B`, organic net revenues grew `3.0%`, volume/mix fell `0.5%`, diluted EPS rose to `$0.44`, and adjusted EPS was `$0.67`.
- Cash flow and capital return: cash provided by operating activities was `$0.5B`, free cash flow was `$0.2B`, and capital returned to shareholders was `$0.6B`.
- Management message: management highlighted strong top-line growth in Emerging Markets and signs of improvement in Developed Markets, while describing the quarter as consumer-centric execution backed by heavier brand and growth-platform investment.
- Guidance: Mondelez reaffirmed `2026` guidance at organic net revenue growth of flat to `2%`, adjusted EPS growth of flat to `5%` on a constant-currency basis, and free cash flow of approximately `$3B`.
- Structural read: Q1 still looks like a transition quarter. Pricing remains the main driver, but the company is trying to rebuild volume, support brands, and keep developed-market demand from weakening further under macro volatility.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: the Q4 / FY`2025` chain showed Q4 net revenues of `$10.496B`, full-year net revenues of `$38.537B`, full-year organic net revenue growth of `4.3%`, full-year diluted EPS of `$1.89`, full-year adjusted EPS of `$2.92`, and full-year free cash flow of `$3.2B`.
- Capital return and guidance: FY`2025` capital returned to shareholders was `$4.9B`, and Mondelez entered `2026` expecting organic net revenue growth of flat to `2%`, adjusted EPS growth of flat to `5%` on a constant-currency basis, and free cash flow of approximately `$3B`.
- Management message: management said `2025` was dynamic and challenging, stressed that cocoa cost headwinds hit profitability hard, and framed `2026` around improved volumes, brand investments, structural cost savings, disciplined capital allocation, and stabilizing cocoa costs.
- Structural read: Q4 `2025` is the bridge quarter that makes Mondelez analytically useful in this archive. It shows a global indulgence-and-snacking platform that still has pricing power and brand strength, but whose earnings quality is unusually exposed to commodity inflation and the limits of consumer tolerance.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Affordable indulgence and habit: Mondelez is one of the clearest examples in the repo of a consumer system built on habitual snacking and affordable treats rather than on strict necessity or premium-status spending.
- Commodity sensitivity: cocoa and other raw-material costs are not background noise here. They are central to the earnings story and make Mondelez a good counterpoint to more routine or necessity-led consumer names.
- Brand investment under pressure: Oreo, Cadbury, Milka, Ritz, LU, Clif Bar, and Tate's give Mondelez real pricing and shelf strength, but the quarter chain shows management still needing reinvestment and structural savings to defend profitability.
- Emerging markets and geographic breadth: management repeatedly leans on Emerging Markets strength while watching developed-market improvement, which makes geography a major stabilizer in the story.
- Comparison value: PepsiCo is broader and more convenience-portfolio driven, Coca-Cola is a cleaner routine beverage and cultural-distribution system, Monster is a more intense functional-energy habit system, and Mondelez is the strongest direct indulgence-snacking and affordable-treat case.
- Capital allocation: Mondelez remains a mature cash-return name, but the current operating frame is not simple shareholder harvesting. It is cash return plus brand investment plus cost savings under commodity stress.

## Working interpretation

- Mondelez materially improves the archive because it gives Consumer Goods a direct indulgence-and-snacking case instead of another routine beverage or household-care routine.
- The packet sharpens a useful four-way food-and-beverage split:
  - PepsiCo as broad convenience and snacks-plus-beverages
  - Coca-Cola as global routine beverage and cultural distribution
  - Monster as functional-energy and identity-heavy habit
  - Mondelez as affordable indulgence and branded snacking
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Mondelez is strong evidence that global consumer demand can still support repeated treat purchases even while commodity inflation and input-cost volatility meaningfully squeeze profitability.
