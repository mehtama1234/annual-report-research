## What changed

- Indulgence and snacking layer: Mondelez adds the clearest branded snacking and affordable-treat case inside Consumer Goods, rather than another necessity-led staples or routine-beverage system.
- Revenue and earnings path: FY`2025` net revenues were `$38.537B`; the next two quarters then came in at `$10.080B` in Q1 `2026` and `$9.355B` in Q2 `2026`.
- Mix and pricing quality: FY`2025` volume/mix was down `3.7%`, Q1 `2026` volume/mix was down `0.5%`, and Q2 `2026` volume/mix turned positive at `0.7%`, which makes the `2026` story partly about rebuilding healthier demand quality.
- Commodity pressure: cocoa costs are a central earnings variable here and are one of the clearest reasons Mondelez is a useful contrast with Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, and the household-care names.
- Geographic shape: management repeatedly highlights Emerging Markets strength, stronger North America execution, and early improvement in Europe.
- Capital allocation: Mondelez still returned meaningful cash to shareholders, but the operating story is more about balancing brand investment, cost savings, and commodity pressure than simply harvesting a mature franchise.

## Open questions

- Will the improving volume and share signals in Q2 `2026` hold if cocoa and other inputs stay volatile?
- How durable is the affordable-treat and indulgence demand pattern if consumer budgets weaken further?
- As the archive adds more direct food names, will Mondelez prove to be the strongest pure snacking case, or mostly a cocoa-cost management case with strong brands attached?
