## Annual report takeaways

- Burlington is a useful archive addition because it gives the consumer-facing work a second strong off-price operator without relying on the same AnnualReports taxonomy lane as Ross.
- AnnualReports already exposed the `2025` annual-report year on `2026-08-09`, but Burlington's official IR pages were unstable in this shell environment, so the local packet relies on the SEC-hosted annual-report PDF, the `10-K`, and verified official result URLs for the quarter layer.
- The `2025` annual filing frames Burlington as a scaled store-based off-price system rather than a digital interface system. The company says more than `99%` of net sales come from stores and reports `1,212` stores, `52.8 million` gross square feet, `33.9 million` selling square feet, and six distribution centers as of `2026-01-31`.
- The annual filing also shows an operator preparing to keep expanding. Burlington amended its ABL line on `2025-07-25`, increasing commitments from `$900 million` to `$1,000 million`, and ended fiscal `2025` with `$934.5 million` of remaining borrowing capacity.
- The broader read is that Burlington gives the archive a cleaner pure value-and-bargain case. There is no loyalty-membership or ad-platform widening story here. The business still depends mainly on store traffic, merchandise turn, branded bargains, and off-price shopping behavior.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` total sales increased `14%`; comparable store sales increased `6%`; net income was `$115 million`; and diluted EPS was `$1.80`.
- Operating posture: the `10-Q` says Burlington had `1,242` stores as of `2026-05-02`, after opening `40` new stores including six relocations and closing four stores during the quarter.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows an off-price retailer still driving both comp growth and unit growth at the same time, which strengthens the trade-down and branded-bargain thesis in the current consumer environment.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` total sales grew `11%`; comparable store sales increased `4%`; net income was `$310 million`; diluted EPS was `$4.84`; and FY25 net income was `$610 million`.
- Operating posture: official result-page coverage also said FY25 total sales grew `9%` and comparable store sales increased `2%`, which implies the year ended with momentum rather than fading.
- Structural themes: this is the key quarter for the packet because it closes the fiscal year with clean evidence that the off-price model was strengthening into year-end rather than only benefiting from a single promotional burst.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` total sales grew `7%`; comparable store sales increased `1%`; net income was `$105 million`; diluted EPS was `$1.63`; and adjusted EPS was `$1.80`.
- Operating posture: the official release coverage also cited adjusted EBIT margin improvement, which fits the broader message that Burlington was not only chasing traffic but improving the economics of that traffic.
- Structural themes: Q3 is useful because it shows the earlier stage of the acceleration. The business was already growing before the stronger Q4 and Q1 results made the pattern obvious.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Burlington widens `Consumer Goods` beyond staples, beauty, beverages, packaging, and home improvement into off-price retail behavior that still sits inside the consumer-goods taxonomy.
- Consumer: this is a direct read on bargain-hunt and value-seeking behavior in the U.S. consumer. Stronger recent comps suggest shoppers were still responding to branded off-price discovery even in a pressured environment.
- Cultural: Burlington matters because off-price is partly functional and partly experiential. The value proposition is not just low prices; it is the repeated discovery loop around recognizable brands at sharp discounts.
- Channel and format: Burlington is overwhelmingly physical-store-driven. That makes it useful as a counterpoint to digital membership, media, or marketplace-heavy consumer models elsewhere in the archive.
- Business model: the key signal is that a store-dense, inventory-turn, opportunistic-buying model can still drive real growth when consumers remain price-sensitive and open to trade-down.
- Competitive positioning: Burlington is most useful next to Ross. Together they improve the archive's read on off-price as its own consumer-interface lane instead of treating it as a weaker version of department stores or a generic discount bucket.

## Working interpretation

- Burlington materially improves the archive because it turns the off-price claim from a Ross-only reading into a stronger pattern.
- The important point is not just that Burlington posted good recent quarter growth. It is that the recent sequence suggests acceleration: `Q3 2025` was positive, `Q4 2025` was stronger, and `Q1 2026` strengthened again while the store base kept expanding.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Burlington is a strong archive addition for understanding how bargain discovery, branded value hunting, and trade-down behavior remain potent consumer forces even without a formal membership system or a large digital relationship layer.
