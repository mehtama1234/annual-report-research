## What changed

- Burlington adds a direct off-price and bargain-discovery retailer to the archive, but under `Consumer Goods` taxonomy rather than `Services` or formal `Retail`.
- The `2025` annual package shows a scaled physical-store model rather than a digital-membership or marketplace model. The `10-K` describes Burlington as a nationally recognized off-price retailer, says more than `99%` of net sales come from stores, and shows `1,212` stores, `52.8 million` gross square feet, `33.9 million` selling square feet, and six distribution centers as of `2026-01-31`.
- Burlington also expanded its liquidity capacity in the middle of the year. The `10-K` says the ABL line increased from `$900 million` to `$1,000 million` on `2025-07-25`, and remaining borrowing capacity was `$934.5 million` at `2026-01-31`.
- The quarter trail matters because it shows an accelerating off-price demand line rather than a repair story. Reported official quarter headlines moved from `Q3 2025` total sales growth of `7%` and comparable store sales growth of `1%`, to `Q4 2025` growth of `11%` and `4%`, to `Q1 2026` growth of `14%` and `6%`.
- That makes Burlington a useful counterpart to Ross. Ross is the archive's cleaner `Services / Apparel Stores` off-price case, while Burlington shows similar bargain-hunt behavior through a different AnnualReports sector bucket and a more home-and-general-merchandise-adjacent assortment mix.

## Open questions

- How much of Burlington's recent momentum is broad consumer trade-down and bargain-seeking versus company-specific execution?
- Can Burlington keep comp growth and store growth working at the same time as the store base pushes beyond `1,200` units?
- Does the archive need one more off-price operator after Ross and Burlington, or is the current pair already enough to treat bargain discovery as a durable consumer-interface track?
