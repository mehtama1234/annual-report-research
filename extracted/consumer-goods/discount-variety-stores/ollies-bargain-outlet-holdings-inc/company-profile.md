## What changed

- AnnualReports blind spot: Ollie's is covered by `AnnualReports.com`, but the `Consumer Goods / Discount, Variety Stores` label and lagging `2024` annual package flatten an analytically stronger closeout and bargain-discovery case into a generic discount-store bucket.
- Value-hunt structure: Ollie's is more usefully understood as a closeout and treasure-hunt retail system built on flexible buying, low-ticket perceived value, recognizable brands, and repeated in-store discovery.
- Relationship layer: the model is not as thick as a marketplace or paid-membership ecosystem, but `Ollie's Army` still matters because it reinforces repeat traffic, coupons, and direct communication around the value proposition.
- Growth logic: the annual and quarter materials show a system that scales through contiguous geographic expansion, opportunistic real estate, and merchandise availability rather than through digital platform monetization or premium brand control.
- Tariff and sourcing posture: the `2025` annual and `2026` quarter filings make tariff exposure explicit, but they also show how a flexible closeout buyer can sometimes benefit from broader inventory dislocation and excess-product availability.

## Open questions

- How much of Ollie's advantage comes from structural closeout buying skill versus a consumer base that is simply more value-sensitive than in earlier cycles?
- Is the best comparison set TJX and Ross, or does Ollie's belong partly in a different lower-ticket lane closer to Dollar General and Dollar Tree even though the shopping behavior is less convenience-led?
- If tariffs and macro pressure intensify, does Ollie's gain more sourcing opportunity than cost pressure, or does the balance become less favorable once value competitors all lean harder into bargain messaging?
