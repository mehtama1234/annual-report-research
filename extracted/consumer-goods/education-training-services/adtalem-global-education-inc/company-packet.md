# Adtalem Global Education Inc. / Covista Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Company: Adtalem Global Education Inc. / Covista Inc.
- Sector: Consumer Goods
- Industry: Education & Training Services
- Ticker: `ATGE` before `2026-02-24`; `CVSA` after the rename transition
- Fiscal year-end: June
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q4 FY2026
  - quarter 2: Q3 FY2026
  - quarter 3: Q2 FY2026

## Annual report takeaways

- Adtalem is a core human-capital and institutional-interface company because it sits inside the pipeline that turns social labor shortages into formal training demand, credential volume, and employer-linked educational throughput.
- The `2025` annual report still reflects the pre-`Covista` identity, but the operating logic is already visible: this is not a broad liberal-arts story. It is a targeted workforce-production story centered on healthcare, nursing, medicine, veterinary education, and career-linked graduate programs.
- That matters for this frontier because the institutional question is no longer just whether people want education. It is whether employers, students, and regulators will increasingly treat education as labor-market infrastructure for sectors with chronic shortages.
- The annual and quarter chain together show a company moving from education brand management toward pipeline management. It wants to own the interface between labor scarcity, student access, employer partnerships, campus capacity, digital delivery, and professional readiness.
- The later rename to `Covista` makes that direction more explicit. Management is trying to reposition the company around mission-critical workforce production rather than around the more generic and historically mixed reputation of for-profit education.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q4 FY2026 revenue was about `$501.4M`; GAAP net income was about `$71.7M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$126.9M`; adjusted EPS was about `$2.09`. Full-year FY2026 revenue was about `$1.95B`; GAAP net income was about `$251.6M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$521.7M`; adjusted EPS was about `$8.25`.
- Student metrics: total Q4 enrollment was `99,472`, up `8.4%` year over year. Walden enrollment was up `14.0%`; Medical and Veterinary enrollment was up `7.3%`; Chamberlain total enrollment growth accelerated to `1.6%`.
- Strategic signals: Covista said it completed its three-year `Growth with Purpose` strategy and was entering `Purpose at Scale`. Management framed the company as “America’s largest healthcare educator” and tied growth directly to the need to close the healthcare workforce shortage.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY2026 shows education being reframed as a scaled labor-supply system. The company is not mainly selling prestige or general credentials. It is selling capacity into shortage occupations with visible employer need.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter is one of the clearest archive examples of workforce training becoming infrastructure. The company highlighted employer collaborations with major health systems, AI-oriented credentials, and geographic expansion as mechanisms for increasing throughput into care systems that cannot staff themselves.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q3 FY2026 revenue was about `$487.0M`, up `4.5%` year over year, or about `8.4%` adjusted for a Walden one-week academic shift. GAAP net income was about `$41.6M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$127.9M`; adjusted EPS was about `$1.98`.
- Student metrics: total enrollment reached `100,585`, up `6.8%` year over year, with the company surpassing `100,000` students. Chamberlain returned to positive total enrollment growth and delivered its highest enrollment in history. Walden posted its eleventh straight quarter of total enrollment growth.
- Strategic signals: Covista raised FY2026 guidance to revenue of about `$1.93B` to `$1.945B` and adjusted EPS of about `$7.95` to `$8.15`. It also refinanced debt and extended maturities to `2033`.
- Interpretation: Q3 matters because it shows this is not only a policy or social-need story. It is also an operating system story. Enrollment growth, timing normalization, and financial flexibility all matter if the company wants to convert social shortages into durable returns.
- Bigger-picture read: management explicitly said the country needs more nurses, physicians, and behavioral-health professionals than the current system is on pace to produce. That is exactly the societal lens this frontier needs: institutions are paying for adaptation because existing pipelines are undersupplying critical labor.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q2 FY2026 revenue was about `$503.4M`, up `12.4%` year over year. GAAP net income was about `$76.4M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$154.9M`; adjusted EPS was about `$2.43`.
- Student metrics: total enrollment was `97,010`, up `6.3%` year over year, marking a tenth straight quarter of growth. Walden posted its tenth straight quarter of growth and reached its highest total enrollment in history. Chamberlain continued pre-licensure BSN growth for a fourteenth straight quarter.
- Strategic signals: the company completed a prior share-repurchase program, launched a new `$750M` authorization, and raised FY2026 guidance. Management also emphasized BSN online growth, physician-assistant expansion, and greater AI fluency in educational pathways.
- Interpretation: Q2 shows why this company belongs in the lane. It is a clean example of institutions and consumers paying for structured upskilling where the social demand signal is unusually concrete: nursing shortages, provider shortages, and employer need are all visible and measurable.
- Bigger-picture read: education here functions less like a discretionary consumer purchase and more like a regulated pathway into shortage occupations. That gives the category unusual resilience, but it also exposes it to scrutiny over quality, access, and alignment with real labor-market outcomes.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Workforce training as infrastructure: Covista is a strong anchor for the idea that some education companies are effectively labor-market infrastructure providers. Their economic role is to move people into critical professions faster and at larger scale.
- Institutional spending behavior: healthcare systems, regulators, and students are all part of the demand loop. Employer partnerships, clinical sites, and credential pathways show that institutional buyers and labor shortages increasingly shape education demand.
- Cultural and consumer shift: the value proposition is moving away from broad education identity and toward practical, career-linked, flexible pathways. Students appear increasingly willing to choose education products that map directly to employability, licensing, and wage relevance.
- Trust and outcomes pressure: this category only works if quality, student outcomes, and workforce readiness remain credible. The more education is sold as shortage relief, the more it will be judged on throughput and job readiness rather than on enrollment alone.
- Durable operating pressure: scaling this model still requires campus expansion, regulatory approvals, student support, technology integration, and capital discipline. The lane is not asset-light in practice even if it lacks physical utility infrastructure.

## Working interpretation

- Adtalem/Covista helps explain why the human-capital lane belongs in this frontier at all. It shows that institutional interfaces are not only advisory firms and staffing intermediaries. They also include scaled training operators that translate public shortages into private enrollment and throughput systems.
- The most important societal shift here is that education is increasingly judged by direct labor-market usefulness. That favors providers that can point to shortage occupations, employer partnerships, clinical placements, and measurable readiness rather than only to brand identity.
- The strongest cultural signal is that healthcare credentialing is becoming a national capacity issue. Nursing, physician, behavioral-health, and veterinary shortages create persistent demand that can support both social urgency and investor returns if the operator stays operationally credible.
- In frontier terms, this packet gives the archive a cleaner exact-category human-capital anchor. Omnicom explains how attention is monetized, Charter and AT&T explain who owns connectivity, TTM explains the hardware substrate, and Adtalem/Covista explains how institutions buy labor-force adaptation when society cannot produce enough qualified people through legacy channels alone.
