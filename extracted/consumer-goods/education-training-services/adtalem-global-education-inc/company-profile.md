## What changed

- Taxonomy and lane fit: this is a clean exact-category `Education & Training Services` packet, which makes the human-capital lane more defensible than relying only on adjacent consulting or staffing names.
- Identity shift: the company changed its name from `Adtalem Global Education Inc.` to `Covista Inc.` effective `2026-02-05`, and its ticker changed from `ATGE` to `CVSA` effective `2026-02-24`. The rename is not cosmetic. It coincides with a more explicit framing around healthcare-workforce infrastructure and scaled training pathways.
- Demand thesis: the company increasingly presents itself less as a generic post-secondary operator and more as a targeted workforce pipeline for nursing, medicine, behavioral health, and allied healthcare shortages.
- Operating profile: the quarter chain shows recurring enrollment growth, especially at Walden and Chamberlain, while also showing that the model still depends on execution in campus expansion, program approvals, student outcomes, and regulatory credibility.
- Financial profile: strong revenue growth, rising adjusted EPS, and low leverage give the company a different profile from many challenged education providers. This is a scaled growth-and-throughput story, not just a defensive cash-harvest story.

## Open questions

- How durable is the healthcare-shortage demand tailwind if affordability pressure, regulation, or student-finance policy change?
- Can Covista keep translating labor scarcity into profitable enrollment growth without quality erosion or reputational backlash?
- Does the transition from Adtalem to Covista materially strengthen employer and student trust, or is the core advantage still operational scale inside healthcare credential pathways?
