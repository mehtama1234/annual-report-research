# Eastman Kodak Company Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Eastman Kodak Company
- Sector: Consumer Goods
- Industry: Electronic Equipment
- Frontier role: legacy imaging, print, and materials platform that links the everyday-device lane back to industrial substrate and commodity exposure
- Ticker: KODK
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Kodak is useful for this frontier precisely because it is no longer a straightforward consumer-camera story. The `2025` annual and year-end chain show a company operating across Print, Advanced Materials & Chemicals, motion-picture film, workflow systems, and industrial manufacturing rather than around a dominant household device franchise.
- The key annual point is that Kodak's current relevance comes from the physical and process layer behind imaging. The company is trying to translate legacy brand recognition and technical know-how into print infrastructure, film demand, chemicals, and adjacent industrial growth initiatives.
- Full-year `2025` revenue was `$1.069B`, gross profit was `$232M`, and operational EBITDA was `$62M`. Those figures matter more than the headline GAAP loss because the loss was heavily distorted by the non-recurring KRIP termination and excise-tax impact.
- The annual chain also highlights what is actually driving improvement: better pricing, operational efficiencies, lower spend on organizational-structure investments, lower EPS inventory-reserve adjustments, and favorable FX, partially offset by higher manufacturing and aluminum costs.
- For this frontier, Kodak matters because it turns imaging into a three-model comparison. `GoPro` shows stressed creator-hardware economics, `Canon` shows durable system-level imaging demand, and `Kodak` shows legacy visual infrastructure surviving through print, film, and materials rather than through a high-growth endpoint ecosystem.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$311M`, up `18%` year over year; gross profit was `$82M`, up `61%`; gross profit margin was `26%`, up from `19%`; GAAP net income was `$17M`; and operational EBITDA was `$36M`, up from `$9M`.
- Segment signals: AM&C revenue was `$105M`, up `40%`, and Print revenue was `$195M`, up `10%`.
- Balance-sheet and operating signal: quarter-end cash was `$290M` versus `$337M` at `2025-12-31`, with the decrease driven largely by term-loan repayment and higher inventory tied partly to silver and aluminum commodities.
- Management read: Kodak framed the quarter as a fourth consecutive period of year-over-year growth in revenue, gross profit, and operational EBITDA, and said the company was entering a new phase where it could focus more directly on growth and R&D.
- Bigger-picture read: this is the packet's clearest proof that Kodak now behaves more like a lean industrial print-and-materials platform than like a nostalgic camera brand. Growth is coming from execution, pricing, and manufacturing leverage, but commodity inputs still constrain the upside.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$265M`, up `7%` year over year; gross profit was `$57M`, up `24%`; gross profit margin was `22%`, up from `19%`; GAAP net loss was `$(16)M`; and operational EBITDA was `$15M`, up from `$2M`.
- Segment signals: AM&C revenue was `$76M`, up `3%`, and Print revenue was `$180M`, up `9%`.
- Pressure points: Kodak explicitly said higher silver and aluminum prices offset part of the operational gain. Quarter-end cash was `$299M` versus `$337M` at year-end, though cash flow from operations improved by `$8M` year over year.
- Management read: Kodak called this the third consecutive quarter of year-over-year growth in revenue, gross profit, and operational EBITDA, with both Print and AM&C contributing.
- Bigger-picture read: Q1 shows that the lane is not just about whether demand exists. It is also about whether a legacy manufacturer can keep pricing, mix, and productivity ahead of commodity and infrastructure cost pressure.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$290M`, up `9%` year over year; gross profit was `$67M`, up `31%`; gross profit margin was `23%`; GAAP net loss was `$(108)M`; and operational EBITDA was `$22M`, up from `$9M`.
- Full-year frame: FY`2025` revenue was `$1.069B`, with AM&C revenue of `$316M`, Print revenue of `$715M`, gross profit of `$232M`, operational EBITDA of `$62M`, year-end cash of `$337M`, and cash flow from operations improvement of `$487M`.
- Distortion and underlying improvement: Kodak said the decline in full-year GAAP net income was driven by the non-recurring KRIP termination and related excise tax, while operational EBITDA improved on pricing, efficiencies, lower structural spend, and lower inventory-reserve adjustments.
- Bigger-picture read: Q4/FY2025 is the cleanest statement of Kodak's modern economic reality. The business is not being rescued by mass consumer-camera recovery; it is trying to stabilize and grow through print-system relevance, film and chemical demand, balance-sheet repair, and industrial discipline.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Imaging is not one market: Kodak is a useful counterweight to `GoPro` and `Canon` because it shows that visual-economy demand still lives in film stock, print consumables, chemicals, and industrial workflow, not just in cameras or creator endpoints.
- Legacy franchises move down the stack: cultural brand recognition can survive while the economic core migrates into narrower infrastructure, materials, and specialized-process businesses.
- Commodity inputs matter: silver, aluminum, manufacturing costs, and inventory build are explicit drivers in the quarter chain, which makes Kodak a strong bridge between the everyday-device lane and the physical-infrastructure lane.
- Operational discipline is the growth engine: the improvement story centers on pricing, manufacturing efficiency, de-levering, and infrastructure investment rather than on broad consumer demand acceleration.
- Visual systems still depend on physical substrate: Kodak keeps motion-picture film, print systems, and advanced materials inside the archive's definition of consumer interface, which helps prevent the frontier from collapsing into software and subscription narratives alone.
- Frontier fit: Kodak materially deepens the everyday-device and office-edge lane because it shows how a legacy imaging company can remain relevant through industrial substrate, print workflow, and materials economics even after the consumer-photo story has faded.

## Working interpretation

- Kodak materially strengthens the frontier because it gives the archive a legacy imaging operator whose economics live in print systems, film, chemistry, and industrial process rather than in a broad consumer-device platform.
- The recurring pattern is that old consumer brands can survive by moving deeper into infrastructure and process layers. Kodak's modern relevance sits in narrower but still economically meaningful niches that support visual production and print workflow.
- The social pattern is that image culture remains broad even while the consumer ownership layer fragments. Kodak is not winning by owning a dominant endpoint; it is surviving by supplying substrate and systems that persist behind publishing, packaging, film, and industrial imaging.
- Kodak also sharpens a frontier-wide pressure point already visible in `TTM`, `First Solar`, and `Whirlpool`: even when demand improves, margins and cash still hinge on raw materials, manufacturing efficiency, debt structure, and the ability to keep investing through volatile input costs.
