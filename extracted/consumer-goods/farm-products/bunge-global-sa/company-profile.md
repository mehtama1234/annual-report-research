## What changed

- Operating identity: Bunge is no longer just a grain trader shorthand. The `2025` annual report and `2026` quarter set need to be read as a global food, feed, and fuel infrastructure company whose edge comes from origination, logistics, processing, merchandising, risk management, and downstream ingredient capability.
- Viterra scale effect: the key structural change is the combination with Viterra. Management said more than `$70M` of cost synergies were realized by year-end `2025`, while the quarter materials repeatedly point to additional network and commercial synergies still to come.
- Segment breadth: the annual report shows four real operating lanes rather than one commodity bucket: soybean processing and refining, softseed processing and refining, other oilseeds and specialty ingredients, and grain merchandising and milling. That matters because Bunge monetizes crop flows in multiple ways across food, animal feed, biofuels, ocean freight, and ingredients.
- Decarbonization pull-through: Bunge is a useful archive name because the physical crop chain is being pulled by energy policy as well as food demand. The annual report and `2026` materials tie growth to lower-carbon feedstocks, soy and softseed oils, renewable diesel, SAF-linked supply agreements, and the EPA’s favorable renewable volume framework.
- Traceability and market access: the annual report says Bunge reached `100%` monitoring and traceability for direct and indirect soy sourcing in priority regions of Argentina, Brazil, and Paraguay, and increased global palm oil traceability to plantation to `95.7%`. That is not just ESG language. It increasingly functions as commercial permission to sell into regulated and reputation-sensitive end markets.
- 2026 quarter pattern: `Q1 2026` and `Q2 2026` show how the post-Viterra platform handles uncertainty. Management kept citing geopolitical stress, shifting trade flows, changing weather patterns, and policy volatility, yet results improved because the enlarged network could redirect volumes, capture margin pockets, and keep serving both farmers and end customers.

## Open questions

- How much of the `2026` earnings improvement is cyclical margin recovery versus durable synergy capture from the larger network?
- How exposed is the current earnings step-up to renewable-fuels policy staying constructive in the U.S., Brazil, Indonesia, and Europe?
- Can Bunge keep translating traceability, lower-carbon feedstock positioning, and specialty-ingredient investment into sustained margin quality rather than just better market access?
