## What changed

- Revenue: full-year `2025` net sales were `$24.942 billion`; then the next three quarter chain moved from Q4 `2025` sales down `3.4%`, to Q1 `2026` sales up `0.8%`, to Q2 `2026` Organic Net Sales down `1.3%`.
- Margin: the key earnings pattern is still pressure. Adjusted Operating Income fell `15.9%` in Q4 `2025`, `11.8%` in Q1 `2026`, and `18.4%` in constant currency in Q2 `2026` as the company spent more to rebuild the business.
- Guidance: the company introduced a repair-oriented `2026` plan in February 2026, maintained that outlook on May 6, 2026, then raised its Organic Net Sales outlook on August 5, 2026 while simultaneously increasing planned investment.
- Cash generation: despite the weaker top-line and margin profile, Free Cash Flow remained strong enough to support reinvestment, the dividend, and leverage management.
- Portfolio mix: Taste Elevation is the clearest strength pocket, while Meats and Meals remain more pressured and U.S. Retail still needs improvement.
- Consumer / cultural signals: the business is a direct read on everyday grocery-shelf relevance. Affordability, opening price points, smaller packs, and mainstream pantry usefulness matter more here than premium aspiration.
- Management tone: materially more self-critical and repair-oriented than the old Kraft Heinz framing. The new tone is that the business is underinvested, fixable, and worth rebuilding through sharper commercial execution.

## Open questions

- Can Kraft Heinz convert current share stabilization and brand investment into durable volume-led growth, or is this still mainly a temporary recovery effort?
- How much of the business weakness is company-specific underinvestment versus broader category pressure in legacy center-store grocery?
- Does the stronger Emerging Markets and Heinz-led Taste Elevation momentum eventually outweigh the drag from slower U.S. categories like Meats and Meals?
