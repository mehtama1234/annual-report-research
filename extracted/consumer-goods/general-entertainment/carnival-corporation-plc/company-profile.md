## What changed

- Cruise exposure: this is the first direct cruise operator in the archive, which matters because it extends the travel stack beyond airlines, hotels, gaming, and booking marketplaces into a destination-heavy vacation format.
- Taxonomy tension: AnnualReports classifies Carnival as `Consumer Goods / General Entertainment`, while the SEC currently points to `Water Transportation`. The archive keeps the AnnualReports path but makes the operating reality explicit in the packet.
- Legal structure: the company materially changed in-scope. On `2026-05-07`, Carnival completed the dual-listed-company unification and redomiciled from Panama to Bermuda under the new legal name `Carnival Corporation Ltd.`.
- Business model read: Carnival is not just a fare seller. The core loop is booked occupancy, pricing power, onboard monetization, pre-cruise sales, deposits collected well ahead of travel, and destination control through exclusive ports and islands.
- Balance-sheet repair: the `2025` through `2026` chain shows a post-pandemic cruise operator still focused on yield growth and operating leverage, but also clearly working down leverage, restoring dividends, and widening shareholder returns.

## Open questions

- How durable is Carnival's pricing power if geopolitical disruption or consumer weakness hits longer-haul itineraries again?
- Does cruise behave more like a hotel-plus-air bundle, a gaming-and-destination product, or its own category because deposits, onboard spend, and itinerary control all matter at once?
- Are exclusive destinations and pre-cruise spend becoming a bigger strategic moat than ship capacity alone?
