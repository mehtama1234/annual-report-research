Date baseline: 2026-08-09

## Snapshot

- Company: Lowe's Companies, Inc.
- Sector: Consumer Goods
- Industry: Home Improvement Stores
- Ticker: LOW
- Fiscal year-end: January
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q1 2026
  - quarter 2: Q4 2025
  - quarter 3: Q3 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Lowe's is a high-value archive addition because it turns home improvement into a two-company track instead of leaving Home Depot as a one-name read.
- AnnualReports.com still lagged as of `2026-08-09`, showing Lowe's most recent package as the `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, while Lowe's own investor-relations site already exposed the `2025 Annual Report`.
- The fiscal `2025` annual filing showed a stable but still pressured business. Net sales for fiscal `2025` increased `3.1%` to `$86.3B`, comparable sales increased `0.2%`, net earnings decreased `4.4%` to `$6.7B`, and diluted earnings per common share decreased `3.1%` to `$11.85`.
- The annual filing also makes the strategy explicit. Lowe's frames the business through five Total Home pillars: drive Pro penetration, accelerate online sales, expand home services, create a loyalty ecosystem, and increase space productivity.
- That makes Lowe's useful for the archive in a slightly different way than a plain retail label suggests. The company still lives inside consumer demand, but the real engine increasingly runs through Pro relationships, larger-project categories, delivery infrastructure, home services, and loyalty-linked workflow.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` total sales were `$23.1B` versus `$20.9B` in the prior-year quarter; comparable sales increased `0.6%`.
- Operating posture: management said the quarter was driven by strong spring execution plus continued strength in appliances, home services, and Pro sales.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows Lowe's using multiple channels at once rather than waiting for a broad housing rebound. Online sales growth reached `15.5%`, and the corporate profile attached to the release described a network of over `1,750` home improvement stores, `540` branches, and `120` distribution centers.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` total sales were `$20.6B` versus `$18.6B` in the prior-year quarter; comparable sales increased `1.3%`; diluted EPS was `$1.78`.
- Operating posture: management said growth came from continued strength in Pro, online, and home services sales, plus strong holiday performance.
- Structural themes: this is the key quarter for the packet because it closes fiscal `2025` while also making the acquisition posture visible. The company recognized `$149M` of pre-tax expenses associated with `FBM` and `ADG`, which shows Lowe's broadening the Pro and building-products side of the model even while the headline still looks like retail.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` total sales were `$20.8B` versus `$20.2B` in the prior-year quarter; comparable sales increased `0.4%`; diluted EPS was `$2.88`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$3.06`.
- Operating posture: management said the quarter was helped by `11.4%` online sales growth, double-digit growth in home services, and continued growth in Pro sales.
- Structural themes: this quarter is especially useful because it shows the internal demand mix clearly. DIY is not the whole story; Lowe's is leaning into Pro, digital conversion, and service attachment while absorbing acquisition costs tied to `FBM` and `ADG`.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Lowe's technically enters the archive through `Consumer Goods` because that is how AnnualReports.com currently classifies it, even though the operating model overlaps heavily with the `Services` and built-environment layer.
- Consumer: this is a direct read on repair-and-remodel demand, housing-affordability pressure, project timing, weather sensitivity, and the split between DIY behavior and more professional project spend.
- Cultural: Lowe's matters because home improvement sits between necessity and aspiration. It covers upkeep, trade access, seasonal maintenance, household capability, and bigger-ticket home projects that consumers can defer but not ignore forever.
- Labor and industrial overlap: Lowe's sits unusually close to industrial and contractor workflow because Pro sales, delivery, installers, and home-services execution directly shape outcomes.
- Business model: the key business-model signal is that Lowe's is trying to become more system-like through Pro penetration, online growth, home services, loyalty, and distribution rather than acting like a simple store chain.
- Competitive positioning: Lowe's is especially valuable next to Home Depot because the pair helps distinguish company-specific execution from broader housing and built-environment conditions. The interesting archive signal is that AnnualReports.com splits the two into different sectors even though their industry label is the same.

## Working interpretation

- Lowe's materially improves the archive because it makes the home-improvement lane comparable rather than anecdotal. The filings and releases show a business exposed to housing pressure but expressing the response through a somewhat different mix of Pro, online, home services, loyalty, and acquisitions.
- The important evidence is not just that sales were positive. It is that Lowe's keeps describing a system that is becoming more professional and infrastructure-like even while consumer housing conditions remain constrained.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Lowe's is a strong archive addition because it sharpens the built-environment theme and also exposes a taxonomy mismatch: the same `Home Improvement Stores` industry is currently split between `Consumer Goods` and `Services` on AnnualReports.com.
