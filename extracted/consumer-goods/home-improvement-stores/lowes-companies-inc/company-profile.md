## What changed

- Lowe's gives the archive a second direct home-improvement operator, which is important because it lets the built-environment track stop depending on one company.
- The fiscal `2025` annual package was steady rather than booming. Net sales increased `3.1%` to `$86.3B`, comparable sales increased `0.2%`, net earnings decreased `4.4%` to `$6.7B`, and diluted EPS decreased to `$11.85`.
- The structural point is broader than stores. The annual filing says Lowe's is executing a Total Home strategy built around Pro penetration, online sales, home services, loyalty, and space productivity.
- The quarter set shows why that matters. `Q3 2025` and `Q1 2026` both point to growth in online, home services, and Pro, while the annual filing also says Lowe's has over `540` branch locations serving larger Pro customers.
- The packet also matters because it reveals a classification issue in the archive inputs themselves: AnnualReports.com currently places Lowe's in `Consumer Goods`, while it places Home Depot in `Services`, even though both are in `Home Improvement Stores`.

## Open questions

- How much of Lowe's medium-term growth will come from housing normalization versus the more structural Pro, home-services, and distribution work?
- Do `FBM` and `ADG` make Lowe's meaningfully more distributor-like, or do they remain an adjacency around a still predominantly retail core?
- Is the Home Depot versus Lowe's taxonomy split on AnnualReports.com just a metadata quirk, or does it reflect a meaningful difference in how the two models are presented to investors?
