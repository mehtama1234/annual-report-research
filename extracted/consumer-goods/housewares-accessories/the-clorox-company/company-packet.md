## Annual report takeaways

- Clorox adds a strong household-cleaning, disinfecting, and everyday-care platform to the archive, but the `2025` annual read is not just another simple staples-resilience case. It shows a trusted routine-use business that was still working through the aftereffects of the `2023` cyberattack while rebuilding margin, modernizing its systems, and reshaping the portfolio.
- The FY`2025` annual materials frame the company as a roughly `$7.1 billion` net-sales business with about `80%` of sales coming from No. `1` or No. `2` brands, operations in `~100` markets globally, and `~7,600` employees worldwide.
- Management said FY`2025` grew overall market share and organic sales while delivering more than `200` basis points of gross-margin expansion and solid adjusted EPS growth, which makes the annual packet a clean example of operational repair rather than effortless pricing power.
- The most important portfolio and transformation actions in the annual read were the completed divestiture of the VMS business, the planned wind-down of the Glad bags and wraps joint venture with Procter & Gamble by the end of January `2026`, and an AI-enabled digital core that management said cut innovation cycle time by `65%`.
- Compared with Church & Dwight, Clorox is more directly tied to household cleaning, disinfecting, litter, trash, and professional hygiene. Compared with Procter & Gamble and Kimberly-Clark, it is narrower but more concentrated in cleaning and health-hygiene use cases. Compared with Kenvue, it overlaps in trust and daily routine but leans much harder into household-cleaning and professional-sanitation demand.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: in Q4 FY`2026`, net sales decreased `2%` to `$1.95 billion`, organic sales decreased `13%`, gross margin decreased `520` basis points to `41.3%`, diluted EPS decreased `50%` to `$1.34`, and adjusted EPS decreased `42%` to `$1.66`.
- Guidance: on `2026-08-03`, Clorox provided FY`2027` outlook framing around a still-challenging operating environment with cost volatility and a value-seeking consumer.
- Management message: management said FY`2026` was an important transition year, highlighted the completed GOJO acquisition and the completed U.S. ERP implementation, and said the company exited the year with improved execution and a stronger innovation pipeline.
- Structural themes: this quarter is the clearest evidence that Clorox is trying to turn a routine-use staples platform into a cleaner health-hygiene and superior-value system while absorbing ERP comparisons, GOJO integration, and a more price-sensitive consumer backdrop.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q3 FY`2026` net sales were `$1.67 billion`, flat year over year, organic sales decreased `1%`, gross margin decreased `140` basis points to `43.2%`, diluted EPS increased `3%` to `$1.54`, adjusted EPS increased `13%` to `$1.64`, and year-to-date operating cash flow fell to `$282 million` from `$687 million`.
- Guidance: on `2026-04-30`, Clorox updated its FY`2026` outlook and said the ERP-related shipment comparison was a major transitory driver in the year's numbers.
- Management message: management called the quarter mixed, said market share recovery was slower than anticipated in some businesses, and pointed to GOJO integration plus a wider innovation pipeline as the main reasons it still expected more consistent profitable growth over time.
- Structural themes: Q3 shows the middle stage of the transition. The company had completed the GOJO acquisition, had some traction in stronger categories such as cleaning and professional products, but was still uneven in lifestyle categories and still dealing with slower share recovery than planned.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q2 FY`2026` net sales decreased `1%` to `$1.67 billion`, organic sales decreased `1%`, gross margin decreased `60` basis points to `43.2%`, diluted EPS decreased `16%` to `$1.29`, adjusted EPS decreased `10%` to `$1.39`, and year-to-date operating cash flow was `$404 million`.
- Guidance: on `2026-02-03`, Clorox reaffirmed its fiscal-year outlook for net sales, gross margin, and adjusted EPS.
- Management message: management said the quarter was generally in line with expectations, emphasized final-phase ERP completion, and framed the GOJO deal as an expansion of Clorox's health-and-hygiene position.
- Structural themes: this quarter shows a routine-use company still under pressure but staying on plan. The consumer base remained value-seeking, manufacturing and logistics costs were still elevated, and management was asking the market to look through near-term disruption toward a more modernized portfolio and systems base.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Clorox strengthens the case that staples resilience still depends on execution, system modernization, and category positioning. Trusted daily-use demand is valuable, but it does not remove the need for operational repair.
- Consumer: this is a strong read on value-seeking but still routine-dependent household behavior across cleaning, trash, litter, food, water filtration, natural personal care, and professional hygiene.
- Cultural: the company is less about aspiration and more about trust, efficacy, sanitation, and repeat household use, though brands like Burt's Bees and Hidden Valley keep part of the portfolio closer to lifestyle and self-expression.
- Regulatory: the more important external pressures here are operating-complexity, ERP transition risk, and acquisition integration rather than classic regulation alone.
- Labor: labor is not the core story; productivity, operating simplification, and systems modernization matter more than workforce scarcity in the current packet.
- Technology: technology is a real part of the thesis because ERP modernization, AI-enabled innovation acceleration, and digital capabilities are directly tied to margin repair and execution quality.
- Capital allocation: capital allocation is defined less by pure shareholder-return framing and more by portfolio evolution, the GOJO acquisition, digital transformation spend, and the tradeoff between near-term pressure and longer-term growth quality.
