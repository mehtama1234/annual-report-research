## Annual report takeaways

- eBay is a useful archive addition because it adds a scaled non-travel marketplace model that sits between retail, community, advertising, and seller tooling rather than behaving like a normal store operator.
- AnnualReports still lagged at `2024` on `2026-08-10`, while the official investor stack already exposed the `2025` annual package and the Q4 `2025` through Q2 `2026` results chain.
- The `2025` full-year package was stronger than the older shorthand around a mature marketplace would suggest. Revenue reached `$11.1B`, up `8%`; GMV reached `$79.6B`, up `7%`; GAAP net income from continuing operations reached `$2.0B`; non-GAAP net income reached `$2.6B`; and operating margins were `20.5%` GAAP and `27.8%` non-GAAP.
- The more important point is strategic, not only financial. Management described `2025` as a milestone year, tied the momentum to strategic priorities, and kept leaning into recommerce, collectibles, fashion, enthusiast demand, and advertising rather than trying to sound like a broad everything store.
- That makes eBay analytically useful for the archive. It is a commerce relationship business, but the relationship is organized around discovery, trust, resale, seller supply, and category enthusiasm rather than around paid membership or owned-fulfillment density.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$3.1B`, up `15%`; GMV was `$22.4B`, up `15%`; GAAP net income from continuing operations was `$552M`; and non-GAAP net income from continuing operations was `$727M`.
- Operating posture: GAAP and non-GAAP operating margins were `21.6%` and `28.5%`; total advertising revenue was `$596M`; first-party advertising revenue was `$570M`, up `25%`; and the Depop acquisition closed on `2026-07-30`.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows eBay widening the marketplace into stronger circular-fashion and enthusiast behavior while keeping advertising growth strong enough to matter economically.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$3.1B`, up `19%`; GMV was `$22.2B`, up `18%`; GAAP net income from continuing operations was `$512M`; and non-GAAP net income from continuing operations was `$760M`.
- Operating posture: GAAP and non-GAAP operating margins were `19.8%` and `29.4%`; total advertising revenue was `$581M`; first-party advertising revenue was `$555M`, up `33%`; and free cash flow from continuing operations was `$898M`.
- Structural themes: this is the cleanest quarter for the packet because management explicitly tied the momentum to `Focus Categories`, `C2C`, and `recommerce`, while also announcing the pending Depop acquisition to deepen younger fashion resale demand.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$3.0B`, up `15%`; GMV was `$21.2B`, up `10%`; GAAP net income from continuing operations was `$525M`; and non-GAAP net income from continuing operations was `$648M`.
- Operating posture: GAAP and non-GAAP operating margins were `20.3%` and `26.1%`; full-year `2025` revenue was `$11.1B`; full-year `2025` GMV was `$79.6B`; and total advertising revenue in the quarter was `$544M`.
- Structural themes: this is the anchor quarter for the packet because it closes the year with the clearest evidence that eBay is trying to turn enthusiast discovery, collectibles, pre-loved fashion, and seller supply into a more durable ad-and-marketplace system.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: eBay gives the archive a marketplace operator that AnnualReports places in `Consumer Goods`, even though the economic reality cuts across retail, advertising, and platform behavior.
- Consumer: this is a direct read on resale, collector behavior, value-seeking discovery, and how community supply can create recurring demand without owned inventory.
- Cultural: eBay matters because the company is leaning into pre-loved fashion, collectibles, live shopping, automotive enthusiasts, and other taste-and-identity surfaces rather than only utilitarian e-commerce.
- Loyalty and technology: eBay is a useful counterexample to membership-heavy systems. The lock-in here is weaker and more interest-led, but it is still monetizable through seller density, ad products, trust features, and category specialization.
- Business model: the model compounds when enthusiast demand pulls in supply, supply improves discovery and trust, and those interactions create better ad inventory and higher-value seller services.
- Competitive positioning: eBay is especially useful because it separates marketplace intermediation from owned-retail ecosystem models like Amazon and from travel marketplaces like Booking or Expedia.

## Working interpretation

- eBay materially improves the archive because it lets the consumer-interface work say something more precise than "marketplaces connect buyers and sellers." The packet shows a category-led commerce network where demand is concentrated around enthusiasts, resale, and unique inventory, and where advertising becomes more valuable as trust and discovery improve.
- The most important evidence here is not only the full-year revenue growth. It is that the quarter chain keeps reinforcing recommerce, collectibles, live shopping, C2C fashion, and advertising as the practical mechanisms of growth.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, eBay is a strong archive addition for understanding how a marketplace without a thick membership bundle can still create sticky behavior through category culture, supply depth, and seller-buyer interaction density.
