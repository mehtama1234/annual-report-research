## What changed

- eBay is a strong archive addition because it adds a commerce-marketplace operator whose behavior is driven by enthusiast demand, resale, trust, and seller density rather than by owned-inventory scale or a thick paid-membership bundle.
- The annual and quarter materials repeatedly point to the same operating shape: collectibles, pre-loved fashion, C2C activity, recommerce, advertising, and category-led discovery matter more than a generic "online retail" description.
- The quarter chain shows the monetization layer getting thicker. Advertising revenue went from `$544M` in Q4 `2025` to `$581M` in Q1 `2026` and `$596M` in Q2 `2026`.
- The cultural angle is also important. eBay is not only moving transactions; it is leaning into collector behavior, live shopping, vintage fashion, cars and parts, and other identity-heavy categories where the marketplace itself becomes the discovery venue.
- The most useful bridge to downstream theme work is that eBay sits between retail, media, and platform economics while AnnualReports still classifies it under `Consumer Goods`, making it another source-taxonomy mismatch that is analytically productive rather than noisy.

## Open questions

- How durable is eBay's recent growth if collectibles and pre-loved fashion enthusiasm cools?
- Does the Depop acquisition deepen the marketplace structurally with younger fashion users, or does it mainly broaden category reach without changing the core economics?
- Compared with Amazon, is eBay better understood as a lighter marketplace with stronger cultural category depth but weaker everyday habit formation?
