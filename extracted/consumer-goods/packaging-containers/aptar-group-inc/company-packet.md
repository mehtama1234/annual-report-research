## Annual report takeaways

- AptarGroup is the cleanest current extension for the behind-the-shelf consumer infrastructure lane because it adds packaging-interface control: dispensing, dosing, sealing, protection, closures, and drug-delivery components that sit between the packaged product and the user's actual dose, spray, pump, squeeze, pour, or application behavior.
- AnnualReports classified AptarGroup under `Consumer Goods / Packaging & Containers` and surfaced `2025 Annual Report and Form 10K` as the most recent annual package as of Monday, August 10, 2026.
- The direct investor-relations site was Cloudflare challenge-blocked in this shell environment, so the authoritative source chain for the packet is the AnnualReports company page plus SEC primary filings and SEC-hosted earnings-release exhibits.
- Aptar says it is a global leader in drug and consumer product dosing, dispensing, and protection technologies, serving pharmaceutical, beauty, food, beverage, personal-care, and home-care markets.
- Full-year `2025` net sales were `$3.777 billion`, net income was `$392.5 million`, diluted EPS was `$5.89`, operating income was `$501.0 million`, and adjusted EBITDA margin was `21.6%`.
- Segment mix makes the blind spot explicit: `Pharma` represented about `46%` of 2025 sales and `69%` of adjusted EBITDA excluding non-allocated corporate costs, `Beauty` represented about `35%` of sales, and `Closures` represented about `19%`.
- This is not generic packaging substrate or carton conversion. Aptar's control layer is small, engineered, and usage-critical: nasal drug delivery, injectable elastomeric components, metered-dose inhaler valves, fragrance and skincare pumps, dispensing closures, flow-control systems, food closures, beverage closures, and active material science solutions.
- The consumer and societal read is that everyday goods increasingly depend on controlled user interfaces: measured medication, hygienic dispensing, premium fragrance application, clean no-drip food dispensing, beverage convenience, ophthalmic and nasal delivery, and product protection across e-commerce and household use.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` reported sales increased `6%` to `$1.03 billion`, crossing `$1 billion` in quarterly sales for the first time. Reported net income was `$88 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.36`, adjusted EPS was `$1.42`, and adjusted EBITDA margin was `20.7%`.
- Segment picture: reported sales growth was `4%` in `Pharma`, `10%` in `Beauty`, and `7%` in `Closures`; core sales growth was `1%`, `1%`, and `4%`, respectively.
- Management message: Aptar cited double-digit growth in consumer healthcare, high-single-digit growth in injectables and prescription excluding emergency medicine, prestige fragrance demand, and continued strength in beverage dispensing.
- Structural themes: Q2 is the clearest proof that Aptar belongs in the blind-spot lane as packaging-interface and drug-delivery control infrastructure rather than as another packaging-container company. Growth was visible in consumer healthcare, GLP-1 and biologics-related components, prestige fragrance dispensing, beverage dispensing, and eye-care or nasal-decongestant applications.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` reported sales increased `11%` to `$982.9 million`, reported net income was `$73 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.12`, adjusted EPS was `$1.19`, and adjusted EBITDA margin was `19.2%`.
- Segment picture: reported sales growth was `7%` in `Pharma`, `19%` in `Beauty`, and `5%` in `Closures`; core sales growth was `(1)%`, `3%`, and `0%`, respectively.
- Management message: management highlighted growing demand in `GLP-1` therapies, biologics, systemic nasal drug delivery, nasal decongestants, ophthalmic dispensing, active material solutions, prestige fragrance, and beverage applications, while emergency medicine destocking pressured the quarter.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows how the same company can be tied to multiple behavior systems at once: medicine adherence and delivery, beauty identity and sensory application, beverage convenience, food and household dispensing, and packaging protection.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` reported sales increased `14%` to `$963 million`, core sales increased `5%`, reported net income was `$74 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.13`, adjusted EPS was `$1.25`, and adjusted EBITDA margin was `19.8%`.
- Segment picture: reported sales growth was `10%` in `Pharma`, `24%` in `Beauty`, and `5%` in `Closures`; core sales growth was `4%`, `10%`, and `1%`, respectively.
- Full-year frame: full-year `2025` sales increased `5%` to `$3.777 billion`, core sales increased `2%`, and the company returned `$486 million` to shareholders through dividends and repurchases.
- Structural themes: Q4 shows the interface-control thesis across all three segments. Pharma growth was driven by injectable components, systemic nasal drug delivery, asthma treatments, and consumer healthcare recovery; Beauty growth reflected fragrance and dispensing technologies; Closures benefited from food and beverage dispensing.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Aptar broadens `Consumer Goods / Packaging & Containers` beyond substrate, carton conversion, and broad container formats into the engineered interface that controls how a packaged product is dispensed, dosed, sealed, protected, and experienced.
- Consumer: direct consumer behavior appears through use moments: pump, spray, dose, apply, squeeze, pour, reclose, protect, and comply with a treatment or routine.
- Cultural: the relevant cultural layer is controlled convenience and trust. Prestige fragrance, skincare, eye care, nasal decongestants, food dispensing, and beverage closures are all places where packaging interface shapes the product experience.
- Societal: Aptar links ordinary consumer packaging to health-adjacent infrastructure because drug delivery, injectable components, ophthalmic dispensing, nasal delivery, and consumer-health applications are central parts of the portfolio.
- Industrial: the important operating questions are clean-room manufacturing, regulatory requirements, proprietary component design, raw-material and resin exposure, supplier concentration, customer qualification cycles, product launches, litigation or IP protection, and the ability to scale engineered components across global customers.
- Capital allocation: the company continues to spend on capex, acquisitions, dividends, and repurchases, but the key strategic asset is not only plant capacity. It is product-interface know-how, customer qualification, and embedded position inside customers' packaged goods and therapies.

## Why this company matters for the blind-spot lane

- `Graphic Packaging` already anchors consumer-facing packaging conversion.
- `Sonoco` already anchors broader packaging-format and container-control infrastructure.
- `Clearwater Paper` already anchors SBS paperboard substrate.
- `International Paper` already anchors corrugated and containerboard throughput.
- `AptarGroup` is the best current extension because it adds the control-rich packaging interface: closures, pumps, sprays, valves, elastomeric drug-delivery components, active material protection, and dosing systems.
- That makes the useful comparison `conversion` versus `broader packaging format` versus `SBS substrate` versus `corrugated throughput` versus `dispensing and dosing interface control`, not simply one more packaging company.
