# Ball Corporation Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Ball Corporation
- Sector: Consumer Goods
- Industry: Packaging & Containers
- Ticker: BALL
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Ball adds a distinct materials-processing and packaging-infrastructure packet to the branch. This is not paperboard or fiber packaging. It is a global aluminum-container system built around beverage cans, aerosol packaging, recloseable bottles, aluminum slugs, long-term customer contracts, metal pass-through, and circularity economics.
- The `2025` annual and release chain show a focused post-aerospace company. Full-year sales were `$13.16 billion`, net earnings attributable to the corporation were `$912 million`, diluted EPS was `$3.30`, comparable net earnings were `$985 million`, and comparable diluted EPS were `$3.57`.
- Management's `2025` framing was strongly cash- and return-oriented. Ball generated record adjusted free cash flow of `$956 million` and returned `$1.54 billion` to shareholders through repurchases and dividends in `2025`.
- The annual filing shows a company positioning aluminum itself as the strategic moat. Ball describes itself as one of the world's leading suppliers of aluminum packaging for beverage, personal care, and household products, with long-term customer relationships and a strategy built around scale, sustainability, customer proximity, and complexity management.
- This makes Ball useful well beyond simple packaging coverage. It is an infrastructure layer behind beverage distribution, personal-care refill and aerosol systems, household-products throughput, and the wider shift toward recyclable and circular packaging.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` sales were `$4.00 billion`, U.S. GAAP diluted EPS were `83 cents`, comparable diluted EPS were `$1.03`, and comparable operating earnings were `$433 million`.
- Throughput read: global aluminum packaging shipments increased `4.3%` in the second quarter.
- Capital-return read: Ball said it returned `$222 million` to shareholders via repurchases and dividends in the first six months of `2026` and remained on track to return at least `$800 million` by year-end.
- Management read: management attributed the quarter to higher volumes, strong customer partnerships, disciplined commercial and operational execution, and the resilience of a business model built over decades.
- Structural read: Ball highlighted the continuing importance of contractual pass-through mechanisms, which matters because this is a packaging name whose economics are heavily shaped by aluminum cost pass-through rather than only by unit growth.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` sales were `$3.60 billion`, U.S. GAAP diluted EPS were `77 cents`, comparable diluted EPS were `94 cents`, and comparable operating earnings were `$387 million`.
- Throughput read: global aluminum packaging shipments increased `0.8%` in the first quarter.
- Guidance read: management stayed on track for `10-plus percent` comparable diluted EPS growth and free cash flow greater than `$900 million` in `2026`, while expecting to return at least `$800 million` to shareholders.
- Operating-model read: the quarter also formalized changes to segment reporting and profitability measures, reinforcing that Ball wants investors to focus on operating earnings and throughput rather than legacy corporate structure noise.
- Interpretation: `Q1 2026` shows Ball as a stable industrial-conversion system. The main questions are not demand collapse or turnaround survival, but volume cadence, operating leverage, and how effectively the company converts scale into cash returns.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` sales were `$3.35 billion`, U.S. GAAP diluted EPS were `75 cents`, comparable diluted EPS were `91 cents`, and comparable net earnings were `$243 million`.
- Full-year read: `2025` comparable net earnings were `$985 million`, or `$3.57` per diluted share, and adjusted free cash flow reached a record `$956 million`.
- Shareholder-return read: Ball returned about `$1.54 billion` to shareholders through repurchases and dividends during `2025`.
- Strategic read: management said `2025` was a record year for Ball after the business simplification around aerospace, with robust volume growth, operating earnings, and stronger focus on the Ball Business System and operational excellence.
- Working interpretation: `Q4 2025` is the cleanest expression of Ball's role in the archive. It is a global aluminum-packaging conversion and distribution layer where circular-material positioning, customer retention, throughput efficiency, and pricing pass-through all matter more than consumer-brand marketing.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Ball expands the branch from extraction, chemicals, and heavy industrial inputs into the packaging-conversion layer where raw aluminum becomes recurring consumer and household infrastructure.
- Consumer: this is not a branded demand packet, but it sits directly behind beverage, personal-care, and household-product consumption. If drink volumes, can mix, and packaging format preferences move, Ball feels it quickly.
- Cultural and societal: Ball is one of the cleaner packets for the social shift toward circularity, recyclability, and packaging formats that can satisfy both regulators and consumer sustainability expectations without giving up scale.
- Industrial: the company shows how a materials-processing business can convert operational discipline, plant utilization, metal pass-through, and long-term contracts into high cash generation even without dramatic organic growth.
- Commodity exposure: aluminum prices matter, but Ball's contractual pass-through mechanisms mean the real question is not raw commodity direction alone. It is whether throughput, mix, and manufacturing execution stay strong enough to preserve margins and cash conversion.
- Capital allocation: Ball is a strong proof that post-divestiture simplification can sharpen a business. The archive now has another case where management is explicitly steering the company toward disciplined capex, free-cash-flow delivery, and shareholder returns rather than empire building.
- Comparative read: `Graphic Packaging` is the fiber-based packaging middleware packet exposed to affordability pressure and capital-reset drag. `Ball` is the cleaner aluminum-packaging throughput and circularity packet, where scale, metal pass-through, and customer-relationship depth matter more than paperboard project cleanup.

## Working interpretation

- Ball materially improves the branch because it adds the aluminum-container and packaging-conversion layer that sits between primary materials and the shelf.
- The packet works best when the company is read in three layers:
  - a global beverage-can and aluminum-packaging throughput system
  - a circularity and sustainability platform aligned with regulatory and consumer packaging shifts
  - a post-divestiture capital-allocation story centered on free cash flow, disciplined capex, and shareholder returns
- That makes Ball one of the better bridges between raw-material systems, logistics, consumer behavior, and industrial cash conversion. It is not just a packaging label. It is part of the physical delivery architecture behind modern beverage and household consumption.
