## What changed

- Ball is now a focused aluminum-packaging packet rather than a legacy packaging-plus-aerospace story. The aerospace business had already been divested, so the `2025` and `2026` read is about beverage cans, aerosol containers, recloseable bottles, slugs, customer partnerships, and circularity-driven packaging demand.
- The annual `2025` materials show a company in a strong post-divestiture cash-harvest and capital-return phase. Sales were `$13.16B`, net earnings attributable to the corporation were `$912M`, comparable net earnings were `$985M`, and adjusted free cash flow was a record `$956M`.
- The quarterly chain then showed continued earnings quality in `2026`, with comparable operating earnings of `$387M` in `Q1 2026` and `$433M` in `Q2 2026`, plus maintained guidance for `10-plus percent` comparable diluted EPS growth and free cash flow greater than `$900M`.
- Ball remains exposed to classic packaging variables like aluminum pass-through, customer volume, regional mix, plant start-up costs, and logistics execution, but the current management tone is much more about disciplined throughput, operating leverage, and shareholder returns than about rescue or restructuring.

## Open questions

- How durable is aluminum-substitution and circular-packaging demand if consumer beverage growth softens?
- Can Ball sustain high capital returns while still funding capacity and innovation projects in a competitive global can market?
- How much of the current strength is structural post-aerospace simplification versus a favorable point in the packaging-throughput cycle?
