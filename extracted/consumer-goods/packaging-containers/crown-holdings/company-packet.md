# Crown Holdings, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Crown Holdings, Inc.
- Sector: Consumer Goods
- Industry: Packaging & Containers
- Ticker: CCK
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Crown adds a distinct packaging-infrastructure packet to the branch. This is not just another beverage can name. It is a broader rigid-packaging and packaging-equipment system spanning beverage cans, food cans, aerosol cans, closures, beverage tooling, and transit packaging.
- The `2025` annual and filing chain show a company improving earnings while reducing leverage and continuing to build beverage-can capacity. Full-year net sales were `$12.365 billion`, income from operations was `$1.553 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was a best-ever `$2.092 billion`, net debt was about `$5.2 billion`, and adjusted net leverage finished at `2.5x`.
- Management's annual framing was `Positioned to deliver`, which fits the actual operating read. Crown is not being presented as a turnaround or a fragile packaging cyclical. It is being framed as a scaled converter with enough cash flow to expand selectively, keep deleveraging discipline, and return capital.
- The annual packet also makes Crown useful beyond consumer-goods taxonomy. The company sits inside the physical throughput layer behind global beverage, food, aerosol, and industrial distribution systems, where metal costs, energy, freight, sourcing flexibility, and plant placement matter as much as consumer end demand.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net sales were `$3.668 billion`, income from operations was `$464 million`, segment income was `$501 million`, net income attributable to Crown was `$245 million`, diluted EPS was `$2.23`, and adjusted diluted EPS was `$2.49`.
- Throughput read: global beverage can volumes increased `5%`, reinforcing that Crown is still seeing healthy can demand across major geographies rather than only price-led revenue growth.
- Capital-allocation read: the company repurchased `$305 million` of shares during the quarter, had repurchased nearly `7%` of outstanding shares over the prior twelve months, and kept adjusted net leverage at `2.5x`.
- Guidance read: full-year `2026` adjusted diluted EPS guidance was increased to `$8.30` to `$8.50`, and expected adjusted free cash flow remained at least `$900 million` after capex of about `$550 million`.
- Structural read: Crown tied the quarter to capacity expansion and location choice, citing projects in Brazil, Greece, Spain, and Northern India and reiterating that customers and consumers increasingly prefer aluminum cans as a sustainable and responsible packaging option.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net sales were `$3.259 billion`, income from operations was `$365 million`, segment income was `$405 million`, net income attributable to Crown was `$175 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.56`, and adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.86`.
- Throughput read: global beverage shipments increased `5%`, led by Europe and Asia-Pacific, while North America was mixed early in the quarter because of winter-storm disruption before March strengthened.
- Cost and sourcing read: the company said it was still navigating headwinds tied to the Middle East conflict, but highlighted global sourcing flexibility and operating continuity across facilities.
- Guidance read: Crown reiterated full-year adjusted diluted EPS guidance of `$7.90` to `$8.30`, adjusted free cash flow of about `$900 million`, and capex around `$550 million`.
- Working interpretation: `Q1 2026` shows the business model clearly. The company can absorb uneven regional shipment patterns, pass through higher material costs, and still grow adjusted earnings if plant utilization and mix remain healthy.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` and full-year `2025` net sales were `$12.365 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.092 billion`, and adjusted net leverage ended the year at `2.5x`.
- Cash and balance-sheet read: Crown reduced total debt to `$5.964 billion`, cut net debt to about `$5.2 billion`, and positioned itself with more flexibility for both repurchases and selective growth spending.
- Strategic read: management emphasized beverage-can capacity expansion, better operating execution, and the continuing migration toward cans as a favored packaging format.
- Working interpretation: `Q4 2025` is the cleanest setup quarter for the packet. Crown closed `2025` with stronger earnings quality, lower leverage, and a clear argument that packaging infrastructure can still grow when it is tied to beverage throughput, material pass-through, and disciplined network investment rather than undifferentiated commodity containers.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Crown extends the branch from extraction, chemicals, and materials processing into the rigid-packaging conversion and distribution layer.
- Consumer: this is not a branded consumer packet, but it sits directly behind beverage, food, and household-product consumption. Changes in can mix, convenience demand, and packaging substitution show up quickly.
- Cultural and societal: Crown is one of the better packets for the continuing shift toward cans as a more accepted sustainability and recyclability format, especially where brands and retailers need high-volume packaging that can still fit circularity narratives.
- Industrial: the company shows how packaging behaves like industrial infrastructure. Profitability depends on plant footprint, line speed, sourcing flexibility, energy and freight conditions, foreign exchange, and the ability to pass through metal costs while keeping customers supplied.
- Commodity exposure: aluminum, steel, energy, and transportation all matter, but the main analytical question is not the raw commodity line alone. It is whether Crown can keep utilization high enough and its network flexible enough to convert those inputs into durable cash flow.
- Capital allocation: Crown is a useful case where capex and buybacks are both active at the same time. Management is not harvesting a stagnant box business. It is expanding in growth geographies while also shrinking the share count and defending leverage targets.
- Comparative read: `Graphic Packaging` is the fiber-based, paperboard, and foodservice middleware packet under affordability and cost pressure. `Ball` is the cleaner aluminum-container and circular-packaging throughput packet after aerospace simplification. `Crown` is the broader rigid-packaging infrastructure packet where beverage cans still lead, but food, aerosol, closures, equipment, and transit packaging make the system more diversified and more operationally complex.

## Working interpretation

- Crown improves the branch because it adds the broader rigid-packaging layer between heavy inputs and the shelf.
- The packet works best when the company is read in three layers:
  - a global beverage-can demand and capacity-expansion system
  - a broader rigid-packaging and packaging-equipment network spanning food, aerosol, closures, and transit packaging
  - a cash-generation and leverage-discipline story where capex, buybacks, and packaging substitution all interact
- That makes Crown one of the better bridges between metals, manufacturing, transport, beverage and food throughput, and consumer-adjacent sustainability narratives. It is not just another container company. It is part of the industrial delivery architecture behind recurring consumption.
