## What changed

- Crown reads as a broader rigid-packaging infrastructure platform than Ball. Beverage cans remain the main growth engine, but the company also spans food cans, aerosol cans, closures, beverage equipment, and transit packaging.
- The `2025` annual materials show a company that improved earnings while continuing to reduce leverage and expand beverage-can capacity. Full-year net sales were `$12.365B`, income from operations was `$1.553B`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.092B`, and net debt fell to about `$5.2B`.
- The `2026` quarter chain reinforced the same structure. `Q1 2026` showed stronger beverage shipments, higher material pass-through, and a maintained growth plan despite Middle East-related sourcing and freight friction. `Q2 2026` then showed sharper operating leverage, larger buybacks, and a guidance increase.
- Crown remains exposed to metal costs, energy, transport, FX, and volume swings, but the more important read is that packaging here behaves like industrial infrastructure behind beverage and food systems rather than a simple low-growth container label.

## Open questions

- How durable is global beverage-can growth once recent capacity additions move from ramp mode to normal utilization?
- Can Crown keep both leverage discipline and aggressive buybacks while still funding its global can-capacity program?
- How much margin upside still depends on favorable mix in Europe and Asia versus a broader structural shift toward cans?
