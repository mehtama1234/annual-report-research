## What changed

- Revenue: full-year 2025 net sales were `$8.617B`; quarterly sales then moved from `$2.103B` in Q4 2025 to `$2.156B` in Q1 2026 and `$2.188B` in Q2 2026.
- Margin: profitability compressed sharply. Full-year adjusted EBITDA fell to `$1.395B` in 2025, Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA fell to `$232M`, and Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA was only `$247M` as inflation and weaker price-volume mix stayed heavy.
- Guidance: management began 2026 with adjusted cash-flow guidance of `$700M` to `$800M`, then cut that range in Q2 2026 to `$600M` to `$700M` while also lowering adjusted EPS.
- Capital allocation: the company is still paying dividends, but the emphasis has shifted toward debt reduction, working-capital release, lower capital spending, and footprint optimization after the Waco buildout.
- Segment mix: the business is a packaging-infrastructure layer serving food, beverage, foodservice, household products, beauty, and health care rather than a pure branded-consumer franchise.
- Operational issues: affordability pressure at consumer-brand customers is flowing upstream into packaging price-volume mix, while inflation and excess footprint costs are forcing closures, divestitures, and structural cost actions.
- Consumer / cultural signals: sustainable fiber-based packaging, foodservice formats, convenience, and the physical presentation of everyday consumer products remain central, but the tone is much more about cost takeout and free-cash-flow discipline than about premiumization.
- Management tone: management is defensive and operational, focused on cost reduction, inventory control, capital avoidance, debt paydown, and portfolio review rather than growth-led expansion.

## Open questions

- How much of GPK's weaker 2026 margin profile is cyclical packaging demand pressure versus a longer-lived reset in containerboard and folding-carton economics?
- Does the Waco project ultimately strengthen the moat enough to offset the near-term capital burden and footprint dislocation?
