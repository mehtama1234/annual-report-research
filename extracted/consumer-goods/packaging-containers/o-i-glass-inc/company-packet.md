# O-I Glass, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: O-I Glass, Inc.
- Sector: Consumer Goods
- Industry: Packaging & Containers
- Ticker: OI
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- O-I adds the glass-container version of packaging infrastructure. This is not paperboard conversion, and it is not just another aluminum-can packet. It is a global glass-manufacturing and furnace-utilization system tied to beverage, food, and pharmaceutical containers, where network density, energy intensity, customer proximity, and plant optimization matter constantly.
- The `2025` annual report showed a large global footprint: `64` manufacturing plants across `18` countries, with reportable segments in `Americas` and `Europe`. The company explicitly competes not just with other glass makers but also with aluminum cans and plastic containers.
- Management's central framework was `Fit to Win`, `Profitable Growth`, and `Strategic Optionality`. That already signals that the right lens is not simple unit growth. The company was trying to reduce redundant capacity, improve network performance, and reset its earnings base.
- `2025` was a genuine improvement year before the `2026` Europe deterioration became more obvious. Net sales were `$6.426B`, segment operating profit rose to `$846M` from `$748M`, adjusted EPS rose to `$1.60` from `$0.81`, cash from operating activities was `$600M`, and free cash flow improved to `$168M` from negative free cash flow in `2024`.
- Fit to Win delivered about `$300M` of benefits in `2025`, and management initially lifted the cumulative multi-year target to at least `$750M`. That matters because O-I is best understood as a packaging company whose economics are shaped heavily by asset configuration, cost structure, and furnace economics rather than just consumer brand strength downstream.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net sales were `$1.668B` versus `$1.706B` a year earlier, segment operating profit fell to `$171M` from `$225M`, adjusted EPS fell to `$0.09` from `$0.53`, and sales volumes declined `4.5%`.
- Europe stress read: the quarter was dominated by Europe. O-I recorded an `$873M` goodwill impairment charge and a further `$96M` increase in deferred-tax valuation allowances, both tied to weaker European expectations.
- Segment split: `Americas` segment operating profit rose to `$165M` with margins of `17.4%`, while `Europe` segment operating profit collapsed to only `$6M` with margins of `0.9%`.
- Guidance read: O-I cut `2026` adjusted EBITDA guidance again to `$1.0B` to `$1.1B`, moved free-cash-flow guidance to `($50M)` to `$150M`, removed adjusted-EPS guidance because the effective tax rate had become too unstable at lower earnings levels, and reset the cumulative net Fit to Win target to `$650M` from the earlier `$750M`.
- Working interpretation: `Q2 2026` shows why glass deserves its own packaging lane. The combination of energy intensity, furnace disruption, local freight constraints, and regional pricing pressure can produce a much harsher earnings swing than a simpler packaging label would imply.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net sales were `$1.540B` versus `$1.567B`, segment operating profit fell to `$142M` from `$209M`, reported diluted EPS was `(0.48)` versus `(0.10)`, and adjusted EPS fell to `$0.05` from `$0.40`.
- Volume read: shipments declined `8%`, though management said March volumes were down only `2%`, suggesting some intra-quarter stabilization.
- Fit to Win read: the program still delivered benefits, with roughly `$50M` gross and `$35M` net in the quarter.
- Cost-and-pricing read: management revised `2026` guidance lower because of higher global energy costs and worsening European net price pressure. O-I also noted that roughly `75%` to `80%` of `2026` European gas needs were secured at favorable prices, which shows how central energy procurement is to the model.
- Interpretation: `Q1 2026` already made clear that O-I was no longer just executing a clean restructuring story. The company was trying to preserve the benefits of Fit to Win while energy, competition, and utilization pressure worked against it.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: full-year `2025` net sales were `$6.426B`, reported EPS was `(0.84)`, adjusted EPS was `$1.60`, segment operating profit was `$846M`, cash from operating activities was `$600M`, and free cash flow was `$168M`.
- Improvement read: management described `2025` as a significantly improved year, driven partly by about `$300M` of Fit to Win benefits.
- Forward-cost read: even at the annual close, the company warned that `2026` would absorb about a `$150M` step-up in energy costs after favorable European energy contracts expired at the end of `2025`.
- Strategic read: this is the cleanest annual frame for the company. O-I looked like a network-optimization and margin-repair story with real progress, but the underlying manufacturing model was still highly exposed to energy and regional price pressure.
- Working interpretation: `Q4 2025` and the `2025` annual report show that O-I is not just a packaging producer. It is an energy-intensive industrial system where furnace uptime, local customer density, energy procurement, and restructuring discipline all sit between the commodity input base and branded products on the shelf.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: O-I broadens packaging coverage beyond fiber and aluminum into the glass-container layer, which is one of the clearest ways to connect heavy industrial operations to everyday consumer and beverage demand.
- Consumer: glass sits behind alcoholic beverages, food preservation, nonalcoholic drinks, and some pharmaceutical products. Consumer demand matters, but it reaches O-I through packaging-format choice and customer plant economics rather than direct brand marketing.
- Cultural and societal: glass has a strong premium, inert, and recyclable identity. It often aligns with brand heritage, flavor protection, refillability, and sustainability claims. But the packet also shows that circular-material narratives do not guarantee easy economics.
- Industrial: O-I is one of the better packets for furnace utilization, restructuring execution, local freight constraints, and the difference between a packaging format that looks attractive at retail and one that is brutally energy-intensive to manufacture at scale.
- Commodity and energy exposure: energy is not background noise here. Management explicitly indicated that energy can represent `10%` to `20%` of manufacturing costs, especially in Europe. That makes procurement, contract rollover, and regional power conditions central earnings drivers.
- Comparative read: `Ball` is the cleaner aluminum-container throughput and circularity packet, and `Crown` is the broader rigid-packaging infrastructure packet. `O-I Glass` adds the glass-furnace version, where utilization, energy reset, and premium-format competition with cans and plastics define the cycle much more directly.
- Capital intensity: this packet is a good reminder that packaging is still heavy industry. O-I expected about `$425M` of capital spending in `2026`, while the first half of `2026` already showed weaker operating cash flow and higher financial stress.

## Working interpretation

- O-I materially improves the branch because it separates glass from the rest of packaging. A rigid-container market can look similar from far away while actually running on very different physical economics.
- The packet works best when the company is read in three layers:
  - a global glass-container manufacturing network
  - a furnace-, energy-, and utilization-sensitive industrial system
  - a premium-format and circularity story that still has to survive brutal regional cost and pricing realities
- That makes O-I one of the better bridge packets between heavy inputs, energy exposure, local transport constraints, and the consumer-facing shelf. It is not just packaging. It is a reminder that even a recyclable, brand-friendly container format can still behave like hard manufacturing.
