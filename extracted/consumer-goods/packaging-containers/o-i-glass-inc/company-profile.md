## What changed

- O-I entered `2025` and early `2026` as a restructuring and network-optimization story rather than a simple volume-growth packaging name. Fit to Win was already the core management narrative, with the company targeting redundant-capacity removal, cost resets, and a more profitable manufacturing footprint.
- `2025` looked like a meaningful improvement year. Net sales were `$6.426B`, segment operating profit rose to `$846M` from `$748M`, adjusted EPS rose to `$1.60` from `$0.81`, and free cash flow turned positive at `$168M` versus negative free cash flow in `2024`.
- `2026` then split sharply by region. The `Americas` segment held up comparatively well, while `Europe` saw much heavier pressure from competitive pricing, higher energy costs after favorable contracts expired, and restructuring or furnace disruption.
- By `Q2 2026`, the reported picture had deteriorated enough that O-I took an `$873M` goodwill impairment and a further `$96M` deferred-tax valuation-allowance increase tied to Europe, while removing adjusted-EPS guidance because tax-rate sensitivity had become too unstable at lower earnings levels.

## Open questions

- Can O-I stabilize European margins without giving up too much volume in a more competitive pricing environment?
- How much of the current pressure is cyclical energy and pricing stress versus a deeper structural issue in the European glass-container footprint?
- Does Fit to Win remain a durable margin-reset program, or does it become partially absorbed by a harsher energy and utilization backdrop?
