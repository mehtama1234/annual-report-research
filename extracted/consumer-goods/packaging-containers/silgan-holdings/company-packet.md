## Annual report takeaways

- Silgan adds the multi-format consumer-staples packaging system. This is not just cans, and it is not just closures. It is a broad rigid-packaging network spanning `Dispensing and Specialty Closures`, `Metal Containers`, and `Custom Containers`, with exposure to fragrance, beauty, food, beverage, personal care, health care, home care, lawn and garden, and pet food.
- The `2025` annual filing showed a business with approximately `$6.5B` of net sales and `121` manufacturing plants across North America, Europe, Asia, and South America. That makes Silgan one of the clearer packets for seeing how multiple packaging substrates and end markets can sit inside one disciplined operating and capital-allocation model.
- The company is not a generic packaging conglomerate. Its strongest higher-value lane is dispensing and specialty closures, but it also remains a major food-can and metal-container platform and a meaningful custom-container supplier. In North America, the `10-K` said Silgan remained the largest manufacturer of metal food containers in the United States with more than half of the market by unit volume in `2025`.
- `2025` was a growth year with meaningful portfolio reshaping benefits. Net income rose to `$288.4M` from `$276.4M`, diluted EPS rose to `$2.70` from `$2.58`, adjusted EPS rose to `$3.72` from `$3.62`, and free cash flow rose to `$445.2M` from `$391.3M`.
- The annual filing also showed where the value creation is concentrating. The `Dispensing and Specialty Closures` segment produced `$2.7B` of sales and `$321.5M` of EBIT in `2025`, or roughly half of consolidated EBIT excluding corporate expense. That is important because Silgan is increasingly not just a staple food-can story. It is also a packaging-components and dispensing-technology system.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net sales were `$1.64B` versus `$1.54B`, net income was `$75.8M` versus `$89.0M`, diluted EPS was `$0.72` versus `$0.83`, adjusted EPS was `$0.98` versus `$1.01`, and EBIT was `$151.0M` versus `$167.5M`.
- Segment split: segment EBIT in `Q2 2026` was `$85.5M` in `Dispensing and Specialty Closures`, `$55.4M` in `Metal Containers`, and `$25.5M` in `Custom Containers`.
- Operating read: management highlighted high single-digit growth in dispensing products for fragrance markets, high single-digit growth in metal containers for pet food markets, and a new long-term vegetable-pack customer agreement.
- Guidance read: Silgan confirmed `2026` free cash flow guidance of about `$450M`, expected capital expenditures of about `$310M`, and guided `Q3 2026` adjusted EPS to `$1.21` to `$1.31`.
- Working interpretation: `Q2 2026` shows Silgan as a disciplined, mixed-format packaging platform where raw-material pass-through still supports sales growth, but profit cadence depends on segment mix, rationalization charges, and how much high-value dispensing growth can offset more mature container lanes.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net sales were `$1.56B` versus `$1.47B`, net income was `$63.0M` versus `$68.0M`, diluted EPS was `$0.60` versus `$0.63`, adjusted EPS was `$0.78` versus `$0.82`, and EBIT was `$126.6M` versus `$130.5M`.
- Segment split: segment EBIT in `Q1 2026` was `$77.3M` in `Dispensing and Specialty Closures`, `$45.0M` in `Metal Containers`, and `$19.9M` in `Custom Containers`.
- Operating read: management emphasized double-digit volume growth in dispensing products for fragrance and beauty markets and double-digit growth in metal containers for pet food markets.
- Guidance read: the company increased its `2026` earnings estimate while confirming `2026` free cash flow expectations, which matters because it signals confidence in the full portfolio despite only modest first-quarter profit pressure.
- Interpretation: `Q1 2026` is a good example of how Silgan works. It can show lower year-over-year EBIT and still look operationally healthy if the decline is driven by charges, mix, or timing while high-value dispensing and stable-staples demand remain intact.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: full-year `2025` net sales were `$6.5B`, net income was `$288.4M`, diluted EPS was `$2.70`, adjusted EPS was `$3.72`, and free cash flow was `$445.2M`.
- Quarter read: fourth-quarter `2025` net sales were `$1.47B`, EBIT was `$101.1M`, and segment EBIT was `$56.6M` in `Dispensing and Specialty Closures`, `$39.5M` in `Metal Containers`, and `$15.2M` in `Custom Containers`.
- Strategic read: management described `2025` as a year of progress on integrating `Weener`, driving growth in dispensing products, expanding pet-food exposure, completing a multi-year cost-savings program, and building another year of earnings and free-cash-flow growth in `2026`.
- Free-cash-flow read: free cash flow in `2025` reached `$445.2M`, above the prior estimate, and Silgan returned about `$160M` to shareholders while still funding internal growth and integration work.
- Working interpretation: `Q4 2025` and the full-year close make Silgan one of the more useful packaging packets in the archive because it shows how a staples-oriented packaging company can pair stable, cash-generative legacy container economics with a faster-growing, higher-value dispensing-components platform.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Silgan adds the packaging-components and closures layer that sits between consumer goods and industrial conversion. It is closer to the shelf than basic metals or chemicals, but it still behaves like hard manufacturing and procurement.
- Consumer: this is a packaging name with real end-market pattern exposure. Fragrance and beauty, pet food, home care, and beverage closures all behave differently, so Silgan is a strong packet for seeing how staples resilience and pockets of premiumization can coexist.
- Cultural and societal: the company is useful for the bigger packaging split between habitual staples consumption and identity-driven or higher-value usage occasions. Fragrance and beauty dispensing components are a different cultural system than steel cans for food preservation, yet both sit inside the same company.
- Industrial: Silgan is one of the cleaner examples of how packaging profitability is shaped by substrate pass-through, local manufacturing networks, closures and components complexity, customer-specific tooling relationships, and disciplined capacity and acquisition management.
- Commodity exposure: sales growth can be flattered by raw-material pass-through even when underlying profit growth is slower. That matters because Silgan should not be read only through top-line momentum.
- Comparative read: `Ball` is the cleaner aluminum-throughput packet, `Crown` is the broader rigid-packaging infrastructure packet, `O-I Glass` is the glass-furnace packet, and `Silgan` adds the mixed-format consumer-staples and dispensing-components packet where closures, pumps, food cans, pet food, and custom plastic containers all materially change the earnings model.
- Capital allocation: Silgan is a strong packet for disciplined portfolio building. The Weener integration, cost-savings execution, stable cash generation, and continued shareholder returns all show a packaging operator using free cash flow to compound rather than just defend a legacy substrate position.

## Working interpretation

- Silgan materially improves the branch because it breaks packaging into yet another real operating archetype: not just substrate conversion, but packaging components, closures, dispensing systems, and staple-food containment all inside one company.
- The packet works best when the company is read in three layers:
  - a broad consumer-staples packaging platform
  - a higher-value dispensing and closures franchise with more design and customer-embedded economics
  - a disciplined free-cash-flow allocator that uses portfolio breadth and integration execution to keep compounding
- That makes Silgan one of the better bridge packets between staples demand, premium product architecture, packaging pass-through economics, and industrial cash generation. It is not simply another can company. It is a multi-format packaging control point behind a large share of everyday consumer and food-product distribution.
