## Annual report takeaways

- Colgate's 2025 annual materials show a tightly focused daily-use consumer-goods system rather than a broad conglomerate. Full-year net sales reached `$20.382 billion`, organic sales rose `1.4%`, base business EPS rose `3%` to `$3.69`, and net cash provided by operations reached a record `$4.198 billion`.
- The annual 2025 mix matters: Oral Care accounted for `44%` of total net sales, Personal Care `17%`, Home Care `16%`, and the separate Hill's Pet Nutrition segment remains a major growth and category-quality asset.
- This is one of the clearest science-and-routine consumption stories in the consumer-goods set. Management repeatedly frames growth around brand penetration, science-based innovation, advertising support, and category leadership rather than price or broad portfolio reshaping alone.
- The 2025 package also shows concentration risk and channel reality. Walmart represented about `11%` of 2025 net sales, and lower private-label pet volume remains a recurring issue in the quarterlies.
- Compared with P&G and Kimberly-Clark, Colgate reads as narrower and more category-specialized. Compared with PepsiCo, it is much more directly tied to repeat-use hygiene and pet-care routines than to consumer snacking, hydration, or convenience trends.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 2026 net sales were `$5.361 billion`, organic sales grew `2.4%`, GAAP EPS was `$0.86`, base business EPS was `$0.99`, and gross profit margin reached `61.5%`.
- Guidance: on July 31, 2026, Colgate maintained sales and GAAP EPS guidance and updated gross-profit-margin and base-business EPS guidance for full-year 2026.
- Management message: management stressed balanced top-line growth, strong gross-margin expansion, continued category leadership, and disciplined advertising support.
- Structural themes: this quarter reinforces that everyday oral care and pet nutrition are still attractive defensive categories, but even here pricing and mix are doing important work alongside volume.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 2026 net sales were `$5.324 billion`, organic sales grew `2.9%`, GAAP EPS was `$0.80`, base business EPS was `$0.97`, and gross profit margin was `60.6%`.
- Guidance: Colgate maintained sales and earnings guidance and updated gross-margin guidance for full-year 2026.
- Management message: management called the quarter a strong start to 2026 with broad-based growth, better free cash flow, and year-over-year advertising spending increases.
- Structural themes: the quarter shows a fairly clean staples formula: balanced volume and pricing growth, category leadership, brand support through advertising, and steady operating execution.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 2025 net sales were `$5.230 billion`, organic sales rose `2.2%`, GAAP EPS was `$(0.05)`, base business EPS was `$0.95`, and gross profit margin was `60.2%`.
- Guidance: the January 30, 2026 release guided full-year 2026 net sales up `2%` to `6%`, organic sales growth of `1%` to `4%`, and included a low-single-digit positive foreign-exchange effect.
- Management message: management highlighted market-share leadership in toothpaste and manual toothbrushes and framed the skin-health impairment as the main reason GAAP EPS diverged from the underlying business trend.
- Structural themes: this is a good example of why base-business metrics matter in staples. Reported EPS looked weak because of impairment charges, but the operating business still showed steady growth, strong cash generation, and continued reinvestment capacity.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Colgate strengthens the case that consumer-goods resilience is strongest where demand is habitual, science-supported, and daily-use rather than discretionary.
- Consumer: oral care, personal hygiene, and specialty pet nutrition look structurally steadier than many other categories, but private-label pressure and channel mix still matter.
- Cultural: this is less a trend-driven brand story than a routine, trust, efficacy, and professional-adjacency story. The consumer signal is reliability and habit more than aspiration.
- Regulatory: tariffs matter less in the visible narrative here than at some peers, but gross-margin guidance updates and foreign-exchange assumptions still shape the quarter framing.
- Labor: labor is not a primary signal. Advertising support, innovation, and operating efficiency are much more visible than workforce themes.
- Technology: technology shows up mainly through science-based product development, digital marketing emphasis, and operating discipline rather than as a standalone customer-facing theme.
- Capital allocation: Colgate remains a clean cash compounder with strong operating cash flow, dividends, and repurchases, even when GAAP optics are temporarily distorted.
