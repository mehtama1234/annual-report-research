## What changed

- Revenue: full-year 2025 net sales reached `$20.382 billion`; quarterly net sales then moved from `$5.230 billion` in Q4 2025 to `$5.324 billion` in Q1 2026 and `$5.361 billion` in Q2 2026.
- Organic growth: full-year 2025 organic sales rose `1.4%`; Q4 2025 organic sales rose `2.2%`; Q1 2026 organic sales rose `2.9%`; and Q2 2026 organic sales rose `2.4%`, showing steady but not explosive growth.
- Margin: Q4 2025 gross profit margin was `60.2%`, Q1 2026 gross profit margin was `60.6%`, and Q2 2026 gross profit margin improved to `61.5%`, which is one of the cleanest margin profiles in the current consumer-goods set.
- Earnings quality: full-year 2025 GAAP EPS fell to `$2.63` because of skin-health goodwill and intangible impairment charges, but full-year base business EPS still rose `3%` to `$3.69`.
- Cash generation: net cash provided by operations reached a record `$4,198 million` in 2025 and `$1,742 million` in the first six months of 2026.
- Portfolio and mix: Colgate is more concentrated in oral care and pet nutrition than P&G, PepsiCo, or Kimberly-Clark. That makes it a useful signal for science-based daily-use categories rather than broad household breadth alone.
- Operational issues: lower private-label pet volume is a recurring drag in the filings, while Hill's still adds growth support and the Prime100 acquisition extends the portfolio into fresh pet food.
- Management tone: disciplined and steady. The recurring messages are balanced pricing and volume, category leadership, gross-margin management, advertising support, and dependable compounding rather than aggressive transformation rhetoric.

## Open questions

- How durable is Colgate's margin improvement if private-label pet pressure persists and pricing becomes less available as a lever?
- Does Colgate's oral-care and specialty-pet concentration make it more resilient than other personal-products peers, or just different from them?
