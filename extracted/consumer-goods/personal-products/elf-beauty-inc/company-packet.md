## Annual report takeaways

- e.l.f. Beauty is the strongest next blind-spot beauty case because it adds a brand-owner and community-shaped beauty interface rather than another staples platform or another beauty retailer. `Ulta` already gives the archive the discovery-and-loyalty retailer. e.l.f. Beauty adds the operator behind the brands, the pricing architecture, the retailer-plus-eCommerce mix, and the creator- and culture-facing growth engine.
- The source mismatch is already useful on its own. As of `2026-08-10`, AnnualReports.com still only showed a `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, while the local official IR and SEC chain already supported the current `2025` annual package and the in-scope `FY2027 Q1`, `FY2026 Q4`, and `FY2026 Q3` set.
- Full-year FY`2026` net sales increased `25%` to `$1,636.5 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$335.2 million`, or `20%` of net sales. Management also framed fiscal `2026` as the `7th consecutive year` of net sales and market share growth.
- The annual and full-year quarter chain make the platform widening explicit. Management said all five brands grew in fiscal `2026`, with `rhode` and `Naturium` particularly strong. That matters because the company is no longer just one low-price cosmetics label. It is becoming a broader beauty platform spanning value-access cosmetics, skincare, newer acquired brands, and creator-linked demand.
- This is analytically different from both the staples lane and the weaker beauty lane. `P&G`, `Kimberly-Clark`, and `Colgate` are routine-use trust systems. `Coty` is a more stressed and uneven beauty portfolio. e.l.f. Beauty instead reads as an accessible beauty growth platform where disruptive marketing, retailer and eCommerce execution, social and creator energy, and cultural relevance drive the numbers.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: FY`2027 Q1` net sales increased `36%` to `$479.4 million`; gross margin rose to `83%`; GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.12`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.75`; and adjusted EBITDA was `$168.2 million`, or `35%` of net sales.
- Balance sheet: as of `2026-06-30`, cash and cash equivalents were `$344.2 million` and total debt was `$834.2 million`.
- Management message: management said the quarter marked the `30th consecutive quarter` of net sales growth and raised fiscal `2027` net sales guidance to `18%` to `20%` growth from `12%` to `14%` previously.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest current proof that e.l.f. Beauty is more than a shelf brand. The quarter was driven by strong retailer and eCommerce performance in the U.S. and internationally, while the filing also disclosed outperformance from `rhode` through the contingent-consideration revaluation.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: FY`2026 Q4` net sales increased `35%` to `$449.3 million`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.32`; and adjusted EBITDA was `$58.8 million`, or `13%` of net sales.
- Full-year anchor: the same release closed fiscal `2026` with net sales up `25%` to `$1,636.5 million`, adjusted diluted EPS of `$3.13`, and adjusted EBITDA of `$335.2 million`.
- Management message: management said fiscal `2026` marked the `7th consecutive year` of net sales and market share growth and that all five brands grew, with `rhode` and `Naturium` particularly strong.
- Structural themes: this is the key bridge quarter because it closes the annual picture. It shows a beauty company still compounding growth through accessibility, innovation, and brand widening rather than through prestige-only pricing or purely defensive routine-use demand.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: FY`2026 Q3` net sales increased `38%` to `$489.5 million`; GAAP diluted EPS was `$0.65`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.24`; and adjusted EBITDA was `$123.0 million`, or `25%` of net sales.
- Nine-month view: through the first nine months of FY`2026`, net sales increased `21%` to `$1,187.2 million` and adjusted EBITDA reached `$276.3 million`, or `23%` of net sales.
- Management message: management highlighted `130` basis points of market share gains for the core `e.l.f. Cosmetics` brand and called out a record-breaking `rhode` launch in Sephora in the U.K.
- Structural themes: Q3 is the clearest quarter for seeing the cultural engine directly. Market-share gains, retailer and eCommerce strength, international growth, and the `rhode` launch show that beauty demand here is being pulled by value-access positioning plus creator- and trend-aware brand expansion rather than by prestige scarcity alone.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer: e.l.f. Beauty is one of the archive's clearest examples that consumers are still spending aggressively in beauty when the mix is accessible, trend-aware, and culturally active rather than simply premium.
- Cultural: the company sits inside beauty not just as a product vendor but as a system for self-presentation, community, disruptive marketing, and social transmission. The `rhode` and `Naturium` expansion makes that wider than the legacy e.l.f. Cosmetics label alone.
- Channel structure: management repeatedly ties growth to both retailer and eCommerce channels. That makes e.l.f. Beauty a better blind-spot comparison for `Ulta` than another generic personal-products issuer, because both sides of the beauty relationship are now visible in the archive.
- International: recent quarters explicitly cite U.S. and international growth together, which makes the platform more than a domestic value-cosmetics story.
- Business model: this is a direct challenge to the idea that personal products should be read as a single defensive bucket. e.l.f. Beauty is more trend-sensitive, creator-sensitive, and execution-sensitive than routine-use staples, but it is also structurally stronger and more coherent than the weaker beauty repair stories.
- Capital and risk: the quarter chain shows strong growth but also a materially higher debt load and explicit contingent-consideration effects tied to the `rhode` acquisition. This is not a low-motion staples model.

## Working interpretation

- e.l.f. Beauty materially improves the archive because it closes the missing beauty-brand and community-interface gap between `Ulta` on the retail side and `Coty` / `Estee Lauder` on the portfolio side.
- It is one of the stronger proofs that the blind spot in `AnnualReports` is real: the formal label `Consumer Goods / Personal Products` says very little about a business being driven by accessibility, creator-linked heat, community, retailer-plus-eCommerce strength, and multi-brand beauty expansion.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, e.l.f. Beauty is strong evidence that accessible beauty, disruptive marketing, and community-shaped demand are still expanding quickly, even while other beauty and personal-products models remain more stressed or more defensive.
