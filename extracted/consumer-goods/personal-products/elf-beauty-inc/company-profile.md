## What changed

- Revenue: the recent chain is unusually strong even by beauty standards. FY`2026` net sales reached `$1,636.5 million`, FY`2026 Q4` net sales were `$449.3 million`, FY`2026 Q3` net sales were `$489.5 million`, and FY`2027 Q1` net sales were `$479.4 million`.
- Growth quality: management's framing is not just top-line growth. It is repeated market-share gains, retailer and eCommerce strength, international expansion, and a widening multi-brand platform.
- Brand structure: by FY`2026` year-end management said all five brands grew, with `rhode` and `Naturium` particularly strong. That makes e.l.f. Beauty a broader beauty platform rather than a single-label growth story.
- Margin and earnings: FY`2027 Q1` adjusted EBITDA was `$168.2 million`, or `35%` of net sales, and adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.75`. FY`2026` adjusted EBITDA was `$335.2 million`, or `20%` of full-year net sales.
- Channel and geography: multiple quarters explicitly tie performance to both retailer and eCommerce channels in the U.S. and internationally. That makes the company a useful bridge between beauty retail and beauty brand ownership.
- Capital and balance sheet: the latest quarter also shows a much larger debt base than the year-ago period, reflecting acquisition-linked platform widening rather than a low-motion staples profile.
- Consumer / cultural signals: this is one of the archive's clearest cases where accessibility, disruptive marketing, creator-linked demand, and beauty community formation are driving results faster than prestige-only or routine-use frameworks would suggest.

## Open questions

- How durable is the current growth rate once the latest `rhode` and tariff-refund effects normalize?
- Does e.l.f. Beauty become a broader multi-brand beauty platform with durable community power, or does the story remain concentrated in a few especially strong brands and launches?
