## Annual report takeaways

- Kimberly-Clark's 2025 annual materials show a hygiene and household-care staples platform still growing through portfolio reshaping: full-year net sales were `$16.4 billion`, reported net sales declined `2.1%`, and organic sales grew `1.7%`.
- The bridge between reported decline and organic growth matters. Management explicitly attributes the reported pressure to the PPE divestiture, the exit of the U.S. private-label diaper business, and foreign exchange rather than underlying consumer demand collapse.
- This is one of the clearest consumer-goods examples of a transformation-led staples story. The recurring management frame is `Powering Care`, which links innovation, productivity, overhead efficiency, and category leadership into a durable-growth model.
- Kimberly-Clark reads as more focused than P&G and less category-diverse than PepsiCo, which makes it useful as a test of whether the consumer-goods resilience pattern still holds in a narrower hygiene and personal-care portfolio.
- The annual and quarterlies also show a more explicit value-spectrum posture. Management repeatedly references investing in trial, improving price:value tiers, and supporting broad consumer access rather than leaning on premiumization alone.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 2026 net sales were `$4.2 billion`, gross margin was `38.3%`, adjusted gross margin was `38.8%`, operating profit was `$633 million`, adjusted operating profit was `$757 million`, diluted EPS attributable to Kimberly-Clark was `$1.04`, and adjusted EPS attributable to Kimberly-Clark was `$2.12`.
- Guidance: on August 4, 2026, Kimberly-Clark updated 2026 outlook while still describing strong base-business momentum.
- Management message: management emphasized sustained innovation-driven volume-plus-mix gains, industry-leading productivity, and strategic transformation progress, but also called out a discrete China social-media disruption as a meaningful near-term headwind.
- Structural themes: this quarter shows the sector's current pattern clearly. Brand trust, value tiers, and productivity still support earnings, but local reputation shocks and channel-specific disruption can now hit results quickly.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 2026 net sales were `$4.2 billion`, organic sales grew `2.5%`, gross margin was `36.8%`, adjusted gross margin was `37.9%`, diluted EPS attributable to Kimberly-Clark was `$2.00`, and adjusted EPS attributable to Kimberly-Clark was `$1.97`.
- Guidance: Kimberly-Clark reaffirmed 2026 outlook in the April 28, 2026 release.
- Management message: management said the company was delivering resilient demand, strong volume-plus-mix, consumer-inspired innovation, and meaningful cost savings while preparing for a larger strategic expansion through Kenvue.
- Structural themes: this is the cleanest quarter for the current Kimberly-Clark set. It reinforces that personal-products growth can still be volume-plus-mix led when innovation and value architecture line up.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 2025 net sales were `$4.1 billion`, organic sales grew `2.1%`, gross margin was `35.9%`, adjusted gross margin was `37.0%`, and adjusted EPS attributable to Kimberly-Clark was `$1.86`.
- Guidance: management used the Q4 2025 package to frame 2026 around balanced net sales, continued adjusted-EPS growth, and cash flow in line with the Powering Care algorithm.
- Management message: the pre-recorded management discussion repeatedly describes Powering Care as creating a virtuous cycle of growth, with approximately `$2 billion` of cash flow and leverage below `2.0x` net debt-to-EBITDA.
- Structural themes: this is the best evidence that Kimberly-Clark is using transformation not just as cost cutting, but as a way to improve innovation cadence, fund commercial spend, and widen operating resilience.

Note: for many companies this third slot will be a late-2025 quarter needed to complete the trailing three-quarter set as of 2026-08-08.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Kimberly-Clark strengthens the view that consumer-goods resilience is still real, but increasingly tied to productivity, transformation, and value-tier management rather than loose pricing power.
- Consumer: the company is a strong read on necessity-led household demand. Consumers still buy care and hygiene essentials, but they are clearly sensitive to trust, value, and local perception shocks.
- Cultural: care, hygiene, safety, and household reliability are more central than fashion or indulgence themes. The China disruption also shows how social-media narratives can now become a direct operating issue even for staples categories.
- Regulatory: tariffs, transformation actions, and transaction-related restructuring are visible enough to affect quarter math and management tone.
- Labor: labor is not the primary disclosure theme. Overhead savings, organization wiring, and scalable capabilities are much more prominent.
- Technology: technology appears mainly through innovation systems, productivity, and next-generation materials rather than as a direct consumer-facing thesis.
- Capital allocation: the company still reads as disciplined on cash flow and leverage, but with more strategic-transformation content than a pure dividend-compounder story.
