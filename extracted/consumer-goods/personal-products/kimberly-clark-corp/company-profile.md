## What changed

- Revenue: full-year 2025 net sales were `$16.4 billion` with organic sales growth of `1.7%`; quarterly net sales then moved from `$4.1 billion` in Q4 2025 to `$4.2 billion` in Q1 2026 and `$4.2 billion` in Q2 2026.
- Margin: Q4 2025 adjusted gross margin reached `37.0%`; Q1 2026 adjusted gross margin was `37.9%`; Q2 2026 adjusted gross margin improved to `38.8%`, showing steady productivity-led margin expansion even with disruption.
- Guidance: management reaffirmed 2026 outlook in Q1 2026, then updated 2026 outlook in the August 4, 2026 Q2 release after factoring in discrete headwinds.
- Capital and cash model: the Q4 2025 management discussion says the company remains on an algorithm of approximately `$2 billion` of cash flow with leverage below `2.0x` net debt-to-EBITDA.
- Portfolio and value architecture: filings repeatedly describe investment in innovation, brand propositions, and better price:value tiers across the portfolio rather than relying on price alone.
- Operational issues: business exits, transformation charges, tariff items, and the China social-media disruption are all material enough to shape reported 2026 quarter framing.
- Consumer / cultural signals: hygiene, baby care, and household paper demand remain resilient, but the company is managing around trust, safety, value tiers, and broad care-category necessity rather than trend-heavy premium language.
- Management tone: disciplined and transformation-oriented. The recurring language is Powering Care, durable growth, productivity, and strategic repositioning.

## Open questions

- How much of Kimberly-Clark's 2026 margin strength is structural versus temporary support from one-time tariff refunds and transformation timing?
- Does the Powering Care playbook generalize across the wider personal-products group, or is Kimberly-Clark unusually well positioned because of category concentration and portfolio simplification?
