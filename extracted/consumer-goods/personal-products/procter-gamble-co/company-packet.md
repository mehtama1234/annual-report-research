# Procter & Gamble Co. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-08

## Snapshot

- Company: Procter & Gamble Co.
- Sector: Consumer Goods
- Industry: Personal Products
- Ticker: PG
- Fiscal year-end: June
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: FY2026 Q4
  - quarter 2: FY2026 Q3
  - quarter 3: FY2026 Q2

## Annual report takeaways

- P&G's 2025 annual materials show a high-quality staples platform still growing through a difficult backdrop: fiscal 2025 net sales were `$84.3 billion`, organic sales grew `2%`, core EPS grew `4%`, operating cash flow reached `$17.8 billion`, and adjusted free cash flow productivity was `87%`.
- Management framed 2025 as durable execution rather than breakout demand. The annual-report site highlights the `39th` consecutive quarter of top-line growth and the `9th` consecutive fiscal year of core EPS growth.
- The strategic language is consistent across the annual package and the later quarterlies: focused daily-use categories, superiority across product and value, productivity-funded reinvestment, and constructive disruption.
- This is a broad household and personal-care portfolio, so the analysis signal is less about single-category fashion and more about how a global staples system manages value, mix, and cash generation under pressure.
- Compared with PepsiCo, P&G reads as even more explicit about reinvestment discipline and shareholder return, with less dependence on health-forward consumer language and more dependence on brand superiority plus operating execution.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: FY2026 Q4 net sales were `$21.2 billion`, organic sales were unchanged, diluted EPS was `$1.26`, core EPS was `$1.43`, and adjusted free cash flow productivity was `133%`.
- Guidance: on July 29, 2026, P&G guided FY2027 all-in sales growth and organic sales growth to `1%` to `3%`, core EPS to `$6.89` to `$7.11`, adjusted free cash flow productivity to `85%` to `90%`, dividends around `$10 billion`, and buybacks near `$5 billion`.
- Management message: management said FY2026 was a year of foundation building and argued that fiscal 2027 progress will come from stronger integrated execution and productivity-funded investments despite continued volatility.
- Structural themes: this quarter introduces clearer friction around tariffs, reinvestment, and mix. Organic sales stalled even though reported sales still grew, which is a useful warning sign for consumer-goods breadth stories.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: FY2026 Q3 net sales were `$21.2 billion`, organic sales rose `3%`, diluted EPS was `$1.63`, core EPS was `$1.59`, operating cash flow was `$4.0 billion`, and adjusted free cash flow productivity was `82%`.
- Guidance: P&G maintained fiscal-2026 sales, EPS-growth, and cash-return guidance.
- Management message: management called out broad-based acceleration, with all ten categories and all regions growing, while also saying it was increasing investment to accelerate momentum.
- Structural themes: this is the cleanest proof point that the portfolio can still deliver broad-based growth when execution and category support line up. It also shows how heavily the model depends on productivity to fund incremental spend.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: FY2026 Q2 net sales were `$22.2 billion`, organic sales were unchanged, diluted EPS was `$1.78`, core EPS was `$1.88`, operating cash flow was `$5.0 billion`, and adjusted free cash flow productivity was `88%`.
- Guidance: P&G maintained fiscal-2026 sales, core-EPS, and cash-return guidance while updating GAAP EPS expectations for restructuring.
- Management message: management said the company was still on track despite significant U.S. headwinds and a challenging consumer and geopolitical environment.
- Structural themes: the quarter shows the core staples playbook clearly: hold the line on organic sales, defend earnings with productivity and portfolio breadth, and keep returning cash while waiting for a stronger second half.

Note: for many companies this third slot will be a late-2025 quarter needed to complete the trailing three-quarter set as of 2026-08-08.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: P&G reinforces that consumer-goods resilience is still intact, but growth is operationally managed and not especially loose or price-led.
- Consumer: value and affordability matter even for a premium brand portfolio. The company repeatedly references a challenging consumer backdrop and U.S. headwinds.
- Cultural: P&G's key cultural signal is not trend language but brand trust, daily-use necessity, and superior value. This is a more defensive household-staples posture than PepsiCo's hydration/protein/functional framing.
- Regulatory: tariffs, restructuring, and geopolitics are now visible enough to matter in earnings framing, especially by FY2026 Q4.
- Labor: labor is not a dominant disclosure theme. Productivity, simplification, and reinvestment are much more visible than workforce expansion.
- Technology: technology appears mainly as an operating lever through productivity, disruption, and execution rather than as a direct customer-facing thesis.
- Capital allocation: P&G remains one of the clearest cash-return models in the set, pairing large dividends and repurchases with tight operating discipline.
