## What changed

- Revenue: fiscal `2025` net sales were `$10.253 billion`; then the next three quarters in fiscal `2026` showed reported sales declines of `3%`, `5%`, and `4%`.
- Margin: adjusted EBIT was solid in fiscal `2025`, but all three fiscal `2026` quarters showed pressure, especially from inflation, tariffs, and weaker Snacks volume.
- Guidance: Campbell's reaffirmed fiscal `2026` guidance in Q1, lowered it in Q2, and reaffirmed the lower range in Q3.
- Cash generation: fiscal `2025` operating cash flow was `$1.131 billion`; fiscal year-to-date cash flow from operations was `$740 million` after Q2 and `$839 million` after Q3.
- Portfolio mix: Meals & Beverages remained the more durable side of the business, while Snacks stayed under pressure. Rao's stands out as the most visible premium-growth brand inside the system.
- Consumer / cultural signals: the company is a direct read on value-conscious but still food-at-home-oriented consumers. Convenience, quality, pantry trust, and meal-prep habits matter more here than impulse indulgence.
- Management tone: constructive on the long term, but more pressured and execution-focused than the annual report alone would suggest.

## Open questions

- Is Campbell's mainly a stable pantry-and-meals platform with a temporary Snacks problem, or does the Snacks weakness reveal a broader packaged-food fragility?
- How durable is the at-home cooking support for Campbell's, Rao's, and Swanson if consumer routines shift again?
- Does Rao's become the core growth engine that meaningfully changes the earnings quality of the portfolio, or does the broader system remain too mixed for that to dominate the story?
