## Annual report takeaways

- General Mills adds the broader pantry, cereal, snack, baking, pet, and foodservice replenishment layer that sits between Campbell's narrower at-home-cooking case and Kraft Heinz's larger center-store repair case. It is still a staples platform, but the `2025` and `2026` materials make clear that staples status alone is not enough to produce easy growth.
- The saved official `2025` annual filing is a Form `10-K` artifact for the fiscal year ended `2025-05-25`. The company finished fiscal `2025` with organic net sales down `2%`, adjusted operating profit down `7%`, and adjusted diluted EPS down `7%`, after stepping up investment midyear to bring consumers more value.
- Management's main `2025` message was that more prolonged value-seeking behavior and weaker consumer sentiment, especially in North America Retail, forced a midyear pivot. General Mills increased investment in value, product news, innovation, advertising, and what it calls brand `remarkability` to restore volume-driven organic growth.
- That pivot appears to have improved operating traction before it fully repaired the topline. Management said fiscal `2025` organic volume was flat, a `3-point` improvement from fiscal `2024`, and that household penetration in North America Retail stabilized for the first time in three years.
- Compared with Campbell's, General Mills is less concentrated in soup, broth, and meal preparation and more diversified across cereal, snacks, pet, foodservice, and international platforms. Compared with Kraft Heinz, the tone is less bluntly turnaround-heavy and more structured around reinvestment, cost productivity, and rebuilding competitiveness.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q4 FY`2026` organic net sales were `flat`, adjusted operating profit grew `13%`, and adjusted diluted EPS grew `27%`. Management said the quarter benefited from favorable trade phasing, shipment timing, HMM cost savings, and the `53rd` week.
- Full-year context: for fiscal `2026`, organic net sales finished down `2%`, and adjusted operating profit and adjusted diluted EPS were both down `16%` in constant currency.
- Guidance: on `2026-07-01`, General Mills introduced fiscal `2027` guidance of organic net sales growth of `-1.5% to +0.5%`, adjusted operating profit growth of `-13% to -8%`, adjusted diluted EPS of `$3.00 to $3.20`, and free cash flow conversion of about `95%`.
- Management message: management said General Mills finished fiscal `2026` on a positive note, improved base volume and household penetration, and was planning a `step change in remarkability` for fiscal `2027`.
- Structural themes: the better quarter does not erase the weaker year. It shows how much the current earnings profile still depends on trade timing, cost savings, and execution while the company tries to convert better competitiveness into real growth.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q3 FY`2026` organic net sales declined `3%`, and adjusted operating profit and adjusted EPS were down by more than `30%`, with retailer inventory headwinds and weather-related supply-chain disruptions adding new pressure to an already reinvestment-heavy year.
- Guidance: on `2026-03-18`, General Mills reaffirmed fiscal `2026` guidance and said favorable timing comparisons, the `53rd` week, and continued market-share momentum should help Q4.
- Management message: management said the core reinvestment strategy was working underneath the surface, citing progress in household penetration, baseline sales, distribution, and market share even though financial results were still difficult.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest reminder that pantry staples can still be operationally fragile. Retailer inventory shifts, trade timing, and weather can overwhelm the underlying demand signal in a given quarter.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q2 FY`2026` organic volume was `flat` and organic net sales were down `1%`, which management described as sequential improvement from Q1. The company also said North America Retail posted organic volume growth for the first time in more than four years and North America Pet returned to organic sales growth.
- Guidance: on `2025-12-17`, General Mills reaffirmed fiscal `2026` guidance.
- Management message: management framed the quarter around stronger execution on the `remarkability` agenda, the Love Made Fresh fresh-pet launch, ongoing Digital and AI initiatives, and continued HMM and transformation work.
- Structural themes: this quarter shows what the company is trying to become. The growth recipe is not passive staples resilience; it is value resets, better product and packaging, better brand communication, pet expansion, and cost-funded reinvestment.

Note: the relevant trailing three-quarter set as of 2026-08-10 is fiscal `Q4 FY2026`, `Q3 FY2026`, and `Q2 FY2026` because General Mills had already reported full-year fiscal `2026` results by `2026-07-01`.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: General Mills strengthens the case that necessity-led packaged food is still resilient, but the resilience is conditional on value architecture, innovation, and competitive execution rather than automatic pricing power.
- Consumer: households remain value-seeking and sentiment remains soft, but consumers still respond when trusted food brands improve price-value positioning and product relevance.
- Cultural: the company adds a broader pantry-and-replenishment read than Campbell's because it spans cereal, snacks, baking, pet, and foodservice rather than meal preparation alone.
- Regulatory: tariffs remain an explicit risk factor in the annual and quarter chain, while labeling, advertising, and trade-policy risks remain part of the operating backdrop.
- Labor: labor is not the central thesis, but supply-chain execution, transformation, and productivity all matter materially to outcomes.
- Technology: technology is mainly an execution lever here. Management explicitly ties Digital and AI initiatives to transformation and efficiency rather than to a consumer-facing software thesis.
- Capital allocation: the model remains cash-generative and disciplined, with management emphasizing dividends, capital returns, HMM savings, and a larger multi-year cost-savings target to support reinvestment.
