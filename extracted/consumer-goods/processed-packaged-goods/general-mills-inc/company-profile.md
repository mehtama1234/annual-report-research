## What changed

- Revenue trajectory: fiscal `2025` organic net sales were down `2%`; then fiscal `2026` quarter trends moved from a modestly better Q2 to a difficult Q3 and a better-finished Q4.
- Earnings profile: management used fiscal `2026` as a reinvestment year, which pressured operating profit and EPS before the Q4 rebound.
- Consumer read: value-seeking behavior and weaker sentiment are central. Management repeatedly links weaker topline performance to a consumer who still buys staples but demands better value and clearer product relevance.
- Operating model: the strategy is now explicitly built around `remarkability` across product, packaging, brand communication, omnichannel execution, and value.
- Portfolio mix: the pet platform matters more than in Campbell's or Kraft Heinz, and foodservice plus international make the pantry read broader than a U.S. grocery-shelf-only story.
- Cost structure: HMM productivity, transformation, and a larger fiscal `2030` cost-savings target are central to funding the growth reset.

## Open questions

- Does the stronger competitive signal in penetration, volume, and share convert into real organic growth in fiscal `2027`, or does the value-seeking consumer remain too soft for that to show up cleanly?
- Is General Mills best understood as a durable broad pantry-and-replenishment platform, or as a staples portfolio that now needs constant reinvestment just to hold position?
- How much of the Q4 FY`2026` improvement was durable operating progress versus favorable timing and the `53rd` week?
