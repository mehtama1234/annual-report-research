# PepsiCo Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-08

- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026

## Annual report takeaways

- PepsiCo’s 2025 annual report shows a large but slower-growth staples platform: net revenue reached `$93.9 billion`, core operating profit reached `$14.9 billion`, free cash flow reached `$8.2 billion`, and dividends paid reached `$7.6 billion`.
- 2025 is framed as a pivot point around consumer value, convenience, and portfolio evolution rather than simple price-led growth. Management is explicit that shoppers want value, convenience, and new experiences at the same time.
- The company remains unusually balanced: roughly `56%` of net revenue came from the U.S. and `44%` from outside the U.S., while the mix was about `58%` food and `42%` beverage.
- The annual materials make international breadth a core advantage. PepsiCo’s international business is positioned as a growth and resilience buffer while North America is worked through affordability, innovation, and execution changes.
- Risk concentration still matters: Walmart and its affiliates represented about `14%` of consolidated net revenue in 2025, and the company remains exposed to retailer power, consumer-budget pressure, packaging and commodity costs, and litigation.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 2026 net revenue was `$24.2 billion`, operating profit was `$4.0 billion`, EPS was `$2.18`, and core EPS was `$2.20`.
- Guidance: PepsiCo affirmed full-year 2026 guidance again.
- Management message: management highlighted strong organic volume growth in both global convenient foods and global beverages, but also said U.S. food and beverage categories moderated as consumer budgets tightened under inflation pressure.
- Structural themes: international organic revenue growth accelerated to `7%`, convenient foods organic volume rose `3%`, beverages organic volume rose `2%`, and management stressed productivity plus affordability as the operating response.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 2026 net revenue was `$19.4 billion`, operating profit was `$3.2 billion`, EPS was `$1.70`, and core EPS was `$1.61`.
- Guidance: PepsiCo again affirmed 2026 guidance and reiterated the planned shareholder return framework.
- Management message: management said both North America and international performance improved sequentially, with PepsiCo Foods North America posting volume growth and PBNA benefiting from Alani Nu distribution and the poppi acquisition.
- Structural themes: the quarter shows the company using brand restaging, affordability moves, and functional categories to regain momentum while still depending on productivity savings to protect earnings quality.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 2025 net revenue rose `5.6%`, full-year net revenue reached `$93.9 billion`, Q4 EPS was `$1.85`, full-year EPS was `$6.00`, and full-year core EPS was `$8.14`.
- Guidance: PepsiCo affirmed 2026 outlook and announced a `4%` increase in the annualized dividend, marking the `54th` consecutive annual increase.
- Management message: management said both North America and International improved sequentially, with productivity savings helping fund growth investments and affordability measures.
- Structural themes: this is the bridge quarter where PepsiCo’s story shifts from post-recall cleanup and mixed North America execution toward a more deliberate value-and-productivity playbook.

Note: for many companies this third slot will be a late-2025 quarter needed to complete the trailing three-quarter set as of 2026-08-08.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: PepsiCo is a strong read on staples demand being steadier than discretionary demand, but not immune to affordability pressure and retailer power.
- Consumer: households are still spending on snacks and beverages, but management repeatedly points to tighter budgets, value sensitivity, and the need for affordability architecture.
- Cultural: health, convenience, zero sugar, hydration, protein, fiber, portion control, and permissible snacks are now central consumer-facing themes rather than side categories.
- Regulatory: packaging, food regulation, litigation, and retailer concentration remain background risks, with the Baltimore plastics case worth tracking as a live issue.
- Labor: labor is not the center of the disclosure set; productivity, automation, digitalization, and simplification are much more prominent than hiring themes.
- Technology: this is not a technology-led company packet, but digitalization and automation are used as operating levers to defend margins and fund reinvestment.
- Capital allocation: PepsiCo remains a classic cash-return compounder, pairing dividends and buybacks with operating reinvestment, acquisitions, and portfolio refresh.
