## What changed

- Revenue and earnings: Dutch Bros is visible here as a scaled ritual-frequency beverage system rather than a generic restaurant operator. Full-year `2025` revenue was `$1.64B`, full-year net income was `$117.3M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$302.6M`.
- Unit and geography growth: the company opened `154` shops in `2025`, then opened `41` more in Q1 `2026` and `48` more in Q2 `2026`. The official overview page also described `1,225` locations across `26` states as of `2026-03-31`.
- Habit and frequency: the Q4 `2025` through Q2 `2026` chain kept showing same-shop transaction growth, not only price-led sales growth. Management explicitly described Q2 `2026` as the thirteenth consecutive quarter of positive same-shop sales growth and the eighth consecutive quarter of same-shop transaction growth.
- Relationship layer: the `10-K` identifies `Dutch Rewards` as an app-based digital loyalty program and highlights order-ahead functionality, which matters because the customer relationship is built around repeat ritual, convenience, and daypart frequency rather than a one-time restaurant occasion.
- Strategy and expansion: the Q2 `2026` `8-K` also disclosed an agreement to acquire up to `65` Salad and Go site assets across Arizona, Nevada, Oklahoma, and Texas, showing that real-estate capture is now part of the growth system.
- Blind-spot fit: this is a useful correction to source taxonomy because the company behaves more like a stimulation-heavy, drive-thru beverage routine than a generic `Restaurants` issuer.

## Open questions

- How durable is the transaction-led growth once the opening base gets larger and system same-shop sales normalize?
- How much of the model’s edge comes from beverage innovation and speed versus the broader Dutch Rewards and customer-relationship layer?
- Does the newer real-estate acquisition path deepen the moat, or does it mainly accelerate the unit-growth burden?
