## Annual report takeaways

- YETI is a strong blind-spot fit because `Consumer Goods / --None--` is almost an admission that the source taxonomy does not know what to do with the company. The annual and trailing-quarter materials show a premium outdoor-utility, gear-attachment, and DTC-control system built around brand-led functional products rather than a plain sporting-goods shelf label.
- AnnualReports.com still lagged here. On `2026-08-10`, the saved company page still showed `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, so the usable current annual anchor had to come from the official `2025` annual-report PDF, the fiscal `2025` SEC filing chain, and official-site verification.
- Full-year `2025` sales were `$1.8685B`, net income was `$165.4M`, and operating income was `$213.6M`.
- The full-year `2025` release also said `DTC` channel sales increased `4%` to `$1.1278B`, wholesale channel sales decreased slightly to `$740.7M`, and international sales increased `16%` to `$394.4M`.
- The `10-K` makes the relationship layer explicit. It says the company sells through its websites, `YETI Authorized` on the Amazon Marketplace, retail stores, and a corporate sales program, while `DTC` already represented `60%` of `2025` net sales.
- The annual-plus-quarter chain therefore reads less like an ordinary hardgoods manufacturer and more like a premium utility brand where outdoor identity, gifting, direct relationship ownership, and product-led cultural attachment all reinforce the economics.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q1 2026` sales were `$380.4M`, net income was `$9.9M`, and adjusted net income was `$19.8M`.
- Channel and category posture: wholesale channel sales increased `19%` to `$183.6M`, `DTC` channel sales were flat at `$196.8M`, Coolers & Equipment sales increased `11%` to `$156.1M`, and Drinkware sales increased `5%` to `$216.9M`.
- Geographic and guidance posture: US sales increased `8%` to `$293.1M`, international sales increased `9%` to `$87.3M`, the lower end of full-year `2026` sales growth guidance was raised to `7%` to `8%`, adjusted operating income margin guidance was raised to about `14.6%`, and EPS guidance was raised to `$2.83` to `$2.89`.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows a real hybrid control model. Consumer demand across websites, Amazon Marketplace, and YETI retail remained strong, but the mix still shifted visibly between `DTC`, wholesale, and corporate sales.
- Margin nuance: profitability was pressured by incremental tariffs even as the topline stayed strong, which is exactly the kind of operating tension a premium physical-goods brand has to manage.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q4 2025` sales were `$583.7M`, net income was `$58.2M`, and adjusted net income was `$71.8M`.
- Full-year close: the same release also reported full-year `2025` sales of `$1.8685B`, `DTC` sales of `$1.1278B`, wholesale sales of `$740.7M`, and international sales of `$394.4M`.
- Channel and category posture: `DTC` channel sales increased `7%` to `$394.3M`, wholesale channel sales increased `6%` to `$189.4M`, Drinkware sales increased `6%` to `$380.0M`, and Coolers & Equipment sales increased `7%` to `$192.3M`.
- Structural themes: this is the key annual-close quarter because it shows the brand scaling through both utility attachment and channel control. The important unit is not only a one-time gear purchase. It is a direct relationship that can widen into Amazon Marketplace, corporate sales, retail stores, and international growth.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q3 2025` sales were `$487.8M`, net income was `$39.4M`, and adjusted net income was `$49.6M`.
- Channel and category posture: `DTC` channel sales increased `3%` to `$288.7M`, wholesale channel sales increased `1%` to `$199.0M`, Drinkware sales decreased `4%` to `$263.8M`, and Coolers & Equipment sales increased `12%` to `$215.4M`.
- Geographic and capital-return posture: international sales increased `14%` to `$100.4M`, while the company raised its `2025` share-repurchase target to `$300M`.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because it shows the business staying resilient even while drinkware was softer, wholesale was cautious in the US, and tariffs were pressuring margins. That is a better signal of category depth and brand utility than a single strong holiday quarter alone.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: YETI technically strengthens Consumer Goods, but the better read is as a premium outdoor-utility and channel-control interface that source taxonomy flattens into `--None--`.
- Consumer: the demand system is built on product reliability, premium utility, outdoor and lifestyle attachment, gifting, and strong willingness to pay for products that signal durability and design quality.
- Cultural: this is one of the archive's clearer proofs that a physical hardgoods brand can become an identity system even without being fashion-first, app-first, or membership-first.
- Societal: the packet reflects a broader willingness to attach identity and aspiration to practical equipment, not just apparel, beauty, or digital participation.
- Operating model: the important move is not just that `DTC` is larger than wholesale. It is that YETI controls multiple direct surfaces at once, including its own sites, retail stores, Amazon Marketplace presence, and corporate-sales layer.
- Competitive positioning: YETI is most useful next to `Crocs`, `Levi Strauss`, `Deckers`, `Nike`, `Birkenstock`, and `Amazon`, not only next to plain sporting-goods or equipment issuers.

## Working interpretation

- YETI materially strengthens the blind-spot lane because it adds a company where AnnualReports not only lags on the current annual package, but effectively fails to classify the operating model at all.
- `Crocs` proves that comfort and familiarity can support a scaled DTC-heavy consumer brand. `Levi Strauss` proves that heritage apparel can widen through direct control and category extension. `Deckers` and `Birkenstock` prove that utility, comfort, and premium positioning can coexist across partner and direct channels. YETI adds the hardgoods version where performance utility, gifting, outdoor aspiration, and direct-relationship control reinforce one another.
- Together, `Crocs`, `Levi Strauss`, `Deckers`, `Birkenstock`, and YETI show why source taxonomy is too blunt. They all sit inside consumer-facing product categories, but the better comparison is within a broader channel-control and identity-utility system:
  - a heritage and denim-led relationship through `Levi Strauss`
  - a mass comfort and casualization relationship through `Crocs`
  - a performance-plus-comfort and premium-footwear relationship through `Deckers`
  - a practical-wellness and premium-utility relationship through `Birkenstock`
  - a premium outdoor-utility and gear-attachment relationship through YETI
