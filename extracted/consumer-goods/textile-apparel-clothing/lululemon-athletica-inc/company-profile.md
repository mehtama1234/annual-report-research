## What changed

- Demand profile: lululemon behaves less like a classic household consumer-goods operator and more like a premium athletic-apparel and wellness-identity platform.
- Geographic mix: the central operating split is much stronger international growth against softer Americas trends.
- Margin quality: revenue is still growing, but gross and operating margins weakened across the trailing quarter set, which keeps the packet grounded in execution rather than brand mythology.
- Comparison value: lululemon is a stronger cross-taxonomy comparison for Nike than another generic retailer or another pantry-and-staples name would have been.

## Open questions

- Can lululemon restore healthier North America full-price momentum without diluting the brand or leaning too hard on promotions?
- Is the stronger international growth a durable multi-year expansion engine, or partly a temporary offset to home-market softness?
- How much of lululemon's long-term advantage comes from technical product leadership and community credibility versus the more general premium-athletic brand halo?
