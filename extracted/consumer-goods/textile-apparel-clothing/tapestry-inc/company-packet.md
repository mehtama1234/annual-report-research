## Annual report takeaways

- Tapestry materially improves the archive because it adds an accessible-luxury and status-expression apparel case instead of another general mall-apparel or performance-wellness packet. The core read is a house-of-brands system built around `Coach` and `Kate Spade New York`.
- AnnualReports.com still lagged here. On Monday, August 10, 2026, it showed `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, so the current annual package had to be verified through official IR and the SEC filing chain.
- The official investor overview shows FY25 revenue of `$7.0B`, gross margin of `75%`, operating margin of `20%`, EPS of `$5.10`, and free cash flow of `$1.3B`.
- The strategic read is that Tapestry is explicitly trying to turn luxury and accessories demand into a repeat relationship system by building emotional connection, acquiring younger consumers, leading with handbags and leathergoods, and scaling in North America, Greater China, and Europe.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q3 2026` revenue was `$1.9B`, up `21%` reported and `19%` in constant currency; pro forma revenue growth was `25%`; GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.65`; and non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.66`.
- Operating posture: management said the quarter was led by `Coach`, which grew `31%` reported and `29%` in constant currency, while direct-to-consumer revenue grew `23%` in constant currency and digital growth was about `25%`.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows a brand-heat and premium-accessories operator still growing rapidly even while explicitly absorbing a tariff and duty headwind of about `180` basis points. It also makes the post-`Stuart Weitzman` simplification visible.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q2 2026` revenue was `$2.5B`, up `14%`; pro forma revenue growth was `18%`; GAAP diluted EPS was `$2.68`; and non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$2.69`.
- Operating posture: Q2 set record quarterly revenue, operating profit, and EPS, with gross margin at `75.5%`, inventory at `$896M` versus `$937M` a year earlier, and management raising the full-year outlook again.
- Structural themes: this is the cleanest holiday-quarter proof that the model is working as an affordable-luxury relationship engine rather than only as a wholesale accessories company. The combination of margin expansion, inventory discipline, and continued guidance raises points to brand desire plus operating control.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q1 2026` revenue was `$1.70B`, up `13%`; pro forma revenue growth was `16%`; GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.28`; and non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.38`.
- Operating posture: Tapestry acquired over `2.2 million` new customers globally in the quarter, about `35%` of them `Gen Z`, while `Coach` revenue grew `22%` and Europe grew `32%` in constant currency.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because management says the quiet part out loud. The company is explicitly trying to recruit younger consumers, deepen emotional connection, and widen global brand desirability while using data, analytics, and direct-to-consumer channels to do it.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Tapestry strengthens `Consumer Goods` with an accessible-luxury and accessories-led consumer operator that AnnualReports classifies under `Consumer Goods / Textile - Apparel Clothing`.
- Consumer: this is a read on consumers still willing to spend for status, self-expression, gifting, and brand-coded accessories when the product feels current and the brand heat is strong.
- Cultural: Tapestry is one of the clearer identity-and-aspiration packets in the archive because management explicitly centers emotional connection, Gen Z acquisition, and brand love.
- Societal: the quarter chain points to a consumer culture where ownership still matters, but the real value is symbolic and social as much as functional. Handbags, leathergoods, and visible accessories remain status carriers.
- Operating model: the company combines house-of-brands coordination, direct-to-consumer distribution, digital demand generation, international expansion, and premium-margin discipline.
- Competitive positioning: Tapestry is most useful next to `lululemon athletica inc.`, `Levi Strauss & Co.`, `Abercrombie & Fitch Co.`, `Victoria's Secret & Co.`, and `Urban Outfitters, Inc.`. It is more status-coded than Levi, more premium-accessories-led than Abercrombie, less wellness-coded than lululemon, and less mall-banner-driven than Gap or American Eagle.

## Working interpretation

- Tapestry is not just another apparel datapoint. It is the current archive's clearest accessible-luxury and visible-status case, and it makes the consumer trend work sharper because it shows how aspiration, brand heat, Gen Z recruitment, and direct channel control combine in practice.
- The most important evidence is that the annual and quarter chain all point in the same direction: stronger `Coach` momentum, high margin structure, direct-to-consumer growth, international acceleration, and explicit management language around emotional connection and global growth.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Tapestry is one of the archive's better proofs that discretionary consumer demand is still healthy in selected premium categories when the product carries identity value and the company maintains brand and channel discipline.
