## What changed

- Revenue and margins: the annual and quarter chain shows a high-margin model with FY25 revenue of `$7.0B` on the overview page and quarterly revenue stepping through `$1.70B`, `$2.5B`, and `$1.9B` with repeated operating-margin expansion.
- Brand mix: `Coach` is the key engine, while `Kate Spade New York` remains part of the house-of-brands mix and `Stuart Weitzman` has been removed from the ongoing operating structure after the August 4, 2025 divestiture.
- Consumer recruitment: management repeatedly emphasizes younger-customer acquisition, especially `Gen Z`, which makes this packet more explicitly generational than most consumer-goods apparel names.
- Geography: North America remains central, but Europe and Greater China are also explicit growth priorities and showed strong momentum in the quarter chain.
- Channel posture: direct-to-consumer, digital, and data-backed consumer analytics are clearly part of the strategy, not just supporting details.
- Consumer and cultural signals: this is a stronger visible-status and self-expression packet than the mall-apparel names and a less wellness-centered one than lululemon.
- Operating pressure: the quarter chain also shows tariff and duty headwinds, so the company is not escaping the broader cost and trade environment.

## Open questions

- How durable is the current `Coach` strength once comparisons get harder?
- Can `Kate Spade New York` become a stronger second growth engine inside the post-`Stuart Weitzman` structure?
- Does the explicit Gen Z acquisition push turn into durable lifetime value, or mostly faster customer-file replenishment?
