## What changed in this period

- Funko moved from a tariff-disrupted and debt-constrained `2025` into a visibly stronger first half of `2026`, with better gross margins, stronger `Core Collectibles` momentum, and improving debt levels.
- The company also sharpened the strategic distinction between:
  - `Core Collectibles`
  - `Loungefly`
  - other emerging brands such as `Mondo`
- By `Q2 2026`, management was explicitly rationalizing `Loungefly` SKU count while concentrating on higher-return products and leaning into stronger collector demand.

## Why this name matters in the archive

- Funko adds a fandom-expression and collector-merchandise layer that differs from both:
  - owned-world operators such as Disney and Take-Two
  - licensed-product executors such as JAKKS
- It does not own the deepest canon layer, but it does own a recognizable aesthetic system and repeatable shelf-presence logic around fandom identity.
- That makes it a strong comparison point for:
  - JAKKS's costume and retail-expression model
  - Hasbro and Mattel's broader play-and-brand systems
  - Etsy's meaning-rich purchase behavior outside formal franchise ownership
