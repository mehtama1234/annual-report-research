# Mattel, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Mattel, Inc.
- Sector: `Consumer Goods`
- Industry: `Toys, Games & Hobbies`
- Ticker: `MAT`
- Fiscal year-end: December 31
- Target annual report year: `2025`
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: `Q2 2026`
  - quarter 2: `Q1 2026`
  - quarter 3: `Q4 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- Mattel is an important addition because it shows a large toy platform trying to become something broader than a toy manufacturer without yet escaping the frictions of the physical-products model. The `2025` annual filing describes Mattel as a leading global toy and family entertainment company, then lays out a portfolio of owned and licensed brands that spans dolls, vehicles, preschool, action figures, games, and digital extensions.
- The `2025` annual and year-end materials make the strategic turn explicit. Management said `2026` would begin a new `brand-centric strategy` to grow an `IP-driven play and family entertainment business`, with growth expected from toy innovation, major IP partnerships, theatrical releases, and expansion of digital games. That is a stronger archive signal than a generic product-refresh story. It is a statement that play is being managed as a cross-format cultural system.
- Full-year `2025` results show why the company is in transition rather than at the end state. Net sales were `$5,348M`, down `1%`, while adjusted operating income was `$620M` and adjusted EPS was `$1.41`. Category demand was uneven: Vehicles grew `11%` and Action Figures/Building Sets/Games/Other grew `14%`, while Dolls fell `7%` and Infant/Toddler/Preschool fell `17%`.
- That internal mix is the packet's real analytical value. Mattel's strongest demand pockets are tied to collector culture, challenge-oriented play, theatrical tie-ins, and broader game participation, while some older child-and-family categories are softer. The company is responding by investing into digital games, entertainment, direct-to-consumer, first-party data, AI, and infrastructure.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: net sales were `$1,125M`, up `10%` reported and `9%` in constant currency; adjusted operating income was `$39M`; and adjusted EPS was `$0.01`.
- Demand and growth: Vehicles gross billings rose `14%` reported, Action Figures/Building Sets/Games/Other rose `35%`, Dolls declined `5%`, and Infant/Toddler/Preschool declined `11%`. Management tied growth to Hot Wheels, digital games following the Mattel163 acquisition, and action figures tied to theatrical releases.
- Management message: management stressed continued execution of a multi-year strategy to grow an `IP-driven play and family entertainment business`, cited the global release of the `Masters of the Universe` movie, noted the launch of a first self-published mobile game, and said demand strength continued into `Q3 2026`.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because it shows physical toys, movies, and digital games being used together as a demand-amplification system. It also shows the cost of that model: tariffs, inflation, higher royalties, unfavorable foreign exchange, and heavier advertising all kept profitability constrained even with strong top-line growth.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: net sales were `$862M`, up `4%` reported and `1%` in constant currency; adjusted operating loss was `$70M`; and adjusted loss per share was `$0.20`.
- Demand and growth: Vehicles gross billings rose `17%`, Action Figures/Building Sets/Games/Other rose `21%`, Dolls fell `8%`, and Infant/Toddler/Preschool fell `16%`. Games growth included the partial-quarter Mattel163 contribution.
- Management message: management said the company was off to a good start, completed full ownership of Mattel163 in early March, kept full-year guidance intact, and framed the quarter as progress on the strategy to grow an `IP driven play and family entertainment business`.
- Structural themes: this quarter shows the transition costs more clearly than the annual summary alone. Consumer demand was positive, but gross margin still absorbed tariff costs, foreign exchange, and inflation, while operating loss widened on higher advertising and SG&A. The company is investing ahead of the hoped-for monetization payoff.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025 / full-year 2025

- Results: full-year net sales were `$5,348M`, down `1%`; adjusted operating income was `$620M`; adjusted EPS was `$1.41`; and the company ended the year with more than `$1.2B` of cash. Fourth-quarter net sales were `$1,766M`, up `7%` reported and `5%` in constant currency.
- Demand and growth: full-year Vehicles gross billings rose `11%`, Action Figures/Building Sets/Games/Other rose `14%`, Dolls fell `7%`, and Infant/Toddler/Preschool fell `17%`. In Q4 alone, Vehicles rose `20%` and Action Figures/Building Sets/Games/Other rose `16%`.
- Management message: management announced the Mattel163 acquisition, a new `$1.5B` share-repurchase authorization through `2028`, and roughly `$150M` of strategic investments for digital games, data, direct-to-consumer, AI, and infrastructure. The year-end release repeatedly positioned `2026` as the start of a more overt brand-centric operating model.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` is where Mattel stops looking like a pure seasonal toy story and starts looking like a company trying to build repeat access points into owned and partnered brands across toys, film, and digital games. But the annual baseline also makes clear that the company is still carrying uneven category demand and substantial physical-goods friction.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer trend: Mattel supports the view that spending on play is moving toward categories with collector energy, repeat engagement, and world-building potential. Hot Wheels, theatrical tie-ins, and games showed more momentum than weaker household and preschool categories.
- Cultural and societal shift: this packet points to play becoming more cross-age and identity-bearing. Mattel is not only chasing children in traditional toy aisles. It is explicitly leaning into adult fans, collectors, nostalgia, franchise communities, and family entertainment formats that travel across screens, devices, and shelves.
- Industrial and operating pressure: tariffs, inflation, royalties, foreign exchange, advertising intensity, and the capital needs of digital and entertainment expansion are not side notes. They are major determinants of how much of the cultural demand can actually convert into earnings.
- Bigger-picture pattern: Mattel repeatedly shows that physical product now works best when attached to broader brand systems. Movies, mobile games, and partner IP are being used to reopen demand loops rather than simply to support one-off product launches.
- Cross-company pattern: compared with Hasbro, Mattel is earlier in the migration from toy maker to broader franchise allocator. Hasbro currently shows a cleaner high-margin fandom engine through Magic and digital licensing. Mattel shows a more mixed but still important model in which physical toys, entertainment, and digital games are being assembled into a broader participation system without yet eliminating the operational drag of a global physical-products platform.

## Working interpretation

- Mattel materially strengthens the archive because it turns the toys-and-play lane into a real comparative frontier instead of a one-company packet. With Hasbro and Mattel together, the repo now has a much better basis for testing how franchise ownership, digital extension, collector demand, and entertainment tie-ins convert into economics across different toy portfolios.
- The broader social read is that play is becoming less age-bounded, less single-format, and more culture-like. Consumers are spending on systems of identity, nostalgia, challenge, collection, and repeat participation, not only on isolated toy purchases. Mattel's brand portfolio gives that pattern a mass-market and family-entertainment expression.
- The industrial read is equally important. Mattel shows that even when the cultural demand exists, monetization still depends on surviving tariffs, inflation, royalties, advertising intensity, and the fixed costs of global product infrastructure. That contrast makes the packet useful for cross-company work instead of letting the toy/IP story drift into pure narrative.
