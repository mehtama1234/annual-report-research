## What changed

- Hasbro is a strong archive addition because it adds a consumer and cultural system that standard consumer-goods coverage misses: play, fandom, collectibles, organized play, and franchise-IP monetization.
- The official materials repeatedly describe Hasbro as moving toward a `digital-first play and IP company`, not just a toy seller. That matters because the demand engine is increasingly spread across tabletop, digital gaming, licensing, and screen-linked engagement.
- Wizards of the Coast and Digital Gaming is the clearest proof. The segment drove `45%` growth in full-year `2025`, followed by `26%` growth in Q1 `2026` and `27%` growth in Q2 `2026`.
- The pattern is not just broad gaming. `Magic: The Gathering` appears as the central flywheel. The materials point to Universes Beyond sets, backlist strength, Secret Lair, and crossover releases as repeatable cultural and commercial mechanisms.
- Hasbro is therefore useful for the blind-spot lane because it sits at the overlap of physical products, organized play, digital monetization, fan identity, and IP extension.

## Open questions

- How concentrated is the growth engine becoming around `Magic: The Gathering` and Wizards relative to the rest of the portfolio?
- Can Hasbro keep widening the digital and licensed gaming layer without becoming overly dependent on a smaller set of blockbuster franchises?
- Does the franchise-first model deepen long-term fandom and engagement, or does it mainly improve near-term monetization through crossovers and licensing?
