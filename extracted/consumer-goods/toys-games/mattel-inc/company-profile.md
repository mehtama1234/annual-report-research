## What changed

- Mattel is a strong archive addition because it turns the play-and-franchise lane from a single-company proof into a comparison set.
- The filings and official materials make clear that Mattel is trying to widen the model beyond shelves through entertainment, licensing, digital games, DTC, first-party data, and AI-enabled operations.
- The important contrast with `Hasbro` is structural. `Hasbro` centers more on ruleset fandom, organized participation, and collectible ecosystems. `Mattel` centers more on iconic brand portfolios, movie and content extension, digital games, and adult-fan broadening across brands.
- That difference is exactly the kind of blind spot the source taxonomy misses when it places both names inside the same old `Toys & Games` bucket.

## Open questions

- How much of Mattel's next leg of growth will come from entertainment and digital extensions versus the core toy portfolio?
- Can the company translate brand heat and movie visibility into sustained game, DTC, and first-party-data economics?
- Will the new `brand-centric strategy` create a durable cross-format flywheel, or mostly improve monetization around a smaller set of blockbuster brands and releases?
