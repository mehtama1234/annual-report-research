## Annual report takeaways

- Antero is one of the strongest missing packets for the branch because it adds a liquids-rich Appalachian gas system rather than another oil-heavy shale operator or another pure dry-gas volume story. The annual and year-end materials make clear that the company is built around unconventional liquids-rich natural gas in Appalachia, not just around generic U.S. gas exposure.
- The `Q4 2025` and annual-close evidence show why this matters. Antero ended `2025` with estimated proved reserves of `19.1 Tcfe`, up `7%` from the prior year, with reserves composed of about `61%` natural gas, `38%` NGLs, and `1%` oil. That mix makes the company a different economic system from `EQT` and `Expand`, because NGL prices and transport-linked liquids economics matter almost as much as gas prices.
- The annual-close financial picture is also useful. `Q4 2025` results reported `\$422M` of adjusted EBITDAX, `\$371M` of operating cash flow, and `\$204M` of adjusted free cash flow before working-capital changes on `3.5 Bcfe/d` of production. This is a more modest scale than `Expand`, but with a much clearer liquids-rich premium-realization angle.
- The year-end materials also frame `2026` as a transition year. The company highlighted the `HG` acquisition, the Ohio Utica divestiture, and a production step-up toward `4.1 Bcfe/d` on about `\$1.0B` of drilling and completion capital. That is not just maintenance-mode upstream behavior. It is a reshaped Appalachia portfolio that is trying to grow into stronger gas and liquids market positioning.
- The annual source chain is clean. AnnualReports already displayed the `2025` annual package, the official company IR page exposed the direct annual-report PDF, and the SEC `ARS` plus filed `10-K` complete the authoritative annual evidence set.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` production was a company record at more than `4.1 Bcfe/d`, up `21%` from the prior-year period. Adjusted EBITDAX was `\$595M`, operating cash flow was `\$439M`, and adjusted free cash flow before working-capital changes was `\$220M`.
- Operating system: total cash operating costs were `\$2.38` per Mcfe, down `11%` from the prior-year period, while management increased full-year `2026` production guidance to `4.15 - 4.2 Bcfe/d`.
- Capital allocation and positioning: Antero bought back `1.1 million` shares for about `\$38M` during the quarter and completed `\$315M` of strategic acquisitions in July 2026 in the core Marcellus footprint, including `125 MMcfe/d` of net production and `15` net drilling locations.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` shows Antero as an Appalachia gas-and-liquids optimizer, not just a driller. Production growth, lower costs, buybacks, and bolt-on Marcellus acquisitions are all being used to deepen a differentiated market position rather than to chase volume for its own sake.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` production averaged a company-record `3.9 Bcfe/d`, up `13%` from the prior-year period, with natural gas production of `2.6 Bcf/d` and liquids production of `206 MBbl/d`.
- Pricing and cash flow: Antero realized a pre-hedge natural gas price of `\$5.57` per Mcf, a `\$0.53` premium to NYMEX, and a pre-hedge `C3+` NGL price of `\$37.83` per barrel, a `\$0.94` premium to benchmark. Adjusted EBITDAX was `\$723M`, operating cash flow was `\$859M`, and adjusted free cash flow was `\$657M`.
- Portfolio changes: the company closed the `HG` acquisition in early February and completed the Ohio Utica Shale divestiture in late February.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is the clearest quarter for understanding Antero's differentiation. Management explicitly said Antero had the highest LNG exposure among Appalachian producers, selling `2.3 Bcf/d` of production to sales points along the LNG fairway, while also benefiting from U.S. NGL demand and export pull. This is a realized-price and infrastructure-access packet as much as a production packet.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` production averaged `3.5 Bcfe/d`, up `2%` from the prior-year quarter. Adjusted EBITDAX was `\$422M`, operating cash flow was `\$371M`, and adjusted free cash flow before working-capital changes was `\$204M`.
- Pricing and execution: Antero realized a pre-hedge natural gas equivalent price of `\$3.97` per Mcfe, a `\$0.42` premium to NYMEX, and a pre-hedge `C3+` NGL price of `\$35.41` per barrel, a `\$1.52` premium to Mont Belvieu.
- Strategic posture: year-end guidance assumed production averaging `4.1 Bcfe/d` in `2026` on `\$1.0B` of D&C capital, with the `HG` acquisition already closed by early February 2026.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the best quarter for understanding why Antero is not interchangeable with the other gas names. The company combines liquids-rich Appalachian geology, premium pricing, NGL linkage, and market-access strategy inside one upstream packet.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Antero adds the liquids-rich Appalachian gas-and-NGL version of independent upstream exposure.
- Consumer: end demand remains indirect, but this packet sits unusually close to export-linked gas demand, petrochemical and NGL demand, and LNG pull-through.
- Cultural and societal: management repeatedly frames the company around U.S. supply security, export demand, and the strategic value of domestic gas and NGL barrels in a tighter global energy system.
- Industrial: Antero is a strong example of an upstream operator whose economics are shaped by realized-price premiums, NGL marketing, takeaway positioning, and transaction-driven acreage reshaping, not only by drilling performance.
- Commodity exposure: the company is highly exposed to gas and NGL pricing, but the packet shows that price realization relative to NYMEX and Mont Belvieu is central to how commodity exposure becomes cash flow.
- Capital allocation: share repurchases remain active, but the bigger story is disciplined reinvestment plus targeted acquisitions inside the core footprint rather than blunt growth spending.
- Infrastructure dependence: Antero is one of the strongest upstream-to-midstream bridge packets in the branch because LNG-fairway sales points, NGL export dynamics, and Appalachian transport access are essential to its realized pricing.
