## What changed

- Antero adds a different gas packet than either `EQT` or `Expand Energy`. `EQT` is more about basis, compression, and premium-power linkage. `Expand` is more about multi-basin scale and national commercial integration. Antero is the liquids-rich Appalachia case where NGL realizations, export-linked pricing, and transport positioning are central to the story.
- The `2025` annual-close materials are especially useful because they show a gas producer whose value proposition is not simply dry-gas volume. The fourth-quarter and year-end materials emphasize `19.1 Tcfe` of proved reserves, a reserve mix of roughly `61%` natural gas, `38%` NGLs, and `1%` oil, plus a visible premium-realization strategy tied to both gas and liquids.
- The quarter chain then shows rapid scaling through transactions and improved market positioning. `Q4 2025` framed the initial `HG` acquisition and `2026` step-up. `Q1 2026` brought the first quarter of `HG` impact and the Ohio Utica exit, while management emphasized that Antero had the highest LNG exposure among Appalachian producers, with `2.3 Bcf/d` sold to LNG-fairway sales points. `Q2 2026` then added further core-Marcellus acquisitions and higher full-year guidance.
- This makes Antero a strong bridge packet between upstream gas production and the NGL and export stack. The key variable is not just Henry Hub. It is whether Antero can keep converting liquids-rich gas, NGL premiums, LNG-linked sales points, and acquisition-led scale into durable free cash flow.
- The source chain is also clean. AnnualReports already displayed the `2025` annual package, the official IR site exposed direct annual-report and quarterly-results materials, and the SEC annual plus quarter chain is complete.

## Open questions

- Can Antero keep its premium-realization advantage if NGL export conditions weaken or if Appalachian takeaway becomes less differentiated?
- Does acquisition-led scale strengthen the liquids-rich Appalachia model, or does it make cost discipline and integration more difficult just as gas markets become more volatile again?
- If LNG demand and U.S. export pull stay strong, does Antero's LNG-fairway exposure become a durable structural edge over other Appalachian producers?
