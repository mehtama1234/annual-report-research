## Annual report takeaways

- APA is one of the better missing upstream packets because it adds a genuinely different system than the branch's shale-heavy independents. The company still depends on the `Permian`, but the annual filing makes clear that the real portfolio includes `Egypt`, the `North Sea`, and an exploration and future-development lane in `Suriname` that no other current independent packet captures as cleanly.
- The annual filing explicitly frames APA as a diversified independent operating in three main producing geographies: the U.S., Egypt, and offshore the U.K. in the `North Sea`, while also maintaining active development, exploration, and appraisal operations in `Suriname`. That matters because the packet should not be read only through U.S. oil volumes. Political exposure, PSC structure, and long-cycle exploration timing are central to the economics.
- APA’s `2025` production and reserve base were still substantial. Full-year `2025` reported production averaged `464,000` BOE/d and adjusted production averaged `392,000` BOE/d. Worldwide estimated proved reserves grew `9%` to `1,056 MMBOE` at year-end `2025`, of which `734 MMBOE` were proved developed.
- The geographic mix is what makes the packet distinctive. The `10-K` says about `38%` of `2025` production on a barrel-equivalent basis was outside the U.S., and about `26%` of estimated proved reserves at `2025-12-31` were located outside the U.S. That is a much more international and contract-sensitive operating profile than the branch's more concentrated domestic shale names.
- APA is also a good packet for understanding how upstream discipline can coexist with long-cycle exploration optionality. In `2025`, the company returned `\$640M` to shareholders, representing more than `60%` of free cash flow, reduced total debt to below `\$4.5B`, and pushed net debt below `\$4.0B`. At the same time, it continued to fund `GranMorgu` development and `Block 58` exploration in `Suriname`.
- The `Suriname` structure is material, not decorative. The annual filing says APA and `TotalEnergies` each hold a `50%` working interest in `Block 58`, which comprises about `1.4 million` gross acres offshore `Suriname`. The joint-venture terms allocate a proportionately larger share of appraisal and development capital to `TotalEnergies`, recoverable through hydrocarbon participation. That makes APA a useful packet for showing how exploration upside can be preserved without fully overwhelming the base-business balance sheet.
- The annual-close materials also show where the stress still sits. APA said `2025` U.S. and North Sea production were pressured by natural decline, non-core U.S. asset sales, operational downtime, and volume curtailments at `Alpine High` because of extreme `Waha` basis differentials. That gives the packet a transport-and-basis-risk dimension that fits well alongside the branch's other gas and Permian names.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` reported production was about `410,000` BOE/d, while adjusted production was `347,000` BOE/d. U.S. oil production averaged `123,500` barrels per day and exceeded guidance by `2,500` barrels per day.
- Cash-flow read: APA generated `\$1.7B` of net cash provided by operating activities, `\$738M` of free cash flow, and `\$1.8B` of adjusted EBITDAX in the quarter. Upstream capital investment was `\$546M`, and lease operating expense was `\$353M`, both below guidance.
- Balance-sheet and return read: the company repaid `\$752M` of near-term bond debt in the first half of `2026`, including `\$673M` in the second quarter. Total debt had declined by `\$2.3B` since year-end `2024`, and net debt was `\$3.3B` at quarter-end. APA also returned `\$189M` to shareholders in `Q2`, including `2.8 million` repurchased shares at an average price of `\$35.26`.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is the clearest quarter for why APA belongs in this branch. The company is simultaneously running a high-discipline base business, raising U.S. oil guidance, reducing debt, and still advancing a broader exploration portfolio through the pending `Savant` Alaska acquisition and a new `Eni` partnership in `Uruguay`.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` reported production was `442,000` BOE/d and adjusted production was `363,000` BOE/d. U.S. oil production averaged `124,000` barrels per day and beat prior guidance.
- Cash-flow read: the company generated `\$554M` of net cash provided by operating activities, `\$477M` of free cash flow, and `\$1.6B` of adjusted EBITDAX.
- Operating read: APA said `Egypt` adjusted production averaged `71,000` BOE/d, with underlying gross oil performance helped by strong wells and gross gas production around `518 MMCF/d`. Management also highlighted Permian efficiency gains and improved uptime.
- Balance-sheet read: through April `2026`, APA repaid `\$634M` of near-term bond maturities. Management said those actions, combined with the prior `2025` deleveraging, were expected to lower `2026` interest expense by more than `\$60M`.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` shows APA as a base-business repair and optimization story first, but not only that. The company is using Permian efficiency, Egypt gas weighting, and cost reduction to support capital returns and future exploration capacity at the same time.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` reported production was `460,000` BOE/d and adjusted production was `387,000` BOE/d. Full-year `2025` reported production averaged `464,000` BOE/d and adjusted production averaged `392,000` BOE/d.
- Cash-flow and return read: in the fourth quarter, APA generated `\$808M` of net cash provided by operating activities, `\$425M` of free cash flow, and `\$1.2B` of adjusted EBITDAX. For full-year `2025`, the company generated `\$4.5B` of operating cash flow, `\$1.0B` of free cash flow, and returned `\$640M` to shareholders.
- Portfolio read: management used the year-end quarter to emphasize three things:
  - the base-business transformation through lower costs and better capital efficiency
  - the validation of roughly `10 years` of economic `Permian` inventory
  - the future-growth option in `Suriname`
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the cleanest setup quarter for the APA thesis. It shows a company trying to become more focused and capital-efficient while still carrying a real long-duration exploration and first-oil story that could reshape the medium-term portfolio.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: APA adds the internationally exposed and exploration-option-heavy version of independent E&P rather than another mostly domestic shale-manufacturing packet.
- Consumer: end demand is indirect, but the packet still matters to affordability and energy-security analysis because the company supplies multiple oil and gas systems across U.S., Egyptian, and North Sea markets while trying to preserve a future-growth lane through `Suriname`.
- Cultural and societal: APA’s filings read less like a growth-at-any-cost exploration story and more like a disciplined base-business repair story that is trying to earn the right to keep its long-cycle upside. That is an important difference.
- Industrial: this is one of the better packets in the branch for showing how upstream economics can be governed by contract structure, host-country terms, basis differentials, cost savings, and long-cycle offshore development, not only by rig count or acreage scale.
- Commodity exposure: APA remains exposed to crude, gas, and NGL prices, but it also carries `Waha` basis sensitivity, PSC effects in `Egypt`, and future offshore-development timing in `Suriname`.
- Capital allocation: the company combines dividends, buybacks, debt reduction, and staged exploration or development funding. This is not a pure "grow production" model.
- Infrastructure and political dependence: APA’s profile is materially shaped by international fiscal arrangements, host-government relationships, and long-cycle offshore execution. That makes it a cleaner international political-risk and contract-structure comparator than the branch’s domestic independents.
