## Annual report takeaways

- Canadian Natural is one of the clearest missing flagship packets for the upstream commodity layer because it is not just another shale operator or another integrated major. It is a very large independent producer built around a diverse long-life low-decline asset base that includes oil sands mining and upgrading, thermal in situ, conventional crude, natural gas, and marketing or midstream support.
- The `2025` annual report shows the scale of that machine. CNQ generated `C$15.460B` of adjusted funds flow in `2025`, reported net earnings of `C$10.820B`, and delivered record annual production of `1,571,000 boe/d`, including record annual liquids production of `1,146,000 bbl/d`, record annual oil-sands-mining-and-upgrading production of `565,102 bbl/d` of SCO, and record natural-gas production of `2,547 mmcf/d`.
- The reserve base is one of the main reasons the packet matters. Year-end `2025` total proved reserves were `15.910 billion boe`, total proved plus probable reserves were `20.750 billion boe`, about `73%` of total proved reserves were long life low decline, and the company reported a total proved reserve life index of `31` years and a total proved plus probable reserve life index of `40` years.
- The `2025` portfolio changes also matter. CNQ closed the `AOSP` asset swap with Shell on `November 1, 2025` and had previously added Chevron's Alberta assets, which strengthened oil-sands-mining exposure and expanded the reserve base. This is not just a hold-steady legacy producer. It is still using acquisition and portfolio reshaping to scale cash flow and reserve depth.
- The annual report makes the economic model explicit. CNQ emphasizes low maintenance capital, low break-even costs, long reserve life, and a free-cash-flow allocation policy that ties direct shareholder returns to net-debt thresholds. This is a useful contrast with many upstream packets where discipline is discussed more loosely. Here, the capital-allocation rule is part of the core investment logic.
- The useful difference from `Occidental`, `ConocoPhillips`, `EOG`, `Devon`, `Coterra`, `Murphy`, and `Range` is that CNQ is less about a single basin, shorter-cycle drilling cadence, or one commodity expression. It is a broad Canadian upstream platform where long-duration oil-sands and thermal assets sit beside large conventional gas and liquids production, making it one of the better branch cases for scale, reserve depth, and infrastructure-heavy commodity optionality.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` adjusted funds flow was `C$6.866B`, net earnings were `C$4.503B`, and adjusted net earnings were `C$4.568B`.
- Production read: CNQ delivered record quarterly production of `1,677,000 boe/d`, including record quarterly liquids production of `1,249,000 bbl/d`. Oil-sands-mining-and-upgrading SCO production averaged `624,754 bbl/d`, and total corporate output exceeded budgeted levels.
- Cost and margin read: the company reported industry-leading oil-sands-mining-and-upgrading operating costs of `C$22.19/bbl` of SCO, captured an average `US$8.37/bbl` SCO premium to `WTI`, and said oil-sands-mining-and-upgrading achieved its highest per-barrel netback ever in a quarter at about `C$78/bbl`.
- Capital-allocation read: CNQ returned about `C$4.0B` to shareholders in the quarter, including `C$2.4B` of direct returns through dividends and repurchases, and reduced net debt to `C$14.5B`, generating another `C$1.6B` of indirect return through deleveraging.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is the strongest proof that CNQ is a scale cash-flow machine rather than a narrow commodity trade. Record volumes, strong SCO pricing, low operating costs, and lower net debt all reinforced each other. The packet’s central point is that long-life assets can still produce record quarterly torque when pricing and utilization line up.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` adjusted funds flow was `C$4.374B`, net earnings were `C$1.348B`, and adjusted net earnings were `C$2.446B`.
- Production read: total production averaged `1,643,000 boe/d`, total liquids production averaged about `1,198,000 bbl/d`, and North American conventional production reached a record `773,000 boe/d`, including record liquids production of `329,000 bbl/d` and record natural-gas production of `2,668 mmcf/d`.
- Oil-sands read: oil-sands-mining-and-upgrading SCO production averaged `587,946 bbl/d`, with industry-leading operating costs of `C$23.73/bbl`.
- Capital-allocation read: CNQ returned roughly `C$1.5B` directly to shareholders in the quarter, including `C$1.2B` in dividends and `C$0.3B` in repurchases. The company also emphasized that crossing below `C$16B` of net debt accelerates the next stage of direct shareholder returns under its policy.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` shows why CNQ is not simply an oil-sands packet. It also has major conventional gas and liquids leverage, and that matters because it broadens the upstream cycle beyond one crude stream. The quarter reinforces the company’s identity as a diversified upstream platform with multiple volume and pricing levers.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` adjusted funds flow was `C$3.748B`, while full-year `2025` adjusted funds flow was `C$15.460B`. Full-year net earnings were `C$10.820B`.
- Production read: CNQ exited `2025` with record annual production of `1,571,000 boe/d`, record annual liquids production of `1,146,000 bbl/d`, and record annual oil-sands-mining-and-upgrading production of `565,102 bbl/d` of SCO.
- Balance-sheet and returns read: the company returned about `C$9.0B` to shareholders in `2025`, including `C$4.9B` in dividends and `C$1.4B` in share repurchases, while also reducing net debt by about `C$2.7B` to below `C$16B` at year-end.
- Portfolio read: `Q4 2025` fully reflects the impact of the `AOSP` asset swap and the enlarged reserve base from the Chevron transaction. CNQ used those changes to strengthen zero-decline and long-life production rather than to chase a more speculative growth profile.
- Structural read: this is the quarter that makes the CNQ packet work. It ties reserve growth, acquisition integration, oil-sands scale, natural-gas strength, debt reduction, and shareholder returns into one model. CNQ behaves like an upstream heavyweight, but one with a disciplined capital-distribution framework rather than an endless reinvestment bias.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: CNQ adds the large-scale independent upstream commodity system rather than another pipeline, refiner, or equipment name.
- Consumer: direct consumer exposure is limited, but the packet matters because the company supplies a large share of the physical crude and gas system that ultimately feeds transport fuels, industrial activity, electricity, petrochemicals, and export markets.
- Cultural and societal: CNQ is one of the strongest packets for the practical endurance of hydrocarbons in North American and Canadian economic life. It is a reminder that energy transition narratives coexist with very large, long-duration hydrocarbon systems that still generate record cash flow, attract capital, and support national energy security.
- Industrial: CNQ depends on several linked physical systems:
  - oil-sands-mining and upgrading at Horizon and AOSP-related assets
  - thermal in situ production
  - conventional liquids and natural gas production
  - marketing and midstream support
  - pipeline and market-access infrastructure
  - large ongoing maintenance and turnaround execution
- Commodity exposure: CNQ is highly exposed to crude, gas, and differential pricing, but the packet is more interesting than simple oil beta because asset diversity, SCO pricing, and gas scale create multiple commodity channels.
- Capital intensity: this is one of the branch’s clearest cases for long-cycle capital intensity. Much of the value sits in assets with long lives, low decline, and large sunk capital rather than in short-cycle drilling flexibility.
- Upstream and downstream cycles: CNQ is mostly an upstream packet, but it still helps the broader branch because it clarifies what the rest of the system feeds on. Refiners and pipelines depend on volume and quality from names like CNQ, while CNQ depends on transport access and market outlets to fully realize barrel value.
- Infrastructure dependence: CNQ is a strong case for the importance of pipelines, export corridors, oil-sands processing capacity, upgrader reliability, and heavy-barrel market access. The resource base is not enough by itself. Realized value depends on the transport and processing network around it.
- Shareholder-return architecture: CNQ is especially useful because it formalizes direct returns through a net-debt-threshold policy. That makes the packet a strong reference for how large upstream companies are increasingly marketed as disciplined cash-return vehicles rather than just reserve-growth stories.
