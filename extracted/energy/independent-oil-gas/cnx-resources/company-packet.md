## Annual report takeaways

- CNX is one of the more useful missing independent-E&P packets because it is not best read as another generic shale-volume company. The annual filing explicitly frames CNX as an ultra-low carbon intensity natural gas development, production, midstream, and technology company centered in the Appalachian Basin. That puts it in a different analytical lane than the branch's Permian-heavy or internationally exposed names.
- The core system is Appalachian gas, but the operating stack is broader than just dry-gas drilling. CNX's main unconventional position sits in the `Marcellus` and `Utica` across `Pennsylvania`, `Ohio`, and `West Virginia`, while the company also operates `CBM` properties in `Virginia`. The annual filing emphasizes held-by-production acreage, midstream infrastructure ownership, large data advantages, and low-cost operations as structural advantages.
- The `2025` annual-close metrics show a large but maintenance-oriented business. CNX reported total `2025` sales volumes of `629.0` Bcfe and average production of `1,723,178` Mcfe per day. The filing also said CNX repurchased `16.9M` shares for `\$528M` at an average price of `\$31.00`, which matters because management is clearly optimizing around long-term per-share value rather than simple production expansion.
- The lower-carbon adjacency is real and differentiating. In `Virginia`, CNX had rights to extract `CBM` from approximately `283,000` net `CBM` acres and to capture `RMG` from active and abandoned mines in the region. The filing also says the company had rights to extract `CBM` from approximately `1,862,000` net `CBM` acres and to capture `RMG` in several other states, even though there were no near-term plans to scale those areas. This gives CNX a methane-remediation and environmental-attribute lane that the branch's other gas packets do not capture as directly.
- CNX is also a good packet for understanding how a gas producer can present itself as a technology and policy-enabled value compounder. The annual materials and quarter prepared remarks repeatedly tie the business to `RMG`, environmental attributes, tax-credit monetization, and Radical Transparency, while still grounding the economics in low-cost Appalachia gas production and buyback-driven capital allocation.
- The annual-close commentary also shows a disciplined `2026` setup. Management described a maintenance-of-production plan, not a growth program, with `2026` annual production expected between `605` and `620` Bcfe and total capital expenditures expected between `\$540M` and `\$570M`, plus the first of three annual `\$16M` payments tied to the acquired `Utica` rights beneath the legacy `Apex` footprint.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` total sales volumes were `151.5` Bcfe and average daily production was `1,664.8` MMcfe per day. Production stayed broadly stable versus the prior two quarters even as the drilling and completion mix shifted.
- Cash-flow read: CNX generated `\$138M` of free cash flow in the quarter, marking its `26th` consecutive quarter of free cash flow generation according to management. Since the start of the company's seven-year plan in `2020`, cumulative free cash flow reached roughly `\$3.1B`.
- Capital-allocation read: CNX repurchased approximately `5.6M` shares for `\$199M` at an average price of `\$35.28` during the quarter, then another `1.2M` shares for `\$40M` after quarter-end. The company also settled the remaining portion of its convertible notes during the quarter and said year-to-date net debt had fallen by `\$193M`.
- Lower-carbon premium-product read: CNX recognized about `\$13M` of environmental-attribute net sales in `Q2` associated with approximately `4.0` Bcf of `RMG`. After quarter-end, CNX sold `45Z` tax credits for about `\$30M` in net cash proceeds and said updated federal pathway treatment should push annual `45Z` credit sales to roughly `\$40M` beginning in `2027`.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is the clearest expression of why CNX belongs in this archive. The company is not just selling gas; it is trying to convert a stable Appalachian gas base, methane-remediation rights, policy-linked tax credits, and buyback discipline into a differentiated per-share value system.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` total sales volumes were `152.4` Bcfe and average daily production was `1,693.0` MMcfe per day. Management highlighted long-lateral execution in the `Marcellus` and continued `Utica` activity rather than an aggressive volume-growth push.
- Cash-flow read: CNX generated `\$139M` of free cash flow in the quarter, marking its `25th` consecutive quarter of free cash flow generation. Management said cumulative free cash flow since `2020` had reached about `\$3.0B`.
- Capital-allocation read: CNX repurchased roughly `1.4M` shares for `\$54M` at an average price of `\$37.32` during the quarter, then an additional `0.5M` shares for `\$19M` after the quarter. The company kept emphasizing free-cash-flow-per-share rather than gross output.
- Lower-carbon premium-product read: CNX generated around `\$12M` of environmental-attribute net sales tied to about `4.2` Bcf of `RMG` in the quarter. It also expected roughly `\$20M` of `45Z` cash proceeds in `2026`, subject to final guidance, on top of approximately `\$50M` of Pennsylvania `Tier 1 AEC` net sales at current market prices.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` shows CNX as a hybrid of gas producer, methane-remediation monetizer, and capital allocator. It is much closer to an Appalachian cash-engine with policy and infrastructure adjacencies than to a pure commodity-growth name.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` total sales volumes were `152.3` Bcfe and average daily production was `1,654.8` MMcfe per day. Full-year `2025` sales volumes were `629.0` Bcfe, modestly above the high end of prior guidance.
- Cash-flow read: CNX generated `\$132M` of free cash flow in the fourth quarter, its `24th` consecutive free-cash-flow quarter, and cumulative free cash flow since `2020` reached about `\$2.9B`.
- Capital-allocation read: CNX repurchased approximately `2.9M` shares for `\$100M` at an average price of `\$34.05` during the quarter, then another `0.6M` shares for `\$22M` after quarter-end. It also reduced secured-credit-facility borrowings by `\$35M` and reduced its `2026` convertible notes principal by `\$122M` through an exchange transaction.
- Lower-carbon premium-product read: CNX recognized about `\$14M` of net environmental-attribute sales in the quarter associated with `4.3` Bcf of `RMG`. For full-year `2025`, the company captured `17.3` Bcf of `RMG`, resulting in about `\$66M` of net environmental-attribute sales.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the clean setup quarter for the CNX thesis. Management laid out a maintenance-of-production `2026` plan with commodity-sensitive free-cash-flow guidance, while showing that `RMG`, environmental attributes, and buybacks are core to the value proposition rather than side themes.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: CNX adds the Appalachian gas, `CBM`, methane-remediation, and midstream-backed version of independent E&P rather than another oil-weighted shale-manufacturing story.
- Consumer: end demand is indirect, but CNX matters to affordability and energy-security analysis because it is trying to position low-cost Appalachian gas as a lower-cost and lower-carbon supply source for domestic energy systems.
- Cultural and societal: CNX is useful because it shows a management team trying to turn environmental scrutiny into a monetizable operating model through Radical Transparency, methane monitoring, and `RMG` plus `45Z` economics rather than treating those issues as pure compliance burdens.
- Industrial: this is a better packet for methane-remediation workflow, long-lateral Appalachian execution, midstream integration, and policy-linked attribute monetization than for pure growth drilling.
- Commodity exposure: CNX is still a gas-price-sensitive upstream operator, but the filings show a larger role for hedging, basis management, environmental-attribute pricing, and tax-credit treatment than most of the branch's other gas producers.
- Capital allocation: buybacks, convertible cleanup, debt management, and free-cash-flow-per-share discipline are central to the story. This is not a volume-maximization model.
- Infrastructure and policy dependence: CNX depends on Appalachian infrastructure, hedging strategy, methane-remediation pathways, environmental-attribute markets, and tax-credit rules in a way that makes it structurally different from both Permian oil names and simpler dry-gas packets.
