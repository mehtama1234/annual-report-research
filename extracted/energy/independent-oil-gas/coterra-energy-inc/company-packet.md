## Annual report takeaways

- Coterra is a useful missing independent-E&P packet because it sits between several archetypes already in the branch. It is not a pure gas specialist like `CNX Resources`, not a cleaner single-basin manufacturing story like some Permian-focused names, and not an international optionality story like `APA Corporation`. The `2025` annual materials frame it as a premier U.S.-focused exploration and production company with a diversified operating base across the `Permian Basin`, `Marcellus Shale`, and `Anadarko Basin`.
- The `2025` filings make clear that this was still a real scale independent before the merger endpoint. Full-year `2025` revenue was about `\$7.3B`, net income was about `\$1.7B`, and cash flow from operating activities was about `\$4.0B`. The annual-close earnings release separately said free cash flow reached `\$2.0B` for full-year `2025`, up sharply from `2024`.
- The packet is especially useful because it captures a company that had just finished integrating its `2025` Delaware Basin acquisitions while still preserving a diversified commodity and basin mix. Management explicitly described the portfolio as offering scale, duration, commodity optionality, and resilient investment returns, with capital allocation able to shift across `Permian`, `Marcellus`, and `Anadarko` through commodity cycles.
- Capital discipline was central to the `2025` story. The annual-close materials said Coterra generated `\$4.0B` of operating cash flow and `\$2.0B` of free cash flow in `2025`, with an annual reinvestment rate of `54%`. The company returned `\$820M` to shareholders in `2025`, including `\$680M` of dividends and `\$140M` of repurchases, and also reduced acquisition-related term-loan debt.
- The balance-sheet framing matters because it shows why Coterra was attractive merger material rather than a stressed seller. Management highlighted year-end cash of `\$114M`, about `\$2.1B` of total liquidity, and net debt to adjusted EBITDAX of `0.8x`. This was not a distressed combination story. It was a scale-and-synergy story built on two relatively strong shale operators.
- The annual materials also add a transport-and-basis-risk dimension. Management tied part of the `2025` operating upside to lower operating costs and optimization of midstream commitments after the Delaware acquisitions, while the later quarter filings continued to note `Waha` basis weakness and expected late-`2026` pipeline relief. That makes Coterra a cleaner bridge between upstream production economics and midstream constraints than a simpler reserve-growth packet.
- The annual source chain is also worth stating directly. AnnualReports still lagged at `2024`, and the saved Coterra IR route had already become a post-merger `404` shell. In this environment, the defensible annual anchor is the filed `2025` `10-K`, not a separately recovered shareholder annual-report PDF.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q1 2026` was the last standalone reported quarter before merger close. The filed `10-Q` showed total production of `69.4` MMBoe, or `771.0` MBoe per day. Oil production was `14.7` MMBbl, or `163.7` MBbl per day. Natural gas production was `257.9` Bcf, or `2,866.0` MMcf per day. NGL production was `11.7` MMBbl, or `129.7` MBbl per day.
- Cash-flow read: the filed `10-Q` showed `\$466M` of net income and `\$1.646B` of net cash provided by operating activities in the quarter. Quarter-end cash and cash equivalents rose to `\$485M` from `\$114M` at year-end `2025`.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is important less because it was a normal standalone results quarter and more because it was not. By early May `2026`, the public disclosure chain had already shifted into merger execution. The local source set preserves the `10-Q`, the merger-approval `8-K`, and the `2026-05-07` merger-close `8-K`, which is enough to show that standalone Coterra was ending while still producing strong cash flow.
- Industrial read: the quarter filing also reinforced how basis and infrastructure conditions shape the economics. Management discussed negative early-`2026` `Waha` pricing in the `Permian` because of oversupply and maintenance, with additional late-`2026` pipeline capacity expected to ease the spread. That is exactly the kind of transport sensitivity this branch is supposed to capture.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q4 2025` was the clean annual-close quarter for the standalone story. Management reported `\$970M` of operating cash flow and `\$507M` of free cash flow in the quarter.
- Shareholder returns and debt: Coterra returned `\$263M` to shareholders in the quarter, including `\$170M` of declared dividends and `\$93M` of share repurchases that retired `4 million` shares at an average price of `\$24.37`. It also repaid `\$100M` of remaining term loans in the quarter, leaving `\$300M` outstanding at year-end to be fully repaid in February `2026`.
- Full-year setup: the same release confirmed full-year `2025` operating cash flow of `\$4.0B`, free cash flow of `\$2.0B`, an annual reinvestment rate of `54%`, and total shareholder returns of `\$820M`. It also laid out standalone `2026` guidance while separately announcing the Devon merger signed on `February 2, 2026`.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the best quarter for understanding the pre-merger Coterra thesis. It shows a disciplined, cash-generative, acquisition-integrated shale operator with enough strength to enter a strategic combination from a position of quality rather than necessity.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q3 2025` showed the system one quarter earlier, before the merger announcement but after the Delaware acquisitions had started changing the mix. Net income was `\$322M`, adjusted net income was `\$312M`, cash flow from operating activities was `\$971M`, discretionary cash flow was `\$1.148B`, and free cash flow was `\$533M`.
- Operating read: total production averaged `785.0` MBoepd, with oil at `166.8` MBopd, natural gas at `2,894.6` MMcfpd, and NGLs at `135.8` MBopd. The company turned in line `48` net wells during the quarter, including `38` in the `Permian`, `6` in `Anadarko`, and `4` in the `Marcellus`.
- Capital and leverage read: cash capex was `\$615M`, incurred capex was `\$658M`, and the company repaid `\$250M` of term loans during the quarter. Through September `2025`, it had retired `\$600M` of the `\$1.0B` term-loan balance issued for the Delaware acquisitions.
- Structural read: `Q3 2025` is the clearest pre-merger operating quarter in the chain. It shows the core earnings model: a diversified shale producer using commodity mix, basin spread, and capital discipline to produce real free cash flow while integrating acquisitions and paying down debt.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Coterra adds the diversified domestic shale and consolidation-endpoint version of independent E&P.
- Consumer: end demand is indirect, but the packet still matters to affordability and energy-security analysis because it sits inside oil, gas, NGL, power, industrial-fuel, and petrochemical-feedstock systems at meaningful scale.
- Cultural and societal: Coterra is another proof that shale companies now seek credibility through balance-sheet discipline, free-cash-flow conversion, and shareholder returns rather than through production-growth rhetoric alone.
- Industrial: this is one of the better packets for showing how basin spread, acquisition integration, transport commitments, and basis differentials can change the meaning of the same molecule. The company is upstream, but the economics are not purely geological.
- Commodity exposure: Coterra remains exposed to oil, gas, and NGL prices, but it is also exposed to basis dislocation, especially around `Waha`, and to the capital-allocation tradeoff between basin optionality and scale concentration.
- Capital allocation: dividends, repurchases, acquisition integration, and term-loan reduction all mattered at once. This was not a simple grow-volumes model.
- Consolidation: Coterra's biggest analytical value is that it becomes a structural endpoint case. The merger close on `May 7, 2026` means the packet captures how a strong standalone operator can stop having a normal public cadence because the market increasingly rewards scale, Delaware concentration, and synergy capture.
- Infrastructure dependence: Coterra also helps connect upstream and midstream. The filings show that pipeline capacity, maintenance, and regional basis can materially alter upstream realizations, even when drilling quality and operating execution remain strong.
