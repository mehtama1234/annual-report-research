## What changed

- Devon adds a distinct independent-oil-and-gas archetype to the branch: a multi-basin shale operator built around free-cash-flow generation, fixed-dividend growth, and share repurchases, rather than the broader global diversification of `ConocoPhillips`, the inventory-quality purity of `EOG Resources`, or the leveraged portfolio-transition story of `Occidental`.
- The `2025` annual close is especially useful because it captures standalone Devon before the Coterra merger changed the scale of the business. The company closed `2025` with `851,000 Boe/d` of fourth-quarter production, `390,000` barrels of oil per day, `\$8.4B` of outstanding debt, `2.4 billion Boe` of proved reserves, and a `193%` reserve-replacement rate.
- The quarter chain then shows a real structural shift:
  - `Q4 2025` and `Q1 2026` still reflect the pre-merger Devon cash-return model
  - `Q2 2026` becomes the first combined-company quarter after the `2026-05-07` merger close
- That makes Devon a useful packet for reading both upstream discipline and consolidation. The same management team that emphasized cost control and shareholder returns in standalone Devon quickly moved to a larger, more infrastructure-sensitive combined shale platform with higher scale, larger debt capacity, and bigger Delaware Basin exposure.
- AnnualReports is not authoritative for the `2025` annual year here. The direct company page still showed `2024` as of `2026-08-10`, so the packet is intentionally anchored in the SEC `ARS`, the SEC `10-K`, and the SEC quarter and exhibit chain, while the official IR route structure is preserved through a verification note because local HTML captures were blocked by Cloudflare.

## Open questions

- How much of the combined Devon story is still the old fixed-dividend and buyback discipline, and how much becomes a scale-and-integration story after absorbing Coterra?
- Does the Delaware Basin concentration create stronger free-cash-flow durability, or does it deepen exposure to local bottlenecks such as Waha pricing and midstream constraints?
- Can Devon preserve the clean shareholder-yield identity that made standalone Devon distinctive once the merged company starts carrying more portfolio complexity, larger capital needs, and post-merger integration demands?
