## What changed

- Diamondback adds a cleaner Permian pure-play than `Occidental Petroleum` or `Devon Energy Corporation`. The story is less about broad portfolio complexity and more about concentrated shale manufacturing, capital efficiency, and per-share compounding from a single core basin.
- The `2025` annual-close evidence is unusually useful because it shows a scaled independent producer that already looked like a manufacturing system, not a growth-at-any-cost driller. Diamondback ended `2025` with `3,617,856 MBOE` of estimated proved reserves, average production of `497.2 MBO/d` and `921.0 MBOE/d` for the year, and a contiguous acreage position management explicitly describes as suited to centralized development.
- The quarter chain then shows a sharp `2026` acceleration. `Q1 2026` lifted annual guidance and loosened the prior formulaic return-of-capital commitment so management could allocate cash more cyclically. `Q2 2026` pushed production above `1.0 million BOE/d`, kept capital spending roughly flat, and used the stronger oil environment to accelerate debt reduction.
- The packet also makes Diamondback a useful transport-and-bottleneck case even inside independent E&P. Management repeatedly highlighted Waha weakness, long-haul gas takeaway expansion, and the potential for Permian gas to serve LNG and data-center demand. That makes Diamondback relevant not only to upstream cycles but also to midstream dependence and regional infrastructure optionality.
- AnnualReports was not current enough for the `2025` annual year, so this packet is intentionally anchored in the SEC `ARS`, the filed `10-K`, and the `Q4 2025` through `Q2 2026` filing and exhibit chain, with official IR used to confirm company identity and route structure.

## Open questions

- Can Diamondback keep presenting itself as a low-friction free-cash-flow compounder if Waha and other Permian gas constraints persist longer than expected?
- Does the move away from a formulaic return-of-capital framework improve long-cycle value creation, or does it make the shareholder-yield story more discretionary and therefore less predictable?
- If Permian power and data-center demand becomes real rather than aspirational, does Diamondback gain a structural in-basin monetization advantage that separates it further from other shale operators?
