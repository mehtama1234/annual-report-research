  - quarter 1: Q2 2026

## Annual report takeaways

- EQT is one of the strongest packets for showing that independent energy exposure is not just an oil story. The `2025` `10-K` frames EQT as an integrated natural-gas company with upstream, gathering, and transmission operations focused in the Appalachian Basin, which immediately makes it a different system from the Permian and multi-basin oil producers already in the branch.
- The annual-close evidence shows meaningful reserve scale and a long-duration gas inventory. `Q4 2025` materials reported proved reserves up `7%` year over year to roughly `28 Tcfe`, with the increase driven by extensions, discoveries, and Olympus acquisition additions, partly offset by production. Management also highlighted a standardized measure of discounted future net cash flows of about `\$21B` and a PV-10 value of about `\$26B` at the SEC price deck.
- The annual financial picture also matters. In `Q4 2025`, EQT generated `\$1.125B` of operating cash flow, `\$1.423B` of adjusted operating cash flow attributable to EQT, and `\$744M` of free cash flow attributable to EQT on `\$655M` of capital expenditures. Full-year `2025` results showed `\$5.126B` of operating cash flow and `\$2.503B` of free cash flow attributable to EQT.
- Balance-sheet repair remained central. Year-end `2025` total debt and net debt were about `\$7.8B` and `\$7.69B`, down materially from the prior year. That matters because EQT is explicitly trying to convert Appalachia scale, operational efficiency, and gas-market positioning into a cleaner lower-leverage free-cash-flow franchise.
- The annual source chain is complete even though AnnualReports lagged at `2024` and the official EQT IR routes were Cloudflare-blocked locally. The authoritative annual packet comes from the SEC `ARS`, the filed `10-K`, and the annual-close Exhibit `99.1` chain.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` sold `634 Bcfe`, above the comparable prior-year level, and management raised full-year `2026` production guidance by about `90 Bcfe` to `2,375 - 2,450 Bcfe`.
- Cash flow and balance sheet: operating cash flow was `\$1.048B`, adjusted operating cash flow attributable to EQT was about `\$1.014B`, and free cash flow attributable to EQT was `\$330M`. Net debt exited the quarter at about `\$5.54B`, with an additional `\$115M` of `2026` debentures repaid after quarter end.
- Operating system: capital expenditures were `\$666M`, `9%` below the low end of guidance, and management also lowered full-year capital-spending guidance by `\$25M`. The release tied stronger performance to compression benefits, system optimization, lower curtailments, and marketing execution despite wider basis.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is the quarter that makes EQT strategically important to the branch. Management signed a `5-year` LNG offtake SPA for `0.5 MTPA` beginning in `2028` and a `10-year` premium power supply agreement with `CPV Shay Energy Center` for `325,000 Dth/d`, explicitly linking Appalachian gas to LNG and power-demand monetization rather than just spot Henry Hub exposure.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` was a very strong gas-price and execution quarter. EQT sold `618 Bcfe`, above the high end of guidance, while realizing natural-gas pricing above `\$5/Mcf`.
- Cash flow and balance sheet: operating cash flow reached `\$3.055B`, and free cash flow attributable to EQT hit a record `\$1.832B`. Net debt ended the quarter at about `\$5.67B`, meaning the company moved sharply closer to its long-term debt target within a single quarter.
- Operating system: capital expenditures were `\$608M`, `4%` below the low end of guidance, helped by operational efficiency and lower-than-expected infrastructure spending. Management also highlighted strong performance during `Winter Storm Fern`, which is useful evidence that execution and system pressure optimization mattered alongside price.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is the clearest quarter for understanding EQT as a gas-harvest platform. When price and execution line up, the company can generate outsized free cash flow quickly, de-lever rapidly, and still preserve dry-gas growth optionality tied to future LNG and domestic power demand.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` closed the year with `609 Bcfe` of sales volume, `\$1.125B` of operating cash flow, and `\$744M` of free cash flow attributable to EQT.
- Annual-close metrics: proved reserves increased `7%` year over year to around `28 Tcfe`, with Olympus assets adding to the inventory base. Total debt and net debt were about `\$7.8B` and `\$7.69B`, respectively.
- Capital allocation and growth posture: management elected to invest the first `\$580 - \$640M` of post-dividend free cash flow in `2026` into high-return, infrastructure-focused growth projects rather than treating every dollar of excess cash as immediate shareholder distribution. That is a meaningful difference from more formulaic shale-return stories.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the best quarter for understanding why EQT is a flagship gas packet. It combines Appalachian reserve depth, curtailment and basis awareness, infrastructure-led growth choices, and a visible debt-reduction path inside one domestic natural-gas system.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: EQT adds the gas-heavy Appalachia version of independent upstream exposure.
- Consumer: end demand is still indirect, but this packet sits closer than the oil names to power generation, heating, industrial gas demand, LNG exports, and data-center-adjacent electricity demand.
- Cultural and societal: the broader signal is that natural gas companies increasingly justify growth not just through commodity-price upside but through claims of reliability, domestic energy security, export relevance, and power demand support.
- Industrial: EQT is useful because its economics are shaped by compression, gathering, transmission, curtailment management, basis exposure, and infrastructure timing just as much as by drilling quality or reserve volume.
- Commodity exposure: Henry Hub still matters, but Appalachia basis, curtailment strategy, and marketing optimization materially change how gas prices reach cash flow. This packet is therefore more transport- and market-structure-sensitive than the oil-heavy upstream names.
- Capital allocation: EQT is one of the clearest examples in the branch of a management team using free cash flow for both aggressive de-levering and selective infrastructure growth rather than only dividends and buybacks.
- Infrastructure dependence: this packet is a direct bridge from upstream into pipeline, LNG, and power-infrastructure research. The value of EQT's gas increasingly depends on how successfully it can reach Gulf Coast LNG demand and premium domestic power markets.
