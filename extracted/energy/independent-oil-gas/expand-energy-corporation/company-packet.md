  - quarter 1: Q2 2026

## Annual report takeaways

- Expand Energy is one of the clearest flagship packets for the branch's natural-gas buildout because the `2025` annual filing frames it as the largest natural gas producer in the U.S. after the Southwestern merger. That makes it a different upstream system from the branch's oil-heavy names and also different from `EQT`'s more Appalachia-specific gas packet.
- The annual filing is useful not only for scale but for geography and system structure. Expand's `10-K` describes operations across Louisiana, Texas, Pennsylvania, West Virginia, and Ohio, which means its strategic value is tied to multi-basin optionality and to how it connects production into demand markets, not just to a single rock-quality story.
- The annual-close financial picture also matters. `Q4 2025` materials reported `\$956M` of operating cash flow, `\$553M` of net income, `\$481M` of adjusted net income, and `\$1.425B` of adjusted EBITDAX. Full-year `2025` operating cash flow reached `\$4.575B`, showing how much gas-scale cash generation the post-merger system can produce when conditions cooperate.
- `Q4 2025` is also where management laid out the first clean post-merger operating template: `11 - 12` rigs, about `\$2.85B` of capex, and roughly `7.5 Bcfe/d` of expected daily production in `2026`. That matters because Expand is not being presented as a pure harvest story. It is being presented as a gas-scale platform with enough inventory and market access to defend volume while improving the balance sheet.
- The annual source chain is unusually clean for this branch. AnnualReports already displayed the `2025` annual package, the official IR site exposed the `2025 Annual Report` route directly, and the SEC `ARS` plus filed `10-K` complete the authoritative annual evidence set.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` delivered `\$1.096B` of operating cash flow, `\$522M` of net income, `\$317M` of adjusted net income, `\$1.183B` of adjusted EBITDAX, and net production of about `7.48 Bcfe/d`, with `92%` natural gas.
- Balance sheet and capital allocation: total debt fell to `\$3.7B`, quarter-end net debt was about `\$3.1B`, and management highlighted a peer-leading leverage ratio of roughly `0.5x`. The company also repurchased about `\$530M` of stock in the quarter and about `\$850M` year to date through July 24, 2026.
- Operating system: management reaffirmed `2026` production guidance of `7.4 - 7.6 Bcfe/d` and capex of about `\$2.75B - \$2.95B`, while noting 12 operated rigs in the quarter, 55 wells drilled, and 48 wells turned in line.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is the quarter that makes Expand strategically distinct. Management tied the story not only to operating discipline and leverage reduction, but also to commercial expansion through the announced `Twin Eagle` acquisition, which it said would create North America's leading integrated natural gas company. That is a bigger claim than just being a low-cost producer.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` was a high-cash-flow gas quarter. Expand generated `\$2.402B` of operating cash flow, `\$1.159B` of net income, `\$923M` of adjusted net income, and `\$1.968B` of adjusted EBITDAX.
- Balance sheet and returns: quarter-end total debt was `\$5.0B`, down by about `\$1.3B` from the April senior-note redemption, and quarter-end net debt was about `\$2.8B`, down `\$1.6B` from year-end `2025`. Management also disclosed `\$150M` of buybacks through April 24, 2026.
- Operating system: production was about `7.44 Bcfe/d`, with `93%` natural gas, while management reaffirmed full-year guidance near `7.5 Bcfe/d`.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` shows Expand as a gas-scale deleveraging and market-extension machine. It was not just harvesting commodity upside. It used the quarter to strengthen the balance sheet and signed a `20-year` `Delfin` SPA for about `1.15 million tonnes` of LNG offtake per year, extending the gas story further into export-linked demand.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` reported `\$956M` of operating cash flow, `\$553M` of net income, `\$481M` of adjusted net income, and `\$1.425B` of adjusted EBITDAX.
- Operating profile: production was about `7.40 Bcfe/d`, with `92%` natural gas, up `15%` from the prior-year quarter.
- Full-year picture: full-year `2025` operating cash flow reached `\$4.575B`, which helps explain why management entered `2026` prioritizing both debt reduction and eventual shareholder returns.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the best quarter for understanding the initial post-merger setup. Expand closed the year as a large, gas-heavy, multi-basin platform with enough scale to talk credibly about debt reduction, capital discipline, and market reach all at once rather than as separate objectives.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Expand adds the merger-built, multi-basin, scale-gas flagship inside independent upstream.
- Consumer: end demand remains indirect, but this packet sits close to electricity, heating, industrial fuel, LNG exports, and broader energy-affordability claims.
- Cultural and societal: management increasingly sells natural gas as a reliability and energy-security fuel, not just as a commodity. The filings and releases repeatedly tie the company to affordability, reliability, and growing demand centers.
- Industrial: Expand is a strong example of how upstream value increasingly depends on commercial reach, LNG linkage, and marketing infrastructure, not only on drilling quality or reserve depth.
- Commodity exposure: gas pricing still matters, but the company's strategy is increasingly to reduce simple commodity dependence by widening market access and deepening commercial integration.
- Capital allocation: the quarter chain shows a fast progression from debt reduction to mixed capital returns, with meaningful buybacks layered on top of deleveraging.
- Infrastructure dependence: this is a direct bridge packet between upstream gas production and the infrastructure stack behind it, especially LNG, market access, and now commercial optimization through `Twin Eagle`.
