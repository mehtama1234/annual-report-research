## What changed

- Expand Energy adds a different natural-gas flagship than `EQT`. `EQT` is primarily the Appalachia-gas and basis-management packet. Expand is the post-merger, multi-basin, market-connected gas-scale packet that explicitly tries to turn production leadership into national commercial reach.
- The `2025` annual filing matters because it frames Expand as the largest natural gas producer in the U.S. after the Southwestern merger, with operations in Louisiana, Texas, Pennsylvania, West Virginia, and Ohio. That makes the company a better bridge between Haynesville, Appalachia, LNG pull-through, and commercial gas-market integration than a single-basin shale story.
- The quarter chain shows a very fast balance-sheet and capital-allocation transition. `Q4 2025` still emphasized debt reduction and post-merger operating plans. `Q1 2026` converted strong gas cash flow into material debt paydown and added a long-term `Delfin` LNG offtake agreement. `Q2 2026` then pivoted harder toward shareholder returns and commercial expansion, including large buybacks and the announced `Twin Eagle` acquisition.
- That makes Expand especially useful for the branch's bigger-picture theme map. This is not just commodity exposure. It is a gas platform whose strategy increasingly depends on marketing reach, LNG access, balance-sheet flexibility, and the ability to turn scale into integrated market position.
- Unlike many peers in this branch, both AnnualReports and the official IR site were usable for this name as of `Monday, August 10, 2026`, so the packet can preserve a cleaner confirmation chain alongside the SEC filings.

## Open questions

- Can Expand convert production leadership into durable commercial and margin advantages, or does scale still mostly leave it exposed to the same gas-price cyclicality as other independents?
- Does the move toward marketing and commercial integration through `Twin Eagle` create a stronger moat, or does it simply add another layer of complexity to a business already digesting the Southwestern merger?
- If LNG and power demand stay supportive, does Expand become one of the clearest winners from U.S. gas-demand growth, or do infrastructure and basis constraints still absorb too much of the upside?
