## Annual report takeaways

- Matador is one of the stronger missing upstream packets because it adds a more infrastructure-shaped version of independent oil and gas exposure. The main story is not just Delaware drilling scale. It is control over upstream execution through a coordinated midstream system, especially `San Mateo`, plus the ability to keep extending inventory through selective acquisitions and operational density.
- The annual-close materials show a large and concentrated Delaware platform. At year-end `2025`, Matador reported `667.0 MMBOE` of total proved reserves, up from `612.7 MMBOE` at year-end `2024`, with approximately `56%` oil and roughly `99%` of reserves tied to the Delaware Basin. Standardized measure was `\$6.99B` and PV-10 was `\$8.24B`.
- Production and reserve depth both moved higher in `2025`. Full-year output reached `220,334` BOE/d, including `43.7 million` Bbl of oil and `193.3 Bcf` of natural gas. The company said it completed and turned to sales `258` gross (`129.3` net) horizontal wells in the Delaware Basin during `2025`, while identifying `5,295` gross (`1,802` net) engineered future drilling locations there.
- The infrastructure angle is material in the annual report, not an afterthought. San Mateo expanded the Marlan processing plant during `2025`, increasing designed cryogenic processing capacity by `38%` to `720 MMcf/d`. At year-end `2025`, San Mateo's midstream system included `340` miles of natural gas gathering pipelines, `43` miles of large-diameter gathering pipelines, `16` commercial salt water disposal wells, approximately `195` miles of produced-water gathering pipelines, and designed produced-water disposal capacity of `475,000` Bbl/d.
- Cash generation and capital returns were strong, but they were tied to that integrated system. In `2025`, Matador generated `\$2.43B` of operating cash flow and `\$437.0M` of adjusted free cash flow, received `\$136.7M` of cash distributions from `San Mateo`, increased the quarterly dividend, repurchased `1,351,328` shares for `\$55.8M`, and amended San Mateo's credit facility to raise commitments to `\$1.10B`.
- The annual report also makes clear that infrastructure dependence is a risk, not only an advantage. The filing repeatedly notes exposure to gathering, processing, transportation, minimum-volume commitments, Delaware Basin federal-land permitting, and borrowing-base risk. That makes Matador a useful packet for the archive because it shows how an upstream company increasingly behaves like an asset-and-network orchestrator rather than just a reserve holder.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` average production was `215,631` BOE/d, including `126,106` Bbl/d of oil and `537.1 MMcf/d` of natural gas. Net cash provided by operating activities was `\$937.1M`, adjusted EBITDA was `\$781.0M`, and adjusted free cash flow was `\$303.2M`.
- Operating read: management said it delivered record average oil production despite elective Waha shut-ins and maintenance-related downtime that reduced second-quarter volumes by about `10,000` BOE/d. It still exceeded the guided oil range of `123,000` to `125,000` Bbl/d and turned to sales `23.7` net operated wells, including the first `3.4`-mile laterals on the Guss pad.
- Strategic read: `Q2 2026` is the clearest quarter for why Matador belongs in this branch. The company used free cash flow to repay over `\$200M` of borrowings tied to the May `2026` Federal lease sale, announced the Paloma and Ridge Runner acquisitions, and highlighted that San Mateo closed the Cardinal Midstream acquisition on `July 31, 2026`, pushing designed gas-processing capacity above `1.0 Bcf/d`.
- Structural read: the quarter shows an upstream company that is actively using midstream control, water handling, processing capacity, and acreage additions to change well economics and inventory quality. That is a different signal than a plain "oil prices were supportive" reading.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` average production was `207,594` BOE/d, including `120,277` Bbl/d of oil and `523.9 MMcf/d` of natural gas. Net cash provided by operating activities was `\$470.5M`, adjusted EBITDA was `\$577.2M`, and adjusted free cash flow was `\$113.3M`.
- Operating read: the company said newer wells outperformed initial expectations by roughly `2,600` Bbl/d of oil and that the combined Matador-San Mateo system helped preserve flow assurance through Winter Storm Fern and negative Waha pricing. It also highlighted field-produced natural gas use in completions and produced-water recycling as direct sources of cost savings.
- Midstream and liquidity read: San Mateo distributed `\$31.6M` to Matador during the quarter. Combined midstream adjusted EBITDA was `\$82.2M`. Management said it anticipated fully repaying RBL borrowings later that month, lifting liquidity to about `\$2.2B` on a `\$2.25B` elected commitment and a borrowing-base framework with capacity up to `\$3.25B`.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is the best quarter for seeing Matador as an operating-system packet rather than a simple Delaware driller. Flow assurance, recycled water, owned infrastructure, and balance-sheet repair all showed up together.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` was the annual-close quarter. Full-year `2025` average production reached `220,334` BOE/d, including `119,743` Bbl/d of oil. Full-year adjusted free cash flow was `\$437.0M`.
- Reserve and inventory read: year-end proved reserves were `667.0 MMBOE`, up `9%` year over year, while the company continued building a long inventory runway in the Delaware Basin. It also emphasized improved well performance, larger batch developments, and lower future finding-and-development costs.
- Capital and shareholder-return read: management tied the `2025` close to stronger San Mateo distributions, dividend growth, buybacks, and a growing network effect from Delaware infrastructure and processing expansion rather than to one single-quarter spike in realized prices.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the cleanest setup quarter for the Matador thesis. It shows that the franchise is built on coordinated acreage, infrastructure, development cadence, and capital discipline, not only on commodity beta.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Matador adds the upstream-plus-midstream-coordination version of shale exposure rather than a pure drilling-volume case.
- Consumer: end demand is indirect, but this packet still matters to broader affordability and energy-security analysis because the company is explicitly trying to increase reliable domestic oil and gas throughput into Gulf Coast and downstream demand systems.
- Cultural and societal: management frames the business around domestic energy supply, disciplined returns, and operational self-help rather than empire-building. The repeated emphasis is on reliability, resilience, and practical infrastructure control.
- Industrial: this is one of the better packets in the branch for showing how water handling, gas processing, gathering, treatment capacity, and flow assurance shape upstream economics. The industrial system behind the well matters almost as much as the rock.
- Commodity exposure: oil drives the earnings narrative, but gas pricing and takeaway still materially shape field decisions. Negative Waha conditions are not abstract here; they alter shut-in choices, completion economics, and the value of dedicated transport and processing.
- Capital allocation: Matador combines dividends, debt paydown, selective M&A, and midstream expansion. It is not running a single-metric return model.
- Infrastructure dependence: this is the main reason the company is useful. Matador shows how independent E&P increasingly depends on owned and aligned infrastructure to defend margins, maintain drilling cadence, and support acquisitions.
