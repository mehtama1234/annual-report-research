## What changed

- Matador adds a different independent-E&P archetype than `Permian Resources` or `Diamondback`. It is still Delaware-focused, but the defining mechanism is not only scale or acreage capture. It is upstream-plus-midstream coordination through `San Mateo`, flow assurance, water handling, gas processing, and infrastructure-backed drilling execution.
- The `2025` annual materials make that distinction explicit. Matador finished `2025` with `667.0 MMBOE` of total proved reserves, up `9%` year over year, with about `56%` oil and roughly `99%` of reserves tied to the Delaware Basin. It also highlighted `\$136.7M` of cash distributions from `San Mateo`, a `38%` increase in designed processing capacity to `720 MMcf/d`, and a Delaware inventory measured not just in acres but in thousands of engineered locations.
- The quarter chain shows how the infrastructure layer changes the operating outcome. `Q4 2025` framed the annual close through reserve growth, cash returns, and San Mateo expansion. `Q1 2026` showed how Matador used internally aligned midstream and recycled-water systems to hold volumes through winter weather and negative Waha conditions while paying down its RBL and moving toward `\$2.2B` of liquidity. `Q2 2026` then showed how the same system supported record oil production, additional acquisitions, San Mateo's Cardinal Midstream step-up, and another round of raised guidance.
- This makes Matador a strong packet for the archive's broader thesis that upstream economics are increasingly infrastructure-shaped. It is not enough to own good rock. Throughput, gathering, water recycling, processing, transport contracts, and control of development cadence all influence free cash flow and resilience.
- The source chain is clean. AnnualReports lagged at `2024`, but the official company IR site directly exposed the `2025` annual report and the quarter materials, while SEC provides the authoritative filed annual and quarter chain.

## Open questions

- Can Matador keep funding its acquisition and inventory-extension strategy while still reaching its stated `1.0x` leverage target by the end of `2027`?
- How durable is the midstream edge if Waha remains structurally weak, competing processors add capacity, or San Mateo needs heavier capital to maintain its flow-assurance advantage?
- Do the Federal lease sale, Paloma, and Ridge Runner additions improve long-run drilling returns enough to justify the added debt and execution burden, or are they mostly a near-term inventory extension?
