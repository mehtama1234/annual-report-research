## Annual report takeaways

- Murphy is a strong missing independent-E&P packet because it adds a different structure than the branch's shale-manufacturing names. The official IR profile explicitly frames the company as an independent oil and natural gas exploration and production company with a focused portfolio of onshore and offshore assets, with Gulf of America and offshore Canada complementing the North American onshore business and exploration focused in `Gulf of America`, `Vietnam`, and `Cote d'Ivoire`.
- That portfolio mix matters. Murphy still has the familiar onshore base in `Eagle Ford`, `Tupper Montney`, and `Kaybob Duvernay`, but the `2025` and early-`2026` materials keep emphasizing offshore and international optionality rather than only shale scale. This makes the packet more useful for reading exploration risk, offshore development timing, and geopolitical exposure than a simpler domestic shale case.
- The annual-close materials show a company that stayed disciplined while still seeding a longer-cycle opportunity set. Full-year `2025` production was `182,300 BOEPD`, at the high end of annual guidance. Murphy returned `\$286M` to shareholders in `2025`, including `\$186M` of dividends and `\$100M` of buybacks, while paying down credit-facility debt, extending liquidity, and increasing the quarterly dividend by `8%` for `2026`.
- Reserve durability was central to the `2025` story. The company said preliminary year-end `2025` proved reserves were `715 MMBOE`, reserve replacement was `103%`, and reserve life remained `11 years`, with `57%` proved developed reserves. That makes Murphy a useful contrast to faster-turn, inventory-heavy onshore names because management is explicitly trying to show that exploration and development are replenishing the base rather than merely adding volatility.
- The balance-sheet framing also matters. At `December 31, 2025`, Murphy said it had about `\$1.6B` of liquidity, including `\$377M` of cash and `\$1.25B` undrawn under the revolver before the facility was later upsized to `\$2.0B`. Total debt at year-end was about `\$1.4B`, and management emphasized both debt paydown and refinancing work. This is not an "explore first, repair later" packet.
- The most distinctive annual theme is the coexistence of capital discipline and frontier or semi-frontier optionality. In the same year-end materials, Murphy highlighted a successful `Hai Su Vang-2X` appraisal in offshore `Vietnam`, a new petroleum agreement in Morocco covering more than `4 million` acres with a `75%` working interest, and continued progress toward `Lac Da Vang` first oil in `Q4 2026`.
- AnnualReports still lagged at `2024`, but Murphy's own IR site already exposed the official `2025 Annual Report` and a clean quarter-results chain. This is one of the cleaner examples in the branch of why AnnualReports should be used for taxonomy while company IR and SEC remain the real source of record.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` produced `169,000 BOEPD`, at the upper end of guidance, and Murphy earned `\$232.2M` of net income attributable to Murphy, or `\$1.59` per diluted share.
- Cash-flow read: the official quarter-results page showed `\$655.9M` of net cash provided by continuing operations activities, `\$588.4M` of operating cash flow excluding working-capital adjustments, `\$592.7M` of adjusted EBITDA, `\$632.0M` of adjusted EBITDAX, and `\$110.0M` of free cash flow.
- Strategic read: the quarter is most useful as an exploration-and-development acceleration read. Murphy announced the `Bubale-1X` oil discovery in Block `CI-709` offshore `Cote d'Ivoire`, completed the `Hai Su Vang` appraisal program, and finalized pipeline installation plus FSO launch at `Lac Da Vang` in `Vietnam`.
- Capital read: subsequent to the quarter, management increased the full-year capital midpoint from `\$1.25B` to `\$1.55B` to advance appraisal and development opportunities. That is a real signal that Murphy is choosing to lean harder into offshore and future-growth projects rather than simply maximizing near-term distribution.
- Balance-sheet and return read: Murphy still paid `\$50M` of quarterly dividends in `Q2`, had about `\$2.48B` of liquidity at `June 30, 2026`, and carried about `\$1.55B` of total debt. There were no share repurchases in the quarter, which helps show the capital-allocation tradeoff directly: more project spending, less buyback activity.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` beat the top end of guidance with production of `174 MBOEPD`. The SEC Exhibit `99.1` earnings release showed `\$53.0M` of net income attributable to Murphy, `\$46.5M` of adjusted net income, `\$382.9M` of adjusted EBITDA, `\$465.7M` of adjusted EBITDAX, `\$429.2M` of operating cash flow excluding working-capital adjustments, and `\$41.4M` of free cash flow.
- Operating read: the quarter shows Murphy's portfolio breadth more clearly than almost any annual summary line. Management highlighted activity in the Gulf of America, `Hai Su Vang-3X` in `Vietnam`, and `Bubale-1X` in `Cote d'Ivoire`, all while keeping onshore production and well performance competitive.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is a good bridge quarter between the `2025` reserve-and-balance-sheet framing and the more ambitious `Q2 2026` capital step-up. It still looks like a disciplined, cash-aware operator, but the project queue is visibly broadening.
- Industrial read: this quarter is also useful because it captures the tradeoff at the heart of the Murphy packet. Higher exploration expense and active offshore appraisal work make the earnings profile less mechanically clean than a pure shale packet, but they also create the optionality that management is trying to monetize over the next several years.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` produced `181,400 BOEPD`, exceeding the midpoint of quarterly guidance, while full-year `2025` production reached `182,300 BOEPD` at the high end of guidance.
- Cash-flow and return read: the quarter-results page showed `\$11.9M` of quarterly net income attributable to Murphy and `\$104.2M` for full-year `2025`, plus `\$249.6M` of quarterly operating cash flow and about `\$1.25B` for full-year `2025`. Free cash flow was `\$109.6M` in the quarter. The cleaner year-end message, though, is the capital-return and balance-sheet mix: `\$286M` returned to shareholders in `2025`, `\$50M` of revolver debt paid down in `Q4`, and improved liquidity through both refinancing and revolver expansion.
- Portfolio read: the year-end quarter was packed with strategic markers. Murphy highlighted successful `Hai Su Vang-2X` appraisal in offshore `Vietnam`, `Lac Da Vang` development progress, `11` years of reserve life, `715 MMBOE` of preliminary proved reserves, and entry into Morocco's `Gharb Deep Offshore` block.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the best quarter for understanding the Murphy thesis before the `2026` offshore acceleration became more explicit. It shows a company that is not merely harvesting a mature base. It is using the base to finance a wider portfolio of international and offshore opportunities while still trying to look disciplined enough for public-market capital.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Murphy adds the offshore-and-exploration-option-heavy independent-E&P version of the energy lane rather than another pure shale-manufacturing case.
- Consumer: end demand is indirect, but the packet still matters to affordability and energy-security analysis because Murphy sits inside crude, natural gas, export, refining-feedstock, and power-linked hydrocarbon systems that depend on both short-cycle onshore output and longer-cycle offshore development.
- Cultural and societal: Murphy is another proof that public upstream companies now have to sell discipline first, even when they still want to fund technically ambitious exploration. The filings are trying to reassure investors that offshore optionality will not come at the expense of liquidity or shareholder returns.
- Industrial: this is one of the cleaner packets for showing how an upstream company can operate across very different industrial clocks at once:
  - quick-cycle North American onshore development
  - offshore development projects with multiyear first-oil timing
  - exploration wells whose value is probabilistic rather than immediate
- Commodity exposure: Murphy remains exposed to oil and gas prices, but its economics are also shaped by exploration success, development timing, and the capital burden of offshore execution in `Vietnam`, `Cote d'Ivoire`, and other frontier or emerging areas.
- Capital allocation: dividends, selective buybacks, debt maturity management, liquidity expansion, and rising offshore-development capex all matter at once. This is not a simple "drill more" or "buy back more" model.
- Infrastructure dependence: `Lac Da Vang` pipeline work, FSO launch, Gulf of America development activity, and offshore execution all make infrastructure and project sequencing central. For Murphy, barrels are not enough; delivery systems and project timing determine when those barrels become cash flow.
- Geopolitical and jurisdictional exposure: Murphy is a useful contrast to more domestic independents because a meaningful part of its long-term upside depends on host-country arrangements, offshore permits, development partners, and execution in places like `Vietnam`, `Cote d'Ivoire`, and Morocco.
