## What changed

- Murphy adds a cleaner offshore-and-exploration option layer than the branch's more manufacturing-style shale independents. The company still has a meaningful North American onshore base, but the filings make clear that the portfolio also depends on offshore Canada, the Gulf of America, and exploration and development activity in `Vietnam` and `Cote d'Ivoire`.
- The `2025` annual and year-end materials frame Murphy as a disciplined independent that is trying to fund high-impact offshore optionality without abandoning shareholder returns or balance-sheet control. In `2025`, management emphasized reserve life, liquidity, dividend growth, and debt-maturity work at the same time it highlighted `Hai Su Vang`, `Lac Da Vang`, and Morocco entry.
- The target quarter window is a normal one for this name as of `2026-08-10`:
  - `Q2 2026`
  - `Q1 2026`
  - `Q4 2025`
- The quarter chain matters because it shows the system evolving from reserve replacement and Vietnam development setup at year-end `2025` into a more explicitly exploration-led `2026` story with `Bubale-1X`, `Hai Su Vang` follow-up, and increased capital spending.
- AnnualReports lagged at `2024`, but Murphy's own investor-relations site did not. That makes this a cleaner proof that the archive should use AnnualReports for taxonomy and company IR plus SEC as the authoritative reporting chain.

## Open questions

- Can Murphy keep balancing capital discipline with a more ambitious offshore opportunity set, or does higher appraisal and development spending eventually start to crowd out the simpler return-of-capital story?
- How much of the long-term value case depends on the offshore pipeline actually converting into commercial barrels rather than remaining a sequence of technically interesting but capital-hungry option projects?
- Does Murphy deserve to trade more like a disciplined North American independent, or more like a smaller exploration-and-development platform with a stronger offshore and geopolitical edge?
