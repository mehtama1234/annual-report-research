## Annual report takeaways

- Occidental is one of the more useful upstream packets for showing that "independent oil and gas" is still too blunt a label. The company is fundamentally an upstream producer, but it also carries a meaningful midstream and marketing earnings stream, Western Midstream equity exposure, enhanced-oil-recovery capabilities, and a corporate identity that explicitly ties energy production to carbon management and lower-carbon technologies.
- The official `2025` annual report framed the year as a scale-plus-discipline story: record annual production around `1.43 million BOEPD`, roughly `\$4.3B` of free cash flow before working capital, principal debt reduced to about `\$13.8B`, an expanded resource base, and large cost reductions. That is a different read from `EOG`, which is more about drilling inventory quality and clean shareholder-return discipline, and from `ConocoPhillips`, which is more about upstream breadth and capital-allocation scale.
- The filed `10-K` and `Q4 2025` results show full-year `2025` ending with softer fourth-quarter pricing but still meaningful operating strength. `Q4 2025` operating cash flow reached about `\$2.6B`, operating cash flow before working capital about `\$2.7B`, quarterly free cash flow before working capital about `\$1.0B`, and production averaged `1,481 Mboed`. Year-end proved reserves were about `4.6 billion BOE`, with an all-in reserves replacement ratio of `98%` and an organic reserves replacement ratio of `107%`.
- The annual-close packet is also a transition story. Occidental closed the sale of OxyChem on `2026-01-02`, and the `Q4 2025` / annual materials already frame the chemicals business as discontinued operations. That matters because the branch should now read Occidental less as a mixed upstream-chemicals company and more as a deleveraging upstream-plus-midstream-plus-carbon-management platform.
- The annual-source chain is clear even though AnnualReports was not locally usable. The official Oxy annual-reports page cleanly exposes the `2025 Annual Report`, and the SEC `10-K` provides the authoritative filed annual content.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income attributable to common stockholders was `\$2.8B`, diluted EPS was `\$2.75`, and adjusted income from continuing operations attributable to common stockholders was `\$2.4B`, or adjusted EPS of `\$2.40`.
- Cash flow and balance sheet: operating cash flow from continuing operations was `\$5.1B`, operating cash flow before working capital was `\$4.6B`, free cash flow before working capital from continuing operations was `\$3.0B`, and capital expenditures were `\$1.6B`. Principal debt was reduced by `\$1.9B` in the quarter to `\$11.8B`, moving closer to the `\$10.0B` milestone.
- Operating system: total global production averaged `1,433 Mboed`, above the high end of guidance. Pre-tax oil-and-gas income was `\$2.8B`, helped by sharply stronger oil prices, while midstream and marketing swung to `\$1.3B` of pre-tax income from a prior-quarter loss.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is a clean demonstration that Occidental is not only an upstream volume-and-price story. Better crude margins related to timing of crude sales, gas transportation optimizations, and the company’s ability to convert price strength into cash and debt reduction all matter materially.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income attributable to common stockholders was `\$3.2B`, diluted EPS was `\$3.13`, and adjusted income from continuing operations attributable to common stockholders was `\$1.1B`, or adjusted EPS of `\$1.06`.
- Cash flow and balance sheet: free cash flow before working capital from continuing operations was `\$1.7B`, capital expenditures were `\$1.6B`, and by `2026-05-05` Occidental had repaid `\$7.1B` of principal debt, reducing principal debt to `\$13.3B`.
- Operating system: production averaged `1,426 Mboed`, above guidance, led by the Permian, Rockies, and Gulf of America. Oil-and-gas pre-tax income rose to `\$1.0B` from `\$0.7B` in `Q4 2025`. Midstream and marketing reported a pre-tax loss of `\$87M`, though management said adjusted results still exceeded guidance.
- Transition signal: `Q1 2026` includes a large gain on the sale of OxyChem inside discontinued operations, partially offset by derivative losses and early debt-redemption premiums. This is the key bridge quarter for understanding how the post-chemicals portfolio should be evaluated.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is less clean than `Q2`, but more strategically important. It shows Occidental trying to simplify the portfolio, reduce debt, and preserve operating discipline even while reported earnings are distorted by divestiture accounting and market volatility.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net loss attributable to common stockholders was `\$68M`, or `\$0.07` per diluted share, while adjusted income attributable to common stockholders was `\$315M`, or `\$0.31` per diluted share.
- Cash flow and capital: operating cash flow was `\$2.6B`, operating cash flow before working capital was `\$2.7B`, quarterly free cash flow before working capital was about `\$1.0B`, and capital spending including discontinued operations was `\$1.8B`.
- Operating system: total company production averaged `1,481 Mboed`, above guidance. Oil-and-gas pre-tax income was `\$0.7B` as lower realized prices weighed on results. Midstream and marketing reported `\$204M` of pre-tax income, helped by better gas margins from Permian transportation optimization, lower long-haul crude transportation costs, and higher sulfur prices at Al Hosn.
- Strategic context: year-end proved reserves were `4.6 billion BOE`. The quarter also made explicit that OxyChem closed on `2026-01-02` and would be treated as discontinued operations across all periods presented.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the best quarter for seeing Occidental’s hybrid character. Even when upstream price realization weakens, the company is still leaning on cost control, reserve replacement, midstream and marketing, and portfolio repair rather than just chasing production growth.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Occidental adds the leveraged, deleveraging, and portfolio-transition version of independent oil and gas exposure.
- Consumer: end demand is indirect, but this packet still sits inside transportation fuels, petrochemical feedstocks, export systems, and industrial energy use.
- Cultural and societal: the broader signal is that large hydrocarbon companies are trying to preserve legitimacy and shareholder support by pairing conventional production with cost discipline, balance-sheet repair, and a carbon-management narrative.
- Industrial: this packet is useful because it ties upstream operations to midstream and marketing optimization, sulfur economics, WES equity income, and enhanced recovery rather than treating barrels as the whole story.
- Commodity exposure: Occidental remains highly exposed to crude and gas prices, but the midstream/marketing swing and the discontinued-operations transition show that portfolio structure can materially change how commodity moves reach reported earnings.
- Capital allocation: debt reduction is a central operating output here, not a side project. The packet should be read through the lens of cash conversion, debt milestones, and post-divestiture simplification.
- Carbon management: Occidental stands out from the other upstream packets because management consistently frames the company as a producer that also leverages carbon-management capabilities to advance lower-carbon technologies and products. Even where the quarter numbers remain dominated by hydrocarbons, that strategic overlay is part of the identity.
