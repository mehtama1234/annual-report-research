## Annual report takeaways

- Permian Resources is one of the more useful missing upstream packets because it adds a cleaner Delaware-Basin manufacturing-and-consolidation model instead of another large diversified independent. The core story is not only drilling volume. It is low-cost Delaware execution, aggressive bolt-on land capture, working-interest improvement, and a strong free-cash-flow-per-share orientation.
- The annual-close materials show the scale of that system. At year-end `2025`, Permian reported `1,116 MMBoe` of total proved reserves versus `1,027 MMBoe` a year earlier, with reserves consisting of `43%` oil, `26%` NGLs, and `31%` natural gas. Proved developed reserves were `794 MMBoe`, or `71%` of total proved reserves.
- The financial and operating profile was strong in `2025`. Full-year average production was `392.6 MBoe/d`, including `181.8 MBbls/d` of oil. The company generated `\$3.6B` of cash provided by operating activities and `\$1.6B` of adjusted free cash flow. It also reduced D&C costs per foot by `10%` year over year and reduced total debt by more than `\$600M` from year-end `2024`.
- The land-and-inventory angle is central. Management highlighted roughly `\$1.1B` of accretive acquisitions during `2025`, spanning more than `700` transactions, `30,000` net acres, and `19,000` net royalty acres. The company also said it replaced `100%` of developed inventory through accretive M&A for the third consecutive year.
- The `2026` setup was framed as a more capital-efficient extension of the same model. Initial guidance called for `186 - 192 MBbls/d` of oil production and `400 - 430 MBoe/d` of total production on `\$1.75B - \$1.95B` of cash capex, with approximately `250` gross wells turned in line, average lateral lengths of about `11,000` feet, and most activity still concentrated in New Mexico and the Texas Delaware Basin.
- Source quality is good. AnnualReports was still stale at `2024`, but the official company IR site directly exposed the `2025` annual report PDF and the trailing-quarter release chain, while SEC supplies the `ARS`, `10-K`, `10-Q`, and `8-K` filings.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` average production was `376.4 MBoe/d`, including `198.1 MBbls/d` of oil, `86.2 MBbls/d` of NGLs, and `552.9 MMcf/d` of natural gas. Cash provided by operating activities was `\$1.506B`, adjusted operating cash flow was `\$1.272B`, and adjusted free cash flow was `\$751M`.
- Market and operating read: Waha averaged `$(3.14)` per Mcf during the quarter and traded as low as `$(9.52)` per Mcf. Permian curtailed part of its high-GOR production to protect free cash flow, yet still delivered unhedged gas realizations of `$(1.74)` per Mcf, a `\$1.40` premium to Waha, and an all-in netback of `\$0.38` per Mcf after hedges.
- Capital and land strategy: the company spent `\$521M` of cash capital, drilled its first four-mile laterals, and announced roughly `190` transactions totaling `\$1.05B` for about `54,000` net leasehold acres, `20,000` NRAs, and around `330` net `10,000'` locations, plus more upside inventory.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is the strongest evidence that Permian is not just another oil driller. It is a land-consolidation and capital-efficiency machine that can cut high-GOR volumes, protect oil, exploit local M&A, and still lift guidance.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` average production was `412.9 MBoe/d`, including `192.3 MBbls/d` of oil, `103.3 MBbls/d` of NGLs, and `703.0 MMcf/d` of natural gas. Cash provided by operating activities was `\$815M`, adjusted operating cash flow was `\$979M`, and adjusted free cash flow was `\$513M`.
- Cost and transport positioning: D&C costs declined to about `\$685` per lateral foot. The company said its natural gas portfolio delivered a `\$1.21` per Mcf premium to Waha on an unhedged basis and a `\$2.44` per Mcf premium to Waha after hedges, while expected firm transport exposure to Gulf Coast and DFW markets would exceed `700 MMcf/d` in `2027`.
- Balance-sheet and structure changes: management highlighted around `40` transactions for `\$205M`, investment-grade ratings from S&P and Moody's, a new `\$3.0B` revolver, redemption of `\$550M` of legacy Earthstone `2027` notes, and completion of the corporate simplification into a traditional single-share-class C-Corp.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is the clearest quarter for the infrastructure and capital-markets side of the story. Waha management, transport optionality, debt reduction, and structure simplification all support the same goal: higher free cash flow per share from a tighter Delaware footprint.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` average production was `401.5 MBoe/d`, including `188.6 MBbls/d` of oil. Cash provided by operating activities was `\$904M`, adjusted operating cash flow was `\$884M`, and adjusted free cash flow was `\$403M`.
- Cost and execution: D&C costs were about `\$700` per lateral foot, down `14%` from `2024`, while controllable cash costs fell to `\$7.24` per Boe. Management also emphasized record field performance in feet drilled per day and completion cost per foot.
- Strategic posture: the company added `7,700` net acres and `1,300` NRAs during the quarter for `\$240M`, kept leverage at `0.9x`, raised the base dividend to `\$0.16` per share, and laid out a `2026` plan that aimed for more oil production with less capital.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the best annual-close quarter for understanding the Permian Resources model. The company is trying to compound shareholder value through low-cost drilling, local land capture, and a capital plan that treats the Delaware Basin as a scalable manufacturing system.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Permian adds the Delaware-Basin low-cost-manufacturing and bolt-on-consolidation version of upstream exposure.
- Consumer: end demand is indirect, but this packet is closely tied to affordable oil supply, natural gas transport optionality, and refinery and petrochemical demand that still depend on Permian barrels.
- Cultural and societal: management repeatedly frames the business around affordable domestic energy, shareholder returns, and the ability to keep adding inventory through local relationships and basin knowledge rather than through empire-building.
- Industrial: this is one of the clearest shale-manufacturing packets in the branch. Lateral length, D&C cost per foot, working interest, land adjacency, and transport routing directly affect free cash flow.
- Commodity exposure: oil still drives the franchise, but Waha gas dislocations and NGL realizations matter enough that the company has to actively manage gas takeaway and high-GOR curtailments rather than just accept headline basin pricing.
- Capital allocation: the company combines base dividends, debt reduction, and acquisitions rather than leaning on one simple return framework. The stated model is explicitly "all of the above."
- Infrastructure dependence: Permian is a useful upstream-to-midstream bridge packet because gas transportation capacity, Gulf Coast and DFW exposure, and the ability to avoid full Waha pain materially shape economics.
