## What changed

- Permian Resources adds a cleaner Delaware-Basin manufacturing packet than `Diamondback`. `Diamondback` is more about large-scale Permian manufacturing, electrified completions, and basin-scale scale economics. Permian is more explicitly about low-cost Delaware concentration, bolt-on land capture, working-interest improvement, and free-cash-flow-per-share growth through disciplined M&A.
- The `2025` annual-close materials make that distinction clear. The company exited `2025` with `1,116 MMBoe` of proved reserves, with a reserve mix of `43%` oil, `26%` NGLs, and `31%` natural gas, while emphasizing that it replaced `100%` of developed inventory through accretive M&A for the third straight year.
- The quarter chain then shows the operating model in motion. `Q4 2025` framed the low-cost and high-capital-efficiency setup. `Q1 2026` showed the gas-transport and working-interest story more clearly, including over `700 MMcf/d` of expected `2027` gas exposure to Gulf Coast and DFW markets. `Q2 2026` then showed how Permian can curtail high-GOR gas at bad Waha prices, keep oil growing, and still raise free cash flow through workovers, high-return projects, and bolt-on land deals.
- This makes Permian a strong bridge between pure oil-weighted manufacturing and midstream dependence. It is still an upstream operator, but cash generation is materially shaped by ground-game acreage capture, working-interest control, gas transport positioning, and the ability to keep lowering cost per lateral foot.

## Open questions

- Can Permian keep replacing inventory through bolt-on and grassroots acquisitions at attractive prices once more private Delaware inventory gets consolidated?
- How durable is the current free-cash-flow advantage if Waha stays structurally weak for longer and the company has to keep curtailing high-GOR production?
- Does the low-cost Delaware model remain differentiated as more large operators push longer laterals, higher working interests, and tighter cost control across the basin?
