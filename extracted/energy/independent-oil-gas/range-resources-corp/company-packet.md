## Annual report takeaways

- Range is a strong missing independent-E&P packet because it adds the Appalachian gas-and-NGL system rather than another oil-weighted shale manufacturer. The official company profile is explicit: Range is a leading U.S. independent natural gas and NGL producer focused in the `Appalachian Basin`, and management still leans heavily on the company's role as a pioneer of the `Marcellus Shale`.
- The `2025` annual-close materials show a company whose core logic is not just drilling. It is contracted takeaway, premium market access, capital discipline, and long-duration gas inventory. Full-year `2025` production averaged `2.24 Bcfe per day`, about `69%` natural gas, while proved reserves were `18.1 Tcfe` with positive performance revisions for the `18th` consecutive year.
- Cash generation and capital returns were also central to the `2025` case. Range reported `\$1.2B` of cash flow from operating activities and `\$1.3B` of cash flow from operations before working-capital changes. It repurchased `\$231M` of stock, paid `\$86M` in dividends, and reduced net debt by `\$186M` in `2025`.
- The balance-sheet read matters because Range is trying to present growth as controlled rather than speculative. Management highlighted year-end debt to EBITDAX of `0.8x`, net debt of about `\$1.22B` at `December 31, 2025`, and a larger `\$1.5B` repurchase authorization. This is not a distressed gas producer trying to talk its way into credibility.
- The most distinctive annual theme is how the company links current output to future contracted demand. Range said it signed a `10-year` supply agreement for `75 Mmcf per day` for a Midwest power plant, and management emphasized that incremental production through `2027` is tied to additional contracted takeaway and diverse domestic and global end markets, including volumes sold at margin-enhancing premiums to support new Midwest power demand.
- The `2026` setup also reveals why this packet matters for the bigger-picture industrial story. Range enters `2026` with `500,000+` lateral feet of DUC inventory, expects all-in capital spending of `\$650M - \$700M`, and guides to `2.35 - 2.40 Bcfe per day` in `2026`, growing to `2.6 Bcfe per day` in `2027` assuming similar capital. This is a gas platform trying to convert infrastructure readiness and operational efficiency into a visible medium-term growth path.
- AnnualReports still lagged at `2024`, but the official IR and SEC chain were clean. That makes Range another useful example of why the archive should rely on company IR and SEC rather than on AnnualReports recency for the actual packet.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` production averaged `2.30 Bcfe per day`, approximately `67%` natural gas. The detailed production table showed average daily output of `1,548,871` mcf of natural gas, `118,113` barrels of NGLs, and `6,475` barrels of oil.
- Cash-flow read: Range reported `\$235M` of cash flow from operating activities and `\$333M` of cash flow from operations before working-capital changes. GAAP net income was `\$195M`, while adjusted net income was `\$186M`.
- Market and margin read: realized price including hedges was `\$3.53` per mcfe, natural gas differential including basis hedging was `(\$0.47)` per mcf to `NYMEX`, and pre-hedge NGL realizations were `\$29.10` per barrel, a `\$3.49` premium over the `Mont Belvieu` equivalent. That is the operating heart of the packet: Range is monetizing market access and NGL premium capture, not just well productivity.
- Capital and balance-sheet read: the company repurchased `\$78M` of shares, paid `\$24M` in dividends, spent about `\$222M` of capital in the quarter, and ended June with net debt of roughly `\$881M`. Even with much lower operating cash flow than `Q1`, the company remained in a capital-return posture.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is useful because it shows that the Range system is not simply a commodity-beta story. Record drilling and completion efficiency mattered, but so did NGL pricing, basis management, and the ability to fund shareholder returns while preserving the larger growth plan.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` production averaged `2.21 Bcfe per day`, approximately `32%` liquids. The detailed production table showed average daily output of `1,508,842` mcf of natural gas, `108,193` barrels of NGLs, and `8,239` barrels of oil.
- Cash-flow read: Range reported `\$619M` of cash flow from operating activities and `\$545M` of cash flow from operations before working-capital changes. GAAP net income was `\$342M`, while adjusted net income was `\$360M`.
- Market and pricing read: realized price including hedges was `\$4.84` per mcfe, natural gas differential including basis hedging was a `\$0.18` per mcf premium to `NYMEX`, and pre-hedge NGL realizations were `\$26.62` per barrel, a record `\$4.41` premium over the `Mont Belvieu` equivalent.
- Shareholder and balance-sheet read: the company repurchased `\$27M` of shares, paid `\$24M` in dividends, and reduced net debt by `\$384M`. Net debt at `March 31, 2026` was about `\$834M` after the January redemption of the `\$600M` 8.25% senior notes due 2029 through borrowings on the credit facility.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is the best quarter for seeing Range at full strength. Premium markets, NGL uplift, capital discipline, and a much stronger balance sheet all reinforced the same story: this is an Appalachian gas platform using commercial positioning to convert volumes into shareholder-friendly cash flow.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` and full-year `2025` framed the current cycle. Full-year production averaged `2.24 Bcfe per day`, about `69%` natural gas. The detailed production table showed full-year average daily output of `1,536,690` mcf of natural gas, `111,101` barrels of NGLs, and `5,352` barrels of oil.
- Cash-flow and return read: the annual-close materials reported `\$1.2B` of operating cash flow and `\$1.3B` of cash flow from operations before working-capital changes. Range returned `\$317M` to shareholders through `\$231M` of buybacks and `\$86M` of dividends, while reducing net debt by `\$186M`.
- Capital and growth read: all-in capital spending was `\$674M` in `2025`, and the `2026` guide called for `\$650M - \$700M` of all-in capital. Management also highlighted `500,000+` lateral feet of DUC inventory and a plan to convert about `400,000` feet of that inventory to production across `2026` and `2027`.
- Reserve and infrastructure read: proved reserves were `18.1 Tcfe`, proved developed reserves increased `7.3%` to `12.8 Tcfe`, and the company tied growth to contracted takeaway and market diversity rather than to a simple "drill more gas" argument. This is a real transport-and-demand-linkage packet, not just an acreage packet.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the best quarter for understanding the Range thesis. It shows a disciplined gas platform using DUC inventory, premium markets, NGL strength, contracted end demand, and balance-sheet repair to create a different kind of upstream growth story than the branch's oil-heavy independents.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Range adds the Appalachian gas-and-NGL premium-market-access version of independent E&P.
- Consumer: end demand is indirect, but this packet matters to affordability, power reliability, and industrial and export demand because Range is explicitly linking its growth plan to domestic power generation, international gas demand, and premium NGL markets.
- Cultural and societal: Range is a good example of how gas producers now frame themselves as reliability and demand-linkage businesses rather than just as commodity extractors. The company is trying to sell long-life supply, transport readiness, and disciplined reinvestment as a public-market virtue.
- Industrial: this is one of the clearest packets in the branch for showing that gas economics are not only about Henry Hub or `NYMEX`. Takeaway, basis hedging, NGL pricing versus `Mont Belvieu`, export access, and DUC conversion timing all materially shape returns.
- Commodity exposure: Range remains exposed to gas, NGL, and oil prices, but the quarter chain shows that marketing and premium capture can meaningfully change what the same molecules earn.
- Capital allocation: dividends, buybacks, debt reduction, and a measured growth plan all matter simultaneously. This is not a pure grow-volumes model and not a pure deleveraging model.
- Infrastructure dependence: Range is one of the cleaner upstream-to-midstream bridge packets because contracted takeaway, premium end markets, and future production growth are directly tied together. Without market access, the growth plan would mean something very different.
- Regional specialization: unlike multi-basin names, Range shows what a concentrated Appalachian specialist can look like when the real moat is not geographic diversity but cost structure, market access, and deep `Marcellus` inventory.
