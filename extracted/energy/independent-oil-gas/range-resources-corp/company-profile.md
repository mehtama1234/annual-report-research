## What changed

- Range adds a different independent-E&P system than the branch's oil-heavy or internationally diversified names. This is an Appalachian gas-and-NGL platform where the core edge comes from `Marcellus` scale, long-life inventory, contracted takeaway, premium market access, and marketing execution rather than from offshore exploration or Delaware manufacturing.
- The `2025` annual-close materials show a company leaning into a multi-year growth plan, but doing so from a position of capital discipline. Management emphasized `\$1.2B` of operating cash flow, `\$231M` of buybacks, `\$86M` of dividends, lower net debt, and a `0.8x` debt-to-EBITDAX ratio, while still preparing for output growth through `2027`.
- The target quarter window is the normal cadence as of `2026-08-10`:
  - `Q2 2026`
  - `Q1 2026`
  - `Q4 2025`
- The quarter chain matters because it shows a very specific upstream operating model:
  - premium NGL realizations versus `Mont Belvieu`
  - natural-gas differentials managed around `NYMEX`
  - DUC inventory and takeaway shaping the growth plan
  - capital returns funded by cash generation rather than by balance-sheet strain
- AnnualReports lagged at `2024`, but Range's company IR and SEC chain were current and clean. This is another direct example of why the archive should treat AnnualReports as taxonomy only when the official reporting stack is better.

## Open questions

- How much of Range's edge is truly structural marketing and takeaway advantage, and how much is a favorable but cyclical period for Appalachian gas and NGL premiums?
- Can the company keep prioritizing free cash flow and shareholder returns while also stepping into the next leg of production growth through `2027`?
- Does Range deserve to trade more like a gas producer, a low-cost resource platform, or a logistics-and-market-access system sitting on top of Appalachia?
