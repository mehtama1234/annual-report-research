## Annual report takeaways

- Tourmaline is one of the clearest missing upstream packets because it is the Canadian natural-gas-heavy counterweight to the branch's larger oil sands and diversified liquids platforms. The `2025` annual materials describe Tourmaline as Canada's largest and most active natural gas producer, focused on the lowest-development-cost natural gas in North America.
- The `2025` annual package shows the scale of that machine. Tourmaline averaged `638,196 boe/d` in `2025`, generated `C$3.396B` of cash flow, reported `C$262.7M` of net earnings, spent `C$2.932B` on capital, and ended the year with net debt of `C$1.5B`. `Q4 2025` production was `77%` natural gas, which makes the company a direct gas-system packet rather than a broad mixed-energy holding.
- The annual file set matters because it combines reserve depth and infrastructure control. The AIF says Tourmaline operates from two core Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin areas, carries a large reserve base and long drilling inventory, and has meaningful self-help infrastructure across gas processing, gathering, NGL handling, and storage-linked commercialization. The same materials point to approximately `1.8 Bcf/d` gross processing capability in the Alberta Deep Basin and approximately `1.7 Bcf/d` net processing capability in NEBC.
- The `2025` through `2026` transition also shows an upstream company that is not just drilling harder. Tourmaline's `2025` production growth was supported by acquisitions including `Crew`, `Todd`, `Saguaro`, and a Strathcona property package, while `2026` began with the Spirit River disposition. This is portfolio engineering as much as raw drilling cadence.
- The useful difference from `Canadian Natural`, `Occidental`, `EOG`, `Devon`, `Range`, and `Coterra` is that Tourmaline is much more explicitly a continental natural-gas and condensate optimization story. Storage, processing choices, export-pipeline constraints, gas marketing, and condensate or NGL mix all materially shape realized value.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` cash flow from operating activities was `C$643.8M`, cash flow was `C$786.1M`, free cash flow was `C$192.1M`, and net earnings were `C$184.4M`.
- Production read: reported production averaged `594,198 boe/d`, while total produced volumes averaged `603,065 boe/d`. Natural gas remained `81%` of sold production. The sold-volume decline versus `Q2 2025` was driven mainly by the Spirit River disposition and the end of discretionary deep-cut gas-plant deliveries in the Alberta Deep Basin.
- Capital and balance-sheet read: `Q2 2026` capital expenditures were `C$554.8M`, including `C$575.9M` of exploration-and-production spending. Net debt remained about `C$1.5B`, with long-term debt down to about `C$0.7B`.
- Market-access read: the quarter is one of the better branch examples of transport bottlenecks showing up inside upstream results. Tourmaline said it had to purchase additional natural-gas volumes to fulfill sales contracts because of export-pipeline volume restrictions during the first half of `2026`. That is the molecule-movement problem in plain form.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` shows why Tourmaline matters beyond generic gas exposure. Storage injections, lower NGL output from changed processing choices, third-party gas purchases, and the new liquids-marketing agreement all show that value comes from placing molecules well, not just producing more of them.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` cash flow from operating activities was `C$942.6M`, cash flow was `C$862.2M`, and net earnings were `C$657.6M`.
- Production read: Tourmaline delivered record quarterly production of `666,089 boe/d`, with total produced volumes of `663,221 boe/d`. Natural gas averaged `3,134,536 mcf/d`, condensate averaged `40,707 bbl/d`, and NGLs averaged `96,770 bbl/d`.
- Portfolio and capital read: reported total capital expenditures were negative `C$91.3M` because the Spirit River disposition brought in roughly `C$751.8M` of net proceeds, while exploration-and-production spending still ran at `C$643.5M`.
- Strategy read: the company kept `2026` EP spending guidance at `C$2.55B`, but also said it would terminate discretionary deep-cut gas-plant deliveries in the Alberta Deep Basin as contracts expired, lowering NGL production by about `20,000 boe/d`. This is a good example of prioritizing returns over headline liquids volumes.
- Infrastructure read: the storage system became more important. Tourmaline said storage capacity was `4.0 Bcf` at March 31, `2026`, scheduled to increase to `10.0 Bcf` in April `2026` and `14.0 Bcf` in April `2027` as Dimsdale agreements commenced. That matters because the company explicitly uses storage to inject in lower-price periods and withdraw in higher-price periods.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` cash flow from operating activities was `C$700.1M`, cash flow was `C$890.1M`, and net loss was `C$655.0M`, while full-year `2025` cash flow was `C$3.396B` and full-year net earnings were `C$262.7M`.
- Production read: `Q4 2025` production averaged `659,204 boe/d` and full-year `2025` production averaged `638,196 boe/d`, both up from `2024`. Natural gas averaged `2,946,447 mcf/d` for the year, and condensate averaged `40,071 bbl/d`.
- Capital and leverage read: full-year capital expenditures were `C$2.932B`, while year-end net debt was `C$1.5B` versus `C$1.7B` at the end of `2024`.
- Portfolio read: `2025` made the Tourmaline case bigger and more complex. The company integrated multiple acquisitions, then began `2026` by selling Spirit River. That indicates a management model built around basin consolidation and value-driven repositioning rather than simple organic drilling repetition.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the best entry point for understanding the company. It ties together scale natural-gas production, condensate and NGL economics, acquisition-driven growth, low net debt, and a willingness to move assets in and out of the portfolio to sharpen returns.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Tourmaline adds the natural-gas-led upstream flagship inside the energy batch.
- Consumer: direct consumer exposure is limited, but the packet matters because gas and condensate from producers like Tourmaline feed heating, electricity, industrial demand, LNG pull-through, petrochemical systems, and blending or diluent demand.
- Cultural and societal: this is a strong reminder that the North American energy system still depends heavily on gas-heavy producers with large physical footprints, real infrastructure needs, and a durable role in affordability and energy security debates.
- Industrial: Tourmaline depends on several linked physical systems:
  - Alberta Deep Basin and NEBC Montney drilling
  - gas gathering and processing
  - condensate and NGL handling
  - storage capacity at Dawn, PG&E Citygate, and Dimsdale-linked arrangements
  - export pipeline access
  - gas and liquids marketing
- Commodity exposure: Tourmaline is highly exposed to gas pricing, but the packet is more interesting than plain Henry Hub or AECO beta because storage, processing choice, export restrictions, and purchased third-party volumes all affect realized outcomes.
- Capital intensity: this is a meaningful upstream capital case, but with a different profile from oil sands. The company still spends heavily on drilling, completions, and facilities, yet the key strategic edge is low-cost gas development plus owned or controlled infrastructure, not giant upgrading complexes.
- Upstream and downstream cycles: Tourmaline is firmly upstream, but it shows how upstream economics are shaped by downstream and midstream constraints. Gas plant contracts, storage economics, pipeline restrictions, and liquids-marketing agreements can change the profitability of the same molecules.
- Infrastructure dependence: this is one of the branch's clearest examples of infrastructure dependence inside upstream reporting. When export pipes tighten, Tourmaline buys third-party gas to meet contracts. When storage expands, it gains seasonal optionality. When deep-cut processing economics worsen, it changes the production mix.
- Shareholder-return architecture: the AIF emphasizes base dividends and surplus free-cash-flow distributions. Tourmaline is useful because it presents gas-heavy upstream not just as production growth, but as a cash-return system built on low-cost molecules and disciplined balance-sheet management.
