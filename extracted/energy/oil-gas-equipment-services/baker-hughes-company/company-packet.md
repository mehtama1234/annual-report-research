## Annual report takeaways

- Baker Hughes is one of the clearest energy names in the branch for why `Oil & Gas Equipment & Services` is too narrow a label on its own. The company is still exposed to oilfield-services spending through `OFSE`, but it is also building a larger industrial energy-technology platform through `IET`, with meaningful exposure to LNG equipment, turbomachinery, gas infrastructure, power systems, digital monitoring, and adjacent industrial markets.
- The annual-close materials and filed `10-K` show a more industrialized and less purely cyclical framing than the classic service names. Management described the company moving into `Horizon Two`, with recent portfolio actions positioning Baker Hughes as a stronger industrialized energy-solutions company with a more production-oriented mix and more durable cash generation.
- `Q4 2025` and full-year `2025` results show the scale of that transition. In the fourth quarter, Baker Hughes generated orders of approximately `$7.9B`, revenue of approximately `$7.4B`, attributable net income of approximately `$876M`, and adjusted EBITDA of approximately `$1.337B`. For full-year `2025`, Baker Hughes generated orders of approximately `$29.6B`, revenue of approximately `$27.7B`, attributable net income of approximately `$2.588B`, and adjusted EBITDA of approximately `$4.825B`.
- The annual-close framing also makes clear why Baker Hughes matters for the broader infrastructure map. Management highlighted record full-year `IET` orders of approximately `$14.9B`, year-end `IET` backlog of approximately `$32.4B`, and continued demand from LNG, FPSO, gas infrastructure, and power systems. This is not just an upstream service recovery story.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: for `Q2 2026`, Baker Hughes generated orders of approximately `$10.5B`, revenue of approximately `$6.7B`, attributable net income of approximately `$681M`, adjusted diluted EPS of approximately `$0.64`, and adjusted EBITDA of approximately `$1.231B`.
- Backlog and mix: remaining performance obligations reached approximately `$40.1B`, including record `IET` RPO of approximately `$37.1B`. Within `IET`, Gas Technology Equipment and Gas Technology Services were approximately `$15.0B` and `$16.7B`, respectively.
- Capital and cash generation: cash flow from operating activities was approximately `$1.345B`, and free cash flow was approximately `$1.109B`. Capital expenditures net of asset disposals were approximately `$236M`.
- Strategic context: management emphasized strength across data-center power, gas infrastructure, and LNG, while also completing the `Chart Industries` acquisition in July `2026`. The quarter included notable LNG and gas-technology awards from Venture Global, Cheniere, Golar, Nigeria LNG, and others.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is the cleanest proof that Baker Hughes belongs in the archive as more than an oilfield-service beta. It is increasingly a gas, LNG, turbomachinery, and power-infrastructure packet with large recurring service exposure and a broader installed-base strategy.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: for `Q1 2026`, Baker Hughes generated orders of approximately `$8.2B`, revenue of approximately `$6.6B`, attributable net income of approximately `$930M`, adjusted diluted EPS of approximately `$0.58`, and adjusted EBITDA of approximately `$1.158B`.
- Backlog and mix: remaining performance obligations ended the quarter at approximately `$36.1B`, including record `IET` RPO of approximately `$33.1B`.
- Cash generation and portfolio actions: cash flow from operating activities was approximately `$500M`, and free cash flow was approximately `$210M`. The company also closed the `PSI` sale to Crane, contributed `SPC` into a JV with Cactus, saw `HMH` complete its IPO, and announced the planned `Waygate` sale to Hexagon.
- Strategic context: management said the quarter exceeded guidance despite Middle East disruptions and specifically highlighted `IET` record orders, including around `$1.4B` in Power Systems orders, with continued progress in LNG, gas infrastructure, and CCS.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` shows the portfolio-management story and the industrial-energy story working together. Baker Hughes is not just chasing activity growth; it is actively reshaping the company toward longer-cycle energy infrastructure and technology-heavy recurring-service revenue.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: for `Q4 2025`, Baker Hughes generated orders of approximately `$7.9B`, revenue of approximately `$7.4B`, attributable net income of approximately `$876M`, adjusted diluted EPS of approximately `$0.78`, and adjusted EBITDA of approximately `$1.337B`.
- Full-year context: for full-year `2025`, orders reached approximately `$29.6B`, revenue reached approximately `$27.7B`, attributable net income reached approximately `$2.588B`, and adjusted EBITDA reached approximately `$4.825B`. Free cash flow for the year reached approximately `$2.732B`.
- Strategic context: management highlighted record `IET` orders and backlog, continued LNG momentum, expected strength in FPSO and gas-infrastructure awards, and a shift toward becoming a more industrialized energy-solutions company in `2026-2028`.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` is the annual-close proof that Baker Hughes is not simply competing on rigs, frac, or drilling exposure. The company is increasingly shaped by whether it can capture LNG liquefaction modules, compressors, turbines, generators, gas-processing equipment, and lifecycle service contracts across global energy and industrial infrastructure.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Baker Hughes adds the industrialized energy-technology and LNG-equipment layer inside oilfield services.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is absent, but the packet still maps to real end-use systems because its equipment increasingly supports LNG exports, gas processing, grid stability, and data-center power.
- Cultural: the broader signal is that energy security is no longer only about wells, pipes, and exports. It also depends on compressors, turbines, liquefaction trains, monitoring systems, and the industrial technology stack that keeps those systems working.
- Regulatory: Baker Hughes is exposed to export-project timing, sanctions, trade policy, local-content rules, and the approval cycles behind LNG, FPSO, and gas-infrastructure projects.
- Labor: labor intensity shows up through field execution, integrated project delivery, manufacturing, installation, and service capacity across a global installed base.
- Technology: this is one of the strongest branch packets for the crossover between energy hardware and industrial software. Management repeatedly ties the story to digital monitoring, AI-enabled power optimization, and lifecycle services rather than only equipment shipment.
- Capital allocation: portfolio reshaping is central. Divestitures, the `Chart` acquisition, and the split between shorter-cycle `OFSE` and longer-cycle `IET` all matter for how the company should be read.
- Infrastructure dependence: Baker Hughes is a direct proof that LNG and gas-infrastructure buildout require a specialized supply layer behind the headline project owner. Its equipment sits inside the bottlenecks that let large projects actually operate.
