## What changed

- Baker Hughes is a stronger next service packet than another pure completions or drilling name because it adds the industrialized energy-technology layer. The business still has an oilfield-services segment, but it also has a large and increasingly important Industrial & Energy Technology segment tied to LNG, gas infrastructure, turbomachinery, power systems, carbon capture, digital monitoring, and data-center power demand.
- The local IR host is a source-quality nuance. The official Baker Hughes IR pages and document routes were live-verified, but direct local captures returned Cloudflare challenge shells. That means the packet should rely on the SEC `10-K`, `10-Q`, and `8-K` chain for primary local evidence, while using the live-verified official IR URLs and their reported figures as the official management framing.
- The quarter chain shows a company that is not simply tracking rig counts. `Q1 2026` and `Q2 2026` both showed record or near-record IET activity, rising backlog, and notable demand from LNG, gas infrastructure, power generation, and data-center-adjacent projects. This makes Baker Hughes a bridge between conventional energy service exposure and broader industrial power-infrastructure demand.

## Open questions

- As Baker Hughes becomes more weighted toward IET, LNG, gas infrastructure, and power systems, how much cyclicality does it truly shed relative to the more oilfield-intensive service names?
- Does the Chart acquisition further strengthen the industrialized energy-solutions thesis, or does it add integration complexity before the market fully values the broader portfolio?
- If global LNG and gas infrastructure demand remains strong while OFSE stays mixed, can Baker Hughes keep compounding margins and backlog through product mix alone, or does it still need a healthier global upstream cycle to unlock the full model?
