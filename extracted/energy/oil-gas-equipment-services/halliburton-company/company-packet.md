# Halliburton Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Halliburton Company
- Sector: Energy
- Industry: Oil & Gas Equipment & Services
- Ticker: HAL
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Halliburton is one of the branch's clearest examples of the service-and-execution layer that sits between upstream ambition and actual well delivery. The company does not own the reserves or the long-haul infrastructure; it monetizes drilling complexity, completions intensity, intervention needs, software, automation, and field execution.
- The official `2025` annual package framed the strategy in a clean five-part way: profitable international growth, maximize value in North America, increase capital efficiency, deploy digital and automation solutions, and advance a sustainable energy future.
- The annual-close evidence showed a still-large but more selective oilfield-services franchise. Full-year `2025` revenue was `$22.2B`, cash flow from operations was about `$2.926B`, year-end cash was about `$2.206B`, and stockholders' equity was about `$10.461B`.
- The annual package also made capital discipline part of the core model rather than a side note. Management highlighted capital expenditures at about `6%` of revenue and total shareholder returns of about `$1.6B` through dividends and repurchases.
- Halliburton's bigger-picture identity is not simply "higher oil price means better quarter." The filing set shows a company trying to win through international depth, differentiated completion and drilling tools, software and automation, electric fracturing, and integrated well delivery rather than pure North American volume chasing.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income was `$534M`, or `$0.64` per diluted share. Adjusted net income per diluted share was `$0.55`. Revenue was `$5.7B` and operating margin was `14%`.
- Cash and capital return: quarter cash flow from operations was `$824M`, free cash flow was `$668M`, and Halliburton repurchased about `$200M` of stock. In the filed `10-Q`, cash at `2026-06-30` was about `$2.048B`, stockholders' equity was about `$11.010B`, and six-month operating cash flow was about `$1.097B`.
- Operating system: Completions and Production revenue was `$3.2B`, up `6%` sequentially, while Drilling and Evaluation revenue was `$2.5B`, up `5%` sequentially. North America revenue was `$2.3B`, up `7%` sequentially, while international revenue was `$3.4B`, up `5%`.
- What mattered: `Q2 2026` is the cleanest quarter in the packet for Halliburton's two-engine thesis. International demand stayed strong through contract awards and pipeline visibility, while North America finally showed a more visible recovery in stimulation, well construction, and Gulf activity.
- Thematic read: this is not just an oilfield upcycle packet. It is a workflow-intensity and execution-quality packet where digital integration, automation, and integrated well-construction contracts are meant to hold margins better than a generic service company.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income was `$461M`, or `$0.55` per diluted share. Revenue was `$5.4B` and operating margin was `13%`.
- Cash and capital return: quarter cash flow from operations was `$273M`, free cash flow was `$123M`, and Halliburton repurchased about `$100M` of stock. In the filed `10-Q`, cash at `2026-03-31` was about `$2.003B`, stockholders' equity was about `$10.780B`, and quarter operating cash flow was about `$273M`.
- Operating system: management said North America was in the early innings of a recovery, while international performance outpaced disruptions from the Middle East conflict.
- What mattered: `Q1 2026` showed a company still navigating geopolitical disruption and uneven customer timing, but doing so from a base where international breadth and capital discipline prevented a much weaker quarter.
- Thematic read: Halliburton's service cycle is no longer just a pure North America shale indicator. The business is more globally distributed, more contract and technology dependent, and more exposed to customer workflow complexity than to simple U.S. rig-count direction alone.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net income was `$589M`, or `$0.70` per diluted share. Adjusted net income per diluted share was `$0.69`. Revenue was `$5.7B`, operating margin was `13%`, adjusted operating margin was `15%`, and quarter cash flow from operations was `$1.2B`.
- Full-year close: Halliburton reported full-year `2025` share repurchases of `$1.0B` and said it returned about `85%` of free cash flow to shareholders. The filed `10-K` showed year-end cash of about `$2.206B`, stockholders' equity of about `$10.461B`, and full-year operating cash flow of about `$2.926B`.
- Operating system: management described the international business as strong, with the collaborative value proposition winning and technology delivering, while North America remained under a maximize-value strategy rather than a growth-at-any-cost posture.
- What mattered: the annual close showed the company trying to widen the gap between activity and value. Even with softer full-year revenue versus `2024`, Halliburton still emphasized capital efficiency, differentiated technology, and returns discipline.
- Thematic read: `Q4 2025` is the best annual-close proof that Halliburton is a service-architecture packet, not merely a commodity beta packet. It is trying to turn operating know-how, digital tooling, electric fracturing, and international scale into a higher-quality cycle profile.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Halliburton adds the oilfield-services and execution layer, where customer capex decisions only become revenue after being translated into drilling, completions, intervention, and workflow intensity.
- Consumer and societal: direct consumer demand is absent, but the broader social layer is still present through energy affordability, energy security, and the practical need to keep hydrocarbon supply functioning even while the energy-transition narrative continues.
- Cultural: one recurring cultural signal in these energy filings is the shift from transition idealism toward operational pragmatism. Halliburton's annual package explicitly leaned into that reality.
- Industrial: this is a pure industrial-complexity packet. Earnings depend on customer budgets, basin activity, service intensity, geopolitical disruption, contract mix, product differentiation, software adoption, and whether international national-oil-company work can offset North American volatility.
- Technology: digital and automation are not window dressing here. Halliburton is explicitly trying to use software, AI, automation, electric fracturing, and integrated execution models to become more embedded in customer workflows.
- Labor: the packet remains exposed to skilled-labor availability and field execution quality, because service companies convert planning into real activity with people, equipment, and operational coordination.
- Capital allocation: Halliburton is notable for pairing capital returns with strict capex discipline. It is one of the clearest branch examples of a company trying to stay cycle-exposed without letting capital intensity run away from returns.
- Infrastructure dependence: the packet shows that upstream output depends on a specialized delivery layer. Wells do not simply appear because reserves exist or commodity prices rise; they require the service and execution stack Halliburton sells.
