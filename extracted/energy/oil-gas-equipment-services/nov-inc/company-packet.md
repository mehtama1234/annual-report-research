## Annual report takeaways

- NOV is one of the stronger missing equipment-and-services packets because it is not best understood as a pure services contractor. The `2025` annual filing explicitly frames NOV as a leading independent equipment and technology provider to the global energy industry, which makes its earnings power more tied to capital equipment, installed-base support, and industrial backlog than to day-rate labor intensity alone.
- The annual filing gives a clean structural read. NOV said it served customers in `57` countries, operated under two segments, `Energy Equipment` and `Energy Products and Services`, and relied on a capital-light manufacturing model, a large installed base of OEM equipment, and long-term aftermarket relationships to defend margins and market position.
- The `2025` annual-close numbers show a resilient but not carefree cycle position. Full-year revenue was `\$8.74 billion`, net income was `\$145 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `\$1.03 billion`, cash flow from operations was `\$1.25 billion`, free cash flow was `\$876 million`, full-year bookings were `\$2.34 billion`, and ending backlog was `\$4.34 billion`.
- The full-year operational interpretation matters more than the headline revenue. Management said Energy Equipment delivered its fourth consecutive year of revenue growth and EBITDA-margin expansion, with strong execution on production-oriented backlog and offshore demand offsetting weaker aftermarket activity and a softer shorter-cycle land-weighted business inside Energy Products and Services.
- The annual filing also shows why NOV belongs beside, but not inside, the `SLB` or `Halliburton` frame. NOV is monetizing drilling, completion, offshore production, and field modernization through OEM equipment, spares, service, automation upgrades, and digital controls. That creates a different dependency chain than reservoir services or completion intensity alone.
- Capital allocation is part of the thesis. During `2025`, NOV returned `\$505 million` of capital to shareholders. The filed `10-K` also says the company repurchased `22.8 million` shares for `\$315 million` during the year under its current buyback program, reinforcing that this is an equipment-cycle name with meaningful free-cash-flow conversion rather than a balance-sheet-stretched buildout story.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` revenue was `\$2.13 billion`, net income was `\$112 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `\$283 million`.
- Orders and backlog: bookings were `\$474 million`, Energy Equipment book-to-bill was `74%`, and Energy Equipment backlog ended the quarter at `\$4.08 billion`.
- Capital allocation: NOV returned `\$127 million` of capital to shareholders through share repurchases and dividends. Prepared remarks said the quarter included repurchases of `3.2 million` shares for `\$63 million` and dividends of `\$64 million`, including a supplemental dividend tied to the `2025` return-of-capital true-up.
- Segment read: Energy Equipment revenue was `\$1.22 billion` with adjusted EBITDA of `\$200 million`, helped by offshore production-project execution and better mix. Energy Products and Services revenue was `\$974 million` with adjusted EBITDA of `\$144 million`, helped by market-share gains in drill bits, artificial lift, and digital services.
- Management message: management said improving demand across major regions, better navigation of Middle East logistics, and a growing capital-equipment pipeline supported a more constructive setup, while also noting that commodity-price volatility and geopolitics were still causing temporary project deferrals.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is the clearest NOV quarter in the set because it shows the upside of the model when execution, offshore projects, and installed-base support begin to recover at the same time. The tariff-refund benefit mattered, but the more important signal is that equipment backlog, offshore production systems, and shorter-cycle industrial demand can all help earnings inflect together.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` revenue was `\$2.05 billion`, net income was `\$19 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `\$177 million`.
- Orders and backlog: bookings were `\$520 million`, Energy Equipment book-to-bill was `80%`, and Energy Equipment backlog ended the quarter at `\$4.23 billion`.
- Capital allocation: NOV returned `\$100 million` to shareholders through share repurchases and dividends. Prepared remarks said the quarter included repurchases of `3.5 million` shares for `\$67 million` and dividends of `\$33 million`.
- Segment read: Energy Equipment revenue was `\$1.19 billion` with adjusted EBITDA of `\$131 million`. Energy Products and Services revenue was `\$897 million` with adjusted EBITDA of `\$96 million`.
- Management message: management said the Middle East conflict created significant disruption, delaying deliveries of equipment and spares, impairing offshore service and repair work, and raising regional operating costs. NOV estimated the conflict reduced quarterly revenue by about `\$54 million` and adjusted EBITDA by about `\$32 million`.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` shows the vulnerable side of NOV's model. When logistics break, customer sites become harder to access, and offshore work is interrupted, the pain lands not just in new equipment but also in aftermarket support and quarter-end shipment timing. That is exactly why this packet is useful for the branch's bigger-picture infrastructure and bottleneck work.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` revenue was `\$2.28 billion`, net loss was `\$78 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `\$267 million`.
- Cash-flow and annual-close read: fourth-quarter cash flow from operations was `\$573 million` and free cash flow was `\$472 million`. Full-year `2025` cash flow from operations was `\$1.25 billion` and free cash flow was `\$876 million`.
- Orders and backlog: capital-equipment orders for the quarter were `\$532 million`, full-year bookings were `\$2.34 billion`, full-year book-to-bill was about `91%`, and year-end backlog was `\$4.34 billion`.
- Capital allocation: prepared remarks said NOV repurchased `5.7 million` shares for `\$85 million` and paid `\$27 million` of dividends in the quarter, bringing total capital returned in `2025` to `\$505 million`.
- Segment read: management highlighted that offshore-production demand, subsea flexible pipe, offshore construction equipment, and processing modules drove offshore-related backlog growth of more than `10%` during `2025`, while lower offshore-drilling-contractor aftermarket demand pressured the spares-and-services side.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the right baseline quarter for NOV because it shows a company that can produce strong free cash flow and backlog durability even while the market stays cautious. The quarter also cleanly separates the growth pocket, offshore production and backlog execution, from the weaker pocket, shorter-cycle land and offshore-drilling aftermarket activity.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: NOV adds the capital-equipment and OEM-aftermarket layer of the oilfield stack, which is distinct from the producer, midstream, and refiner packets already in the branch.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is remote here. The real demand drivers are offshore and land capital spending, field modernization, replacement cycles, spare-parts demand, and the willingness of producers and contractors to commit to large multi-quarter projects.
- Cultural and societal: this packet is a reminder that the energy system's public face is usually the producer or commodity price, but the operating reality depends on specialized equipment makers and installed-base support businesses that keep rigs, production assets, and offshore systems functioning.
- Industrial: NOV is one of the stronger names in the branch for capital intensity without being balance-sheet-heavy. The company repeatedly emphasizes a capital-light operating model, but its earnings still depend on industrial backlog quality, order intake, manufacturing throughput, offshore timing, and aftermarket access.
- Commodity exposure: NOV does not own the barrel or molecule, but it is still cyclical because customer budgets, activity levels, and project timing are shaped by oil and gas prices and by broader energy-security incentives.
- Transport and bottlenecks: `Q1 2026` is especially useful for showing how geopolitical conflict and logistics constraints propagate through the equipment layer, delaying parts, quarter-end deliveries, and offshore repair work.
- Technology: NOV is a good example of industrial digitalization being operational rather than cosmetic. The annual filing and prepared remarks tie the `Max` platform, machine learning, automation upgrades, and condition monitoring directly to equipment uptime, field analytics, and installed-base monetization.
- Infrastructure dependence: the packet helps map how offshore projects, subsea flexible pipe, production modules, spares, and service fleets all depend on large, slow-moving industrial systems rather than quick commodity-price reactions alone.
- Capital allocation: NOV belongs in the branch's capital-discipline theme set because it pairs strong free-cash-flow conversion with buybacks, dividends, and a return-of-capital framework rather than chasing pure capacity expansion.
