## What changed

- NOV adds a different oilfield-services lane than `SLB`, `Halliburton`, or `Baker Hughes`. The annual filing is explicit that NOV is a leading independent equipment and technology provider, not just a labor-heavy field-service contractor. That matters because the earnings profile is tied to capital-equipment backlog, offshore production systems, aftermarket support, and installed-base modernization.
- The annual filing gives the right structural frame. NOV serves customers in `57` countries across two segments, `Energy Equipment` and `Energy Products and Services`, and repeatedly emphasizes a capital-light model, a large installed base, and OEM aftermarket support as its core advantage. That makes NOV a better packet for understanding how the equipment layer monetizes the installed asset base behind rigs, offshore projects, and production systems.
- The `2025` close shows a business that stayed resilient despite a weak activity backdrop. Full-year revenue was `\$8.74B`, net income was `\$145M`, adjusted EBITDA was `\$1.03B`, cash flow from operations was `\$1.25B`, free cash flow was `\$876M`, full-year bookings were `\$2.34B`, and ending backlog was `\$4.34B`.
- The quarter set then shows both the fragility and leverage of the model. `Q1 2026` was hit hard by Middle East disruption, with management estimating a `\$54M` revenue and `\$32M` EBITDA impact, but `Q2 2026` rebounded sharply as logistics improved, offshore production projects executed well, and tariff refunds boosted results.
- The segment mix is also important. `Energy Equipment` is where backlog, offshore production projects, and modernization demand are most visible, while `Energy Products and Services` carries shorter-cycle exposure through drill bits, digital services, artificial lift, drill pipe, fiberglass, and land-weighted service activity. That is a more diversified but also more cyclical operating stack than the branch's other equipment-and-services packets.
- NOV is also a strong bridge between classic hydrocarbon servicing and broader infrastructure or automation themes. The annual filing and prepared remarks emphasize digitalization, the `Max` platform, AI and machine learning on operational data, OEM condition monitoring, and adjacent energy or infrastructure markets. The point is not that NOV is a software company; it is that installed machinery, data capture, and aftermarket control create a durable monetization layer behind the commodity system.

## Open questions

- How much of the `Q2 2026` margin rebound was underlying operational improvement versus temporary tariff-refund benefit?
- Does the anticipated offshore and capital-equipment recovery actually produce a sustained orders upcycle, or does commodity-price volatility keep backlog conversion ahead of replenishment?
- Can NOV keep growing digital and modernization revenue fast enough to offset the structurally weaker short-cycle land and aftermarket pockets when drilling customers stay cautious?
