## Annual report takeaways

- SLB is the clearest oilfield-equipment and services name for this batch because the `2025` annual-report chain shows a business exposed to upstream activity, but not reducible to rig counts alone. The useful read is the mix of field services, production systems, digital software, and adjacent infrastructure.
- The `2025` annual filing frames SLB as a global technology company serving energy security, affordability, and decarbonization at once, with operations in more than `100` countries and a business organized across `Digital`, `Reservoir Performance`, `Well Construction`, and `Production Systems`.
- Full-year `2025` revenue was `$35.71 billion`, net income attributable to SLB was `$3.37 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$8.46 billion`, cash flow from operations was `$6.49 billion`, and free cash flow was `$4.11 billion`.
- The annual chain is especially important because it shows SLB becoming more than a classic oilfield-services contractor. Management highlighted the ChampionX acquisition, digital annualized recurring revenue above `$1 billion`, and Data Center Solutions revenue growth of `121%` year on year to `$460 million`.
- AnnualReports still lagged at the `2024` package as of `2026-08-10`, while the official IR site already hosted the `2025 10-K`, so the authoritative annual chain here comes from company IR and SEC.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` revenue was `$8.97 billion`, net income attributable to SLB was `$786 million`, GAAP EPS was `$0.52`, EPS excluding charges and credits was `$0.55`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$1.899 billion`.
- Cash flow and capital allocation: cash flow from operations was `$1.36 billion`, free cash flow was `$716 million`, the quarterly dividend stayed at `$0.295` per share, and SLB repurchased `12 million` shares for `$648 million`.
- Operations: ChampionX contributed about `$870 million` of revenue and `$207 million` of adjusted EBITDA; Production Systems benefited from U.S. unconventionals, offshore momentum, and subsea activity, while Data Center Solutions revenue reached `$186 million`, up `33%` sequentially and `80%` year on year.
- Management message: management said broad-based international growth more than offset continued Middle East disruptions, with stronger offshore activity in Latin America, Europe and Africa, and Asia.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` shows why the service layer matters. SLB monetizes field activity, production intensity, digital workflow adoption, and growing adjacent infrastructure demand without needing to own the commodity itself.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` revenue was `$8.72 billion`, net income attributable to SLB was `$752 million`, GAAP EPS was `$0.50`, EPS excluding charges and credits was `$0.52`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$1.773 billion`.
- Cash flow and capital allocation: cash flow from operations was `$487 million`, free cash flow was negative `$23 million`, and the dividend remained `$0.295` per share.
- Operations: ChampionX contributed about `$838 million` of revenue and `$199 million` of adjusted EBITDA; Production Systems revenue rose `23%` year on year to `$3.51 billion` including ChampionX, while excluding the acquisition that division was down `6%` year on year.
- Management message: management said the Middle East conflict disrupted activity, and that excluding ChampionX the company’s first-quarter global revenue declined `7%` year on year.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` is useful because it shows the less forgiving side of the model. SLB is still exposed to geopolitics, customer spending pauses, and basin-level disruption even when the portfolio has growing digital and production-chemicals support.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` revenue was `$9.75 billion`, net income attributable to SLB was `$824 million`, GAAP EPS was `$0.55`, EPS excluding charges and credits was `$0.78`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$2.331 billion`.
- Cash flow and capital allocation: fourth-quarter cash flow from operations was `$3.01 billion`, free cash flow was `$2.29 billion`, and the board approved a `3.5%` dividend increase to `$0.295` per share.
- Operations: ChampionX contributed `$879 million` of revenue and `$206 million` of adjusted EBITDA in the quarter, while Digital annualized recurring revenue exceeded `$1 billion` and Data Center Solutions finished the year at `$460 million` of revenue.
- Management message: management said global upstream activity had stabilized, that the year had been challenging because of lower spending and weakness in places such as Saudi Arabia, Mexico, and Sub-Saharan Africa, and that ChampionX plus digital growth had offset part of that pressure.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` closes the year by showing a service-and-equipment company evolving into a more hybrid platform. The business is still linked to drilling and production cycles, but it is also building recurring digital revenue and infrastructure-adjacent exposure.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: SLB adds the oilfield-equipment and services layer that neither ConocoPhillips, Marathon Petroleum, nor Kinder Morgan covers.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is distant here. The real demand drivers are customer E&P budgets, field development, production optimization, offshore activity, and the durability of global energy demand.
- Cultural: the social signal is that the energy system still depends on highly specialized service and engineering layers that sit behind the commodity owner. The public conversation often focuses on producers or prices, but the service stack is what keeps complex projects moving.
- Industrial: this is one of the strongest packets for capital intensity, project complexity, and operating leverage. SLB depends on customer activity levels, technology adoption, reservoir difficulty, and international execution rather than only on commodity price direction.
- Geopolitical: the quarter set is especially useful for showing how conflict in the Middle East can disrupt activity, margins, and regional revenue even when other geographies continue to grow.
- Technology: SLB is one of the clearer examples of digital becoming operational rather than cosmetic. The company ties software, AI-enabled workflows, digital operations, and recurring ARR directly to field execution and customer productivity.
- Infrastructure dependence: Data Center Solutions adds a non-obvious bridge from oilfield capability into modular infrastructure and hyperscaler demand, which broadens the archive’s read on how industrial engineering capacity can migrate across sectors.
- Capital allocation: the combination of free cash flow, dividend growth, and share repurchases makes SLB a useful example of a services name that can still produce substantial shareholder returns while funding technology and portfolio expansion.
