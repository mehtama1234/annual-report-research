## What changed

- Earnings mix: SLB is no longer just a drilling-cycle proxy. The packet shows a business that still depends on global upstream activity but is adding recurring and higher-value revenue streams through digital software and ChampionX.
- Demand mix: customer spending in oil and gas still anchors the business, but digital operations, production chemicals, artificial lift, subsea, and modular data-center infrastructure are becoming more important to the growth story.
- Geography and geopolitics: the quarter set makes the Middle East conflict operationally real. Revenue and margins were directly hit in several divisions even while other international regions grew.
- Technology posture: this is one of the stronger names in the archive for making AI and digital adoption concrete rather than promotional. Management repeatedly ties Digital ARR and workflow automation to customer economics and field execution.
- Capital allocation: despite activity volatility, SLB still produced enough cash to grow the dividend, repurchase shares, and integrate ChampionX.
- Cross-sector read: Data Center Solutions makes SLB especially useful as a bridge packet between oilfield capability and the broader infrastructure buildout behind hyperscaler demand.

## Open questions

- How durable is the digital and Data Center Solutions expansion if upstream spending remains uneven?
- Does ChampionX integration keep improving margins, or does the acquired revenue base become more cyclical than management suggests?
- How much of the current offshore and production-recovery momentum can offset prolonged geopolitical disruption in the Middle East?
