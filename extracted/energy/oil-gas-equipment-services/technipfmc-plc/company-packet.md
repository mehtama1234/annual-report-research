## Annual report takeaways

- TechnipFMC is one of the most useful remaining equipment-and-services packets because it adds the subsea and integrated offshore-project layer, not just another general oilfield-services exposure. The annual filing explicitly frames the company around fully integrated projects, products, and services across traditional and new energy, but the economic core is still subsea development, offshore infrastructure, and surface-production systems.
- The operating structure matters. TechnipFMC runs through two segments, `Subsea` and `Surface Technologies`, and the annual filing repeatedly emphasizes that the company’s differentiation comes from combining subsea production systems, SURF, installation, and life-of-field services into a more integrated commercial model.
- The signature commercial idea is `iEPCI`, which the company says has grown to nearly one-third of the addressable subsea market. The annual filing also highlights `Subsea 2.0` as a configure-to-order product suite and says the services business is levered to a large and expanding installed base. That is the clearest reason TechnipFMC belongs in this archive: it monetizes offshore complexity, installed assets, and project integration rather than just labor utilization.
- The `2025` annual close was strong. The filing says inbound orders reached `\$11.2 billion`, backlog grew `15%` year over year to `\$16.6 billion`, cash flow from operating activities increased `84%` to `\$1.8 billion`, free cash flow increased `113%` to `\$1.4 billion`, and shareholder distributions more than doubled to `\$1.0 billion`.
- The Subsea lane is especially important for the bigger picture. The annual filing says TechnipFMC delivered on its commitment to achieve `\$30 billion` in Subsea inbound over the three-year period ending `2025`, including `\$10.1 billion` of orders in `2025`, while services inbound increased for a fifth consecutive year to more than `\$1.8 billion`. That makes this a strong packet for offshore backlog quality, aging-infrastructure service demand, and energy-system capital intensity.
- Surface Technologies adds a second, shorter-cycle layer. The filing says `2025` inbound orders of `\$1.1 billion` were driven by international markets and highlights commercial success of `iComplete`, the company’s integrated surface pressure-containment system. That matters because TechnipFMC is not just a deepwater pure play; it also has a surface and land-adjacent pressure-control lane.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` revenue was `\$2.763 billion`, net income was `\$362.7 million`, diluted EPS was `\$0.90`, and adjusted EBITDA was `\$581.9 million`.
- Orders and backlog: inbound orders were `\$2.727 billion` and backlog was `\$16.440 billion`. Management also highlighted `\$2.5 billion` of Subsea orders in the quarter.
- Cash-flow and capital allocation: cash flow from operations was `\$548 million`, free cash flow was `\$488 million`, and total shareholder distributions were `\$440 million`, representing `90%` of free cash flow.
- Operating message: management said clients are increasingly taking a portfolio approach to offshore development and brownfield expansion, which helps TechnipFMC win integrated work across multiple fields rather than one-off projects.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` shows why TechnipFMC matters to the branch. This is not just a “service company had a good quarter.” It is an offshore-project platform converting subsea orders, high-value installed-base work, and integrated execution into cash generation and shareholder distributions.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` revenue was `\$2.493 billion`, net income was `\$260.5 million`, diluted EPS was `\$0.64`, and adjusted EBITDA was `\$466.0 million`.
- Orders and backlog: inbound orders were `\$2.152 billion` and backlog was `\$16.468 billion`. The release also highlighted `\$1.9 billion` of Subsea orders.
- Cash-flow and capital allocation: cash flow from operations was `\$332 million`, free cash flow was `\$277 million`, and total shareholder distributions were `\$285 million`, including `\$265 million` of share repurchases.
- Operating message: despite sequentially lower inbound, the company still posted double-digit year-over-year revenue growth and much stronger margins than the prior-year quarter. That shows the benefit of working through a richer offshore backlog mix even when quarterly order timing is uneven.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is useful because it shows the less dramatic but still important side of the model. Even when orders soften sequentially, a large subsea backlog and integrated project mix can keep revenue, earnings, and capital returns moving in the right direction.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` revenue was `\$2.517 billion`, net income was `\$242.7 million`, diluted EPS was `\$0.59`, and adjusted EBITDA was `\$440.5 million`.
- Orders and backlog: inbound orders were `\$2.588 billion` and ending backlog was `\$16.572 billion`. The release highlighted `\$2.3 billion` of Subsea inbound in the quarter and `\$10.1 billion` of full-year Subsea orders.
- Capital allocation: shareholder distributions were `\$188 million` in the quarter and `\$1.0 billion` for the full year.
- Guidance message: management raised Subsea revenue and Subsea adjusted EBITDA margin guidance for `2026`, signaling confidence that the subsea backlog mix and service attachment were supporting a stronger earnings profile.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the clean setup quarter for the TechnipFMC thesis. It closes a year where offshore and subsea demand became more portfolio-driven, services became more important, and high-quality inbound supported large free-cash-flow generation rather than just headline backlog.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: TechnipFMC adds the subsea and offshore project-delivery layer of the energy system, which is different from the more generalized oilfield-service exposure in `SLB`, `Halliburton`, `Baker Hughes`, or `NOV`.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is distant here. The demand system is offshore project sanctioning, subsea-field development, brownfield expansion, installed-base maintenance, and operator willingness to invest in complex long-cycle hydrocarbon infrastructure.
- Cultural and societal: this packet is a reminder that the offshore energy system is not only about commodity prices. It is also about trust in execution, project integration, asset-life optimization, and the ability to reduce schedule risk on multi-year developments.
- Industrial: this is one of the strongest packets in the branch for project complexity, backlog quality, and infrastructure intensity. The company’s economics depend on subsea architecture, installation planning, standardization, services attachment, and portfolio-style customer relationships.
- Commodity exposure: TechnipFMC does not own reserves, but it is deeply exposed to customer capital spending confidence. If offshore spending slows, the impact shows up through inbound, backlog conversion, and services cadence rather than through direct realized prices.
- Transport and bottlenecks: subsea and offshore systems are inherently bottlenecked businesses. Vessel access, installation timing, brownfield coordination, and long-lead equipment matter more here than in simpler onshore short-cycle service models.
- Technology: `iEPCI`, `Subsea 2.0`, `iComplete`, and the services layer all show how industrial technology can be monetized through standardization, integration, and installed-base control rather than through software subscriptions alone.
- Infrastructure dependence: this packet is central to the branch’s offshore-capital-intensity and infrastructure-dependence themes because it sits behind the delivery of subsea production systems, SURF, asset services, and offshore tiebacks.
- Capital allocation: TechnipFMC is also a strong cash-conversion packet. It combines backlog growth with large free-cash-flow generation and an explicit shareholder-return framework, making it more than just a “future project optionality” name.
