## What changed

- TechnipFMC adds the subsea and integrated offshore-project version of oilfield equipment and services rather than another more generic services or rig-cycle name.
- The annual filing makes the distinction explicit. The company is organized into `Subsea` and `Surface Technologies`, and its defining operating logic is integration: subsea production systems, SURF, installation, life-of-field services, and offshore project execution rather than just standalone tools or completions work.
- The `2025` filing also shows a business with scale and a differentiated commercial system. TechnipFMC said it had approximately `22,000` employees, a large and expanding installed base, and a business model shaped around `iEPCI`, `Subsea 2.0`, and services tied to aging offshore infrastructure.
- The company is also useful because it sits at the intersection of backlog quality, offshore capital spending, and installed-base monetization. The filing says direct awards, `iEPCI` projects, and services exceeded `80%` of Subsea inbound orders in `2025`, which is a very different operating read than a short-cycle North American service company.
- Capital return is part of the picture, not an afterthought. The annual filing said shareholder distributions more than doubled in `2025`, reaching `\$1.0B`, and committed the company to return at least `70%` of free cash flow to shareholders in `2026`.

## Open questions

- Does TechnipFMC’s current inbound and backlog profile support a sustained multi-year offshore upcycle, or is the business still vulnerable to project timing noise despite better portfolio-style customer planning?
- How much of the current quality improvement in Subsea inbound is structural, meaning more direct awards, services, and integrated work, versus cyclical, meaning a favorable part of the offshore award calendar?
- Can Surface Technologies keep benefiting from international growth and `iComplete` adoption if North American land markets stay uneven?
