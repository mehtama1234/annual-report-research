## Annual report takeaways

- Cheniere adds a distinct energy-infrastructure packet because it sits at the LNG conversion and export chokepoint rather than at the wellhead or the refinery gate. The company owns and operates two major natural-gas liquefaction and export systems: Sabine Pass in Louisiana and Corpus Christi in Texas, alongside the related pipeline and marketing infrastructure that feed them.
- The official IR description and the `2025` `10-K` frame Cheniere as a combined liquefaction, export, and marketing platform. Sabine Pass had six operational trains for approximately `30 mtpa` of production capacity, while Corpus Christi had three operational trains for approximately `15 mtpa` plus the expanding Corpus Christi Stage 3 buildout. This is a useful packet for infrastructure dependence because the company monetizes scarce export capacity, not just gas prices.
- The annual-close materials show the scale clearly. During the three and twelve months ended `December 31, 2025`, Cheniere generated approximately `$5.5B` and `$20.0B` of revenue, approximately `$2.3B` and `$5.3B` of net income, approximately `$2.0B` and `$6.9B` of Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA, and approximately `$1.5B` and `$5.3B` of Distributable Cash Flow.
- Operationally, `2025` was a major transition year. Cheniere reported a record `670` LNG cargoes exported in `2025`, celebrated the tenth anniversary of its first LNG export, and reached substantial completion on the first four trains of the Corpus Christi Stage 3 project. This means the packet is not just about using existing infrastructure; it is also about continuing to deepen the export corridor.
- Capital allocation matters almost as much as operating throughput in this packet. Management said the `20/20 Vision` capital-allocation plan was completed ahead of schedule with over `$20B` deployed since `2022`, and the board upsized share-repurchase authorization to over `$10B` through `2030`. The branch should therefore read Cheniere as an infrastructure-plus-capital-allocation system, not just a commodity exporter.
- The annual-source chain is cleaner than many other energy names in this branch. The official IR annual-reports page exposed a direct `2025 Annual Report` PDF, and the filed SEC `10-K` provides the authoritative annual filing anchor.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: during the three months ended `June 30, 2026`, Cheniere generated approximately `$5.7B` of revenue, approximately `$1.8B` of Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA, approximately `$1.2B` of Distributable Cash Flow, and approximately `$3.1B` of net income.
- Guidance and capital deployment: Cheniere raised full-year `2026` Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA guidance from `$7.25B-$7.75B` to `$7.90B-$8.40B` and Distributable Cash Flow guidance from `$4.75B-$5.25B` to `$5.30B-$5.80B`. It also noted approximately `$884M` of capital deployment in the quarter and approximately `$2.1B` through the first half, including buybacks and dividends.
- Throughput and buildout: Cheniere exported `184` cargoes in the quarter and `371` in the first half. In June `2026`, substantial completion of Midscale Train `6` of the Corpus Christi Stage 3 project was achieved, following Midscale Trains `1-4` in `2025` and Midscale Train `5` in March `2026`. First LNG from Midscale Train `7` was expected imminently.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` shows Cheniere as an LNG-export compounding machine. The economic story is not only higher volumes; it is also the value of controlling scarce liquefaction capacity, finishing expansion trains on schedule, and using a larger platform to raise forward cash guidance again.

This makes the packet useful for understanding the difference between cash-generation infrastructure economics and mark-to-market merchant noise inside an LNG platform.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: during the three and twelve months ended `December 31, 2025`, Cheniere generated approximately `$5.5B` and `$20.0B` of revenue, approximately `$2.3B` and `$5.3B` of net income, approximately `$2.0B` and `$6.9B` of Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA, and approximately `$1.5B` and `$5.3B` of Distributable Cash Flow.
- Guidance and capital allocation: Cheniere introduced full-year `2026` guidance of `$6.75B-$7.25B` of Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA and `$4.35B-$4.85B` of Distributable Cash Flow. It also announced an increase in share-repurchase authorization to over `$10B` through `2030`.
- Operating context: Cheniere exported a record `670` cargoes in `2025`, and the first four trains of Corpus Christi Stage 3 reached substantial completion in March, August, October, and December `2025`, respectively. In February `2026`, LNG was produced for the first time from Train `5`.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` is the annual-close proof that LNG export capacity itself is the scarce asset. Cheniere’s financial model is being strengthened by train completions, high utilization, contracted visibility, and the option value of brownfield expansion at a time when global energy security still supports additional U.S. LNG capacity.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Cheniere adds the LNG liquefaction and export bottleneck layer, which is different from a pure pipeline toll road and different from an upstream producer.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is remote, but the packet still ties back to end-use energy security because Cheniere is effectively a bridge between U.S. gas supply and global power, industrial, and heating demand.
- Cultural: the broader signal is that energy security is now partly an export-infrastructure story. Cheniere’s filings consistently frame LNG as clean, secure, and affordable energy delivered through scarce and strategically valuable infrastructure.
- Regulatory: this is a high-regulation packet shaped by FERC approvals, export authorization, trade policy, permitting, and the timing of approvals for incremental brownfield expansion.
- Labor: labor matters here through project execution, construction sequencing, commissioning, and safe operation of a growing liquefaction complex rather than through conventional retail or field-service labor intensity.
- Technology: Cheniere is not a software company, but optimization and Integrated Production Marketing matter materially. The filings make clear that merchant and hedging structures can reshape reported results even when the core liquefaction machine is operating well.
- Capital allocation: this is one of the strongest branch packets for linking growth capex, buybacks, dividends, debt management, and long-duration contracted cash flow under one model.
- Infrastructure dependence: Cheniere is a direct proof that U.S. energy advantage increasingly depends on conversion and export capacity. Pipelines alone are not enough; liquefaction trains, berths, storage, interconnects, and expansion timing are their own bottlenecks.
