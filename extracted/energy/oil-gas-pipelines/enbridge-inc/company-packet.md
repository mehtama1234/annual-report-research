## Annual report takeaways

- Enbridge is one of the stronger missing flagship packets for the cross-border and utility-adjacent side of North American midstream. The company is not just moving crude through long-haul pipes. Its operating system spans `Liquids Pipelines`, `Gas Transmission`, `Gas Distribution and Storage`, and `Renewable Power Generation`, with reporting in Canadian dollars and a strategic posture that blends pipeline throughput, utility regulation, storage optionality, and power infrastructure.
- The `2025` annual-close evidence is strong and unusually coherent. Enbridge reported full-year `2025` GAAP earnings attributable to common shareholders of `C$7.1B` or `C$3.23` per common share, adjusted earnings of `C$6.6B` or `C$3.02` per share, adjusted EBITDA of `C$20.0B`, and distributable cash flow of `C$12.5B`. The annual-close framing also emphasized a secured backlog of `C$39B`, up about `35%` since Enbridge Day, and `C$5B` of organic growth capital placed into service during `2025`.
- The more important read is structural rather than numerical. Enbridge belongs in this archive because it shows what happens when a pipeline company becomes part transport corridor, part gas utility, part storage network, and part lower-carbon power developer. That makes the packet a better fit for energy-security, infrastructure dependence, and capital-allocation analysis than for a simple commodity beta read.
- The `2025` materials also make the asset intensity obvious. Management highlighted Mainline Optimization Phase 1, storage expansions, utility-rate growth, and renewable projects as parts of the same capital program. This is useful because it shows that reliable energy delivery depends on many different pieces of infrastructure moving at once rather than on crude production growth alone.
- AnnualReports still lagged at `2024` on `Monday, August 10, 2026`, so the packet correctly treats AnnualReports as taxonomy confirmation only. The authoritative chain is the official Enbridge investor-materials set plus the filed SEC `10-K`, `10-Q`, and `8-K` sequence.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` GAAP earnings attributable to common shareholders were `C$1.4B`, or `C$0.64` per common share, versus `C$2.2B`, or `C$1.00` per common share, in `Q2 2025`.
- Earnings power read: adjusted EBITDA was `C$4.776B`, up from `C$4.644B` a year earlier. Adjusted earnings were `C$1.382B`, or `C$0.63` per common share, versus `C$1.418B`, or `C$0.65` per common share, in `Q2 2025`.
- Cash and guidance read: Enbridge generated `C$4.111B` of cash provided by operating activities and `C$2.948B` of distributable cash flow, then reaffirmed `2026` full-year financial guidance and the medium-term financial outlook.
- Capital and infrastructure read: management sanctioned and began construction of the `US$1.0B` Line 5 Relocation project in Wisconsin, explicitly framing it around secure and reliable energy delivery to the Midwest, Ontario, and Quebec. It also signed an exclusive option on the `TTC Connector` pipeline and highlighted Gas Transmission expansion demand exceeding expectations on Algonquin.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is the cleanest proof that Enbridge adds more than another crude corridor. The quarter tied together Line 5, Gulf Coast gas connectivity, utility rate-case progress, more than `7 million` utility customers, and a renewable-power pipeline of over `1.5 GW` of safe-harbored opportunities.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` GAAP earnings attributable to common shareholders were `C$1.7B`, or `C$0.77` per common share, compared with `C$2.3B`, or `C$1.04` per common share, in `Q1 2025`.
- Earnings power read: adjusted earnings were `C$2.130B`, or `C$0.98` per common share, compared with `C$2.242B`, or `C$1.03` per common share, in `Q1 2025`. Adjusted EBITDA was `C$5.810B`, effectively in line with the prior year, and distributable cash flow was `C$3.851B`, up from `C$3.777B`.
- Capital and project read: the quarter expanded the secured backlog to `C$40B` and sanctioned projects across very different demand channels: the `US$0.7B` Cone wind project supporting Meta data-center operations, the `US$0.4B` Tres Palacios storage expansion, a `US$0.1B` Vector Pipeline expansion, and more storage at the Dawn Hub in Ontario.
- Strategic read: management said Mainline volumes averaged `3.2 million barrels per day` and the system had been apportioned all year. That matters because it shows Enbridge still owns a throughput bottleneck even while the broader story is shifting toward gas, utilities, and power demand.
- System read: `Q1 2026` is the best bridge quarter for the bigger theme layer. It connects pipeline optimization, LNG-linked gas demand, utility load growth, data-center power demand, and cross-border energy security inside one company packet.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` GAAP earnings attributable to common shareholders were `C$1.9B`, or `C$0.89` per common share, versus `C$0.5B`, or `C$0.23` per common share, in `Q4 2024`. Full-year `2025` GAAP earnings attributable to common shareholders were `C$7.1B`.
- Earnings power read: `Q4 2025` adjusted EBITDA was `C$5.213B`, up from `C$5.130B` a year earlier, while full-year adjusted EBITDA reached `C$19.952B`. Full-year adjusted earnings were `C$6.578B`, and full-year distributable cash flow was `C$12.454B`.
- Allocation read: Enbridge said it placed `C$5B` of organic growth capital into service during `2025`, sanctioned `C$14B` of organic growth projects during the year, and increased the secured backlog to `C$39B`.
- Strategic read: this is where the infrastructure mix becomes legible. Management highlighted Mainline Optimization Phase 1, U.S. Gulf Coast storage, utility-rate progress, and renewable projects with technology-company counterparties. That is not the profile of a narrow single-basin pipeline operator.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the quarter that makes Enbridge especially valuable for this branch. It shows how midstream, utility regulation, storage buildout, and renewable-power contracts can sit inside one capital-intensive energy-delivery platform.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Enbridge adds the cross-border, utility-like, capital-allocation-heavy version of energy infrastructure rather than just another U.S. crude or gas pipe packet.
- Consumer: consumer demand is indirect but real here. The company sits behind fuel delivery, home heating, local utility reliability, and rising power-load needs, which makes it part of the delivery layer behind households and commercial activity rather than just an upstream throughput name.
- Cultural and societal: this packet is strong for the energy-security lens. Management repeatedly framed North America as a more critical global supplier amid geopolitical volatility, while presenting Enbridge as part of the infrastructure base that makes that role possible.
- Industrial: Enbridge is useful because it shows infrastructure dependence across multiple bottleneck types at once:
  - crude egress
  - gas transmission
  - storage capacity
  - regulated distribution systems
  - power-generation buildout
- Commodity exposure: Enbridge is not commodity-insulated, but its risk mix is different from upstream. The relevant exposures are utilization, rate cases, regulatory approvals, capital-project execution, and whether demand growth continues across LNG, utilities, and power generation.
- Capital intensity: this is one of the better packets for showing how backlog size, sanctioned project cadence, and long-cycle buildouts become a core part of the business model rather than an occasional add-on.
- Transport bottlenecks: Mainline apportionment, Flanagan South and Southern Access expansions, Line 5, and Gulf Coast storage and connector projects all reinforce the same point: throughput systems remain constrained even when resource supply is abundant.
- Infrastructure dependence: Enbridge is a clean archive example of why energy systems cannot be understood as production alone. Delivery, storage, utility rate base, and contracted power assets all matter to the final system outcome.
- Refining and downstream adjacency: Enbridge is not a refiner, but it still connects upstream and downstream realities because liquids-pipeline optimization, Gulf Coast gas connectivity, and utility/service territory demand all affect whether fuel and energy reach end markets reliably.
