## What changed

- Enbridge adds a different midstream packet than the branch's mostly U.S. corridor and gathering names. It is a cross-border energy-infrastructure system spanning crude pipelines, gas transmission, gas distribution and storage utilities, and renewable power generation.
- The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows a company that behaves less like a pure throughput toll road and more like a regulated and utility-adjacent capital allocator. Mainline throughput still matters, but so do rate cases, gas-utility rate base growth, storage expansions, and renewable projects linked to power demand.
- The packet is particularly useful for the energy-security and infrastructure-dependence lens. Management repeatedly framed Enbridge as a North American delivery system serving crude export capacity, LNG-linked gas demand, power-generation growth, and residential and commercial utility customers.
- AnnualReports lagged at `2024`, but the official Enbridge IR chain was materially cleaner than several other names in this branch. That makes Enbridge a good proof case for the intended research hierarchy: AnnualReports for taxonomy, official IR for current company materials, and SEC for hard filing confirmation.

## Open questions

- How much incremental value can Enbridge create by remaining diversified across liquids, gas transmission, gas utilities, and renewable power rather than leaning harder into one faster-growing lane?
- Does the growing utility and storage mix make the company more durable than a pure pipeline operator, or does it also raise the regulatory drag and capital-approval burden enough to slow returns?
- If North American power demand, LNG infrastructure, and crude export requirements keep rising together, does Enbridge become one of the clearest infrastructure bottleneck owners in the archive rather than just another large pipeline company?
