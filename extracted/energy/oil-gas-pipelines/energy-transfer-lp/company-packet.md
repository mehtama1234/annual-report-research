## Annual report takeaways

- Energy Transfer is one of the clearest packets in the branch for why "midstream" is too narrow a label by itself. The system now includes approximately `140,000` miles of pipeline and associated infrastructure across `44` states, with earnings exposure spanning natural gas midstream, intrastate and interstate transportation and storage, crude and refined-product logistics, NGL transportation and terminalling, fractionation, export loading, and related equity interests in `Sunoco LP`, `SunocoCorp`, and `USA Compression`.
- The `2025` annual-close materials show a diversified earnings model rather than a single-asset toll-road model. Management said no single business segment contributed more than one-third of consolidated adjusted EBITDA for the three months or full year ended `2025-12-31`, that about `40%` of adjusted EBITDA came from natural-gas-related assets, and that the vast majority of segment margin was fee-based with limited commodity-price sensitivity.
- The filed `10-K` shows full-year `2025` adjusted EBITDA of roughly `$15.984B`, up from about `$15.483B` in `2024`. The `Q4 2025` results release paired that with quarterly adjusted EBITDA of `$4.18B`, distributable cash flow attributable to partners as adjusted of `$2.04B`, and quarterly net income attributable to partners of `$928M`.
- Capital allocation is part of the story, not a side note. In `December 2025`, Energy Transfer suspended development of the `Lake Charles LNG` export project to redirect capital toward a large backlog of natural-gas infrastructure projects with better risk-return profiles. That makes the packet especially relevant to the energy-security and infrastructure-dependence themes: the company is not just moving molecules, it is reprioritizing capital toward the bottlenecks management thinks are most valuable.
- The annual-source chain is clean. AnnualReports only confirmed taxonomy through the legacy `Energy Transfer Partners, L.P.` page and still showed `2024` as the latest archive row locally, while the official IR annual-reports page exposed the `2025 10-K` and the SEC filing provides the authoritative annual content.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income attributable to partners was `$2.09B` versus `$1.16B` in `Q2 2025`, net income per common unit was `$0.59`, adjusted EBITDA was `$5.07B` versus `$3.87B`, and distributable cash flow attributable to partners as adjusted was `$2.59B` versus `$1.96B`.
- Capital and guidance: growth capital expenditures were about `$1.10B` and maintenance capital expenditures were about `$307M`. Energy Transfer raised full-year `2026` adjusted-EBITDA guidance to `$18.8B` to `$19.1B` from `$18.2B` to `$18.6B` and raised expected `2026` growth capital to `$5.6B` to `$5.9B`.
- Operating system: record NGL transportation volumes rose `13%`, NGL exports rose `25%`, NGL fractionation volumes rose `3%`, crude-oil transportation volumes rose `4%`, and midstream gathered volumes rose `4%`.
- Project and infrastructure signals: the company announced a fully subscribed Nederland export expansion that adds `240,000 bpd` of ethane export capacity and `55,000 bpd` of additional LPG capacity, along with expanded Mont Belvieu-to-Nederland NGL pipeline capacity and two additional ship docks. It also completed Lone Star Express upgrades that add more than `90,000 bpd` of incremental Permian NGL takeaway and signed long-term transportation and/or fractionation agreements for about `300,000 bpd` on y-grade assets extending into the `2030s`.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is strong proof that Energy Transfer is not just a tariff pipe owner. The quarter was driven by export loading, fractionation, takeaway relief, and long-term commercial contracts tied to demand for natural-gas and NGL infrastructure.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income attributable to partners was `$1.25B` versus `$1.32B` in `Q1 2025`, net income per common unit was `$0.35`, adjusted EBITDA was `$4.94B` versus `$4.10B`, and distributable cash flow attributable to partners as adjusted was `$2.70B` versus `$2.31B`.
- Capital and guidance: growth capital expenditures were about `$1.53B` and maintenance capital expenditures were about `$175M`. Energy Transfer raised `2026` adjusted-EBITDA guidance to `$18.2B` to `$18.6B` from `$17.45B` to `$17.85B`, with growth capital expected at `$5.5B` to `$5.9B`.
- Operating system: NGL and refined-product terminal volumes rose `19%`, NGL exports rose `19%`, NGL transportation volumes rose `12%`, NGL fractionation volumes rose `11%`, and crude-oil transportation volumes rose `8%`.
- Project and infrastructure signals: the Gateway NGL Pipeline debottlenecking project entered service, increasing Delaware Basin deliveries into Mont Belvieu. Construction continued on a new `3 million barrel` ethane storage cavern at Mont Belvieu to support a ninth fractionator and future ethane-export expansion. At Nederland, the company extended the majority of existing ethane export agreements into `2041`.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` shows how much Energy Transfer’s economics are linked to long-cycle export and storage buildout rather than only short-cycle pipe tariffs. The value comes from controlling connected corridor infrastructure from basin gathering through fractionation and export docks.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net income attributable to partners was `$928M` versus `$1.08B` in `Q4 2024`, net income per common unit was `$0.25`, adjusted EBITDA was `$4.18B` versus `$3.88B`, and distributable cash flow attributable to partners as adjusted was `$2.04B` versus `$1.98B`.
- Annual-close capital context: growth capital expenditures in the quarter were `$1.40B` and maintenance capital expenditures were `$355M`. The partnership entered `2026` with adjusted-EBITDA guidance of `$17.45B` to `$17.85B`, later raised, and expected to invest `$5.0B` to `$5.5B` in growth capital, primarily on natural-gas network projects.
- Operating system: NGL and refined-product terminal volumes rose `12%`, NGL transportation volumes rose `5%`, NGL fractionation volumes rose `3%` to a record, NGL exports rose `12%`, crude-oil transportation volumes rose `6%` to a record, and midstream gathered volumes rose `4%`.
- Strategic context: management suspended `Lake Charles LNG` development in favor of natural-gas infrastructure projects with stronger risk-return profiles. The quarter also included the close of the `J-W Power` acquisition through `USA Compression`, showing that compression and related power-support assets are increasingly part of the broader infrastructure stack.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the best annual-close expression of Energy Transfer’s identity. This is a diversified, fee-heavy infrastructure platform, but the growth layer increasingly comes from bottleneck relief, export infrastructure, storage, fractionation, and capital discipline around where the next wave of gas and NGL demand will actually need physical capacity.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Energy Transfer adds the full-system transport and export layer inside energy, where value comes from connecting gathering, pipe, storage, fractionation, terminals, and marine export assets rather than from direct commodity ownership.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is indirect, but the packet still maps to the physical delivery layer behind household energy use, industrial feedstocks, transportation fuels, LNG exports, and petrochemical demand.
- Cultural and societal: the broader signal is that energy security is increasingly an infrastructure problem. Growth is going to the owners of corridors, docks, storage, and bottleneck-clearing projects, not just to upstream resource holders.
- Industrial: this is one of the stronger branch examples of how capital intensity and project sequencing shape results. Export growth, basin bottlenecks, fractionation support, compression acquisitions, and natural-gas pipeline expansions all matter together.
- Commodity exposure: Energy Transfer is more protected than an upstream producer because the margin base is largely fee-based, but it is still exposed to commodity-driven flow patterns, export demand, basis differentials, and utilization across key corridors.
- Infrastructure dependence: this packet is a strong proof that hydrocarbon systems increasingly depend on connected downstream logistics. Mont Belvieu, Nederland, Permian takeaway, fractionation capacity, and export docks are all part of the same earnings machine.
- Capital allocation: Lake Charles LNG is the clearest allocation signal. Management chose to defer a headline LNG project in favor of a larger set of natural-gas infrastructure projects with better economics, which is exactly the kind of strategic tradeoff this archive is supposed to capture.
