## Annual report takeaways

- Enterprise is one of the strongest heavy-input packets for the branch because it turns the pipeline lane into a much broader integrated midstream system: gathering, processing, fractionation, storage, pipelines, export docks, and petrochemical-and-refined-products logistics all sit inside the same network.
- The `2025` annual chain showed a business with enough scale to behave like national energy infrastructure rather than a narrow transport asset. Consolidated revenue was `$52.6B`, net income attributable to common unitholders was `$5.81B`, total gross operating margin was `$10.03B`, adjusted EBITDA was `$9.96B`, adjusted CFFO was `$8.71B`, and operational DCF was `$7.90B`.
- The segment mix matters more than the headline revenue line. In `2025`, revenue contributions were `$17.3B` from `NGL Pipelines & Services`, `$20.8B` from `Crude Oil Pipelines & Services`, `$4.2B` from `Natural Gas Pipelines & Services`, and `$10.4B` from `Petrochemical & Refined Products Services`.
- The annual-close materials also showed why Enterprise is a distinct pipeline packet from `Kinder Morgan`. Kinder Morgan is closer to a contract-heavy natural-gas transport and storage toll road. Enterprise is a denser molecules-and-exports system where more of the value comes from processing, fractionation, storage, terminals, and integrated margin capture across liquids, gases, and petrochemical chains.
- Full-year `2025` capital investment, net of asset-sale proceeds, was about `$5.6B`, including `$4.4B` of growth capital, `$632M` for acquired gathering and storage assets, and `$620M` of sustaining capital. Management entered `2026` expecting another large organic-growth year tied to Permian, export, and processing demand.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income attributable to common unitholders reached a record `$1.8B`, diluted EPS was `$0.84`, adjusted EBITDA reached a record `$2.8B`, operational DCF reached a record `$2.3B`, and adjusted CFFO reached a record `$2.5B`.
- Cash flow and capital allocation: operational DCF covered the cash distribution `1.9x`, Enterprise retained about `$1.1B` of DCF, repurchased `$159M` of common units during the quarter, and had used about `34%` of its `$5.0B` buyback authorization on a cumulative basis.
- Volumes and operating system: equivalent pipeline volumes reached a record `14.7 MMBPD`, marine terminal volumes reached a record `2.8 MMBPD`, natural-gas processing inlet volumes were `8.1 Bcf/d`, and the second phase of the `Neches River Terminal` entered service.
- Capital program: second-quarter `2026` capital investments were about `$1.2B`, including about `$1.0B` of growth capital and `$140M` of sustaining capital. Management also approved `Frac 15` at Mont Belvieu and two more `300 MMcf/d` gas-processing plants in the Permian.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is the cleanest proof that Enterprise is monetizing both volume growth and dislocation. Export demand, Permian growth, processing and fractionation density, marine-terminal optionality, and global shipping disruption all fed into the quarter at once. This is not just pipe utilization. It is infrastructure control over how U.S. hydrocarbons are processed and moved to domestic and export demand.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` operating income was about `$1.9B`, net income attributable to common unitholders was about `$1.5B`, diluted EPS was `$0.68`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.7B`, operational DCF was `$2.1B`, and adjusted CFFO was `$2.3B`.
- Balance sheet and liquidity: total debt principal outstanding was about `$34.2B` at `2026-03-31`, and consolidated liquidity was about `$3.3B`.
- Volumes and operating system: the quarter set records in natural-gas processing inlet volumes at `8.3 Bcf/d`, equivalent pipeline transportation at `14.2 MMBPD`, marine-terminal volumes at `2.3 MMBPD`, and NGL fractionation volumes at `1.9 MMBPD`.
- Capital program: first-quarter `2026` capital investments were about `$988M`, including `$783M` of growth capital and `$205M` of sustaining capital. Net `2026` growth-capital spending was expected in the range of `$2.3B` to `$2.6B`, with about `$580M` of sustaining capital.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` showed the integrated-system economics more clearly than a simple commodity view would. New assets such as `Bahia`, `Frac 14`, and newer Permian processing plants drove records across several linked assets at once. The important read is that Enterprise can keep adding throughput and cash flow because it owns the connective tissue between wellhead growth, Mont Belvieu processing, and downstream delivery.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` operating income was `$2.02B`, net income attributable to common unitholders was about `$1.6B`, diluted EPS was `$0.75`, total gross operating margin was `$2.74B`, adjusted EBITDA was `$2.71B`, adjusted CFFO was `$2.43B`, and operational DCF was `$2.16B`.
- Cash flow and capital allocation: operational DCF covered distributions `1.8x`, Enterprise retained about `$1.0B` of DCF, and repurchased roughly `$50M` of common units in the quarter.
- Volumes and operating system: Enterprise closed `2025` with record natural-gas processing inlet volumes of `8.1 Bcf/d`, record NGL fractionation volumes of `1.9 MMBPD`, record ethane marine-terminal volumes of `334 MBPD`, and record total pipeline volumes of `14.1 MMBPD-equivalent`.
- Guidance and infrastructure buildout: management expected `2026` organic growth capital, net of asset-sale proceeds, in a range of roughly `$1.9B` to `$2.3B`, with about `$580M` of sustaining capital. Debt principal outstanding at year-end was about `$34.7B`, and consolidated liquidity was about `$5.2B`.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` made the long-term thesis explicit. Enterprise tied surplus U.S. energy to international demand, rising gas-and-NGL intensity in the Permian, export-terminal expansion, and new gas-pipeline demand from AI and data-center load growth, industrial reshoring, and electrification. This is one of the branch's clearest examples of physical infrastructure bottlenecks becoming monetizable through multiple connected assets rather than a single tariff schedule.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Enterprise adds the integrated midstream and export-complex layer, which is broader than a pure interstate-pipeline or gas-storage story.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is still indirect, but the real demand system now includes export customers, petrochemical chains, utility and industrial load growth, and refined-product logistics in addition to upstream production growth.
- Cultural: the broader social signal is that the energy system still runs on dense hydrocarbon infrastructure even while the public conversation emphasizes newer energy narratives. Enterprise is physically embedded in how the U.S. converts resource abundance into global supply.
- Regulatory: the packet is exposed to environmental and safety regulation, marine-terminal permitting, interstate and intrastate transport policy, export policy, and the build pace of large Gulf Coast infrastructure.
- Labor: labor shows up through construction execution, processing-plant operations, marine-terminal expansion, and the engineering depth required to keep new projects entering service.
- Technology: technology matters here through processing intensity, fractionation, terminal and pipeline optimization, and the ability to densify existing networks, not through a software-first narrative.
- Capital allocation: Enterprise is a useful example of a midstream company trying to self-fund large organic growth while still growing distributions and running buybacks. The branch should treat it as a capital-discipline packet, not only a volume packet.
- Infrastructure dependence: this is one of the strongest packets in the archive for the idea that U.S. energy security, export reach, petrochemical throughput, and even AI-driven power demand depend on old-economy hydrocarbon infrastructure that remains hard to replace.
