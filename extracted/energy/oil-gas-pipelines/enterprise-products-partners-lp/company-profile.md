## What changed

- Enterprise is a stronger second pipeline packet than another pure gas-haul name because it is not only a transport toll-road operator. It is an integrated midstream system spanning gathering, processing, fractionation, pipelines, storage, marine terminals, exports, and petrochemical-and-refined-products logistics.
- The `2025` filing showed the scale clearly. Consolidated revenue was `$52.6B`, net income attributable to common unitholders was `$5.81B`, total gross operating margin was `$10.03B`, adjusted EBITDA was `$9.96B`, and adjusted cash flow from operations was `$8.71B`.
- The quarter chain showed acceleration into `2026`. `Q1 2026` established record processing, fractionation, pipeline, and marine-terminal volumes, while `Q2 2026` delivered record net income, adjusted EBITDA, operational DCF, adjusted CFFO, equivalent pipeline volumes, and marine-terminal volumes.
- The management commentary also sharpened the physical-system story. Enterprise is using Permian gas-and-NGL growth, Mont Belvieu fractionation, Neches River and Houston Ship Channel export infrastructure, and petrochemical and refined-products connectivity to convert U.S. hydrocarbon surplus into fee-rich throughput and processing economics.

## Open questions

- How much of the recent strength is durable fee-based throughput growth versus unusually strong marketing margins and geopolitically distorted export flows?
- Can Enterprise keep self-funding a larger organic-growth queue while still supporting buybacks and distribution growth if energy spreads or export arbitrage cool?
- Does the data-center and electrification gas-demand thesis become a major incremental leg of growth, or does export infrastructure remain the dominant value driver?
