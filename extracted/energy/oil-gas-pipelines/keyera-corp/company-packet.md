## Annual report takeaways

- Keyera is one of the clearest missing packets for the Canadian NGL and condensate infrastructure layer. It is not mainly a simple gas-transmission corridor. The company sits inside gathering and processing, condensate systems, fractionation, storage, terminals, rail-linked logistics, and marketing, which makes it a stronger fit for the branch's midstream and infrastructure lens than a generic pipeline label would suggest.
- The `2025` year-end report shows a company already operating an integrated platform before the `2026` acquisitions fully landed. Annual net earnings were `C$432.3M`, annual adjusted EBITDA was `C$1.131B`, annual funds from operations were `C$853.6M`, annual distributable cash flow was `C$735.2M`, and net debt at year-end was `C$3.629B`.
- The annual segment mix is what makes the packet useful. In `2025`, realized margin was `C$1.333B`, split across Gathering and Processing at `C$439.4M`, Liquids Infrastructure at `C$593.3M`, and Marketing at `C$300.4M`. That makes Keyera a more complete NGL value-chain case than a pipeline-only company.
- The annual report also frames the strategic read directly. Management described `2025` as a transformational year, highlighted record annual fee-based EBITDA, emphasized progress on capital-efficient growth projects, and said the Plains Canadian NGL transaction would create a more integrated cross-Canada NGL system under Canadian ownership. That language makes Keyera especially useful for the branch themes around energy security, infrastructure dependence, and domestic control of critical hydrocarbon logistics.
- The practical economic point is that Keyera is a midstream integrator rather than a pure commodity owner. Its value comes from linking producer volumes to storage, fractionation, condensate handling, rail, and downstream market access. That means utilization, contract quality, project execution, frac spreads, and asset integration matter as much as simple throughput or price exposure.
- The useful difference from `Pembina` is that `Pembina` reads more like corridor-plus-facilities infrastructure across pipelines, processing, fractionation, export, and industrial feedstocks, while `Keyera` reads as the more NGL-market-structure and integrated-liquids-platform case where condensate handling, iso-octane processing, frac spreads, storage, and marketing matter more directly.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net earnings were `C$308M`, adjusted EBITDA was `C$267M`, and distributable cash flow was `C$65M`, or `C$0.25` per share. Excluding transaction costs related to the Plains acquisition, adjusted EBITDA was `C$309M` and DCF was `C$101M`, or `C$0.39` per share.
- Segment read: fee-based realized margin was `C$350M`, up `37%` year over year. Gathering and Processing generated record realized margin of `C$128M`, and Liquids Infrastructure generated record realized margin of `C$222M`.
- Marketing read: the Marketing segment generated realized margin of only `C$36M`, below `Q2 2025`, because the `AEF` outage and risk-management timing reduced iso-octane sales in the quarter even though the facility resumed full operations in early June.
- Balance-sheet and strategy read: the company ended the quarter with net debt to adjusted EBITDA of `3.3x`, above the long-term target range of `2.5x` to `3.0x`, reflecting the higher debt from the Plains and KAPS transactions. Management explicitly said the focus now shifts to integration and deleveraging.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is the quarter that makes the Keyera packet work. It shows the company becoming more strategically valuable even while near-term cash metrics are noisy. Record fee-based margins, higher leverage, acquisition integration, and a still-recovering marketing segment can all happen at once in a midstream consolidator.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` adjusted EBITDA was `C$203M`, or `C$232M` excluding Plains transaction costs. DCF was `C$101M`, or `C$133M` excluding acquisition-related items. Net loss was `C$122M`.
- Segment read: Gathering and Processing generated record quarterly realized margin of `C$118M`, driven by record throughput at the Wapiti gas plant and contributions from the Simonette East interest. Liquids Infrastructure generated realized margin of `C$141M` and included record throughput across Keyera's condensate handling systems.
- Marketing read: the Marketing segment generated only `C$13M` of realized margin because the `AEF` outage disrupted iso-octane production and created timing mismatches between settled risk-management contracts and physical product sales.
- Financial-position read: the company ended the quarter with net debt to adjusted EBITDA of `2.2x`, temporarily below its long-term range because of hybrid issuance proceeds. That left Keyera with balance-sheet flexibility just before the Plains closing.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` shows the split inside the Keyera model very clearly. The underlying fee-for-service infrastructure was strong, but the integrated platform still had real exposure to outages and marketing timing. This is a better packet than a pure pipeline name for showing how midstream can be both steady and operationally lumpy at the same time.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net earnings were `C$90.3M`, quarterly adjusted EBITDA was `C$300.9M`, quarterly funds from operations were `C$234.5M`, and quarterly distributable cash flow was `C$205.5M`.
- Annual-close read: full-year `2025` net earnings were `C$432.3M`, adjusted EBITDA was `C$1.131B`, and DCF was `C$735.2M`.
- Segment read: full-year realized margin was `C$1.333B`, composed of `C$439.4M` in Gathering and Processing, `C$593.3M` in Liquids Infrastructure, and `C$300.4M` in Marketing.
- Strategic read: management described `2025` as a transformational year, highlighted record annual fee-based EBITDA, and explicitly framed the pending Plains acquisition as a way to establish a fully integrated cross-Canada NGL system under Canadian ownership.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the baseline quarter for the packet. It shows Keyera before the `2026` acquisition effects fully appear but after the company had already committed to using portfolio management, project execution, and integrated liquids infrastructure to build a larger competitive platform.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Keyera adds the Canadian NGL and condensate logistics layer rather than another crude or dry-gas transmission backbone.
- Consumer: direct consumer exposure is limited, but this packet is still closer to end-use value chains than many upstream names because the company helps convert and route hydrocarbons into usable products and downstream markets.
- Cultural and societal: this is a useful packet for the idea that energy security is not only about production. It is also about who owns and controls the storage, fractionation, terminal, and transport systems that determine whether production can actually reach valuable markets.
- Industrial: Keyera depends on several linked physical systems:
  - gas gathering and processing
  - condensate handling
  - NGL pipelines and KAPS connectivity
  - fractionation and storage
  - terminals and rail-linked logistics
  - AEF iso-octane production
  - marketing and risk management
- Commodity exposure: Keyera is less exposed than upstream producers to direct commodity ownership, but its marketing and processing economics still depend on frac spreads, iso-octane timing, and other margin conditions.
- Capital intensity: Keyera is a strong midstream case for capital intensity that is less about one giant trunkline and more about expanding integrated liquids infrastructure piece by piece across a network.
- Infrastructure dependence: this is one of the better branch packets for infrastructure interdependence. The value comes from combining assets, not from any single pipe or plant by itself.
- Transport bottlenecks: Keyera helps explain how condensate, NGLs, and processed products need storage, fractionation, terminal access, and rail or downstream connectivity in addition to upstream supply.
- Bigger-picture distinction: Keyera is especially useful for separating pipeline systems into different economic types:
  - long-haul gas backbones
  - utility-like transmission systems
  - integrated NGL facility corridors
  - marketing-linked liquids platforms like Keyera, where the infrastructure and margin book work together
