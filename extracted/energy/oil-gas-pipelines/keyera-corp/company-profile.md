## What changed

- Keyera adds the Canadian NGL logistics, storage, condensate, fractionation, and marketing layer rather than another long-haul gas transmission system.
- The packet is distinctive because it is not mainly a toll-road pipeline story. It is about how gathering and processing, condensate systems, fractionation, storage, terminals, rail, and marketing fit together to move and monetize NGLs.
- The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain also shows a company actively reshaping its platform through the Plains Canadian NGL acquisition and the move to full ownership of KAPS.
- This makes Keyera one of the stronger branch cases for integrated midstream economics, NGL infrastructure dependence, fee-based growth, market access, and the difference between steady contracted infrastructure margins and more volatile marketing or processing exposure.

## Open questions

- How quickly can Keyera translate the Plains Canadian NGL acquisition and full KAPS ownership into durable per-share cash-flow growth instead of one-time transaction noise?
- Does the long-term value sit more in stable fee-based condensate and fractionation infrastructure, or in the ability to combine that infrastructure with a marketing book and export-oriented logistics?
- How much of the next cycle depends on AEF normalizing and on frac spread realization versus on the steadier contracted side of the business?
