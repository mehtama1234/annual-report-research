## Annual report takeaways

- MPLX is one of the stronger missing flagship packets for the integrated gas-and-liquids side of midstream. The company describes itself as a diversified, large-cap master limited partnership that owns and operates midstream energy infrastructure and logistics assets, including crude and refined-product pipelines, terminals, storage, and marine assets, but the current strategic emphasis is clearly moving through natural-gas and NGL gathering, processing, fractionation, treating, and export-connected infrastructure.
- The `2025` annual-close narrative is straightforward. MPLX reported full-year `2025` net income attributable to MPLX of `\$4.9B`, adjusted EBITDA of `\$7.0B`, net cash provided by operating activities of `\$5.9B`, and distributable cash flow of `\$5.8B`, while management emphasized both the scale of growth investment and the continued return of capital to unitholders.
- The more important read is structural rather than numerical. MPLX is not just a passive toll-road operator. The annual and quarter materials show the partnership actively building a natural-gas and NGL value chain through sour-gas treating, processing plants, pipeline expansions, fractionation, and export-linked infrastructure. This is a system-buildout packet.
- The `2025` packet also matters because it pairs capital intensity with cash-return discipline. MPLX said full-year `2025` growth investments were `\$5.5B` while capital returned to unitholders was `\$4.4B`. That is a useful archive case for how a large midstream partnership can finance expansion without presenting itself as a pure growth-at-all-costs story.
- The Marathon Petroleum relationship is another key distinction. MPLX is not simply a basin-pipeline company. Parts of the asset base and growth strategy remain tied to Marathon's fuels value chains, refinery adjacency, and Gulf Coast logistics footprint, which gives the packet a more integrated infrastructure read than a single-molecule transmission business.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income attributable to MPLX was `\$1.077B`, versus `\$1.048B` in `Q2 2025`.
- Earnings power read: adjusted EBITDA attributable to MPLX was `\$1.775B`, up from `\$1.690B` a year earlier. Segment adjusted EBITDA was `\$1.161B` for Crude Oil and Products Logistics and `\$614M` for Natural Gas and NGL Services.
- Cash-return read: MPLX generated `\$1.702B` of net cash provided by operating activities, `\$1.450B` of distributable cash flow, and `\$668M` of adjusted free cash flow. It declared a `\$1.0765` per unit quarterly distribution with `1.3x` coverage and ended the quarter at `3.7x` leverage.
- Capital and project read: management raised the `2026` growth-capital outlook by `\$500M` to `\$2.9B`, with more than `90%` of organic growth capital aimed at natural-gas and NGL infrastructure. The release tied that step-up to accelerated Gulf Coast fractionation execution and explicitly framed the investments around global demand for U.S. energy.
- System-buildout read: `Q2 2026` is the clearest evidence that MPLX belongs in this lane as a transport-bottleneck and infrastructure-dependence packet. The company highlighted Harmon Creek III beginning operations in August, additional Permian sour-gas treating progress, the BANGL pipeline, Gulf Coast fractionators, and the Gulf Coast LPG Export Terminal JV. This is about building the connective tissue that lets molecules actually move through the system.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income attributable to MPLX was `\$912M`, compared with `\$1.126B` in `Q1 2025`.
- Earnings power read: adjusted EBITDA attributable to MPLX was `\$1.729B`, with `\$1.111B` from Crude Oil and Products Logistics and `\$618M` from Natural Gas and NGL Services.
- Cash-return read: MPLX generated `\$1.347B` of net cash provided by operating activities, `\$1.408B` of distributable cash flow, and `\$549M` of adjusted free cash flow. The quarterly distribution remained `\$1.0765` per common unit, coverage stayed at `1.3x`, and leverage stayed at `3.7x`.
- Capital and operating read: management emphasized Delaware Basin sour-gas treating expansion to more than `400 MMcf/d` by year-end and bringing Harmon Creek III into service in the third quarter. That makes `Q1 2026` the quarter where the growth thesis was still partly promise and construction rather than already-operating capacity.
- Structural read: the key lesson from `Q1 2026` is that MPLX could absorb a lower year-over-year net-income figure while preserving the broader cash-generation and distribution machine. That is one of the reasons midstream deserves its own analytical lane rather than being collapsed into generic energy exposure.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net income attributable to MPLX was `\$1.193B`, versus `\$1.099B` in `Q4 2024`. Full-year `2025` net income attributable to MPLX was `\$4.9B`.
- Earnings power read: adjusted EBITDA attributable to MPLX was `\$1.804B` for the quarter and `\$7.0B` for the full year.
- Cash and capital read: in `Q4 2025`, MPLX generated `\$1.496B` of net cash provided by operating activities, `\$1.417B` of distributable cash flow, and `\$1.567B` of adjusted free cash flow. For full-year `2025`, it generated `\$5.9B`, `\$5.8B`, and `\$1.0B`, respectively.
- Allocation read: management said full-year `2025` growth investments were `\$5.5B`, capital returned to unitholders was `\$4.4B`, and the initial `2026` organic growth-capital plan was `\$2.4B`. The point is not only that MPLX is big. It is that the partnership is funding major infrastructure additions while still presenting itself as a yield-and-return vehicle.
- Strategic read: `Q4 2025` is where the current natural-gas and NGL buildout becomes legible: Harmon Creek III, BANGL expansion, Gulf Coast fractionators near Marathon's Galveston Bay refinery, and the Gulf Coast LPG Export Terminal JV all sit inside a single infrastructure thesis rather than as disconnected projects.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: MPLX adds the integrated natural-gas and NGL value-chain version of midstream, not just another long-haul pipeline name.
- Consumer: consumer demand is indirect, but this packet still matters for fuel reliability, heating and power inputs, petrochemical feedstocks, and export-linked energy availability because it sits in the infrastructure layer behind molecules reaching end markets.
- Cultural and societal: MPLX is useful for the energy-security and infrastructure-dependence lens. The company is effectively arguing that reliable energy systems require more than production growth; they require buildout across gathering, treating, processing, fractionation, storage, terminaling, and export corridors.
- Industrial: this is one of the clearest packets for transport bottlenecks and value-chain choke points. Processing plants, de-ethanizers, fractionators, and export terminals can be just as economically decisive as pipelines themselves.
- Commodity exposure: MPLX is less directly commodity-price-driven than an upstream producer, but it is still exposed to basin activity, producer drilling economics, project utilization, and the broader competitiveness of U.S. natural-gas and NGL supply.
- Capital allocation: the packet is valuable because it combines three things at once:
  - heavy organic growth spending
  - large cash distributions and capital return
  - disciplined leverage framing
- Infrastructure dependence: MPLX is a strong bridge case for showing how upstream growth, refining connectivity, petrochemical demand, and exports all depend on a midstream layer that is expensive, slow to build, and operationally specific.
- Refining adjacency: Marathon linkage matters here. MPLX helps show that some midstream systems are not just basin-to-hub toll roads; they are partially embedded in downstream and export value chains.
