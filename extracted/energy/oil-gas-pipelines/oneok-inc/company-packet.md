## Annual report takeaways

- ONEOK is one of the clearest packets in the branch for why "midstream" should not be treated as a single asset type. Management describes the company as a leading midstream operator providing gathering, processing, fractionation, transportation, storage, and marine export services through an approximately `60,000-mile` pipeline network.
- The packet is stronger than another pure gas-transmission case because the system now spans natural gas, NGLs, refined products, and crude, with integration benefits flowing through multiple segments instead of one tariff schedule. The annual and quarter materials repeatedly tie the story to volume growth, synergy capture, optimization and marketing activity, and operating leverage created by acquisitions and project completions.
- The `Q4 2025` annual-close materials effectively summarize the `2025` year. Full-year net income attributable to ONEOK reached `$3.393B`, diluted EPS reached `$5.42`, and adjusted EBITDA reached `$8.02B`, or `$8.085B` excluding transaction costs. Management called out another year of double-digit earnings growth and continued synergy capture from a multi-year acquisition plan.
- The branch should read ONEOK as an integration-heavy logistics platform rather than a slow-moving utility-like pipeline packet. The quarter materials show `Magellan`, `EnLink`, `Medallion`, `Northern Border`, and `Matterhorn` all showing up in the economic story, along with higher NGL throughput, refined-products shipping, natural-gas processing, and optimization around basis differentials.
- The annual-source nuance matters. AnnualReports still lagged at `2024` in the saved company-page capture, while ONEOK's official annual-reports page already listed a `2025` annual-report route dated `2026-04-01`. The official annual-report route pointed to an external Issuu experience whose local capture was only a stub, so the filed `10-K` remains the authoritative annual content source.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income attributable to ONEOK was `$966M` versus `$841M` in `Q2 2025`, diluted EPS was `$1.53`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$2.121B` versus `$1.981B` a year earlier.
- Volumes and operating system: management highlighted record NGL raw feed throughput volumes, including a `15%` increase in the Gulf Coast and Permian region, an `8%` increase in refined products volumes shipped, and a `2%` increase in natural gas volumes processed.
- Guidance and capital program: ONEOK increased `2026` net income guidance to `$3.41B` to `$3.79B`, raised adjusted EBITDA guidance to `$8.2B` to `$8.5B`, and kept total `2026` capital-expenditure guidance unchanged at roughly `$2.7B` to `$3.2B`.
- Capital allocation and balance-sheet context: the quarter included a declared dividend of `$1.07` per share, or `$4.28` annualized. Capital expenditures for the quarter were about `$613M`, including about `$101M` of maintenance capital.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is a clean proof that ONEOK is monetizing an integrated logistics system. Higher NGL, refined-products, and processing volumes mattered, but so did optimization and marketing activity across multiple segments. This is broader than a pipeline toll-road story because the company benefits from moving and repositioning multiple hydrocarbon streams across one network.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income attributable to ONEOK was `$774M` versus `$636M` in `Q1 2025`, diluted EPS was `$1.23`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$1.997B` versus `$1.775B` a year earlier.
- Volumes and operating system: the company highlighted a `15%` increase in NGL raw feed throughput volumes, a `12%` increase in refined-products volumes shipped, and a `5%` increase in natural gas volumes processed. Gulf Coast and Permian NGL raw feed throughput volumes rose `31%`.
- Guidance and capital program: ONEOK increased `2026` net income guidance to `$3.21B` to `$3.79B`, raised adjusted EBITDA guidance to `$8.0B` to `$8.5B`, and left total `2026` capital expenditures at approximately `$2.7B` to `$3.2B`.
- Balance sheet and capital actions: the quarter included a noncash impairment charge of `$60M` tied to a refined-products-and-crude JV investment, the relocation of a `150 MMcf/d` plant to the Permian, redemption of the remaining `$491M` of `4.85%` senior notes due July `2026`, and entry into a `$1.2B` term loan agreement. ONEOK also declared a quarterly dividend of `$1.07` per share.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` showed how much of ONEOK's recent strength is tied to integration and optionality, not just base tariff growth. Higher NGL and refined-products volumes mattered, but so did optimization and marketing linked to `Waha`-to-`Katy` differentials and a more constructive market environment.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net income attributable to ONEOK was `$977M` versus `$923M` in `Q4 2024`, diluted EPS was `$1.55`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$2.145B`. Full-year `2025` net income attributable to ONEOK was `$3.393B`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$8.02B`.
- Guidance and capital program: ONEOK entered `2026` with net income guidance centered around `$3.45B`, adjusted EBITDA guidance centered around `$8.1B`, and total capital expenditures expected at approximately `$2.7B` to `$3.2B`.
- Strategic context: management said `2025` earnings growth reflected increased volumes and continued synergy capture from a multi-year acquisition plan, while key project completions created significant operating leverage and stable fee-based earnings. The quarter materials also show how `EnLink` was already contributing meaningfully to adjusted EBITDA.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` is the clearest annual-close proof that ONEOK is not just a legacy natural-gas pipe company. The packet combines steady fee-based earnings, acquisition-led scale, NGL and refined-products flow growth, and growing optimization upside. It therefore sits between a classic pipeline infrastructure story and a broader hydrocarbon logistics and integration story.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: ONEOK adds the integrated multi-product midstream and logistics layer, sitting between a pure gas-transmission case and a broader export-complex packet.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is indirect, but the packet still maps to real end-use systems through refined-products delivery, natural-gas transportation, NGL processing, and the broader need to move hydrocarbons into domestic and export markets.
- Cultural: the broader social signal is that energy infrastructure still compounds by integration. ONEOK's value is not just in owning pipes, but in owning a connected system that can absorb acquisitions, redirect flows, and monetize complexity.
- Regulatory: the packet is exposed to pipeline regulation, environmental and safety oversight, rate cases, refined-products and blending regulation, and broader trade and tariff effects.
- Labor: labor shows up through plant relocation, project execution, ongoing field operations, acquisitions integration, and the maintenance burden of a large multi-product network.
- Technology: technology matters here through optimization, network management, and system control rather than through a software-first product story.
- Capital allocation: ONEOK is useful as a packet where acquisitions, synergies, dividends, project capex, and leverage management all matter together. The branch should treat it as an integration-and-allocation packet, not just a throughput packet.
- Infrastructure dependence: this is a strong archive example of how hydrocarbon infrastructure value increasingly comes from connected systems that handle natural gas, NGLs, refined products, and crude together instead of as isolated asset silos.
