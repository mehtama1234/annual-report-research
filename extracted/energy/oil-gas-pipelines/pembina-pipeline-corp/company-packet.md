## Annual report takeaways

- Pembina is one of the stronger missing flagship packets for the integrated Canadian midstream and NGL system. This is not just a long-haul pipeline operator. The `2025` annual-close materials and annual filing package position Pembina as a transport, facilities, fractionation, logistics, and export-linked infrastructure company whose assets sit between upstream production, industrial-feedstock demand, and export markets.
- The `2025` close was financially solid. Pembina reported full-year `2025` revenue of `C$7.778B`, net revenue of `C$4.877B`, earnings of `C$1.694B`, adjusted EBITDA of `C$4.289B`, and adjusted cash flow from operating activities of `C$2.854B` or `C$4.91` per common share. `Q4 2025` itself produced revenue of `C$1.913B`, net revenue of `C$1.139B`, earnings of `C$489M`, adjusted EBITDA of `C$1.075B`, and adjusted cash flow from operating activities of `C$731M`.
- The more useful read is structural rather than numerical. Pembina highlighted record annual Pipelines and Facilities volumes of `3.7 million barrels of oil equivalent per day`, two sanctioned pipeline expansion projects totaling `C$425M`, Cedar LNG remarketing progress, and new take-or-pay agreements with Tourmaline and PGI that combine gas processing, Peace Pipeline transportation, and Redwater Complex fractionation. That combination shows why this packet belongs in the branch: it explains how multiple infrastructure layers have to line up for hydrocarbon and NGL systems to work.
- The annual-close packet also matters because it expands the theme lens beyond crude takeaway. Pembina is a cleaner example of infrastructure dependence across gas processing, NGL fractionation, and logistics than several U.S.-heavy pipeline names already in the branch. The company becomes more useful when read as an integrated corridor-and-facilities operator rather than as a generic fee-based midstream name.
- AnnualReports still lagged at `2024` on `Monday, August 10, 2026`, so the packet correctly treats AnnualReports as taxonomy confirmation only. The authoritative chain is Pembina's official investor-material set plus the filed SEC `40-F` and `6-K` sequence.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` revenue was `C$1.792B`, net revenue was `C$1.322B`, earnings were `C$512M`, basic earnings per common share were `C$0.83`, adjusted earnings were `C$415M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `C$1.064B`.
- Cash and capital read: cash flow from operating activities was `C$897M`, adjusted cash flow from operating activities was `C$778M` or `C$1.34` per common share, and capital expenditures were `C$218M`.
- Project and strategy read: Pembina sanctioned two significant new growth projects totaling about `C$3B` net to Pembina, the `Greenlight Electricity Centre` and the `Heartland Extraction Plant`. It also highlighted participation in a nation-building West Coast oil pipeline project, expanded integrated ethane solutions for `Dow Path2Zero`, and put `RFS IV` into service in late May, adding `55,000 bpd` of propane-plus fractionation at the Redwater Complex.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is the clearest proof that Pembina adds more than another pipe operator. The quarter ties together fractionation, extraction, industrial-feedstock infrastructure, and export-linked optionality. That makes the packet valuable for transport bottlenecks and infrastructure-dependence analysis, not just for throughput or tariff-rate tracking.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` revenue was `C$2.106B`, net revenue was `C$1.291B`, earnings were `C$498M`, basic and diluted earnings per common share were `C$0.80`, adjusted earnings were `C$505M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `C$1.131B`.
- Cash and segment read: cash flow from operating activities was `C$335M`, adjusted cash flow from operating activities was `C$790M` or `C$1.36` per common share, and capital expenditures were `C$187M`. Segment adjusted EBITDA was `C$647M` in `Pipelines`, `C$363M` in `Facilities`, and `C$188M` in `Marketing & New Ventures`.
- Asset-system read: Pembina said the `Wapiti Expansion` entered service, adding `115 MMcf/d` at the Wapiti Plant; the `K3 Cogeneration Facility` with `28 MW` of capacity was placed into service; and `RFS IV` was nearing completion. It also highlighted Peace Pipeline recontracting and increased the quarterly common share dividend by about `3.5%`.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is the best bridge quarter for the integrated-NGL thesis. The segment mix shows Pembina making money not only from pipe movement but from facilities and commercial optimization. That is exactly the distinction this branch needs inside the pipelines lane.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` revenue was `C$1.913B`, net revenue was `C$1.139B`, earnings were `C$489M`, basic earnings per common share were `C$0.78`, and adjusted EBITDA was `C$1.075B`. Full-year `2025` earnings were `C$1.694B`, adjusted EBITDA was `C$4.289B`, and adjusted cash flow from operating activities was `C$2.854B`.
- Allocation and demand read: Pembina announced a first-quarter `2026` common share dividend of `C$0.71` per share and paired the year-end close with record annual Pipelines and Facilities volumes of `3.7 million` boe/d.
- Strategic read: this is where the integrated value chain becomes legible. The quarter paired `C$425M` of pipeline expansions with Cedar LNG remarketing progress and the Tourmaline and PGI agreements covering `270 mmcf/d` of gas processing plus Peace Pipeline transportation and Redwater fractionation.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the key annual-close quarter for the packet because it shows how upstream volume growth, processing, pipeline transportation, fractionation, and export capacity all reinforce each other inside one infrastructure system.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Pembina adds the Canadian integrated-NGL and facilities version of the pipeline lane rather than another U.S. crude or gas corridor packet.
- Consumer: consumer demand is indirect but real here. Pembina sits behind fuel, petrochemical-feedstock, and energy-delivery systems rather than at the household interface, which makes it part of the embedded infrastructure layer behind manufacturing, export, and end-market energy consumption.
- Cultural and societal: this packet is strong for the energy-security and nation-building lens. The filings and management commentary frame Canadian export infrastructure, industrial-customer solutions, and transport reliability as strategic assets rather than as simple tariffed pipes.
- Industrial: Pembina is useful because it shows infrastructure dependence across several bottleneck types at once:
  - gas gathering and processing
  - liquids transportation
  - NGL fractionation
  - extraction and petrochemical-feedstock infrastructure
  - export-linked logistics
- Commodity exposure: Pembina is less direct commodity beta than upstream names, but it is still exposed to basin volumes, customer activity, recontracting, and whether integrated projects such as Cedar LNG, Heartland, and Redwater remain attractive enough to justify capital.
- Capital intensity: this is one of the better packets for showing how repeatable expansions, fractionators, extraction plants, cogeneration, and integrated-customer projects become part of the operating model rather than isolated one-off builds.
- Transport bottlenecks: Pembina helps broaden the bottleneck theme beyond crude takeaway. The relevant constraints include Peace Pipeline capacity, gas-processing throughput, fractionation at Redwater, and the need to connect upstream supply to industrial and export demand.
- Infrastructure dependence: Pembina is a clean archive example of why hydrocarbon systems cannot be understood as production alone. Processing, transportation, fractionation, and industrial-end-market linkage all matter to the final economics.
- Refining and downstream adjacency: Pembina is not a refiner, but it still connects upstream and downstream realities because the company helps move and process NGL and hydrocarbon streams into feedstock, logistics, and export channels that downstream systems depend on.
