## What changed

- Plains adds a crude-focused midstream system that is meaningfully different from the branch's gas-heavy and NGL-heavy pipeline names. The company is built around gathering, transportation, terminalling, storage, and marketing for crude oil across producing basins, transport corridors, major hubs, and export outlets.
- The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows an explicit portfolio transition. Plains used the `Cactus III` acquisition plus the sale of the Canadian NGL business to reframe itself as a more focused crude-oil midstream platform rather than a broader crude-and-NGL mix.
- The packet is useful because it shows how midstream economics can change without a refinery or upstream reserve base changing hands. Contract structure, pipeline expansions, rate resets, synergy capture, debt reduction, and asset mix all materially change the earnings profile.
- AnnualReports lagged at `2024`, but the official IR site was clean. This is a strong example of why the archive should rely on company IR and SEC for the actual annual-and-quarter packet even when AnnualReports cleanly confirms taxonomy.

## Open questions

- How durable is Plains' crude-focused uplift once the one-time Canadian NGL sale gain is stripped out and Permian long-haul pipeline rate resets continue to pressure parts of the system?
- Does the cleaner pure-play crude narrative improve valuation quality, or does it also reduce the diversification cushion that the Canadian NGL business previously provided?
- How much future growth depends on physical expansions like the `75 Mbbl/d` Cactus III expansion and Permian gathering additions versus simple macro support from a constructive crude market?
