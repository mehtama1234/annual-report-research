## Annual report takeaways

- Targa is one of the clearest packets in the branch for the idea that midstream should not be reduced to a simple long-haul pipeline toll road. Its `2025` annual filing describes an integrated system spanning natural-gas gathering and processing, crude gathering and terminaling, NGL transportation, fractionation, marketing, and LPG-export-adjacent services.
- The business structure matters. Targa operates in two primary segments: `Gathering and Processing` and `Logistics and Transportation`, with the latter explicitly framed as the downstream business. That makes it a more growth-heavy NGL-and-export platform than a narrower gas-transmission name.
- Geography and infrastructure concentration also matter more here than the headline revenue line. The `2025` filing ties the upstream side to the `Permian`, `Eagle Ford`, `Barnett`, `Anadarko`, `Ardmore`, `Arkoma`, `SCOOP`, `STACK`, `South Central Kansas`, `Williston`, and `Louisiana Gulf Coast`, while the downstream side is concentrated around `Mont Belvieu`, `Galena Park`, and `Lake Charles`.
- Management's strategic posture at the end of `2025` was unusually expansionary. Full-year `2025` adjusted EBITDA was a record `$4.957B`, fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA was a record `$1.341B`, and the company entered `2026` expecting `$5.4B` to `$5.6B` of adjusted EBITDA with about `$4.5B` of net growth capital. The branch should read that as a buildout machine, not a mature yield-only midstream story.
- The strongest annual thematic point is that Targa's economics are tied to physical-system density. The company needs Permian volume growth, plant additions, fractionation capacity, pipeline connectivity, and export throughput to all line up. This is fee-based infrastructure, but it is still operationally exposed to outages, weather, project timing, and producer behavior in a way that a slower-growth toll-road name may not be.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income attributable to Targa Resources Corp. was `$765M` versus `$629M` in `Q2 2025`, and adjusted EBITDA was a record `$1.603B` versus `$1.163B` a year earlier.
- Cash flow and capital allocation: Targa kept the quarterly common dividend at `$1.25` per share, or `$5.00` annualized, with cash dividends of roughly `$268M` to be paid in August. During the quarter it repurchased `308,102` shares at a weighted average price of `$259.93` for a net cost of `$80M`, leaving `$1.239B` under the repurchase programs at `2026-06-30`.
- Balance sheet and liquidity: total consolidated debt was `$19.578B` net of issuance costs and discount, with `$17.9B` of senior unsecured notes, `$600M` under commercial paper, `$451M` under the securitization facility, and `$794M` of finance lease liabilities. Consolidated liquidity was approximately `$3.2B`.
- Volumes and operating system: management reported record Permian inlet, NGL transportation, fractionation, and LPG export volumes. The quarter also included the start-up of `Train 11`, the `Delaware Express` NGL Pipeline expansion, and the `East Driver` processing plant.
- Guidance and growth program: Targa said full-year `2026` adjusted EBITDA should land toward the top end of the `$5.7B` to `$5.9B` range while keeping net growth capital at roughly `$4.5B`.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` is the cleanest evidence that Targa is monetizing a linked NGL system rather than one standalone asset class. Record Permian inlet volumes fed record NGL transportation, fractionation, and LPG export volumes, and project completions directly changed the earnings base inside the quarter.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income attributable to Targa Resources Corp. was `$480M` versus `$271M` in `Q1 2025`, and adjusted EBITDA was a record `$1.403B` versus `$1.179B` a year earlier.
- Cash flow and capital allocation: the quarterly common dividend rose to `$1.25` per share, or `$5.00` annualized, a `25%` increase over the prior-year quarter. Targa repurchased `227,801` shares at an average of `$241.43` for a net cost of `$55M`, leaving `$1.319B` under the repurchase programs at `2026-03-31`.
- Balance sheet and liquidity: total consolidated debt was `$19.132B`, and consolidated liquidity was about `$3.1B`. Targa also completed a March `2026` notes offering of `$750M` due `2031` and `$750M` due `2056`.
- Volumes and operating system: the company highlighted record Permian inlet volumes and record fractionation volumes. Sequentially, adjusted EBITDA rose `5%` over `Q4 2025`, driven by acquired Permian assets, Falcon II start-up, strong producer activity, and higher marketing margin, even with winter weather, producer curtailments, and an export-facility outage late in the quarter.
- Guidance and growth program: management increased full-year `2026` adjusted EBITDA guidance to `$5.7B` to `$5.9B` and continued to expect approximately `$4.5B` of net growth capital.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` showed that Targa's fee-based framing does not make it immune to operating friction. Weather, negative `Waha` pricing, producer curtailments, and an unplanned LPG-export outage all mattered, but the breadth of the system still pushed earnings higher because more projects and more volumes were entering the network.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net income attributable to Targa Resources Corp. was `$545M` versus `$351M` in `Q4 2024`. Full-year net income attributable was `$1.923B`. Fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA was a record `$1.341B` versus `$1.122B` a year earlier, and full-year adjusted EBITDA was a record `$4.957B`.
- Cash flow and capital allocation: Targa paid a `Q4 2025` dividend of `$1.00` per share, or `$4.00` annualized, and said it expected to recommend an annual common dividend of `$5.00` per share for `2026`. In the quarter it repurchased `226,987` shares for `$37M`, while full-year `2025` repurchases totaled `$642M`.
- Volumes and operating system: management described record full-year `2025` Permian, NGL transportation, fractionation, and LPG export volumes, and record `Q4 2025` Permian, NGL transportation, and fractionation volumes.
- Guidance and growth program: Targa began `2026` expecting adjusted EBITDA of `$5.4B` to `$5.6B`, net growth capital of about `$4.5B`, and maintenance capital of about `$250M`. It also described a deep project list that included `Falcon II`, `East Pembrook`, `East Driver`, `Copperhead`, `Yeti I`, `Yeti II`, `Train 11`, `Train 12`, `Train 13`, `Speedway`, `Delaware Express`, and the `GPMT` LPG-export expansion.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` made the capital-intensity story explicit. Targa was already converting record volumes into record EBITDA, but management still leaned harder into fractionators, pipelines, processing plants, and export infrastructure. This is a growth-heavy midstream system where scale comes from continuous physical expansion, not just tariff escalation on a static asset base.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Targa adds the high-growth NGL midstream and export-complex layer, which is distinct from both a pure natural-gas transmission system and a broader all-molecules network.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is indirect, but the packet still maps to larger demand systems through LPG exports, petrochemical feedstock flows, industrial energy use, and the need to move Permian-associated hydrocarbons to domestic and international buyers.
- Cultural: the broader social signal is that energy abundance only becomes economically useful after a large physical processing and transport machine is built around it. Targa is part of that machine.
- Regulatory: Targa is exposed to environmental and safety rules, plant and pipeline permitting, Gulf Coast export infrastructure approvals, and broader hydrocarbon policy and trade uncertainty.
- Labor: labor shows up through construction execution, plant start-ups, maintenance, field operations, export-terminal operations, and the engineering intensity of rapid network expansion.
- Technology: technology matters through processing efficiency, fractionation throughput, pipeline and export optimization, and the ability to stage multiple linked projects on time. This is an operational technology story, not a software-first story.
- Capital allocation: Targa is useful as a packet where rising dividends and buybacks coexist with a very large growth-capital queue. The branch should treat it as a capital-allocation balancing act rather than a simple throughput story.
- Infrastructure dependence: this is one of the strongest packets in the archive for the idea that U.S. hydrocarbon growth depends on fractionation hubs, export docks, residue-gas pipes, NGL pipes, and processing plants, not just drilling activity.
