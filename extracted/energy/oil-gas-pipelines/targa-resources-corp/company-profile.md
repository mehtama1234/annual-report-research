## What changed

- Targa is a stronger next midstream packet than another pure gas transmission name because it pushes the lane deeper into the NGL and Permian buildout system. The company is not just a toll-road pipeline operator. It combines gathering, processing, fractionation, NGL transportation, LPG export, and downstream hubs in `Mont Belvieu`, `Galena Park`, and `Lake Charles`.
- The `2025` filing showed the operating structure clearly. Targa described itself as a leading midstream-services provider and one of the largest independent infrastructure companies in North America. It operates through two primary segments: `Gathering and Processing` and `Logistics and Transportation`, with the latter functioning as the downstream business.
- The quarter chain showed acceleration through `2026`. Adjusted EBITDA moved from `$1.341B` in `Q4 2025` to `$1.403B` in `Q1 2026` and then to `$1.603B` in `Q2 2026`, while management repeatedly highlighted record Permian inlet, fractionation, pipeline transportation, and LPG export volumes.
- The source chain also sharpened an archive lesson. AnnualReports was only usable here as a legacy archive hint, and the attempted annual-reports route on the company IR site returned a `404` page. The clean evidence chain came from the SEC `10-K`, `10-Q`, and `8-K` materials plus company-hosted quarterly releases and supplements.

## Open questions

- How much of the recent upside is durable fee-based infrastructure growth versus unusually strong marketing and optimization margins inside the downstream and export complex?
- Can Targa keep funding a roughly `$4.5B` net growth-capital program without stretching leverage if Permian growth pauses, producer curtailments recur, or LPG export conditions soften?
- Does Targa's economics become more stable as new fractionators, pipeline expansions, and export projects enter service, or does the company remain more operationally sensitive than slower-growth midstream peers because it is building so aggressively?
