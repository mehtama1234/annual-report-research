## Annual report takeaways

- TC Energy is one of the more useful missing flagship packets for the gas-backbone side of North American energy infrastructure. The key change is structural: after the `October 1, 2024` spinoff of the Liquids Pipelines business into `South Bow Corporation`, TC Energy is no longer best read as a mixed liquids-and-gas operator. It is now a more focused continuing-operations system built around `Canadian Natural Gas Pipelines`, `U.S. Natural Gas Pipelines`, `Mexico Natural Gas Pipelines`, and `Power and Energy Solutions`.
- The `2025` annual-close results show a large and still-expanding core system. In `Q4 2025`, TC Energy reported comparable earnings of `C$1.0B` or `C$0.98` per common share, net income attributable to common shares of `C$1.0B` or `C$0.92` per common share, comparable EBITDA of `C$3.0B`, and segmented earnings of `C$2.2B`. For full-year `2025`, comparable EBITDA reached `C$11.0B`, up from `C$10.0B` in `2024`.
- The annual-close narrative is especially strong on operating relevance. Management highlighted record delivery volumes across the gas system, a `26th` consecutive year of dividend growth, and a plan to fully allocate about `C$6B` of annual net capital expenditures through `2030`. That is a useful packet because it shows a large midstream name that is still in expansion mode even after a major portfolio separation.
- The more important read is thematic rather than numerical. TC Energy is not mainly a crude-toll-road company and not a utility-distribution company either. It sits in the middle: a continental gas transportation and contracted power corridor whose fortunes depend on LNG demand, gas-fired generation, cross-border flows, in-corridor expansions, and asset reliability.
- This packet also sharpens the internal structure of the pipeline lane. Enbridge is diversified across liquids, gas transmission, local distribution, and renewables. Plains is a more focused crude corridor. MPLX is an integrated gas-and-NGL buildout. TC Energy is the major post-liquids spinoff gas backbone.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` comparable earnings were `C$984M`, or `C$0.94` per common share, versus `C$848M`, or `C$0.82` per common share, in `Q2 2025`. Net income attributable to common shares was `C$1.0B`, or `C$0.95` per common share, versus `C$0.9B`, or `C$0.83` per common share, in `Q2 2025`.
- Earnings power read: comparable EBITDA was `C$2.948B`, up from `C$2.625B` a year earlier, while segmented earnings rose to `C$2.173B` from `C$1.954B`.
- Capital and outlook read: management said it now expected to finish `2026` at the upper end of its `C$11.6B` to `C$11.8B` comparable EBITDA range. Over the first half of `2026`, it had sanctioned about `C$3B` of new growth projects across the North American natural-gas portfolio.
- Operating read: `Q2 2026` showed why TC Energy belongs in this lane. `U.S. Natural Gas Pipelines` average flows rose to `27.0 Bcf/d`, LNG facility deliveries averaged `3.9 Bcf/d`, and Mexico flows averaged `3.4 Bcf/d`. `Bruce Power` availability reached `98.5%`.
- Structural read: this quarter reinforces TC Energy as a gas-demand and infrastructure-throughput packet rather than a broad multi-molecule system. The growth emphasis is on in-corridor expansions, LNG connectivity, and dependable gas and power infrastructure.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` comparable earnings were `C$1.031B`, or `C$0.99` per common share, compared with `C$983M`, or `C$0.95` per common share, in `Q1 2025`. Net income attributable to common shares was `C$899M`, or `C$0.86` per common share, versus `C$978M`, or `C$0.94` per common share, in `Q1 2025`.
- Earnings power read: comparable EBITDA was `C$3.088B`, up from `C$2.709B`, while segmented earnings rose to `C$2.171B` from `C$1.966B`.
- Capital and project read: the major project signal was the `US$1.5B` Appalachia Supply Project on the Columbia Gas system, described as a low-risk strategic expansion with capacity of up to `0.8 Bcf/d`. That is exactly the kind of in-corridor expansion that makes TC Energy a transport-bottleneck and demand-linkage packet.
- Operating read: `Canadian Natural Gas Pipelines` deliveries averaged `29.7 Bcf/d`, `U.S. Natural Gas Pipelines` flows averaged `32.6 Bcf/d`, and LNG deliveries averaged `3.9 Bcf/d`. Several systems set delivery records, reinforcing the read that the backbone is already heavily used before the next growth leg.
- Strategic read: `Q1 2026` is the best quarter for understanding TC Energy's post-`South Bow` identity. The company is presenting itself as a disciplined gas-and-power infrastructure allocator with attractive repeatable expansion opportunities rather than a broad integrated energy-transport portfolio.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` comparable earnings were `C$1.0B`, or `C$0.98` per common share, compared with `C$1.1B`, or `C$1.05` per common share, in `Q4 2024`. Net income attributable to common shares was `C$1.0B`, or `C$0.92` per common share, versus `C$1.1B`, or `C$1.03` per common share, in `Q4 2024`.
- Earnings power read: `Q4 2025` comparable EBITDA was `C$3.0B`, up from `C$2.6B`, while segmented earnings reached `C$2.2B` versus `C$1.9B`. Full-year `2025` comparable EBITDA reached `C$11.0B` versus `C$10.0B` in `2024`.
- Allocation read: the board approved a `3.2%` increase in the quarterly common share dividend, marking the `26th` consecutive year of dividend growth. Management also said `2026` capital expenditures were expected at `C$6.0B` to `C$6.5B` before adjustments for non-controlling interests.
- Operating read: the annual-close materials emphasized that `Canadian Natural Gas Pipelines` deliveries averaged `27.2 Bcf/d`, `U.S. Natural Gas Pipelines` flows averaged `29.6 Bcf/d`, and LNG deliveries averaged `3.9 Bcf/d`, while multiple systems set all-time flow records.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is where the post-spinoff thesis becomes fully visible. Without liquids pipelines in the main earnings mix, TC Energy reads as a continental gas-flow and contracted-power platform whose core job is moving molecules to power plants, LNG facilities, and end-use demand centers.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: TC Energy adds the post-liquids-spinoff gas-backbone version of the pipeline lane, which is distinct from crude-heavy corridors, local gas utilities, and NGL-growth systems.
- Consumer: end demand is indirect, but this packet still matters for home heating, power reliability, LNG-linked supply chains, industrial demand, and the delivery layer behind both residential and commercial energy use.
- Cultural and societal: TC Energy is useful for the energy-security and infrastructure-reliability lens because the company’s relevance comes from dependable gas and power delivery rather than from direct commodity ownership.
- Industrial: this is one of the clearer packets for showing how North American gas demand is turning into a transport-and-expansion story:
  - LNG demand growth
  - gas-fired power demand
  - Appalachian and Southeast corridor expansion
  - Mexico gas delivery
  - nuclear and cogeneration availability
- Commodity exposure: TC Energy is less exposed to commodity-price swings than upstream producers, but it still depends on throughput, demand growth, regulatory outcomes, project sanctions, and the continued attractiveness of gas-fired and LNG-linked infrastructure.
- Capital intensity: this packet is useful because it combines high asset reliability with a visible sanctioned growth queue and a dividend-growth culture. It is a good example of a large infrastructure company still leaning into expansion rather than simply harvesting cash.
- Transport bottlenecks: the recurring delivery records, LNG facility flows, and projects like Appalachia Supply show why gas infrastructure constraints matter as much as production growth. Supply can exist, but without takeaway and contracted delivery capacity, the system cannot fully convert that supply into earnings.
- Infrastructure dependence: TC Energy is a clean proof that the North American gas system has its own critical delivery layer separate from crude pipelines, local gas utilities, or upstream drilling programs.
- Strategic differentiation: the `South Bow` spinoff matters analytically. It sharpened TC Energy into a purer gas-and-power infrastructure franchise, which makes comparison against Enbridge, Williams, and Energy Transfer more precise.
