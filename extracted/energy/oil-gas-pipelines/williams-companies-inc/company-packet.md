# Williams Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Sector: Energy
- Industry: Oil & Gas Midstream

- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Williams is one of the cleanest examples in the branch of how natural-gas infrastructure has moved beyond the simple "pipeline toll road" frame. The business still owns major regulated and contracted transport assets, but the filing set makes it clear that the real system now includes `Transco`, gathering and processing, Gulf Coast and deepwater connectivity, storage, Haynesville and Marcellus exposure, and an emerging power-infrastructure platform.
- The `2025` annual-close package showed a company entering a new growth cycle with strong historical momentum. Full-year `2025` GAAP net income reached `$2.615B`, adjusted net income reached `$2.571B`, adjusted EBITDA reached a record `$7.750B`, cash flow from operations reached `$5.898B`, and AFFO reached `$5.858B`.
- The annual chain also showed that Williams is becoming more explicitly natural-gas-forward. Management framed the company as delivering roughly one-third of U.S. natural gas while tying future growth to domestic demand, LNG exports, and the changing power-generation landscape.
- The segment structure matters. The `2025` `10-K` and `2026` `10-Q`s define `Transmission, Power & Gulf` as the largest strategic layer, combining interstate pipelines and storage with Gulf Coast gathering and processing and the newer power-innovation projects. That is a different configuration from both `Kinder Morgan` and `Enterprise Products Partners`.
- The quarter-zero setup for `2026` already pointed to a larger ambition set: raised dividend, a larger transmission-project queue, more than `$7B` of power-innovation capital in execution at the analyst-day stage, and a gas demand thesis tied not only to LNG but also to data centers, industrialization, and power reliability.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` GAAP net income was `$827M`, or `$0.68` per diluted share, adjusted net income was `$614M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$1.921B`, and AFFO was `$1.450B`.
- Coverage and leverage: dividend coverage was `2.26x` on an AFFO basis, and debt-to-adjusted EBITDA at quarter-end was about `3.67x`.
- Guidance and capital allocation: management raised the `2026` adjusted EBITDA midpoint by `$200M` to `$8.4B`, while using the `Momentum Midstream` acquisition and the `Blackstone` power-innovation joint venture to support a higher long-term growth path.
- Operating system: the quarter featured completed phase one of `Socrates`, commercial progress on `Transco` projects including `Leidy Access`, `Garden Connector`, and a larger `Power Express`, plus a stronger Haynesville position through `Momentum Midstream`.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` makes Williams the branch's clearest LNG-plus-power packet. The company is still a natural-gas infrastructure operator, but the economic engine is increasingly tied to serving Gulf Coast export pull and fast-growing power demand with both pipelines and behind-the-meter solutions.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` GAAP net income was `$864M`, or `$0.70` per diluted share, adjusted EBITDA reached a record `$2.254B`, adjusted EPS was `$0.73`, and AFFO was `$1.770B`.
- Coverage and leverage: AFFO dividend coverage was `2.76x`, debt-to-adjusted EBITDA at quarter-end was about `3.61x`, and first-quarter capital investments were `$1.642B`.
- Operating system: the quarter showed strength in `Transco` higher net rates and expansion projects, new Gulf volumes, higher storage revenues, and stronger gathering in the West. Williams also highlighted `Aristotle` and `Socrates`, `Power Express`, and early project wins across transmission and power innovation.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` is the best evidence that Williams is not merely a legacy interstate-pipeline operator. The company is using the same gas-network footprint to monetize Gulf deepwater volumes, winter storage needs, transmission expansions, and data-center-related power infrastructure.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: full-year `2025` GAAP net income was `$2.615B`, adjusted net income was `$2.571B`, adjusted EBITDA was `$7.750B`, cash flow from operations was `$5.898B`, and AFFO was `$5.858B`.
- Guidance and capital allocation: Williams raised the dividend by `5%` to `$2.10` annualized for `2026`, ended `2025` with `2.40x` dividend coverage on an AFFO basis, and initially guided to `2026` adjusted EBITDA of `$8.05B` to `$8.35B`.
- Growth program: management described `2025` as a year of robust execution, with completed pipeline, gathering, and deepwater projects, new project announcements, the `Rimrock` and `Saber` acquisitions, the Haynesville E&P sale, and larger power-innovation commitments including `Socrates the Younger`.
- Structural themes: the annual close made Williams' broader economic identity very clear. The company is becoming a gas-and-power backbone business rather than only a transport or gathering company. LNG exports, domestic power growth, and industrial reshoring are all now part of the same strategic map.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Williams adds a gas-heavy transmission, gathering, and power-infrastructure layer that sits between a classic long-haul pipe packet and a broader liquids-and-exports midstream packet.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand is still indirect, but the true demand system now includes utility power loads, LNG export pull, industrial customers, Gulf volumes, and data-center-driven electricity demand.
- Cultural: the broader social signal is that the "energy transition" discussion keeps circling back to natural gas because reliability, fast-ramping power, and available infrastructure still matter. Williams is one of the clearest filings-based proofs of that.
- Regulatory: the packet is deeply exposed to FERC, pipeline and storage regulation, integrity spending, project permitting, and the economics of transmission expansions.
- Labor: labor matters through construction, operations, and the ability to execute multiple large projects at once across pipeline and power systems.
- Technology: technology matters both through traditional gas-system optimization and through the newer power-innovation model, where Williams is trying to turn its gas access and infrastructure execution capabilities into a data-center and power-reliability product.
- Capital allocation: this is a key packet for the archive's "legacy asset base plus new growth platform" theme. Williams is simultaneously funding transmission expansions, G&P growth, power infrastructure, and acquisitions while trying to maintain balance-sheet control and shareholder returns.
- Infrastructure dependence: this is one of the strongest packets in the branch for the idea that future U.S. demand growth still depends on old and new gas infrastructure at once: regulated pipes, gathering, storage, LNG corridor access, and new behind-the-meter generation platforms.
