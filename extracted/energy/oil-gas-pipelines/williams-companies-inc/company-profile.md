## What changed

- Williams adds a distinct gas-heavy infrastructure packet rather than a third generic pipeline packet. The business is still midstream, but it is organized around `Transmission, Power & Gulf`, `Northeast G&P`, `West`, and `Gas & NGL Marketing Services`, with `Transco` and power-innovation projects increasingly shaping the forward story.
- The `2025` annual and annual-close materials showed a company leaning hard into natural-gas centrality. Full-year `2025` GAAP net income reached `$2.615B`, adjusted net income reached `$2.571B`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$7.750B`, cash flow from operations reached `$5.898B`, and AFFO reached `$5.858B`.
- The quarter chain then broadened the thesis. `Q1 2026` was a record adjusted EBITDA quarter with growth from `Transco` rates and expansions, new Gulf volumes, storage, and West gathering. `Q2 2026` added the `Socrates` power project, the `Blackstone` power-innovation joint venture, the `Momentum Midstream` acquisition, and a raised `2026` EBITDA midpoint.
- This makes Williams one of the branch's clearest bridges between traditional natural-gas infrastructure and the new demand stack tied to LNG exports, power reliability, industrial reshoring, and data-center electricity load.

## Open questions

- How much of Williams' next leg of growth remains conventional regulated-and-contracted gas infrastructure, and how much becomes contingent on the still-new power-innovation platform?
- Can the company keep leverage controlled while funding larger growth capital, acquisitions, and power projects at the same time?
- Does the Haynesville and Gulf Coast demand corridor become a long-duration advantage large enough to offset any slowdown in older basin or gathering exposures?
