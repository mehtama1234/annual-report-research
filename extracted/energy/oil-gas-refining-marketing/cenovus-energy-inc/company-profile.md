## What changed

- Cenovus adds the acquisition-scaled oil-sands-heavy integrated model rather than another generic Canadian integrated major.
- The `MEG` acquisition and the `WRB` divestiture both matter because they changed the shape of the company at the same time: more upstream oil-sands scale, but a narrower downstream mix than before.
- The packet is useful because it links heavy-barrel production, Canadian and U.S. refining throughput, market-capture economics, debt paydown, and shareholder returns inside one system.
- This makes Cenovus one of the better branch cases for how transport, condensate supply, refining margins, heavy-oil differentials, and refinery utilization all interact inside an integrated commodity business.

## Open questions

- How durable is Cenovus's integrated edge after the `WRB` divestiture if U.S. refining market capture remains uneven?
- Does the next value-creation leg come more from oil-sands scale and reliability, or from restoring stronger downstream utilization and capture after maintenance and portfolio reshaping?
- How much of the bull case depends on continued infrastructure relief for Canadian heavy barrels versus company-specific operational gains at Christina Lake, Foster Creek, Lloydminster, and the U.S. refining system?
