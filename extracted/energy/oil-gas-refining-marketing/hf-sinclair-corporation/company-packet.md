## Annual report takeaways

- HF Sinclair is one of the better downstream packets for showing why "refining and marketing" is still too blunt a label. The company operates a classic refiner's asset base, but it also owns renewable diesel units, branded fuel marketing reach, midstream infrastructure, and a lubricants-and-specialties platform that looks closer to a specialty-industrials business than to a plain crack-spread vehicle.
- The official `2025` annual report and filed `10-K` frame the system clearly. HF Sinclair operates refineries in Kansas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, Wyoming, Washington, and Utah; produces renewable diesel at facilities in Wyoming and New Mexico; supplies high-quality fuels to more than `1,800` branded stations while licensing the Sinclair brand to more than `350` additional locations; and runs a lubricants-and-specialties network across the United States, Canada, and the Netherlands that exports to more than `80` countries.
- The annual report also helps explain why the `Lubricants & Specialties` business matters. It disclosed about `34,000 BPD` of lubricants-and-specialty-product production capacity, a globally distributed product set, and a portfolio that includes white oils, process oils, waxes, food-grade lubricants, industrial lubricants, railroad engine oils, and other specialty products. That mix is materially different from the fuel-heavy downstream stories in `Valero` or `Marathon Petroleum`.
- Full-year `2025` results show a mixed but still productive downstream year. HF Sinclair reported `2025` net income attributable to stockholders of about `\$579M`, adjusted net income of about `\$951M`, reported EBITDA of about `\$1.8B`, adjusted EBITDA of about `\$2.3B`, and about `\$724M` returned to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases.
- The annual-close release is especially useful because management said `2025` delivered record earnings in Midstream and Marketing and the company’s lowest annual refining operating expense per barrel, even though `Q4 2025` itself was pressured by seasonal crack weakness, the Puget Sound turnaround, and the unplanned Artesia event. That gives the packet a different read from a pure "big quarter / bad quarter" refiner story.
- There is one accounting-process nuance to keep explicit. HF Sinclair’s `2026-02-18` fourth-quarter release said the audit of `2025` financial statements was not yet complete because the Audit Committee was assessing certain disclosure-process matters, but it also said those matters did not affect the announced `Q4 2025` or full-year `2025` results. The filed `10-K` followed on `2026-02-27`, so the annual chain is complete and authoritative.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income attributable to HF Sinclair stockholders was `\$892M`, diluted EPS was `\$4.93`, and adjusted net income attributable to stockholders was `\$960M`, or adjusted EPS of `\$5.31`.
- Cash flow and balance sheet: reported EBITDA was `\$1.404B`, adjusted EBITDA was `\$1.482B`, net cash provided by operations was `\$1.510B`, cash and cash equivalents ended the quarter at `\$2.262B`, and consolidated debt was `\$2.772B`. HF Sinclair returned `\$265M` to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases and raised the quarterly dividend `5%` to `\$0.525`.
- Operating system: refining segment income before interest and income taxes was `\$877M` and adjusted EBITDA was `\$1.023B`; adjusted refinery gross margin reached `\$25.95` per produced barrel sold; and crude oil charge averaged `639,680 BPD`. Renewables segment income before interest and income taxes was `\$30M` and adjusted EBITDA was `\$123M`, with volumes of `60M` gallons. Marketing income before interest and income taxes was `\$20M`, with branded sales volumes of `387M` gallons. Lubricants & Specialties income before interest and income taxes was `\$181M` and adjusted EBITDA was `\$207M`. Midstream income before interest and income taxes was `\$95M` with adjusted EBITDA of `\$112M`.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is not just a strong refining quarter. It is a broad-based downstream earnings quarter where refining, renewables, lubricants, marketing, and midstream all worked at once. That breadth is the reason HF Sinclair deserves its own packet instead of being treated as a smaller substitute for the larger refiners.
- Strategic signal: the quarter also brought the biggest thematic development in the packet. HF Sinclair announced plans to pursue a separation of `Lubricants & Specialties` through the capital markets and retire the Mississauga base-oil refining assets over `2027`. That turns the company into a live test of whether higher-value specialty downstream assets should remain inside a refining complex or be valued on their own.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income attributable to HF Sinclair stockholders was `\$648M`, diluted EPS was `\$3.56`, and adjusted net income attributable to stockholders was `\$127M`, or adjusted EPS of `\$0.69`.
- Cash flow and balance sheet: reported EBITDA was `\$1.097B`, adjusted EBITDA was `\$426M`, net cash provided by operations was `\$457M`, cash and cash equivalents were `\$1.148B`, and consolidated debt was `\$2.771B`. HF Sinclair returned `\$167M` to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases.
- Operating system: refining segment income before interest and income taxes was `\$514M`, though adjusted EBITDA was only `\$55M` once a `\$604M` lower-of-cost-or-market inventory valuation benefit is excluded. Adjusted refinery gross margin was `\$9.95` per produced barrel sold and crude oil charge averaged `613,050 BPD`. Renewables income before interest and income taxes was `\$182M` and adjusted EBITDA was `\$133M`, with total sales volumes of `52M` gallons and `\$49M` of prior-year PTC benefits recognized after the February `2026` proposed Treasury / IRS ruling. Marketing income before interest and income taxes was `\$20M`, with branded volumes of `325M` gallons. Lubricants & Specialties income before interest and income taxes was `\$78M` and adjusted EBITDA was `\$103M`. Midstream income before interest and income taxes was `\$94M`.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is the quarter that shows why the packet needs interpretation instead of just number gathering. Reported results were flattered by inventory and tax-credit effects, but the broader story is that HF Sinclair still had multiple non-refining earnings supports even before the much stronger `Q2` margin environment arrived.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net loss attributable to HF Sinclair stockholders was `\$28M`, or `\$(0.16)` per diluted share, while adjusted net income attributable to stockholders was `\$221M`, or `\$1.20` per diluted share.
- Cash flow and balance sheet: reported EBITDA was `\$235M`, adjusted EBITDA was `\$564M`, net cash provided by operations totaled only `\$8M`, cash and cash equivalents ended the quarter at `\$978M`, and consolidated debt was `\$2.769B`. HF Sinclair returned `\$230M` to stockholders in the quarter and `\$724M` over full-year `2025`.
- Operating system: refining segment loss before interest and income taxes was `\$49M`, but adjusted EBITDA was `\$403M`; adjusted refinery gross margin was `\$16.28` per produced barrel sold; and crude oil charge averaged `556,460 BPD`. Renewables segment loss before interest and income taxes was `\$35M`, with adjusted EBITDA of `\$(6)M` and total sales volumes of `57M` gallons. Marketing income before interest and income taxes was `\$14M`, with branded fuel volumes of `337M` gallons. Lubricants & Specialties income before interest and income taxes was `\$19M` and EBITDA was `\$43M`. Midstream income before interest and income taxes was `\$96M` and EBITDA was `\$114M`.
- Strategic context: management explicitly attributed the weak quarter to seasonal crack softness, the Puget Sound turnaround, and the unplanned Artesia event, while still emphasizing record `2025` Marketing and Midstream earnings and the company’s lowest annual refining operating expense per barrel.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the best quarter for seeing how HF Sinclair differs from the bigger refiners. When refining cracks are weak, the story still includes store mix, branded gallons, specialty products, renewables policy economics, and midstream cash generation instead of only refinery utilization and fuel margins.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: HF Sinclair adds the inland-and-specialty downstream layer, not just another large integrated refiner.
- Consumer: this packet still touches consumer fuel demand directly through branded station volumes and retail-oriented gasoline distribution, unlike purely wholesale or export-led refiners.
- Cultural and societal: the bigger signal is that parts of downstream energy infrastructure are trying to separate higher-value specialty businesses from lower-multiple commodity refining so capital markets can assign different valuations and strategic priorities.
- Industrial: the business is shaped by crack spreads, turnaround timing, refinery gross margins, feedstock and RIN economics, and also by specialty-product mix, branded retail distribution, and logistics throughput.
- Commodity and spread exposure: refining results still swing with crack spreads and crude-product dislocations, but renewables credits, branded gallons, specialty-product pricing, and midstream volumes all change how that exposure reaches earnings.
- Capital allocation: the company maintained dividends and repurchases through the whole window, but the more important strategic capital question now is whether `Lubricants & Specialties` should stay inside the downstream complex.
- Infrastructure dependence: HF Sinclair reinforces the point that refining economics depend on transport and terminal control, not just refinery hardware. Midstream assets, joint ventures, and branded distribution are part of the earnings system.
- Comparison value: compared with `Valero`, HF Sinclair is less about sheer refining scale and low-carbon-fuels scale; compared with `Marathon Petroleum`, it is less about giant-system throughput and MPLX-supported capital returns; compared with `Phillips 66`, it is less about broad integrated downstream reconfiguration and more about whether a specialty lubricants platform belongs outside the refiner.
