## Annual report takeaways

- Imperial is one of the clearest missing packets for the Canada-centered integrated refining and fuels-distribution layer. AnnualReports still labels it as `Major Integrated Oil & Gas`, but the actual archive value on this branch is more specific: Imperial is Canada's largest petroleum refiner, a leading fuels marketer, a major crude producer, and a petrochemical manufacturer whose system ties Kearl, Cold Lake, Syncrude, refining, branded wholesale distribution, and lower-emissions fuels together.
- The `2025` annual report and `10-K` show a large but disciplined integrated system. Revenue was `C$46.918B`, net income was `C$3.268B`, and cash flow from operating activities was `C$6.708B`. Annual production averaged `438,000` gross oil-equivalent barrels per day, refinery throughput averaged `402,000` barrels per day, and petroleum product sales averaged `470,000` barrels per day. Year-end proved reserves were `2.036B` oil-equivalent barrels.
- The physical footprint matters because it is unusually concentrated and coherent. Imperial's `2025` report describes Kearl, Cold Lake, and Syncrude on the upstream side; three Canadian refineries with aggregate rated capacity of `434,000` barrels per day on the downstream side; a nationwide distribution system; about `2,600` branded wholesaler sites under `Esso` and `Mobil`; and adjacent petrochemical production in Sarnia.
- The annual report also makes the intended economics explicit. Imperial says its integrated business model generally reduces risk from changes in commodity prices. That is not just generic integrated-major language. In practice, it means upstream bitumen and synthetic-crude output can be pulled into a domestic refining and fuels-marketing system rather than being monetized as a pure commodity bet.
- `2025` also showed why Imperial is not just another legacy refiner. The year included the start-up of Canada's largest renewable diesel facility at Strathcona, first oil at the Leming SAGD project at Cold Lake, record annual crude production, and continuing restructuring to lower unit cash costs and centralize work through global capability centres.
- The useful difference from `Suncor` is that Imperial reads as the more Canada-focused refining-and-marketing expression of integration. `Suncor` is a broader extraction, upgrading, trading, and Petro-Canada retail packet. Imperial is tighter around Kearl, Cold Lake, Syncrude, three refineries, Esso and Mobil branded wholesale, petrochemicals, and an Exxon-linked operating system.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income was `C$2.190B`, or `C$4.52` per diluted share, on revenue of `C$16.062B`. Cash flow from operating activities was `C$2.704B`, and six-month net income reached `C$3.130B`.
- Operating read: quarterly upstream production averaged `414,000` gross oil-equivalent barrels per day. Kearl gross production averaged `257,000` barrels per day, or `182,000` barrels per day on Imperial's share. Cold Lake averaged `149,000` barrels per day, and Syncrude averaged `73,000` barrels per day on Imperial's share.
- Downstream read: refinery throughput was only `331,000` barrels per day, capacity utilization was `76%`, and petroleum product sales were `446,000` barrels per day. Management attributed the weaker throughput mainly to planned turnaround work and a short-term rail logistics challenge at Strathcona.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is a useful stress test for the packet. Even with a maintenance-heavy downstream quarter, Imperial still produced very strong earnings because higher crude realizations and stronger refining margins more than offset lower volumes. This shows that the integrated model still has material commodity leverage, but that leverage is shaped by where along the chain the operational bottleneck appears.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income was `C$940M`, or `C$1.94` per diluted share, on revenue of `C$12.446B`. Cash flow from operating activities was `C$756M`.
- Operating read: upstream production averaged `419,000` gross oil-equivalent barrels per day. Kearl gross production averaged `259,000` barrels per day, or `183,000` barrels per day on Imperial's share. Cold Lake averaged `155,000` barrels per day and Syncrude averaged `72,000` barrels per day on Imperial's share.
- Downstream read: refinery throughput averaged `384,000` barrels per day, refinery utilization was `88%`, and petroleum product sales were `441,000` barrels per day. Management tied the weaker downstream quarter to unplanned downtime and disruption of synthetic crude feedstock caused by the Syncrude coker outage.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` shows the other side of the integration story. Imperial is not just hedged by owning multiple assets. It is also exposed to internal linkage risk. When Syncrude has trouble, downstream feedstock conditions and refinery performance can also tighten. The same integration that reduces external commodity risk can increase dependence on internal operating reliability.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net income was `C$492M`, or `C$1.00` per diluted share, while net income excluding identified items was `C$968M`. Cash flow from operating activities was `C$1.918B`.
- Annual-close read: full-year `2025` net income was `C$3.268B`, net income excluding identified items was `C$4.299B`, and cash flow from operating activities was `C$6.708B`.
- Operating read: quarterly upstream production averaged `444,000` gross oil-equivalent barrels per day, the highest annual production in over `30` years was `438,000` gross oil-equivalent barrels per day, refinery throughput averaged `408,000` barrels per day, capacity utilization was `94%`, and petroleum product sales were `479,000` barrels per day.
- Strategic read: `Q4 2025` also closed the year with a visible shareholder-return machine. Imperial returned `C$2.072B` to shareholders in the quarter, including `C$361M` in dividends and `C$1.711B` in share repurchases, and raised the quarterly dividend by `20%` to `C$0.87` per share.
- Structural read: this is the quarter that makes the Imperial packet work. It ties high oil-sands production, national refining scale, strong retail and supply-channel sales, renewable diesel start-up, and large cash returns into one integrated Canadian energy-delivery system.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Imperial adds the Canada-centered integrated refining and fuels-marketing system rather than another U.S. pure refiner or another pure upstream producer.
- Consumer: consumer demand is not direct in the discretionary sense, but it is more visible than in upstream packets because Imperial reaches into everyday transport-fuel demand through Esso and Mobil branded wholesale distribution, industrial sales, and national supply channels.
- Cultural and societal: this is one of the better packets for understanding how hydrocarbons remain embedded in daily mobility and commercial life. The company is physically inside the delivery layer behind a large share of Canadian transport fuels rather than only inside upstream extraction economics.
- Industrial: Imperial is useful because its earnings depend on several linked physical systems:
  - oil sands mining and bitumen output at Kearl
  - in-situ production at Cold Lake
  - synthetic crude from Syncrude
  - three Canadian refineries
  - nationwide fuel distribution and branded wholesale
  - petrochemical manufacturing in Sarnia
  - renewable diesel expansion at Strathcona
- Commodity exposure: Imperial still has large oil-price and heavy-light differential exposure, but the packet is more informative as an integrated heavy-barrel conversion and domestic-fuels-delivery story than as a pure crude-beta story.
- Capital intensity: Imperial is a strong branch case for long-cycle oil sands investment paired with large fixed downstream assets. The value comes from sustained reliability, margin capture, and distribution scale rather than from a single high-growth project narrative.
- Upstream and downstream cycles: Imperial's own annual report says the integrated model generally reduces risk from commodity-price changes. The quarters show that this is partly true, but they also show a second-order issue: integration lowers some external price risk while increasing dependence on internal system coordination.
- Refining margins: Imperial is especially useful because its downstream segment is not just about refining margins in isolation. It is about how those margins interact with Canadian feedstock position, synthetic crude availability, domestic distribution, and branded-site economics.
- Infrastructure dependence: Imperial is a clean archive case for domestic energy infrastructure. It depends on pipelines, refinery uptime, storage and terminal networks, rail logistics, branded wholesale density, and continued market access improvements such as tighter heavy-crude differentials after TMX expansion.
