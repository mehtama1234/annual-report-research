## What changed

- Imperial adds a more explicitly Canada-centered integrated refining and fuels-marketing packet than the U.S. refiners already on the branch.
- The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows a company where Kearl, Cold Lake, and Syncrude volumes matter, but the distinctive economic layer comes from being Canada's largest petroleum refiner, a leading national fuels marketer, and a petrochemical producer with a large branded wholesale network.
- This makes the packet especially useful for the bigger-picture lens around refining margins, domestic transport-fuel distribution, branded wholesale density, Canadian heavy-barrel logistics, renewable diesel buildout, and how integrated downstream positioning can cushion uneven upstream quarters.
- The annual report also states directly that Imperial's integrated business model generally reduces risk from changes in commodity prices, which makes it a strong contrast case next to both pure refiners and pure upstream producers.

## Open questions

- How durable is Imperial's advantage as a Canada-centered integrated refiner and marketer if refinery downtime or synthetic crude feedstock disruption becomes more frequent?
- Does the main edge come from owning upstream barrels plus refineries, or from controlling a larger domestic fuels-distribution system with branded wholesale density and scale economics?
- How much of the next margin story is still conventional refining, and how much shifts toward the Strathcona renewable diesel complex and lower-emissions fuel positioning?
