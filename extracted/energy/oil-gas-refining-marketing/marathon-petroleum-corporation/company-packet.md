## Annual report takeaways

- Marathon Petroleum is the clearest refining-and-marketing candidate for this batch because the `2025` annual report framing is explicitly about disciplined planning, operational rigor, commercial execution, and the strength of integrated value chains rather than pure commodity extraction.
- The company is useful as a downstream and infrastructure packet, not just as a refining earnings story. The important system is the combination of refining assets, logistics dependence, MPLX cash support, and capital-return discipline.
- Full-year `2025` net income attributable to MPC was `$4.0 billion`, adjusted net income was `$3.3 billion`, cash provided by operating activities was `$8.3 billion`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$12.0 billion`.
- Full-year `2025` refining utilization was `94%` and margin capture was `105%`, which makes the annual filing set especially useful for studying how execution quality can matter as much as absolute commodity prices in downstream economics.
- Full-year `2025` capital returns were `$4.5 billion`, and management explicitly argued that growing MPLX distributions should more than fund the `2026` dividend and standalone capital, which is a major differentiator versus a simpler stand-alone refiner.
- AnnualReports still lagged at the `2024` package as of `2026-08-10`, so the real authoritative annual-report chain here comes from company IR and SEC.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income attributable to MPC was `$5.1 billion`, diluted EPS was `$17.73`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$8.5 billion`.
- Operations: Refining and Marketing segment adjusted EBITDA rose to `$6.655 billion`, Midstream adjusted EBITDA was `$1.778 billion`, Renewable Diesel adjusted EBITDA was `$258 million`, crude capacity utilization was `94%`, throughput was about `2.9 million barrels per day`, and Refining and Marketing margin reached `$36.33` per barrel.
- Guidance: management kept emphasizing MPLX distribution growth of `12.5%` in `2026` and `2027`, with `2026` capital spending excluding MPLX of about `$1.5 billion`.
- Management message: management highlighted resilient consumer demand, strong commercial and operational execution, and the completion of yield-enhancing refining projects at El Paso and Robinson.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` shows how a refiner can become a cash-gusher when margins, utilization, and logistics all line up at once. The more important read is not only the earnings spike, but the dependence on throughput discipline, asset upgrades, and value-chain coordination.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income attributable to MPC was `$511 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.73`, cash provided by operating activities was `$1.1 billion`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$2.8 billion`.
- Operations: Refining and Marketing segment adjusted EBITDA was `$1.377 billion`, Midstream adjusted EBITDA was `$1.598 billion`, and Renewable Diesel adjusted EBITDA was `$38 million`.
- Guidance: management said roughly `40%` of planned `2026` turnaround activity had been completed and highlighted the Garyville jet project going online, with the El Paso FCC upgrade targeted for `Q2 2026` and the Robinson jet project for `Q3 2026`.
- Management message: management emphasized safe and reliable operations during heavy turnaround activity while still returning `$1.0 billion` of capital and authorizing an incremental `$5 billion` of share repurchases.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` is useful because it shows the lower-margin side of the model. Refining is capital-intensive and operationally scheduled, so maintenance cadence and turnaround load can matter materially even before you get to crude or product pricing.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net income attributable to MPC was `$1.5 billion`, diluted EPS was `$5.12`, adjusted net income was `$1.2 billion`, adjusted EBITDA was `$3.5 billion`, and full-year operating cash flow was `$8.3 billion`.
- Operations: fourth-quarter Refining and Marketing segment adjusted EBITDA was `$1.997 billion`, Midstream adjusted EBITDA was `$1.680 billion`, and Renewable Diesel adjusted EBITDA was `$7 million`.
- Guidance: management said MPLX's growing distribution should more than fund MPC's `2026` dividend and standalone capital, reinforcing that the parent-level capital-return case depends heavily on the midstream support layer.
- Management message: management framed `2025` as a year of strong refining performance and commercial execution, not merely a lucky margin year.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` closes the year by making the archive's downstream lesson very clear. The business is not just selling fuel; it is managing a refining system where utilization, margin capture, midstream integration, and capital-return priorities all have to work together.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Marathon Petroleum adds the downstream, transport-linked, refining-margin, and asset-optimization layer that ConocoPhillips does not cover.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand matters here more than in an upstream packet because end-product pull, especially for transportation fuels, can visibly tighten or loosen refining economics.
- Cultural: the broader social signal is not lifestyle branding but infrastructure dependence. People still consume mobility, freight, and fuel-intensive activity through systems they rarely see directly, and refiners monetize that hidden dependence.
- Regulatory: the packet is sensitive to environmental regulation, renewable fuels policy, permitting, pipeline and terminal reliability, and product-spec compliance across regions.
- Labor: turnaround execution, refinery reliability, and project completion remain labor- and contractor-intensive, even when management language focuses more on operations and safety than on labor markets directly.
- Technology: technology matters through yield-enhancing projects, margin capture, refinery optimization, and value-chain coordination rather than through a software-led story.
- Capital allocation: Marathon Petroleum is one of the stronger examples in the archive of a company whose capital-return case is partially underwritten by affiliated infrastructure cash flows rather than by refining margins alone.
- Infrastructure dependence: this packet is especially useful for the bigger-picture theme mandate because it ties together refining margins, transport bottlenecks, throughput, and the embedded value of pipelines, terminals, and connected logistics.
