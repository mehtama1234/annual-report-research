## What changed

- Earnings power: the packet swings from a moderate but still profitable `Q1 2026` to a very strong `Q2 2026`, showing how large the earnings leverage can be when refining conditions improve.
- Cash flow: full-year `2025` operating cash flow was `$8.3 billion`. `Q1 2026` operating cash flow was only `$1.1 billion` during heavier turnaround activity, then `Q2 2026` converted the stronger margin environment into much larger earnings and capital-return capacity.
- Segment mix: Refining and Marketing is still the earnings torque point, but Midstream provides a stabilizing cash layer and Renewable Diesel adds a smaller, more policy- and execution-sensitive component.
- Capital allocation: management kept the same industry-leading capital-return posture throughout the window, supported by refining cash generation and by the expectation that MPLX distribution growth will help fund the parent.
- Asset intensity: the packet makes clear that this is an infrastructure-and-maintenance-heavy system. Garyville, El Paso, and Robinson project timing all matter because yield enhancement and asset uptime directly affect economics.
- Demand exposure: unlike an upstream producer, Marathon Petroleum is more directly exposed to end-fuel demand, utilization rates, throughput, and product margins, which makes it a better downstream read on transport and industrial activity.
- Management tone: management repeatedly frames the business as an integrated value-chain system with durable competitive advantages, not just a refiner taking price.

## Open questions

- How much of the `Q2 2026` margin and EBITDA surge is cyclical versus structurally improved by the latest project work and commercial execution?
- If consumer fuel demand softens or policy pressure intensifies, how much downside protection does the MPLX-linked support layer really provide at the parent level?
