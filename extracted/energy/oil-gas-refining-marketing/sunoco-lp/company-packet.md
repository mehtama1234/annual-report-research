## Annual report takeaways

- Sunoco is one of the most useful missing downstream packets because it is not just a refining-margin story. The `2025` annual report frames the company as a large fuel-distribution and energy-infrastructure partnership with over `14,000` miles of pipeline, over `160` terminals, and more than `15 billion` gallons of annual fuel distribution to approximately `11,000` branded, partner-branded, dealer, and commercial locations.
- That makes Sunoco distinct from the branch's other refining-and-marketing names. `Valero`, `HF Sinclair`, and `Phillips 66` still read more directly through refinery utilization, crack spreads, and product margins. Sunoco is more about the physical movement, storage, branding, and wholesale placement of fuel, with refining as only one part of the model.
- The `2025` annual report also shows the degree to which acquisitions changed the business. On `October 31, 2025`, Sunoco completed the Parkland acquisition, paying roughly `$2.60B` in cash and transferring `51,517,198` SunocoCorp common units. The annual report explicitly says Parkland is a leading international fuel distributor, marketer, and convenience retailer with operations in `26` countries across the Americas.
- Full-year `2025` results show the scale jump. Net income was `$527M`, consolidated adjusted EBITDA was `$2.047B`, and segment adjusted EBITDA was `$990M` for Fuel Distribution, `$718M` for Pipeline Systems, `$299M` for Terminals, and `$40M` for Refinery. The annual discussion attributes much of the earnings change to the acquisitions of Parkland, NuStar, and Zenith European terminals.
- The packet matters because it gives the branch a clean downstream-distribution interpretation of energy infrastructure dependence. Pipelines, terminals, fuel branding, dealer networks, convenience adjacency, airport and commercial supply, and retail-site control all matter here. This is not just a commodity-processing system. It is a molecule-delivery and margin-capture system.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income was `$283M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$982M`, and distributable cash flow was `$608M`.
- Fuel-distribution read: Fuel Distribution adjusted EBITDA was `$504M`, the segment sold approximately `4.1 billion` gallons of fuel, and fuel margin was `17.1` cents per gallon. This is the quarter that makes the distribution thesis obvious: sheer gallon movement plus margin capture drives the story.
- Infrastructure read: Pipeline Systems adjusted EBITDA was `$190M` on approximately `1.3 million` barrels per day of throughput, while Terminals adjusted EBITDA was `$113M` on approximately `1.1 million` barrels per day of throughput. That shows the business is not only about retail-facing gallons. The pipes and storage network are a major earnings layer.
- Refinery read: the Refinery segment generated `$175M` of adjusted EBITDA with approximately `57 thousand` barrels per day of throughput. Burnaby was much more visible here than in the prior quarter and proves that Parkland added real refining economics, not just more stations and wholesale fuel.
- Balance-sheet and capital-allocation read: long-term debt was about `$13.3B`, liquidity was about `$2.3B`, leverage was approximately `3.7x`, and total capital spending was `$202M`. The quarterly distribution rose to `$1.0023` per unit annualized to `$4.0092`.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` shows Sunoco as a full movement-and-delivery stack. Fuel distribution, pipelines, terminals, and refinery throughput all contributed, which is why this company belongs in a bigger-picture lane around logistics throughput and infrastructure dependence, not just refining.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income was `$644M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$858M`, and distributable cash flow was `$535M`.
- Fuel-distribution read: Fuel Distribution adjusted EBITDA was `$529M`, helped by `3.8 billion` gallons sold and fuel margin of `17.0` cents per gallon. This quarter included a gain on sale of inventory, so the raw number needs some caution, but the magnitude still shows how large the distribution machine had become after Parkland.
- Infrastructure read: Pipeline Systems adjusted EBITDA was `$179M` on approximately `1.3 million` barrels per day of throughput, and Terminals adjusted EBITDA was `$107M` on approximately `1.0 million` barrels per day of throughput.
- Refinery read: Refinery adjusted EBITDA was only `$43M`, with throughput of approximately `22 thousand` barrels per day because of the planned `50`-day maintenance turnaround. That matters because it shows how much quarter-to-quarter volatility in one refining asset can be absorbed by the broader distribution and logistics platform.
- Capital-allocation read: long-term debt was about `$13.9B`, liquidity about `$2.2B`, leverage roughly `4.0x`, and capital spending `$199M`. The quarterly distribution was raised by `6.25%`, including a one-time `5%` step-up plus the normal quarterly increase.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` is the clean proof that Sunoco is not a pure refinery packet. Even with Burnaby constrained by maintenance, the business still produced strong earnings because the distribution and infrastructure network carried the quarter.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net income was `$97M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$646M`, and distributable cash flow was `$442M`. Excluding one-time transaction-related expenses, the headline adjusted EBITDA figure was framed at `$706M`.
- Fuel-distribution read: Fuel Distribution adjusted EBITDA was `$332M`, with approximately `3.3 billion` gallons sold and fuel margin of `17.7` cents per gallon.
- Infrastructure read: Pipeline Systems adjusted EBITDA was `$187M` on approximately `1.4 million` barrels per day of throughput, while Terminals adjusted EBITDA was `$87M` on approximately `715 thousand` barrels per day of throughput.
- Refinery read: the Refinery segment generated `$40M` of adjusted EBITDA with approximately `49 thousand` barrels per day of crude throughput. Burnaby had entered the reporting mix after the Parkland close, but it was still only one layer of the broader system.
- Full-year `2025` read: Sunoco ended the year with adjusted EBITDA of `$2.047B`, net income of `$527M`, distributable cash flow of `$1.38B`, and long-term leverage at approximately `4.0x`.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` is the transition quarter that makes the packet work. It is where Sunoco visibly stops being just a fuel distributor with some infrastructure and starts becoming a much broader downstream platform shaped by Parkland integration.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Sunoco adds the wholesale fuel-distribution and infrastructure-delivery side of downstream energy.
- Consumer: Sunoco has more real end-market closeness than most energy packets because it touches branded retail stations, convenience demand, airport fuel, commercial fleets, and dealer channels.
- Cultural and societal: this packet is useful because it shows how invisible the delivery layer is behind everyday mobility. Consumers think in terms of pumps, brands, loyalty, and convenience. The filings show the hidden network of terminals, pipelines, storage, and wholesale contracts behind that routine behavior.
- Industrial: Sunoco depends on several linked physical systems:
  - large-scale branded and unbranded fuel distribution
  - dealer and retail-site networks
  - pipeline systems
  - terminal throughput and storage
  - commercial and aviation fuel supply
  - Burnaby refinery operations
  - acquisition integration across Parkland, NuStar, and TanQuid
- Commodity exposure: Sunoco still has fuel-margin and inventory sensitivity, but the packet is less about pure crude-to-products spread exposure than the other refiners. Route density, gallon volumes, storage, throughput, and network control matter more here.
- Capital intensity: this is a downstream capital case with a different profile from pure refining. Capital still matters, but much of the value is in connected logistics assets and acquired operating networks rather than in giant refining complexes alone.
- Upstream and downstream cycles: this packet sits deep in the downstream layer. It benefits when wholesale and retail channels are strong, but it also depends on upstream and midstream systems to keep the network physically supplied.
- Infrastructure dependence: this is one of the branch's clearest proofs that infrastructure and retail distribution are inseparable. Terminals, pipes, dealer channels, and storage are not support functions here; they are the earnings architecture.
- Dealer economics and after-sales relevance: although this is not an autos packet, it still intersects with the movement stack because it monetizes the recurring refueling layer behind passenger cars, fleets, aviation, and commercial customers.
