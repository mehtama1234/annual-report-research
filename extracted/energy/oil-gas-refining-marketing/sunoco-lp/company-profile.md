## What changed

- Sunoco adds the fuel-distribution and downstream-infrastructure packet rather than another pure refiner.
- The packet is distinctive because it combines wholesale and branded fuel movement, retail-facing distribution, pipelines, terminals, and a small refinery position inside one capital-allocation system.
- The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain also makes the Parkland acquisition central. Sunoco stopped being only a traditional U.S. fuel-distribution MLP and became a larger international fuel distributor with a much more visible Canadian refining, terminals, convenience, and commercial-fuels footprint.
- This makes Sunoco one of the strongest branch cases for downstream distribution scale, logistics throughput, dealer and retail dependence, infrastructure linkage, and how acquisitions can reshape the meaning of a fuel-marketing business.

## Open questions

- How durable is the post-Parkland step-up in Fuel Distribution EBITDA once acquisition synergies normalize and transaction noise fades?
- Does the market keep valuing Sunoco mainly as a high-yield distribution vehicle, or does it start to value the business more like a broader downstream platform with meaningful infrastructure and refining optionality?
- How much of the current strength comes from a structurally better portfolio versus a favorable near-term margin and integration window?
