## Annual report takeaways

- Valero is a strong downstream flagship because the `2025` annual filing is not really about exploration upside or fee-based transport. It is about running a large refining system reliably, capturing product margins, converting uptime into cash flow, and using renewable diesel and ethanol to widen the economic and policy perimeter around the core refinery base.
- The packet is especially useful as a second downstream anchor next to `Marathon Petroleum` because it is more directly a refining, renewable diesel, and ethanol system rather than a refiner whose equity story is partly stabilized by a separately visible midstream affiliate.
- Full-year `2025` net income attributable to Valero stockholders was `$2.3 billion`, adjusted net income attributable was `$3.3 billion`, and net cash provided by operating activities was `$5.8 billion`.
- Management framed `2025` as Valero's best year for mechanical availability, personnel safety, and environmental performance, while also highlighting record refining throughput and record ethanol production in both the fourth quarter and the full year.
- The annual period also makes the portfolio mix more legible: refining is still the earnings core, but Diamond Green Diesel and the ethanol fleet matter enough that the company reads as a transportation-fuels system with low-carbon and agricultural-fuels adjacency, not just a conventional refiner.
- AnnualReports still lagged at the `2024` package as of `2026-08-10`, so the authoritative annual chain here comes from the SEC `10-K`, with Valero's official financials and governance materials used as supplementary IR context.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net income attributable to Valero stockholders was `$3.7 billion`, diluted EPS was `$12.62`, and adjusted net income attributable was `$3.7 billion`, or `$12.54` per share.
- Operations: Refining segment operating income was `$4.5 billion`, adjusted refining operating income was `$4.4 billion`, refining throughput averaged `3.0 million barrels per day`, renewable diesel operating income was `$717 million`, and ethanol operating income was `$318 million`.
- Cash and balance sheet: `Q2 2026` net cash provided by operating activities was `$5.6 billion`, stockholder cash returns totaled `$2.6 billion`, and quarter-end cash and cash equivalents were `$7.9 billion`.
- Guidance: management kept pointing to the St. Charles FCC optimization project, still expected to be completed and begin operations in `Q3 2026`, while also emphasizing financial flexibility and cash-return capacity.
- Management message: management described the quarter as a strong result driven by excellent operations and commercial execution across all three segments, with resilient transportation-fuel demand still visible in the system.
- Structural themes: `Q2 2026` shows why downstream packets matter. A refiner with high uptime, strong margin capture, and functioning low-carbon side businesses can generate enormous cash very quickly, but the result still depends on a physically intensive asset base and favorable product-market conditions.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net income attributable to Valero stockholders was `$1.3 billion`, diluted EPS was `$4.22`, and net cash provided by operating activities was `$1.4 billion`.
- Operations: Refining operating income was `$1.8 billion`, refining throughput averaged `2.9 million barrels per day`, renewable diesel operating income was `$139 million`, and ethanol operating income was `$90 million`.
- Capital allocation and financing: Valero increased its quarterly cash dividend by `6%` to `$1.20` per share on `January 22, 2026`, issued `$850 million` of `5.150%` senior notes due `2036` on `March 10, 2026`, and returned `$938 million` of cash to stockholders.
- Management message: management emphasized system-wide optimization, disciplined financial decision-making, and good execution during a volatile commodity environment rather than presenting the quarter as a simple margin gift.
- Structural themes: `Q1 2026` is useful because it shows the lower-but-still-healthy side of the model. Even before the much larger `Q2` cash surge, Valero was already demonstrating that downstream earnings are shaped by operations, market disruption, and financial discipline as much as by raw crude direction.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net income attributable to Valero stockholders was `$1.1 billion`, diluted EPS was `$3.73`, adjusted net income attributable was `$1.2 billion`, and full-year `2025` operating cash flow was `$5.8 billion`.
- Operations: fourth-quarter refining operating income was `$1.7 billion`, refining throughput averaged `3.1 million barrels per day`, renewable diesel operating income was `$92 million`, and ethanol operating income was `$117 million`.
- Management message: management highlighted `2025` as the company's best year for mechanical availability, personnel safety, and environmental performance, and said it also achieved record refining throughput and ethanol production in both the quarter and full year.
- Capital allocation: Valero returned `$1.4 billion` of cash to stockholders in `Q4 2025` and `$4.0 billion` across the full year, while still funding sustaining capital and the St. Charles upgrade path.
- Structural themes: `Q4 2025` closes the year with the core downstream lesson intact. Refining is not merely a commodity pass-through business. It is an uptime, logistics, compliance, turnaround, and commercial-execution machine where even small operating advantages can materially change cash generation.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Valero adds the pure refiner-plus-low-carbon-fuels layer that is distinct from the upstream, pipeline, and service packets already in the branch.
- Consumer: the packet connects most directly to end demand for mobility and freight because the company monetizes transportation-fuel consumption rather than production acreage or throughput tolls.
- Cultural: the broader social signal is hidden dependence on fuel systems. Modern life still depends on high-functioning refining, logistics, and compliance infrastructure even when public discussion focuses more on technology or electrification than on fuel manufacturing.
- Regulatory: this packet is highly exposed to environmental rules, low-carbon fuel policy, renewable-fuels compliance, California operating pressure, tariff developments, and the permitting and redevelopment path around assets like Benicia.
- Labor: refinery reliability, turnarounds, safety execution, and project delivery remain contractor- and skilled-labor-intensive even when management emphasizes operations and commercial execution more than labor-market language.
- Technology: technology matters here through refinery optimization, throughput reliability, yield-enhancing projects, renewable diesel and SAF capability, and system-wide commercial coordination rather than through a software-led narrative.
- Capital allocation: Valero is a useful example of a downstream company using cash spikes to support dividends and large stockholder returns while still protecting the balance sheet and funding sustaining capital.
- Infrastructure dependence: this packet is one of the clearest demonstrations that energy security depends on downstream uptime, not only on crude supply. Transport fuels still require refineries, feedstock handling, compliance systems, export access, and physical asset reliability.
