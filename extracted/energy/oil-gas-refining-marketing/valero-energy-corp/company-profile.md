## What changed

- Earnings power: the packet moves from a solid `Q1 2026` into an exceptionally strong `Q2 2026`, showing how sharply downstream cash generation can inflect when refining margins and system execution line up.
- Cash flow: full-year `2025` operating cash flow was `$5.8 billion`; `Q1 2026` operating cash flow was `$1.4 billion`; and `Q2 2026` operating cash flow jumped to `$5.6 billion`.
- Segment mix: Refining remains the earnings engine, but the packet is more useful than a simple refiner because renewable diesel and ethanol are material enough to show how low-carbon and agricultural-fuels adjacency changes the story.
- Capital allocation: Valero kept a strong cash-return posture through the whole window, including a dividend increase to `$1.20` per share and large stockholder cash returns in every period.
- Asset intensity: the packet remains heavily shaped by turnarounds, sustaining capital, regulatory spending, and yield-enhancing work such as the St. Charles FCC optimization project.
- Demand exposure: Valero is directly exposed to transportation-fuel demand, margin swings, throughput, and market disruptions rather than reserve ownership, which makes it a cleaner downstream read than an upstream producer or toll-road pipeline name.
- Management tone: management repeatedly framed results around operational reliability, commercial execution, and system optimization, while also tying future performance to low-carbon fuels strategy, California actions, tariffs, and the Port Arthur situation.

## Open questions

- How much of the `Q2 2026` surge reflects temporary refining conditions versus more durable execution and asset-quality improvements?
- How stable is the renewable diesel earnings contribution once policy support, feedstock economics, and SAF positioning are separated from core refining economics?
- What does the Benicia decommissioning and redevelopment shift imply for how Valero wants to manage California regulatory risk inside a still asset-heavy downstream platform?
