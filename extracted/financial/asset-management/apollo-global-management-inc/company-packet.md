## Annual report takeaways

- Apollo is the clearest counterpoint to Blackstone in the current alternative-manager set. The model is not just management fees plus realizations; it is management fees plus spread earnings through Athene, backed by origination, retirement services, and insurance-scale balance-sheet sourcing.
- Full-year `2025` closed with record origination activity of more than `$300B`, inflows of more than `$225B`, fee-related earnings of about `$2.5B`, spread-related earnings of about `$3.4B`, adjusted net income of about `$5.2B`, total AUM of about `$938B`, and fee-generating AUM of about `$709B`.
- The key structural variable here is not only private-markets fundraising. It is Apollo’s ability to manufacture and distribute investment-grade and private credit at scale into insurance and retirement channels.
- This makes Apollo a bridge between alternative asset management and balance-sheet-intensive financial intermediation. The business reads partly like an asset manager and partly like a spread-and-origination platform.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: 2Q26

- Results: fee-related earnings of about `$785M`, spread-related earnings of about `$877M`, and adjusted net income of about `$1.31B` or `$2.11` per share.
- Scale detail: total AUM reached about `$1.05T`, fee-generating AUM about `$858B`, quarterly inflows about `$60B`, and last-twelve-month inflows about `$298B`.
- Operating read: Apollo kept compounding despite weaker private-equity realizations because the fee-and-spread stack is broader than classic carry-heavy alternatives models.
- What to watch: whether spread-related earnings and retirement-services momentum remain strong if credit conditions or investment spreads become less favorable.

### Quarter minus 1: 1Q26

- Results: record fee-related earnings of about `$728M`, spread-related earnings of about `$719M`, and adjusted net income of about `$1.21B` or `$1.94` per share.
- Scale detail: total AUM of about `$1.03T`, fee-generating AUM of about `$836B`, and record inflows of about `$115B`.
- Structural read: Apollo’s first quarter shows how quickly the platform can scale when origination, retirement assets, and fee-generating capital all move together.

### Quarter minus 2: 4Q25

- Results: quarter fee-related earnings of `$690M`, quarter spread-related earnings of `$865M`, and adjusted net income of about `$1.54B` or `$2.47` per share.
- Full-year readthrough: `2025` fee-related earnings reached about `$2.5B`, spread-related earnings about `$3.4B`, adjusted net income about `$5.2B`, total AUM about `$938B`, and fee-generating AUM about `$709B`.
- Strategic message: management framed `2025` around origination scale, retirement-services growth, and a model that can keep compounding through recurring fee and spread earnings rather than depending only on private-equity exits.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Apollo gives Financial a second alternative-manager evidence set and immediately shows that “alternatives” already contains multiple internal models.
- Balance-sheet outsourcing: Apollo is a strong read on how insurance and retirement liabilities are becoming a major asset pool for private-credit origination.
- Capital formation: compared with Blackstone, Apollo pushes the archive harder toward credit manufacturing, retirement solutions, and spread capture rather than toward realization timing.
- Technology and infrastructure: Apollo’s growth still benefits from private-credit and infrastructure demand, but the core operating signature is funding and origination architecture more than AI-themed deployment alone.
- Competition: the live questions are fee-generating AUM growth, origination capacity, credit quality, Athene spread economics, and whether this integrated model is more durable through a weaker exit environment.
