# Ares Management Corporation Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-08

## Snapshot

- Company: Ares Management Corporation
- Sector: Financial
- Industry: Asset Management
- Ticker: ARES
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: 2Q26
  - quarter 2: 1Q26
  - quarter 3: 4Q25

## Annual report takeaways

- Ares widens the alternative-manager set again, but with a different signature from Blackstone, Apollo, and KKR. The recurring stack here is management-fee growth, fee-paying AUM expansion, fundraising scale, and very large available capital.
- Full-year `2025` closed with AUM of about `$622.5B`, FPAUM of about `$384.9B`, available capital of about `$156.0B`, gross fundraising of about `$113.2B`, net inflows of about `$107.7B`, capital deployment of about `$145.8B`, fee related earnings of about `$1.78B`, and realized income of about `$1.85B`.
- The strategic frame from management is a diversified global origination platform spanning credit, real estate, private equity, infrastructure, and secondaries. That makes Ares less dependent on one product lane than a simpler alternatives label suggests.
- The more specific read-through is that fee-paying asset growth, fundraising consistency, and large undeployed capital are core scaling variables.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: 2Q26

- Results: GAAP net income attributable to Ares of about `$150.6M`, GAAP EPS of about `$0.49`, fee related earnings of about `$491.1M`, and realized income of about `$521.5M`.
- Scale detail: AUM reached about `$671.3B`, FPAUM about `$409.9B`, available capital about `$170.0B`, gross fundraising about `$36.4B`, and capital deployment about `$35.9B`.
- Operating read: Ares described the period as another record quarter of fundraising, with more than `$36B` of inflows and record dry powder.
- What to watch: whether the firm can convert very large available capital and a rising investment pipeline into continued fee growth without needing a major improvement in exits.

### Quarter minus 1: 1Q26

- Results: GAAP net income attributable to Ares of about `$142.6M`, GAAP EPS of about `$0.46`, fee related earnings of about `$464.4M`, and realized income of about `$502.7M`.
- Scale detail: AUM of about `$644.3B`, FPAUM of about `$399.6B`, available capital about `$158.1B`, gross fundraising about `$29.5B`, and capital deployment about `$32.3B`.
- Structural read: the quarter makes clear that fee-paying AUM growth and broad fundraising breadth are doing most of the work even in a slower transaction environment.

### Quarter minus 2: 4Q25

- Results: GAAP net income attributable to Ares of about `$54.2M`, GAAP EPS of about `$0.08`, fee related earnings of about `$527.7M`, and realized income of about `$589.1M`.
- Full-year readthrough: `2025` fee related earnings reached about `$1.78B`, realized income about `$1.85B`, AUM about `$622.5B`, FPAUM about `$384.9B`, and available capital about `$156.0B`.
- Strategic message: management framed `2025` around crossing `$600B` of AUM, record annual fundraising and investing above `$100B`, and management-fee growth of about `25%` for the year.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Ares sharpens the point that Financial alternatives already contain multiple internal business models, not one.
- Fee engine: Ares is the cleanest current example in this archive of an alternative manager where management-fee scale and FPAUM growth dominate the read.
- Dry powder and origination: the very large available-capital base and broad origination network make deployment capacity and fundraising durability central variables.
- Competitive read: Ares helps separate a credit-heavy, fundraising-heavy, fee-paying-AUM-growth model from Blackstone's realization-and-perpetual-capital emphasis, Apollo's spread-and-retirement-services engine, and KKR's insurance-plus-monetization breadth.
