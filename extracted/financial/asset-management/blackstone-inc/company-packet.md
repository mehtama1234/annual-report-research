## Annual report takeaways

- Blackstone is a different asset-management model from T. Rowe Price or State Street. The key operating variables here are fee-related earnings, distributable earnings, fee-earning AUM, fundraising, deployment, realizations, and the durability of perpetual capital.
- Full-year `2025` materials show total GAAP revenues of about `$14.45B`, GAAP net income attributable to Blackstone of about `$3.0B`, fee-related earnings of about `$5.7B`, distributable earnings of about `$7.1B`, and record AUM of about `$1.27T`.
- The scale variables matter directly: fee-earning AUM ended `2025` at about `$921.7B`, perpetual capital AUM at about `$523.6B`, inflows at about `$239.4B`, deployment at about `$138.2B`, and realizations at about `$125.6B`.
- This is a private-markets operating system, not just an investment portfolio. Credit, insurance, infrastructure, real estate, private equity, and wealth channels all show up as earnings drivers.
- The strategic readthrough is that fundraising quality and permanence of capital are as important as mark-to-market asset levels. Blackstone is effectively monetizing institutional outsourcing, insurance balance-sheet demand, and rising wealth access to private assets.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: 2Q26

- Results: total revenues of about `$5.04B`, GAAP net income attributable to Blackstone of about `$1.23B`, fee-related earnings of about `$1.78B`, distributable earnings of about `$1.98B`, and record AUM of about `$1.346T`.
- Flow and activity detail: inflows of about `$68.3B`, deployment of about `$34.2B`, and realizations of about `$31.8B`.
- Strategic message: management tied current momentum heavily to AI-related data centers, digital infrastructure, power demand, private credit, insurance, and wealth distribution.
- What to watch: whether current infrastructure and AI positioning creates a longer runway for fundraising and realizations, or mainly amplifies a favorable current cycle.

### Quarter minus 1: 1Q26

- Results: total revenues of about `$3.62B`, GAAP net income attributable to Blackstone of about `$649.7M`, fee-related earnings of about `$1.55B`, distributable earnings of about `$1.76B`, and AUM of about `$1.304T`.
- Flow and activity detail: inflows of about `$68.5B`, deployment of about `$35.6B`, and realizations of about `$35.9B`.
- Operating read: the quarter showed that Blackstone can still raise large capital pools in volatile public-market conditions because so much of the model now sits in insurance, perpetual, and private-wealth channels rather than only classic drawdown funds.

### Quarter minus 2: 4Q25

- Results: full-year `2025` total revenues of about `$14.45B`, GAAP net income attributable to Blackstone of about `$3.0B`, fee-related earnings of about `$5.7B`, distributable earnings of about `$7.1B`, and year-end AUM of about `$1.2749T`.
- Flow and activity detail: full-year inflows of about `$239.4B`, deployment of about `$138.2B`, and realizations of about `$125.6B`.
- Structural readthrough: alternative management here is not mainly about quarterly market moves. It is about converting scale fundraising and long-duration capital relationships into recurring fees, then using realizations and deployment timing to amplify earnings.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Blackstone adds an alternative-manager model that is materially different from money-center banks, premium payments, insurance, public-markets asset management, custody, exchanges, or ratings.
- Capital allocation: this evidence set shows how much modern financial power sits in private capital formation, insurance asset management, and large institutional outsourcing.
- Technology and infrastructure: the `2Q26` materials make AI, data centers, and power infrastructure explicit fundraising and deployment themes rather than background macro noise.
- Consumer and wealth behavior: the wealth channel matters because Blackstone is increasingly distributing private-market exposure beyond institutions.
- Competition: the pressure points are fundraising mix, permanence of capital, insurance penetration, realization windows, and whether other large private-capital firms can match Blackstone’s breadth.
