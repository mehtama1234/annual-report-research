## What changed

- Blackstone adds a fifth distinct fee-based Financial model to the archive: large-scale alternative asset management driven by fee-related earnings, realizations, fundraising, perpetual capital, and deployment pace rather than public-market fee rates or custody spreads.
- AnnualReports metadata confirms the company still sits in `Financial / Asset Management`, but the site was still lagged at `2024` and still used the legacy `The Blackstone Group L.P.` naming on `2026-08-08`.
- Full-year `2025` materials show total GAAP revenues of about `$14.45B`, GAAP net income attributable to Blackstone of about `$3.0B`, fee-related earnings of about `$5.7B`, distributable earnings of about `$7.1B`, record AUM of about `$1.27T`, fee-earning AUM of about `$921.7B`, inflows of about `$239.4B`, deployment of about `$138.2B`, and realizations of about `$125.6B`.
- The annual and late-2025 read is not a simple market-beta story. Blackstone’s operating engine is fundraising plus fee base plus realization timing, with private credit, infrastructure, insurance, real estate, and wealth distribution all mattering directly.
- `1Q26` kept that pattern intact even in a choppier environment. Results showed total revenues of about `$3.62B`, GAAP net income attributable to Blackstone of about `$649.7M`, fee-related earnings of about `$1.55B`, distributable earnings of about `$1.76B`, and AUM of about `$1.304T`, alongside inflows of about `$68.5B`, deployment of about `$35.6B`, and realizations of about `$35.9B`.
- `2Q26` extended the same story with higher scale and stronger monetization. Results showed total revenues of about `$5.04B`, GAAP net income attributable to Blackstone of about `$1.23B`, fee-related earnings of about `$1.78B`, distributable earnings of about `$1.98B`, and record AUM of about `$1.346T`, with inflows of about `$68.3B`, deployment of about `$34.2B`, and realizations of about `$31.8B`.
- The `2Q26` investor call also sharpened the strategic angle. Management tied current momentum to AI-related data-center and power investment, described a data-center platform with about `$185B` of total asset value, a credit and insurance platform of nearly `$550B`, insurance AUM of about `$290B`, and wealth AUM of about `$324B`.

## Open questions

- How much of recent distributable-earnings strength depends on realizations staying open versus recurring fee growth alone?
- Can perpetual capital, insurance, and wealth channels keep offsetting cyclicality in more realization-dependent strategies?
- Does Blackstone’s AI, power, and infrastructure positioning prove durable enough to become a cross-sector investment theme rather than a temporary fundraising hotspot?
