## Annual report takeaways

- Brookfield gives CLI 6 the large-scale alternative-capital and operating-asset allocator model, where value capture comes from fee-bearing capital, wealth solutions, operating-business cash flow, and long-duration principal investing.
- The `2025` annual and year-end materials framed the business around record distributable earnings before realizations of about `$5.4B`, or `$2.27` per share, total distributable earnings of about `$6.0B`, or `$2.54` per share, and net income of about `$3.2B`.
- The annual interpretation is that Brookfield sits at the center of several structural demand pools at once: institutions wanting real assets, households needing retirement products, and governments and corporates needing large-scale capital partners.
- Compared with Markel, Brookfield is the more global, capital-markets-oriented allocator; compared with the brokers, it is much more capital intensive but also more exposed to secular infrastructure, insurance-float, and private-capital growth.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent reported quarter as of Monday, August 10, 2026: `1Q26`

- Timing note: as of Monday, August 10, 2026, Brookfield had not yet reported `2Q26`; its second-quarter conference call was scheduled for Thursday, August 13, 2026, so `1Q26` remained the latest reported quarter.
- Results: Brookfield said distributable earnings increased to about `$1.6B`; the interim report said net income was about `$1.0B`, with about `$102M` attributable to common shareholders.
- Operating detail: the company highlighted raising about `$67B` since the prior quarter, including `$23B` for investment strategies and `$44B` of insurance capital, while total insurance assets expanded to about `$180B`.
- Strategic read: `1Q26` shows Brookfield's main edge in this lane: when capital formation, asset monetization, and insurance-linked growth are all working, the model can compound in a way few simpler financial businesses can match.

### Quarter minus 1: `4Q25`

- Results: Brookfield reported record `2025` distributable earnings before realizations of about `$5.4B`, or `$2.27` per share; total distributable earnings including realizations were about `$6.0B`, or `$2.54` per share.
- Operating detail: net income for the year was about `$3.2B`, and management emphasized strong fundraising, asset-management results, wealth-solutions scaling, and operating-business cash flow.
- Structural read: `4Q25` anchors Brookfield as a capital-allocation machine whose moat depends on scale, fundraising credibility, and access to complex transactions rather than on one product franchise.

### Quarter minus 2: `3Q25`

- Results: Brookfield said distributable earnings before realizations increased `18%` to about `$5.4B` on a last-twelve-month basis, and the quarter was described as financially strong.
- Operating detail: the company emphasized about `$30B` of organic fundraising in the quarter, about `$75B` of monetizations year to date, and several major strategic moves across insurance, AI-linked infrastructure, and Oaktree.
- Structural read: `3Q25` is useful because it shows that Brookfield's real product is not a single asset class but a platform for moving capital across secular themes at global scale.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and societal pattern: Brookfield indirectly monetizes retirement demand, infrastructure build-out, and the institutionalization of private capital rather than direct consumer behavior.
- Industrial pressure: fundraising cadence, monetization timing, transaction markets, leverage discipline, and cross-segment execution all matter.
- Cultural shift: more capital is flowing toward firms that can pair operating control with large-scale financing and insurance-linked capital, not just toward pure managers or pure owners.
- Bigger-picture read: Brookfield is the lane's most ambitious answer to the broker quality thesis because it can aggregate capital and deploy it across many structural growth systems, but it is inherently more complex and more reliant on market confidence.
