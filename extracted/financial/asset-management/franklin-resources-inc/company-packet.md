# Franklin Resources, Inc. Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-08

## Snapshot

- Company: Franklin Resources, Inc.
- Sector: Financial
- Industry: Asset Management
- Ticker: BEN
- Fiscal year-end: September
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q3 2026
  - quarter 2: Q2 2026
  - quarter 3: Q1 2026

## Annual report takeaways

- Franklin is a useful contrast case inside asset management because it is neither a pure alternatives manager nor a narrower active-public-markets house. The `2025` annual set shows a global hybrid model spanning mutual funds, institutional accounts, SMAs, ETFs, alternatives, multi-asset, and cash management.
- Fiscal `2025` ended with AUM of `$1.661T`, down slightly year over year, and long-term net outflows of `-$97.4B`, which confirms that distribution and product-mix pressure were still real at the annual-report stage.
- Operating revenues reached `$8.771B`, and the annual materials make clear that fee-rate pressure remained active: the effective investment-management fee rate was `40.5` basis points in fiscal `2025` versus `41.1` basis points in fiscal `2024`.
- Franklin still returned meaningful capital while navigating that pressure, with dividends of `$683.7M` and repurchases of `$240.3M`.
- The strategic answer is visible in the product mix: more emphasis on non-U.S. equity and multi-asset funds, U.S. alternatives, ETFs, SMAs, cash-management scale, and private-markets capabilities rather than relying only on legacy active-fund channels.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q3 2026

- Results: total operating revenues of `$2.358B`, operating income of `$215.8M`, net income of `$171.5M`, diluted EPS of `$0.31`, adjusted net income of `$386.3M`, and adjusted diluted EPS of `$0.72`.
- AUM and flow signal: total AUM reached `$1.792T`, average AUM reached `$1.750T`, and long-term net inflows were `+$18.4B`.
- Product-mix and strategy signal: alternatives AUM reached a record `$294.2B`, international AUM reached roughly `$525B`, and management highlighted a record won-but-unfunded pipeline of `$28.6B`.
- Important date note: Franklin announced that its corporate name will change from Franklin Resources, Inc. to Franklin Templeton, Inc. effective `August 17, 2026`, which is still in the future as of the `2026-08-08` research baseline.

### Quarter minus 1: Q2 2026

- Results: total operating revenues of `$2.295B`, operating income of `$323.3M`, net income of `$268.2M`, diluted EPS of `$0.49`, adjusted net income of `$384.5M`, and adjusted diluted EPS of `$0.71`.
- AUM and flow signal: total AUM was `$1.682T`, average AUM was `$1.702T`, and long-term net inflows were `+$16.9B`.
- Product and channel tone: ETFs and Canvas both reached record AUM, with `+$4.5B` and `+$5.3B` of net inflows respectively, while alternatives fundraising reached `$14.3B`.
- Readthrough: Franklin looked healthier than a simple fee-compression story. Flows were positive, richer products were growing, and the biggest drag remained Western Asset rather than the entire platform.

### Quarter minus 2: Q1 2026

- Results: total operating revenues of `$2.327B`, operating income of `$281.0M`, net income attributable to Franklin Resources of `$255.5M`, diluted EPS of `$0.46`, adjusted net income of `$378.4M`, and adjusted diluted EPS of `$0.70`.
- AUM and flow signal: total AUM was `$1.684T`, average AUM was `$1.676T`, and long-term net inflows were `+$28.0B`; management also highlighted `+$34.6B` of long-term net inflows excluding Western Asset Management.
- Fee and mix signal: the effective investment-management fee rate improved to `40.6` basis points for the quarter.
- Strategic message: SMA, ETF, Canvas, and private-markets growth were doing real work already, which set Franklin apart from a weaker legacy-active-only asset-management narrative.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Franklin adds a second public-markets asset-management model and sharpens the distinction between broad hybrid managers and both pure alternatives managers and narrower legacy active houses.
- Consumer and institutional behavior: investor demand is not disappearing. It is migrating toward lower-cost wrappers, higher-utility channels, private-markets access, and more flexible advice-oriented formats.
- Culture and business model: the company’s central challenge is not only market beta. It is where investors want exposure, how they want to access it, and what they are willing to pay for active management.
- Competition: Franklin’s evidence set suggests that the more durable asset managers may be the ones able to combine public-markets scale, international distribution, ETF and SMA relevance, and credible private-markets product depth.
- Watch item: Western Asset remains a drag even while the broader Franklin platform is improving, so the internal quality split matters.
