  - quarter 1: 2Q26

## Annual report takeaways

- KKR broadens the alternative-manager set again. The business is not best summarized as only fee-related earnings plus realizations, and not as only a spread-and-retirement-services engine either. The recurring stack here is fee growth plus monetization plus insurance-linked economics plus large-scale capital deployment.
- Full-year `2025` closed with record annual FRE and ANI per share, record capital raised, and record capital invested. The year-end release showed FRE of about `$3.7B`, TOE of about `$5.0B`, ANI of about `$4.4B`, AUM of about `$744B`, FPAUM of about `$604B`, capital raised of about `$129B`, capital invested of about `$95B`, and uncalled commitments of about `$118B`.
- The strategic frame from management is a three-engine platform: asset management, insurance, and strategic holdings. That gives KKR a different operating signature from a simpler fee-plus-carry manager.
- The more specific read-through is that private wealth, asset-based finance, insurance-linked AUM, and monetization depth are all becoming important scaling variables.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: 2Q26

- Results: FRE of about `$1.2B`, TOE of about `$1.5B`, ANI of about `$1.5B`, and GAAP net income attributable to common stockholders of about `$660M`.
- Scale detail: AUM reached about `$796B`, FPAUM about `$638B`, quarterly capital raised about `$34B`, quarterly capital invested about `$24B`, and uncalled commitments about `$143B`.
- Operating read: KKR reported record quarterly FRE, TOE, and ANI per share, described the period as its strongest monetization quarter ever, and paired that with record last-twelve-month inflows.
- What to watch: whether the monetization pace and private-markets demand remain strong enough to keep ANI accelerating at the current pace.

### Quarter minus 1: 1Q26

- Results: FRE of about `$1.0B`, TOE of about `$1.3B`, ANI of about `$1.25B`, and GAAP net income attributable to common stockholders of about `$365M`.
- Scale detail: AUM of about `$758B`, FPAUM of about `$615B`, quarterly capital raised about `$28B`, quarterly capital invested about `$22B`, and uncalled commitments about `$125B`.
- Structural read: the `1Q26` call makes clear that insurance economics, asset-based finance, credit fundraising, and wealth distribution are all core parts of KKR's earnings architecture rather than adjacent businesses.

### Quarter minus 2: 4Q25

- Results: quarter FRE of about `$1.0B`, quarter TOE of about `$1.3B`, and quarter ANI of about `$1.0B`; excluding the carried-interest repayment obligation, ANI per share would have been about `$1.30`.
- Full-year readthrough: `2025` FRE reached about `$3.7B`, TOE about `$5.0B`, ANI about `$4.4B`, AUM about `$744B`, FPAUM about `$604B`, capital raised about `$129B`, and capital invested about `$95B`.
- Strategic message: management framed `2025` around record fundraising, record deployment, rising insurance economics, record embedded gains, expanding private-wealth distribution, and the Arctos acquisition as another route into long-duration fee growth.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: KKR sharpens the point that Financial alternatives already contain multiple internal business models, not one.
- Insurance and balance-sheet demand: KKR's Global Atlantic relationship matters, but as one component inside a broader capital-formation and fee-compounding engine.
- Credit and ABF: asset-based finance is a major live growth vector, which links KKR more directly to nonbank credit creation and private-credit distribution than a pure PE label suggests.
- Wealth distribution: K-Series growth shows how large alternatives firms are moving deeper into advisor and individual channels, not only institutions and sovereigns.
- Competition: the live questions are fee quality, monetization durability, insurance-economics repeatability, deployment discipline, and whether wealth plus ABF can keep widening the model.
