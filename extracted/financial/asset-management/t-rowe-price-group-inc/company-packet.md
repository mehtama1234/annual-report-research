## Annual report takeaways

- T. Rowe Price is the clearest asset-management read added to the workspace so far: end-of-period AUM, average AUM, net flows, effective fee rate, product mix, and distribution channel all matter directly to earnings.
- The `2025` annual report shows record ending AUM of `$1.78 trillion` and average AUM of `$1.677 trillion`, but those headline asset levels coexisted with `-$56.9 billion` of net outflows.
- Total revenue reached `$7.31 billion`, investment advisory revenue reached `$6.60 billion`, GAAP diluted EPS was `$9.24`, and adjusted EPS was `$9.72`.
- The most important structural pressure in the annual materials is fee compression. Effective fee rate dropped to `39.4` basis points from `41.0` basis points because clients kept shifting toward lower-priced vehicles and strategies.
- Management’s growth answer is not only market appreciation. It is product and channel repositioning: fixed income, multi-asset, ETFs, alternatives, retirement, separately managed accounts, and partnerships such as the Goldman Sachs alliance.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: 2Q26

- Results: net revenues of about `$1.9 billion`; adjusted diluted EPS of `$2.57`.
- Cost and mix detail: administrative, distribution, servicing, and other fees were `$144.2 million`; adjusted operating expenses were `$1.203 billion`.
- Operating tension: earnings improved, but the quarter still reflected the same basic sector tension as the annual report, namely fee-rate pressure and ongoing net outflows alongside higher market-driven AUM.
- What to watch: how much of recent earnings strength depends on markets staying high versus better flow quality or richer product mix.

### Quarter minus 1: 1Q26

- Results: AUM of `$1.71 trillion`, net flows of `-$13.7 billion`, GAAP net revenues of `$1.857 billion`, GAAP diluted EPS of `$2.23`, and adjusted diluted EPS of `$2.52`.
- Fee and flow signal: effective fee rate with performance fees was `38.6` basis points, down from `39.2` basis points in `4Q25`, reinforcing that product mix remains a live earnings headwind.
- Product and channel tone: management continued emphasizing fixed income, alternatives, ETFs, retirement products, and wealth channels as the better growth lanes.

### Quarter minus 2: 4Q25

- Results: AUM of `$1.78 trillion` at year-end, net client outflows of `$25.5 billion` for the quarter and `$56.9 billion` for the year, GAAP EPS of `$1.99` for the quarter and `$9.24` for the full year, and adjusted EPS of `$2.44` for the quarter and `$9.72` for the full year.
- Strategic message: management said the firm made progress on partnerships, ETF growth, and alternatives, even while legacy mutual-fund and equity outflows continued.
- Structural readthrough: asset management here is not a simple bull-market beneficiary; it is a mix-shift and fee-rate-management business under steady channel pressure.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: T. Rowe Price adds a different financial model centered on AUM economics, flows, fee rates, performance persistence, retirement distribution, and product migration.
- Consumer and institutional behavior: this evidence set is a clean read on where savings and retirement dollars are moving, especially away from higher-fee legacy mutual-fund structures.
- Culture and business model: the asset manager’s problem is not raw demand absence. It is how investors want to hold exposures and how much they are willing to pay for active management.
- Technology: technology matters here mostly as modernization, operating leverage, and distribution enablement rather than as the main topline narrative.
- Competition: public/private product design, ETFs, alternatives, model portfolios, and wealth distribution are all active competitive battlegrounds.
