# The Carlyle Group Inc. Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-08

## Snapshot

- Company: The Carlyle Group Inc.
- Sector: Financial
- Industry: Asset Management
- Ticker: CG
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: 2Q26
  - quarter 2: 1Q26
  - quarter 3: 4Q25

## Annual report takeaways

- Carlyle widens the alternative-manager set again, but with a more classic fee-plus-carry-plus-fundraising profile than Apollo, KKR, or Ares. The recurring stack here is fee-related earnings, fundraising, carry monetization, and a broad segment mix across Global Private Equity, Global Credit, and Carlyle AlpInvest.
- Full-year `2025` closed with DE of about `$1.7B`, FRE of about `$1.2B`, realized net performance revenues of about `$357M`, total AUM of about `$477B`, fee-earning AUM of about `$337B`, available capital of about `$88B`, inflows of about `$53.7B`, and deployment of about `$54.5B`.
- The strategic frame from management is a diversified alternatives platform with three business segments, not a pure buyout house. That matters because Carlyle AlpInvest and Global Credit are now large enough to shape fundraising quality, fee mix, and earnings resilience.
- The more specific read-through is that Carlyle sits between the more realization-heavy stereotype of private equity and the steadier fee engines of the larger diversified alternatives managers.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: 2Q26

- Results: GAAP net income attributable to common stockholders of about `$137.1M`, diluted EPS of about `$0.37`, DE of about `$472M`, FRE of about `$358M`, and realized net performance revenues of about `$115M`.
- Scale detail: total AUM reached about `$485B`, fee-earning AUM about `$334B`, perpetual capital fee-earning AUM about `$113B`, available capital about `$97B`, inflows about `$16.8B`, and deployment about `$14.3B`.
- Operating read: management described `2Q26` as one of Carlyle's strongest quarters in recent years, with record FRE and the highest distributable earnings in nearly four years.
- What to watch: whether realization activity and fundraising momentum can stay strong enough to keep the carry-plus-fee model from slipping back into a more volatile pattern.

### Quarter minus 1: 1Q26

- Results: GAAP net loss attributable to common stockholders of about `$132.2M`, diluted EPS of about `-$0.37`, DE of about `$327M`, FRE of about `$300M`, and realized net performance revenues of about `$21M`.
- Scale detail: total AUM of about `$475B`, fee-earning AUM about `$333B`, available capital about `$96B`, inflows about `$13.0B`, and deployment about `$10.0B`.
- Structural read: the quarter is useful because it shows Carlyle's fee engine still holding up even when GAAP investment income and performance allocations swing hard.

### Quarter minus 2: 4Q25

- Results: GAAP net income attributable to common stockholders of about `$358.1M`, diluted EPS of about `$0.96`, DE of about `$436M`, FRE of about `$290M`, and realized net performance revenues of about `$123M`.
- Full-year readthrough: `2025` ended with total AUM of about `$477B`, fee-earning AUM of about `$337B`, perpetual capital fee-earning AUM of about `$111B`, available capital of about `$88B`, and net accrued performance revenues of about `$2.9B`.
- Strategic message: management framed the year around stronger fundraising, especially in Carlyle AlpInvest and Global Credit, higher fee-earning AUM, and a better path to steadier FRE.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Carlyle sharpens the point that Financial alternatives already contain multiple internal business models, not one.
- Earnings mix: Carlyle is useful because it sits closer to a classic alternatives fee-and-carry model than Apollo, KKR, or Ares do.
- Segment mix: Global Credit and AlpInvest matter strategically because they diversify fundraising and fee sources away from a pure private-equity cycle.
- Competitive read: Carlyle helps separate a diversified but still realization-sensitive alternatives platform from Blackstone's perpetual-capital-heavy scale, Apollo's spread engine, KKR's insurance-and-monetization breadth, and Ares's fee-paying-AUM and dry-powder emphasis.
