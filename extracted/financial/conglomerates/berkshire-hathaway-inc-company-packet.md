## Annual report takeaways

- Berkshire gives CLI 6 the capital-allocation superstructure: a conglomerate that uses insurance float, retained earnings, rail, utilities, energy, manufacturing, service, and retail cash generation to keep reallocating capital across the portfolio.
- Full-year `2025` net earnings attributable to Berkshire shareholders were `$66.968B` and operating earnings were `$44.486B`.
- Segment operating earnings in `2025` were roughly `$7.258B` for insurance underwriting, `$12.513B` for insurance investment income, `$5.476B` for BNSF, `$3.979B` for Berkshire Hathaway Energy, and `$13.647B` for manufacturing, service, and retailing.
- The annual packet matters because Berkshire is the clearest proof in CLI 6 that some balance-sheet-heavy systems should be read less as sectors and more as permanent capital-allocation machines.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: 2Q26

- Results: net earnings attributable to Berkshire shareholders were `$25.667B` and operating earnings were `$12.983B`.
- Capital detail: Berkshire said it repurchased about `$4.5B` of treasury shares in the second quarter and about `$4.8B` in the first six months of `2026`.
- Strategic read: `2Q26` shows Berkshire at its most distinctive when the market backdrop is noisy. The company does not need a single operating theme because its comparative advantage is capital allocation across unlike businesses with a giant liquidity base.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and societal pattern: Berkshire owns pieces of baseline economic participation, from insurance and freight to energy and consumer-facing brands.
- Industrial pressure: rail regulation, energy investment, insurance reserving, and environmental obligations all matter, but Berkshire can absorb them because it sits above the operating units with capital and patience.
- Cultural shift: Berkshire remains one of the clearest examples that trust in capital allocation itself can function like an enduring corporate franchise.
- Bigger-picture read: Berkshire is the batch's strongest argument that CLI 6 should treat balance-sheet architecture and reinvestment judgment as primary research subjects, not just supporting details.
