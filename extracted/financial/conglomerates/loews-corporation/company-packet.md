## Annual report takeaways

- Loews gives CLI 6 a diversified holding-company case that is less dominated by one giant insurance-float machine than Berkshire and more dependent on allocation across CNA, Boardwalk Pipelines, Loews Hotels, and Altium Packaging.
- In `2025`, Loews reported net income attributable to Loews Corporation of about `$1.7B`, or roughly `$7.97` diluted net income per share.
- The annual read is that Loews remains a conservative allocator first and a story stock second, with value created through patient capital, portfolio mix, and willingness to let subsidiary economics work through cycles.
- Compared with Berkshire, Loews looks smaller, more operating-mix dependent, and more exposed to subsidiary-level swings, but still clearly belongs in CLI 6 because the holding-company architecture is itself the strategic asset.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: 2Q26

- Results: Loews reported net income of about `$444M`, or `$2.16` per share, in the second quarter of `2026`.
- Operating detail: the release said six-month `2026` net income reached about `$781M`, or `$3.79` per share, with improvement coming from a mixed set of subsidiary drivers rather than one dominant segment.
- Strategic read: `2Q26` shows the benefit of diversified cash-flow streams when insurance, hospitality, energy, and packaging conditions are not moving in lockstep.

### Quarter minus 1: 1Q26

- Results: Loews reported net income of about `$337M`, or `$1.63` per share, in the first quarter of `2026`.
- Operating detail: the quarter was weaker than the comparable `2025` period, showing that the model still carries meaningful subsidiary-level variability.
- Structural read: `1Q26` is helpful because it shows that conglomerate resilience does not mean smooth earnings, only better absorption capacity across unlike businesses.

### Quarter minus 2: 4Q25

- Results: Loews reported net income of about `$402M`, or `$1.94` per share, in the fourth quarter of `2025`.
- Full-year readthrough: the year-end results reinforced Loews's conservative posture and long-term value framing rather than a near-term growth narrative.
- Structural read: `4Q25` makes Loews the batch's patient-capital contrast case: not the strongest quarter-to-quarter compounder, but a durable allocator with optionality.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and societal pattern: Loews indirectly monetizes the persistence of insurance demand, energy transport, business travel and hospitality, and packaging demand through subsidiaries rather than through a single customer interface.
- Industrial pressure: subsidiary execution, catastrophe and insurance cycles, travel demand, energy infrastructure regulation, and capital deployment timing all matter.
- Cultural shift: the company benefits from investor willingness to back conservative, diversified capital allocators when macro visibility is uneven.
- Bigger-picture read: Loews shows that CLI 6 conglomerates do not need Berkshire-like scale to matter; they need patience, mix, and discipline.

## Working interpretation

- Loews is the smaller-scale patient-capital contrast case in CLI 6. It lacks Berkshire's scale and Markel's underwriting identity, but it still proves that diversified holding-company architecture can be a meaningful source of resilience.
- The quarter chain repeatedly points to the same pattern: Loews is not smooth, but it does not need one subsidiary to be perfect because capital and cash flow are spread across unlike businesses.
- In the context of CLI 6, Loews helps show that conglomerate logic exists on a spectrum, from Berkshire's superstructure to smaller but still disciplined allocation systems.
