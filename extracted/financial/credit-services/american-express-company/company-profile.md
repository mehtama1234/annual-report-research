## What changed

- American Express broadens the Financial sector beyond large banks into a premium-card, merchant-network, and travel-lifestyle payments model.
- The `2025` annual report shows record revenue of `$72.2 billion`, net income of `$10.8 billion`, and EPS of `$15.38`, with card spending up `7%` FX-adjusted, net card fee revenue reaching `$10 billion`, and `12.5 million` proprietary cards acquired.
- Management frames the business around a differentiated `Membership Model`, a global closed-loop network, premium value propositions, rich first-party data, and high-spending customers rather than around balance-sheet scale alone.
- The annual and quarterly materials are explicit that younger affluent cohorts matter: Millennial and Gen Z customers remain the fastest-growing consumer segment and are increasingly driving U.S. consumer spend.
- The quarterlies reinforce that the story is still about spending, fee growth, premium engagement, and credit quality. `1Q26` reported revenue of `$18.9 billion`, net income of `$3.0 billion`, and EPS of `$4.28`; `2Q26` reported revenue of `$19.6 billion`, net income of `$3.1 billion`, and EPS of `$4.53`.
- AmEx also adds a different AI angle to the archive: agentic commerce, AI-powered product discovery and payments, and internal data-rich personalization tied directly to its closed-loop network.

## Open questions

- How durable is AmEx’s premium-spend and card-fee model if travel, dining, or higher-income consumer sentiment softens materially?
- Does AmEx’s closed-loop data and merchant network create a more defensible moat than the deposit-and-balance-sheet advantages held by the money-center banks?
