## Annual report takeaways

- Synchrony adds a different `Financial / Credit Services` model from `American Express`, `PayPal`, `Visa`, `Mastercard`, and `Ally`. The recurring engine is not a premium travel-and-dining card network, not an open-loop acceptance rail, not a wallet, and not a deposit-funded auto lender. It is merchant-linked consumer finance spread across private label, co-brand, promotional financing, installment products, and buy now pay later.
- AnnualReports still showed only the `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K` on `2026-08-10`, even though the official Synchrony investor-relations pages already exposed the `2025` annual report and the current quarter chain. This is another direct reminder that the aggregator is useful for taxonomy but not always for current-year coverage.
- The full-year `2025` result was large enough to matter as a real consumer and commerce system. Synchrony generated more than `$182B` of sales or purchase volume, added more than `20M` new accounts, engaged nearly `70M` existing customers, produced full-year net earnings of `$3.6B`, earned `$9.28` per diluted share, and delivered a return on average assets of `3.0%`.
- Funding matters to the model too. Deposits in Synchrony Bank ended `2025` at `$81.1B` and represented `84%` of total funding, which means this is not just a fee or merchant-tech story. It is a merchant-linked credit platform supported by a meaningful deposit-funded liability base.
- The annual narrative makes the structural lane clear. Synchrony keeps emphasizing a multi-product partner strategy spanning private label and co-brand cards, promotional financing, installment products, and buy now pay later, while using `Synchrony PRISM` and point-of-sale integration to extend credit more precisely and keep credit outcomes inside target ranges.
- The annual materials also show why this matters for the broader project. Synchrony sits at the moment where household affordability, merchant conversion, point-of-sale financing, and local-service-provider economics meet. This is a different recurring behavior system from either premium status spending or general-purpose digital wallet usage.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent reported quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: net earnings were `$885M`, diluted EPS were `$2.59`, purchase volume increased `8%` to `$49.8B`, and loan receivables increased `2%` to `$102.2B`.
- Operating read: average active accounts were roughly flat at `68.3M`, net interest margin increased `30` basis points to `15.08%`, return on assets was `2.9%`, and CET1 was `13.2%`.
- Demand read: management said new accounts continued to grow, average active accounts inflected back to growth, and higher spend per account across all five sales platforms drove all-time-high purchase volume.
- Mix read: the release explicitly highlighted stronger spend with value-oriented partners, diversified offerings, and double-digit growth in out-of-partner discretionary spend on co-branded products.
- Structural read: this quarter shows why Synchrony matters. It monetizes repeat consumer financing attached directly to merchants and providers, not only generic card swipe behavior.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: net earnings were `$805M`, diluted EPS were `$2.27`, purchase volume increased `6%` to `$43.0B`, and loan receivables were flat at `$100.1B`.
- Operating read: average active accounts declined `1%` to `68.8M`, net interest margin increased `76` basis points to `15.50%`, return on assets was `2.7%`, and CET1 was `12.7%`.
- Capital read: Synchrony announced a new share repurchase authorization of up to `$6.5B` and a planned `13%` dividend increase beginning in Q3 `2026`.
- Demand read: management said record first-quarter purchase volume reflected higher spend per account across all five sales platforms plus sequential improvement in active-account trends.
- Structural read: Q1 reinforces that this is not a one-lane credit business. Consumer finance demand is distributed across multiple partner categories and monetized through both merchant relationships and balance-sheet spread economics.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: net earnings were `$751M`, diluted EPS were `$2.04`, purchase volume increased `3%` to `$49.5B`, and loan receivables decreased `1%` to `$103.8B`.
- Full-year `2025` read: Synchrony added over `20M` new accounts, engaged nearly `70M` customers, generated over `$182B` of purchase volume, and returned about `$3.3B` of capital to shareholders.
- Operating read: average active accounts declined `1%` to `69.3M`, net interest margin increased `82` basis points to `15.83%`, and return on assets was `2.5%`.
- Credit read: management said the full-year net charge-off rate returned to the long-term target range of `5.5%` to `6.0%`, which is important because this lane only works if merchant-linked consumer credit can keep scaling without breaking credit discipline.
- Structural read: year-end `2025` is the clearest baseline for the broader theme. Synchrony is not just extending credit. It is helping merchants and providers close transactions across retail, home, health, auto, and local-services settings where financing availability itself changes demand.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Synchrony adds a merchant-linked consumer-finance and private-label-credit interface to `Financial`, which is different from premium closed-loop cards, open-loop acceptance networks, wallets, PSP infrastructure, or branchless auto finance.
- Consumer and cultural behavior: this packet is a direct read on how normalized point-of-sale financing has become across everyday and aspirational purchase categories, especially where households want flexibility without revolving that decision through a generic bank branch relationship.
- Merchant and local-economy behavior: Synchrony is partly a demand-conversion system for merchants, providers, and local businesses. Financing is being embedded closer to the purchase moment and used as a sales tool, not only as a back-end credit product.
- Business model: the key insight is that Synchrony monetizes recurring consumer credit behavior through partner relationships, multi-product financing, underwriting infrastructure, deposit funding, and the spread economics on receivables rather than through network tolls or pure software subscriptions.
- Technology and operations: `Synchrony PRISM`, point-of-sale integrations, and financing embedded into merchant and practice-management workflows show that underwriting and distribution software are central to the moat, not support functions.
- Watch item: the next useful comparison in this lane is not another broad bank. It is another point-of-sale finance, installment, or merchant-linked consumer-credit operator that can test how durable Synchrony’s partner-centric model is versus other embedded-credit structures.
