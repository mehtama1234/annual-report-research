## What changed

- Synchrony adds a new financial-interface type to the archive: merchant-linked consumer finance and private-label credit.
- The business is best understood as a partner-facing and purchase-moment credit platform spanning private label, co-brand, installment, promotional financing, buy now pay later, deposit funding, and merchant conversion support rather than a classic bank branch model or a pure card-network toll road.
- Full-year `2025` net earnings reached about `$3.6B`, diluted EPS reached `$9.28`, return on average assets reached `3.0%`, purchase volume exceeded `$182B`, and deposits reached about `$81.1B`, which makes this a large and economically central recurring-behavior system.
- The annual materials repeatedly show that Synchrony sits closer to the sale than most lenders do. Its merchant and provider relationships, point-of-sale integrations, and multi-product financing tools are designed to lift conversion and broaden access to credit at the exact purchase moment.
- The quarterly chain reinforces that the model still has real operating discipline. `Q4 2025`, `Q1 2026`, and `Q2 2026` all showed solid purchase-volume behavior, stable to improving receivable trends, strong capital levels, and explicit management emphasis on credit restraint rather than growth-at-any-cost.
- Synchrony also adds a different technology and operating angle to the archive: `Synchrony PRISM`, merchant and practice-management integrations, and embedded financing workflows matter because the underwriting and distribution system itself is part of the moat.

## Open questions

- How durable is Synchrony’s merchant-linked financing model if U.S. household affordability pressure worsens or if discretionary big-ticket categories soften more sharply?
- Does Synchrony’s combination of private label, co-brand, installment, and deposit funding create a more defensible recurring interface than thinner buy now pay later or merchant-acquiring models?
