## What changed

- Upstart adds a new financial-interface type to the archive: AI lending marketplace and bank-partner credit infrastructure.
- The business is best understood as a software-mediated credit-origination and funding network spanning borrower acquisition, AI underwriting, bank and credit-union distribution, product expansion into auto, home, and revolving credit, and funding-market coordination rather than as a classic deposit-funded lender.
- Full-year `2025` originations reached about `$11.0B`, revenue reached about `$1.0B`, revenue from fees reached about `$950M`, net income reached about `$53.6M`, and adjusted EBITDA reached about `$230M`, which makes this a scaled and now profitable operating layer rather than an experimental fintech niche.
- The annual and quarterly materials keep reinforcing that the moat is increasingly operational: more than `100` banks and credit unions on the platform, more than `90%` of loans fully automated, improving conversion rates, and a widening product set across personal, auto, home-equity, small-dollar, and new revolving-credit surfaces.
- The quarter chain shows real movement in both growth and structure. `Q4 2025` restored profitability, `Q1 2026` kept high growth while pushing into charter and secured-product expansion, and `Q2 2026` returned to GAAP profitability with a much less negative secured-product contribution profile.
- Upstart also strengthens the broader AI and industrial-angle read across the repo: this is one of the clearest cases where AI is not marketing garnish but the core product mechanism that changes approval rates, pricing, automation, funding fit, and lender economics.

## Open questions

- How durable is Upstart’s marketplace model if credit conditions worsen and funding partners become more risk-averse again?
- Does the company ultimately behave more like AI underwriting infrastructure, a bank-partner distribution layer, or a future specialty-credit balance-sheet platform with more direct funding control?
