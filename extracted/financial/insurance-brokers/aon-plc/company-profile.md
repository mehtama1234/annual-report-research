## What changed

- Aon keeps proving that advice and workflow control can compound without the heaviest balance-sheet burden. The quarter chain stays centered on organic growth, margin discipline, and cash generation rather than on underwriting or funding leverage.
- The business is explicitly model-driven. Management keeps tying performance to the `3x3` operating plan, which makes Aon the most transformation-and-analytics-heavy broker in this comparison set.
- Client complexity remains monetizable. Commercial risk, reinsurance, health, and human-capital advisory demand keep showing up as recurring revenue rather than episodic crisis work.
- The company is still exposed to labor and execution pressure, but the core economics stay cleaner than direct risk carriers because Aon owns judgment, access, and client workflow instead of warehousing the largest liabilities.
- CLI 6 role: Aon is one of the clearest proofs that trust intermediation can become a capital-light infrastructure layer for global corporate risk management.

## Open questions

- How much of Aon's resilience depends on continued pricing firmness in insurance markets versus deeper structural client dependence on its advisory stack?
- Can Aon keep expanding operating leverage while still investing enough in data, analytics, and talent to preserve its premium position?
