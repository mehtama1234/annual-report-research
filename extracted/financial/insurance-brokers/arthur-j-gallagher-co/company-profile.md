## What changed

- Gallagher shows how consolidation itself becomes a moat in a fragmented insurance-distribution market. The packet stays centered on acquisition integration, brokerage scale, and fee growth rather than on direct risk-bearing.
- The model remains capital-light relative to banks or REITs, but it is more integration-intensive than Aon or Marsh. That makes execution quality, culture, and acquisition discipline part of the core economics.
- Brokerage remains the anchor, while risk management adds another recurring service layer that reinforces client stickiness without creating a loan-book or property-owner burden.
- The company benefits from the same outsourcing trend as Aon and Marsh, but it monetizes it with a more roll-up-heavy operating model.
- CLI 6 role: Gallagher broadens the trust-intermediation thesis by proving that distributed local relationships can still scale into strong central economics when the consolidator keeps integrating well.

## Open questions

- How durable is Gallagher's acquisition-led growth advantage if valuation discipline tightens or integration gets harder at larger scale?
- Does the broker franchise keep improving organically, or is more of the future economics dependent on continued dealmaking?
