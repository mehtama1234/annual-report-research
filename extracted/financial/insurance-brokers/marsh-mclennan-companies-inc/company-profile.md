## What changed

- Marsh remains the cleanest quality proof in this broker trio. The quarter chain keeps showing recurring revenue, margin discipline, and strong adjusted earnings with much lighter balance-sheet burden than banks or REITs.
- The company monetizes institutional anxiety and outsourcing. Risk placement, reinsurance, consulting, and advisory demand remain durable because clients keep paying to navigate complexity rather than self-manage everything.
- One-off legal noise did not break the underlying earnings pattern in `1Q26`, which makes the distinction between reported volatility and structural business quality important here.
- The economics are still people- and expertise-intensive, but the business captures a premium because it owns trusted workflow, data, and market access rather than direct asset risk.
- CLI 6 role: Marsh is one of the strongest examples in the whole archive of trust monetization with fee-heavy economics and low direct funding burden.

## Open questions

- How durable is Marsh's current growth and margin profile if insurance-pricing support eases and consulting demand softens at the same time?
- Does the company keep widening the quality gap through data and advisory depth, or does broker competition eventually compress the current advantage?
