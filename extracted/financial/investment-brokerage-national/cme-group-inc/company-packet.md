  - quarter 2: 1Q26

## Annual report takeaways

- CME is the strongest market-infrastructure and exchange operator read added to the workspace so far.
- The `2025` annual and `4Q25` materials show total revenue of `$6.5 billion`, operating income of `$4.2 billion`, net income of `$4.1 billion`, diluted EPS of `$11.16`, and record annual market-data revenue of `$803 million`.
- The core business model depends on benchmark product liquidity, contract volume, average rate per contract, clearing, data sales, and collateral efficiency.
- Management is explicit that a `risk-always-on` environment is supporting client demand. The business benefits when more institutions need to hedge, post margin, source liquidity, and access benchmark price discovery.
- The firm also looks culturally different from the rest of the financial set: it behaves like a regulated market utility with network effects and pricing power in data and clearing rather than like a lender, card issuer, insurer, or active asset manager.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: 2Q26

- Results: revenue of `$1.7 billion`, operating income of `$1.1 billion`, net income of `$1.0 billion`, and diluted EPS of `$2.88`.
- Volume and monetization: second-quarter ADV reached `29.8 million` contracts; clearing and transaction fees totaled `$1.4 billion`; market-data revenue reached a record `$238 million`.
- Management message: the first half of `2026` was the strongest in CME history, with the quarter supported by very high volume, margin-efficiency value to clients, and continued product innovation.
- Capital and balance sheet: CME ended June 30, 2026 with `$2.3 billion` in cash and `$3.4 billion` of debt, while repurchasing shares and paying dividends.

### Quarter minus 1: 1Q26

- Results: revenue of `$1.9 billion`, operating income of `$1.3 billion`, net income of `$1.2 billion`, and diluted EPS of `$3.18`.
- Volume and monetization: ADV reached a record `36.2 million` contracts, clearing and transaction fees reached `$1.5 billion`, and market-data revenue reached a record `$224 million`.
- Structural readthrough: CME monetizes volatility and risk-transfer demand directly; when clients need to hedge more aggressively across rates, equity indexes, FX, energy, agriculture, and metals, its infrastructure captures that activity.

### Quarter minus 2: 4Q25

- Results: quarter revenue of `$1.6 billion`, operating income of `$1.0 billion`, net income of `$1.2 billion`, and diluted EPS of `$3.24`; full-year revenue reached `$6.5 billion`.
- Market structure signal: fourth-quarter ADV reached `27.4 million` contracts, clearing and transaction fees totaled `$1.3 billion`, market-data revenue reached `$208 million`, and the company highlighted approximately `$80 billion` of average daily margin efficiencies for clients.
- Strategic message: new tools such as U.S. Treasury clearing, 24/7 crypto trading, and prediction markets were framed as part of the next expansion leg.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: CME adds exchange, clearinghouse, and market-data economics to the financial archive.
- Regulation: this is one of the clearest files in the workspace where regulation, benchmark access, clearing, collateral, and market-structure policy are part of the core business model.
- Culture: client dependence on hidden financial plumbing is economically significant; infrastructure providers can monetize uncertainty rather than merely endure it.
- Technology: product design, electronic trading, distribution, and resilience matter directly, but the moat is ultimately market structure and benchmark centrality.
- Competition: market-data pricing, contract innovation, clearing, and network liquidity are the key battlegrounds rather than consumer acquisition or loan growth.
