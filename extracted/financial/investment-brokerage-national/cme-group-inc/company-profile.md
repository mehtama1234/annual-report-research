## What changed

- CME adds a fifth distinct Financial-sector model to the archive: market infrastructure and exchange economics driven by trading volume, rate per contract, market-data monetization, clearing, collateral balances, and benchmark-product network effects.
- AnnualReports metadata and official materials confirm CME fits `Financial / Investment Brokerage - National`, with `CME` on `NASDAQ`, `1001-5000` employees, and headquarters in Chicago.
- The `2025` annual and `4Q25` materials show full-year revenue of `$6.5 billion`, operating income of `$4.2 billion`, net income of `$4.1 billion`, diluted EPS of `$11.16`, and record annual market-data revenue of `$803 million`.
- Management frames the company less like a conventional broker and more like a global risk-transfer utility: record `2025` ADV of `28.1 million` contracts, benchmark liquidity across every major asset class, central counterparty clearing, and margin-efficiency savings for clients.
- `1Q26` was exceptionally strong. CME reported revenue of `$1.9 billion`, operating income of `$1.3 billion`, net income of `$1.2 billion`, diluted EPS of `$3.18`, record ADV of `36.2 million` contracts, clearing and transaction fees of `$1.5 billion`, and record market-data revenue of `$224 million`.
- `2Q26` stayed strong even after the Q1 surge. The company reported revenue of `$1.7 billion`, operating income of `$1.1 billion`, net income of `$1.0 billion`, diluted EPS of `$2.88`, ADV of `29.8 million` contracts, clearing and transaction fees of `$1.4 billion`, and record market-data revenue of `$238 million`.
- The market-structure angle is the important addition here: CME monetizes volatility, benchmark centrality, data distribution, and collateral efficiency rather than balance-sheet spread, premium-card spend, or active-management fees.
