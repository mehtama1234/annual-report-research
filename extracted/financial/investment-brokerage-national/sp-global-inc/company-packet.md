## Annual report takeaways

- S&P Global broadens the same AnnualReports industry label used by CME into a very different model: ratings, data, benchmarks, pricing, and workflow software rather than exchange clearing and trading volume.
- The `2025` annual filing shows revenue of `$15.336 billion`, operating income of `$6.478 billion`, net income of `$4.471 billion`, and diluted EPS of `$14.66`.
- The business mix matters more than the top line alone. Ratings, Market Intelligence, Commodity Insights, and Indices each contribute meaningful fee pools tied to different types of financial activity.
- This is one of the clearest names in the repo for understanding how benchmarks, ratings, indices, and information products monetize decision-making itself.
- The annual and quarterly materials also sit inside a live portfolio transition, with Mobility separating in `2026`, which makes S&P Global even more concentrated around its highest-value information and ratings assets.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: 2Q26

- Results: revenue of `$4.146 billion`, operating income of `$1.812 billion`, net income of `$1.217 billion`, and diluted EPS of `$4.12`.
- Official IR overview metrics: revenue `+11%`, adjusted operating profit margin `+200 bps`, adjusted diluted EPS `+23%`.
- Interpretation: the company is still demonstrating strong operating leverage in its data, ratings, and benchmark businesses even after the Mobility separation.

### Quarter minus 1: 1Q26

- Results: revenue of `$4.171 billion`, operating income of `$2.002 billion`, net income of `$1.395 billion`, and diluted EPS of `$4.69`.
- Business readthrough: Ratings and benchmark-linked franchises remained strong, while the broader portfolio still reflected workflow, data, and market-activity exposure rather than direct balance-sheet risk.

### Quarter minus 2: 4Q25

- Results: full-year revenue of `$15.336 billion`, net income of `$4.471 billion`, and diluted EPS of `$14.66`; official company materials frame `2025` as another year of strong earnings growth and high-value recurring information revenue.
- Strategic message: S&P Global is monetizing financial decision infrastructure through ratings, indices, commodity pricing, market intelligence, and analytics rather than through lending or underwriting risk.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: S&P Global adds ratings, benchmarks, and data-workflow economics to the financial archive.
- Infrastructure: this is another hidden-finance operating-system company, but unlike CME it monetizes information, models, indices, pricing, and ratings rather than trade execution and clearing.
- Regulation and trust: credibility, market structure, benchmark reliance, and rating authority are core economic assets.
- Technology: the moat is a combination of proprietary data, benchmark position, embedded workflows, and decision support tools.
- Competition: recurring subscriptions, ratings share, commodity-price benchmarks, and index licensing matter more here than balance-sheet scale.
