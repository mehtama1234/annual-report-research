## What changed

- Citi now gives the financial sample a third money-center bank with a more explicitly cross-border and network-driven strategy than BAC, and a more transformation-heavy narrative than either BAC or JPM.
- The `2025` annual report shows revenue of `$85.2 billion`, net income of `$14.3 billion`, EPS of `$6.99`, RoTCE of `7.7%`, and CET1 of `13.2%`, with adjusted revenue up `7%` and record revenue across all five businesses.
- Management frames the core franchise around `Services`, `Markets`, `Banking`, `Wealth`, and `U.S. Personal Banking`, with the global network, payments, liquidity management, and capital-markets connectivity positioned as the differentiating asset.
- Citi’s annual and quarterly materials are unusually explicit that the operating story is shifting from remediation to performance: transformation programs are moving toward target state while management tries to convert that cleanup into better returns and lower friction.
- The quarterlies reinforce the same structure. `1Q26` reported revenue of `$24.6 billion`, net income of `$5.8 billion`, EPS of `$3.06`, and RoTCE of `13.1%`; `2Q26` reported revenue of `$24.8 billion`, net income of `$5.8 billion`, EPS of `$3.15`, and RoTCE of `13.0%`.
- Citi is also one of the clearest financial names in the archive on AI as operating infrastructure: management ties AI to coding capacity, onboarding, underwriting, client service, advisor workflows, and broader productivity while continuing to emphasize digital payments and tokenized-cash rails.

## Open questions

- Does Citi’s stronger cross-border and Services-heavy mix make it more resilient than BAC or simply more exposed to geopolitical and trade-route volatility?
- How much of the recent return improvement is durable operating leverage versus the easier phase of exiting noncore assets and winding down transformation spend?
