## What changed

- Chubb adds a third distinct Financial-sector model to the archive: insurance underwriting plus investment-float economics rather than deposit-spread banking or premium closed-loop payments.
- The `2025` annual and fourth-quarter materials show year net income of `$10.31 billion`, core operating income of `$9.95 billion`, core operating EPS of `$24.79`, consolidated net premiums written of `$54.8 billion`, and a record full-year P&C combined ratio of `85.7%`.
- Management consistently frames earnings through three engines: P&C underwriting income, investment income, and life income. That makes the company a useful bridge between traditional risk pricing, capital management, and asset-income leverage.
- The quarterlies show that underwriting discipline is still intact even while management acknowledges softer conditions in some property, excess and surplus, and financial-lines markets.
- `1Q26` reported total net premiums written above `$14 billion`, up `10.7%`, P&C underwriting income of `$1.8 billion`, a combined ratio of `84.0%`, and adjusted net investment income of `$1.84 billion`.
- `2Q26` reported net income of `$2.85 billion`, core operating income of `$2.84 billion`, EPS of `$7.30`, core operating EPS of `$7.26`, net premiums written of `$14.7 billion`, a combined ratio of `83.8%`, and record adjusted net investment income of `$1.88 billion`.
- Chubb is also a clean signal on financial-market structure: catastrophe exposure, reserve development, reinsurance, pricing cycles, and fixed-income portfolio yield all matter materially to near-term earnings.

## Open questions

- How far does pricing softness extend across commercial property, E&S property, and financial lines if competition keeps increasing?
- Does the current investment-income tailwind remain strong if rates fall or reinvestment yields compress?
