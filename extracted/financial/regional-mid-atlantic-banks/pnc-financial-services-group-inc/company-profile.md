## What changed

- PNC brings a larger, more diversified bank balance sheet into CLI 6 rather than a smaller pure relationship-bank read.
- The business still depends on classic banking burdens: deposits, loan growth, integration, credit, liquidity, and capital rules.
- The positive version of the story is clear in `1Q26` and `2Q26`: size, branch density, treasury management, and fee revenue can still produce very large earnings.
- The negative constraint is also clear: this resilience still sits on top of a heavily supervised balance sheet rather than a clean fee-only model.
- CLI 6 role: PNC is the useful contrast point that keeps the lane honest about what direct balance-sheet intermediation looks like next to brokers, REITs, and insurers.

## Open questions

- How durable is the recent earnings strength once acquisition effects normalize and if credit conditions soften?
- Does PNC meaningfully narrow the quality gap to lighter intermediation models, or does the regulatory and funding burden eventually dominate the comparison?
