## Annual report takeaways

- AGNC gives CLI 6 the missing second pure mortgage-REIT anchor against `Annaly`, which matters because it lets the archive compare spread-risk, leverage, repo funding, and agency-MBS portfolio management across two real mortgage-REIT operators rather than treating the bucket as a single-name curiosity.
- The `2025` read is not about conventional property ownership. It is about leveraged balance-sheet management around Agency residential mortgage-backed securities, TBA positions, hedging, repo funding, and dividend-bearing equity.
- The annual filing said AGNC's investment portfolio totaled about `$94.8B` at December 31, `2025`, up from about `$73.3B` a year earlier, with roughly `95%` of the portfolio in 30-year fixed-rate Agency RMBS and TBAs.
- `2025` economic return on tangible common equity was about `22.7%`; fiscal `2025` net spread and dollar roll income available to common stockholders was about `$1.535B`, or about `$1.50` per diluted share.
- Compared with a bank or insurer, AGNC is a more explicit funding-and-spread machine: the quality of the model depends on book value protection, hedge ratio discipline, prepayment behavior, repo access, and the ability to earn spread income without letting leverage and duration risk overwhelm shareholder equity.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: net income available to common stockholders was about `$610M`, comprehensive income available to common stockholders was about `$603M`, and both net income and comprehensive income were about `$0.52` per common share.
- Operating detail: tangible net book value per common share rose to about `$8.58` from about `$8.38` at March 31, `2026`; economic return on tangible common equity was about `6.7%`; and net spread and dollar roll income available to common stockholders was about `$462M`, or about `$0.40` per diluted share.
- Portfolio and leverage signal: the investment portfolio was about `$97.2B` at June 30, `2026`; `at risk` leverage was about `7.4x`; and the company declared about `$0.36` per common share of dividends for the quarter.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` shows why mortgage REITs belong in CLI 6. The business can produce a strong quarter not because housing demand is simple, but because the manager correctly handles spread, hedge, funding, and book-value dynamics inside a levered mortgage portfolio.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: comprehensive loss available to common stockholders was about `$(200)M`, or about `$(0.18)` per common share, while net loss available to common stockholders was about `$(192)M`, or about `$(0.17)` per common share.
- Operating detail: tangible net book value per common share fell to about `$8.38` from about `$8.88` at year-end `2025`; economic return on tangible common equity was about `-1.6%`; and net spread and dollar roll income available to common stockholders was about `$475M`, or about `$0.42` per diluted share.
- Portfolio and leverage signal: the investment portfolio totaled about `$94.7B` at March 31, `2026`, with about `$84.4B` of Agency MBS and about `$9.5B` of net TBA securities; `at risk` leverage was about `7.4x`.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` makes the model's fragility clear. Spread income can stay positive while mark-to-market and book-value pressure still create a negative economic-return quarter. That is exactly the kind of balance-sheet-heavy burden CLI 6 is supposed to document.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: the company said `2025` economic return on tangible common equity was about `22.7%`, and `2025` total stock return with dividends reinvested was about `34.8%`.
- Full-year readthrough: the year closed with a portfolio of about `$94.8B`, tangible net book value `at risk` leverage of about `7.2x`, and `2025` net spread and dollar roll income available to common stockholders of about `$1.535B`.
- Strategic read: `Q4 2025` anchors AGNC as a strong second mortgage-REIT comparison because it shows a period when the levered agency-MBS model worked unusually well, which gives the archive something to compare against more pressured quarters and against other spread-risk financial structures.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and societal pattern: AGNC monetizes the financial plumbing around U.S. housing finance rather than household demand directly. It sits one layer removed from the borrower, earning from market structure rather than from rent collection or homebuilding.
- Industrial pressure: funding costs, hedge effectiveness, prepayment speeds, mortgage spreads, TBA market conditions, and book-value sensitivity matter far more than simple top-line growth.
- Cultural shift: the company reinforces that a large part of the economy depends on institutions that absorb and price duration, convexity, and spread risk that ordinary households never see directly.
- Bigger-picture read: AGNC matters because it gives CLI 6 a cleaner example of a business where balance-sheet engineering, funding access, and risk management are the product. That is a different kind of infrastructure from banking, brokerage, or property REIT ownership, but it is still part of the archive's capital-structure layer.
