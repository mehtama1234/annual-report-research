## Annual report takeaways

- WaFd gives CLI 6 the missing thrift-style and savings-and-loans analog, but the more important read is that this is not a nostalgic household-savings institution frozen in old form. It is a former thrift trying to turn itself into a more business-oriented western regional bank.
- That category drift is visible in the evidence itself. AnnualReports still places WaFd under `Savings & Loans`, while the SEC filing set classifies the company under `National Commercial Banks`, and management repeatedly frames `2025` as a business-model shift away from mortgage lending and toward business banking.
- Fiscal `2025` net income was about `$226M`, or about `$2.63` per diluted common share. Return on average assets was about `0.84%` and return on tangible common equity was about `9.20%`.
- At September 30, `2025`, total assets were about `$26.7B`, deposits were about `$21.4B`, and borrowings had fallen to about `$1.8B` from about `$3.3B` a year earlier. Transaction deposits rose while time deposits declined, which matters because deposit mix improvement is part of the strategic thesis.
- The annual message was not just about reporting another year of profits. Management explicitly described a large shift in `2025` from mortgage lending toward providing solutions for businesses, and argued that this should make the branch system more relevant and improve long-term shareholder returns.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q3 FY2026 ended 2026-06-30

- Results: quarterly net income was about `$66.1M`, and diluted earnings per common share were about `$0.84`, up from about `$0.82` in the prior quarter and about `$0.73` in the year-ago quarter.
- Operating detail: return on tangible common equity reached about `11.0%`; net interest income was about `$181M`; net interest margin held at about `2.81%`; and the efficiency ratio improved to about `53.69%`.
- Balance-sheet read: total assets were about `$27.6B`; total deposits were about `$20.9B`; loans receivable were about `$20.0B`; and borrowings plus junior subordinated debentures were about `$3.3B`.
- Structural read: this quarter shows why WaFd belongs in the archive. The company is trying to prove that a thrift-like deposit franchise can be retooled into a higher-relevance commercial relationship machine without losing earnings discipline.

### Quarter minus 1: Q2 FY2026 ended 2026-03-31

- Results: quarterly net income was about `$65.5M`, and diluted earnings per common share were about `$0.82`, up from about `$0.79` in `Q1 FY2026` and about `$0.65` in the year-ago quarter.
- Operating detail: return on tangible common equity was about `10.8%`; net interest income rose to about `$178M`; and net interest margin widened to about `2.81%` from about `2.70%` in the prior quarter.
- Credit and activity read: non-performing assets improved to about `0.48%` of total assets from about `0.75%` in `Q1`, while delinquencies fell to about `0.78%` of total loans from about `1.07%`. Management said active loan segments grew at a `12%` annualized rate.
- Capital and balance-sheet signal: total assets were about `$27.6B`, total deposits were about `$21.1B`, and borrowings plus junior subordinated debentures were about `$3.1B`. The company also repurchased about `2.74M` shares during the quarter.

### Quarter minus 2: Q1 FY2026 ended 2025-12-31

- Results: quarterly net income was about `$64.2M`, and diluted earnings per common share were about `$0.79`, up from about `$0.72` in `Q4 FY2025` and about `$0.54` in the year-ago quarter.
- Operating detail: return on tangible common equity was about `10.6%`; net interest income was about `$171M`; net interest margin was about `2.70%`; and the efficiency ratio improved to about `55.25%`.
- Balance-sheet and credit read: total assets were about `$27.3B`, deposits were about `$21.4B`, and loans-to-customer-deposits improved to about `92.67%`. At the same time, non-performing assets rose to about `0.75%` of total assets and delinquencies rose to about `1.07%` of total loans.
- Strategic read: `Q1 FY2026` is useful because it shows both sides of the story. Share repurchases, stronger EPS, and a better efficiency ratio support management's repositioning case, but the quarter also shows that the loan book still needs active monitoring while the franchise changes shape.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and societal pattern: WaFd sits where household savings behavior, branch trust, small-business borrowing, commercial real estate, and regional credit formation meet. It is a useful reminder that consumer finance habits still feed local business systems and property activity.
- Industrial pressure: deposit mix, loan demand, branch relevance, credit quality, margin management, and funding costs matter more than flashy growth narratives.
- Cultural shift: the company shows how an old thrift identity gets remade under pressure. Management is not selling nostalgia. It is trying to preserve the trust and funding base of a thrift while moving the earnings engine toward business customers and higher-value relationship banking.
- Bigger-picture read: WaFd matters because it adds a missing capital-structure layer between mortgage REIT carry and larger regional-bank scale. It shows a smaller, branch-based, deposit-funded model that still has to decide whether it is mainly a household savings institution, a real-estate lender, or a commercial relationship bank.
