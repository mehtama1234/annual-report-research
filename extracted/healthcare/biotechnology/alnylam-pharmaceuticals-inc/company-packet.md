# Alnylam Pharmaceuticals, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Alnylam Pharmaceuticals, Inc.
- Sector: Healthcare
- Industry: Biotechnology
- Ticker: ALNY
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Alnylam expands the healthcare-frontier archive into a more concentrated and platform-defined biotechnology model than the current Regeneron packet.
- The `2025` annual record shows why the company matters: it is a commercial RNAi therapeutics business with real scale, but the current value story is increasingly dominated by one franchise, one disease complex, and the need to keep future pipeline waves credible.
- This makes Alnylam one of the strongest healthcare names in the archive for understanding how a biotech can move from development narrative into real operating leverage without escaping concentration risk.
- The annual evidence also sharpens a key archive pattern: breakthrough science can produce durable recurring revenue only if commercialization, reimbursement, and next-generation pipeline planning stay aligned.
- Alnylam is especially useful because it shows a different flavor of frontier-healthcare monetization from Regeneron. The moat is not only IP around a single molecule family, but a broader RNAi platform trying to scale across multiple indications while one flagship franchise does most of the current financial work.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` TTR revenues reached about `$1.03 billion`; `AMVUTTRA` and `ONPATTRO` contributed about `$1.012 billion` and `$18 million`; Rare franchise revenue from `GIVLAARI` and `OXLUMO` reached about `$142 million`.
- Guidance: management lowered TTR product-sales guidance after early launch learnings in ATTR-CM, even while emphasizing the long-term strength of the franchise.
- Management message: the quarter framed Alnylam around continued TTR leadership, expanding diagnosis and treatment infrastructure in ATTR amyloidosis, and a still-advancing high-value pipeline.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest quarter for seeing the tension inside the story. The business is scaling fast, but it is already having to prove that blockbuster growth can normalize without breaking the long-term thesis.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` global net product revenues reached `$1.036 billion`, up `121%`; total TTR revenues reached `$910 million`, up `153%`.
- Guidance: Q1 reiterated combined net product revenue guidance of `$4.9-$5.3 billion` and total TTR net product revenue guidance of `$4.4-$4.7 billion`.
- Management message: the quarter emphasized that Alnylam had crossed `$1 billion` in quarterly product revenues for the first time while continuing to advance a deep RNAi pipeline toward multiple `2026` clinical readouts.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the cleanest quarter for seeing Alnylam at peak confidence, when commercial scale, pipeline optionality, and platform identity all reinforce each other.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` global net product revenues reached `$995 million`; full-year net product revenues reached `$2.987 billion`, up `81%`; the company attained GAAP and non-GAAP profitability for full-year `2025`.
- Guidance: the company reiterated net-product sales guidance while adding broader `2026` financial framing and explicit pipeline goals.
- Management message: the quarter centered on the landmark `AMVUTTRA` approval in ATTR-CM, the launch of the `Alnylam 2030` strategy, and multiple upcoming pipeline milestones.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the bridge quarter where Alnylam stops looking like a pre-scale biotech and starts looking like a concentrated specialty-therapeutics company with real operating leverage and real franchise risk.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Alnylam is one of the best packets in the current healthcare set for understanding how biotech risk changes once commercial success arrives. It does not disappear; it concentrates.
- Consumer: there is limited direct consumer behavior here, but patient adoption and referral behavior still matter indirectly because ATTR-CM diagnosis rates, physician awareness, and treatment sequencing determine how much of the market becomes visible.
- Cultural: the company reflects a broader healthcare shift toward precision specialty treatment, earlier disease recognition, and willingness to use new modalities when clinical differentiation is clear.
- Societal: the business is tied to underdiagnosed disease, specialty access, and whether health systems can identify and route patients into newer therapies quickly enough.
- Regulatory: label expansion, approvals across geographies, reimbursement durability, and competitive positioning remain core to value capture.
- Technology: Alnylam’s edge is genuinely platform-shaped. The RNAi identity matters, but it still has to convert platform promise into repeatable commercial products rather than only scientific prestige.
- Industrial: launch execution, payer access, diagnostic intensity in ATTR-CM, geographic mix, and pipeline readout cadence matter more than simple top-line growth alone.
- Capital allocation: Alnylam is a strong example of a biotech trying to balance current commercialization with future optionality. Profitability improved, but the model still depends on disciplined reinvestment into pipeline programs and franchise defense.

## Thematic interpretation

- Alnylam helps explain a larger healthcare pattern: frontier-biotech upside increasingly depends on what happens after approval, not just before it. The major challenge is turning a scientific platform into a durable treatment system and then replenishing it before concentration risk hardens.
- The company also shows why franchise and IP monetization should be treated as a core output of the archive, not a side note. The current economics are being driven heavily by one TTR franchise, which is precisely what creates both extraordinary upside and future vulnerability.
- A second repeating pattern is that not all biotech risk looks binary in the same way. Regeneron shows concentration inside an established blockbuster set. Alnylam shows the earlier stage of concentration, where one recent launch can reset the whole income statement and still leave launch-durability questions unresolved.
- Alnylam adds a useful contrast to the infrastructure-heavy healthcare names. Unlike Quest, Labcorp, Option Care, or West, this business does not earn mainly from repeated service demand or embedded workflow. It earns from therapy adoption, franchise expansion, and the ability of a platform to keep generating commercially meaningful products.
- The final cross-company theme is that frontier healthcare runs on linked layers: diagnostics, validation, delivery, monitoring, and therapy. Alnylam is the clearest packet in the current set for the therapy-franchise and platform-science layer once it starts operating at scale.
