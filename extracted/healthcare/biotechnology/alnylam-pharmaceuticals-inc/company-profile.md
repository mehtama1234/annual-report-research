## What changed

- Revenue: Alnylam entered `2026` with a transformed revenue base, driven primarily by the TTR franchise and especially by `AMVUTTRA` in ATTR-CM.
- Mix: the company’s economics are increasingly concentrated in one growth engine. The TTR franchise now overwhelms the rest of the portfolio, while the Rare franchise remains meaningful but secondary.
- Guidance: the year began with confidence around combined net product revenues of `$4.9-$5.3 billion`, but Q2 introduced a more nuanced read when management lowered TTR product-sales guidance to reflect learnings from the early ATTR-CM launch phase.
- Strategic read: Alnylam is one of the best frontier-healthcare packets for understanding a biotech that is no longer purely pipeline-driven in the abstract, yet still remains highly exposed to franchise concentration, launch durability, and pipeline replenishment.

## Open questions

- How durable is AMVUTTRA’s ATTR-CM growth once early pent-up demand is absorbed and the market normalizes?
- Does Alnylam’s current revenue concentration create a future fragility similar to other franchise-heavy biotech stories?
- Can the broader RNAi pipeline add enough second-wave growth to keep the company from becoming too dependent on one therapeutic platform and one disease cluster?
