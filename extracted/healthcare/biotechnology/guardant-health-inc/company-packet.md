# Guardant Health Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Guardant Health Inc.
- Sector: Healthcare
- Industry: Biotechnology
- Ticker: GH
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Guardant expands the healthcare-frontier archive into precision-oncology information infrastructure rather than drug ownership, hospital care, or recurring service delivery.
- The `2025` annual record matters because it shows a company trying to turn cancer blood testing, biopharma data services, companion diagnostics, and large-scale screening into one recurring platform.
- This makes Guardant one of the best current healthcare packets for understanding how diagnostics can behave like a hybrid of biotech, data infrastructure, and consumer screening.
- The annual evidence also sharpens a key archive pattern: healthcare value increasingly accrues to companies that control who gets tested, when they get tested, and how test results influence treatment routing and follow-up monitoring.
- Guardant is especially useful because it combines two different profit narratives inside one company: precision oncology for specialist decision support and Shield for broad population screening.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$335.0 million`; oncology revenue was `$219.1 million`; biopharma and data revenue was `$60.9 million`; screening revenue was `$52.9 million`; Shield tests reached about `66,000`; free cash flow was about `$(69.5) million`.
- Guidance: management raised full-year `2026` revenue guidance to `$1.34 billion` to `$1.36 billion`, implying growth of `36%` to `38%`.
- Management message: the quarter emphasized broad growth across oncology and screening, FDA approvals for Guardant360 Liquid CDx, and stronger commercial validation for Shield through guideline inclusion and UnitedHealth coverage.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest quarter for seeing Guardant move beyond an oncology liquid-biopsy story into a larger screening and access platform.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$301.7 million`; oncology revenue was `$205.0 million`; biopharma and data revenue was `$53.0 million`; screening revenue was `$41.6 million`; Shield tests reached about `44,000`; free cash flow was about `$(71.2) million`.
- Guidance: management raised full-year `2026` revenue guidance to `$1.30 billion` to `$1.32 billion`, implying growth of `32%` to `34%`.
- Management message: the quarter highlighted strong oncology volumes, growing Shield adoption, a nationwide Quest collaboration, and direct-to-consumer and influencer campaigns to increase colorectal cancer screening awareness.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the best quarter for seeing how much of Guardant’s future depends on making cancer screening more behaviorally normal and commercially scalable, not just clinically useful.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$281.3 million`; oncology revenue was `$189.9 million`; biopharma and data revenue was `$54.0 million`; screening revenue was `$35.1 million`; Shield tests reached about `38,000`.
- Guidance: management entered `2026` expecting total revenue growth of `27%` to `30%`, before later upward revisions.
- Management message: the year-end release framed performance around strong growth across oncology and screening, plus rising test volume and improving non-GAAP gross margin.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the bridge quarter where Guardant most clearly looks like a commercial-scale information franchise rather than a narrower liquid-biopsy innovator.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Guardant is one of the best current packets for understanding how diagnostics, biopharma data, and screening can sit inside the healthcare frontier without being a therapy owner.
- Consumer: the strongest consumer signal is that blood-based screening is easier to adopt than more burdensome alternatives, which matters when demand depends on convenience, awareness, and willingness to act.
- Cultural: the company reflects the consumerization of cancer information, where patients increasingly expect earlier detection, lower-friction screening, and more precise treatment-selection tools.
- Societal: the business sits inside a larger social push toward earlier cancer detection, better recurrence monitoring, and shifting more decision-making toward data-rich testing workflows.
- Regulatory: FDA approvals, guideline inclusion, reimbursement expansion, companion-diagnostic wins, and commercial-insurer coverage remain central to value capture.
- Technology: the edge is a stack of proprietary tests, data sets, companion-diagnostic relationships, and analytics rather than one standalone kit.
- Industrial: test volume, average reimbursement, channel access, commercial partnerships, coverage wins, and laboratory scale matter more than simple top-line growth alone.
- Capital allocation: Guardant is a strong example of a growth company still trading current profitability for platform expansion, especially in Shield, while trying to prove the operating leverage of a scaled testing network.

## Thematic interpretation

- Guardant helps explain a larger healthcare pattern: some of the most powerful healthcare businesses do not own the therapy, but instead own the information layer that determines who gets screened, diagnosed, monitored, or matched to treatment.
- The company also shows why diagnostics should not be treated as a single lane. `Labcorp` and `Quest` are broad national infrastructure networks. `Natera` is a narrower molecular franchise. Guardant sits closer to precision oncology routing plus the early-stage consumerization of cancer screening.
- A second repeating pattern is that healthcare consumerization increasingly includes tests, not just devices or apps. Shield matters because it tries to reduce the friction of colorectal cancer screening in the same way consumer technology reduces friction in other categories.
- Guardant adds a useful cultural and societal lens because it ties demand to fear, reassurance, convenience, and earlier action. That makes parts of cancer care behave more like information markets than traditional provider markets.
- The final cross-company lesson is that healthcare’s frontier increasingly depends on repeated data generation. The recurring value is not only in treatment administration or device consumables. It is also in ongoing molecular reading, screening cadence, and companion-diagnostic relevance across more points of care.
