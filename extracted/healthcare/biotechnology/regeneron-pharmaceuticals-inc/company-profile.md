## What changed

- Revenue: the core story is acceleration from `Q4 2025` into `Q1 2026` and `Q2 2026`, driven by Dupixent, EYLEA HD, and broader commercial execution.
- Margin and EPS: earnings remain powerful but can move sharply with acquired IPR&D and other development spending, which matters for reading franchise quality versus reported-period volatility.
- Capital allocation: the company initiated a quarterly dividend in the `Q4 2025` reporting cycle and also increased share-repurchase capacity, showing that the cash profile is strong enough to support both pipeline funding and shareholder return.
- Franchise mix: the business is still heavily shaped by a small number of large immunology and ophthalmology franchises, which makes concentration a central feature rather than a side risk.
- Operating issues: EYLEA franchise transition pressure is still real even as EYLEA HD expands, so biologics lifecycle management remains one of the most important operating questions in the packet.
- Strategic read: Regeneron is not just a “drug company” placeholder. It is a clean case for franchise economics, label expansion, pipeline intensity, and the way a biotechnology model can become both highly profitable and highly concentration-sensitive.

## Open questions

- How durable is current Dupixent-led growth if the business remains economically concentrated in a handful of major franchises?
- Does EYLEA HD offset enough of the legacy EYLEA pressure to keep ophthalmology a durable earnings pillar?
- How much of current EPS strength should be treated as recurring operating quality versus period-specific pipeline and IPR&D noise?
