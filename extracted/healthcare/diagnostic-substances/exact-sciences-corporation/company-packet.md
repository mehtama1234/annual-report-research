# Exact Sciences Corporation Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Exact Sciences Corporation
- Sector: Healthcare
- Industry: Diagnostic Substances
- Ticker: EXAS
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q4 2025
  - quarter 2: Q3 2025
  - quarter 3: Q2 2025
- Quarter-window note: these are the latest three reported quarters as of `2026-08-10` because the Abbott merger closed on `March 23, 2026`.

## Annual report takeaways

- Exact Sciences expands the healthcare-frontier archive into a more consumer-shaped diagnostics model than `Quest`, `Labcorp`, `Natera`, or `Guardant`.
- The `2025` annual record matters because it shows a business trying to own screening behavior, not just sell tests. `Cologuard`, `Cologuard Plus`, `Oncodetect`, and `Cancerguard` together make the company a platform for getting people tested earlier, more conveniently, and more often.
- This makes Exact one of the strongest healthcare names in the archive for understanding how diagnostics can function like a demand-creation category rather than a passive lab service.
- The annual evidence also sharpens a key archive pattern: lower-friction healthcare formats can unlock growth when they fit around patient avoidance, physician workflow, payer coverage, and channel access.
- Exact is especially useful because it sits at the boundary between established screening franchise economics and more speculative future layers like blood-based screening and multi-cancer early detection.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent reported quarter

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$878 million`; screening revenue was `$695 million`; precision oncology revenue was `$183 million`; full-year `2025` revenue reached `$3.25 billion`.
- Guidance: Q4 framed the business around continued portfolio expansion and stronger growth, but it was also the final reported quarter before the merger close on `March 23, 2026`.
- Management message: the quarter emphasized that Exact had screened more people than ever, launched three new tests in `2025`, and expanded into blood-based CRC screening through the Freenome license.
- Structural themes: Q4 is the clearest quarter for seeing Exact as a commercial-scale screening-and-diagnostics platform whose value comes from demand formation, channel access, and product-stack expansion.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q3 `2025` revenue was `$851 million`; screening revenue was `$666 million`; precision oncology revenue was `$184 million`; adjusted EBITDA was `$135 million`; operating cash flow was `$220 million`; free cash flow was `$190 million`.
- Guidance: management raised full-year `2025` revenue and adjusted EBITDA guidance again.
- Management message: the quarter centered on the launch of `Cancerguard`, the company’s multi-cancer early detection test, plus a Quest Diagnostics collection partnership to expand patient access.
- Structural themes: Q3 is the best quarter for seeing how Exact tried to turn cancer screening into a broader behavior and access system rather than a single-product franchise.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q2 `2025` revenue was `$811 million`; screening revenue was `$628 million`; precision oncology revenue was `$183 million`; adjusted EBITDA was `$138 million`; operating cash flow was `$89 million`; free cash flow was `$47 million`.
- Guidance: management raised full-year `2025` revenue and adjusted EBITDA guidance midpoints by about `$55 million` and `$25 million`, respectively.
- Management message: the quarter emphasized strong `Cologuard Plus` launch momentum and Medicare coverage for `Oncodetect`.
- Structural themes: Q2 is the clearest quarter for seeing Exact’s diagnostic stack widen from stool-based screening into residual-disease monitoring and a fuller portfolio of recurring cancer-information products.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Exact is one of the strongest packets in the current healthcare set for understanding how diagnostics can create their own demand rather than only respond to physician orders.
- Consumer: this is one of the clearest healthcare packets where consumer friction matters directly, because screening adoption depends on whether the test feels easy enough, familiar enough, and trustworthy enough to overcome avoidance behavior.
- Cultural: the company reflects a broader shift toward earlier detection, more acceptable at-home or lower-friction testing, and more willingness to treat health information as something patients actively seek rather than passively receive.
- Societal: Exact sits inside powerful public-health pressures including under-screening, younger-age colorectal cancer concern, earlier-intervention messaging, and the push to normalize routine cancer detection before symptoms appear.
- Regulatory: Medicare coverage, guideline inclusion, laboratory-developed-test status, potential FDA pathways, and broader payer adoption remain central to monetization.
- Technology: the moat is not only assay science. It is the combination of biomarkers, test format, commercialization, patient-routing infrastructure, and provider access points.
- Industrial: volume growth, reimbursement, test mix, commercial execution, collection-network reach, and marketing efficiency matter more than simple laboratory capacity alone.
- Capital allocation: Exact shows how a diagnostics business can reinvest aggressively into adjacent screening and oncology products while trying to widen a strong incumbent franchise.

## Thematic interpretation

- Exact helps explain a larger healthcare pattern: some of the highest-value healthcare categories are becoming closer to consumer-behavior businesses, where convenience and compliance are as important as clinical utility.
- The company also makes the user’s “consumer and cultural shifts” requirement concrete. Colorectal screening is not only a medical need. It is a behavior problem shaped by discomfort, avoidance, time burden, fear, and the willingness to act before symptoms appear. A test that reduces friction changes economics because it changes participation.
- A second repeating pattern is that healthcare value increasingly goes to companies that control when people enter the care pathway. `Quest` and `Labcorp` own broad testing infrastructure. `Natera` and `Guardant` own narrower precision-information layers. Exact is the best packet for owning the front door of screening behavior itself.
- Exact adds a useful comparison against `Guardant`. Guardant’s screening story is more blood-based, oncology-adjacent, and still scaling. Exact’s story is more established, broader in public-health footprint, and more visibly tied to large-scale patient adoption and payer normalization.
- The final cross-company lesson is that diagnostics can monetize not only specialist decision support, but also participation. If a company can get more people screened by making testing easier, more distributed, and more culturally acceptable, it can build a durable franchise before treatment even begins.
