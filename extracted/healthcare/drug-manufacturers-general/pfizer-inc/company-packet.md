# Pfizer Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-08

## Snapshot

- Company: Pfizer Inc.
- Sector: Healthcare
- Industry: Drug Manufacturers - General
- Ticker: PFE
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Pfizer gives the healthcare set a large-cap biopharma read that is structurally different from insurers, providers, or devices. The central issue is not simple healthcare demand. It is portfolio replacement: how quickly Pfizer can offset the COVID unwind with oncology, specialty care, vaccines outside the COVID franchise, and acquired or launched assets.
- The 2025 annual materials show a business that is still in transition but no longer purely shrinking. Full-year 2025 revenue was `$62.579 billion`, and management highlighted `6%` operational growth for the non-COVID portfolio.
- The company repeatedly points to focused commercial execution, late-stage pipeline depth, oncology expansion, and the enterprise-wide cost realignment program. This is a science-and-productivity story at the same time.
- Pfizer is especially useful for theme work because it shows how healthcare can post decent underlying operating performance even while reported optics are still distorted by a large product-cycle reset.
- This is not a defensive-stability story in the same way that managed care would be. Patent cliffs, product mix, clinical execution, government pricing pressure, and acquisition integration are all first-order variables here.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 2026 revenue was `$15.0 billion`, representing `1%` year-over-year operational growth; excluding Comirnaty and Paxlovid, revenue grew `5%` operationally. Adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.77`.
- Guidance: on August 4, 2026 Pfizer raised the midpoint of full-year 2026 revenue guidance by `$500 million` to `$60.5 billion` to `$62.5 billion` and reaffirmed adjusted diluted EPS guidance of `$2.80` to `$3.00`.
- Management message: management emphasized `18%` operational growth from launched and acquired products, continued oncology strength, and the expansion of productivity initiatives.
- Structural themes: the key read is that Pfizer is increasingly being carried by non-COVID portfolio execution plus cost discipline, not by a return of the old COVID economics.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 2026 revenue was `$14.5 billion`, with `2%` operational growth; excluding Comirnaty and Paxlovid, revenue grew `7%` operationally. Adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.75`.
- Guidance: management reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance of `$59.5 billion` to `$62.5 billion` in revenue and `$2.80` to `$3.00` in adjusted diluted EPS.
- Management message: management highlighted `22%` operational growth from launched and acquired products and called out momentum in oncology and obesity pipeline areas.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows the cleanest early-2026 proof that the underlying business is stronger than the headline COVID drag would suggest.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 2025 revenue was `$17.557 billion`, down `1%` reported, while full-year 2025 revenue reached `$62.579 billion`. Q4 adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.66`, and full-year adjusted diluted EPS was `$3.22`.
- Guidance: management reaffirmed all components of full-year 2026 guidance, including revenue of `$59.5 billion` to `$62.5 billion` and adjusted diluted EPS of `$2.80` to `$3.00`.
- Management message: management said non-COVID performance, financial discipline, and pipeline progress had strengthened the company heading into 2026.
- Structural themes: this quarter closes the book on the post-COVID reset phase and sets the baseline for 2026 as a portfolio-rebuild year, not merely a decline year.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Pfizer shows that healthcare earnings are heavily shaped by portfolio cycles, pipeline execution, and pricing and reimbursement structure, not just by steady end demand.
- Consumer: direct consumer behavior is not the main signal; payer and prescriber dynamics, vaccination patterns, and treatment uptake matter more than classic discretionary spending behavior.
- Cultural: trust in vaccines and treatment adoption still matters, but the more important cultural and strategic layer is whether innovation can replace fading blockbusters quickly enough.
- Regulatory: healthcare policy is a live earnings variable here through IRA redesign, pricing pressure, and product-approval timing.
- Labor: labor is not the main framing variable in the current evidence set; R&D deployment and operating-efficiency redesign matter more.
- Technology: Pfizer's story is still science- and pipeline-led, but digital enablement and productivity improvements are increasingly part of the margin story.
- Capital allocation: cost realignment, pipeline investment, and acquisition integration are more important than a simple cash-return framing.
