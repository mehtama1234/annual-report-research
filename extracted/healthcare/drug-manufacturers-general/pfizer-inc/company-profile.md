## What changed

- Revenue: full-year 2025 revenues were `$62.579 billion`, down `2%` reported year over year, but Pfizer said non-COVID portfolio growth was much stronger. Q4 2025 revenue was `$17.557 billion`, Q1 2026 revenue was `$14.5 billion`, and Q2 2026 revenue was `$15.0 billion`.
- Growth quality: Pfizer's key reframing is that the business should now be read as ex-COVID plus launched-and-acquired growth rather than as a COVID unwind story alone. Full-year 2025 non-COVID operational revenue grew `6%`; Q1 2026 revenue excluding Comirnaty and Paxlovid grew `7%` operationally; and Q2 2026 revenue excluding those two products grew `5%` operationally.
- Earnings: full-year 2025 adjusted diluted EPS was `$3.22`; Q4 2025 adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.66`; Q1 2026 adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.75`; and Q2 2026 adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.77`.
- Portfolio mix: growth is increasingly tied to launched and acquired products, oncology, vaccines outside the core COVID franchise, and specialty-care assets. Q1 2026 launched-and-acquired products grew `22%` operationally and Q2 2026 launched-and-acquired products grew `18%` operationally.
- Oncology: oncology remains a major strategic engine. Q2 2026 oncology revenue was about `$4.165 billion`, with visible support from Padcev, Lorbrena, and oncology biosimilars.
- COVID reset: Comirnaty and Paxlovid remain large drags. Management repeatedly frames underlying momentum net of those products, which means Pfizer still carries a visible transition burden even as the rest of the portfolio improves.
- Guidance: on February 3, 2026 Pfizer reaffirmed 2026 guidance with revenue of `$59.5 billion` to `$62.5 billion` and adjusted diluted EPS of `$2.80` to `$3.00`. On August 4, 2026 Pfizer raised the midpoint of revenue guidance to `$60.5 billion` to `$62.5 billion` while reaffirming adjusted diluted EPS guidance.
- Cost structure: the enterprise-wide cost realignment program remains central. Q2 2026 materials added another `$1.0 billion` of anticipated net savings beyond earlier targets, extending savings realization into `2027` through `2029`.
- Management tone: pragmatic and transition-focused. Pfizer is not describing a clean broad-based growth cycle; it is describing a business that is replacing COVID-era earnings with oncology, specialty-care, acquired-asset, and productivity-driven support.

## Open questions

- How much of Pfizer's next leg of growth can come from oncology and launched-and-acquired assets before more loss-of-exclusivity pressure arrives?
- Does the current improvement represent durable portfolio renewal, or mainly a successful bridge away from COVID-era revenue declines?
