# Teva Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Teva Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd.
- Sector: Healthcare
- Industry: Drug Manufacturers - Other
- Ticker: TEVA
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Teva is a strong frontier-healthcare packet because it shows how global drug manufacturing economics look when affordability, scale, and portfolio transition all matter at once.
- The annual evidence makes clear that Teva is no longer just a generic-volume company. It is trying to preserve global generics scale while shifting the earnings center toward branded neuroscience, immunology, and long-acting or differentiated assets.
- This matters because the frontier-healthcare brief is not only about scientific breakthrough. It is also about what happens when huge treatment systems depend on lower-cost access, manufacturing footprint discipline, and margin repair.
- The annual read also highlights a recurring pattern across healthcare: old profit pools decay while newer, more defensible growth engines require more investment, sharper execution, and cleaner capital allocation.
- Teva therefore improves both the `Drug Manufacturers - Other` and `Drugs - Generic` lenses at the same time.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was about `$4.1 billion`; management raised outlook for all three key innovative brands and now expects their combined `2026` revenue around `$3.7 billion`.
- Guidance: the company’s most important guidance move was not broad sector language, but a raised outlook around the innovative portfolio.
- Management message: Teva kept emphasizing execution of its “pivot to growth” strategy rather than defending generics as the sole engine.
- Structural themes: generics still provide reach and scale, but branded and biosimilar growth increasingly determine whether the model improves or merely stabilizes.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was about `$4.0 billion`, up `2%`, and key innovative brands grew `41%` to `$838 million`.
- Guidance: the quarter reinforced that the branded growth set is meant to become a larger share of the company over time.
- Management message: management framed the quarter as strong execution despite lower lenalidomide contribution and broader generic competition.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest quarter for seeing how a former pure-scale manufacturer tries to become more mix-driven and less exposed to commodity price pressure.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$4,711 million`, up `11%`, and management described the year as a third consecutive year of growth.
- Guidance: the year-end quarter positioned `2026` around continued innovative-brand growth, transformation savings, and better portfolio quality.
- Management message: management highlighted higher AUSTEDO revenues, improving innovative mix, and continued transformation work.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the bridge quarter where Teva looks less like a repair story and more like a transition story, but the transition is still incomplete.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Teva is the best current packet for understanding how healthcare scale can coexist with persistent portfolio repair pressure.
- Consumer: the relevant demand signal is affordability and broad drug access, especially when generic availability remains central to system-wide treatment use.
- Cultural: healthcare systems still depend heavily on lower-cost medicine access, but the profit is increasingly pushed toward more differentiated therapies and delivery forms.
- Regulatory: generic competition, pricing pressure, biosimilar approvals, patent exposure, and market-access rules remain first-order variables.
- Labor: manufacturing, regulatory, and commercial execution matter directly because a company of this scale cannot repair itself through narrative alone.
- Technology: the technology edge is increasingly tied to complex generics, biosimilars, long-acting formulations, and portfolio management rather than simple tablet volume.
- Capital allocation: Teva is a useful case for capital-discipline analysis because debt, transformation costs, R&D spending, and mix improvement all compete for room on the same balance sheet.
