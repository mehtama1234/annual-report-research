## Why this name matters

- Cardinal Health is the strongest next healthcare channel add because it deepens the institutional supply and replenishment lane with a second flagship distributor rather than another adjacent healthcare operator.
- It broadens the frontier beyond Henry Schein’s office-based procurement model. Cardinal Health sits closer to hospitals, health systems, pharmacies, ambulatory surgery centers, labs, physician offices, and home patients at far larger scale, which makes it a more system-wide read on how medical products, pharmaceuticals, and healthcare services actually move.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual and filing chain describe Cardinal Health as a global healthcare services and products company providing customized solutions for hospitals, healthcare systems, pharmacies, ambulatory surgery centers, clinical laboratories, physician offices, and patients in the home.
- The segment structure matters. Cardinal reported two major segments, `Pharmaceutical and Specialty Solutions` and `Global Medical Products and Distribution`, while `Other` included `Nuclear and Precision Health Solutions`, `at-Home Solutions`, and `OptiFreight Logistics`.
- Fiscal `2025` revenue was `$222.6 billion`. That was down `2%` as reported because of a previously communicated customer contract expiration, but management said revenue increased `18%` excluding that contract effect.
- Fiscal `2025` GAAP operating earnings were about `$2.3 billion`, non-GAAP operating earnings were about `$2.8 billion`, GAAP diluted EPS was `$6.45`, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$8.24`.
- The annual signal is that Cardinal’s moat is not only scale. It is channel control across branded and specialty pharma flow, medical-product distribution, logistics attachment, and newer service platforms that make the company more embedded in provider economics than a plain low-margin wholesaler headline suggests.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q3 FY2026

- Results: revenue increased `11%` to `$60.9 billion`. GAAP operating earnings decreased `30%` to `$509 million` and GAAP diluted EPS decreased `20%` to `$1.69`.
- Non-GAAP view: non-GAAP operating earnings increased `18%` to `$956 million` and non-GAAP diluted EPS increased `35%` to `$3.17`.
- Important distortion: GAAP results were hit by a `$184 million` pre-tax goodwill impairment in the `Navista & ION` reporting unit.
- Operating themes: Pharma segment profit increased `18%` to `$784 million`, while GMPD segment profit fell `36%` to `$25 million` because of the adverse net impact of tariffs. Other segment profit increased `34%` to `$179 million`.
- Why it matters: the quarter shows both the strength and the complexity of the model. Cardinal is benefiting from branded and specialty pharmaceutical momentum and service-platform growth, but it is also exposed to tariff pressure and business-model recalibration inside specific acquired or service-adjacent units.

### Quarter minus 1: Q2 FY2026

- Results: revenue increased `19%` to `$65.6 billion`. GAAP operating earnings increased `29%` to `$707 million` and GAAP diluted EPS increased `19%` to `$1.97`.
- Non-GAAP view: non-GAAP operating earnings increased `38%` to `$877 million` and non-GAAP diluted EPS increased `36%` to `$2.63`.
- Operating themes: gross margin increased `23%` to about `$2.4 billion`, with growth tied to MSO platform acquisitions, ADS, branded and specialty pharmaceutical products, and better contribution from the broader channel mix. Pharma segment profit increased `29%` to `$687 million`, while GMPD segment profit increased to `$37 million` and Other segment profit increased `52%` to `$179 million`.
- Why it matters: Q2 shows the service-attach logic clearly. Growth was not only in pharmaceuticals. The company was also monetizing a broader healthcare-services and logistics layer while lifting outlook.

### Quarter minus 2: Q1 FY2026

- Results: revenue increased `22%` to `$64.0 billion`. GAAP operating earnings increased `18%` to `$668 million`.
- Non-GAAP view: non-GAAP operating earnings increased `37%` to `$857 million`. GAAP diluted EPS increased `11%` to `$1.88`, and non-GAAP diluted EPS increased `36%` to `$2.55`.
- Cash and capital: net cash from operating activities was about `$1.0 billion` in the quarter even after opioid-litigation-related payments totaling `$403 million`. The company also deployed capital toward debt repayment and share repurchases.
- Why it matters: Q1 shows how much of Cardinal’s model is now supported by branded and specialty pharmaceutical demand plus acquisition-enabled service attachment, not only by legacy broadline distribution volumes.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Cardinal is not consumer-facing, but it sits directly under several large social shifts: healthcare complexity moving outward from hospitals, more care at home, more specialty-drug dependence, more fragmented provider operations, and more pressure for the healthcare system to move products and therapies with fewer mistakes and lower friction.
- The cultural story is institutional dependence. Most patients never think about Cardinal, but hospitals, pharmacies, ambulatory centers, labs, and home-care channels rely on this kind of intermediary to keep care flowing.
- The company also reflects healthcare decentralization. The business reaches physician offices and home patients, not just traditional acute-care settings, which makes it a better read on where care is operationally migrating.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Scale is an advantage, but it comes with thin-margin discipline. Mix, supplier terms, customer concentration, and reimbursement-sensitive healthcare demand all matter.
- Tariffs and medical-product costs are still real pressures, especially inside GMPD. The quarter chain makes that visible rather than letting the story collapse into “pharma strength fixes everything.”
- Acquisitions and service-platform expansion improve the quality of the moat, but they also raise integration and execution risk. The `Navista & ION` goodwill impairment is a reminder that not every service adjacency compounds cleanly.
- The model is also exposed to litigation cash demands, financing cost, and working-capital intensity in ways that make operating discipline central rather than secondary.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Healthcare channel power is broader than office-based procurement.` Henry Schein proves one version; Cardinal proves the larger institutional and system-throughput version.
- `Distribution gets stronger when service layers attach to product flow.` Cardinal’s Other segment, at-home solutions, nuclear and precision health tools, and OptiFreight logistics show how intermediaries widen beyond classic wholesaling.
- `Specialty growth raises the importance of channel intermediaries.` The more healthcare shifts toward branded and specialty therapies, the more valuable the operators become that coordinate access, movement, and provider-side throughput.
- `Institutional dependence can coexist with operating fragility.` Cardinal is mission-critical infrastructure, but the quarter chain still shows how tariffs, impairments, contract churn, and financing costs shape outcomes.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Henry Schein, Inc.`: Henry Schein explains office-based practitioner procurement and recurring medical-and-dental distribution; Cardinal explains larger institutional, pharmacy, and system-throughput healthcare intermediation.
- Against `McKesson Corporation` and `Cencora`: all three show healthcare distribution, but Cardinal adds a better mixed read across pharma, medical products, at-home solutions, and logistics attachment rather than leaning as heavily on specialty-drug flow alone.
- Against `Option Care Health, Inc.`: Option Care explains therapy routing and alternate-site delivery, while Cardinal explains the larger channel infrastructure that keeps products and provider systems supplied.
