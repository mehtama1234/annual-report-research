## What changed

- Cardinal Health materially improves CLI 8 because it adds a second flagship healthcare distributor with a more institutional and system-level profile than Henry Schein’s office-based practitioner channel.
- The company is broader than a pure drug spread business. The annual and quarter chain show a healthcare intermediary spanning pharmaceutical and specialty distribution, medical products distribution, at-home solutions, nuclear and precision health solutions, and OptiFreight logistics.
- Fiscal `2025` revenue was `$222.6 billion`. Revenue was down `2%` as reported because of a previously disclosed customer contract expiration, but management said revenue was up `18%` excluding that effect, which is an important signal about the underlying demand and channel position.
- Fiscal `2025` GAAP operating earnings were about `$2.3 billion`, non-GAAP operating earnings were about `$2.8 billion`, GAAP diluted EPS was `$6.45`, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$8.24`.
- The quarter chain then showed continued strength into fiscal `2026`, especially in branded and specialty pharmaceutical demand, service-platform attachment, and the broader Other segment, while tariffs remained a visible pressure point inside Global Medical Products and Distribution.

## Working interpretation

- Cardinal Health shows that healthcare middle-layer economics do not stop at manufacturers, hospitals, or insurers. The operating leverage sits inside recurring replenishment, channel access, specialty distribution, logistics, and the service attach around fragmented provider systems.
- It also helps the archive distinguish between different healthcare channel forms. Henry Schein is the cleaner office-based and practitioner-distribution proof; Cardinal Health is the larger institutional healthcare-intermediation and system-throughput proof.
