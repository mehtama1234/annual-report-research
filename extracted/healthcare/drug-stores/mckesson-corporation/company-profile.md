## What changed

- McKesson is one of the strongest healthcare middle-layer companies in the archive because it sits inside the flow of medicines, specialty products, provider procurement, and healthcare operating infrastructure.
- The 2025 annual report shows the older version of McKesson’s model clearly: fiscal `2025` revenue reached `$359.1 billion`, up `16%`, and diluted EPS reached `$25.72`.
- Fiscal `2025` segment mix also matters for this frontier. Segment revenues were about:
  - U.S. Pharmaceutical: `$327.7 billion`
  - Prescription Technology Solutions: `$5.2 billion`
  - Medical-Surgical Solutions: `$11.4 billion`
  - International: `$14.7 billion`
- That mix shows a company whose core is still scale distribution, but whose strategic value increasingly depends on adjacent platforms such as specialty distribution, provider solutions, technology services, and medical-surgical infrastructure.
- By Q3 FY2026, McKesson was already signaling the new direction more clearly. Official quarter materials showed record revenue and adjusted operating profit, with continued momentum across oncology and multispecialty, biopharma services, and the core distribution businesses.
- Q4 FY2026 then closed with `$96.3 billion` of quarterly revenue and `$403.4 billion` of full-year revenue. Adjusted EPS reached `$11.69` for the quarter and `$39.11` for the full year, while free cash flow reached `$5.4 billion`.
- Q1 FY2027 extended the same pattern: revenue reached `$105.4 billion`, adjusted EPS increased `20%` to `$9.93`, North American Pharmaceutical revenue reached `$86.8 billion`, Oncology & Multispecialty revenue reached `$14.2 billion`, and Prescription Technology Solutions revenue reached `$1.6 billion`.
- The latest quarter also makes the portfolio shift concrete. McKesson advanced the planned separation of Medical-Surgical Solutions, sold an approximately `13%` minority interest in that business to Apollo funds, and introduced Wellverse as the future standalone name.

## Trend interpretation

- Societal pattern: healthcare systems increasingly depend on a small number of scaled intermediaries to move drugs, specialty therapies, and medical products reliably. McKesson is not a side participant in healthcare delivery; it is part of the operating backbone.
- Institutional demand pattern: this is one of the clearest cases of institutional replenishment in the whole frontier. Pharmacies, providers, health systems, oncology practices, and care sites depend on continuous supply and reimbursement-linked workflow support.
- Cultural shift: healthcare is becoming more specialized, data-driven, and site-flexible. Growth in oncology, multispecialty, and biopharma services shows demand moving toward higher-complexity care models rather than just basic drug distribution.
- Industrial pressure: pharmaceutical distribution remains a scale, regulation, reimbursement, and working-capital business. Volumes are massive, margins are thin, and operational errors are expensive.
- Portfolio pattern: McKesson is actively reshaping itself away from lower-value or less strategic assets and toward higher-growth, higher-service platforms. The Norway exit, MMS separation path, oncology expansion, and technology services all fit that pattern.
- Capital pattern: the company pairs disciplined capital allocation with aggressive shareholder returns. It also uses transactions and separations to re-price businesses and redirect capital toward growth areas.
- Frontier implication: McKesson shows that the middle layer in healthcare is not only about warehousing and shipping. It also includes specialty distribution, provider solutions, technology, financing, and embedded operational relationships that make care delivery possible.

## Open questions

- How much further can oncology, multispecialty, and biopharma services expand before they meaningfully rebalance the company away from pure core distribution?
- Does the planned Medical-Surgical Solutions separation sharpen McKesson’s strategic focus, or reduce useful diversification inside the healthcare middle layer?
- How resilient is McKesson’s model if reimbursement pressure, drug-mix shifts, or healthcare policy changes intensify?
