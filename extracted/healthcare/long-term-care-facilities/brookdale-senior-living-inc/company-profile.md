## What changed

- Brookdale adds the age-linked operating model that the healthcare archive was missing. This is not a payer, product, or hospital-system story. It is a senior-living and care-intensity story where occupancy, resident rates, labor discipline, acuity, and community mix matter directly.
- The annual report frames Brookdale as the nation's largest senior-living operator, with `647` communities in `41` states serving about `58,000` residents at year-end `2025`.
- Fiscal `2025` was still a transition year rather than a fully repaired one. Total revenue was `$3.18B`, same-community RevPAR increased `7.6%`, adjusted EBITDA rose `19.1%` to `$450.3M`, and Brookdale generated positive operating income of `$131.7M`, but net loss from continuing operations remained `($263.5M)`.
- Occupancy and rate are the two main earnings levers. Q3 `2025`, Q4 `2025`, and Q1 `2026` all show occupancy hovering around `79%` to `80%` while RevPAR stays near `6%` growth, suggesting the current recovery is more about steady community-level improvement than about a single explosive quarter.
- Brookdale is useful for `the-graying-market` because it puts age-linked daily-life demand into the archive directly: assisted living, independent living, memory care, resident acuity, resident fees, community operations, and the labor-heavy reality of serving an older population.

## Open questions

- Does Brookdale primarily express a demographic tailwind, or is it still mostly an operational turnaround with age-linked demand as the backdrop rather than the full current driver?
