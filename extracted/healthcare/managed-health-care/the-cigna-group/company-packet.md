## Annual report takeaways

- Cigna gives CLI 4 the missing second pure payer and health-benefits flagship against UnitedHealth, which matters because it helps separate lane-wide managed-care burden from company-specific repair stories.
- Full-year `2025` total revenues increased `11%` to about `$274.9B`, shareholders' net income was about `$6.0B` or `$22.18` per share, and adjusted income from operations was about `$8.0B` or `$29.84` per share.
- The annual read is that Cigna is less a simple insurer than a health-benefits and pharmacy-services platform whose quality depends on disciplined underwriting, pharmacy-benefit execution, specialty-care services, client retention, and operating efficiency.
- Compared with UnitedHealth, Cigna looks cleaner on the evidence available here because the `2025` and early-`2026` chain is not dominated by cyberattack fallout, governance reset, or reserve-repair language. It is more of a portfolio-shaping and execution-discipline story.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: total revenues were about `$71.668B`, shareholders' net income was about `$1.66B` or `$6.29` per share, and adjusted income from operations was about `$2.054B` or `$7.78` per share.
- Operating detail: adjusted income from operations increased about `6%` year over year, driven by higher contributions from Cigna Healthcare; the SG&A expense ratio improved to about `4.8%`, and adjusted SG&A to about `4.6%`.
- Guidance and strategic read: the company raised full-year `2026` adjusted income from operations outlook to at least `$30.45` per share, reinforcing that the payer model can still produce cleaner economics when underwriting, pharmacy-services mix, and operating efficiency hold together.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: total revenues were about `$68.494B`, shareholders' net income was about `$1.654B` or `$6.26` per share, and adjusted income from operations was about `$2.058B` or `$7.79` per share.
- Operating detail: adjusted income from operations increased about `12%` year over year, driven by higher contributions from both Cigna Healthcare and Evernorth Health Services; the debt-to-capitalization ratio was about `42.3%`.
- Structural read: `Q1 2026` showed a cleaner version of the model than many burden-heavy healthcare names because growth was tied to portfolio shaping, operating efficiency, and continued Evernorth scale rather than to emergency repair or policy relief.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: fourth-quarter total revenues were about `$72.472B`, shareholders' net income was about `$1.234B` or `$4.64` per share, and adjusted income from operations was about `$2.148B` or `$8.08` per share.
- Full-year readthrough: year-end `2025` adjusted revenues were about `$274.651B`; shareholders' net income was about `$5.957B`; adjusted income from operations was about `$8.014B`; and the debt-to-capitalization ratio was about `43.0%`.
- Structural read: `Q4 2025` anchors Cigna as a strong second payer comparison because it combines health-benefits burden with a very large pharmacy and specialty-services engine, making it a different type of managed-care business from UnitedHealth even before any pure margin comparison.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and societal pattern: Cigna monetizes the persistent need for employer-sponsored health coverage, pharmacy-benefit management, specialty drug access, and simplified care navigation.
- Industrial pressure: medical-cost trend, drug-pricing pressure, client retention, regulatory scrutiny, and administrative efficiency remain decisive.
- Cultural shift: the company keeps framing technology, data, transparency, and easier navigation as operating requirements rather than optional service enhancements.
- Bigger-picture read: Cigna is important because it lets CLI 4 ask whether pure payer burden is inherently messy or whether some managed-care models can stay cleaner when they are less disrupted and more pharmacy-services-heavy than UnitedHealth.
