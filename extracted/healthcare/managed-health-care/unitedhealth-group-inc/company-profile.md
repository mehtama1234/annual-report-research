## What changed

- Revenue: full-year 2025 revenue was `$447.6 billion`, up `12%` year over year. Q1 2026 revenue was `$111.7 billion`, and Q2 2026 revenue was `$112.0 billion`, showing stabilization after the reset actions taken in the second half of 2025.
- Earnings: full-year 2025 earnings from operations were `$19.0 billion`, but reported results were distorted by a `$2.8 billion` charge tied to cyberattack-related costs, divestitures, business exits, and restructuring. Q1 2026 earnings from operations recovered to `$9.0 billion`, then Q2 2026 came in at `$8.0 billion`.
- Medical-cost management: the 2025 adjusted medical care ratio was `88.9%`. It improved to `83.9%` in Q1 2026 and then moved to `86.7%` in Q2 2026, with Q2 benefiting from `$860 million` of net favorable prior-period development and better aligned pricing and product design.
- Guidance: initial 2026 outlook in January 2026 called for revenue greater than `$439.0 billion` and adjusted EPS greater than `$17.75`. In April 2026 the adjusted EPS floor was raised to greater than `$18.25`. In July 2026 the range moved to `$19.50` to `$20.00` adjusted EPS and `$18.45` to `$18.95` GAAP EPS.
- Segment mix: UnitedHealthcare remains the core insurance engine, while Optum is the platform layer spanning care delivery, analytics, and pharmacy. In Q2 2026 UnitedHealthcare revenue was `$86.0 billion` with `$3.9 billion` of earnings from operations, while Optum revenue was `$65.7 billion` with `$4.0 billion` of earnings from operations.
- Operational issues: the core operating story is not simple healthcare utilization. It is repricing, benefit redesign, cost discipline, reserve development, governance repair, and the continuing recovery from the 2024 cyberattack plus 2025 restructuring actions.
- Consumer / cultural signals: trust, transparency, prior-authorization reform, ACA affordability, and pharmacy-pricing transparency are now explicit operating themes. This is unusual for a classic defensive-healthcare framing and suggests that reputation and system-friction issues are directly affecting strategy.
- Technology: artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, digital simplification, and intelligent workflow tools are described as active operating levers rather than distant innovation themes, especially across Optum Insight and service operations.
- Management tone: pragmatic and reform-oriented. Management is framing UnitedHealth as a system-repair and operating-discipline story, not as a clean volume-growth story.
