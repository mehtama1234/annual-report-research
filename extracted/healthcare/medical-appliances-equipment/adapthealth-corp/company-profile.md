## What changed

- AdaptHealth gives CLI 8 a direct healthcare-at-home middle-layer packet where recurring resupply, discharge follow-through, referral-channel access, payor alignment, and home-service execution matter more than manufacturing novelty.
- The `2025` annual filing says the company serviced approximately `4.3 million` patients annually in all `50` states through approximately `640` locations in `48` states as of December `31`, `2025`.
- The annual and quarter chain makes the operating model explicit: Sleep Health, Respiratory Health, Diabetes Health, and Wellness at Home were the four reportable segments through `Q1 2026`, then the announced Diabetes sale pushed the company toward a three-segment continuing-operations framing by `Q2 2026`.
- The quarter chain also captures a major strategic shift: after pushing a large capitated contract live and then signing a Humana OneHome agreement, the company chose to sell Diabetes Health for `$235.0M` and tighten its identity around core sleep, respiratory, and wellness-at-home workflows.

## Working interpretation

- AdaptHealth materially improves CLI 8 because it turns home-medical replenishment and post-acute channel control into a direct packet rather than leaving the healthcare middle layer concentrated in drugs, office supply, hospital products, and procedure-adjacent names.
- It shows how institutional healthcare dependence persists outside the hospital: referral sources, payors, equipment access, recurring consumables, and digital resupply all sit in the same operating stack.
