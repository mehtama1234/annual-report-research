## What changed

- HCA adds the provider and hospital-operator layer that the healthcare archive was still missing.
- The `2025` annual report and `10-K` show a large operating footprint: `190` hospitals, `121` freestanding ambulatory surgery centers, `31` freestanding endoscopy centers, and facilities across `19` states and England.
- The annual report message to shareholders says HCA completed another successful year in `2025`, with broad-based volume growth, disciplined operations, investments in hospital capacity, new outpatient facilities, artificial intelligence, and a new electronic medical record platform.
- Fiscal `2025` ended with revenue of `$75.600B`, net income attributable to HCA Healthcare of `$6.784B`, diluted EPS of `$28.33`, and Adjusted EBITDA of `$15.566B`.
- `Q4 2025` produced revenue of `$19.513B`, net income of `$1.878B`, diluted EPS of `$8.14`, adjusted diluted EPS of `$8.01`, Adjusted EBITDA of `$4.114B`, same-facility admissions growth of `2.4%`, and same-facility equivalent-admissions growth of `2.5%`.
- `Q1 2026` produced revenue of `$19.109B`, net income of `$1.620B`, diluted EPS of `$7.15`, Adjusted EBITDA of `$3.802B`, same-facility admissions growth of `0.9%`, and same-facility equivalent-admissions growth of `1.3%`.
- `Q1 2026` also exposed how quickly provider economics can swing on utilization and reimbursement details: respiratory-related admissions were down `42%`, respiratory-related ER visits were down `32%`, and the quarter relied partly on recognition of certain Medicaid supplemental programs that were not in the initial `2026` guidance.
- `Q2 2026` produced revenue of `$20.230B`, net income of `$1.699B`, diluted EPS of `$7.62`, adjusted diluted EPS of `$7.59`, Adjusted EBITDA of `$4.027B`, same-facility admissions growth of `2.5%`, and same-facility equivalent-admissions growth of `2.7%`.
- `Q2 2026` makes the healthcare-policy link especially concrete: HCA said exchange-related coverage losses created about `$400M` of pretax payer-mix pressure during the quarter, while incremental Medicaid Supplemental Payment Program benefit also added about `$400M`, primarily from Florida.

## Open questions

- How durable is HCA's earnings power if exchange-related coverage attrition persists and surgical mix stays softer?
- Are Medicaid directed and supplemental-payment benefits becoming a structural part of provider earnings, or are current results unusually helped by timing and approval dynamics?
- Does HCA's ongoing investment in capacity, outpatient expansion, AI, and the electronic medical record platform create measurable operating leverage, or mainly defend the base?
