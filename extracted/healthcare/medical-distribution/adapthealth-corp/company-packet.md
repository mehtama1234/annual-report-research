## Annual report takeaways

- AdaptHealth extends the healthcare-frontier archive into the home medical equipment, respiratory, sleep, diabetes, and resupply layer that sits underneath a large amount of recurring care.
- The `2025` annual record matters because this is not a visit-based healthcare operator. It is a logistics, documentation, reimbursement, and resupply business tied to patients who stay at home but still need ongoing equipment and consumable support.
- As of December 31, `2025`, AdaptHealth serviced approximately `4.3 million` patients annually in all `50` states through about `640` locations in `48` states and completed about `38,500` equipment and supply deliveries per day.
- This packet is especially useful because it shows another physical layer behind home-based care: getting devices, disposables, and reorder cycles to the patient on time can matter as much as nursing visits or physician relationships.
- The annual evidence also strengthens a broader archive pattern: recurring healthcare revenue is often built on boring but indispensable workflow, paperwork, and delivery systems rather than on science or brand alone.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` net revenue was `$740.3 million`, up `12.7%`; organic revenue growth was `15.9%`; adjusted EBITDA was `$132.0 million`; net loss attributable to AdaptHealth was `$145.3 million`.
- Operating detail: the large loss was driven mainly by a `$144.2 million` pre-tax goodwill write-down rather than an operating collapse.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest quarter for seeing the split between real underlying organic demand strength and messy accounting / sentiment drag that can weigh on home medical equipment names.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` net revenue was `$819.8 million`, up `5.4%`; organic revenue growth was `9.1%`; adjusted EBITDA was `$121.2 million`; net loss attributable to AdaptHealth was `$16.0 million`.
- Operating detail: growth occurred across the reportable segments, but adjusted EBITDA declined against the prior year and working capital remained a constraint on cash conversion.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the best quarter for seeing how this model can grow while still looking financially uneven because reimbursement timing, mix, and inventory dynamics do not move cleanly with revenue.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` net revenue was `$846.3 million`, down `1.2%`; full-year `2025` net revenue was `$3,244.9 million`, down `0.5%`; organic revenue growth was `1.7%` for both the quarter and the year; adjusted EBITDA for `2025` was `$616.7 million`.
- Guidance: the company provided `2026` net revenue guidance of `$3.275 billion` to `$3.400 billion` and adjusted EBITDA guidance of `$610 million` to `$650 million`.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the bridge quarter where AdaptHealth most clearly looks like a recurring home-health infrastructure name whose headline reported revenue can mislead if you ignore organic trends and reorder dynamics.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: AdaptHealth is one of the best current packets for understanding recurring home-based healthcare supply and device economics.
- Consumer: patient behavior matters through adherence, reorder persistence, therapy continuation, and willingness to remain compliant with sleep and respiratory protocols.
- Cultural: the company reflects the normalization of more chronic treatment support happening in the home, with less institutional mediation and more dependence on reliable delivery and resupply.
- Societal: the packet sits inside aging, obesity, sleep disorders, respiratory disease, diabetes management, and the pressure to maintain more medically complex patients at home.
- Regulatory: reimbursement documentation, coding, audits, and payor requirements remain central to value capture.
- Technology: the edge is not frontier software. It is fulfillment systems, documentation workflow, equipment servicing, patient onboarding, and reorder logistics.
- Industrial: inventory control, resupply cadence, delivery productivity, equipment mix, and cash conversion matter as much as revenue growth.
- Capital allocation: this is a useful packet for seeing how scale can still look operationally messy when cash conversion and accounting charges cloud the underlying recurring base.

## Thematic interpretation

- AdaptHealth materially improves the healthcare frontier batch because it adds the equipment and resupply layer behind home-based recurring care.
- `Addus`, `Enhabit`, `BrightSpring`, and `Aveanna` tell the labor-and-clinical side of home care. `AdaptHealth` tells the device, consumable, and logistics side.
- That distinction matters because it shows that home-based healthcare only works at scale when both the people layer and the physical supply layer function reliably.
- A second repeated pattern is that recurring healthcare can be economically attractive even when the market narrative is weak. Equipment, sleep, respiratory, and diabetes resupply may look less glamorous than therapy or diagnostics, but they can create sticky reorder behavior and durable home-use dependence.
- The broader cross-company lesson is that the physical layer behind recurring care is not only drug delivery and packaging. It is also the equipment-and-supplies infrastructure that keeps chronic treatment going in the home.
