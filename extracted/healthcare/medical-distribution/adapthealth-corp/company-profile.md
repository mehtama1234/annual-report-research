## What changed

- Care model: AdaptHealth adds the home equipment, respiratory, sleep, diabetes, and resupply layer that sits beside the clinical home-care operators in the healthcare batch.
- Financial profile: the recent quarter chain shows better organic growth than the reported revenue line alone suggests, but also highlights margin pressure, working-capital swings, and in Q2 `2026` a large non-cash goodwill write-down.
- Strategic read: this is one of the clearest packets for seeing recurring healthcare revenue built on home medical equipment and reorder behavior rather than on visits, episodes, or admissions.
- Industrial read: the company is a reminder that home-based care is not only a labor story. It also depends on logistics, inventory turns, patient onboarding, reimbursement documentation, sleep and respiratory compliance, and resupply persistence.

## Open questions

- How much of AdaptHealth’s improving organic growth reflects durable underlying demand versus easier comparisons and pricing actions?
- Can the company convert home medical equipment scale into cleaner cash generation, or will working-capital and reimbursement friction continue to dilute the model?
- Does the recurring resupply and sleep/respiratory equipment base create more defensibility than the market currently gives it credit for?
