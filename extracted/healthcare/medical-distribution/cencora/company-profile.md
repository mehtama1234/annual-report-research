## What changed

- Cencora adds the drug-distribution and pharmaceutical-access infrastructure layer that the healthcare archive did not yet have.
- The annual and quarter materials show a business built around scale, low margins, and deep workflow embedment rather than patented-product economics or provider reimbursement capture.
- AnnualReports frames the company as a global pharmaceutical-solutions organization with `46,000+` team members and a business that connects manufacturers, providers, and patients.
- The annual filing makes the operating model clear: `U.S. Healthcare Solutions` is anchored in human-health distribution, specialty pharmaceuticals, and services to health systems, physician practices, pharmacies, and manufacturers, while `International Healthcare Solutions` adds pharmaceutical wholesale, commercialization services, and global biopharma logistics.
- Fiscal `2025` ended with revenue of about `$321.3B` and adjusted diluted EPS of `$16.00`, which makes Cencora one of the largest-scale but lower-margin healthcare infrastructure operators in the archive.
- `Q4 FY2025` showed how noisy GAAP earnings can be in this model: revenue still rose to about `$83.7B`, but operating expenses were distorted by a large `PharmaLex` goodwill impairment charge.
- `Q1` through `Q3 FY2026` show the core current pattern: specialty and physician-administered product growth remain important, `GLP-1` volume supports revenue but can dilute margin quality, and `OneOncology` is strengthening the specialty-services footprint.
- This makes Cencora a useful contrast with `UnitedHealth`, `HCA`, `Abbott`, `Baxter`, and `Thermo Fisher`: it sits behind pharmacies, providers, manufacturers, and specialty access rather than owning the payer spread, the hospital bed, the device franchise, or the lab workflow alone.

## Open questions

- How durable is current gross-profit improvement once `OneOncology` integration matures and `GLP-1` mix remains elevated?
- Does Cencora become more of a specialty-services and manufacturer-services platform over time, or does pharmaceutical distribution remain the overwhelming economic center of gravity?
- How much of the current healthcare earnings picture depends on these low-visibility intermediaries that sit behind provider and pharmacy access rather than on the more visible product and care-delivery companies?
