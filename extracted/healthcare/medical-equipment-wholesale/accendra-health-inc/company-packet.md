## Why this name matters

- Accendra is the strongest next healthcare add because it gives CLI 8 a non-drug medical-supply continuation case rather than another pharmaceutical intermediary or another adjacent healthcare operator.
- It is not a simple broad-distributor packet. That is exactly why it is useful. The company shows what the medical-supply channel looks like after payor stress, business divestiture, and a forced narrowing toward home-based care and direct-to-patient replenishment.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` filing chain introduces Accendra as the renamed continuation of Owens & Minor after the sale of the `Products & Healthcare Services` business on `2025-12-31`.
- The continuing business is now built around supporting health beyond the hospital through home medical equipment, disposable medical supplies, and related services across diabetes, home respiratory therapy, sleep therapy, wound care, urology, ostomy, and related in-home categories.
- The customer and payor structure matters. This is a business sitting between patients, providers, managed-care plans, Medicare, Medicaid, home health agencies, and direct-to-patient supply channels, which makes it a real middle-layer operator even though it is no longer the old broad medical wholesaler.
- The `2025` proxy states that total company revenue for the year ended `2025-12-31` was about `$10.672 billion` and total adjusted EBITDA was about `$424 million`, while the Q4 `2025` earnings release shows continuing-operations net revenue of about `$2.8 billion` and operating cash flow of `$154 million`.
- The annual story is therefore a split one. The old enterprise was far larger, but the surviving public company is narrower, more downstream, more payor-exposed, and more directly tied to recurring home-based supply and equipment flow.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: net revenue was `$613.2 million` versus `$681.9 million` a year earlier. Year-to-date revenue was `$1.241 billion` versus `$1.356 billion`.
- Profitability: GAAP loss from continuing operations, net of tax, was `(89.1)` million in the quarter and `(95.5)` million year to date. Adjusted loss from continuing operations, net of tax, was `(14.3)` million in the quarter and `(17.4)` million year to date.
- Balance-sheet message: management highlighted a `$385 million` reduction in total debt during the quarter.
- Why it matters: Q2 is the clearest stress-case read. It shows a narrower healthcare channel operator trying to stabilize after separation, commercial payor exits, and capital-structure repair rather than simply harvesting scale.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: net revenue was `$627.8 million` versus `$673.9 million` a year earlier.
- Profitability: GAAP loss from continuing operations, net of tax, was `(6.5)` million versus `(3.8)` million. Adjusted net loss from continuing operations was `(3.1)` million versus positive `23.2` million. Adjusted EBITDA was `$58.4 million` versus `$96.0 million`.
- Important distortion: first-quarter exit and realignment figures included a `$(52) million` gain on sales of patient service equipment in response to a commercial payor contract termination, partly offset by separation costs.
- Why it matters: Q1 makes the transformation explicit. Management described Accendra as becoming a pure-play home-based care company, which is analytically valuable because it shows how channel operators get remade when reimbursement and payor concentration break the old model.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: the Q4 `2025` earnings release says continuing-operations net revenue for full-year `2025` was `$2.8 billion`, up `3%` versus the prior year.
- Cash and capital: operating cash flow for continuing operations was `$154 million`, and management emphasized strong cash flow and debt reduction in the quarter.
- Structural event: the company completed the sale of the `Products & Healthcare Services` business on `2025-12-31`.
- Why it matters: Q4 is the hinge quarter. It closes the old Owens & Minor configuration and opens the narrower Accendra configuration, making the packet a useful archive marker for how medical-supply channels can contract and refocus under stress.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Accendra sits close to a large social shift: more care happening outside hospitals and inside homes, with patients needing recurring supplies, respiratory support, sleep therapy equipment, diabetes tools, and chronic-care replenishment through less visible intermediaries.
- The cultural pattern is not consumer delight. It is household medical dependence. Patients may never know the channel operator, but the system still depends on products, equipment, reimbursement, and logistics arriving reliably into the home.
- The packet also reflects healthcare fragmentation. Home-based care requires more coordination across payors, agencies, providers, and patients than a simple institutional stocking model, which makes downstream intermediaries harder to run and more revealing when conditions deteriorate.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Payor concentration is central. The filing chain repeatedly shows how contract terminations and reimbursement pressure can damage volume, margin, and equipment economics.
- This is a lower-working-capital and more direct patient-supply model than the old broad wholesaler, but that does not make it easier. It shifts risk toward collections, reimbursement complexity, and clinical-service execution.
- Separation costs, debt reduction, and capital-structure repair are part of the operating model right now, not side issues.
- The narrower channel is still real, but it is visibly fragile. That makes Accendra a valuable contrast to cleaner healthcare middle-layer winners such as Henry Schein, Cardinal, McKesson, and Cencora.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Healthcare middle layers can narrow rather than only expand.` Accendra shows the opposite of endless platform broadening: channel operators can be forced into a more focused downstream role when reimbursement and portfolio stress break the broader structure.
- `Home-based care still creates a real replenishment channel.` Even after the divestiture, the company remains a recurring intermediary for supplies, equipment, and therapy support outside the hospital.
- `Payor power can reshape the channel itself.` This packet makes it easy to see how reimbursement and contract loss can alter inventory, equipment deployment, and capital structure, not just quarterly margin.
- `The archive needs both winners and stress cases.` Henry Schein and Cardinal show stronger channel-control models; Accendra shows what happens when the same lane faces payor exits, debt pressure, and forced simplification.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Henry Schein, Inc.`: Henry Schein is the cleaner office-based practitioner procurement winner; Accendra is the narrower home-based care and direct-to-patient replenishment continuation case.
- Against `Cardinal Health, Inc.`, `McKesson Corporation`, and `Cencora`: those companies show large-scale drug and provider-system throughput; Accendra shows a smaller non-drug home-medical and patient-supply lane under far more stress.
- Against `Option Care Health, Inc.`: Option Care shows therapy routing and alternate-site care delivery; Accendra shows the equipment-and-supply side of home-based care where payor contracts, equipment economics, and recurring patient replenishment are more exposed.
