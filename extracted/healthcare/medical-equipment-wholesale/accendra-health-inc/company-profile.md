## What changed

- This packet is intentionally a continuity case rather than a clean same-business packet. AnnualReports still classifies `Owens & Minor` inside `Medical Equipment Wholesale`, but the current company is now `Accendra Health` after the sale of the Products & Healthcare Services business on `2025-12-31`.
- The remaining public business is now a narrower home-based care and direct-to-patient medical-supply platform built around `Apria` and `Byram`, not the former broad wholesaler-plus-distribution model.
- That narrower scope is still useful for CLI 8 because it shows what happens when a medical-supply channel gets forced closer to patients, payors, and home-based recurring replenishment rather than broad institutional stocking.
- The `2025` annual materials describe a company supporting health beyond the hospital through diabetes treatment, home respiratory therapy, sleep therapy, ostomy, wound care, urology, and related home medical equipment and supplies.
- The annual and quarter chain also make the pressure visible: payor concentration, contract terminations, balance-sheet repair, separation costs, and the need to produce stable cash flow from a more focused downstream care platform.

## Working interpretation

- Accendra is not the cleanest old-style medical wholesaler in the archive, but it is the strongest available current continuity case for the non-drug medical-supply lane because the public filing chain is current and the SIC lineage still sits in wholesale medical equipment and supplies.
- It improves CLI 8 by adding a healthcare replenishment packet that is neither drug distribution nor hospital capital equipment and that shows how channel stress, payor exits, and portfolio narrowing reshape the middle layer itself.
