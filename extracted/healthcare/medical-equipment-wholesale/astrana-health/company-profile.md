## What changed

- Astrana is a taxonomy-edge addition. AnnualReports places it in `Medical Equipment Wholesale`, but the operating business is better understood as a healthcare routing and control layer rather than a literal medical-equipment distributor.
- The company now reports through three segments: `Care Partners`, `Care Delivery`, and `Care Enablement`. That segmentation makes the middle-layer role explicit because Astrana monetizes provider-network management, delegated-risk administration, care operations, and platform services rather than depending on end-consumer demand alone.
- The `2025` annual chain shows a scaled healthcare intermediary with more than `20,000 providers`, over `1.6 million patients` in value-based care arrangements, and a model built around physician alignment, payer relationships, and technology-enabled clinical infrastructure.
- The `2026` quarter chain shows growth continuing after the Prospect integration, with raised adjusted EBITDA guidance in `Q2 2026`, strong free-cash-flow generation, and expanding delegated-risk lives in Texas and Hawaii.
- This is useful for CLI 8 because it extends the frontier beyond physical product distribution into institutional healthcare channel control: who manages patient flow, provider economics, reimbursement alignment, and recurring operating infrastructure between payers and care delivery.

## Working interpretation

- Astrana is not the clean pure-play medical wholesaler that the AnnualReports label suggests. It is still a legitimate CLI 8 addition because it sits inside the healthcare middle layer where business-customer embeddedness, recurring service attachment, reimbursement management, and operating-system control matter more than retail demand.
- It improves the institutional supply and replenishment lane by showing that healthcare dependence is not only about drugs, devices, or home-medical resupply. It is also about delegated networks, physician infrastructure, care-enablement services, and value-based routing power.
