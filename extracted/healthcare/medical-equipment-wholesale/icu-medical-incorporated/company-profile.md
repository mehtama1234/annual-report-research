## What changed

- ICU Medical is a cleaner healthcare-channel addition than another abstract enablement operator because the annual and quarter chain makes the physical provider-supply workflow explicit: infusion systems placements, recurring consumables pull-through, and high-value critical-care products.
- The `2025` annual filing describes the company as developing, manufacturing, and selling products used in hospitals, alternate sites, and home care, while also stating that it sells globally through its own direct sales force and through independent distributors.
- Revenue recognition language makes the institutional channel visible. ICU Medical says the vast majority of sales across `Consumables`, `Infusion Systems`, and `Vital Care` are stand-alone product sales to hospitals and distributors, with revenue typically recognized at shipment.
- The latest quarter chain shows improving operating quality even with portfolio noise from the IV Solutions divestiture. `Q2 2026` revenue reached `$551.7 million`, gross margin reached `43%`, and adjusted EBITDA reached `$110.0 million`.
- Working-capital and supply-chain management remain part of the story rather than a side detail. The `2025` annual filing says inventories rose partly to build safety stock and partly because tariffs were capitalized into inventory, while the liquidity section highlights working-capital needs, quality-system investment, and debt repayment.

## Working interpretation

- ICU Medical is not a literal wholesaler in the same way Henry Schein or Cardinal Health are, but it is a much cleaner provider-facing channel operator than another pure medtech platform because the company’s economics depend on recurring institutional replenishment, systems-and-consumables attachment, alternate-site continuity, and distributor reach.
- It improves the institutional supply and replenishment lane by narrowing the remaining gap between broad healthcare workflow packets and a more literal product-flow packet tied to hospitals, infusion therapy, and recurring clinical-use demand.
