# Baxter International Inc. Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Sector: Healthcare

- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026

## Why this name matters

- Baxter is a stronger next healthcare extension than another pure device platform because it sits directly in the hospital and institutional supply layer where infusion consumables, pumps, patient-support systems, connected care equipment, acute-care products, and injectable-drug workflow all have to keep moving.
- It improves CLI 8 specifically because it shows that healthcare middle-layer power is not only about drugs wholesale, provider routing, or surgeon preference. It is also about controlling the recurring supply-and-equipment stack that hospitals and care systems repeatedly buy in order to keep treatment capacity functioning.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual package still frames Baxter around three segments: `Medical Products & Therapies`, `Healthcare Systems & Technologies`, and `Pharmaceuticals`. That segmentation is analytically useful for CLI 8 because it makes visible a broad hospital-dependence stack rather than a narrow one-product story.
- The annual report describes a diversified medtech and care-delivery infrastructure company whose products are used in hospitals, physician offices, nursing homes, rehabilitation centers, ambulatory surgery centers, pharmacies, dialysis centers, and patient homes.
- The business mix matters. Baxter is not only selling a therapy product or a one-time capital device. It is linking IV solutions, infusion systems, advanced surgery, connected care, patient-support systems, front-line clinical equipment, injectables, anesthesia, and drug compounding into one recurring provider relationship.
- Fiscal `2025` was still a reset year rather than a clean victory lap. Net income from continuing operations totaled `($900M)`, or `($1.75)` per diluted share, and management described more than `$2B` of special-item drag.
- Even so, the `2025` annual package supports the core CLI 8 case because it shows a provider-facing operator whose economics depend on recurring institutional demand, installed workflow, product availability, and operational execution rather than on headline molecule discovery or consumer brand appeal alone.
- The balance-sheet and capital-allocation angle also matters. Following the Kidney Care sale, Baxter emphasized debt repayment, working-capital adjustments, and a broader performance-improvement push, which makes the packet useful for understanding how healthcare middle layers repair themselves when execution slips.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: worldwide sales totaled approximately `$2.96B`, up `5%` on both a reported and organic basis. GAAP net income was `$135M`, or `$0.26` per diluted share, and adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.56`.
- Operating signal: free cash flow reached `$181M` in the quarter and `$257M` in the first half of `2026`, but results still reflected higher-cost inventory produced at the end of `2025`.
- Segment read: beginning in Q2 `2026`, Baxter updated segment reporting. `Medical Products & Therapies` sales reached approximately `$2.1B`, up `7%` reported and `5%` organic, while `Healthcare Systems & Technologies` sales reached approximately `$801M`, up `4%`.
- Structural read: the quarter shows why Baxter belongs in CLI 8. Strength was driven by drug compounding, IV solutions, patient support systems, and front-line care demand, while the installed and consumable layers continued to matter more than any single product SKU.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: sales from continuing operations totaled approximately `$2.7B`, up `3%` reported but down `1%` organic. GAAP diluted EPS from continuing operations was `($0.03)`, while adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.36`.
- Segment read: `Medical Products & Therapies` sales were approximately `$1.3B`, up `2%` reported but down `2%` organic. `Healthcare Systems & Technologies` sales were approximately `$705M`, flat reported and down `2%` organic. `Pharmaceuticals` sales were approximately `$621M`, up `7%` reported and `1%` organic.
- Pressure points: the quarter was held back by the previously disclosed Novum IQ LVP shipment-and-installation hold, weaker infusion-pump sales, planned portfolio exits in front-line care, and uneven hospital equipment timing.
- Working-capital read: inventories rose to `$2.316B` at `2026-03-31` from `$2.232B` at `2025-12-31`, while accounts receivable fell, which makes the packet useful for reading hospital-supply normalization and internal cleanup alongside top-line trends.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: fourth-quarter sales from continuing operations totaled approximately `$2.97B`, up `8%` reported and `3%` operational. GAAP diluted EPS from continuing operations was `($2.01)`, while adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.44`.
- Segment read: `Medical Products & Therapies` sales were approximately `$1.39B`, up `6%` reported and `4%` operational. `Healthcare Systems & Technologies` sales were approximately `$827M`, up `5%` reported and `4%` operational. `Pharmaceuticals` sales were approximately `$668M`, up `4%` reported and `2%` operational.
- Pressure points: the reported loss included a `$485M` goodwill impairment charge tied to the Front Line Care reporting unit and a `$330M` valuation allowance on U.S. deferred tax assets.
- Structural read: the year-end quarter is the cleanest proof of Baxter's frontier relevance. Even during an execution-stressed quarter, all three segments still grew, showing how sticky the hospital and institutional supply relationship remains.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Baxter is not consumer-facing in a retail sense, but it sits close to major social shifts: aging populations, hospital labor strain, care decentralization, patient-safety pressure, and the need for providers to run clinical environments with tighter operational discipline.
- The social pattern here is dependence on invisible infrastructure. Most end demand in healthcare only works because hospitals, ambulatory sites, and care systems can reliably source IV therapies, connected equipment, patient-support hardware, and compounding capacity without interruption.
- There is also a system-fragility lesson. The Novum IQ LVP hold and the higher-cost inventory drag show how quickly execution issues inside the middle layer can ripple through provider operations and margins even when end clinical demand remains real.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Baxter is under simultaneous pressure from product-quality execution, segment reorganization, pricing discipline, inventory normalization, and portfolio pruning.
- The packet shows a business trying to stabilize after a period of strategic and operating disruption rather than a business coasting on category momentum.
- Its moat is meaningful but not effortless. Hospitals and care systems depend on the products, yet Baxter still has to prove reliability, restore margin quality, and avoid letting equipment or supply issues weaken trust in the channel.
- Working capital and cost discipline matter here as much as demand. Inventory roll-through, mix, product holds, impairment cleanups, and debt reduction all shape the economics of this healthcare middle layer.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Healthcare middle layers are not only drug wholesalers.` Baxter shows a second major dependence stack built around infusion, connected equipment, acute-care products, and hospital workflow.
- `Installed equipment and recurring consumables compound together.` Pumps, patient-support systems, care-connectivity products, and IV-related consumables make the relationship deeper than a one-off sale.
- `Operational reliability is part of channel power.` When Baxter has a hold, impairment, or inventory problem, it affects more than Baxter. It affects providers who depend on the flow.
- `Portfolio simplification can change how the middle layer is seen.` The move to fold the former Pharmaceuticals segment into `Infusion Therapies & Platforms` in Q2 `2026` reinforces that recurring care-delivery workflow is the more important organizing lens than legacy segment labels.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Henry Schein, Inc.`: Henry Schein explains office-based practitioner procurement and recurring dental-and-medical replenishment, while Baxter explains the hospital-use and acute-care equipment-and-supply layer.
- Against `Cardinal Health, Inc.`, `McKesson Corporation`, and `Cencora`: those companies explain high-scale drug and provider distribution, while Baxter explains the adjacent medtech-and-care-delivery infrastructure stack that providers also depend on.
- Against `Stryker Corporation` and `Intuitive Surgical, Inc.`: those names explain procedure and capital-platform procurement, while Baxter explains the less glamorous but equally essential recurring clinical supply-and-equipment substrate underneath everyday care delivery.

## Working interpretation

- Baxter materially strengthens CLI 8 because it gives the healthcare lane a more literal hospital-supply and care-delivery infrastructure operator, not just another medtech brand or another drug channel.
- The bigger pattern is that healthcare middle-layer power often sits inside routine dependency. Baxter shows that recurring control over IV flow, care equipment, compounding, front-line monitoring, and patient-support hardware can be as economically important as control over the molecules themselves.
