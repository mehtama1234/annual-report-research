## What changed

- Baxter materially improves CLI 8 because it sits close to the literal hospital and institutional supply layer rather than only the drug-wholesale or procedure-equipment edges of healthcare.
- The annual package still uses the older three-segment frame of `Medical Products & Therapies`, `Healthcare Systems & Technologies`, and `Pharmaceuticals`, which is useful for CLI 8 because it makes the channel stack visible: infusion and acute-care consumables, connected hospital equipment, patient-support systems, and specialty injectable-drug flow all sit inside one recurring provider relationship.
- Fiscal `2025` was still a stressed reset year. Net income from continuing operations was `($900M)`, or `($1.75)` per diluted share, and management described more than `$2B` of special-item drag.
- Even with that pressure, the quarter chain still shows why Baxter belongs in the frontier. Providers continue to depend on IV solutions, drug compounding, infusion systems, patient support systems, care-connectivity products, and front-line hospital equipment even while Baxter works through product holds, higher-cost inventory, and portfolio changes.
- Q2 `2026` also makes the service-layer and installed-base point more explicit. Baxter reorganized reporting so that the former Pharmaceuticals segment now sits inside `Infusion Therapies & Platforms`, which reinforces that the real middle-layer story is not only drug supply but the broader infusion-and-hospital workflow stack around it.

## Working interpretation

- Baxter gives CLI 8 a better bridge between disposable medical supply, installed care equipment, infusion workflow, and recurring provider dependence than another pure medtech-platform add would have.
- It also broadens the healthcare middle-layer read beyond office-based procurement and drug wholesaling into the hospital-use layer where consumables, connected hardware, pumps, compounding, beds, and patient-support systems all have to keep working regardless of macro noise.
