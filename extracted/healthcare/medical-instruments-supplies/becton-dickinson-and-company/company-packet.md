# Becton, Dickinson and Company Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Becton, Dickinson and Company
- Sector: Healthcare
- Industry: Medical Instruments & Supplies
- Ticker: BDX
- Fiscal year-end: September
- Assigned CLI 8 sub-lane: institutional supply and replenishment
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q3 FY2026
  - quarter 2: Q2 FY2026
  - quarter 3: Q1 FY2026

## Why this name matters

- BD is one of the strongest ways to keep widening CLI 8 after the earlier healthcare additions because it sits at scale inside provider, laboratory, and pharmaceutical workflow rather than only inside office procurement, home-medical continuation, or one installed capital platform.
- It matters for CLI 8 because the company monetizes recurring institutional dependence across medication delivery, specimen management, medication management, patient monitoring, prefillable-drug-delivery systems, and interventional procedure products.
- It also sharpens the healthcare lane by showing a broader middle layer than a classic distributor. BD is not mainly a wholesaler, but it still sits between production and care delivery by controlling equipment, consumables, and workflow systems that hospitals, labs, and drug makers repeatedly depend on.

## Annual report takeaways

- AnnualReports was current to the `2025` package on Monday, `2026-08-10`, and classified BD as `Healthcare / Medical Instruments & Supplies`, so the taxonomy anchor is clean for this packet.
- The saved AnnualReports description says BD serves healthcare institutions, life science researchers, clinical laboratories, industry, and the general public and manufactures and sells a broad range of medical supplies, devices, laboratory equipment, and diagnostic products. That is unusually strong middle-layer language for a non-distributor healthcare name.
- The filed `2025` annual report still reflects the pre-Waters structure. It describes three principal business segments: `Medical`, `Life Sciences`, and `Interventional`.
- The `Medical` segment is the most important bridge into CLI 8. The annual filing says `2025` Medical segment revenue was `$10.074B`, up from `$9.502B`, with growth led by medication-delivery solutions, specimen management, biologic-drug delivery systems, and the newly acquired advanced patient monitoring unit.
- The annual materials also show why this is not just a device-maker packet. BD explicitly frames customer solutions around medication management, patient safety, infection prevention, surgical and interventional support, and laboratory workflow.
- The annual-plus-quarter chain also captures an important structural change. On Monday, `2026-02-09`, BD completed the spin-off of its former Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions business and the combination of that business with Waters Corporation, so the `2026` quarter chain is already a recast continuing-operations view of `New BD`.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent reported quarter as of 2026-08-10: Q3 FY2026

- Results: revenue was `$5.0B`, up `5.4%` as reported and `4.4%` on an `FXN` basis.
- Segment read: Medical Essentials revenue was `$1.675B`, Connected Care was `$1.224B`, BioPharma Systems was `$670M`, and Interventional was `$1.414B`, all up year over year in the release.
- Operating read: the quarter shows broad-based institutional demand rather than one isolated category winner. Medical Essentials, Connected Care, BioPharma Systems, and Interventional all grew on a continuing-operations basis.
- Strategic read: the latest quarter shows how much of BD's lane is tied to repeat provider and pharmaceutical workflow. Management highlighted a Vizient contract for the CentroVena insertion system, a semaglutide-related collaboration in Brazil, and the Elyra kidney-stone platform.

### Quarter minus 1: Q2 FY2026

- Results: revenue was `$4.7B`, up `5.2%` as reported and `2.6%` on an `FXN` basis.
- Profitability and capital read: adjusted diluted `EPS` from continuing operations was `$2.90`, and the company said it executed a `$2.0B` accelerated share-repurchase program and retired `$2.1B` of debt in the quarter.
- Strategic read: management said more than `90%` of the business delivered mid-single-digit growth, which is a strong sign that this is a diversified institutional workflow platform rather than a single-product cycle.
- Product-and-workflow read: the release highlighted the CentroVena launch, a Wellstar Health System medication-management partnership spanning `BD Pyxis Pro` and `BD Alaris`, and a broader connected-care push around AI and pharmacy workflow.

### Quarter minus 2: Q1 FY2026

- Results: revenue was `$5.3B`, up `1.6%` as reported and `0.4%` on an `FXN` basis, while `New BD` revenue increased `2.5%` on an `FXN` basis.
- Strategic read: this was the transition quarter. The release said the combination of BD's Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions business with Waters Corporation was expected to close that day, and management framed the company as fully pivoting to `New BD`.
- Operating read: the first post-transition quarter still showed broad healthcare-system dependence. Highlights included hazardous-drug contamination testing for healthcare facilities and laboratories, the first U.S. hospital implementation of `BD Alaris` `EMR` infusion interoperability with `MEDITECH`, and a `$110M` investment in Nebraska to expand prefillable-syringe capacity for biologics and `GLP-1` drug delivery.
- Middle-layer read: the quarter makes BD's position especially clear. It links hospitals, labs, drug makers, and care teams through medication management, infusion workflow, pharmaceutical-delivery hardware, and procedure-adjacent products rather than through end-consumer demand.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- BD is not a consumer platform in the ordinary sense, but it is still exposed to broad societal shifts: more chronic-disease management, more biologic and self-injection drug use, more demand for safer medication workflow, and more pressure on hospitals and labs to standardize care processes.
- The clearest cultural shift in this packet is the continued migration toward higher-complexity care systems that need dependable drug-delivery, medication-management, and patient-monitoring infrastructure, not just headline therapeutics.
- The quarter chain also points to the reshoring and resilience theme. The Nebraska prefillable-syringe investment was explicitly framed as support for the U.S. pharmaceutical supply chain and biologic-drug delivery.
- BD also shows how AI and connectivity are being pulled into the physical middle layer. In this packet, AI is not a software story by itself; it is tied to medication safety, pharmacy workflow, patient monitoring, and connected hospital operations.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- This is a healthcare middle-layer operator with a very broad institutional footprint. It depends on hospitals, clinical laboratories, pharmaceutical manufacturers, and procedure volumes rather than on direct retail demand.
- The Waters transaction creates a before-and-after reporting issue. The `2025` annual report and the `2026` quarter chain are both valid, but they describe different operating perimeters.
- Segment breadth is a strength, but it also means the company is exposed to several types of pressure at once: procedure mix, hospital capital discipline, pharmaceutical category demand, currency, and the execution burden of a large portfolio transition.
- Working-capital and capital-allocation discipline still matter even here. The second-quarter materials emphasized debt retirement and repurchases, while the quarter chain repeatedly leaned on execution, productivity, and margin expansion language rather than only topline growth.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Healthcare channel power does not stop at drug distribution.` BD shows that large provider, lab, and pharmaceutical workflow systems can be just as important to the middle layer as classic wholesalers.
- `Embedded workflow is a moat.` Medication management, infusion interoperability, specimen handling, prefillable-drug-delivery systems, and procedure support all create stickier institutional dependence than a simple one-time device sale.
- `Replenishment plus platform attachment is stronger than either one alone.` BD combines recurring consumables and recurring clinical-use demand with systems and workflow products that make switching harder.
- `The biggest healthcare middle-layer operators increasingly bridge physical products and digital workflow.` The connected-care and medication-management elements are not side businesses; they are part of the core channel position.
- `Portfolio transitions change the interpretation, not the relevance.` The Waters transaction does not weaken the packet. It actually makes the middle-layer logic more visible by stripping out the former life-sciences stack and sharpening the continuing provider-and-pharma workflow platform.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Henry Schein, Inc.`: Henry Schein shows office-based practitioner procurement and direct distribution, while BD shows a broader hospital, laboratory, and drug-delivery workflow platform with much deeper acute-care and institutional-system exposure.
- Against `Baxter International Inc.`: Baxter shows hospital-use supply and equipment dependence, while BD shows a wider medication-management, specimen, pharmaceutical-delivery, and interventional workflow stack.
- Against `Avanos Medical, Inc.`: Avanos shows a narrower direct-plus-wholesale specialty-medical-products channel, while BD shows a much larger and more diversified institutional infrastructure layer.
- Against `Intuitive Surgical, Inc.` and `Stryker Corporation`: those names show installed procedural and capital platforms, while BD shows a broader recurring middle layer across medication delivery, monitoring, pharmaceutical systems, and interventional support.
