## What changed

- Boston Scientific adds the cleaner pure-play medtech and procedure-volume layer that the healthcare archive still lacked after adding payer, pharma, diversified platform, provider, diagnostics, and serving-science models.
- Fiscal `2025` ended with full-year net sales of `$20.074B`, GAAP EPS of `$1.94`, and adjusted EPS of `$3.06`.
- The annual and quarter trail keep showing the same operating split: MedSurg is growing, but Cardiovascular is the larger and faster strategic engine.
- `Q4 2025` produced net sales of `$5.286B`, adjusted EPS of `$0.80`, MedSurg growth of `11.7%` reported and Cardiovascular growth of `18.2%` reported.
- `Q1 2026` produced net sales of `$5.203B`, adjusted EPS of `$0.80`, MedSurg growth of `7.8%` reported and Cardiovascular growth of `13.5%` reported.
- `Q2 2026` produced net sales of `$5.442B`, adjusted EPS of `$0.86`, MedSurg growth of `5.9%` reported and Cardiovascular growth of `8.3%` reported.
- The quarter trail also sharpens the product-story layer inside healthcare: management repeatedly highlighted `WATCHMAN` and `FARAPULSE`, while `Q2 2026` added the `$1.5B` MiRus investment and more structural-heart and electrophysiology ambition.
- This makes Boston Scientific a more direct procedure-demand and device-innovation read than Abbott, and a more patient-procedure and category-leadership operator than Thermo Fisher.

## Open questions

- Does the heavier cardiovascular skew make the model more powerful or simply more exposed to a narrower set of device and clinical adoption curves?
- How much of the current earnings quality depends on continued electrophysiology and structural-heart momentum versus broader MedSurg balance?
