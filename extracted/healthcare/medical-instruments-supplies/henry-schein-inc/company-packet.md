# Henry Schein, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Henry Schein, Inc.
- Sector: Healthcare
- Industry: Medical Instruments & Supplies
- Ticker: HSIC
- Assigned CLI 8 sub-lane: institutional supply and replenishment
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Why this name matters

- Henry Schein is the strongest direct medical-distribution add now available because it is explicitly built around supplying office-based dental and medical practitioners, alternate-care sites, labs, and institutional clinics through a centralized and automated distribution network.
- It closes a meaningful CLI 8 gap that the prior healthcare set still left open. McKesson and Cencora explain drug flow, Option Care explains alternate-site therapy routing, West explains therapy-enabling components, and Stryker explains hospital-capital procurement. Henry Schein is the cleaner recurring medical-and-dental supply channel that connects manufacturers to everyday provider operations.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual package makes the operating model explicit. Henry Schein describes itself as a healthcare solutions company powered by products, technology, value-added services, supply-chain capabilities, and trusted advisor relationships serving office-based practitioners and alternate-care sites.
- Scale matters because this is a real distribution platform, not a niche specialist. Official company materials show more than `1 million` customers, more than `300,000` products, `55` distribution and manufacturing centers, about `150,000` cartons shipped daily, and a historical `99%` order-fulfillment rate.
- The annual results were solid. Total net sales reached `$13.2 billion`, up `4.0%` from `2024`. GAAP diluted EPS was `$3.27`, non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$4.97`, and adjusted EBITDA reached `$1.101 billion`.
- The business mix is what makes the name valuable for CLI 8. Henry Schein is not only shipping commodity medical consumables. It also layers dental equipment, specialty products, practice technology, home solutions, consulting, and corporate-brand offerings onto the same practitioner relationship.
- The annual narrative shows an operator trying to widen economics beyond pure low-margin distribution. Management’s `2025-2027 BOLD+1` plan emphasizes higher-growth specialty products, technology, corporate-brand penetration, digital workflow, AI-enabled tools, and cost-to-serve improvement.
- Even so, the annual package preserves the core channel-control story. The company still depends on being the trusted procurement and workflow partner for practitioners who need reliable replenishment, equipment access, and supply continuity rather than occasional product novelty.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: Q2 `2026` total net sales were `$3.5 billion`, up `6.7%`. GAAP diluted EPS was `$0.82`, non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.27`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$288 million`.
- Mix: Global Distribution and Value-Added Services sales rose `6.6%`, while Global Medical Distribution sales rose `4.0%`, Global Specialty Products rose `8.7%`, and Global Technology rose `8.2%`.
- Management message: management said internal local-currency sales growth accelerated, value-creation initiatives were helping margins, and the company raised full-year guidance.
- Structural themes: this quarter makes Henry Schein look like a broad healthcare middle-layer operator rather than a simple distributor. Product flow, alternate-care demand, specialty attachment, and technology all contributed, while management kept pushing cost and workflow efficiency.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` total net sales were `$3.4 billion`, up `6.3%`. GAAP diluted EPS was `$0.92`, non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.32`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$289 million`.
- Mix: Global Distribution and Value-Added Services sales rose `6.1%`, including `1.7%` growth in Global Medical Distribution, `9.0%` growth in Global Dental Distribution merchandise, and `10.6%` growth in Global Value-Added Services.
- Management message: management reaffirmed full-year guidance and tied the outlook to market-share gains, gross-margin expansion, and more than `$200 million` of targeted operating-income improvement over the next few years.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows that the company’s moat is not only in the warehouse. It also sits in practitioner workflow, value-added services, equipment relationships, and a distribution footprint that can cross-sell specialty products and technology.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` total net sales were `$3.4 billion`, up `7.7%`. Q4 GAAP diluted EPS was `$0.85`, Q4 non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.34`, and full-year `2025` net sales reached `$13.2 billion`.
- Mix: Global Distribution and Value-Added Services sales rose `7.0%`, including `4.9%` growth in Global Medical Distribution and `12.2%` growth in Global Dental Distribution equipment.
- Management message: management described the quarter as the strongest sales-growth result in `15` quarters and introduced `2026` guidance based on sustained execution of the `BOLD+1` plan.
- Structural themes: the year-end quarter is the cleanest proof that Henry Schein belongs in CLI 8. It ties routine healthcare replenishment to equipment, specialty products, technology, and practitioner relationship depth rather than leaving the company as a plain medical-box distributor.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Henry Schein is not consumer-facing in the usual sense, but it sits close to several major social shifts: more care moving outside hospitals, more pressure on practitioner efficiency, more home and alternate-site treatment, and more demand for office-based providers to act like operationally mature businesses.
- The cultural shift here is that healthcare practices increasingly need a distribution partner that is also a workflow and technology partner. Supplies, equipment, software, financing support, ordering convenience, and specialty product access increasingly live inside one vendor relationship.
- There is also a decentralization pattern. More healthcare activity is happening in dental practices, physician offices, alternate-care sites, and home-adjacent channels rather than only in large acute-care settings. Henry Schein monetizes that fragmented but recurring demand base.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Practitioner demand is recurring, but not frictionless. The company still depends on office traffic, elective procedure cadence, reimbursement conditions, and provider willingness to spend on equipment and technology.
- Margin quality depends on more than volume. Product mix, corporate-brand penetration, supplier relationships, value-added services, and cost-to-serve discipline all matter.
- Technology and specialty growth help the story, but they also raise execution pressure. The company is clearly trying to become more than a traditional distributor, which means integration, product adoption, and operating simplification remain live risks.
- The model is exposed to the usual distribution stresses: supplier concentration, shipping cost, inventory discipline, and large-customer purchasing leverage.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Healthcare middle layers are broader than drug wholesalers.` Henry Schein shows that recurring provider procurement and practice operations are also meaningful healthcare infrastructure.
- `Channel control compounds when product flow and workflow support live together.` The better distributors do not only ship inventory; they become embedded in ordering, equipment, specialty access, and software-linked operational routines.
- `Office-based and alternate-care healthcare are real institutional-demand lanes.` Not all healthcare dependence lives in hospitals. Henry Schein’s practitioner-heavy channel makes that visible.
- `Technology attachment can improve even old-line distribution.` Practice-management tools, digital ordering, AI-linked workflow, and value-added services can widen what would otherwise look like a lower-multiple distribution model.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `McKesson Corporation` and `Cencora`: those companies explain scale drug distribution and specialty manufacturer-provider channels, while Henry Schein explains everyday medical and dental supply distribution into office-based care.
- Against `Option Care Health, Inc.`: Option Care is a therapy-routing and alternate-site care-delivery platform, while Henry Schein is the replenishment, equipment, and workflow partner to the providers themselves.
- Against `Stryker Corporation`: Stryker explains procedure-room capital procurement and surgeon preference, while Henry Schein explains recurring practitioner purchasing and supply-chain attachment outside the pure hospital-capital lane.
