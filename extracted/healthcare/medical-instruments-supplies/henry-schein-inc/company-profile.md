## What changed

- Henry Schein is one of the archive’s clearest direct medical-supply distribution fits. The SEC metadata classifies it under `Wholesale-Medical, Dental & Hospital Equipment & Supplies`, which is exactly the lane CLI 8 still needed.
- The company is broader than a plain box-mover. The annual and quarter chain describe a solutions platform for office-based dental and medical practitioners with products, equipment, technology, value-added services, and alternate-care exposure layered onto a centralized distribution network.
- Fiscal `2025` ended with sales of `$13.2 billion`, GAAP diluted EPS of `$3.27`, non-GAAP diluted EPS of `$4.97`, and operating cash flow of `$712 million`.
- The scale and operating footprint matter. As of the official `2025` annual package and overview materials, Henry Schein served more than `1 million` customers, offered more than `300,000` products, operated in `34` countries and territories, ran `55` distribution and manufacturing centers, and shipped roughly `150,000` cartons daily with a historical order-fulfillment rate of `99%`.
- The quarter chain shows steady demand improvement rather than a one-quarter spike. Q4 `2025`, Q1 `2026`, and Q2 `2026` all showed sales growth, margin-supportive non-GAAP EPS growth, and continued traction in distribution, specialty products, and technology.
- The medical-distribution read is especially useful because the business still has obvious operational detail. In Q4 `2025`, Global Medical Distribution sales rose `4.9%`; in Q1 `2026`, they rose `1.7%`; and in Q2 `2026`, they rose `4.0%`. That gives the archive a real view into healthcare replenishment, alternate-care demand, and practitioner purchasing behavior.
- Henry Schein also adds a better office-based healthcare angle than the current drug-heavy lane. Its stated customer set includes dental and medical practitioners, dental laboratories, government and institutional healthcare clinics, and alternate care sites.

## Working interpretation

- Henry Schein materially improves CLI 8 because it adds the medical-supply and equipment channel that sits between healthcare manufacturers and the office-based practitioners, clinics, and alternate-care sites that need recurring replenishment and workflow support.
- It also broadens the healthcare middle-layer conclusion beyond drug flow and therapy routing. The archive can now show that healthcare dependence also monetizes through routine medical and dental procurement, equipment attachment, practice technology, and high-frequency B2B distribution.
