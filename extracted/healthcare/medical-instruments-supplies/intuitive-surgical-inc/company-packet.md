## Why this name matters

- Intuitive is the strongest ready next healthcare extension because it adds the hospital-workflow, installed-base, recurring-instruments, and service-attachment layer that still sits between Stryker's capital-equipment read and Henry Schein's practitioner-procurement read.
- It improves CLI 8 specifically because it shows how a healthcare operator can turn institutional procurement into a recurring platform relationship rather than a one-time device sale.

## Annual report takeaways

- Intuitive adds the robotic-surgery and installed-system platform model that the healthcare archive still lacked after adding cardiovascular medtech, orthopaedics, diagnostics, and life-science tools.
- Fiscal `2025` revenue was approximately `$10.12B`, up `21%` from `2024`.
- The `2025` annual report makes the business model unusually explicit: systems matter, but the real engine is the installed base, procedure growth, instruments and accessories, service revenue, and utilization.
- This matters because Intuitive gives Healthcare a medtech model built more on recurring platform economics and surgical-workflow embedment than on one-time product cycles alone.
- The annual-report evidence chain is now complete at the artifact level because the official `2025` annual report PDF is saved locally.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: revenue of `$2.89B`, GAAP net income attributable to Intuitive of `$818M`, and non-GAAP net income attributable to Intuitive of `$1.00B`.
- Operating signal: the company was still growing through procedure volume, system placements, and the recurring revenue that follows the installed base.
- Strategic read: Intuitive remains one of the cleanest examples in Healthcare of a durable high-value installed base that compounds through use, not only through fresh capital sales.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: revenue of `$2.77B`, GAAP net income attributable to Intuitive of `$822M`, and non-GAAP net income attributable to Intuitive of `$901M`.
- Operating signal: worldwide procedures grew approximately `17%`, including da Vinci procedure growth of approximately `16%` and Ion procedure growth of approximately `39%`.
- Structural read: this quarter keeps the core model visible. Instruments and accessories revenue rose `23%`, which reinforces that the system base and procedure intensity matter at least as much as the headline hardware placements.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: revenue of `$2.87B`, GAAP net income attributable to Intuitive of `$795M`, and non-GAAP net income attributable to Intuitive of `$914M`.
- Full-year signal: full-year `2025` revenue reached `$10.12B`, with da Vinci and Ion procedure growth still strong and more than `11,100` da Vinci systems and `995` Ion systems installed at year-end.
- Strategic read: Intuitive exited `2025` with a powerful mix of placements, recurring instruments and accessories demand, growing service revenue, and continued platform depth through da Vinci 5 and Ion.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Intuitive adds the robotic-surgery, installed-base, and recurring-procedure platform layer to Healthcare.
- Demand: demand here depends on procedure adoption, surgeon training, hospital workflow embedment, system utilization, and installed-base expansion rather than on payer spreads, drug launches, or one-time implant cycles alone.
- Competition: the moat is not just a product SKU. It comes from platform entrenchment, workflow integration, switching costs, training, data, and recurring instruments-and-service economics.
- Culture and business model: Intuitive reflects a healthcare model where a high-value capital platform becomes more valuable as customers use it more, standardize around it, and buy recurring accessories and services.
- Watch item: the model is strong, but it still depends on sustaining procedure growth, platform upgrades, and international expansion without a serious utilization slowdown.

## Working interpretation

- Intuitive does not solve the pure medical-distribution gap, but it materially widens the healthcare lane by showing that hospitals also depend on a recurring platform layer built on systems, service, accessories, training, and workflow standardization.
- The bigger pattern is that healthcare middle-layer power is not limited to moving medicines or boxes. It also includes controlling the operating platform that hospitals repeatedly fund in order to keep procedure capacity, utilization, and surgeon workflow intact.
