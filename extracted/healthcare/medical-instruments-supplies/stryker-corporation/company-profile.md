## What changed

- Stryker gives the healthcare archive a different medtech read than Boston Scientific or Abbott because it is much more exposed to orthopaedics, robotic surgery enablement, capital products, and hospital procedure rooms.
- Fiscal `2025` ended with net sales of `$25.116B`, reported EPS of `$8.40`, and adjusted EPS of `$13.63`.
- `Q4 2025` showed especially strong demand, with net sales up `11.4%` to `$7.2B`, organic growth of `11.0%`, and management explicitly citing healthy procedural volumes and strong capital-product demand entering `2026`.
- `Q1 2026` was the messy quarter in the trail: net sales still rose to `$6.0B`, but the cyber incident, margin pressure, and segment recast make it a useful example of how even strong medtech operators can suffer operational disruption.
- `Q2 2026` shows the recovery case clearly, with net sales up `9.4%` to `$6.6B`, adjusted EPS up to `$3.69`, and both MedSurg and Orthopaedics back to broad-based growth.
- The quarter trail also makes strategy visible: Ortho Tech now groups Mako, power tools, cutting accessories, and enabling technologies together, while acquisitions such as Inari and Amplitude Vascular Systems widen the cardiovascular and vascular side of the platform.
- This makes Stryker a stronger orthopaedics, robotics, and hospital-capital comparator than Boston Scientific, which remains the cleaner cardiovascular and electrophysiology pure-play.
- In CLI 8 terms, Stryker widens the healthcare middle layer beyond drug flow, alternate-site care, and therapy-enabling components into hospital procurement, procedure-room workflow, and capital-equipment dependency.

## Open questions

- How durable is the current capital-product strength if hospital budgets tighten after the current procedure and replacement cycle?
- Does the Ortho Tech recast make the underlying business easier to read, or does it partly smooth over the distinction between implants, robotics, and instruments?
- How much incremental growth still depends on acquisitions and category expansion versus the core orthopaedics and MedSurg franchises?
