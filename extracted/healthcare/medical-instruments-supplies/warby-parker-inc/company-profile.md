## What changed

- AnnualReports blind spot: Warby Parker is covered by `AnnualReports.com`, but the `Healthcare / Medical Instruments & Supplies` label and lagging `2024` annual package materially understate the real comparison set.
- Direct relationship ownership: the `2025` annual and `2026` quarter chain show a company that owns demand through digital commerce, retail stores, exams, vision tests, contacts, and first-party brand control rather than through wholesale eyewear distribution.
- Identity and fit system: the company is not just selling ophthalmic goods. It is selling vision correction, style, fit confidence, convenience, and an integrated eyewear-plus-care relationship.
- Omnichannel service expansion: the annual materials repeatedly frame Warby Parker as a one-stop shop for eye care needs, with eye exams, contact lenses, virtual tools, and growing insurance participation widening the relationship beyond the initial frame sale.
- Technology extension: `Q1` and `Q2 2026` add a new layer through intelligent eyewear, showing a company extending from eyewear and vision care into wearable-computing behavior without leaving the direct-brand system.

## Open questions

- How much of Warby Parker's long-term moat comes from brand and style versus vision-care service convenience and insurance-network expansion?
- Does intelligent eyewear become a real category extension, or mostly a halo effect on the existing relationship system?
- Is the best comparison set now premium apparel DTC brands, beauty and identity systems, or healthcare-adjacent service operators with stronger first-party customer control?
