# Quest Diagnostics Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Quest Diagnostics Incorporated
- Sector: Healthcare
- Industry: Medical Laboratories & Research
- Ticker: DGX
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Quest is the clearest diagnostics-services and clinical-lab-network addition to the healthcare archive so far.
- Fiscal `2025` ended with net revenues of about `$11.035B`, Diagnostic Information Services revenue of about `$10.785B`, reported diluted EPS of `$8.75`, and adjusted diluted EPS of `$9.85`.
- The annual filing shows a healthcare model built on testing workflow, scale, logistics, access, and information services rather than on drug IP, insurance underwriting, or device placement economics.
- The revenue mix also matters: physician lab services remain the core engine at about `71%` of `2025` revenue, hospital lab services were about `18%`, other Diagnostic Information Services about `9%`, and Diagnostic Solutions about `2%`.
- The annual materials also make the strategic drift visible. Quest is not only a routine lab business. It is expanding through advanced diagnostics, oncology and MRD testing, consumer-initiated testing, employer population-health services, hospital joint ventures, and AI- and automation-enabled productivity.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: net revenues of about `$3.043B`, reported diluted EPS of `$2.84`, and adjusted diluted EPS of `$3.12`.
- Volume signal: requisition volume rose `13.1%`, with `13.0%` organic requisition-volume growth, while revenue per requisition declined.
- Strategic read: the latest quarter shows strong system usage, continued growth across physician, hospital, and consumer channels, and a business trying to convert that demand into a better workflow and service experience through automation and tools like `IntelliDraw`.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: net revenues of about `$2.895B`, reported diluted EPS of `$2.24`, and adjusted diluted EPS of `$2.50`.
- Channel and innovation signal: management highlighted robust growth across the consumer channel, double-digit growth in several `Advanced Diagnostics` areas, `AI Companion` adoption, and continued planning work on `Project Nova`.
- Structural read: Q1 shows that Quest is increasingly a diagnostic-information and patient-interface platform, not just a specimen-processing network.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: quarter net revenues of about `$2.806B`, quarter adjusted diluted EPS of `$2.42`, full-year revenues of about `$11.035B`, and full-year adjusted diluted EPS of `$9.85`.
- Strategic signal: management highlighted growth in `questhealth.com`, clinical innovations including `Quest AD-Detect` and `Haystack MRD`, AI and automation deployment, and the Epic partnership for `Project Nova`.
- Strategic read: the year-end quarter shows Quest widening its role from routine diagnostic testing toward a broader health-data, advanced-testing, and consumer-access platform.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Quest adds the testing-network and diagnostics-services layer that sits between physicians, hospitals, patients, employers, health plans, and consumers.
- Demand: this model is resilient because testing is embedded in everyday care decisions, chronic-condition management, preventive screening, oncology monitoring, employer health, and consumer-directed health behavior.
- Competition: network scale, access points, hospital relationships, physician integration, advanced-test menu breadth, and workflow tools matter more here than one breakthrough product.
- Culture and business model: Quest shows how healthcare increasingly includes hidden information infrastructure, consumer-facing testing interfaces, AI-assisted results interpretation, and operational process modernization.
- Watch item: revenue-per-requisition pressure, payer dynamics, acquisition integration, and the pace at which advanced diagnostics can offset routine-testing commoditization all matter directly.
