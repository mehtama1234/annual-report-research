## What changed

- Quest adds the clinical-laboratory and diagnostic-information-services layer that the healthcare archive did not yet have.
- The annual and quarter materials show a business that sits inside physician workflow, hospital relationships, patient and consumer access, employer population health, and advanced-diagnostics testing rather than inside drug manufacturing, insurance risk, or hospital bed capacity.
- Fiscal `2025` ended with net revenues of about `$11.035B`, Diagnostic Information Services revenue of about `$10.785B`, reported diluted EPS of `$8.75`, and adjusted diluted EPS of `$9.85`.
- The annual filing shows why this matters structurally: Quest serves physicians, hospitals, patients and consumers, health plans, government agencies, employers, retailers, pharmaceutical companies, and insurers through one diagnostic network.
- The `2025` filing also shows a distinctive mix inside the revenue base: physician lab services were about `71%` of `2025` revenue, hospital lab services about `18%`, other Diagnostic Information Services about `9%`, and Diagnostic Solutions about `2%`.
- Management’s strategic language and the quarter releases make the growth model clearer than the legacy lab label suggests. The business is leaning into `Advanced Diagnostics`, consumer-initiated testing, oncology and MRD testing through `Haystack`, hospital joint ventures, AI-enabled service tools, and order-to-cash modernization through `Project Nova`.
- `Q1` and `Q2 2026` show a durable pattern of double-digit requisition growth and repeated guidance raises, even while revenue per requisition remains softer.
- This makes Quest a useful contrast with `Abbott`, `Thermo Fisher`, and `Cencora`: it is not a device platform, not a tools-and-biopharma-services platform, and not a pharmaceutical distributor. It is a diagnostics-services and testing-network platform embedded directly in care-routing and testing workflow.

## Open questions

- How much of Quest’s current strength is durable organic testing demand versus temporary utilization and acquisition support?
- Can `Advanced Diagnostics`, consumer testing, and oncology-testing growth keep lifting earnings quality even if core routine-testing pricing stays pressured?
- Does the lab network become more strategically central as AI, patient self-service, consumer testing, and hospital joint-venture models expand, or does it remain treated as a lower-visibility services layer despite its system importance?
