Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026

## Annual report takeaways

- Quest expands the healthcare-frontier archive into national diagnostic infrastructure with unusually broad exposure to providers, hospitals, health plans, employers, and consumers.
- The `2025` annual record matters because it shows a business built less on one therapy or one device and more on making testing, interpretation, logistics, and connectivity recur across the healthcare system.
- This makes Quest one of the best healthcare names in the archive for understanding how routine diagnostics, specialty assays, and information flow become a durable operating system rather than a one-time clinical event.
- The annual evidence also sharpens an important archive pattern: healthcare value can sit in repeated data collection and repeated clinical routing, not only in drug ownership or care-site ownership.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$3.04 billion`, up `10.2%`; organic revenue growth was `10.0%`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$3.12`, up `19.1%`.
- Guidance: after Q2, Quest raised full-year `2026` revenue guidance to `$11.95-$12.05 billion` and adjusted diluted EPS guidance to `$11.05-$11.25`.
- Management message: the quarter emphasized continued progress in health-system collaborations, strong growth through `questhealth.com` and wellness partners, and double-digit growth in parts of `Advanced Diagnostics`, including Alzheimer's and cardiometabolic testing.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest quarter for seeing Quest as recurring healthcare information infrastructure whose growth depends on test volume, channel breadth, and workflow integration more than on simple list-price expansion.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$2.90 billion`, up `9.2%`; organic revenue growth was `9.0%`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$2.50`, up `13.1%`.
- Guidance: Q1 already prompted management to raise full-year `2026` guidance to `$11.78-$11.90 billion` in revenue and `$10.63-$10.83` in adjusted diluted EPS.
- Management message: the quarter highlighted robust consumer-channel growth through `questhealth.com`, several double-digit `Advanced Diagnostics` areas, early patient engagement with the `AI Companion` tool, and continued planning for `Project Nova`.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the best quarter for seeing the blend of consumer behavior, clinical workflow software, and higher-value assay mix inside the Quest model.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$2.81 billion`, up `7.1%`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$2.42`; full-year revenue was `$11.04 billion`, up `11.8%`; full-year adjusted diluted EPS was `$9.85`.
- Guidance: initial `2026` guidance called for revenue of `$11.70-$11.82 billion` and adjusted diluted EPS of `$10.50-$10.70`.
- Management message: the quarter framed Quest around collaboration-lab growth, `questhealth.com` expansion, more than `150` consumer tests, Alzheimer's and oncology diagnostics, AI and automation deployment, and cost savings through the `Invigorate` program.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the quarter where Quest most clearly looks like a national diagnostics utility that monetizes test demand, logistics density, and workflow dependence across both routine and advanced testing.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Quest is one of the strongest packets in the current healthcare set for understanding diagnostics as recurring infrastructure rather than as a narrow lab-services line item.
- Consumer: Quest captures a direct consumer-health behavior shift because `questhealth.com`, wearables, wellness partners, and app-based result engagement pull diagnostics closer to consumer-managed monitoring and prevention.
- Cultural: the company reflects a broader healthcare culture shift toward self-service screening, earlier detection, consumer data access, and less dependence on the hospital as the only point of care coordination.
- Societal: the model is tied to chronic disease, aging, preventive testing, employer screening, and the social push to identify risk earlier and route people more efficiently through care.
- Regulatory: reimbursement complexity, billing discipline, FDA and state approvals for newer assays, and data-handling obligations remain central to value capture.
- Technology: Quest is not just a specimen processor. The growth story increasingly involves connectivity, workflow software, AI-enabled interpretation, order-to-cash systems, and specialized test innovation.
- Industrial: requisition volume, revenue per requisition, payer mix, acquisition integration, logistics density, automation, and lab productivity all matter more than simple headline revenue growth alone.
- Capital allocation: the model combines recurring demand with large-scale infrastructure upkeep, acquisitions, transformation spending, and productivity programs, so execution quality still matters materially.

## Thematic interpretation

- Quest helps explain a larger healthcare pattern: diagnostics are moving from a background clinical function toward a front-door infrastructure layer for prevention, chronic-disease management, wellness participation, and therapy selection.
- The company also shows why recurring healthcare economics do not have to be drug-like to be durable. Repeated testing, employer and payer relationships, physician workflow dependence, and consumer access points can build their own moat.
- A second repeating pattern is that healthcare is becoming more information-intensive before it becomes more treatment-intensive. Earlier detection, more advanced assay menus, and continuous data collection all increase the importance of scaled testing networks.
- Quest adds a useful consumer and societal lens the current batch needed. The business sits at the intersection of medical necessity and consumerized health behavior, where people increasingly buy tests directly, follow biomarkers more actively, and expect digital access to results.
- The final cross-company theme is that frontier healthcare runs on interconnected layers: discovery, diagnostics, delivery, monitoring, and administration. Quest is the broadest diagnostic-routing and testing-volume layer in that chain.
