## What changed

- Revenue: Addus entered `2026` with slower but still solid top-line growth than `Q4 2025`, which is what you would expect after a large acquisition-heavy year rolls into a steadier base period.
- Mix: the model is still dominated by personal care, but hospice remains the clearest higher-acuity complement and home health adds a smaller clinical bridge that makes the continuum broader than a single-service home-care operator.
- Guidance: the quarter chain supports the idea that Addus can keep compounding through a mix of rate support, local market density, acquisitions, and home-based demand, but not without ongoing sensitivity to state reimbursement and labor execution.
- Strategic read: Addus is one of the best healthcare-frontier packets for understanding home-based recurring services that are less therapy-driven than `Option Care` and less medically mandatory per visit than `DaVita`, but still highly recurring, operational, and payor-shaped.

## Open questions

- How durable is personal-care growth if state budget pressure or managed-care negotiations turn less favorable?
- Can hospice and home health become stronger strategic complements without diluting Addus’ personal-care density advantage?
- Does acquisition-led expansion continue to create attractive density and cross-service leverage, or does it eventually raise integration risk faster than it raises returns?
