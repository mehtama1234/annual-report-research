## Annual report takeaways

- Aveanna extends the healthcare-frontier archive into a different kind of home-based care complexity than `Enhabit`, `Addus`, or `BrightSpring`.
- The `2025` annual record matters because Aveanna is built around a diversified home-care model with private duty services at the center, supported by home health and hospice plus medical solutions.
- This packet is especially useful because it sharpens the pediatric and family-centered side of recurring care, where relationships can last longer, caregiver staffing is harder, and reimbursement advocacy matters as much as operating density.
- The annual evidence also clarifies a recurring archive pattern: some of the most durable healthcare demand comes from medically complex patients who are expensive in institutions but manageable at home if labor, reimbursement, and clinical coordination hold together.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent reported quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$647.9 million`, up `15.9%`; net income was `$41.7 million`; adjusted EBITDA was `$84.4 million`, up `25.2%`.
- Operating detail: the revenue increase was driven by roughly `$75.7 million` of added private duty services revenue, `$9.9 million` of home health and hospice growth, and `$3.2 million` of medical solutions growth versus Q1 `2025`.
- Guidance: full-year `2026` revenue guidance increased to `$2.56 billion` to `$2.58 billion`, and adjusted EBITDA guidance increased to `$328 million` to `$332 million`.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest quarter for seeing Aveanna as a labor-intensive but scaling home-care platform with pediatric private duty as the main growth engine.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$662.5 million`, up `27.4%`; net income was `$178.8 million`; adjusted EBITDA was `$85.0 million`, up `54.0%`.
- Operating detail: the increase was driven by about `$118.5 million` of added private duty services revenue, `$14.9 million` of home health and hospice growth, and `$9.2 million` of medical solutions growth versus Q4 `2024`.
- Strategic context: after quarter-end, Aveanna announced the agreement to acquire Family First Homecare, reinforcing the strategy of deepening the pediatric and high-acuity home-care footprint.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the bridge quarter where Aveanna looks less like a turnaround and more like a scaled home-care consolidator with real operating momentum.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` revenue was `$621.9 million`, up `22.2%`; net income was `$14.1 million`; adjusted EBITDA was roughly `$79 million`.
- Guidance: the company updated full-year `2025` revenue guidance to approximately `$2.425 billion` to `$2.445 billion` and gave initial full-year `2026` revenue guidance of `$2.54 billion` to `$2.56 billion`.
- Structural themes: Q3 `2025` is the best quarter for seeing the momentum in all three operating divisions before the stronger Q4 and Q1 prints confirmed it.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Aveanna is one of the best current packets for understanding recurring home-based care when pediatric private duty and longer-duration family relationships matter.
- Consumer: the relevant behavior is family trust, caregiver continuity, willingness to keep high-acuity care at home, and dependence on reliable staffing rather than discretionary healthcare choice.
- Cultural: the company reflects a broader social expectation that medically complex children and adults should be able to remain at home if the care model is clinically credible and financially supported.
- Societal: the packet sits directly inside family caregiver burden, pediatric complexity, Medicaid and government reimbursement dependence, and the pressure to avoid higher-cost institutional settings.
- Regulatory: reimbursement rates, preferred payor strategy, government affairs work, and state-level rate support remain central to value capture.
- Technology: the edge is not software-led. It is coordination, clinical oversight, field staffing, medical-supplies support, and the operating systems that keep dispersed home care functioning.
- Industrial: caregiver recruitment, retention, reimbursement pass-through, acquisition integration, payor mix, and field productivity matter more than simple headline growth.
- Capital allocation: Aveanna is a useful packet for seeing how home-care consolidators use acquisitions and debt structure alongside reimbursement improvement to scale a complex service network.

## Thematic interpretation

- Aveanna materially improves the healthcare frontier batch because it brings in the pediatric and family-centered side of recurring care, which is not captured as cleanly by the adult-skewed home-health, hospice, infusion, or dialysis names.
- `Addus` is lower-acuity support. `Enhabit` is clinically managed home health and hospice. `BrightSpring` is integrated pharmacy and provider coordination. Aveanna is the clearest packet for long-duration private duty and pediatric home-care complexity.
- That matters because it shows another way home-based care monetizes: not only through site shift or episodic post-acute care, but through very sticky, highly recurring relationships tied to medically complex patients and family routines.
- A second repeated pattern is that some of the biggest healthcare burdens are pushed onto households unless a reliable home-care operator absorbs part of that complexity. Aveanna participates directly in that transfer.
- The broader cross-company lesson is that home-based care is not one lane. It is a stack of very different models with different reimbursement, labor, and relationship dynamics. Aveanna is the best current packet in the set for the pediatric-private-duty end of that stack.
