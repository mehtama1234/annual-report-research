## Annual report takeaways

- BrightSpring extends the healthcare-frontier archive into a broader home- and community-based infrastructure model than the other specialized-services packets.
- The `2025` annual record matters because the company is not only a home-health or hospice operator. It is an integrated platform combining Pharmacy Solutions and Provider Services around complex Senior and Specialty patients in lower-cost home and community settings.
- The annual evidence frames BrightSpring as a scaled operating system for medically complex populations: all `50` states, more than `465,000` patients served daily, roughly `10,500` clinical providers and pharmacists, and approximately `43 million` prescriptions fulfilled in `2025`.
- This packet is especially useful because it brings together pharmacy, infusion, home health, hospice, rehab, supportive care, and home-based primary care under one recurring-care model instead of isolating those services into separate company types.
- The annual record also sharpens a broader archive pattern: some of the strongest healthcare infrastructure businesses are built not around a single therapy or site of care, but around coordinating multiple recurring services for chronically ill patients over long durations.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` net revenue was `$3,873 million`; gross profit was `$492.7 million`; income from continuing operations was `$86.6 million`; adjusted EBITDA was `$205.5 million`.
- Operating detail: home health average daily census reached `46,448`, up `54%` year over year; prescriptions dispensed were broadly stable while revenue per script rose to `$314.20`; gross profit per script rose to `$27.50`.
- Guidance: full-year `2026` revenue guidance increased to `$15,100 million` to `$15,425 million`, and adjusted EBITDA guidance increased to `$820 million` to `$845 million`.
- Balance-sheet signal: leverage fell to `2.15x` as of June 30, `2026`, already below the company’s stated long-term `2.5x` target.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest quarter for seeing BrightSpring as a scaling home-and-community healthcare platform where better mix, pricing, pharmacy economics, and provider density all reinforce each other.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` net revenue was `$3,614 million`; gross profit was `$482 million`; net income was `$74 million`; adjusted EBITDA was `$190 million`.
- Operating detail: home health average daily census reached `46,066`, up `52%` year over year; provider-services volumes grew while pharmacy prescription counts were roughly flat and revenue per script rose sharply.
- Event context: the Community Living divestiture to Sevita closed on `March 30, 2026`, producing a gain on sale in discontinued operations and leaving the continuing company more concentrated in pharmacy and provider infrastructure for complex patients.
- Guidance: BrightSpring increased full-year `2026` revenue guidance to `$14,725 million` to `$15,225 million` and adjusted EBITDA guidance to `$795 million` to `$825 million`.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the best quarter for seeing the strategic pivot from a broader services mix toward a more integrated continuing platform with cleaner payor and margin characteristics.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` net revenue was `$3,551 million`; net income was `$49.6 million`; adjusted EBITDA was `$184 million`; full-year continuing-operations net revenue was `$12,911 million`.
- Operating detail: the company entered `2026` after strong continuing-operations growth, with Q4 revenue up `29.3%` and full-year revenue up `28.2%` over `2024`.
- Guidance: initial full-year `2026` revenue guidance was `$14,450 million` to `$15,000 million`, with adjusted EBITDA guidance of `$760 million` to `$790 million`.
- Capital allocation signal: BrightSpring repurchased `1.5 million` shares for approximately `$43.2 million` in connection with the October `2025` secondary offering.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the bridge quarter where BrightSpring most clearly stops looking like a mixed-service roll-up and starts looking like a more focused home-and-community healthcare platform.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: BrightSpring is one of the strongest packets in the healthcare batch for understanding recurring care infrastructure that combines medication management and provider delivery rather than treating them as separate lanes.
- Consumer: the direct consumer signal is muted, but patient and family behavior still matters through home-setting preference, adherence, referral conversion, and willingness to accept coordinated longitudinal care outside institutions.
- Cultural: the company reflects a deeper shift than simple aging in place. It reflects the normalization of home and community settings as the operating center for complex chronic care, specialty pharmacy, rehab, supportive services, and primary-care coordination.
- Societal: BrightSpring sits directly inside aging, multi-chronic disease burden, behavioral and specialty complexity, and the pressure to care for high-cost patients in lower-cost settings.
- Regulatory: Medicare, Medicaid, commercial reimbursement, drug pricing, specialty-pharmacy contracting, and value-based care incentives remain central to the model.
- Technology: the edge is not consumer software. It is coordination infrastructure, medication management, logistics, field-clinician execution, co-location, and data-enabled value-based care support.
- Industrial: script economics, home-health census, service overlap, market density, acquisitions, de novos, payor mix, and leverage discipline all matter more than simple revenue growth.
- Capital allocation: BrightSpring is a useful packet for seeing how portfolio reshaping, divestiture, debt reduction, and selected repurchases can all support a more coherent infrastructure thesis.

## Thematic interpretation

- BrightSpring materially broadens the healthcare frontier batch because it shows that home-based healthcare infrastructure can become more platform-like, not just more recurring.
- `Addus` monetizes lower-acuity home support. `Enhabit` monetizes skilled home health and hospice. `Option Care` monetizes alternate-site therapy administration. `DaVita` monetizes mandatory chronic treatment. BrightSpring is different because it ties pharmacy and provider workflows together around the same complex populations.
- That makes it one of the best packets in the archive for understanding healthcare coordination as an operating model, not just as a payer slogan. The company’s pitch is that medication, infusion, clinical visits, supportive care, rehab, and home-based primary care work better when they sit inside one front-line network.
- A second repeated pattern is that healthcare is increasingly moving toward lower-cost settings, but the real prize is not only site shift. It is the ability to hold more of the recurring service chain around the patient after the shift happens.
- BrightSpring also adds a useful industrial layer because it shows how scale can be monetized through density, script economics, referrals, co-location, and integrated care rather than just through price increases or blockbuster launches.
- The broader cross-company lesson is that the modern healthcare profit pool is increasingly built around repeated service orchestration for complex patients. BrightSpring is the clearest packet in the current set for that orchestration layer.
