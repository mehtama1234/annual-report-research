# Chemed Corporation Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Chemed Corporation
- Sector: Healthcare for this packet's archive lane, though the parent also owns a non-healthcare business
- Industry: Specialized Health Services
- Ticker: CHE
- Fiscal year-end: December 31
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Chemed is a more limited healthcare-frontier add than the other specialized-services packets because the parent company is split between `VITAS` hospice and `Roto-Rooter`.
- The `2025` annual record still matters because `VITAS` is one of the clearest hospice operators in the archive, which makes Chemed useful for understanding admissions, average daily census, reimbursement, and Medicare Cap sensitivity in end-of-life care.
- The annual framing is not about broad healthcare coordination or platform depth. It is about a recurring hospice service model that depends on clinical staffing, admissions growth, acuity mix, and Medicare policy.
- This packet is most helpful as a focused hospice comparison against `Enhabit` and `BrightSpring`, not as a replacement for those broader home-and-community infrastructure names.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` VITAS net patient revenue was `$443.3 million`, up `11.9%`; average daily census was `23,687`, up `6.1%`; admissions were `19,125`, up `9.0%`; adjusted EBITDA excluding Medicare Cap was `$80.6 million`, up `20.6%`.
- Guidance: full-year guidance was increased mainly because of VITAS outperformance.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest quarter for seeing the hospice recovery after the prior Medicare Cap disruption, with volume and margin both improving.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` consolidated revenue was `$657.5 million`, up `1.6%`; VITAS net patient revenue was `$420.0 million`, up `3.1%`; average daily census was `22,723`, up `2.2%`; admissions were `19,394`, up `6.9%`; adjusted EBITDA excluding Medicare Cap was `$70.8 million`, up `0.6%`.
- Guidance: guidance increased because of stronger VITAS performance and parent-level share repurchases.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the best quarter for seeing that the hospice business was moving back toward a more normal growth cadence after managing the `2025` Medicare Cap issue.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` VITAS net patient revenue was `$418.8 million`, up `1.9%`; average daily census was `22,462`, up `1.3%`; admissions were `17,419`, up `6.0%`; adjusted EBITDA excluding Medicare Cap was `$91.6 million`, down `1.7%`.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the transition quarter where the business still reflects cap-management drag but already shows the beginnings of admissions and census normalization.
- Comparison value: this quarter is particularly useful for measuring how hospice economics respond when volume recovery and reimbursement pressure are moving in opposite directions.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Chemed is useful for the healthcare archive mainly because VITAS is a scaled hospice operator with clear recurring-service economics.
- Consumer: the relevant behavior is family willingness to choose hospice earlier, referral flow, and care-site preference at end of life, not discretionary consumer demand.
- Cultural: the packet sits inside societal reluctance around end-of-life decisions, the gradual normalization of hospice use, and the pressure to provide more dignified lower-cost care outside acute settings.
- Societal: hospice demand is tied to aging, chronic disease burden, and the question of how healthcare systems manage the final stage of care without forcing unnecessary institutional intensity.
- Regulatory: Medicare reimbursement and especially Medicare Cap exposure are core value drivers.
- Industrial: admissions growth, ADC, revenue per day, labor cost, geographic mix, and cap management matter more than expansionary platform narratives.
- Capital allocation: the parent company's buybacks and balance-sheet choices matter, but they should not be confused with the operating quality of the hospice business itself.

## Thematic interpretation

- Chemed adds a narrower but still useful read to the healthcare batch: hospice is one of the purest recurring-care models in the sector, but it is also one of the most reimbursement-sensitive and culturally difficult.
- Compared with `Enhabit`, hospice here is more central to the identity of the healthcare story. Compared with `BrightSpring`, the company is much less of a platform and much more of a direct reimbursement-and-census operating model.
- That makes this packet valuable for one specific recurring pattern: some healthcare businesses do not need elaborate cross-service orchestration to matter. They matter because they sit at a medically inevitable, emotionally difficult, and recurring point in the care journey.
- The limitation is just as important. Because Chemed also owns `Roto-Rooter`, it is not as clean a healthcare flagship as the other specialized-services packets. It works best as a comparison case, not as the batch's defining infrastructure name.
