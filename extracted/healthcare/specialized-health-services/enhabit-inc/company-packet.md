# Enhabit, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Enhabit, Inc.
- Sector: Healthcare
- Industry: Specialized Health Services
- Ticker: EHAB
- Fiscal year-end: December 31
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q1 2026
  - quarter 2: Q4 2025
  - quarter 3: Q3 2025
- Coverage note: the standalone quarter chain ends at `Q1 2026` because the Kinderhook merger closed on `2026-05-15`.

## Annual report takeaways

- Enhabit adds the clinical home-health and hospice layer that the healthcare-frontier archive still needed after `Addus`, `Option Care`, and `DaVita`.
- The `2025` annual record matters because it frames home-based care as a preferred lower-cost care setting, but one where the actual earnings engine depends on Medicare reimbursement, non-Medicare mix, labor productivity, and branch density rather than on blockbuster products.
- As of December 31, `2025`, Enhabit operated `249` home health and `117` hospice locations across `34` states, with home health generating `76.8%` of total net service revenue and average daily census of `41,786`.
- The annual evidence also sharpens a recurring archive pattern: home-based care can look socially inevitable and strategically attractive at the same time that the operator remains exposed to reimbursement resets, payor-mix shifts, labor costs, and de novo execution.
- Enhabit is especially useful because it is cleaner clinically than `Addus`. It is not mostly personal care. It sits closer to skilled nursing, therapy, Medicare episode economics, and hospice census management.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent reported quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` net service revenue was `$264.8 million`; operating income was `$29.3 million`; net income attributable to Enhabit was `$19.2 million`; diluted EPS was `$0.36`.
- Operating detail: total home-health admissions rose `9.5%`; total home-health average daily census rose `3.7%`; revenue per patient day fell `3.1%`; cost per patient day improved `2.2%`.
- Management signal from the filed quarter: home-health revenue increased only `0.6%` because census growth was partly offset by lower unit revenue tied to Medicare reimbursement decline and faster non-Medicare mix.
- Balance-sheet and event context: the company entered an amended and restated credit agreement in February `2026` with a `$315.0 million` term loan A facility and a `$160.0 million` revolver, both maturing in `2031`, and then moved toward the May merger close.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest quarter for seeing Enhabit as a care-at-home operator whose value depends on balancing volume growth against reimbursement pressure and labor intensity, not just on growing visits.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` net service revenue was `$270.4 million`; net loss attributable to Enhabit was `$38.7 million`; adjusted EBITDA was `$28.0 million`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.14`.
- Operating detail: home-health admissions grew `7.3%`; non-Medicare admissions grew `16.0%`; home-health average daily census grew `6.4%`; hospice average daily census grew `9.9%`; home-health cost per patient day decreased `3.5%`.
- Management message: the company described `2025` as an emerging growth story with stronger census, revenue, and adjusted EBITDA, while continuing to reduce debt and open de novo locations.
- Balance-sheet signal: Enhabit reduced bank debt by `$15.0 million` in Q4, reached a leverage ratio of `3.7x`, and noted a cumulative `$125.0 million` reduction in total bank debt from Q4 `2023`.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the bridge quarter where Enhabit most clearly looks like a public-market recovery story in home-based clinical care rather than a stagnant post-spin service operator.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` net service revenue was `$263.6 million`; net income attributable to Enhabit was `$11.1 million`; adjusted EBITDA was `$27.0 million`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.17`.
- Operating detail: home-health average daily census grew `3.7%`; non-Medicare admissions grew `10.4%`; hospice average daily census grew `12.6%`; hospice segment adjusted EBITDA rose `72.0%`; hospice cost per patient day fell `3.1%`.
- Management message: management emphasized execution on revenue, census, and adjusted EBITDA while continuing debt reduction.
- Capacity signal: the company opened two hospice de novo locations in the quarter and noted a seventh straight quarter of debt prepayment.
- Structural themes: Q3 `2025` is the strongest pre-merger evidence that hospice mix, branch pruning, and debt reduction were making the company operationally cleaner even before the takeout.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Enhabit is one of the best current packets for understanding clinical home-based care that sits between lower-acuity support and higher-complexity infusion or dialysis models.
- Consumer: the consumer signal is not discretionary spending. It is patient and family preference for receiving post-acute, chronic, and end-of-life care in the home if the care can be coordinated reliably.
- Cultural: Enhabit reflects the normalization of the home as a preferred care site, not just for personal support but for clinically managed nursing, therapy, and hospice workflows.
- Societal: the packet sits directly inside aging, chronic disease burden, post-acute discharge management, and the public-health push to reduce institutional cost where home care can substitute safely.
- Regulatory: Medicare reimbursement rates, episodic versus non-episodic mix, managed-care share, and quality ratings remain central to value capture.
- Technology: the edge is not software in the consumer-platform sense. It is branch workflow, scheduling, referral management, clinical productivity, and the ability to integrate technology into dispersed operations.
- Industrial: clinician hiring, visit productivity, branch density, de novo execution, hospice mix, debt service, and referral relationships matter more than simple visit growth alone.
- Capital allocation: Enhabit is a useful packet for seeing how balance-sheet repair and operating stabilization can matter as much as top-line growth in a reimbursement-heavy service business.

## Thematic interpretation

- Enhabit materially improves the home-based care lens in this healthcare batch because it provides a cleaner clinical comparison against `Addus`, `Option Care`, and `DaVita`.
- `Addus` is lower-acuity and labor-hour intensive. `Option Care` is therapy-routing and infusion-complexity intensive. `DaVita` is mandatory chronic-treatment density. Enhabit is the clearest packet for the skilled home-health and hospice middle layer.
- That matters because it shows how the same societal trend, care moving toward the home, creates very different business models. Enhabit depends on clinician productivity, census quality, and reimbursement mix in a way that looks more like a distributed post-acute operator than a generic home-care service company.
- A second repeated pattern is that the home is increasingly the preferred site of care, but not the easiest site of care to monetize. The patient preference is obvious. The economic capture remains difficult because unit revenue can fall even while census rises.
- Enhabit also shows how operating improvement in home-based care often comes from unglamorous variables: pruning weaker branches, pushing cost per patient day down, shifting mix toward faster-growing non-Medicare volumes, and carefully adding de novo capacity.
- The merger adds a final strategic layer. Kinderhook’s take-private deal suggests that public markets were not the only natural owner for this kind of reimbursement-sensitive, branch-based healthcare infrastructure. That is a recurring cross-sector pattern: service businesses with stable demand but messy operating optics often migrate toward private ownership once the improvement path becomes more execution-heavy than narrative-friendly.
