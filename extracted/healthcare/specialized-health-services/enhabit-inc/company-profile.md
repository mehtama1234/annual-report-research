## What changed

- Reporting cutoff: Enhabit remained a standalone reporter through `Q1 2026`, then the Kinderhook merger closed on `2026-05-15`, ending the independent quarter chain before `Q2 2026`.
- Mix: the company entered `2026` as a home-health-and-hospice operator trying to offset Medicare reimbursement pressure with faster non-Medicare growth, productivity gains, hospice density, and selective de novo expansion.
- Balance sheet: the recent quarter chain shows a business that was stabilizing operations and paying down debt even before the takeout, with the amended `2026` credit agreement extending maturity to `2031`.
- Strategic read: Enhabit is one of the best healthcare-frontier packets for understanding the clinical home-health and hospice layer inside home-based care, where census growth, payor mix, labor intensity, and reimbursement pressure matter more than IP or device lock-in.

## Open questions

- How durable would Enhabit’s home-health recovery have been if Medicare rate pressure and payor-mix drift had continued without the merger?
- Was the company on a path to becoming a stronger de novo and tuck-in consolidator, or was the operating model still too reimbursement-sensitive for public-market patience?
- How much of the value story was tied to improved branch productivity and debt reduction versus a broader secular move toward the home as a normalized care setting?
