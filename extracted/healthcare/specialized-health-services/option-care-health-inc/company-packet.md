## Annual report takeaways

- Option Care is one of the clearest healthcare-frontier names for understanding recurring care complexity outside the hospital setting.
- The company’s model depends on home and alternate-site infusion, specialty drug administration, care coordination, and national clinician-supported workflow rather than inpatient bed occupancy or drug IP ownership.
- That makes the annual packet strategically useful: it shows how modern healthcare spending increasingly depends on where care is delivered, not just what product is used.
- The `2025` annual package also makes the economics look infrastructure-like. National scale, payer relationships, clinical workforce density, and therapy routing all matter repeatedly.
- In the broader thematic frame, Option Care is a clean way to study site-of-care migration, chronic treatment repetition, and how patients and payers both benefit when expensive therapies move outside traditional acute settings.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` net revenue was about `$1.442 billion`, GAAP net income was about `$53.9 million`, adjusted EBITDA was about `$117.5 million`, and adjusted diluted EPS was about `$0.45`.
- Guidance: the company updated full-year `2026` guidance upward after the quarter.
- Management message: the quarter reinforced Option Care’s position as the largest independent home and alternate-site infusion platform rather than a narrow local provider.
- Structural themes: recurring specialty-therapy complexity supports demand, but therapy mix, payer pressure, and service intensity remain important margin variables.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` net revenue was `$1,350.7 million`, GAAP net income was `$45.3 million`, adjusted EBITDA was `$104.8 million`, and adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.40`.
- Guidance: the financing setup improved through the expanded revolving credit facility, which matters because liquidity and flexibility support both operations and buybacks.
- Management message: management highlighted steady demand, cash discipline, and the continued scale advantages of a national infusion platform.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because revenue growth was modest, which makes it easier to see the model’s operating realities rather than just top-line momentum.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` net revenue was `$1,465.4 million`, up `8.8%`, and the company exited the year with strong cash generation and a constructive 2026 setup.
- Guidance: full-year `2026` planning entered the market already framed around continued revenue growth, EBITDA growth, and cash flow generation.
- Management message: management emphasized execution, therapy breadth, and national scale rather than any single product or reimbursement event.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the bridge quarter where Option Care most clearly looks like recurring treatment infrastructure rather than a niche provider.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Option Care is the cleanest read in the current healthcare set on specialized service delivery and alternate-site treatment economics.
- Consumer: the relevant behavior signal is patient preference for convenience, lower-friction care, and repeat treatment delivery outside the hospital when clinically appropriate.
- Cultural: home-based and alternate-site care are no longer edge cases; they are becoming normal parts of chronic and specialty treatment pathways.
- Regulatory: reimbursement, utilization management, pharmacy-benefit dynamics, and clinical compliance remain central to the economics.
- Labor: this is one of the most labor- and coordination-intensive models in the set because clinician availability, scheduling, and service execution directly affect growth and margins.
- Technology: the company’s “technology” edge is really workflow coordination, scale, and care-routing capability rather than a software or device story.
- Capital allocation: Option Care blends healthcare growth investment with buybacks and liquidity management, which makes it a useful balance-sheet and service-density comparison against more capital-light or product-heavy healthcare models.
