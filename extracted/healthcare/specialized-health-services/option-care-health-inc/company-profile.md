## What changed

- Revenue: growth slowed from the stronger 2025 pace into lower-single-digit 2026 expansion, which is useful because it separates structural demand from pure post-pandemic or acquisition noise.
- Margin: adjusted EBITDA remained solid, but therapy mix and operating-cost intensity still matter because this is a clinician- and service-heavy delivery model.
- Guidance: full-year `2026` guidance was updated after Q2, reinforcing that management still sees growth and cash conversion despite more moderate revenue expansion.
- Capital allocation: the company continued to repurchase shares while also maintaining capacity for organic expansion and workflow support.
- Strategic read: Option Care is the cleanest name in the current healthcare set for understanding how chronic and specialty therapies create recurring non-hospital care infrastructure demand.

## Open questions

- How much of Option Care’s economic value comes from site-of-care migration versus ordinary infusion volume growth?
- Can the model keep expanding without greater pressure from payer reimbursement, labor intensity, or therapy-mix shifts?
- Does this lane become more like healthcare infrastructure over time, or does it remain more exposed to narrow utilization and reimbursement dynamics than that suggests?
