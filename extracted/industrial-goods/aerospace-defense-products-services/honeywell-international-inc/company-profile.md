## What changed

- Revenue: full-year 2025 sales were `$37.442 billion`, with organic sales growth of `7%`. Q4 2025 sales were `$9.758 billion`, Q1 2026 sales were `$9.143 billion`, and Q2 2026 consolidated sales were `$9.719 billion`. After the Aerospace spin on `June 29, 2026`, the comparable Honeywell Technologies-only Q2 revenue base was `$5.187 billion`.
- Orders and backlog: Honeywell closed 2025 with orders up `23%` in Q4 and backlog above `$37 billion`. In Q1 2026 backlog rose to `$38.3 billion`. In Q2 2026 the consolidated backlog remained about `$38 billion`, while the ex-Aerospace Honeywell Technologies backlog was about `$20 billion`.
- Earnings and margins: full-year 2025 adjusted EPS was `$9.78`. Q4 2025 adjusted EPS was `$2.59`, Q1 2026 adjusted EPS was `$2.45`, and Q2 2026 consolidated adjusted EPS was `$4.52`, inflated by the one-time Quantinuum deconsolidation gain, while the post-spin Honeywell Technologies-only adjusted EPS was `$1.95`. Segment margin was `22.8%` in Q4 2025, `23.3%` in Q1 2026, and `19.0%` for Q2 2026 Honeywell Technologies-only.
- Portfolio and separation: 2025 and early 2026 were defined by separation and simplification. The annual report explicitly frames the transition toward independent companies. Q1 2026 announced the Warehouse and Workflow Solutions sale and reaffirmed the Productivity Solutions and Services sale. Q2 2026 reflects the completed Aerospace separation and the rebranding to Honeywell Technologies.
- Segment mix: Honeywell used late 2025 and early 2026 to recast segment reporting. Industrial Automation and Energy and Sustainability Solutions were reorganized to create Process Automation and Technology effective in the first quarter of 2026. After the Aerospace spin-off, Honeywell Technologies now reports three core segments: Building Automation, Process Automation and Technology, and Industrial Automation.
- Guidance: on January 29, 2026 Honeywell issued full-year 2026 guidance of `$38.8 billion` to `$39.8 billion` in sales and `$10.35` to `$10.65` in adjusted EPS. On April 23, 2026 it reaffirmed that framework while lowering operating cash flow expectations. On July 23, 2026 the post-spin Honeywell Technologies business updated guidance to `$19.8 billion` to `$20.0 billion` in sales and `$8.05` to `$8.35` in adjusted EPS, with Johnson Matthey Catalyst Technologies included after its `July 17, 2026` close.
- Capital allocation: the 2025 annual materials say Honeywell deployed `$10.0 billion` to capital expenditures, dividends, share repurchases, and mergers and acquisitions. They also highlight the `$2.2 billion` Sundyne acquisition and the announced `£1.8 billion` Johnson Matthey Catalyst Technologies deal.
- Operational issues: Q4 2025 margins were distorted by impairment tied to PSS and WWS being held for sale and by a Flexjet litigation charge. Q1 2026 called out Middle East conflict disruption in Process Automation and Technology and temporary mechanical supply-chain pressure in Aerospace. Q2 2026 still showed material-supply constraints in Aerospace prior to full separation.
- Technology and end-market signals: the annual report repeatedly ties demand to AI, cloud, `5G`, data, and the industrial shift from automation to autonomy. In quarterlies, Honeywell repeatedly points to data-center, hospitality, utilities, LNG, and fire-safety demand as the highest-signal verticals.
- Management tone: transformation-first and portfolio-manager-driven. Honeywell is not presenting as a generic diversified industrial. It is presenting as a company that used 2025 and the first half of 2026 to simplify itself into a pure-play automation platform with a large installed base and software-intelligence layer.

## Open questions

- How much of the new Honeywell Technologies growth profile is durable once Aerospace is removed and the company is judged only on automation, process, and industrial execution?
- Does the simplified post-spin company sustain margin expansion while integrating Catalyst Technologies and exiting PSS and WWS?
