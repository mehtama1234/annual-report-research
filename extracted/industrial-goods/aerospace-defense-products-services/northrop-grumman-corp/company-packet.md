## Annual report takeaways

- Northrop adds the missing defense-and-deterrence layer to the industrial set. It broadens the archive away from machinery, automation, electrical infrastructure, and HVAC into military aerospace, strategic systems, and national-security backlog.
- The 2025 annual report is explicit that the company sees a more complex global security environment, rising allied spending, and sustained demand for advanced systems. That makes Northrop a clean read on geopolitical and budget-supported industrial demand.
- Full-year 2025 sales were `$42.0 billion`, diluted EPS was `$29.08`, operating cash flow was `$4.8 billion`, free cash flow was `$3.3 billion`, and backlog ended the year at a record `$95.7 billion`.
- Northrop is also useful for theme work because it ties industrial demand to deterrence platforms and capacity scaling, not only to commercial capex. The annual materials center the B-21 Raider, Sentinel, and other next-generation programs as anchors of future growth.
- The annual report also highlights about `$2.5 billion` of R&D and capital investments in 2025, which reinforces that this is an investment-heavy production-scaling story rather than a mature defense-cash-harvest story.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 2026 sales were `$10.9 billion`, operating income was `$1.1 billion`, diluted EPS was `$7.68`, and net awards of `$20 billion` pushed backlog to a new company record of `$105 billion`.
- Guidance: on July 21, 2026 Northrop raised full-year 2026 sales guidance to `$43.75-$44.25 billion` and MTM-adjusted EPS guidance to `$28.60-$29.10`, while reaffirming operating-income and adjusted-free-cash-flow guidance.
- Management message: management emphasized robust global demand, accelerating momentum across the portfolio, and confidence that defense budgets continue to support program demand.
- Structural themes: this quarter makes Northrop look like a backlog-driven defense production platform with direct leverage to national-security spending and strategic-system modernization.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 2026 sales were `$9.9 billion`, operating income was `$989 million`, diluted EPS was `$6.14`, and backlog stayed near `$96 billion`.
- Guidance: on April 21, 2026 Northrop reaffirmed 2026 guidance for sales, segment operating income, MTM-adjusted EPS, and free cash flow.
- Management message: management highlighted agreements with the U.S. Air Force to increase B-21 production capacity and accelerate Sentinel initial operating capability.
- Structural themes: this quarter is the clearest evidence that production capacity and schedule execution are central variables in the Northrop story, not just bookings.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 2025 sales were `$11.7 billion`, full-year 2025 sales were `$42.0 billion`, diluted EPS was `$29.08`, and backlog reached a record `$95.7 billion`.
- Guidance: on January 27, 2026 Northrop entered 2026 expecting mid-single-digit sales growth and continued strong performance and free cash flow.
- Management message: management emphasized record backlog, strong global demand, and confidence that prior investments in capacity and technology positioned the company well for 2026.
- Structural themes: this quarter closes 2025 with Northrop reading as a multi-year defense modernization story rather than a normal industrial demand cycle.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Northrop shows that industrial earnings are also being supported by defense modernization, strategic deterrence, and multi-year government program visibility, not only by commercial infrastructure or private-sector capex.
- Consumer: consumer demand is irrelevant here. The true demand drivers are defense budgets, military modernization, allied security spending, classified and restricted programs, and production-capacity execution.
- Cultural: the cultural layer is security and deterrence rather than convenience or efficiency. This is an industrial story shaped by geopolitical threat perception and national resilience priorities.
- Regulatory: budget appropriations, procurement timing, contract structure, export controls, and mission-priority shifts matter directly.
- Labor: highly skilled engineering and manufacturing labor matter because scaling complex programs like B-21 and Sentinel is a capacity and execution question, not just a bookings question.
- Technology: this is a first-order technology story inside industrials, but the technology expression is defense systems, space, mission electronics, and restricted aerospace programs.
- Capital allocation: Northrop is using capital to deepen technology leadership and manufacturing capacity, with R&D and capital investment as core support for backlog conversion.
