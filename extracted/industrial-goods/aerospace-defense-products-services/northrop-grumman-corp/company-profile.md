## What changed

- Revenue: full-year 2025 sales were `$42.0 billion`, up from `$41.0 billion` in 2024. Q4 2025 sales were `$11.7 billion`, Q1 2026 sales were `$9.9 billion`, and Q2 2026 sales were `$10.9 billion`. The company is not being pulled by broad manufacturing recovery; it is being pulled by defense modernization and program execution.
- Earnings and margins: full-year 2025 diluted EPS was `$29.08`, while MTM-adjusted EPS was `$26.34`. Q4 2025 operating income rose `17%` to `$1.3 billion`. Q1 2026 operating income rose to `$989 million`, and Q2 2026 operating income was `$1.1 billion`. Quarter-to-quarter noise exists, but the larger story is operating leverage against a very large program base.
- Orders and backlog: Northrop is one of the clearest backlog-led names in the archive. The annual report says backlog reached a record `$95.7 billion` at year-end 2025, up from `$91.5 billion` in 2024. Q1 2026 backlog was about `$96 billion`. Q2 2026 net awards of `$20 billion` pushed backlog to a new company record of `$105 billion`. This is a multi-year demand-visibility story, not a short-cycle volume story.
- End-market mix: this is the industrial set's direct read on defense demand, deterrence spending, classified and restricted programs, and space-related national-security programs. The annual report explicitly centers B-21 Raider, Sentinel, and other next-generation systems tied to U.S. and allied security priorities.
- Program and budget linkage: Northrop's annual report says global security complexity, allied spending, and modernization needs are increasing. In Q1 2026 management said it reached agreements with the U.S. Air Force to increase B-21 production capacity and accelerate Sentinel initial operating capability. In Q2 2026 it pointed to continued demand across the portfolio and budgets that still support that demand.
- Capital and investment posture: the annual report highlights about `$2.5 billion` of R&D and capital investments in 2025 to scale production, reduce time to field, and improve cost position. This is useful context because Northrop is investing ahead of sustained program demand rather than simply harvesting a static defense base.
- Cash flow: the annual report highlights `$4.8 billion` of operating cash flow and `$3.3 billion` of free cash flow in 2025. Q2 2026 adjusted free cash flow rose to `$978 million` in the quarter.
- Guidance: on January 27, 2026 Northrop entered 2026 expecting mid-single-digit sales growth and continued strong performance. On April 21, 2026 it reaffirmed 2026 guidance for sales, segment operating income, MTM-adjusted EPS, and free cash flow. On July 21, 2026 it raised 2026 sales guidance to `$43.75-$44.25 billion` and MTM-adjusted EPS guidance to `$28.60-$29.10`, while reaffirming operating-income and adjusted-free-cash-flow guidance.
- Management tone: confident, backlog-backed, and explicitly tied to deterrence and security priorities. This is a geopolitical and budget-supported industrial story, not a conventional cyclical one.

## Open questions

- How much of Northrop's current demand strength depends on sustained U.S. budget support for deterrence and strategic modernization versus broader allied replenishment?
- Can the company keep converting backlog cleanly while scaling B-21, Sentinel, and other complex programs without further margin noise or contract-specific charges?
