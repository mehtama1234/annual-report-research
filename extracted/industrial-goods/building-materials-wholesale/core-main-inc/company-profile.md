## What changed

- Operating identity: Core & Main is not a generic building-products distributor. The annual filing defines it as a specialty distributor focused on water, wastewater, storm drainage, fire protection, and related services across municipal, non-residential, and residential end markets.
- Margin model: the `10-K` makes clear that the company’s strategy is not only scale. Management explicitly highlights private-label expansion, sourcing optimization, data-driven pricing, and value-added products and services as the main route to sustainable margin expansion.
- Demand mix: the quarter materials repeatedly separate soft residential demand from stronger municipal, smart-utility, treatment-plant, and complex-project demand, which makes Core & Main a better infrastructure channel read than a simple housing proxy.
- Expansion path: acquisitions remain a core growth tool, but the story is also about greenfield branch expansion and widening category depth in fusible piping, treatment-plant solutions, geosynthetics, and smart metering.
- Channel role: the company’s real analytical value is that it sits between manufacturers and end projects. That position lets it capture local availability, vendor relationships, pricing discipline, and project-complexity economics in one place.

## Open questions

- How resilient is current margin expansion if municipal funding softens while residential stays weak?
- Does acquisition-led branch and category expansion deepen moat and local density, or mainly increase integration and working-capital complexity?
- Can Core & Main keep outgrowing end markets through value-added and private-label initiatives if supplier terms or pricing power weaken?
