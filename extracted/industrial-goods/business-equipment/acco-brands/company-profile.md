## What changed

- Portfolio mix: ACCO is trying to reposition from mature office-product exposure toward faster-growing technology peripherals, highlighted by the `EPOS` acquisition closed on `2026-01-30`.
- Demand profile: core office and workspace categories remained soft through the `2025` annual and first-half `2026` quarter chain, while learning and creative, gaming accessories, and select accessories categories held up better.
- Seasonal pattern: Q2 `2026` showed that back-to-school sell-in still matters materially, especially in the Americas segment, making the company a useful read on routine household and school replenishment demand.
- Operating posture: the company relied on a multi-year cost reduction program, price actions, and FX support to offset lower organic volumes in mature categories.
- Balance-sheet pressure: leverage remained elevated at about `4.1x` at year-end `2025`, `4.1x` in Q1 `2026`, and `4.3x` in Q2 `2026`, so the category is still a cash-flow and deleveraging story, not a clean growth story.

## Open questions

- Can the technology-peripherals pivot offset the structural weakness in legacy office-product demand, or does it only slow the decline?
- How durable is the Q2 back-to-school strength if consumer budgets or retailer inventories weaken?
- Does elevated leverage limit how much ACCO can keep using acquisitions to reposition the portfolio?
