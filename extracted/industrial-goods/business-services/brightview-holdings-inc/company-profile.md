## What changed

- BrightView adds a different kind of industrial operator to the archive. This is not heavy equipment, defense, rail, or waste. It is national commercial landscape maintenance and development with recurring site-service exposure across offices, campuses, municipalities, hospitals, hotels, airports, retail, education, and other commercial properties.
- The annual filing shows a two-part business model. For the year ended `2025-09-30`, BrightView generated total net service revenues of `$2.67 billion`, with `$1.89 billion` from Maintenance Services and `$789.1 million` from Development Services.
- Profitability was still real but thinner than many other industrial names in the archive. Fiscal `2025` net income was `$56.0 million`. Maintenance Services generated segment adjusted EBITDA of `$245.5 million`, while Development Services generated `$106.8 million`.
- The quarter chain shows why the company is useful. Q1 FY2026 revenue rose to `$614.7 million` but net loss was `($15.2 million)`. Q2 FY2026 revenue rose to `$702.9 million` and net income turned positive at `$1.7 million`. Q3 FY2026 revenue rose again to `$717.6 million` and net income improved to `$6.1 million`. The earnings path is being shaped by weather, maintenance mix, development timing, fuel, self-insurance, and sales-force investment more than by a simple growth story.
- Maintenance Services is the stabilizer. The business repeatedly describes landscape maintenance as a fairly predictable recurring-revenue model, while Development Services remains more exposed to project timing and commercial-construction conditions.
- The labor file is the main reason BrightView matters. The `2025` `10-K` explicitly says the company historically used, and expects to continue using, the U.S. `H-2B` temporary non-immigrant visa program to satisfy part of its seasonal labor needs in certain markets. It employed approximately `1,500` seasonal workers through the program in `2025` and `2,000` in `2024`.
- That makes BrightView the archive's clearest direct `the-immigration-squeeze` anchor so far. The business does not just mention hiring pressure in the abstract. It discloses a measurable dependence on temporary-visa seasonal labor inside a recurring physical-services business.

## Open questions

- How much of the current earnings drag is temporary weather, self-insurance, and fuel noise versus a more durable margin ceiling in a labor-intensive national service model?
- Does BrightView become a cleaner recurring-services compounder if land maintenance keeps growing while development stays soft, or does that shift cap upside by reducing higher-margin project exposure?
