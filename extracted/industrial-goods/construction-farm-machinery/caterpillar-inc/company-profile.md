## What changed

- Revenue: full-year 2025 sales and revenues were `$67.589 billion`, up `4%` year over year. Q1 2026 sales and revenues rose to `$17.4 billion`, and Q2 2026 reached `$20.5 billion`, the first quarter above `$20 billion` in company history.
- Margin: 2025 operating profit margin was `16.5%` and adjusted operating profit margin was `17.2%`. Q1 2026 operating margin was `17.7%` and Q2 2026 rose to `20.9%`, with adjusted operating margin reaching `21.9%` in Q2.
- Guidance: Caterpillar entered 2026 with a constructive outlook tied to healthy end markets, strong backlog, positive price realization, and continued services growth. The 2026 filings continue to describe resilient end markets and stronger second-half throughput despite geopolitical uncertainty.
- Capital allocation: Caterpillar deployed `$7.9 billion` of cash for dividends and share repurchases in 2025. It deployed `$5.7 billion` in Q1 2026, including `$5.0 billion` of share repurchases, and another `$2.2 billion` in Q2 2026.
- Segment mix: the core earnings engine is spread across Power & Energy, Construction Industries, and Resource Industries, with Financial Products adding financing support. In Q2 2026, Power & Energy sales were `$8.238 billion`, Construction Industries sales were `$8.346 billion`, and Resource Industries sales were `$4.648 billion`.
- Backlog: order backlog was approximately `$51.2 billion` at December 31, 2025 versus `$30.0 billion` a year earlier. Q1 2026 call commentary said backlog reached a record `$63 billion`, showing that growth is being pulled forward rather than merely defended.
- Operational issues: tariff exposure remains a live operating variable. Q1 2026 materials flagged unfavorable manufacturing costs from higher tariff costs, while Q2 2026 results included `$392 million` of expected IEEPA tariff recoveries.
- Consumer / cultural signals: direct consumer demand is not the important read here. The real signals are infrastructure buildout, power demand, mining capital discipline, rental-fleet loading, and data-center-linked prime-power demand.
- Technology: AI matters indirectly through physical infrastructure. Caterpillar's annual and quarterly materials repeatedly tie power-generation demand to data centers, cloud computing, electrification, and grid reinforcement.
- Management tone: confident but operationally disciplined. Management is emphasizing broad end-market resilience, order momentum, and execution while still acknowledging tariffs, geopolitical uncertainty, and the need to expand throughput.

## Open questions

- How much of the current growth is durable end-user demand versus backlog conversion and dealer inventory timing?
- Can Power & Energy and data-center demand remain strong enough to offset future cyclicality in mining and construction equipment?
