## What changed

- The core mix shifted sharply toward enterprise and infrastructure demand. Q4 `2025` still showed residential weakness from a low-outage environment, but Q1 and Q2 `2026` increasingly became a C&I and data-center story as C&I external sales grew `28%` and then `29%`.
- Residential and enterprise resilience are now moving on different clocks. Residential generator demand remains tied to outage severity, storm comparisons, and consumer willingness to spend for peace of mind, while enterprise demand is tied to uptime requirements, telecom and rental channels, and hyperscale data-center capacity buildout.
- The company is broadening from generator hardware toward a fuller resilience stack. Annual materials frame Generac as a total energy solutions company spanning residential, commercial, data center, telecom, rental, and industrial markets, with monitoring, storage, energy technology, and grid-services participation layered on top.
- Data-center exposure turned from narrative to backlog. By Q2 `2026`, management had disclosed about `$1.6 billion` of data-center backlog excluding additional committed volumes from a second hyperscale customer, plus nearly `$700 million` of committed `2027` volume under one supply agreement.
- Margin quality improved, but the read requires caution. Q2 `2026` adjusted EBITDA margin reached `24.8%`, yet the quarter included about `$71 million` of tariff-refund benefit and management said that roughly `1.5%` of full-year adjusted EBITDA margin guidance reflected that refund impact.
- Capacity and vertical integration are becoming part of the thesis. The company highlighted Enercon, Allmand, and the Belvidere facility as ways to deepen control over large-megawatt packaging and execution for backup-power systems serving fast-growing mission-critical applications.

## Working interpretation

- Generac is one of the archive's clearest examples of how AI and cloud growth become physical-resilience demand before they appear as software economics. The company sells uptime, backup capacity, and grid insurance into both anxious households and increasingly power-constrained enterprises.
- The most useful recurring pattern is that the same social reality, less tolerance for disruption, supports two different businesses inside one company: weather-and-outage-sensitive residential demand and structurally rising enterprise uptime demand tied to data centers, telecom, rental fleets, and grid instability.

## Open questions

- How durable is the current hyperscale and data-center order surge once vendor approvals normalize and more backup-power capacity comes online across the industry?
- Can enterprise growth offset a prolonged normalization in residential standby demand if outage intensity remains subdued and homeowners defer large-ticket resilience purchases?
