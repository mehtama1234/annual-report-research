## What changed

- Watsco adds the HVAC replacement-and-distribution layer that sits between climate-control hardware demand and actual installation work in homes and businesses.
- The `2025` annual and first-half `2026` quarter chain show a business moving from transition management back toward normal growth conditions. The central change is the gradual completion of the A2L refrigerant transition, which had disrupted volumes, inventory, logistics, and contractor workflows.
- The company also shows how distribution moats now depend on software and data as much as trucks and branches. Watsco keeps emphasizing HVAC Pro+, e-commerce, pricing optimization, AI-driven initiatives, and a widening technology ecosystem.
- Sun Belt density remains central. The company’s largest physical and demand concentrations are in hot-weather, migration-heavy markets where HVAC replacement is a recurring necessity rather than a discretionary purchase.
- The M&A signal also sharpened in `2026` with Jackson Supply, which adds 25 Sun Belt locations and about `$230M` of annualized sales.

## Open questions

- How much of the recent gross-margin pressure is simply the unwind of unusual A2L-era pricing versus a return to structurally lower margin conditions?
- Can Watsco keep growing same-store sales once the regulatory transition tailwind and distortion fade completely?
- Does digital engagement meaningfully deepen customer lock-in, or does it mainly protect share in a still-fragmented market?
- How much additional working capital will be required as inventory is re-optimized and Jackson Supply is integrated?
