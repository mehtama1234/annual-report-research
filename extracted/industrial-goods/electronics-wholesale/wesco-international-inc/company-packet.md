# WESCO International, Inc. Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026

## Why this name matters

- WESCO is the right industrial-and-electronics-distribution flagship for this batch because it covers the stocked-inventory, procurement, logistics, and workflow layer that sits between infrastructure demand and actual project execution.
- It also fills a genuine gap in the archive. Core & Main covers water-and-fire channel power. United Rentals covers access over ownership. WESCO adds electrical, communications, security, utility, and broadband distribution at a scale that makes it a physical-layer platform rather than a simple wholesaler.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual report and `10-K` frame WESCO as a leading provider of business-to-business distribution, logistics services, and supply chain solutions rather than as a narrow box mover.
- Scale matters here because the model depends on density, availability, and workflow integration. The `10-K` says WESCO employed about `21,000` people, worked with more than `35,000` suppliers, served nearly `130,000` customers, and operated more than `700` sites in about `50` countries.
- The business mix is broad but coherent. WESCO organizes itself around `Electrical & Electronic Solutions`, `Communications & Security Solutions`, and `Utility & Broadband Solutions`, which together place it at the center of commercial construction, data-center fit-out, networking, utility projects, and broader industrial maintenance and upgrade cycles.
- `2025` was a record year. The `Q4 2025` / full-year results say net sales reached `$23.5B`, up `8%`, with organic sales up `9%` and data-center sales of `$4.3B`, up about `50%`.
- The annual and full-year materials make the thematic shift clear: WESCO is benefiting from AI-driven data centers, increased power generation, and supply-chain reshoring, while trying to turn that demand into more durable cross-sell, margin expansion, and working-capital efficiency through digitalization.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Net sales were `$6.665B`, up `13.0%` year over year and `9.6%` sequentially. Organic sales grew `12.6%`.
- Operating margin was `5.7%`, adjusted EBITDA margin was `7.3%`, diluted EPS was `$4.23`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$4.57`, operating cash flow was `$54M`, and free cash flow was `$32M`.
- Data-center sales reached `$1.5B`, up about `45%`, and management said WESCO had now posted four consecutive quarters of double-digit sales growth fueled by data centers.
- The most important qualitative change is backlog behavior. Management said backlog was up about `60%` to a record level, driven by multi-year customer commitments and secular infrastructure demand.
- The quarter also shows how WESCO is moving up the value stack. Management highlighted a significant multi-year grid-services award from a hyperscale data-center customer and described that as an important step in extending data-center exposure into power solutions, not just white-space and gray-space product categories.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Net sales were `$6.080B`, up `13.8%`, with organic sales up `12.3%`.
- Operating margin was `4.8%`, adjusted EBITDA margin was `6.4%`, diluted EPS was `$3.11`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$3.37`, operating cash flow was `$221.4M`, and free cash flow was `$213M`.
- Data-center sales were `$1.4B`, up about `70%`, and management said that already represented `24%` of total company sales.
- Backlog rose `22%` year over year to a record level, which is important because it shows the demand signal was broader than a single-quarter shipment spike.
- The quarter also reveals how execution matters in this model. Management tied earnings growth to gross-margin expansion and operating-cost leverage, not just volume, while the release separately showed digital-transformation costs running above the prior year.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Fourth-quarter net sales were `$6.1B`, up `10%`, with organic sales up `9.2%` and data-center sales of `$1.2B`, up about `30%`.
- Full-year net sales reached `$23.5B`, up `8%`, with full-year organic sales up `8.6%` and data-center sales of `$4.3B`, up about `50%`.
- Backlog finished the year up `19%`, and management explicitly linked that strength to AI-driven data centers, increased power generation, and supply-chain reshoring.
- Segment detail matters here. Management said `Communications & Security Solutions` and `Electrical & Electronic Solutions` performed very well, while `Utility & Broadband Solutions` faced continued public-power weakness even as investor-owned-utility demand improved.
- The quarter also exposed a real operating constraint: strong fourth-quarter sales pulled more working capital into receivables and inventory, which pressured cash generation and sharpened management’s focus on working-capital improvement in `2026`.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- WESCO is not consumer-facing in the usual sense, but it reflects a major social and industrial shift: more of the economy runs through procurement platforms that customers rely on for availability, technical complexity, logistics, and speed rather than trying to self-manage fragmented supplier bases.
- This is one of the clearest physical-economy expressions of AI hype turning into real-world purchase orders. Data-center growth is not just a software or chip story; it also requires wire, cable, connectors, networking gear, power equipment, cooling-adjacent systems, and site-level supply-chain coordination.
- The company also sits inside a wider civic and institutional build cycle. Utilities, telecom providers, commercial buildings, security systems, and industrial operators all increasingly need upgrade work that depends on distributors with large catalogs, local presence, and reliable service.
- There is a cultural dependence angle too. Enterprises and contractors want fewer counterparties, tighter working-capital control, and less procurement friction. That pushes value toward the distributor that can bundle products, services, and supply-chain tools into one customer workflow.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- WESCO is still a margin-sensitive distribution business. Growth can be strong, but earnings depend on mix, gross margin discipline, SG&A leverage, working-capital turns, and the timing of customer project releases.
- Segment mix matters. `CSS` and `EES` were the strongest performers into late `2025`, while `UBS` showed how exposure to slower public-power customers can drag margin and earnings even when the broader company is growing.
- Working capital is a recurring pressure point. The `Q4 2025` release explicitly said higher receivables and inventory tied to strong sales affected quarter cash generation, and the quarter-to-quarter cash pattern in `2026` stayed lumpy even with better first-half growth.
- Digital transformation is strategically important but not free. `Q1 2026` and `Q2 2026` both carried meaningfully higher digital-transformation costs, which means management is paying now for hoped-for future gains in pricing, cross-sell, operating leverage, and working-capital efficiency.
- The risk language around tariffs, export controls, logistics constraints, cybersecurity, AI deployment, and broader demand volatility matters because WESCO is a central node in the physical supply chain rather than a business insulated from those frictions.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Distribution scale becomes infrastructure when customers depend on it to keep projects moving.` WESCO is a good example of how channel control and procurement integration can become a moat in physical industries.
- `AI demand monetizes far beyond chips and software.` WESCO shows how data-center spending flows into electrical, communications, cooling-adjacent, power, and grid-service categories.
- `Backlog quality matters as much as headline sales growth.` The company’s recent quarters are notable because record backlog and multi-year commitments suggest a deeper project pipeline rather than a one-quarter restocking burst.
- `Digital transformation is now part of the industrial margin story.` For WESCO, data lakes, AI-enabled workflow improvement, and a single tech stack are being treated as tools for pricing, cross-sell, working-capital turns, and operating leverage.
- `The physical layer behind enterprise and utility modernization is increasingly platform-dependent.` Customers want fewer suppliers, broader product access, and more integrated service, which shifts value toward distributors that can function like operating platforms.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Core & Main`: Core & Main is a more category-specific water and fire-protection distributor, while WESCO is the broader electrical, communications, security, and utility analogue with more obvious exposure to data-center and grid themes.
- Against `United Rentals`: United Rentals monetizes equipment access and fleet utilization; WESCO monetizes stocked inventory, procurement integration, logistics, and supplier-customer workflow control.
- Against `Mueller Industries`: Mueller shows component and installed-system manufacturing economics, while WESCO shows the high-scale channel layer that moves those kinds of products into real projects and customer operations.
