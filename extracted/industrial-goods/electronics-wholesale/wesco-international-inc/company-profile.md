## What changed

- Operating identity: WESCO is not just a catalog distributor. The `10-K` frames it as a business-to-business distribution, logistics, and supply-chain-solutions platform with millions of products, end-to-end services, and significant digital capabilities.
- Scale and reach: the annual filing says WESCO employed about `21,000` people, worked with more than `35,000` suppliers, served nearly `130,000` customers, and operated more than `700` sites in about `50` countries.
- Growth engine: by `2025` and the first half of `2026`, data-center demand became the clearest growth accelerator, but management repeatedly tied strength to a wider infrastructure set that also includes communications, security, electrical, utility, and power markets.
- Mix and pressure shifted: CSS and EES were the strongest contributors in late `2025`, while UBS faced public-power softness before the `2026` hyperscale and grid-services momentum began to broaden that segment’s setup.
- Transformation matters: management increasingly presents digitalization, AI use, cross-selling, and working-capital improvement as operating levers, not just support functions.

## Open questions

- How durable is data-center-driven growth if hyperscale timing slips or ordering gets lumpier after the current build cycle?
- Can UBS convert backlog and grid awards into cleaner margin improvement after the public-power softness flagged in `Q4 2025`?
- Will digital-transformation spending keep producing enough margin, pricing, and working-capital gains to justify the cost load?
