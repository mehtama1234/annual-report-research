# AECOM Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: AECOM
- Sector: Industrial Goods
- Industry: Engineering Construction
- Ticker: `ACM`
- Fiscal year-end: September
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Why this name matters

- AECOM fills a different infrastructure layer than the heavier contractor and materials names already in this batch. It monetizes design, advisory, and program-management complexity rather than aggregate reserves, rental fleets, utility hardware, or self-perform civil construction.
- That makes it a useful flagship comparison for the physical layer behind AI and software. When data-center, grid, transport, water, and defense buildouts happen, someone still captures value at the planning, permitting, architecture, engineering, and program-orchestration layer before dirt work and installed hardware scale.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual chain describes AECOM as a leading global provider of professional infrastructure consulting and advisory services. The core model is fee-based and knowledge-based rather than commodity- or equipment-based.
- The company says it is the largest general architectural and engineering design firm in the world by `2024` design revenue and the number one ranked water, transportation design, facilities design, environmental engineering, environmental consulting, and environmental science firm in the world according to ENR’s `2025` survey.
- Full-year `2025` revenue was `$16.140B`, adjusted NSR was `$7.573B`, adjusted operating income was `$1.097B`, adjusted EPS was `$5.26`, adjusted EBITDA was `$1.203B`, adjusted EBITDA margin was `16.8%`, and free cash flow was `$685M`.
- The annual read is less about top-line explosiveness and more about mix quality, win rates, and margin expansion. Management emphasized a record backlog, a record pipeline, an `80%` win rate on its largest pursuits, and the achievement of its prior `17%` segment adjusted operating-margin target more than a year ahead of prior expectations.
- The company is also openly reshaping the business around higher-returning lanes. The `Q4 2025` event and investor-day materials framed advisory, AI deployment, and high-value design work as the core long-term earnings engine, and management began a strategic review of the construction-management business before later deciding to retain it.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Revenue increased `1%` to `$3.801B`, adjusted NSR increased to `$1.948B`, adjusted operating income increased `7%` to `$280M`, adjusted EPS increased `27%` to `$1.59`, and total backlog increased `8%` to `$26.204B`.
- The most important quality signal was not GAAP revenue growth. It was that the Americas design business grew `8%` on a constant-currency basis and helped drive a new second-quarter record `16.5%` segment adjusted operating margin.
- The latest quarter also sharpens the geographic split. Americas second-quarter adjusted operating margin reached `20.0%`, while International NSR declined `3%` because of weakness in Asia and the Middle East.
- The company raised full-year guidance for a second consecutive quarter. Adjusted EPS moved to `$5.90-$6.10`, adjusted EBITDA moved to `$1.275B-$1.305B`, full-year adjusted EBITDA margin stayed at `17.0%`, and free cash flow guidance remained about `$400M`.
- The pressure point was cash conversion rather than backlog demand. Free cash flow was negative `($27M)` in the quarter because delayed payment timing in the Middle East and longer-than-anticipated claim resolution weighed on collections, even though management said collections had already recovered in the fiscal third quarter.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Revenue was `$3.831B`, adjusted NSR was `$1.851B`, adjusted operating income was `$264M`, adjusted EPS was `$1.29`, adjusted EBITDA was `$287M`, and total backlog was `$25.962B`.
- The quarter showed why AECOM belongs in this archive as a government- and planning-layer infrastructure name rather than just a generic engineering firm. Management said backlog increased by `9%`, driven by a `1.5x` book-to-burn ratio even while navigating an unprecedented `43`-day U.S. federal government shutdown.
- The strategic message was that AECOM’s moat is getting wider rather than narrower. Management tied the quarter to continued investment in advisory, program management, technology, AI, and business development.
- The transcript also makes the demand picture more concrete. Management highlighted Scottish Water, Brisbane `2032`, Dubai Metro, private-sector data-center work, and defense-related demand as visible proof points that the company is participating in both public-budget and private-capex infrastructure cycles.
- Guidance rose after the quarter. Adjusted EPS moved to `$5.85-$6.05`, adjusted EBITDA moved to `$1.270B-$1.305B`, free cash flow guidance stayed around `$400M`, and the adjusted tax-rate assumption moved lower to `20-22%`.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Q4 revenue was `$4.175B`, adjusted NSR was `$1.967B`, adjusted operating income was `$299M`, adjusted EPS was `$1.36`, adjusted EBITDA was `$329M`, adjusted EBITDA margin was `17.5%`, and total backlog was `$24.830B`.
- Full-year `2025` adjusted NSR was `$7.573B`, adjusted EPS was `$5.26`, adjusted EBITDA was `$1.203B`, and free cash flow was `$685M`.
- The most important year-end signal was backlog quality. Management said total backlog increased `4%`, design backlog increased `3%`, and the company delivered its `20th` consecutive quarter with a book-to-burn ratio above `1.0`.
- This was also the point where management more explicitly connected margin expansion to AI and advisory. Fiscal `2026` guidance assumed accelerating investment in the development and deployment of AI and continued expansion of the Advisory team even while targeting another year of profit growth.
- The strategic-review announcement for construction management also matters for interpretation. It showed management’s willingness to treat capital allocation and executive attention as scarce resources and to prioritize the fastest-growing and highest-returning parts of the platform.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- AECOM is not consumer-facing in the traditional sense, but it sits upstream of systems people increasingly experience as social and political necessities: reliable water, transport capacity, flood mitigation, defense infrastructure, energy reliability, and public-facility modernization.
- The company also captures a broader shift in how infrastructure is being bought. Clients do not only want drawings and project staffing. They increasingly want a strategic partner that can integrate planning, permitting, funding navigation, program management, technology deployment, and risk transfer across larger and more multidimensional projects.
- This packet also helps preserve a cultural shift that sits beneath the AI boom. As digital demand rises, the value does not stop with chips, cloud platforms, and data-center landlords. It also flows to firms that can help design the water, energy, environmental, and facilities systems needed to support faster electrical-load growth and more complex development timelines.
- There is also a state-capacity angle here. AECOM benefits when governments and large institutions have money to spend but need outside technical expertise to actually convert authorization into delivery. That makes it a useful read on public-sector outsourcing, procurement complexity, and the professional-services layer required to execute modern infrastructure agendas.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Government and public-program dependence remains central. IIJA funding, multiyear transportation authorizations, water-cycle programs, defense budgets, and sovereign infrastructure strategies all shape the demand base.
- International execution is less clean than the Americas read. The `Q2 2026` materials point to slower collections and claim resolution in the Middle East, while the `10-Q` shows weaker International gross profit driven by Asia and the Middle East.
- Working capital matters because this is still a project business. DSO moved from `74` days at `2025-09-30` to `83` days at `2026-03-31`, and management had to defend the free-cash-flow story even while operations remained strong.
- The company’s margin story relies on continued discipline, restructuring carryover, enterprise capability centers, and higher-margin advisory growth. That is a more execution-sensitive formula than a simple price-led commodity cycle.
- Balance-sheet risk is manageable but not irrelevant. The `Q2 2026` release cited net leverage of `1.2x`, while the `Q2 2026` `10-Q` shows total debt around `$2.748B` and a newly amended credit-agreement structure, which matters if capital markets or project funding conditions tighten.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Infrastructure demand does not only monetize through contractors and materials.` AECOM shows how value accrues to the design, advisory, and program-management layer before physical construction is completed.
- `Public funding still needs private execution capacity.` Large state and municipal budgets are not enough on their own; outside technical expertise becomes a monetizable bottleneck.
- `AI can expand margins in old-economy service businesses before it becomes a pure software revenue story.` AECOM is trying to use proprietary AI to improve design productivity, strengthen proposals, and capture value in commercial terms, not just lower internal costs.
- `Backlog quality matters more than raw revenue growth.` The company’s strategy is centered on winning more valuable projects at higher rates, then converting that into better NSR mix and margin rather than chasing undifferentiated scale.
- `International growth can be strategically attractive but financially messy.` The same global footprint that expands addressable market can also create claim-resolution, collection, geopolitical, and margin-volatility pressure.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Sterling Infrastructure, Inc.`: Sterling monetizes site development, E-Infrastructure, and project execution tied to data centers and semiconductors, while AECOM monetizes the design, planning, and program-management layer upstream of those physical builds.
- Against `Granite Construction Incorporated`: Granite is the public-works and materials-execution read; AECOM is the advisory, design, and owner-side orchestration read on many of the same transport, water, and government budgets.
- Against `Valmont Industries, Inc.`: Valmont captures value in utility structures, coatings, and grid-support hardware, while AECOM captures value earlier in the infrastructure lifecycle through strategy, engineering, and delivery oversight.
