## Quarter-by-quarter operating read

### Q1 2026

- Revenue rose `12.3%` year over year to `$3.293 billion`.
- Adjusted net revenue rose `8.2%` to `$2.253 billion`.
- Adjusted EPS rose to `$1.53`.
- Backlog reached `$26.308 billion`, including about `$25.902 billion` in `I&AF` and `$406 million` in `PA Consulting`.
- `I&AF` revenue increased to `$2.939 billion`; `PA Consulting` revenue increased to `$354 million`.
- Remaining performance obligations were about `$18.5 billion`, with `46%` expected inside twelve months.
- Management framed the strongest backlog growth around `Advanced Manufacturing`, `Life Sciences`, and `Water`.

### Q2 2026

- Revenue rose `27.0%` year over year to `$3.695 billion`.
- Adjusted net revenue rose `8.8%` to `$2.328 billion`.
- GAAP net income flipped to a loss because of PA acquisition accounting and transaction costs, but adjusted EBITDA still rose `14.2%` to `$327.2 million`.
- Backlog reached `$26.965 billion`.
- Management said `I&AF` growth was led by `Data Center`, `Semiconductor`, `Water`, `Energy & Power`, and `Transportation`.
- Jacobs completed the PA Consulting acquisition on `2026-03-20`, paying about `£1.21 billion` upfront through mostly cash plus `2,043,537` newly issued shares, with another `£75 million` due in fiscal `2028`.
- Liquidity at `2026-03-27` included `$1.37 billion` of cash and cash equivalents and `$994.7 million` of available revolving-credit capacity.
- Remaining performance obligations rose to about `$19.0 billion`, with `47%` expected inside twelve months.

### Q3 2026

- Revenue rose `34.5%` year over year to `$4.076 billion`.
- Adjusted net revenue rose `8.3%` to `$2.417 billion`.
- GAAP net earnings were `$137.4 million`; adjusted EBITDA rose `16.7%` to `$366.8 million`.
- Backlog reached `$28.9 billion`.
- `I&AF` gross revenue rose `39%` year over year, while adjusted net revenue rose `10%` organically.
- Management again named `Data Center`, `Semiconductor`, `Energy & Power`, `Transportation`, and `Water` as the main growth drivers.
- Q3 press-release language explicitly tied performance to AI-compute build-out and increasingly complex manufacturing and compute-facility delivery.
- Remaining performance obligations reached about `$22.2 billion`, with `45%` expected inside twelve months.
- Liquidity at `2026-06-26` included `$1.17 billion` of cash and cash equivalents and the full `$1.50 billion` of available revolving-credit capacity.

## What changed across the window

- Jacobs moved from a post-separation reset story in fiscal `2025` into a cleaner infrastructure-and-consulting growth story in fiscal `2026`.
- Backlog rose from `$23.1 billion` at fiscal year-end to `$28.9 billion` by `Q3 2026`.
- The growth mix shifted further toward advanced facilities, data centers, semiconductors, utility work, water, and related program delivery.
- PA Consulting moved from partially owned contributor to fully controlled consulting platform, but the integration also created tax, compensation, and transaction noise that temporarily distorted GAAP optics.
- Liquidity remained solid even after the PA transaction because the company combined debt issuance, continuing cash generation, and active share repurchases rather than pausing capital returns completely.

## Working interpretation

- Jacobs is now less about classic cyclical engineering and more about being a systems integrator for expensive, complex, compliance-heavy physical build-outs.
- The strongest recurring pattern is that AI demand is not only helping chipmakers and data-center landlords; it is also pulling through design, water, cooling, utility, transport, and program-management spend.
- The company’s revenue quality depends on whether management can keep converting high-level AI and infrastructure enthusiasm into profitable, repeatable `I&AF` execution rather than into low-margin pass-through volume alone.

## Open questions

- How much of the current AI-linked work remains concentrated in a few hyperscaler and semiconductor programs versus broadening into a durable multi-client installed base?
- After the PA acquisition closes into the comparable base, does consulting remain a margin support layer or become a source of integration drag and capital-allocation debate?
- Can backlog continue to expand without a larger mix of lower-quality pass-through revenue suppressing perceived operating leverage?
