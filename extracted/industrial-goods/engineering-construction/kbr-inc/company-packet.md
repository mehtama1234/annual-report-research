# KBR, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: KBR, Inc.
- Sector: Industrial Goods
- Industry: Engineering & Construction
- Ticker: KBR
- Fiscal year-end: early January
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Why this name matters

- KBR is the right next flagship because it adds the engineering, logistics, mission-support, and program-delivery layer that sits underneath both federal modernization and industrial buildout.
- It is especially useful as a bridge name. CACI and Leidos show the software, cyber, and workflow side of government technology. KBR shows the adjacent execution layer: defense support, readiness, space-related programs, civilian-agency contracts, industrial project delivery, and sustainable-process infrastructure.

## Annual report takeaways

- Fiscal `2025` revenue reached `$7.786B`, up from `$7.710B` in fiscal `2024`, while KBR-attributable net income reached `$415M` versus `$375M` in fiscal `2024`. This was not a breakout year, but it was a solid year of continued expansion with higher earnings and stronger cash.
- The segment split is the real story. Fiscal `2025` revenue was `$5.581B` in `Mission Technology Solutions` and `$2.205B` in `Sustainable Technology Solutions`. That split makes KBR a hybrid of federal mission contractor and commercial engineering/process-infrastructure operator.
- The annual backlog picture stayed large and credible. Backlog ended fiscal `2025` at `$16.864B`, up from `$16.605B`, with award options rising to `$6.347B` from `$3.975B`. Importantly, the company said HomeSafe was excluded from backlog because the disposal met discontinued-operations treatment.
- Customer mix reinforces the federal dependence on the MTS side. In fiscal `2025`, KBR generated `$3.370B` from U.S. government defense and intelligence clients, `$1.057B` from U.S. federal civilian clients, and `$905M` from international government clients. Commercial and infrastructure clients contributed only `$249M` within MTS but `$2.205B` inside STS.
- Contract type matters. Fiscal `2025` revenue included `$3.305B` of cost-reimbursable work, `$2.311B` of time-and-materials work, and `$2.170B` of fixed-price work. That means margin quality depends not only on demand but on execution discipline, contract structure, and working-capital timing.
- The annual filing also makes the strategic shift explicit: KBR planned to spin off `Mission Technology Solutions`. That raises the quality of the comparison set because KBR is not just running the current portfolio; it is actively trying to separate a federal mission-tech platform from a commercial and process-technology platform.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: revenue reached `$1.984B` versus `$1.952B` a year earlier. Net income attributable to KBR reached `$96M` versus `$73M`, and diluted EPS attributable to KBR reached `$0.75` versus `$0.56`.
- Segment mix: `MTS` revenue was `$1.308B`, while `STS` revenue was `$676M`. Government defense and intelligence clients remained the largest quarterly customer class at `$834M`.
- Demand read: total backlog rose to `$17.805B` from `$16.864B` at year-end `2025`. `MTS` backlog slipped to `$12.282B` from `$12.552B`, but `STS` backlog jumped to `$5.523B` from `$4.312B`.
- Operating pressures: the planned spin-off was now materially affecting results. KBR said it was targeting completion on `January 4, 2027` and recognized `$31M` of spin-off costs in the quarter and `$46M` year to date, including office-footprint impairment charges.
- Structural themes: Q2 shows KBR as a company being pulled in two directions at once. Federal mission support remains stable and large, but commercial and sustainable-technology backlog is the faster-growth side of the current story.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: revenue was `$1.923B` versus `$2.018B` a year earlier. By segment, `MTS` was `$1.296B` and `STS` was `$627M`.
- Customer mix: U.S. defense and intelligence clients remained the anchor at `$816M`, with federal civilian clients at `$262M`. STS was still overwhelmingly tied to commercial and infrastructure clients, which contributed `$607M` in the quarter.
- Demand read: remaining performance obligations were about `$13.6B`, with around `36%` expected to convert within one year.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the cleanest quarter for seeing soft federal mission mix versus relatively steadier industrial and process-technology work. It also reinforces that KBR is not just a generic engineering firm; it is a portfolio of contract structures and customer types with meaningfully different risk and demand cycles.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: annual revenue reached `$7.786B`, and KBR-attributable net income reached `$415M`. Operating income reached `$778M`, up from `$659M` in fiscal `2024`.
- Backlog read: total backlog was `$16.864B`, with `MTS` at `$12.712B` and `STS` at `$4.152B`. Total backlog plus options reached `$23.211B`.
- Portfolio read: `Readiness & Sustainment`, defense and intelligence, federal civilian work, and commercial and infrastructure projects all remained material. The filing also notes the August `2024` LinQuest acquisition as a contributor to defense and intel program growth.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is where KBR most clearly reads as an execution-and-orchestration company rather than a pure construction or pure services name. It monetizes ongoing program complexity, maintenance and readiness, engineering capacity, and long-cycle client dependence.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- KBR is not consumer-facing, but it sits directly under several large societal shifts: persistent geopolitical tension, rising defense and readiness demand, public-sector dependence on outsourced technical execution, and industrial decarbonization or process-upgrade spending.
- The cultural pattern is that “infrastructure” increasingly includes invisible technical services, program management, and maintenance ecosystems. Consumers may see AI, broadband, or national-security headlines at the top layer, but companies like KBR get paid in the middle layer where the work is specified, engineered, and kept operating.
- There is also a sovereignty and resilience angle. Defense support, readiness, and federal civilian contracts reflect a broader willingness to outsource technically dense but mission-critical functions to trusted contractors, even as governments want more control over the end mission.
- On the industrial side, STS reflects another societal shift: heavy industry and energy systems are being modernized, optimized, and reframed through sustainability, process-technology, and infrastructure-efficiency language rather than only through old commodity-capex language.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Contract mix is a constant economic pressure point. Cost-reimbursable work is lower-risk on inflation and scope creep, while fixed-price work can turn estimating mistakes into margin pressure.
- Backlog quality is more important than raw backlog size. KBR explicitly says contracts can be cancelled, renegotiated, or phased differently than expected, and that only the portion thought probable is included. That matters because this is a business where reported visibility can still shift with client behavior.
- The MTS spin-off introduces real execution risk. Management is trying to simplify the portfolio, but the `Q2 2026` filing already shows direct spin-off costs, office-footprint impairments, and the risk of stranded corporate expense.
- Federal dependence remains large, especially within MTS. The company is exposed to defense and intelligence budgets, civilian-agency priorities, contract awards, audits, and procurement timing in ways that are different from commercial industrial businesses.
- STS ties KBR to industrial capex cycles, energy and LNG projects, and commercial infrastructure execution. That can diversify the company, but it also means process-industry investment discipline matters alongside federal budget discipline.
- Liquidity and working-capital timing matter. Cash fell from `$500M` at fiscal year-end to `$312M` by `Q2 2026`, even as long-term debt remained above `$2.5B`. This is a business where the timing of project receipts and obligations still matters a great deal.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Infrastructure is not only owned assets.` KBR shows the service-and-execution layer that designs, runs, maintains, and upgrades infrastructure after ownership has been allocated elsewhere.
- `Government technology demand often requires engineering and logistics partners, not just software vendors.` CACI and Leidos explain the mission software and workflow layer; KBR explains the fielded, engineered, sustained, and contract-executed layer.
- `Recurring revenue can come from program complexity.` Readiness, sustainment, federal civilian support, and long-term project-management obligations create repetition even when the revenue is not labeled subscription.
- `Industrial modernization and defense modernization increasingly sit closer together.` KBR combines defense support, civilian federal work, and sustainable technology/process infrastructure inside one company, which is useful for understanding how physical and institutional infrastructure stories overlap.
- `Spin-offs are often attempts to separate unlike economic engines.` KBR’s planned `2027` MTS spin-off is an explicit admission that federal mission-tech economics and commercial process-technology economics may deserve different valuation lenses and strategic priorities.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Leidos Holdings, Inc.` and `CACI International Inc.`: KBR is less purely digital or intelligence-workflow-driven and more operationally tied to engineering, logistics, readiness, and program execution.
- Against `MasTec, Inc.` and `Quanta Services, Inc.`: those names are closer to visible physical project execution in utility, communications, and infrastructure construction, while KBR sits higher in technical program delivery and federal mission support.
- Against `AECOM`: AECOM is more asset-light advisory, design, and program management, while KBR has a heavier federal mission-services mix and more direct exposure to contract-type economics and industrial process execution.

## Working interpretation

- KBR materially improves the archive because it adds the program-management, engineering, logistics, and sustainment layer that turns public and private infrastructure priorities into actual operational delivery.
- It also strengthens the broader theme the user has emphasized: the big patterns are not just annual-report collection, but repeated structures. KBR shows one of the strongest repeated structures in the repo so far, namely that AI, defense, energy transition, and industrial modernization all depend on middle-layer contractors that coordinate technical complexity even when they do not own the final asset or platform.
