## What changed

- KBR is increasingly a two-speed company. `Mission Technology Solutions` carries federal mission-support, defense, civilian-agency, space, and readiness exposure, while `Sustainable Technology Solutions` carries commercial and infrastructure exposure tied to engineering, program delivery, and energy-transition projects.
- Fiscal `2025` was still a growth year. Revenue rose to `$7.786B` from `$7.710B`, KBR-attributable net income rose to `$415M` from `$375M`, and cash and cash equivalents ended the year at `$500M`.
- Backlog expanded but shifted in quality and mix. Total backlog rose from `$16.605B` at the prior year-end to `$16.864B` at `Q4 2025`, then to `$17.805B` by `Q2 2026`. The increase came from `STS`, while `MTS` backlog softened modestly.
- The spin-off became a real operating event, not just a strategic talking point. By `Q2 2026`, KBR was targeting completion of the MTS spin-off on `January 4, 2027` and had already recognized `$31M` of spin-off costs in the quarter and `$46M` year to date.
- Balance-sheet liquidity declined through the first half of `2026`. Cash fell from `$500M` at year-end to `$380M` in `Q1 2026` and `$312M` in `Q2 2026`, while long-term debt moved from about `$2.547B` to `$2.503B`.

## Working interpretation

- KBR belongs in the archive because it extends the infrastructure map beyond owned assets and recurring rent streams into the engineering, mission-support, logistics, and delivery layer that converts federal demand and industrial capex into executed programs.
- It also gives the repo a cleaner read on a recurring pattern that keeps showing up: infrastructure is not only ownership of physical assets, but also control over the technical and operational workflows that design, sustain, and coordinate them.

## Open questions

- Does the planned `2027` MTS spin-off unlock cleaner valuation and strategic focus, or does it expose stranded costs and reduce diversification just as federal procurement and mission complexity remain volatile?
- How durable is STS growth if commercial and infrastructure customers slow large energy, LNG, or process-technology projects after the current ordering cycle?
