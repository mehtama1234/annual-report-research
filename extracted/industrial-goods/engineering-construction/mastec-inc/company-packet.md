# MasTec, Inc. Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: MasTec, Inc.
- Sector: Industrial Goods
- Industry: Engineering Construction

- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Why this name matters

- MasTec is the right contractor-heavy flagship for this batch because it captures the project-execution layer between equipment, materials, utility demand, telecom buildout, pipeline activity, and clean-energy deployment.
- It also fills a real archive gap. United Rentals shows fleet access. WESCO and Applied show channel power. MasTec shows what happens when those physical inputs have to be engineered, assembled, installed, upgraded, and maintained at scale across the grid, fiber, pipeline, and clean-energy stack.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual report is unusually explicit about why MasTec belongs in this archive. The shareholder letter says the company built over `15,000` miles of telecommunications networks, laid more than `1,000` miles of oil and gas pipelines, strung or buried more than `14,000` miles of power transmission and distribution lines, and installed more than `3.6` gigawatts of renewable energy in `2025`.
- Management also states the core thesis in plain language: MasTec builds the energy and telecommunications backbone that makes AI a reality. That makes it one of the clearest physical-layer reads in the repo.
- Fiscal `2025` was a record year. The annual materials say revenue rose `16%` to `$14.3B`, net income rose `112%` to `$422.0M`, adjusted EBITDA rose `14%` to about `$1.2B`, and 18-month backlog stood at `$19.0B` at year-end.
- The annual filing frames MasTec as a diversified North American infrastructure engineering and construction company focused on communications, power delivery, pipeline infrastructure, and clean energy and infrastructure. That diversification matters because it reduces dependence on any single infrastructure lane while still keeping the company exposed to the same broad build cycle.
- Risk framing is equally important. The `10-K` emphasizes project-management execution, fixed-price estimate risk, permitting delays, changes to IIJA and IRA support, tariff and trade-policy shifts, labor availability, material costs, customer capex timing, and the concentration that comes from relying on a limited number of large customers.
- Scale is material here. The `10-K` says MasTec had about `36,000` employees and `810` locations at the end of `2025`, which reinforces that this is not a niche contractor but a large execution platform with meaningful labor, fleet, and scheduling complexity.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Revenue was `$4.374B`, up `23%` year over year, diluted EPS was `$1.65`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$2.22`, GAAP net income was `$145.7M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$384.2M`.
- Backlog reached a new record of `$21.4B`, up `$4.9B` year over year and `$1.1B` sequentially, with the release highlighting `58%` year-over-year growth in Clean Energy and Infrastructure.
- The quarter matters because it shows broad-based execution, not just one-hot-segment noise. Management highlighted revenue growth across communications, clean energy and infrastructure, power delivery, and pipeline.
- The Superior acquisition adds a new angle. Management described Superior as a full-service electrical contractor and a recognized leader in building data-center infrastructure, which expands MasTec’s relevance to hyperscale and mission-critical buildouts rather than only utility and pipeline work.
- Guidance was updated again, with full-year `2026` outlook moving to revenue of about `$18.2B`, GAAP diluted EPS of `$6.20`, adjusted diluted EPS of `$9.30`, and adjusted EBITDA of `$1.6B`.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Revenue was `$3.829B`, up `34%` year over year, diluted EPS was `$0.77`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.39`, GAAP net income was `$69.7M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$283.6M`.
- Backlog hit `$20.3B`, up `$4.4B` year over year and `$1.4B` from the prior quarter, led by `65%` year-over-year growth in Clean Energy and Infrastructure.
- This quarter shows how quickly MasTec’s demand base was improving entering `2026`. Management highlighted double-digit growth in all operating segments, led by a `91%` increase in Pipeline Infrastructure and `45%` growth in Clean Energy and Infrastructure.
- The quarter also matters because margin and guidance both inflected. Management said the company exceeded guidance in all respects and raised full-year outlook to diluted EPS of `$6.77` and adjusted diluted EPS of `$8.79`.
- Operationally, the quarter suggests MasTec is not simply riding awards; it is converting them into revenue and earnings with improved discipline across project mix, productivity, and execution.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Fourth-quarter revenue was a record `$3.9B`, up `16%`, diluted EPS was `$1.81`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$2.07`, GAAP net income was `$153.1M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$338.2M`.
- Full-year `2025` revenue was a record `$14.3B`, diluted EPS was `$5.07`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$6.55`, GAAP net income was `$422.0M`, and adjusted EBITDA was about `$1.2B`.
- The main signal here is demand depth. Management described demand across energy, communications, power, and infrastructure markets as unprecedented, with backlog up `13%` sequentially to `$19.0B` at year-end.
- The segment mix also sharpens the story. Management said fourth-quarter growth was led by `50%` year-over-year growth in Pipeline, `23%` growth in Communications, and double-digit growth in Power Delivery and renewables within Clean Energy and Infrastructure.
- Initial `2026` guidance implied another step up, with management calling for strong `19%` revenue growth and `26%` adjusted EBITDA growth, showing that the company viewed the backlog surge as a real multi-quarter revenue engine.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- MasTec is not consumer-facing, but it is one of the clearest ways to read how social and industrial priorities get turned into physical projects. AI, electrification, grid hardening, renewable deployment, pipeline safety, and telecom capacity all need actual crews, equipment, rights-of-way, permitting, and project management.
- The annual letter’s AI framing is not marketing fluff. It is a useful cultural marker: society is talking about AI as software and chips, while MasTec is monetizing the hard-hat and right-of-way side of the same build cycle.
- There is also a public-policy layer here. Infrastructure spending, clean-energy incentives, utility resilience, and domestic buildout matter because they create or delay MasTec’s work pipeline. That makes the company a real transmission point between politics and physical output.
- Labor is another social theme, not just an operating input. A company with tens of thousands of field workers becomes a referendum on whether the U.S. can still mobilize skilled labor at scale to actually build things, not just announce them.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- MasTec’s model is heavily execution-sensitive. Revenue and backlog can look strong while earnings still depend on project selection, estimating discipline, change-order recovery, labor productivity, fleet use, and schedule management.
- Policy dependence is real. The `10-K` explicitly calls out IIJA, IRA, permitting, tariff actions, and tax-law changes, which means the demand story is partly exposed to shifts in government support and regulatory timing.
- Fixed-price and large-project risk matter. Contractors can carry backlog for years, but if cost estimates prove wrong or project timing slips, the earnings bridge can change quickly.
- Labor and material pressure remain recurring issues. The filing highlights labor availability, material and equipment costs, supply-chain issues, and weather or climate-related disruption, all of which can hit margins even when demand stays strong.
- Acquisition integration is becoming more important. Superior broadens MasTec’s data-center and electrical capabilities, but it also raises the usual questions about integration, systems, internal controls, and whether the expected synergies show up cleanly.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Backlog is a demand indicator, not a guarantee.` MasTec shows why backlog matters in infrastructure, but also why investors have to read conversion quality, mix, and margin discipline rather than taking backlog at face value.
- `AI demand monetizes through contractors too.` Data centers, power transmission, substations, fiber routes, and cooling-adjacent electrical systems all need execution partners, not just component suppliers.
- `Industrial policy and physical delivery are different steps.` Funding, incentives, and demand narratives only become economic output if contractors can permit, staff, schedule, and execute the work.
- `Diversification can smooth but not eliminate cycle risk.` MasTec’s multi-segment setup helps, but the company is still tied to capital cycles, energy infrastructure, public-policy support, and large-customer spending plans.
- `The physical layer behind digital and energy transitions remains labor-heavy.` This is not a pure software or asset-light platform story. It is a people, equipment, and execution story with all the complexity that implies.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `United Rentals`: United Rentals supplies the access model for fleet and site equipment; MasTec is the operator that turns rented equipment and labor into delivered infrastructure.
- Against `WESCO International`: WESCO supplies electrical and communications distribution into projects; MasTec executes the field build and installation work that closes the loop.
- Against `Applied Industrial Technologies`: Applied supports uptime and technical maintenance inside industrial systems; MasTec captures the large-scale build and upgrade phase for external infrastructure systems.
