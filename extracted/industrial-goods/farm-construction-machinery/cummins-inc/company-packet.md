## Why this name matters

- Cummins is the right next flagship for the archive because it adds the engine, distributed-power, service-network, and heavy-duty infrastructure layer that sits underneath freight, construction, industrial uptime, and increasingly data-center resilience.
- It also helps the archive avoid an overly narrow view of physical AI infrastructure. Generac and Vertiv show backup power and thermal density from different angles, but Cummins shows how data-center demand also pulls through large generator platforms, natural-gas microgrids, global service capacity, and powertrain-adjacent industrial economics.

## Annual report takeaways

- Cummins is no longer best understood only as a diesel-engine manufacturer. The `2025` annual materials frame it as a global power leader spanning five segments: Engine, Components, Distribution, Power Systems, and Accelera. That matters because the company’s economics now come from a broader installed base and infrastructure-service footprint than from truck engines alone.
- The annual letter makes the current mix shift explicit. Full-year `2025` revenue was `$33.7 billion` and EBITDA was `$5.4 billion`, or `16.0%` of sales, despite a North America truck downturn. Management specifically said Distribution and Power Systems delivered record revenue and profitability because of strong demand for reliable backup power, particularly for data centers.
- The portfolio is working as a shock absorber. Engine and Components were pressured by lower North America medium- and heavy-duty truck demand, but the company’s global footprint, product breadth, and service network let it offset that weakness with power generation, industrial, and aftermarket demand.
- Cummins is also trying to modernize its core rather than only defend it. The annual materials highlight the X10 and B7.2 engine launches, the HELM platform strategy, and continued investment in global production and customer support. This shows a company trying to stay relevant in emissions-sensitive on-highway markets while still treating performance and reliability as its franchise base.
- Power Systems is increasingly central to the story. The annual letter highlighted the S17 Centum launch for compact, mission-critical applications and expanded capacity around higher-output platforms. This is where the company most directly connects to grid strain, outage anxiety, and AI-linked infrastructure demand.
- Accelera is becoming a more disciplined option rather than an unconstrained growth bet. In `2025`, Cummins curtailed future investment in the electrolyzer business and concentrated resources on lower- and zero-emissions opportunities with stronger long-term potential. That already showed up in significant charges, but it also clarifies that management is trying to protect returns rather than subsidize every energy-transition lane indefinitely.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue reached a record `$9.5 billion`, up `9%`, net income reached `$932 million`, or `$6.73` per diluted share, and EBITDA was `$1.7 billion`, or `17.5%` of sales.
- Mix: Engine sales were `$3.1 billion`, Components sales were `$2.9 billion`, Distribution sales were `$3.3 billion`, Power Systems sales were `$2.3 billion`, and Accelera sales were `$145 million`.
- Demand read: management said customer orders were robust for standby power for data centers and North American truck markets were improving. Distribution revenue rose `9%`, driven by demand for power-generation products, and Power Systems revenue rose `19%`, driven primarily by data-center-related power demand in the United States, China, and Asia Pacific.
- Management message: Cummins raised full-year `2026` revenue guidance to up `10%` to `13%` and EBITDA guidance to `18.0%` to `18.5%`. The company also highlighted a natural-gas generator-set and microgrid agreement with Circe Energy for an HPC data center in Texas.
- Structural themes: Q2 is where Cummins most clearly looks like a digital-infrastructure beneficiary. The company is not selling servers, but it is monetizing the electrical reality behind AI buildout: constrained grids, fast-start backup generation, microgrid interest, and the need for serviceable, high-output power systems. Margin optics still need care because higher incentive compensation weighed on EBITDA percentage even amid record sales.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$8.4 billion`, up `3%`, net income was `$654 million`, or `$4.71` per diluted share, and EBITDA was `$1.3 billion`, or `15.4%` of sales.
- Mix: Engine sales were `$2.7 billion`, Components sales were `$2.5 billion`, Distribution sales were `$3.1 billion`, Power Systems sales were `$2.0 billion`, and Accelera sales were `$101 million`.
- Demand read: North America sales fell `6%` overall, but international revenue rose `16%`, mainly on stronger China demand. Distribution revenue rose `7%` and Power Systems revenue rose `19%`, both tied to data-center power-generation demand. Engine and Components remained weak on lower U.S. medium- and heavy-duty truck demand.
- Management message: management raised full-year `2026` guidance to up `8%` to `11%` revenue growth and `17.75%` to `18.50%` EBITDA while describing data-center backup power demand as stronger than expected.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows the split inside Cummins very cleanly. The legacy cycle had not yet recovered in earnest, but the power-generation and service sides of the business were already pulling the company upward. Reported results were also distorted by `$199 million` of charges related to the sale of the low-pressure fuel-cell business, reinforcing that the transition portfolio was being narrowed.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$8.5 billion`, up `1%`, net income was `$593 million`, or `$4.27` per diluted share, and EBITDA was `$1.2 billion`, or `13.5%` of sales. Full-year `2025` revenue was `$33.7 billion` and full-year EBITDA was `$5.4 billion`, or `16.0%` of sales.
- Mix: Q4 Engine sales were `$2.6 billion`, Components sales were `$2.4 billion`, Distribution sales were `$3.3 billion`, Power Systems sales were `$1.9 billion`, and Accelera sales were `$131 million`.
- Demand read: Distribution revenue rose `7%` and Power Systems revenue rose `11%`, with both segments explicitly driven by data-center-related power-generation demand. Engine and Components were dragged down by lower medium- and heavy-duty truck demand in the United States and Mexico.
- Management message: management said `2025` was a historic year despite truck-market weakness and said `2026` should bring slightly better North America truck demand plus continued strength in data-center power-generation markets.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is where Cummins most clearly stopped looking like a pure truck-cycle name. The core engine franchise was soft, but record Distribution and Power Systems profitability made the company look more like a diversified physical-infrastructure platform with service reach and growing exposure to mission-critical electricity continuity.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Cummins is not a consumer brand in the obvious sense, but it sits directly under several major social and economic shifts: rising intolerance for outages, electrification stress, AI-driven datacenter load growth, and the continuing need to move freight and keep industrial assets running even when the grid is constrained.
- The broader cultural pattern is that digital life still depends on noisy, physical, fuel-fed infrastructure. Consumers experience AI as software, chat, streaming, and cloud tools, but Cummins monetizes the less glamorous layer: the generator sets, backup systems, engines, and service capacity that keep warehouses, truck fleets, and datacenters operational.
- There is also a real-economy resilience story here. Freight cycles, construction demand, mining activity, and utility-adjacent power buildout all influence Cummins. That makes it a useful reminder that the physical economy did not disappear when attention shifted to platforms and semiconductors.
- The company also reflects a societal compromise around transition. Customers want lower emissions and more efficient systems, but they also still need dependable diesel, natural gas, and hybrid power in sectors where failure is costly and full electrification is uneven.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- North America truck demand is still cyclical and remains a major swing factor for Engine and Components. Improvement in `2026` helped, but the annual and quarter chain shows how quickly those segments can weaken when fleet replacement or freight conditions soften.
- Regulatory and emissions complexity remains material. Cummins continues to reference U.S. emissions-related legal and compliance matters and is actively managing product-launch timing around proposed Model Year `2027` changes.
- Data-center and backup-power demand are strong, but those markets can attract competition and require capacity discipline. Cummins is investing in mining and power-generation capacity, expanding high-output platforms, and leaning into natural-gas generation and microgrids, which raises execution expectations.
- Accelera remains both strategic option and return risk. The company is reducing losses and narrowing investment scope, but electrolyzer and fuel-cell repositioning already produced sizable charges and could continue to create noise around the broader operating story.
- Working capital still matters. The Q2 `2026` `10-Q` shows inventories near `$6.4 billion` and accounts receivable above `$6.5 billion`, which is manageable at Cummins’ scale but still a reminder that growth in physical infrastructure businesses ties up capital before it converts to cash.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `AI and cloud demand first become power and uptime demand.` Cummins shows that the physical beneficiaries of AI are not just chipmakers and datacenter landlords. Generator platforms, microgrids, service networks, and industrial power systems can also capture the same demand wave.
- `Installed-base service reach can stabilize a cyclical industrial company.` Distribution is not just a reseller arm. It is a recurring support, parts, maintenance, and field-capacity layer that helps offset volatility in original-equipment cycles.
- `Legacy hydrocarbon and lower-emissions transition businesses can coexist inside one capital-allocation problem.` Cummins is using a profitable legacy base to fund future technologies, but the quarter chain shows management getting more selective when market adoption lags.
- `Industrial demand can bifurcate inside the same company.` Truck-cycle softness and record power-generation demand are happening at once. That matters for the archive because it shows why broad macro labels often miss where real demand is forming.
- `The physical layer behind software is still governed by permitting, fuel choice, manufacturing capacity, labor, and serviceability.` Cummins benefits when digital dependence rises, but monetization still runs through real-world constraints rather than software-style scaling alone.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Generac Holdings Inc.`: Generac is more concentrated in standby power and resilience, especially across residential and light-commercial demand, while Cummins adds larger industrial, fleet, and global power-generation exposure plus a much larger heavy-duty installed base.
- Against `Vertiv Holdings Co`: Vertiv monetizes the datacenter interior through power and cooling infrastructure, while Cummins monetizes the backup-generation and microgrid edge that supports facility uptime when grid capacity or reliability is insufficient.
- Against `Quanta Services, Inc.` and `EMCOR Group, Inc.`: those companies convert power and infrastructure demand into field labor and project execution, while Cummins captures it through equipment platforms, power systems, and a global service-and-distribution network.

## Working interpretation

- Cummins materially improves the archive because it adds a larger-scale industrial counterpoint to the backup-power and connectivity names already completed. It links freight, engines, service economics, distributed power, and datacenter-driven generation demand inside one platform.
- It also sharpens the broader conclusion that the physical layer behind AI and software is not just semiconductors and datacenter shells. It is also the generator fleet, the service network, the natural-gas and diesel transition bridge, and the capital-allocation discipline needed to fund future technologies without losing the profitability of the installed base.
