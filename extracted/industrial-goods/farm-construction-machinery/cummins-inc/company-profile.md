## What changed

- Cummins is becoming a more visibly bifurcated industrial platform. Engine and Components remained tied to a cyclical North American truck downturn in `2025`, while Distribution and Power Systems increasingly behaved like infrastructure-growth businesses tied to backup power, industrial demand, and data-center buildout.
- Data-center demand moved from side narrative to central operating driver. The annual letter, Q4 `2025`, Q1 `2026`, and Q2 `2026` all explicitly tied record Distribution and Power Systems performance to strong backup-power demand, especially for data centers.
- Guidance kept stepping up as the mix improved. Initial `2026` revenue guidance of up `3%` to `8%` in February became up `8%` to `11%` in May and up `10%` to `13%` in August, with EBITDA guidance also tightening upward.
- Accelera shifted from broad transition ambition toward stricter capital discipline. Q4 `2025` recorded `$218 million` of electrolyzer-related charges, full-year `2025` absorbed `$458 million` of those charges, and Q1 `2026` added `$199 million` of charges tied to the sale of the low-pressure fuel-cell business.
- Cash generation remained strong even while inventory and working capital stayed heavy. The Q2 `2026` `10-Q` shows first-half operating cash flow of `$1.8 billion`, total cash and marketable securities of about `$3.9 billion`, and inventories of about `$6.4 billion`.

## Working interpretation

- Cummins is one of the archive’s best examples of how AI and digital dependence monetize through the physical power chain before they appear in software earnings. It sells engines and components, but the sharper current story is increasingly about mission-critical generation, service reach, and the ability to keep trucks, industrial sites, and power-constrained digital infrastructure operating.
- The most useful recurring pattern is that one company can simultaneously hold a legacy cyclical franchise and a faster-growing infrastructure franchise. In Cummins’ case, truck-cycle pressure and emissions-transition complexity coexist with record demand for backup generation, industrial support, and power-system capacity.

## Open questions

- How durable is the current data-center power-generation surge once more generator, switchgear, and microgrid capacity comes online across the industry?
- Can Cummins keep using legacy engine, components, and distribution cash flows to fund the energy-transition portfolio without letting Accelera remain a long-running drag on reported returns?
