## What changed

- Revenue and earnings accelerated hard across the whole window. Q4 `2025` revenue was `$2.65 billion`, Q1 `2026` revenue was `$2.87 billion`, and Q2 `2026` revenue reached `$3.27 billion`. Net income moved from `$330.8 million` in Q4 `2025` to `$370.4 million` in Q1 `2026` and `$441.6 million` in Q2 `2026`.
- Backlog is the central operating signal. It grew from `$11.94 billion` at year-end `2025` to `$12.45 billion` in Q1 `2026` and `$14.06 billion` in Q2 `2026`. Same-store backlog also climbed from `$11.58 billion` at year-end `2025` to `$12.21 billion` and then `$13.70 billion`.
- The operating model is not generic construction. Company materials repeatedly describe Comfort Systems as a leading provider of commercial, industrial, and institutional HVAC and electrical contracting services, with construction, building services, modular construction, offsite capability, and building-automation exposure all visible in the source stack.
- Workforce capacity is an explicit risk variable. The filings and releases repeatedly tie results to team execution, and the forward-looking-risk language specifically flags the risk of contractual commitments exceeding labor resources and the risk of failing to utilize the workforce properly.
- The company is analytically useful because it sits inside the skilled-trades bottleneck rather than simply buying labor from someone else. That makes it a cleaner `the-labor-squeeze` operator than the archive previously had.
- Management tone is direct and operational. The company keeps emphasizing execution, burn, bookings, pipelines, and workforce quality rather than broad macro optimism.
- The archive quality is now materially stronger because the official annual-report artifact is on disk rather than only the `10-K` HTML and supporting IR pages.

## Open questions

- How much of the current backlog surge is tied to unusually concentrated hyperscale, industrial, or large-project demand rather than a broad-based construction cycle?
- Can Comfort Systems keep converting strong backlog into revenue and margin without running into tighter craft-labor or subcontractor capacity constraints?
