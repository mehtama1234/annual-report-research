# Granite Construction Incorporated Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Sector: Industrial Goods

- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026

## Why this name matters

- Granite is the right next flagship after Vulcan, Knife River, and Martin Marietta because it shifts the archive from materials ownership into civil-infrastructure execution without losing the physical-layer lens.
- It also broadens the demand map. Granite sits at the point where public funding, local materials networks, transportation repair, federal infrastructure, and newer mission-critical site work get turned into actual roads, bridges, airport and rail work, border facilities, and data-center-adjacent site development.

## Annual report takeaways

- The annual materials frame Granite as one of the largest diversified and vertically integrated civil contractors and construction materials producers in the United States. That dual identity is the key reason it belongs in this archive.
- Full-year `2025` revenue increased `10%` to `$4.4B`, net income was `$193M`, adjusted net income was `$276M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$527M`. Operating cash flow reached `$469M`, or `10.6%` of revenue.
- The quality-of-demand signal was stronger than the income statement alone. Combined assigned and awarded projects, or `CAP`, increased `32%` year over year to a record `$7.0B` at year-end `2025`.
- Management repeatedly tied the margin and cash-flow improvement to a higher-quality project portfolio, better execution, and stronger contribution from the materials business through pricing, operational efficiencies, and vertical integration with construction operations.
- The annual frame is not just cyclical optimism. Granite is explicitly positioning itself as a disciplined allocator of bidding capacity, choosing work where execution quality and local material pull-through matter more than headline revenue alone.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Revenue increased `29%` year over year to about `$1.46B`, adjusted net income increased to about `$101M`, adjusted EBITDA increased `22%` to about `$186M`, and CAP rose to `$7.4B`.
- The quarter is easy to misread if someone focuses only on GAAP net income. The reported net loss of about `($278M)` was driven by a roughly `$360M` non-operating loss tied to convertible-debt transactions rather than by operating deterioration.
- The operating story was stronger than the GAAP headline. Granite raised full-year revenue guidance by `$100M`, lifted its operating-cash-flow target to `11%` of revenue, and reported year-to-date operating cash flow of `$142M`, up `$136M` from the prior year.
- Management also sharpened the strategic read. The release said Granite is expanding beyond traditional public infrastructure into federal portfolio work, rail and transit, mission-critical infrastructure including data-center site development, and a stronger vertically integrated materials platform.
- Risk still showed up. Severe weather in the southeast was called out as a headwind, which keeps the packet grounded in real field-execution volatility rather than a smooth abstract infrastructure story.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Revenue increased `30%` year over year to about `$912.5M`, adjusted net income rose to about `$12M`, adjusted EBITDA increased `106%` to about `$58M`, and CAP increased sequentially to `$7.2B`.
- The quarter matters because it shows Granite entering `2026` with momentum instead of simply digesting a strong prior year. Management raised guidance and said teams were active across federal, state, local, and private end markets.
- The materials segment again mattered. Management highlighted aggregates and asphalt demand, volume growth, and pricing increases as being on plan, reinforcing the idea that Granite is not just a labor-and-equipment contractor.
- Granite also completed the Kenny Seng Construction acquisition in April `2026`, which strengthens the pattern of local-market densification and capability expansion rather than one-off scale buying.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Fourth-quarter revenue increased `19%` to `$1.2B`, net income was `$52M`, adjusted net income was `$65M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$131M`.
- Full-year `2025` revenue increased `10%` to `$4.4B`, net income was `$193M`, adjusted net income was `$276M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$527M`.
- Year-end `CAP` reached a record `$7.0B`, up `$0.6B` sequentially and `$1.7B` year over year, which is the strongest indicator that public-works and civil-demand conversion remained healthy heading into `2026`.
- Management emphasized that strength came from disciplined project selection, better execution across a higher-quality project portfolio, and a standout materials contribution rather than from indiscriminate bidding.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Granite is not consumer-facing, but it is directly tied to how public priorities become lived reality. Road repair, bridge upgrades, airport expansion, transit work, border facilities, water systems, and data-center-ready land all show up here before most people ever see the finished assets.
- This makes Granite a good read on the social gap between infrastructure politics and infrastructure execution. Policy attention can be high, but the real bottleneck is still whether companies can win, price, staff, source, and complete work profitably in messy field conditions.
- There is also a population-shift layer. Granite's relevance increases where migration, logistics growth, and land development require new roads, expanded utility corridors, site grading, and public-works capacity, especially in faster-growing western and Sun Belt markets.
- The newer federal tactical-infrastructure and data-center-site references matter culturally because they show how U.S. infrastructure demand is broadening beyond classic DOT work into security, mission-critical, and digital-economy physical preparation.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Granite's model is highly sensitive to project selection and execution discipline. A large `CAP` number is useful, but profitability still depends on converting awards into work without margin erosion from scheduling, weather, labor, or cost overruns.
- Weather remains a real operating variable. The Q2 release explicitly cited severe weather in the southeast, which is a reminder that physical-infrastructure names carry climate and field-condition risk even in strong funding environments.
- Public funding is supportive, but timing still matters. Federal, state, and local awards can remain healthy while quarterly conversion moves around due to bid timing, project starts, and customer pacing.
- The vertically integrated materials platform is a strength because it supports margin and pull-through, but it also means Granite has to manage both construction execution and plant-and-material efficiency rather than one clean operating loop.
- Balance-sheet actions can distort the surface read. The convertible-debt transaction in Q2 created a large GAAP loss that was economically different from operating performance, so headline earnings alone are a poor guide to underlying trend quality.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `CAP is the civil-infrastructure equivalent of backlog quality.` Granite shows that assigned and awarded projects are a better demand proxy than revenue alone when public work is ramping.
- `Public funding helps demand durability, not quarterly smoothness.` Awards can stay strong while weather, starts, and mix still make quarterly conversion lumpy.
- `Vertical integration matters downstream too.` Granite's materials base improves economics because aggregates and asphalt can be pulled into its own construction work instead of sold only as stand-alone inputs.
- `AI and digital infrastructure still need dirt work.` Data-center expansion is not only a chips-and-power story. It also requires site development, grading, roads, drainage, and other civil work that companies like Granite monetize.
- `The field layer is where national ambitions meet physical constraints.` Infrastructure policy, regional growth, and security priorities only become output if contractors can actually execute at scale.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Vulcan Materials Company`: Vulcan is the cleaner scarcity-and-pricing read on aggregates ownership, while Granite shows how those physical inputs get turned into installed transportation and civil-infrastructure output.
- Against `Knife River Corporation`: both use vertical integration between materials and contracting, but Granite is a better read on large-project public-works execution and federal or mission-critical expansion.
- Against `MasTec, Inc.`: MasTec is the broader utility, telecom, and energy project-execution platform, while Granite is the tighter civil and transportation infrastructure conversion read with clearer public-works and site-development exposure.
