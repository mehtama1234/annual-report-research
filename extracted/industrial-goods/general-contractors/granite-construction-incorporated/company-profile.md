## What changed

- Granite is not just a roads-and-bridges contractor. The annual and quarter chain shows it operating as a vertically integrated civil-infrastructure platform spanning construction, aggregates, asphalt, and related materials with exposure to highways, airports, rail and transit, water, federal work, and mission-critical site development.
- CAP is the key operating signal. It moved from a record `$7.0B` at year-end `2025` to `$7.2B` in Q1 `2026` and `$7.4B` in Q2 `2026`, showing continued award momentum rather than a one-quarter spike.
- The materials segment matters strategically, not cosmetically. Management repeatedly ties margin support to aggregates and asphalt demand, pricing, operational efficiencies, and vertical integration with construction operations.
- Granite is broadening its demand base beyond traditional public transportation work. The `2026` releases explicitly point to tactical federal infrastructure, rail and transit, and data-center site development as newer growth lanes on top of highways, roads, and bridges.
- Reported GAAP volatility in Q2 `2026` is not the right operating read. The large net loss was driven by a non-operating loss tied to convertible-debt transactions, while adjusted net income, adjusted EBITDA, CAP, revenue, and cash flow all moved higher.

## Open questions

- How durable is Granite's current expansion into federal tactical infrastructure and data-center site development if public awards, permitting, or private hyperscale site timing soften?
- Can Granite keep lifting margins through better project selection and vertical integration if weather disruption, labor tightness, or materials-cost pressure intensify?
- How much of the CAP growth reflects structurally stronger infrastructure demand versus peak conversion of unusually supportive public-funding windows?
