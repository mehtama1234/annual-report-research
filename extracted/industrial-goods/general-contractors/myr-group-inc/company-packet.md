## Annual report takeaways

- MYR is the right next Industrial Goods addition because it strengthens the same physical-infrastructure thesis as Quanta while offering a smaller, cleaner, more pure-play read on specialty electrical construction.
- AnnualReports classifies the company as `Industrial Goods / General Contractors`, but the annual materials show a much more specific operating model: a holding company of specialty electrical construction service providers serving electric utility infrastructure and commercial and industrial construction markets in the United States and Canada.
- The annual evidence base shows two distinct but connected operating lanes. `Transmission and Distribution` covers high-voltage transmission lines, substations, overhead and underground distribution systems, clean energy projects, EV charging infrastructure, and emergency restoration. `Commercial and Industrial` covers wiring, systems installation, maintenance, and repair across data centers, airports, hospitals, hotels, stadiums, manufacturing plants, processing facilities, water and waste-water treatment facilities, mining, and other industrial sites.
- Full-year `2025` results were strong. Revenue reached a record `$3.66 billion`, diluted EPS reached `$7.53`, net income reached a record `$118.4 million`, EBITDA reached a record `$232.7 million`, and year-end backlog reached a record `$2.82 billion`.
- Remaining performance obligations add another useful signal. The `2025` 10-K shows RPO of `$2.52 billion` at `2025-12-31`, with roughly `85%` expected to be recognized within twelve months.
- MYR matters for the broader archive because it shows where electrification, grid work, data-center-related electrical demand, and infrastructure investment turn into day-to-day field execution by a more concentrated electrical contractor rather than by a larger diversified infrastructure platform.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was a record `$1.08 billion`, diluted EPS was `$3.17`, net income was a record `$49.9 million`, and EBITDA was a record `$85.0 million`.
- Demand and backlog: backlog rose to a record `$3.16 billion`, up `19.6%` from the comparable prior-year quarter, with `Transmission and Distribution` backlog at `$1.27 billion` and `Commercial and Industrial` backlog at `$1.89 billion`.
- Management message: management said continued strength in core markets, ongoing investment in electrical infrastructure, and sustained customer demand supported the quarter, while recent acquisitions were positioned to deepen capabilities and geographic reach.
- Structural themes: the latest quarter shows that the electrical construction layer is benefiting both from utility-side buildout and from the commercial-and-industrial side of the same cycle, especially where large-load facilities and complex infrastructure need power, wiring, controls, and field execution.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$1.00 billion`, diluted EPS was `$2.99`, net income was a record `$46.8 million`, and EBITDA was a record `$81.5 million`.
- Demand and backlog: backlog reached a record `$2.84 billion`, up `7.7%` from the comparable prior-year quarter, with `Transmission and Distribution` backlog at `$980.7 million` and `Commercial and Industrial` backlog at `$1.86 billion`.
- Management message: management emphasized strong momentum, deeper strategic customer relationships, expanding geographic footprint, and a favorable market outlook.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the cleanest early-2026 proof that MYR is participating in a durable electrical-infrastructure wave rather than just one quarter of project timing. Utility infrastructure and commercial electrical work were both moving at once, while margins improved materially.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$973.5 million`, diluted EPS was `$2.33`, net income was a record `$36.5 million`, and EBITDA was a record `$64.2 million`. Full-year `2025` revenue was a record `$3.66 billion`, diluted EPS was `$7.53`, and net income was a record `$118.4 million`.
- Demand and backlog: year-end backlog reached a record `$2.82 billion`, with `Transmission and Distribution` backlog at `$1.02 billion` and `Commercial and Industrial` backlog at `$1.80 billion`.
- Management message: management said increased electrification demand and continued investment in electrical infrastructure remained encouraging, with steady bidding activity across both segments.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` is where MYR clearly becomes a core archive comp to Quanta. It had already crossed into a regime where electric infrastructure investment, utility work, and complex commercial-and-industrial electrical projects were compounding together.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: MYR widens Industrial Goods beyond electrical components and power equipment into the direct specialty-electrical construction layer.
- Utilities: this is a clean operator for understanding how transmission, distribution, substations, emergency restoration, and utility-side construction demand are converting into backlog and margin.
- Technology and load growth: MYR makes the power-and-data-center thesis more concrete because the `Commercial and Industrial` segment explicitly serves data centers and other large-load facilities.
- Industrial and civic infrastructure: the company also spans airports, hospitals, manufacturing plants, processing facilities, water and waste-water treatment, mining, roadway lighting, signalization, and EV charging, so it broadens the infrastructure read beyond the pure utility lane.
- Labor and execution: MYR is a useful complement to Quanta because its disclosures make the labor-intensive and project-selective nature of electrical contracting more visible, including the caveat that reported backlog can understate forward opportunity on longer-duration service agreements.
- Capital and consolidation: the Q2 `2026` materials show targeted M&A through `Valley Electric` and `Comet Electric`, which suggests the lane is still consolidating around execution capability and local market reach.

## Working interpretation

- MYR materially improves the archive because it adds a more focused electrical-construction operator underneath the same electrification, utility, and load-growth buildout already visible in Eaton, Hubbell, nVent, Powell, Vertiv, Trane, and Quanta.
- It also sharpens the broader conclusion that this is not just an equipment cycle. It is a system build cycle where transmission work, substations, commercial-and-industrial electrical systems, and large-load facility infrastructure all need contractors that can actually execute the work in the field.
