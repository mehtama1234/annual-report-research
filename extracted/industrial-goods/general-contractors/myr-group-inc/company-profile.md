## What changed

- Revenue and earnings kept stepping up through the window. Q4 `2025` revenue was `$973.5 million`, Q1 `2026` revenue was `$1.00 billion`, and Q2 `2026` revenue reached a record `$1.08 billion`. Diluted EPS moved from `$2.33` in Q4 `2025` to `$2.99` in Q1 `2026` and `$3.17` in Q2 `2026`.
- Backlog is the cleanest operating signal. Total backlog moved from `$2.82 billion` at year-end `2025` to `$2.84 billion` in Q1 `2026` and `$3.16 billion` in Q2 `2026`, with the sharpest second-quarter move coming from the `Transmission and Distribution` segment.
- The business mix is more specific than the `General Contractors` label suggests. MYR describes itself as a holding company of specialty electrical construction service providers serving electric utility infrastructure plus commercial and industrial construction markets in the United States and Canada.
- The two-segment structure matters. `Transmission and Distribution` gives direct exposure to transmission lines, substations, overhead and underground distribution systems, clean energy interconnections, and emergency restoration, while `Commercial and Industrial` adds wiring and systems work across data centers, airports, hospitals, manufacturing, water and waste-water treatment, mining, intelligent transportation, and EV charging.
- The backlog framing requires caution. MYR discloses that many unit-price, time-and-equipment, time-and-materials, and cost-plus contracts only contribute projected revenue for a three-month period even when the master-service agreements run for one to four years, so reported backlog can understate forward revenue opportunity.
- The 2026 message is that electrification and infrastructure demand are still broadening. Management repeatedly points to increased electrification demand, continued investment in electrical infrastructure, sustained customer demand, and a healthy pipeline of bidding opportunities. The Q2 release also adds July `2026` acquisitions of `Valley Electric` and `Comet Electric` to deepen commercial and industrial capabilities.

## Open questions

- How much of the current margin strength and backlog lift is structural demand from grid and data-center-adjacent electrical work versus timing and project selectivity after the prior clean-energy project issues?
- Can MYR sustain the recent profitability step-up while absorbing acquisitions and handling the execution risks that come with labor availability, project timing, weather, and backlog that may understate long-duration master-service work?
