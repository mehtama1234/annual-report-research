## Annual report takeaways

- Quanta is the right next Industrial Goods addition because it fills the field-execution and utility-construction layer that sits between power-equipment providers and the actual physical buildout of electric infrastructure.
- AnnualReports classifies the company as `Industrial Goods / General Contractors`, but the business is much broader than a plain commercial contractor. The company frames itself as a specialized infrastructure-solutions operator serving utility, power generation, load center, communications, pipeline, and energy markets.
- The annual evidence base shows a company whose operating model spans design, installation, repair, and maintenance of energy and communications infrastructure, with visible exposure to transmission, distribution, substations, renewable generation, emergency restoration, and other critical-path infrastructure work.
- Full-year `2025` results were strong. Revenue reached `$28.48 billion`, GAAP diluted EPS reached `$6.80`, adjusted diluted EPS reached `$10.75`, net income attributable to common stock reached `$1.03 billion`, and adjusted EBITDA reached `$2.88 billion`.
- Year-end backlog is the key operating signal. Remaining performance obligations ended `2025` at `$23.76 billion`, total backlog ended `2025` at `$43.98 billion`, and management specifically highlighted all-time highs in Electric Infrastructure Solutions segment RPO and total backlog.
- Quanta matters for the broader archive because it shows where electrification, grid modernization, data-center load growth, and infrastructure policy actually become work in the field rather than just order flow at an equipment OEM.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$9.6 billion`, GAAP diluted EPS was `$2.96`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$4.24`, net income attributable to common stock was `$451.4 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$1.1 billion`.
- Demand and backlog: RPO rose to `$33.6 billion` and total backlog rose to `$53.4 billion`, both record quarter-end levels.
- Management message: management said the quarter showed compounding strength in the operating model, strong double-digit growth, robust cash flow, and improving visibility into the back half of `2026`.
- Structural themes: Quanta is increasingly a direct buildout operator for electric-grid, power-generation, mission-critical, and load-center infrastructure. The latest quarter also shows the company extending deeper into electrical, fabrication, and front-end infrastructure services through acquisitions and domestic supply-chain positioning.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$7.9 billion`, GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.45`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$2.68`, net income attributable to common stock was `$220.6 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$686.4 million`.
- Demand and backlog: RPO rose to `$26.2 billion` and total backlog rose to `$48.5 billion`.
- Management message: management emphasized strong double-digit growth, improved margin performance across both segments, and increased substantially all `2026` financial expectations.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the cleanest early-2026 proof that Quanta is not just benefiting from one project cycle. The company is using backlog visibility, craft-skilled execution, and a solutions-based operating model to turn infrastructure intensity into faster growth and higher expectations.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$7.84 billion`, GAAP diluted EPS was `$2.08`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$3.16`, net income attributable to common stock was `$315.5 million`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$845.3 million`. Full-year `2025` revenue was `$28.48 billion`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$10.75`, and free cash flow was `$1.67 billion`.
- Demand and backlog: year-end RPO reached `$23.76 billion` and total backlog reached `$43.98 billion`, with Electric Infrastructure Solutions segment RPO and total backlog at all-time highs.
- Management message: management framed `2026` around another year of double-digit growth and explicitly said it expected to achieve record backlog in `2026`.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` is where Quanta clearly becomes a core archive anchor. The company had already crossed into a regime where utility and electric infrastructure demand, power-related project intensity, and execution capacity were compounding together.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Quanta widens Industrial Goods beyond electrical components, power hardware, thermal systems, and outsourced facilities into the actual utility-construction and infrastructure-deployment layer.
- Utilities: this is one of the archive's strongest bridge names between utility capex, transmission and substation work, load-center growth, and field execution at scale.
- Technology: Quanta is another first-order physical-technology story because management directly links rising electricity demand from data centers, electrification, and grid modernization to the need for more infrastructure work and supply-chain solutions.
- Industrial and energy context: the company also spans power generation, communications, pipeline, industrial, and energy infrastructure, so it broadens the power thesis without confining it to regulated utilities alone.
- Labor and execution: Quanta's repeated emphasis on self-perform capabilities and craft-skilled workforce makes it a high-value operator for understanding how labor, project execution, and infrastructure complexity interact.
- Capital and consolidation: acquisitions are part of the thesis here. Quanta is using M&A to assemble more electrical, mechanical, fabrication, right-of-way, and engineering capability around the same infrastructure wave.

## Working interpretation

- Quanta materially improves the archive because it adds the actual construction and field-services execution layer underneath the power and data-center infrastructure buildout already visible in Eaton, Hubbell, nVent, Powell, Vertiv, and Trane.
- It also sharpens the broader conclusion that this is not just an equipment cycle. It is a system build cycle where critical infrastructure needs equipment vendors, controls, cooling, supply-chain solutions, and large craft-skilled field organizations capable of building and modifying the network itself.
