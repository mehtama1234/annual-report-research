# Dycom Industries Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-09

- Company: Dycom Industries Inc.
- Sector: Industrial Goods
- Industry: Heavy Construction
- Ticker: DY
- Fiscal year-end: January
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q1 2027
  - quarter 2: Q4 2026
  - quarter 3: Q3 2026
- Timeline note: because Dycom reports on an off-calendar basis, the annual-report package labeled `2025` corresponds to the fiscal year ended `2026-01-31`, and the latest reported quarter as of `2026-08-09` was fiscal Q1 `2027`.

## Annual report takeaways

- Dycom is the right next industrial addition because it extends the contractor and field-execution lane away from power systems and building trades into the telecom and digital-infrastructure construction layer.
- AnnualReports classifies the company as `Industrial Goods / Heavy Construction`, but the annual materials show a more specific operating model: a scaled specialty contractor for telecommunications infrastructure, utility-adjacent field work, underground facility locating, and now building-systems work tied directly to critical facilities.
- The official annual package describes a business built around fiber, wireless, and utility-support deployment rather than generic heavy construction. Dycom says it operates through `40` operating companies across all `50` states and provides planning, engineering and design, aerial and underground construction, wireless construction, maintenance, fulfillment, underground locating, and electric and gas utility work.
- The fiscal `2025` annual package was strong. Contract revenues reached `$4.702 billion`, net income reached `$233.4 million`, diluted EPS reached `$7.92`, and fiscal year-end backlog reached `$7.760 billion`.
- The annual report sharpens a major archive theme: AI and digital demand are increasingly physical. Dycom's annual letter explicitly frames long-haul fiber and AI infrastructure as multi-year demand drivers and says customers need high-capacity, ultra-low-latency, private, secure, redundant fiber to connect data centers nationwide.
- Customer concentration is still central to the risk and moat discussion. Fiscal `2025` revenue came about `20.1%` from `AT&T`, `12.1%` from `Lumen`, `8.5%` from `Comcast`, `7.3%` from `Charter`, and another `7.4%` from one additional customer.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: fiscal Q1 `2027`, for the quarter ended `May 2, 2026`, delivered record contract revenues of `$1.965 billion`, net income of `$91.3 million`, adjusted net income of `$134.3 million`, adjusted diluted EPS of `$4.42`, and adjusted EBITDA of `$262.5 million`.
- Demand and backlog: total backlog reached a record `$11.906 billion`, up `46.5%` year over year and up roughly `25%` sequentially, with management calling out a `2.2x` book-to-bill ratio.
- Management message: Dycom raised full-year fiscal `2027` outlook and said fiber infrastructure and data-center-build demand were more robust than ever. Management also said customers were extending contract durations to secure Dycom's skilled workforce.
- Structural themes: National Technology Integrators was announced as an acquisition to extend Dycom into inside-plant structured cabling within data centers, plus audio-visual and security systems, linking rack-level work to long-haul and metro fiber deployment.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: fiscal Q4 `2026`, for the quarter and year ended `January 31, 2026`, delivered quarterly contract revenues of `$1.458 billion`, quarterly adjusted EBITDA of `$162.4 million`, annual contract revenues of `$5.546 billion`, annual adjusted EBITDA of `$737.7 million`, annual free cash flow of `$435.3 million`, and year-end backlog of `$9.542 billion`.
- Demand and backlog: management described continued strong demand from fiber-to-the-home programs, wireless work, long-haul and middle-mile fiber, and growing `inside the fence` opportunities.
- Management message: Dycom said the Power Solutions acquisition accelerated its entry into the high-growth data-center market and justified a new segment structure with `Communications` and `Building Systems`.
- Structural themes: this is the quarter where Dycom becomes a stronger bridge company for the archive. It no longer sits only in telecom construction; it now spans outside-plant communications infrastructure and inside-facility electrical and building infrastructure for critical environments, especially data centers.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: fiscal Q3 `2026`, for the quarter ended `October 25, 2025`, delivered record contract revenues of `$1.452 billion`, record net income of `$106.4 million`, record diluted EPS of `$3.63`, adjusted EBITDA of `$219.4 million`, and backlog of `$8.222 billion`.
- Demand and backlog: Dycom said demand drivers had never been stronger, pointing to accelerating fiber builds, a ramp in data-center needs, and the coming BEAD broadband wave. Backlog included about `$4.99 billion` expected to be completed in the next twelve months.
- Management message: management raised the midpoint of fiscal `2026` revenue outlook and separately announced the planned acquisition of Power Solutions, described as a premier data-center electrical contractor in the Mid-Atlantic.
- Structural themes: this quarter is the cleanest early signal that telecom construction is merging into a wider digital-infrastructure buildout. Management explicitly tied the opportunity set to fiber-to-the-home, hyperscaler fiber infrastructure, BEAD, and data-center power and electrical work.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Dycom gives Industrial Goods a telecom-and-digital-infrastructure field-execution operator instead of another equipment or OEM name.
- Fiber and communications infrastructure: Dycom directly covers planning, engineering, aerial and underground construction, wireless construction, fulfillment, locating, and maintenance for communications networks.
- AI and data centers: the annual letter, Q4 `2026`, and Q1 `2027` materials all push the same conclusion that AI demand is becoming a fiber and facilities story, not just a chip-and-cloud story.
- Outside plant to inside plant: Power Solutions and National Technology Integrators show a shift from backbone and access-network construction toward a broader end-to-end digital-infrastructure execution stack that includes building systems and cabling inside critical facilities.
- Labor and capacity: Dycom is another direct read on the labor-squeeze theme. Management says customers are extending contract durations to lock in skilled workforce capacity, which means field labor is becoming part of the moat.
- Policy and subsidy timing: BEAD is a real variable here. Dycom repeatedly points to state-level approval and subgrantee progress as a future demand unlock.

## Working interpretation

- Dycom materially improves the archive because it captures a part of the build cycle that the other industrial names only touch indirectly: the labor, routing, permitting, trenching, splicing, wireless, and now inside-the-facility cabling work required to make digital infrastructure actually function.
- It also strengthens the broader conclusion that AI and data-center demand are spilling into the real economy through many layers at once: power equipment, cooling, switchgear, utility construction, electrical and mechanical building systems, and now telecom and structured-cabling field execution.
