Date baseline: 2026-08-09

- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026

## Annual report takeaways

- Primoris is the right next contractor addition because it keeps the industrial lane anchored in field execution, but adds a different mix: utilities, gas operations, communications, renewables, natural-gas generation, and heavy civil work.
- The official `2025` annual report shows a very strong base year. Revenue reached `$7.575 billion`, net income reached `$274.9 million`, diluted EPS reached `$5.02`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$531.1 million`, and year-end backlog reached `$11.9 billion`.
- The annual letter makes Primoris a direct read on the same power-demand and AI-linked buildout seen elsewhere in the archive. Management explicitly tied demand to replacing aging infrastructure, hardening the grid, expanding power delivery, building natural-gas generation, scaling solar and battery storage, and communications builds tied to data-center development.
- The annual package also shows a real labor and execution story. Primoris said it increased its labor force by more than `2,800` people in `2025`, highlighted tighter markets for certified journeyman linemen, and emphasized the advantage of self-performing the vast majority of its work.
- Primoris is not a generic heavy-construction company. The annual materials divide the business between `Utilities` and `Energy`, with Utilities spanning power delivery, gas operations, and communications, and Energy spanning renewables, industrial construction, heavy civil, maintenance, and pipeline services.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026`, for the quarter ended `2026-06-30`, delivered revenue of `$1.688 billion`, net loss of `$24.2 million`, diluted loss per share of `$0.45`, adjusted net loss of `$14.6 million`, and adjusted EBITDA of `$11.4 million`.
- Demand and backlog: total backlog reached a record `$13.9 billion`, including `$8.2 billion` of master service agreement backlog, and management highlighted roughly `$1.4 billion` of natural-gas power-generation awards during the quarter.
- Management message: the company said challenged renewables projects were still hurting earnings, but also said it had delivered record bookings and the highest total backlog in company history.
- Structural themes: Primoris is now a useful counterexample inside the industrial set. Demand and bookings stayed strong, but project redesign, sequencing, labor productivity, sub-surface conditions, and weather were enough to flip the quarter into an operating loss.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026`, for the quarter ended `2026-03-31`, delivered revenue of `$1.560 billion`, net income of `$17.4 million`, diluted EPS of `$0.32`, adjusted net income of `$32.2 million`, and adjusted EBITDA of `$60.5 million`.
- Demand and backlog: Utilities improved year over year, but Energy margins weakened sharply, and management updated full-year `2026` guidance to reflect solar-project revenue and margin pressure plus the PayneCrest acquisition.
- Management message: Primoris said the quarter reflected cost pressure on a limited number of renewables projects, while the rest of the business improved and bidding activity remained strong across natural-gas generation, renewables, and pipeline.
- Structural themes: PayneCrest Electric closed on `2026-05-01`, adding electrical construction and services capabilities and explicitly increasing Primoris exposure to industrial, manufacturing, advanced facilities, and data-center services.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` and full-year `2025` delivered quarter revenue of `$1.858 billion`, quarter net income of `$51.8 million`, quarter adjusted EBITDA of `$108.2 million`, full-year revenue of `$7.575 billion`, full-year net income of `$274.9 million`, and full-year adjusted EBITDA of `$531.1 million`.
- Demand and backlog: Primoris exited `2025` with total backlog of `$11.9 billion` and booked nearly `$3.0 billion` of new work in the fourth quarter, including more than `$1.6 billion` of new renewables awards.
- Management message: management described `2025` as another year of record revenue, earnings, and backlog, while also acknowledging higher-than-expected costs on certain renewables projects and lower storm-restoration work in power delivery during the fourth quarter.
- Structural themes: this is the clean setup quarter for the later stress. The annual and quarter-end materials already show very strong end-market demand and big wins in utilities and renewables, but they also reveal renewables margin sensitivity before the `2026` quarterlies made it impossible to ignore.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Primoris adds a broader utility-and-energy construction operator to Industrial Goods rather than another equipment or pure services name.
- AI and digital infrastructure: the annual letter and transcript tie communications builds, substations, and power infrastructure directly to data-center development and rising power demand.
- Natural gas and all-of-the-above power: Primoris is one of the cleaner archive reads on how gas generation is re-entering the industrial story alongside solar and battery storage, not as a substitute for them.
- Renewables execution risk: the `2026` quarterlies make Primoris one of the archive's clearest examples of strong demand coexisting with weak margins because of contract structure, redesigns, sequencing, weather, and labor-productivity issues.
- Labor and self-perform capacity: the company explicitly frames labor availability, project managers, and self-perform depth as operating advantages.
- M&A as capability fill-in: PayneCrest adds another example of contractors buying into adjacent electrical and advanced-facility work to capture more of the project stack around digital and industrial infrastructure.

## Working interpretation

- Primoris materially improves the industrial archive because it shows that the contractor layer is not one clean growth story. Strong demand can coexist with ugly quarter economics when work mix turns toward more troubled renewables projects.
- It also sharpens a broader theme already visible elsewhere: AI and electrification are not only helping electrical-equipment and cooling names. They are also reshaping the economics of field-execution companies through substations, gas generation, communications builds, and advanced electrical-facility work.
