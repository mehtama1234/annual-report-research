## What changed

- Primoris is a useful addition because it widens the industrial contractor lane into a more mixed energy, utilities, and renewables execution platform rather than another pure power-delivery or building-systems name.
- The annual package shows a record `2025` base: revenue reached `$7.6 billion`, diluted EPS reached `$5.02`, backlog reached `$11.9 billion`, and management framed the business as exposed to gas operations, power delivery, communications, solar, natural-gas generation, and pipeline services.
- The annual letter and transcript clearly tie the company to the same AI and load-growth chain seen elsewhere in the archive, but through different plumbing: substations, power delivery, communications builds tied to data centers, solar, battery storage, and natural-gas generation.
- The quarter sequence in `2026` is the important twist. Primoris kept winning work and raised backlog to a record even while earnings quality deteriorated because several renewables projects absorbed redesign, sequencing, labor-productivity, sub-surface, and weather-related costs.
- That makes Primoris a better stress-case read than a momentum read. It shows that industrial demand can remain strong while project economics, mix, and field execution become the real bottleneck.

## Open questions

- Are the challenged renewables projects a contained cleanup issue, or evidence that parts of the utility-scale solar build cycle are becoming structurally harder to execute at acceptable margins?
- How much of the record backlog is likely to convert with healthy margins if the company keeps leaning further into natural-gas generation, utilities, and higher-complexity electrical construction through PayneCrest?
