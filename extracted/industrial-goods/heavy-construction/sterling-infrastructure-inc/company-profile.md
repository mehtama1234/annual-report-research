## What changed

- Sterling is no longer just a general construction name. The `2025` annual and `2026` quarter chain show it becoming an increasingly concentrated e-infrastructure and mission-critical site-development platform rather than a balanced highway-and-building contractor.
- Segment identity is now strategically important. Sterling defines three segments: `E-Infrastructure Solutions`, `Transportation Solutions`, and `Building Solutions`, but recent results show E-Infrastructure driving most of the growth and much of the margin profile.
- Full-year `2025` created the base for the current inflection. Revenue reached `$2.49B`, adjusted net income reached `$336.7M`, adjusted diluted EPS reached `$10.88`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$503.8M`, and operating cash flow reached `$440.0M`.
- Demand visibility is no longer captured by backlog alone. Management now emphasizes signed backlog, combined backlog, and a separate pipeline of high-probability future phase work, which together push Sterling closer to a multi-stage demand funnel than a single backlog number.
- The portfolio mix is shifting by choice, not just by market accident. Management explicitly says it is reallocating resources away from lower-margin transportation work and toward higher-margin e-infrastructure opportunities.

## Open questions

- How durable is Sterling's current e-infrastructure concentration if hyperscale, semiconductor, or broader mission-critical project timing becomes less favorable?
- Can Transportation Solutions keep defending profitability while revenue shrinks as resources are reallocated away from lower-bid highway work?
- How long can Building Solutions remain under pressure from affordability-driven housing weakness before it becomes a more meaningful drag on consolidated mix quality?
