## Annual report takeaways

- nVent is a strong next industrial name because it extends the AI and electrification buildout further upstream into electrical connection, protection, enclosure, power-distribution, and cooling infrastructure.
- The `10-K` gives a clear business description. nVent says it is a leading global provider of electrical connection and protection solutions and that it connects and protects mission-critical equipment, buildings, and essential processes.
- The annual filing also makes the structural demand link explicit. It says trends like electrification, sustainability, and digitalization, including increased use of artificial intelligence, are helping drive the need for nVent's products.
- nVent is not just selling commodity electrical parts. The annual description emphasizes bus systems, cable management, control buildings, cooling solutions, both liquid and air, electrical connections, enclosures, equipment protection, power connections, power distribution solutions, and thermal-management-adjacent infrastructure.
- Full-year `2025` financials were already strong. Reported sales from continuing operations reached `$3.9 billion`, organic sales grew `13%`, adjusted EPS reached `$3.35`, and free cash flow reached `$561 million`.
- The annual setup also explains why the company is useful in the archive. It gives a more component-level and enclosure-level read on the same infrastructure buildout visible at `Eaton`, `Vertiv`, and `Trane`, but from a more focused electrical-protection and mission-critical operating-environment angle.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` reported sales were `$1.471 billion`, organic sales growth was `47%`, adjusted EPS was `$1.45`, and free cash flow was `$167 million`.
- Demand and operating indicators: operating income rose to `$301 million`, adjusted operating income rose to `$323 million`, and management highlighted significant data-center growth with another manufacturing expansion for liquid cooling.
- Management message: management said portfolio transformation continued to drive performance, data-center growth was significant, and new products contributed more than `30` points to sales growth.
- Structural themes: nVent is moving from a steady electrical-components story toward a faster-growth infrastructure platform tied directly to dense compute, cooling, and mission-critical electrical environments.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` reported sales were `$1.242 billion`, organic sales growth was `34%`, adjusted EPS was `$1.09`, and free cash flow was `$54 million`.
- Demand and operating indicators: management said sales and orders were record, backlog increased to `$2.6 billion`, and infrastructure led growth with broad-based data-center expansion across gray and white space.
- Management message: management emphasized scaling investments in new products and capacity to respond to customer demand and raised full-year guidance on the strength of data-center momentum.
- Structural themes: this quarter shows nVent as a backlog and capacity story, not only a quarter-revenue story. The company is benefiting from both current demand and expanding forward visibility.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` reported sales were `$1.067 billion`, organic sales growth was `24%`, adjusted EPS was `$0.90`, and free cash flow was `$189 million`.
- Demand and operating indicators: full-year `2025` sales reached `$3.9 billion`, full-year organic growth reached `13%`, full-year adjusted EPS reached `$3.35`, and full-year free cash flow reached `$561 million`.
- Management message: management said `2025` ended with strong momentum and described nVent as a higher-growth electrical company positioned for continued growth led by the infrastructure vertical, particularly data centers and power utilities.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the clear bridge quarter where the company stops looking like a normal short-cycle electrical name and starts looking like a more direct infrastructure beneficiary.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: nVent strengthens the industrial case that electrical infrastructure winners are spreading across multiple layers, from grid-facing architecture to in-facility protection, connections, enclosures, and thermal support.
- Technology: this is another first-order technology story inside industrials because AI and digitalization are explicitly cited as demand drivers in the annual filing.
- Infrastructure vertical: the company repeatedly highlights the infrastructure vertical, especially data centers and power utilities, which makes it a useful bridge between industrials, utilities, real estate, and technology.
- Product depth: nVent is analytically useful because it sits closer to the physical components and protective systems that must be installed around critical electrical operations.
- Capital and operations: backlog, new-product contribution, liquid-cooling expansion, and manufacturing capacity all matter directly here.
- Cultural read: nVent gives the archive another example of industrial management language shifting from generic end-market recovery toward electrification, digitalization, AI density, and mission-critical resilience.

## Working interpretation

- nVent materially improves the archive because it pushes the electrical bottleneck thesis further upstream into the connection, protection, enclosure, and cooling subsystems that sit around critical power and compute infrastructure.
- It also strengthens the broader interpretation that AI demand is producing a layered industrial buildout, where value accrues not only to grid and facility platforms but also to the component, protection, and liquid-cooling operators that make those systems usable at scale.
