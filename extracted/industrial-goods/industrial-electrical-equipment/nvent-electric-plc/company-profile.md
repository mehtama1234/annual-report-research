## What changed

- nVent deepens the electrical-infrastructure read inside industrials without repeating Eaton or Vertiv. This is a company centered on connection, protection, enclosures, cable management, power distribution, control buildings, and cooling around critical electrical systems.
- The annual business description is unusually useful. The `10-K` says nVent is a leading global provider of electrical connection and protection solutions and emphasizes that it connects and protects mission-critical equipment, buildings, and essential processes.
- The annual filing also tightens the AI link. It says trends like electrification, sustainability, and digitalization, including increased use of artificial intelligence, are helping drive demand for its products. It explicitly positions the business around data centers, power utilities, liquid cooling, and protective infrastructure.
- Full-year `2025` results already showed a stronger growth profile than a normal industrial electrical supplier. Reported sales from continuing operations reached `$3.9 billion`, up `30%`, organic sales grew `13%`, adjusted EPS reached `$3.35`, and free cash flow reached `$561 million`.
- Q1 `2026` turned that into a backlog and orders story. Sales reached `$1.242 billion`, organic growth reached `34%`, adjusted EPS reached `$1.09`, and management said backlog increased to `$2.6 billion` with infrastructure leading and data-center growth broadening across gray and white space.
- Q2 `2026` strengthened the same pattern. Sales reached `$1.471 billion`, organic growth reached `47%`, adjusted EPS reached `$1.45`, and free cash flow reached `$167 million`. Management highlighted significant data-center growth, more than `30` points of sales growth from new products, and another manufacturing expansion for liquid cooling.
- nVent matters because it sits upstream of some of the more visible AI-infrastructure winners already in the archive. It is less about the whole facility stack than Vertiv and less about grid-scale electrical architecture than Eaton. It is about the mission-critical electrical connection, enclosure, power-distribution, and thermal-protection pieces that have to be installed around those systems.

## Open questions

- How durable is the current nVent growth rate once data-center orders normalize from this phase of very intense deployment and capacity expansion?
- Does nVent keep moving toward a more systems-oriented higher-margin infrastructure platform, or does mix eventually revert toward shorter-cycle electrical products once backlog pressure eases?
