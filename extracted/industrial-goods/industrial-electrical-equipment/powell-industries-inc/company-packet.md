# Powell Industries, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-09

## Snapshot

- Company: Powell Industries, Inc.
- Sector: Industrial Goods
- Industry: Industrial Electrical Equipment
- Ticker: POWL
- Fiscal year-end: September
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q3 FY2026
  - quarter 2: Q2 FY2026
  - quarter 3: Q1 FY2026

## Annual report takeaways

- Powell improves the archive because it is a purer switchgear and electrical-energy-control operator than the broader electrical and cooling names already in the set. The company frames itself as a supplier of custom-engineered solutions for the management, control, and distribution of electrical energy.
- That makes Powell a more precise read on one of the hardest physical bottlenecks in the current build cycle: custom engineered medium-voltage and related electrical-control equipment needed to move power into complex industrial and digital projects.
- The business mix also broadens the end-market view. Powell sits across electric utility, oil and gas, petrochemical, LNG, and commercial and industrial work rather than only across hyperscale or generic building electrification.
- The fiscal-year-offset structure is useful. With a September year-end, Powell provides another live read on the same 2026 infrastructure wave but through `Q1` to `Q3 FY2026` rather than the calendar-year pattern used by many peers.
- Powell is smaller than `Eaton`, `Vertiv`, or `nVent`, but that is part of the value. Its results are a sharper operator-level signal on engineered electrical-control demand rather than on a large diversified portfolio.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q3 FY2026 revenue was `$312 million`, gross profit was `$95.3 million` at a `30.6%` margin, and net income was `$52.2 million`, or `$1.42` per diluted share.
- Demand and operating indicators: new orders reached a record `$934 million`, backlog rose to `$2.4 billion`, cash and short-term investments reached `$634 million`, and book-to-bill reached `3.0x`.
- Management message: management said commercial momentum across key end markets kept accelerating, with robust activity in oil and gas, electric utility, and commercial and industrial work.
- Structural themes: this quarter is the strongest confirmation yet that electrical infrastructure demand is broadening beyond generic power spending into data-center power design, LNG export infrastructure, fertilizer-adjacent petrochemical investment, and utility system work.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q2 FY2026 revenue was about `$296.6 million`, gross profit was about `$87.9 million` at a `29.6%` margin, and net income was `$45.9 million`, or `$1.25` per diluted share.
- Demand and operating indicators: Q3 management later referenced Q2 backlog at about `$1.8 billion`, which helps show the step-up into the Q3 record level.
- Management message: the saved local Q2 evidence is thinner than Q1 and Q3, but the filed quarter still shows a business sustaining strong revenue and margin conversion between the initial order acceleration and the Q3 breakout.
- Structural themes: Q2 matters because it shows the Powell story is not only a one-quarter order spike. It held near-`30%` gross margin and continued scaling earnings before the much larger Q3 order wave hit.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q1 FY2026 revenue was `$251.2 million`, gross profit was `$71.4 million` at a `28.4%` margin, and net income was `$41.4 million`, or `$3.40` per diluted share.
- Demand and operating indicators: new orders reached `$439 million`, backlog reached `$1.6 billion`, and cash and short-term investments reached `$501 million`.
- Management message: management highlighted the highest quarterly awards total in over two years, accelerating commercial and industrial activity, a first megaproject order in the data-center end market, and a very large LNG award on the Gulf Coast.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the key bridge quarter where Powell becomes directly relevant to the AI and electrical-capacity theme. It ties utility demand, LNG demand, and data-center power demand together inside one custom-engineered electrical-control operator.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Powell strengthens the industrial case that infrastructure intensity is concentrating in electrical bottlenecks, not only in broader facility and cooling systems.
- Technology: the data-center and AI link is direct here because management explicitly tied larger medium-voltage switchgear opportunities to rapid data-center development and AI investment.
- Utilities and energy: Powell is also a useful bridge into electric-utility generation and grid-strengthening activity, plus LNG and petrochemical capital spending.
- Project economics: backlog, book-to-bill, and project execution matter at least as much as current-quarter shipments because the business is engineered-to-order and megaproject-sensitive.
- Scale read: Powell’s smaller size makes it a good indicator name. Very large orders move the financial profile materially, which can reveal changes in the physical infrastructure cycle earlier and more sharply.
- Cultural read: Powell is one of the cleanest examples in the archive of AI showing up as a power-distribution and switchgear problem rather than as a software or chip narrative.

## Working interpretation

- Powell materially strengthens the archive because it extends the electrical-infrastructure theme into custom switchgear, control, and power-distribution equipment where data-center, LNG, utility, and petrochemical demand all meet.
- It also helps organize the industrial story correctly: the current build cycle is not only about chips, servers, and broad electrical exposure. It is also about engineered electrical-control capacity, project execution, and the ability to move large amounts of power into demanding real-world sites.
