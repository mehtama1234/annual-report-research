## What changed

- Powell sharpens the industrial archive because it is a cleaner upstream switchgear and electrical-energy-control operator than the broader electrical names. The business is explicitly about custom-engineered solutions for the management, control, and distribution of electrical energy.
- The annual and quarter chain gives a direct read on project-heavy electrical infrastructure demand across electric utility, oil and gas, petrochemical, LNG, and commercial and industrial customers.
- Q1 FY2026 already showed a structural shift in the opportunity set. Powell booked its first data-center megaproject, said total data-center orders were well above `$100 million`, and linked rising AI investment to larger medium-voltage switchgear opportunities.
- Q2 FY2026 continued the pattern with revenue around `$297 million`, gross profit around `$88 million`, and net income around `$45.9 million`, indicating that the order wave was converting into steady margin performance rather than only into backlog.
- Q3 FY2026 made the signal much stronger. Revenue reached `$312 million`, new orders surged to a record `$934 million`, backlog rose to `$2.4 billion`, and management highlighted a data-center order above `$400 million` plus LNG and petrochemical mega orders.
- Powell is analytically useful because it sits one layer closer to custom engineered electrical control gear. That adds a more specific bottleneck read than `Vertiv`, which is centered on critical digital infrastructure systems, or `nVent`, which is centered on connection, protection, enclosure, and cooling subsystems.

## Open questions

- How repeatable is the recent data-center order flow for Powell given the custom-engineered and project-specific nature of the work?
- As backlog expands quickly, can Powell keep project execution and margin quality at the current level while scaling delivery capacity?
