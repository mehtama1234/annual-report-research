## What changed

- Revenue: full-year 2025 reported revenues were `$21.3 billion`, up `7%`, with organic revenue growth of `6%`. Q4 2025 revenues were `$5.145 billion`, Q1 2026 revenues were `$4.969 billion`, and Q2 2026 revenues were `$6.354 billion`. The story is not just growth; it is accelerating commercial HVAC demand with a bigger services and controls layer attached to it.
- Earnings and margins: full-year 2025 GAAP continuing EPS was `$13.14`, adjusted continuing EPS was `$13.06`, and adjusted EBITDA margin was `20.1%`, up `70` basis points. Q4 2025 adjusted continuing EPS was `$2.86`, Q1 2026 adjusted continuing EPS was `$2.63`, and Q2 2026 adjusted continuing EPS was `$4.31`. Margins remain strong, but the 2026 quarterlies show management spending into growth and mix rather than simply harvesting volume.
- Orders and backlog: Trane is one of the clearest bookings-and-backlog stories in the repo. Full-year 2025 bookings were `$22.6 billion`, up `11%` organically, and year-end backlog reached about `$7.8 billion`, up `15%`. Q1 2026 pushed backlog to `$10.7 billion`, up more than `30%` from year-end 2025. Q2 2026 pushed backlog further to `$12.1 billion`, up `70%` year over year. Commercial HVAC, especially in the Americas, is the main engine.
- End-market mix: this adds a building-systems and climate-control lens to the industrial set. Trane is tied to commercial HVAC, building controls, energy services, residential heating and cooling, and transport refrigeration. That makes it a useful bridge between industrial capex, energy efficiency, and commercial-building retrofit demand.
- Data-center and power linkage: the 2025 annual report explicitly ties demand to the data economy, rising energy use, and resilient infrastructure. The 10-K also highlights data-center facility controls, data-center HVAC systems, data-center liquid cooling solutions, and data-center services. Trane is therefore another physical-infrastructure expression of the AI buildout, but through cooling, controls, and thermal systems instead of electrical gear alone.
- Acquisition and portfolio moves: the Q1 and Q2 2026 `10-Q` filings note the Stellar Energy Americas acquisition, which management says strengthens Trane's position in modular and scalable data-center cooling solutions. That is a concrete signal that the company is using M&A to deepen thermal-management exposure where demand is strongest.
- Capital allocation and cash flow: the annual report highlights `98%` free-cash-flow conversion in 2025 and about `$3.1 billion` of balanced capital deployment across dividends, share repurchases, acquisitions, and capex. This is not a stressed balance-sheet growth story; it is a high-cash industrial compounder.
- Guidance: on January 29, 2026 Trane entered the year guiding to about `8.5%` to `9.5%` reported revenue growth, `6%` to `7%` organic revenue growth, and full-year GAAP and adjusted continuing EPS of `$14.65` to `$14.85`. On April 30, 2026 it raised guidance to about `9.5%` reported revenue growth, `7%` organic growth, and EPS of about `$14.75` to `$14.95`. On July 30, 2026 it raised guidance again to about `11.5%` reported revenue growth, `9%` organic growth, GAAP continuing EPS of about `$15.00` to `$15.10`, and adjusted continuing EPS of `$15.20` to `$15.30`.
- Management tone: confident, backlog-led, and centered on secular demand. Trane is framing itself around sustainable and resilient infrastructure, not around a short-cycle HVAC rebound.

## Open questions

- How much of the current commercial HVAC surge is directly tied to data-center buildout versus broader retrofit and efficiency demand across conventional buildings?
- Can Trane sustain current backlog conversion and pricing discipline if commercial project lead times normalize in 2027?
