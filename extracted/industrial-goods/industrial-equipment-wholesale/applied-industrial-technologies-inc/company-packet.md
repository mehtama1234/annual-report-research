## Why this name matters

- Applied is the right industrial-equipment-wholesale flagship for this batch because it captures the MRO, motion-control, fluid-power, automation, and technical-distribution layer that keeps industrial systems operating between capital cycles.
- It also adds a more maintenance-and-engineering-heavy counterpart to WESCO’s broader infrastructure distribution mix. WESCO leans into electrical, data-center, utility, and communications distribution. Applied shows what happens when channel power is built around uptime, repair complexity, technical service, and engineered sub-systems.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual report makes clear that Applied is no longer best read as a legacy bearings distributor. Management frames the company as a value-added distributor and technical-solutions provider spanning industrial motion, fluid power, flow control, automation technologies, calibration, and related MRO supply.
- Fiscal `2025` was a record year despite a weak industrial backdrop. The annual report says sales reached `$4.6B`, up `1.9%`, while organic daily sales declined `2.3%`, EPS reached `$10.12`, EBITDA margin was `12.3%`, operating cash flow was `$492M`, and free cash flow was `$465M`.
- The strategic point is mix. Management says Engineered Solutions is approaching `40%` of total sales versus `18%` ten years ago, which means more of the business now depends on technical applications, service depth, automation capability, and faster-growing end markets instead of only stocked replacement parts.
- The annual letter also provides a good pressure read. Customers managed MRO and capital spending conservatively through fiscal `2025` because of election uncertainty, rate sensitivity, and trade-policy noise, with the weakest demand in off-highway mobile OEMs and heavy industry.
- Even in that muted setting, management highlighted encouraging demand tied to datacenters, semiconductor manufacturing, process infrastructure, advanced robotics, and calibration services. That is exactly why this company belongs in the archive: it shows how AI and industrial modernization translate into fluid conveyance, control systems, and technical channel demand, not just chips and software.
- Capital allocation stayed active. Management highlighted record free cash generation, more than `$560M` deployed across capex, M&A, buybacks, dividends, and debt reduction, and the Hydradyne acquisition as a major move to deepen fluid-power scale and technical reach.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Fiscal Q3 2026

- Net sales were `$1.251B`, up `7.3%` year over year. Organic sales rose `6.0%`, with Service Center organic growth of `4.2%` and Engineered Solutions organic growth of `9.3%`.
- Net income was `$99.8M`, diluted EPS was `$2.65`, operating income was `$137.9M`, EBITDA was `$153.9M`, operating cash flow was `$100.1M`, and free cash flow was `$95.4M`.
- This quarter matters because growth broadened rather than narrowed. Management explicitly described strengthening organic sales growth across both segments, with Engineered Solutions benefiting from positive order trends, improving demand across both legacy and emerging verticals, and the company’s application and engineering depth.
- The outlook also improved. Guidance moved to EPS of `$10.64-$10.75`, total sales growth of `7.2%-7.7%`, organic growth of `3.8%-4.2%`, and EBITDA margin of `12.3%-12.4%`.
- The quarter also reinforces the capital-allocation signal: the board replaced the prior buyback plan with a new authorization for up to `3.0M` shares while maintaining the dividend at `$0.51`.

### Quarter minus 1: Fiscal Q2 2026

- Net sales were `$1.163B`, up `8.4%`. Organic sales grew `2.2%`, with Service Center up `2.9%` and Engineered Solutions up `0.5%`.
- Net income was `$95.3M`, diluted EPS was `$2.51`, and EBITDA was `$140.4M`.
- This quarter is useful because it shows Applied was still growing even before the sharper third-quarter acceleration, but the mix was more uneven. Acquisitions carried more of the growth load, while organic demand was positive but modest.
- LIFO expense was a real drag here, and management explicitly called out ongoing inflationary and tariff-related uncertainty when discussing the outlook.
- The company also announced the acquisition of Thompson Industrial Supply in Southern California, expected to add about `$20M` of first-year sales, which fits the pattern of using bolt-on deals to fill local service density and aftermarket support gaps.
- The dividend increase to `$0.51` also matters because it shows management was confident enough in cash generation and balance-sheet capacity to keep lifting shareholder returns while still funding growth investments.

### Quarter minus 2: Fiscal Q1 2026

- Net sales were `$1.200B`, up `9.2%`. Organic sales rose `3.0%`, with Service Center up `4.4%` and Engineered Solutions down `0.4%`.
- Net income was `$100.8M`, diluted EPS was `$2.63`, operating income was `$129.0M`, EBITDA was `$146.3M`, operating cash flow was `$119.3M`, and free cash flow was `$112.0M`.
- The key read is that Service Center strength arrived before Engineered Solutions fully turned. Management tied the quarter to firming technical MRO demand, sales-force initiatives, and channel execution, while still describing capital-spending improvement as gradual and uneven.
- Guidance was raised after the quarter, but only modestly. Management lifted EPS to `$10.10-$10.85` while maintaining sales and EBITDA-margin expectations, which suggests confidence in execution without overcommitting on the macro.
- This quarter is also a clean reminder that Applied’s model is not one macro beta. Service Center and Engineered Solutions can move differently, and that divergence helps show where demand is coming from: break-fix MRO versus more project-oriented technical systems.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Applied is not consumer-facing, but it reflects a major behavior shift inside industrial America: customers increasingly want fewer suppliers, more technical support, and faster outsourced problem-solving rather than managing a fragmented maintenance and repair base themselves.
- The annual letter is explicit that customer labor constraints matter. That is a social and operational trend, not a small footnote. If factories and industrial operators do not have enough skilled internal labor to maintain and upgrade production assets, value moves toward the distributor that can bundle product, service, engineering, and repair depth.
- There is also a reshoring and infrastructure-modernization angle. Management ties opportunity to aged U.S. manufacturing infrastructure, growing technical service requirements, and expanding investment into production capacity, which means the business sits inside a broader national effort to rebuild physical capability rather than just sell replacement parts.
- Applied also shows how AI and automation demand move through the physical layer. Datacenters, semiconductor plants, robotics, process infrastructure, and electric-powered fluid-power systems all require controls, conveyance, motion, and technical integration. That demand monetizes through companies like Applied long after the market has already priced the software narrative.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Applied is still exposed to industrial hesitation. The annual report and first-half commentary repeatedly cite customer caution around MRO and capital spending, especially in off-highway mobile, machinery, and other heavy-industry verticals.
- Trade and tariff uncertainty remain active constraints. Management referenced those issues in both the annual outlook and quarter guidance language, which matters because the company sits in the middle of procurement, pricing, supplier relationships, and customer operating budgets.
- Mix is a source of strength but also interpretive complexity. Service Center demand can benefit from break-fix urgency and deferred maintenance, while Engineered Solutions is more exposed to order timing, project starts, automation investment, and specific growth verticals.
- Inflation and LIFO still matter. Quarter commentary makes clear that earnings are not just a sales story; cost discipline, pricing, and inventory-accounting effects are still part of the margin read.
- The acquisition program is strategically useful, but it raises execution demands. Hydradyne, Thompson Industrial Supply, and other bolt-ons support technical breadth and local density, but they also require integration discipline if the company wants the margin and cross-sell benefits management is underwriting.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Technical distribution is becoming a platform business.` Applied is a strong example of how scale in industrial distribution increasingly depends on engineering support, service, repair, automation, and cross-sell depth rather than only catalog breadth.
- `MRO resilience and capital-cycle recovery are different demand streams.` Service Center strength can firm earlier through break-fix and deferred maintenance, while Engineered Solutions often reflects a later-stage recovery in project and automation spending.
- `AI and software narratives still depend on physical channel operators.` Datacenter, semiconductor, robotics, and process-infrastructure buildouts create demand for fluid conveyance, control systems, calibration, and motion technologies that companies like Applied intermediate.
- `Labor scarcity shifts value toward outsourced expertise.` The more customers struggle to staff technical maintenance and plant-support functions internally, the more valuable the distributor becomes as an operational partner.
- `Industry fragmentation creates share gain for scaled specialists.` Applied’s own letter argues that customer supply-chain focus and system complexity are increasing consolidation across the sector, which is a recurring pattern across this whole batch.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `WESCO International`: WESCO shows broader infrastructure, electrical, and data-center distribution scale; Applied shows a more technical maintenance, fluid-power, automation, and engineered-solutions version of channel power.
- Against `Core & Main`: Core & Main is category-specific distribution tied to water and fire systems, while Applied is a more general industrial uptime and technical-service distributor.
- Against `United Rentals`: United Rentals monetizes fleet access and equipment outsourcing; Applied monetizes uptime, repair complexity, technical inventory, and engineered process support.
