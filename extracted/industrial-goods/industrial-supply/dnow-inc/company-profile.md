## What changed

- Business identity: DNOW is no longer just a legacy energy distributor. The `2025` filing frames it as a premier energy and industrial solutions provider spanning upstream, gas utilities, downstream and industrial, midstream, and energy-transition-adjacent infrastructure.
- Network and product breadth: the business now operates under both `DNOW` and `MRC Global` brands and, after the merger, serves customers through roughly `300` locations with a broader mix of PVF, gas products, pumps, fabricated equipment, valve automation, measurement, instrumentation, and digital sourcing tools.
- Annual baseline: full-year `2025` revenue was `$2,820M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$209M`, operating cash flow was `$155M`, and the company repurchased `$37M` of stock, but GAAP net income was distorted by merger-related charges and purchase-accounting effects.
- Merger scale-up: DNOW completed the `MRC Global` acquisition on `2025-11-06` for about `$1,763M` net of cash acquired, recorded provisional goodwill of `$383M`, and said first-year merger savings were already projected at `$23M`, `35%` above target, while maintaining the `$70M` three-year synergy goal.
- Quarter cadence: `Q1 2026` showed the strain of the first full post-merger quarter, with weak cash conversion and ERP transition friction, while `Q2 2026` showed a clear rebound in revenue, EBITDA, cash flow, and leverage.
- Category and end-market direction: management is leaning more explicitly into gas utilities, midstream, automation, data centers, LNG, RNG, and industrial process demand, which broadens the company beyond pure upstream cycle exposure.

## Open questions

- How much of the `Q2 2026` improvement is durable operating normalization versus a temporary release from working-capital cleanup after the merger?
- Can DNOW keep capturing synergies and improving margins without disrupting service levels during the longer integration and ERP cleanup process?
- Does the company’s broader end-market mix materially reduce cyclicality, or does energy and project timing still dominate the earnings profile more than management suggests?
