# Fastenal Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

  - quarter 1: Q2 2026

## Why this name matters

- Fastenal is the right industrial-supply flagship for this batch because it covers the layer between raw physical inputs and actual work getting done inside factories, maintenance shops, and jobsites.
- It is also a strong comparison point against Ferguson and Applied. Ferguson shows broad contractor and building-systems distribution, Applied shows engineering-heavy MRO and automation support, and Fastenal shows what recurring channel power looks like when procurement is embedded directly at the point of use through onsite staffing, FMI machines, eBusiness workflows, and customer-specific service density.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual filing makes clear that Fastenal is much broader than the name suggests. Management describes a global network selling industrial and construction supplies across more than nine major product lines rather than only fasteners.
- The business is still physical and local, but the control point is increasingly digital. The annual filing says Digital Footprint represented `61.4%` of sales in `2025`, covering FMI and non-FMI eBusiness activity. That is a major clue about what the company has become: a distributor whose moat increasingly depends on workflow integration, vending, replenishment, and procurement software embedded into customer operations.
- Network scale still matters. Fastenal ended `2025` with `1,595` branch locations in `25` countries, `19` distribution centers globally, and `24,489` employees. But management also changed how it talks about network density, shifting disclosure toward `Sites` instead of only public branches and customer-dedicated onsites. That matters because the company is increasingly monetizing presence inside the customer rather than just reach around the customer.
- `2025` was a share-gain year more than a macro year. The annual commentary says market contribution was minimal, tariff-related pricing added `170` to `200` basis points, and the main driver of daily sales growth of `9.1%` was share gains tied to improved contract signings with large key-account customers.
- The full-year financial baseline was strong. Net sales were `$8,200.5M`, gross profit was `$3,691.2M`, gross margin was `45.0%`, SG&A was `24.8%` of sales, operating income was `$1,655.7M`, operating margin was `20.2%`, and net income was `$1,258.4M`.
- Product mix is also widening. The annual materials and subsequent quarter releases emphasize safety, cutting tools, and broader non-fastener lines. That matters because it pushes Fastenal deeper into routine plant spend, jobsite consumables, and recurring indirect procurement rather than a narrow catalog niche.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Net sales were `$2,386.9M`, up `14.7%`, and daily sales were `$37.3M`, also up `14.7%`.
- Operating income was `$501.8M`, operating margin was `21.0%`, and operating cash flow was `$266M`, equal to `69%` of net income.
- Gross margin was `44.6%`, down from `45.3%`, while SG&A improved to `23.5%` of sales from `24.4%`, showing that expense leverage offset gross-margin pressure.
- Management explicitly said the results reflected growth with larger customers supported by onsite, digital, and supply-chain solutions. It also said progress continued through increased sales effectiveness, key-account wins, expansion of digital technologies, and deeper penetration through new customer-site growth.
- The operating-pressure read is important. Management described daily sales growth as driven by share gains with larger customers, pricing actions, and broad-based demand across core end markets, while still flagging unfavorable price / cost as the main gross-margin headwind.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Daily sales increased `12.4%`, operating margin was `20.3%`, operating cash flow was `$378M`, and the company returned `$296M` to shareholders.
- Management said results benefited from key-account wins, expansion of technology, and deeper customer-site growth, which is consistent with the annual share-gain thesis.
- The quarter also showed the limits of growth quality. Product pricing contributed roughly `350` basis points to sales, but gross margin faced an unfavorable price / cost effect of about `50` basis points.
- Management also said the customer mix remained a structural gross-margin headwind because more growth is coming from larger customers. That matters because Fastenal is trading some unit margin for embeddedness, density, and higher long-run share of wallet.
- Reporting changes introduced from `Q4 2025` onward, including a four-category product split of fasteners, safety supplies, cutting tools, and other products, reflect management’s desire to show the breadth of direct and indirect spend it now captures.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Full-year `2025` net sales were `$8,200.5M`, up `8.7%`, and Q4 net sales were `$2,027.4M`, up `11.1%`.
- Full-year daily sales rose `9.1%`, and Q4 daily sales rose `11.1%`.
- Full-year operating margin was `20.2%`, while Q4 operating margin was `19.0%`, slightly above the prior year.
- Management said industrial production was still sluggish in Q4 `2025`, so the growth read again points back to contract signings, share gains, pricing, and deeper customer relationships rather than broad economic strength.
- Pricing contributed `310` to `340` basis points in Q4, but management said price / cost was still slightly negative by `10` basis points.
- Capital allocation was conservative and income-oriented. Fastenal returned `$1,004.2M` to shareholders in `2025`, entirely through dividends, equal to `79.8%` of net income, and ended the year with only `$125.0M` of debt.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Fastenal is not consumer-facing, but it reflects a broad change in how organizations buy basic physical inputs. Customers increasingly want inventory control, fewer suppliers, automated replenishment, and procurement systems that remove manual friction. This is a societal shift toward operational outsourcing and embedded workflow dependence.
- The onsite and FMI model also maps to labor scarcity. If plants, warehouses, utilities, and contractors are short of skilled people who can manage storerooms, purchasing, and replenishment internally, value shifts toward the distributor that can put people, inventory, and software inside the customer’s operation.
- There is a cultural shift here too: procurement is becoming part of uptime management rather than a back-office function. Fastenal monetizes that by moving from “we sell parts” toward “we run a piece of your supply chain.”
- Safety-product growth is also a social and regulatory signal. It reflects a labor environment where compliance, worker retention, and operational continuity matter enough that safety spend becomes a recurring category rather than a discretionary add-on.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Industrial production remained sluggish into late `2025` and only slightly improved in early `2026`, so Fastenal is still operating in a muted macro environment.
- Pricing helped growth, but tariff-related pricing is a double-edged support. It boosts reported sales while also creating price / cost friction and customer sensitivity.
- The shift toward larger customers creates stronger contract visibility and deeper workflow dependence, but it also pressures gross margin because large-account pricing is more competitive.
- The model depends on execution density. Onsite placements, FMI installations, branch coverage, trucking, distribution centers, and digital tools all have to work together. This is a simpler-looking business than software, but it is operationally demanding.
- Category broadening into safety, cutting tools, and other indirect supplies improves wallet share and resilience, but it also raises the need for sales discipline and service consistency across a much wider basket of products.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Industrial distribution is becoming infrastructure software with trucks.` Fastenal shows that a physical distributor can build platform-like lock-in by owning replenishment logic, usage data, vending access, and embedded workflows.
- `Embedded customer presence matters more than catalog breadth alone.` The shift from branch counts to `Sites` signals that the real moat is presence inside customer operations, not just real estate around them.
- `Recurring revenue in the physical layer often hides inside service intensity.` Fastenal does not present like a subscription software company, but recurring demand emerges through consumables, replenishment, onsite labor, and procurement integration.
- `Share gains can outrun the cycle when the channel is fragmented.` Management repeatedly says market growth was weak and share gains drove results. That is a recurring pattern across this batch: scaled operators in messy physical channels can compound even in mediocre macro conditions.
- `AI and cloud demand still depend on mundane supply movement.` Every datacenter, factory retrofit, maintenance program, and electrification buildout consumes the low-glamor items Fastenal distributes. The software narrative still rests on physical uptime and procurement execution underneath it.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Ferguson Enterprises`: Ferguson is broader across plumbing, HVAC, PVF, waterworks, and contractor channels. Fastenal is more focused on plant, jobsite, MRO, and embedded industrial procurement.
- Against `Applied Industrial Technologies`: Applied leans more heavily into technical motion, fluid power, automation, and engineering support. Fastenal is more standardized and scalable around consumables, safety, storeroom control, and workflow integration.
- Against `WESCO International`: WESCO shows electrical, utility, communications, and data-center distribution at larger project scale. Fastenal shows the more frequent-touch consumables and onsite-services layer that keeps everyday operations running.
