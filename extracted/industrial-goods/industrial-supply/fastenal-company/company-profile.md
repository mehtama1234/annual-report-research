## What changed

- Business identity: Fastenal is no longer best read as a simple fastener distributor. Management increasingly frames the company as a supply-chain-services operator with local branches, customer sites, FMI machines, eBusiness tools, and digital procurement infrastructure.
- Network design: the annual filing says Fastenal ended `2025` with `1,595` branch locations in `25` countries, `19` distribution centers globally, and `24,489` employees, but management also shifted disclosure from traditional branch and onsite counts toward a broader `Sites` concept that captures embedded customer-presence models.
- Digital mix: the `2025` annual filing says Digital Footprint represented `61.4%` of sales, making the company a physical-distribution operator with a software and workflow layer increasingly built into procurement.
- Growth engine: annual and quarterly commentary repeatedly says growth was driven mainly by share gains with larger customers, improved contract signings, onsite expansion, FMI placements, and deeper key-account penetration rather than a strong macro cycle.
- Pricing and mix: tariff-related pricing contributed `170` to `200` basis points to `2025` growth and roughly `350` basis points in `Q1 2026` and `290` basis points in `Q2 2026`, but price / cost stayed mildly unfavorable, while the customer mix continued shifting toward larger accounts.
- Category evolution: management elevated safety products, cutting tools, and broader non-fastener categories, showing how Fastenal is monetizing recurring plant and jobsite spend beyond its historical core.

## Open questions

- How much longer can Fastenal offset muted industrial production with pure share gains, onsite wins, and digital procurement expansion?
- Does the larger-customer mix shift create a durable moat through embedded workflows, or does it gradually pressure gross margin more than SG&A leverage can offset?
- How exposed is the current growth pace to tariff-related pricing that may fade or reverse if trade conditions normalize?
