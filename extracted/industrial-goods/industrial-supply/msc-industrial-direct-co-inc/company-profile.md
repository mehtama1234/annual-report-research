## What changed

- The `2025` filing makes the business-model mix clearer: MSC is not just a catalog distributor. It is a plant-floor procurement and inventory-management layer built around metalworking, MRO, Class C consumables, and OEM fasteners.
- Scale still matters. The annual filing says MSC had more than `2.5 million` active saleable SKUs, more than `7,000` associates, `5` customer fulfillment centers, `9` regional inventory centers, `38` warehouses, and `5` manufacturing locations.
- Revenue softness in `2025` was volume-led, not a collapse in pricing power. Net sales were `$3.77B`, down `1.3%`, with lower volume and unfavorable foreign exchange partly offset by improved pricing, mix, and discounting plus acquisitions.
- Gross-margin pressure is structural, not accidental. The annual filing explicitly points to customer mix, product mix, national-account and government-program growth, and the expansion of vending and e-commerce as recurring margin drags.
- The operating playbook is still `Mission Critical`. Management framed phase two around defending existing growth drivers, focusing more tightly on core customers and OEM fasteners, and taking operating expenses down as a percentage of sales through analytics, digital-core upgrades, and order-to-cash / procure-to-pay productivity.
- The quarter arc into `2026` shows a recovery path rather than a straight-line story. Profitability was still compressed in fiscal `Q1` and `Q2 2026`, then improved sharply in fiscal `Q3 2026` as average daily sales growth accelerated and operating margin moved back above `10%`.

## Open questions

- How durable is the fiscal `Q3 2026` margin rebound if metalworking and machine-shop demand cools again?
- Can MSC keep expanding vending and In-Plant density while carrying fewer field sellers without service quality slipping at smaller and more cyclical manufacturing customers?
- Does the push toward national accounts, government, and e-commerce deepen recurring dependence, or does it keep capping gross margin even when industrial demand improves?
