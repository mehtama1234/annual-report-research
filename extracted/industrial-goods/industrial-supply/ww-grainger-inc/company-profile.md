## What changed

- Business model clarity: Grainger is now best understood as a two-engine platform. `High-Touch Solutions N.A.` serves complex enterprise procurement with service-heavy MRO workflows, while `Endless Assortment` monetizes online catalog breadth through `Zoro` and `MonotaRO`.
- Channel mix: the `2025` annual filing shows `HTSNA` generated `$13,993M` of sales and `EA` generated `$3,625M`, with `EA` still the smaller segment but the faster grower.
- Profit mix: `HTSNA` produced `$2,354M` of segment operating earnings in `2025` versus `$345M` for `EA`, showing that the higher-touch enterprise channel still funds the system even as online assortment scales faster.
- Geographic simplification: in the fourth quarter of `2025`, Grainger completed the sale of `Cromwell` and closed `Zoro U.K.`, tightening the operating footprint around North America and Japan.
- Working-capital and capex intensity: the company is still a distributor, not an asset-light software platform. It generated `$2,015M` of operating cash flow in `2025` but also invested heavily in supply-chain capacity, including U.S. projects and MonotaRO distribution-center expansion.
- End-market breadth: the `2026` quarter filings show a durable exposure spread. Manufacturing represented `30%` of company revenue in both `Q1` and `Q2 2026`, government was `15%` to `16%`, healthcare `6%`, contractors `7%`, and utilities `3%`.

## Open questions

- How much of the recent margin strength in `Q2 2026` is repeatable once the `IEEPA` tariff-refund benefit rolls off?
- Can `Endless Assortment` keep compounding fast enough to justify heavy ongoing distribution-center investment at `MonotaRO` without diluting overall returns?
- Does tariff-driven price inflation deepen customer dependence on Grainger’s procurement stack, or does it eventually create substitution and margin pressure in standardized categories?
