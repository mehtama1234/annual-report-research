## What changed

- Business mix: ICF exited `2025` as a more diversified management-services company, with stronger exposure to commercial energy, international work, and state and local programs while U.S. federal work weakened.
- Contract economics: the company emphasized that `93%` of `2025` revenue came from fixed-price and time-and-materials contracts, up from `89%` in `2024`, while cost-reimbursement work declined to `7%`.
- Client mix: in Q4 `2025`, non-federal revenue streams accounted for `62%` of revenue. In Q2 `2026`, commercial clients represented `35%` of revenue while government clients still represented `65%`, showing a hybrid institutional model rather than a pure federal contractor.
- Growth engines: management repeatedly highlighted commercial energy, technology modernization, disaster recovery, health data, and digital transformation as the growth markets carrying the company through a weak federal procurement backdrop.
- Operating posture: the quarter chain shows stable margins, backlog durability, and rising pipeline even while certain federal contracts were cancelled, procurement slowed, and a government shutdown distorted year-on-year comparisons.

## Open questions

- How durable is the federal recovery if procurement delays and policy volatility persist into `2027`?
- Does the commercial-energy growth engine remain strong once utilities lap easy efficiency and electrification comparisons?
- Can ICF keep expanding fixed-price, outcome-based work without eventually taking on more execution risk than its margin profile can comfortably absorb?
