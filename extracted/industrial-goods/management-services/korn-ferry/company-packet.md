## Annual report takeaways

- Korn Ferry is a strong `Management Services` packet because it shows that institutions increasingly buy talent infrastructure, not just one-off executive recruiting. The company sits across executive search, consulting, digital workflow support, professional search, interim talent, and recruitment process outsourcing.
- The `2025` annual materials are especially useful for this frontier because they show a management-services operator trying to turn labor-market volatility into a multi-product institutional interface. The question is no longer only who gets hired. It is who gets paid to map roles, shape leadership, source interim expertise, run recruiting workflows, and keep organizations staffed through change.
- The annual filing also shows that Korn Ferry is not a pure strategy advisory firm. Its operating logic is closer to a talent-access and talent-operations stack. That matters for the frontier because institutions under pressure often buy execution capacity first and high-level advice second.
- The `2025` filing also captures the lane's consolidation pattern. The company disclosed that it completed the Trilogy acquisition on `2024-11-01` for about `$44.4M`, adding digital interim talent capabilities, while previously acquired businesses such as Salo and ICS deepened interim finance, HR, and IT talent coverage. That turns management services into a more direct labor-market adaptation business.
- For this frontier, Korn Ferry strengthens the human-capital and institutional-interface lane by giving it a company that monetizes organizational adaptation through leadership architecture, outsourced recruiting, and specialist labor access rather than through public-sector implementation, research memberships, or education throughput alone.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q4 FY2026 fee revenue was `$759.8M`, up `7%`; full-year FY2026 fee revenue was `$2.9B`; Q4 net income attributable to Korn Ferry was `$73.1M` with margin of `9.6%`; Q4 adjusted EBITDA was `$129.5M` with margin of `17.0%`; and remaining fees under existing contracts ended the quarter at about `$1.9B`, up `10%`.
- Solution and mix signals: management highlighted fifth consecutive quarter of top-line growth. Executive Search Q4 fee revenue was about `$242.0M`, up `7%`; Professional Search & Interim was about `$149.1M`, up `14%`; and RPO was about `$97.6M`, up `5%`.
- Capital-allocation signal: the company repurchased about `1.24M` shares for `$78.8M` in the quarter and paid about `$28.3M` in dividends.
- Interpretation: the quarter shows institutions continuing to spend on leadership, specialist staffing, and outsourced recruiting even in an uneven macro environment. Demand is not broad-based labor exuberance. It is targeted spending on hard-to-build talent infrastructure.
- Bigger-picture read: this is a strong frontier packet because it shows labor-market adaptation being bought in layers. Clients need permanent search, but they also need interim capacity, recruiting workflow support, and organizational design when business conditions stay uncertain.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q3 FY2026 fee revenue was `$717.4M`, up `7%`; net income attributable to Korn Ferry rose `12%`; margin was `9.1%`; adjusted EBITDA margin was `17.2%`; diluted EPS was `$1.23`; and adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.28`.
- Demand and backlog signals: estimated remaining fees under contracts ended the quarter at about `$1.9B`, up `11%`, led by Digital at `16%`, Consulting at `12%`, and RPO at `10%`.
- Interpretation: Q3 matters because it shows that the most durable institutional spending is not isolated to search. Growth spread across multiple solutions, which suggests buyers are purchasing broader organizational capability during uncertainty rather than only backfilling specific roles.
- Bigger-picture read: the quarter makes clear that the human-capital lane is not only about hiring volume. It is about outside operators who help institutions manage capability gaps when technology, structure, and labor supply are all moving at once.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q2 FY2026 fee revenue was `$721.7M`, up `7%`; net income attributable to Korn Ferry was `$72.4M` with margin of `10.0%`; adjusted EBITDA was `$124.8M` with margin of `17.3%`; diluted EPS was `$1.36`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.33`; and estimated remaining fees under existing contracts were about `$1.842B`, up `20%`.
- Solution and acquisition signals: Executive Search fee revenue was about `$226.0M`, up `10%`; Professional Search & Interim was about `$141.1M`, up `17%`; Consulting was about `$172.8M`, up `4%`; and RPO was about `$90.8M`, up `4%`. Management said the Professional Search & Interim growth was helped by the Trilogy acquisition and also noted lower margins in interim work than in permanent placement.
- Operating-friction signal: the company also referenced accelerated depreciation tied to the expected sunsetting of a digital platform, which shows the lane's workflow-modernization cost even when revenue momentum is healthy.
- Interpretation: Q2 is the clearest quarter for seeing the packet's tradeoff. The fastest-growing parts of the talent stack can be more operationally valuable to clients but structurally less margin-rich to the provider.
- Bigger-picture read: that tension is frontier-relevant because institutional buyers increasingly want flexible, outsourced expertise. The service operator can grow by meeting that need, but mix shifts toward interim and more workflow-intensive products can change the economics of the lane.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Institutions are buying talent infrastructure: Korn Ferry is a direct read on how enterprises outsource leadership search, recruiting workflows, interim staffing, and organizational redesign when internal systems are too slow or too thin.
- Labor-market adaptation is now a product stack: the packet shows that the monetization surface includes executive search, consulting, digital tools, interim capacity, and RPO rather than one narrow recruiting fee stream.
- Contracted-fee balances matter: remaining fees under existing contracts stayed strong across the latest quarters, which makes Korn Ferry a useful signal for institutional demand even when daily hiring headlines are noisy.
- Mix quality matters as much as growth: faster interim and professional-search growth improves demand visibility but can pressure margin mix relative to permanent-placement economics.
- Consolidation is part of the lane story: Trilogy, Salo, and ICS show a repeated pattern in which management-services operators buy narrower specialist firms to assemble a fuller labor-market interface.
- Frontier fit: Korn Ferry materially improves the third sub-lane because it gives the archive a management-services packet that is less implementation-heavy than ICF and less education-centered than Grand Canyon while still explaining how institutions buy adaptation.

## Working interpretation

- Korn Ferry materially strengthens the frontier because it makes the institutional-interface lane less dependent on public-sector implementation firms, research memberships, and education platforms. It adds a direct read on how enterprises buy talent access and organizational adaptation itself.
- The recurring social pattern is that institutions are struggling to keep capability, leadership, and staffing aligned with change. Korn Ferry monetizes that strain by sitting between employers and the professional labor market.
- The strongest repeating operating pattern is breadth with tradeoffs. A fuller product stack can stabilize demand and deepen client dependence, but the fastest-growing products are not always the highest-margin ones.
- Korn Ferry also links this lane back to broader frontier themes: when organizations cannot solve complexity internally, they increasingly pay outside operators to source talent, run hiring workflows, and keep transformation programs staffed.
