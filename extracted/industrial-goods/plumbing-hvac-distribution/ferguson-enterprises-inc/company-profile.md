## What changed

- Revenue: The annual and trailing-quarter chain shows steady top-line growth driven by share gains, bolt-on acquisitions, and durable non-residential activity even as housing stayed soft.
- Margin: Gross margin held around `31%`, with supplier-price timing and inflation changing the quarter-to-quarter pattern more than any collapse in execution.
- Guidance: Management kept guidance unchanged on `2026-05-05` and raised it on `2026-08-10`, moving net-sales growth to mid-single digits and lifting the low end of adjusted operating margin.
- Capital allocation: Ferguson paired acquisitions with regular repurchases and dividends while keeping net debt to adjusted EBITDA near `1.0x-1.3x`.
- Segment mix: The most important mix signals are waterworks, commercial / mechanical, HVAC, PVF, and the reappearance of residential growth in `Q2 2026`.
- Operational issues: Residential RMI and new construction remained weak, but bidding, open orders, and large capital project activity stayed healthy.
- Consumer / cultural signals: Repair and replacement work stayed tied to affordability pressure and deferral behavior, while water, comfort, and reliability remained essential rather than discretionary.
- Management tone: The tone stayed disciplined and market-share focused, with repeated emphasis on local scale, specialized service, acquisitions, and structural growth drivers such as water infrastructure and aging housing stock.

## Open questions

- How durable is the return to residential growth in `Q2 2026` if rates and affordability stay restrictive?
- Does the FloWorks acquisition signal a deeper move into process, industrial-flow, and higher-complexity valve channels?
- How much of the margin profile depends on favorable supplier-price timing versus underlying service and mix improvement?
