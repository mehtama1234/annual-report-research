## What changed

- Revenue: full-year 2025 operating revenue was `$24.5 billion`, up `1%`, while freight revenue excluding fuel surcharge grew `3%`. Q4 2025 operating revenue was `$6.1 billion`, down `1%`; Q1 2026 operating revenue was `$6.2 billion`, up `3%`; and Q2 2026 operating revenue was a record `$6.9 billion`, up `12%`. This is a pricing, mix, fuel-surcharge, and network-efficiency story as much as a volume story.
- Earnings and margins: full-year 2025 net income was `$7.1 billion` and diluted EPS was `$11.98`, while adjusted diluted EPS was `$11.66`. The full-year reported operating ratio improved to `59.8%`, and adjusted operating ratio improved to `59.3%`. Q1 2026 diluted EPS was `$2.87` and Q2 2026 diluted EPS was `$3.36`. Union Pacific is presenting a disciplined margin-and-productivity story rather than a pure cyclical volume rebound.
- Volumes, pricing, and mix: the rail evidence set is useful because it separates demand into carloads, core pricing, business mix, and fuel. Q4 2025 revenue carloads declined `4%`, but core pricing gains and fuel surcharge revenue partly offset that pressure. Q1 2026 had `1%` fewer carloads, yet pricing, fuel surcharge, and mix still pushed revenue up. Q2 2026 volume growth was `2%`, with strong domestic intermodal, grain, and plastics helping offset other weaker pockets.
- Operating efficiency: Union Pacific repeatedly frames the story around service and productivity. The 2025 evidence set highlights improved freight car velocity, terminal dwell, train length, workforce productivity, and fuel consumption. Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 continue that pattern, which makes rail a useful operating-discipline counterpoint to backlog-heavy capital-goods names.
- End-market mix: this adds the transport-and-supply-chain layer to the industrial set. Union Pacific is exposed to domestic intermodal, international intermodal, grain, coal and renewables, industrial chemicals and plastics, automotive, and broader merchandise freight. That broadens the industrial archive beyond equipment vendors into freight flows and real-economy shipping intensity.
- Capital allocation and network investment: the annual filing says Union Pacific invested about `$3.5 billion` in capital during 2025 to harden infrastructure, modernize locomotives, grow the business, improve service, and embed new technology. The 2026 outlook maintained a capital plan of `$3.3 billion`.
- Strategy and regulatory overhang: the 2026 evidence set is complicated by merger-related costs and regulatory process language around the proposed Norfolk Southern transaction. Q1 2026 included merger costs of `$36 million`, and Q4 2025 included merger costs of `$30 million`. This makes the story partly about standalone rail execution and partly about strategic consolidation optionality.
- Guidance: on January 27, 2026 management said 2026 was on track for mid-single-digit reported earnings-per-share growth, pricing above inflation, operating-ratio improvement, and strong cash generation. On April 23, 2026 it reaffirmed that outlook. On July 23, 2026 it improved the EPS-growth view to high-single digit while affirming pricing, operating-ratio improvement, cash generation, and the `$3.3 billion` capital plan.
- Management tone: operational, disciplined, and network-focused. Union Pacific is not framing itself as a simple macro freight beta. It is framing itself as a productivity and pricing-led rail franchise with selective volume growth and a large strategic overlay from the proposed transcontinental merger.

## Open questions

- How durable is the current intermodal and domestic volume strength if the broader economy weakens or mix shifts away from the most efficient freight categories?
- Does the proposed Norfolk Southern transaction remain a value-creating strategic option, or does it mainly add regulatory distraction and deal-cost noise to an otherwise strong standalone operating story?
