# Herc Holdings Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Herc Holdings Inc.
- Sector: Industrial Goods
- Industry: Rental & Leasing Services
- Ticker: HRI
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Assigned CLI 8 sub-lane: asset access and operating support
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Why this name matters

- Herc is the strongest next rental-and-leasing expansion because it gives CLI 8 a second flagship equipment-rental operator rather than leaving rental economics mostly represented by `United Rentals`.
- It matters for CLI 8 because it makes outsourced asset access, fleet density, specialty mix, acquisition-driven branch expansion, and rental-versus-ownership logic directly visible.
- It also adds a cleaner read on how construction activity, mega projects, fuel pressure, and capital discipline shape a B2B middle layer that customers use to avoid carrying full equipment ownership themselves.

## Annual report takeaways

- AnnualReports confirms that Herc belongs in `Industrial Goods / Rental & Leasing Services`, but the site still lagged at `2024` on `2026-08-10`, so the packet is anchored to SEC materials for the required `2025` annual-plus-quarter chain.
- The business model is straightforward and important to the frontier: the intermediary owns and optimizes a large fleet so customers can rent equipment when needed instead of tying up capital in owned machines.
- AnnualReports describes Herc as one of the leading equipment-rental suppliers with approximately `270` company-operated locations across North America, spanning commercial and residential construction, industrial and manufacturing, civil infrastructure, energy, emergency response, facilities, entertainment, and agriculture.
- Fiscal `2025` was a scale year shaped by the H&E acquisition. Record equipment rental revenue rose `18%` to `$3.770B`, total revenues rose `23%` to `$4.376B`, adjusted EBITDA rose `15%` to `$1.818B`, and free cash flow reached `$299M`.
- The year also shows the pressure side of the model. Net income was only `$1M`, and adjusted net income fell to `$239M`, because acquisition transaction costs, integration expense, lower acquired-fleet utilization, and a heavier interest burden all weighed on the reported outcome.
- Management’s annual and year-end framing is exactly why this belongs in CLI 8. Herc is monetizing branch network scale, fleet mix, technology migration, route and territory optimization, and cost synergies rather than manufacturing the underlying equipment.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: equipment rental revenue increased `23%` to `$1.072B`, total revenues increased `20%` to `$1.204B`, net income reached `$19M`, adjusted net income reached `$48M`, and adjusted EBITDA increased `19%` to `$487M`.
- Utilization and mix signal: dollar utilization improved to `39.3%` from `38.3%`, which management tied to higher fleet efficiency and a favorable shift toward higher-return fleet on rent.
- Structural read: Herc said growth was led by national accounts, robust mega-project activity, and a higher mix of specialty rentals. That makes the packet useful as a read on industrial and infrastructure demand concentration rather than simple local construction noise.
- Pressure read: the quarter also captured a direct cost headwind from fuel inflation, which is important because this lane is exposed not just to demand but to transport and field-service economics.
- Cash-flow read: first-half `2026` free cash flow reached `$202M` versus `$103M` in the prior year, showing that the fleet model can still generate cash while carrying higher scale.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: equipment rental revenue increased `33%` to `$981M`, total revenues increased `32%` to `$1.139B`, adjusted EBITDA increased `33%` to `$448M`, and free cash flow nearly doubled to `$94M`.
- Profitability read: GAAP net loss was `$24M`, but adjusted net income remained positive at `$7M`, reflecting that the integration year still carried non-trivial cost drag even while the operating platform expanded.
- Operating read: management said the H&E integration was successfully completed in the quarter and highlighted `25%` more specialty locations, a deeper sales network, and greater density in top metro markets.
- Demand read: the company described local markets as stable and national account activity as strong, fueled by continued mega-project growth. That is exactly the kind of industrial pattern CLI 8 should capture.
- Fleet read: as of `2026-03-31`, total fleet at original equipment cost was approximately `$9.4B`, with average fleet at OEC up `36%` year over year.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: equipment rental revenue increased `24%` to `$1.039B`, total revenues increased `27%` to `$1.209B`, net income reached `$24M`, and adjusted EBITDA increased `19%` to `$519M`.
- Full-year signal: fiscal `2025` closed with record equipment rental revenue of `$3.770B`, record total revenues of `$4.376B`, adjusted EBITDA of `$1.818B`, and adjusted free cash flow of `$521M`.
- Utilization read: dollar utilization fell to `37.5%` from `40.6%`, mainly because the acquired fleet had not yet been fully optimized. That is a useful proof that scale alone does not guarantee efficiency in a rental platform.
- Integration read: management said run-rate cost synergies were achieved ahead of plan, branch-network optimization was nearing completion, and sales and territory alignment were already underway.
- Capital-structure read: interest expense doubled to `$134M` from `$67M`, showing that acquisition-driven expansion in this lane comes with real financing consequences.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Herc is not consumer-facing, but it is still a direct read on a broad societal shift away from ownership when asset costs are high and project timing is uncertain.
- The cultural pattern here is operational flexibility. Contractors, industrial operators, and infrastructure projects increasingly want access, uptime, and speed rather than the burden of owning every machine.
- The societal pattern is concentration of expertise and capital. The intermediary becomes more valuable when fleet complexity, safety requirements, technology systems, and project scale exceed what many customers want to carry internally.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- This is a capital-intensity and utilization business. Branch density, fleet mix, dollar utilization, resale discipline, depreciation, and funding costs all sit at the center of the model.
- The quarter chain also shows the tension between growth and integration. The H&E acquisition expanded scale, specialty capacity, and market density, but it also created temporary utilization drag, duplicate costs, and higher leverage.
- Fuel and transportation matter more than in a simpler distributor packet because physical fleet movement is part of the service.
- Mega projects, specialty fleet demand, and national-account concentration are central to current growth. That means this packet also functions as a real-time read on infrastructure and industrial project intensity.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Rental is channel control through capital ownership.` Herc wins because it owns the equipment fleet and timing optionality that customers do not want to finance themselves.
- `Scale is only valuable when fleet efficiency follows.` The packet shows both sides: larger fleet and branch density create opportunity, but utilization and cost absorption still need active optimization.
- `Specialty mix widens the moat.` Management repeatedly points to specialty rentals, metro density, and project execution as differentiators that turn the platform into more than a generic rental yard.
- `A middle layer can sell flexibility as the product.` Herc is not mainly selling steel and machines; it is selling availability, uptime, and project responsiveness.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `United Rentals, Inc.`: United Rentals remains the flagship, while Herc provides the strongest second proof that rental-versus-ownership is a durable frontier pattern rather than a one-company exception.
- Against `ABM Industries, Inc.` and `Cintas Corporation`: those names show recurring outsourced operations and route-based support, while Herc shows the capital-heavy version where the intermediary carries the assets, not just the service labor.
- Against `Builders FirstSource, Inc.`, `Core & Main, Inc.`, and `Ferguson Enterprises Inc.`: those names explain channel ownership in product flow, while Herc explains channel ownership in equipment access and project flexibility.

## Working interpretation

- Herc materially strengthens CLI 8 because it proves that outsourced asset access is a real middle-layer system with its own balance-sheet logic, utilization discipline, and recurring customer dependence.
- The bigger pattern is that customers increasingly outsource not just procurement and maintenance support but also the capital burden of owning mission-critical equipment fleets.
