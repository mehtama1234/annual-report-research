## What changed

- Herc gives CLI 8 a second flagship `Rental & Leasing Services` packet beside `United Rentals`, which turns rental economics from a one-name proof into a more defensible sub-lane.
- The annual and quarter chain make the business model unusually explicit: customers increasingly outsource ownership, maintenance complexity, fleet planning, and timing risk to an equipment-rental intermediary.
- AnnualReports describes Herc as a leading equipment-rental supplier with approximately `270` company-operated locations, and the first-quarter `2026` release says total fleet at original equipment cost reached approximately `$9.4B` as of `2026-03-31`.
- Fiscal `2025` was a transition year shaped by the H&E acquisition. Record equipment rental revenue reached `$3.770B`, total revenues reached `$4.376B`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$1.818B`, and free cash flow reached `$299M`, even while GAAP net income was compressed by transaction costs.
- The quarterly chain shows why the packet matters analytically: branch density, specialty mix, project exposure, fuel costs, utilization, and post-acquisition integration all directly shape middle-layer economics.

## Working interpretation

- Herc strengthens CLI 8 because it captures the asset-access layer between equipment manufacturers and the contractors, industrial operators, and infrastructure projects that increasingly prefer rented access over owned fleets.
- It also widens the frontier beyond replenishment and recurring service routes by showing how capital intensity itself becomes the product when the intermediary owns the fleet, manages utilization, and sells uptime.
