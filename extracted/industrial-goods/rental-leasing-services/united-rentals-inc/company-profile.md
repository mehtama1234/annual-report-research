## What changed

- Operating identity: United Rentals is not just a cyclical equipment lessor. The annual report and quarter materials frame it as an integrated one-stop-shop provider across general rentals, specialty products, technology, and service layers.
- Demand mix: the annual report says industrial and other non-construction rentals and commercial construction rentals each represented about `48%` of rental revenue, which makes the company more balanced than a pure construction proxy.
- Growth engine: large projects and specialty rentals are repeatedly identified as the main growth drivers. The annual letter says large projects drove most of `2025` growth, while quarter materials show specialty continuing to outgrow general rentals.
- Margin pressure has shifted: growth remains strong, but both the annual and quarterly materials show specialty mix, delivery costs, ancillary revenues, and re-rent revenue putting more pressure on rental gross margin than simple topline demand would suggest.
- Capital allocation remains central: the company repeatedly pairs growth with large rental fleet investment, strong free cash flow, share repurchases, dividends, and a managed leverage target.

## Open questions

- How durable is large-project demand if private non-residential spending cools before public or infrastructure demand catches up?
- Can specialty growth stay accretive if ancillary and re-rent mix keeps diluting specialty margins?
- Will elevated gross rental capital expenditures continue earning attractive returns if utilization or pricing normalizes?
