  - quarter 1: Q3 2026

## Annual report takeaways

- D.R. Horton is a high-value archive addition because it adds the direct homebuilder layer to the built-environment work instead of stopping at lumber, building materials, coatings, or home-improvement retail.
- AnnualReports.com already exposed the `2025` annual package as of `2026-08-09`, and the annual filing made the current corporate structure and operating footprint explicit: D.R. Horton said it was the largest homebuilding company in the United States by homes closed, operating in `126` markets across `36` states.
- The annual package also shows the economic scale clearly. For fiscal `2025`, the company reported revenue of `$34.3 billion`, income before taxes of `$4.7 billion`, net income attributable to D.R. Horton of `$3.6 billion`, diluted EPS of `$11.57`, and cash provided by operations of `$3.4 billion`.
- The operating model is broader than plain home sales. D.R. Horton closed `84,863` homes in fiscal `2025` at an average closing price of `$370,400`, but it also ran rental operations, controlled a majority-owned residential lot developer through `Forestar`, and captured mortgage and title activity through financial services.
- This is not just a cyclical housing packet. The annual filing keeps emphasizing geographic scale, land and lot control, decentralized local operators, integrated financing, and disciplined capital allocation, which makes the company useful as a read on how large builders try to defend returns when affordability is tight.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q3 `2026` net income attributable to D.R. Horton was `$904.9 million`, or diluted EPS of `$3.20`; consolidated pre-tax income was `$1.2 billion`; consolidated revenue was `$9.2 billion`; pre-tax profit margin was `13.3%`; homebuilding revenue was `$8.7 billion`; homes closed increased `4%` to `23,983`; and net sales orders totaled `23,084` homes with order value of `$8.4 billion`.
- Operating posture: management said the company delivered results at the high end of its closing guidance range while remaining focused on capital efficiency, returns, and affordability-sensitive execution.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows the homebuilder layer under pressure but not broken. Affordability constraints and cautious consumer sentiment were still central, cancellation rates rose to `20%`, and the company updated fiscal `2026` guidance down to consolidated revenue of `$32.5 billion` to `$33.0 billion` and homebuilding closings of `83,800` to `84,300`, but it still kept emphasizing first-time-buyer access, incentives, lot control, and disciplined returns.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q2 `2026` net income attributable to D.R. Horton was `$647.9 million`, or diluted EPS of `$2.24`; consolidated pre-tax income was `$867.4 million`; consolidated revenue was `$7.6 billion`; pre-tax profit margin was `11.5%`; homebuilding revenue was `$7.1 billion`; homes closed increased `1%` to `19,486`; and net sales orders increased `11%` to `24,992` homes with order value of `$9.2 billion`.
- Operating posture: management described the quarter as solid and above the high end of its guidance range while also saying affordability constraints and cautious consumer sentiment continued to impact new-home demand.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because it shows the builder response more clearly than a generic macro label does. D.R. Horton was keeping incentives elevated, reducing unsold completed homes by `35%` from the prior year, leaning on experienced local operators, and still returning roughly `$1.0 billion` to shareholders through dividends and repurchases.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q1 `2026` net income attributable to D.R. Horton was `$594.8 million`, or diluted EPS of `$2.03`; consolidated pre-tax income was `$798.1 million`; consolidated revenue was `$6.9 billion`; pre-tax profit margin was `11.6%`; homebuilding revenue was `$6.5 billion`; homes closed decreased `7%` to `17,818`; and net sales orders increased `3%` to `18,300` homes with order value of `$6.7 billion`.
- Operating posture: management said the company exceeded the high end of its closings and revenue guidance, even though affordability constraints and cautious consumer sentiment were still pressuring demand.
- Structural themes: this quarter is the cleanest direct read on how the company entered `2026`. D.R. Horton was already telling investors that incentives would remain elevated, that first-time-buyer affordability was still central, and that the main defense was disciplined local execution supported by balance-sheet strength, lot control, and integrated mortgage capture rather than a fast rebound in housing conditions.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: D.R. Horton enters through `Industrial Goods` because it is the direct residential-construction operator in the chain, not only a consumer-facing or real-estate asset owner.
- Consumer: this is one of the clearest consumer-adjacent industrial packets in the archive because homeownership affordability, mortgage rates, and first-time-buyer demand directly shape earnings.
- Cultural: the packet reads on the social mechanics of shelter. Homeownership is being defended through incentives, entry-level price points, integrated financing, and operational efficiency rather than through broad-based pricing power.
- Labor and industrial overlap: D.R. Horton matters because it makes the labor and supply side visible at the actual homebuilder level. The company repeatedly frames execution through local operators, subcontractor availability, lot strategy, construction-cycle discipline, and geographic scale.
- Business model: the key business-model signal is that D.R. Horton is more system-like than a simple home seller. The platform includes homebuilding, rental, mortgage, title, and the Forestar lot-development connection.
- Cross-company positioning: D.R. Horton is especially useful next to Builders FirstSource, West Fraser, Sherwin-Williams, Home Depot, Lowe's, and AvalonBay because it turns the housing chain into a continuous sequence from lots and lumber through builder workflow, coatings, maintenance, and downstream housing demand.

## Working interpretation

- D.R. Horton materially improves the archive because it gives the built-environment work a direct builder packet where affordability pressure, incentives, first-time-buyer access, mortgage capture, and lot control are all visible at once.
- The important evidence is not only that profits are down from the prior year. It is that management keeps describing a very specific operating response: keep community-level discipline, use incentives selectively, protect inventory turns and returns, keep lot exposure controlled, and return cash while demand remains cautious.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, D.R. Horton is a strong archive addition because it makes the shelter chain more complete between upstream materials and downstream maintenance or rental demand.
