## What changed

- D.R. Horton gives the archive a direct homebuilder rather than another upstream supplier, retailer, or housing-adjacent service business.
- The annual package showed the scale of the platform: `126` markets in `36` states, `84,863` homes closed in fiscal `2025`, revenue of `$34.3 billion`, and an operating model that extends into rental, mortgage, title, and Forestar lot development.
- The structural point is broader than closings. D.R. Horton is trying to manage housing cyclicality through local-operator discipline, controlled lot strategy, first-time-buyer focus, and integrated financing rather than through simple price maximization.
- The quarter set shows why that matters. Q1 through Q3 `2026` all frame the same problem: affordability constraints and cautious consumer sentiment remain real, incentives remain elevated, but the company still tries to defend returns, turns, and cash generation through scale and operational control.
- The packet also matters because it completes a missing link in the shelter chain. D.R. Horton sits between Builders FirstSource and downstream housing-maintenance or residential-demand names and makes the actual homebuilder economics visible.

## Open questions

- How durable is D.R. Horton's first-time-buyer engine if mortgage rates stay high and incentives remain elevated into fiscal `2027`?
- Does the integrated Forestar, mortgage, and title model become more valuable as affordability stays tight, or does it mostly help preserve current returns?
- How much more room is there to protect margins through product mix, community-level pacing, and local execution if broad demand remains cautious?
