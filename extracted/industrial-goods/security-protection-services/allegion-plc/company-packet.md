## Why this name matters

- Allegion is one of the strongest next ways to widen CLI 8 because it adds a physical-access and security-infrastructure layer that sits between simple hardware sales and the already-covered monitoring, communications, cash-logistics, and secure-capacity operators.
- It matters for CLI 8 because the company monetizes recurring building-access needs through locks, doors, exit devices, access control, workforce productivity systems, electronics, and adjacent software rather than through direct consumer demand alone.
- It also sharpens the protection-services lane by showing another form of embedded business-customer dependence. Allegion is not a route-density or subscriber-monitoring business first, but it still controls a middle layer where safety, compliance, installed-base upgrades, and non-residential project flow create durable operating dependence.

## Annual report takeaways

- AnnualReports was current to the `2025` package on Monday, `2026-08-10`, and classified Allegion as `Industrial Goods / Security & Protection Services`, so the taxonomy anchor is clean for this packet.
- The official company description is exactly why Allegion belongs in CLI 8: the company sells hardware, software, and electronic solutions across residential and commercial locks, door closer and exit devices, steel doors and frames, access control, and workforce productivity systems.
- Full-year `2025` revenues were `$4,067.3M`, up `7.8%` reported and `4.1%` organically, with adjusted `EPS` of `$8.14` and adjusted operating margin of `23.2%`.
- The annual and year-end materials also show the right segment logic. The company is split between `Americas` and `International`, and the key demand distinction is less consumer versus industrial than non-residential access infrastructure, electronics, and installation-linked workflow versus weaker residential volume.
- The full-year `2025` cash-flow read also matters: available cash flow was `$685.7M`, up `17.6%`, which reinforces that Allegion is not only a product company but a scale access-infrastructure operator with strong conversion.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent reported quarter as of 2026-08-10: Q2 2026

- Results: net revenues were `$1,151.5M`, net earnings were `$184.6M`, diluted `EPS` was `$2.15`, and adjusted `EPS` was `$2.40`.
- Growth signal: reported revenue growth was `12.7%`, and organic revenue growth was `6.9%`, led by the `Americas` region.
- Margin read: operating margin was `22.1%`, and adjusted operating margin was `24.2%`.
- Strategic read: management said the second half of `2026` still showed continued strength in `Americas` non-residential demand, while some European markets remained weaker and required cost discipline.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: net revenues were `$1,033.6M`, net earnings were `$138.1M`, diluted `EPS` was `$1.59`, and adjusted `EPS` was `$1.80`.
- Growth signal: reported revenue growth was `9.7%`, and organic revenue growth was `2.6%`, again led by the `Americas` region.
- Margin read: operating margin was `18.9%`, and adjusted operating margin was `21.2%`.
- Structural read: the quarter shows the same lane but with a less favorable margin shape than Q2. The company still had strong `Americas` non-residential and electronics growth, but volume declines and external pressures weighed on margin rate.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 FY2025

- Results: net revenues were `$1,033.2M`, net earnings were `$147.5M`, diluted `EPS` was `$1.70`, and adjusted `EPS` was `$1.94`.
- Growth signal: reported revenue growth was `9.3%`, and organic revenue growth was `3.3%`, led by the `Americas` region.
- Margin read: operating margin was `20.3%`, and adjusted operating margin was `22.4%`.
- Full-year read: full-year `2025` revenues were `$4,067.3M`, adjusted `EPS` was `$8.14`, and available cash flow was `$685.7M`.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Allegion is not a conventional consumer company, but it is exposed to broad cultural and institutional shifts: building-security hardening, commercial access modernization, digitization of entry and credential workflows, and the rise of electronics and software inside historically mechanical security systems.
- The strongest societal shift in this packet is that access control is becoming a more recurring, software-linked, and systems-oriented layer rather than a one-time lock-and-door purchase.
- The quarter chain also points to institutional behavior changing by geography and end market. `Americas` non-residential remained strong while some European markets were weaker, which makes this a useful read on where access-infrastructure spending is still holding up.
- Allegion also captures the bigger cultural move toward safer, more controlled, and more instrumented workplaces, schools, commercial buildings, and public spaces.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- This is a protection and access middle-layer operator with real exposure to non-residential construction, retrofit cycles, electronics adoption, project timing, and regional macro divergence.
- Segment mix matters a lot. The strongest current demand signal is `Americas` non-residential, while residential and some international markets are weaker, so the company is not simply riding a broad synchronized demand upswing.
- Margin discipline and productivity matter directly here. Both the Q1 and Q2 materials highlighted price and productivity net of inflation and investment, which is the same kind of operating-leverage language that shows up elsewhere in CLI 8.
- Acquisition and divestiture effects also matter to the reported growth story, so the organic versus reported distinction is important rather than cosmetic.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Security hardening is broader than monitoring and alarms.` Allegion shows that physical access infrastructure itself is part of the middle layer.
- `Installed systems create recurring dependence.` Locks, exit devices, doors, access-control electronics, and workforce systems can all become embedded operating infrastructure rather than one-time products.
- `Non-residential channel strength matters.` This packet gives CLI 8 another way to read institutional spending resilience outside healthcare and foodservice.
- `Electronics and software are widening legacy physical categories.` Allegion is useful because it shows how a historically mechanical category becomes a more recurring and more workflow-oriented business.
- `Protection can monetize through controlled access, not only through guarding or monitoring.` That makes the lane broader and more defensible.
