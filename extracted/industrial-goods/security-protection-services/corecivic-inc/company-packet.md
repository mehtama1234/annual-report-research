# CoreCivic, Inc. Company Packet

## Snapshot

CoreCivic adds a labor-heavy outsourced secure-capacity and government-partner operating model that CLI 8 still needed even after `ADT`, `Brink's`, `Motorola Solutions`, and `The GEO Group`. Unlike `ADT`, which is centered on recurring monitoring, or `Brink's`, which is centered on route density and cash logistics, or `GEO`, which leans harder into detention, monitoring, transport, and reentry under one broader supervision platform, `CoreCivic` shows a middle layer built around facility ownership or control, idle-capacity reactivation, staffing, government partnership, and later asset monetization.

## What the business is

CoreCivic describes itself as a diversified government solutions company. The business is organized around:

- `CoreCivic Safety`
- `CoreCivic Community`
- `CoreCivic Properties`

As of December 31, 2025, the company reported:

- `44` correctional and detention facilities in `CoreCivic Safety`
- `20` residential reentry centers in `CoreCivic Community`
- `5` properties in `CoreCivic Properties`
- about `68,000` Safety beds
- about `4,000` Community beds
- about `8,000` Properties beds

Management also described CoreCivic as the nation's largest owner of partnership correctional, detention, and residential reentry facilities.

That matters because the company is not a simple guard-force contractor. It operates a middle layer built from:

- facility capacity
- staffing and startup readiness
- detention and reentry operations
- transportation and secure movement dependence
- property ownership and monetization
- institutional procurement and renewal cycles
- adjacent correctional support services such as pharmacy

This is exactly why it belongs in the frontier. It shows how government customers can outsource secure capacity, operating labor, and readiness rather than directly building, owning, staffing, and maintaining every part of the system themselves.

## Fiscal 2025 annual picture

The annual `10-K` makes several things visible at once:

- `2025` net income was about `$116.5 million`
- diluted EPS was about `$1.08`
- under new `ICE` contracts, CoreCivic activated `four` previously idled facilities during `2025`
- those four facilities had a collective net carrying value of about `$175.6 million` at `2024-12-31`
- the number of remaining idle facilities fell to `five`
- idle-facility operating expense during `2025` was about `$8.5 million`

The annual report also makes clear what drives the business:

- government policy and enforcement intensity
- contract renewals and occupancy
- labor costs and staffing availability
- state and federal budget conditions
- activation of idled owned capacity
- the ability to monetize assets directly or through long-term contracts

This annual setup is useful because it frames secure capacity as a balance-sheet-backed service layer. The company can carry underused assets, absorb readiness costs, and then earn outsized returns when utilization or policy demand changes.

This is the right bridge quarter for CLI 8 because it shows the middle layer moving from dormant capacity to active monetization. The business is not just paid for steady-state custody. It is paid for being prepared when policy shifts and contract demand accelerate.

## Q1 2026

For the quarter ended March 31, 2026, CoreCivic reported:

- total revenue of about `$614.7 million`, up `25.8%`
- net income of about `$37.9 million`, up `51.0%`
- diluted EPS of about `$0.38`
- adjusted diluted EPS of about `$0.40`
- adjusted EBITDA of about `$110.1 million`

Management also highlighted:

- continued contribution from `Dilley`, `West Tennessee`, `California City`, `Diamondback`, and `Farmville`
- `ICE` revenue of about `$261.3 million` versus about `$133.2 million` in `Q1 2025`
- approval and activation progress at the `Midwest Regional Reception Center`
- the quarter-end acquisition of `Clinical Solutions Pharmacy`

The `Clinical Solutions Pharmacy` detail matters more than it first appears. Management described `CSP` as one of the largest mail-order pharmacy providers to correctional facilities, serving more than `600` correctional facilities across `28` states with nearly `300` employees. That widens the read from secure-capacity operation into healthcare-service attachment inside the same institutional system.

This quarter therefore shows a recurring pattern in the frontier: once an operator controls the relationship and the workflow, adjacent service attachment follows. CoreCivic is not only selling bed capacity; it is widening into pharmacy and correctional operating support around that capacity.

Management also emphasized:

- contribution from the `CSP` acquisition
- an `8.9%` reduction in weighted average diluted shares outstanding

The quarter becomes even more interesting because management then paired operating execution with asset monetization. Subsequent to quarter-end, CoreCivic disclosed sales of four detention facilities to a federal government partner for total gross proceeds of about `$2.2 billion`, or about `$307,000` per bed, with an aggregate gain on sale of about `$1.8 billion`.

That pairing matters for CLI 8. It shows a middle-layer company doing three things at once:

- owning and activating strategic capacity
- extracting operating earnings while utilization rises
- monetizing that capacity directly back to the institutional customer when conditions support it

This is a stronger read on channel control than a normal service contract. CoreCivic sits inside the public-security system as both operator and optional infrastructure owner.

## What CoreCivic adds to the cross-company synthesis

This packet adds several high-value signals to the broader map:

- protection services can be a government-outsourced secure-capacity and operating-readiness model, not only a household monitoring, access-control, or cash-logistics model
- idle facilities function like inventory optionality in other CLI 8 lanes, with carrying costs, activation timing, and utilization uplift all directly visible
- channel control here means owning facility readiness, labor systems, and institutional operating relationships rather than shelf space or route density alone
- service attachment can widen from custody and reentry into pharmacy and correctional support once the operator is embedded in the institutional workflow
- government customers may switch between outsourcing operations and buying facilities outright, which makes the middle layer partly a service platform and partly a strategic asset reservoir

## Bigger-picture interpretation

CoreCivic is one of the clearest examples in the frontier of how institutional stress, enforcement intensity, and capital-ownership asymmetry create private middle-layer demand. The surface label is detention or corrections. The deeper operating reality is a company monetizing readiness, labor, secure capacity, reentry infrastructure, and adjacent support services for public-sector customers that do not always want to own or fully staff the underlying system themselves.

That makes CoreCivic useful for the archive in two ways:

1. it deepens the labor-heavy protection-services stack beyond `GEO` by adding a second operator where property ownership, facility reactivation, staffing, and later facility monetization are more explicit
2. it shows how public-policy shifts can first reward operators willing to carry idle strategic assets and then reward them again when those same assets can be sold back into the institutional system

In that sense, CoreCivic is not just another controversial detention name. It is a secure-capacity, staffing, and institutional-readiness intermediary, which is exactly the kind of additional area the CLI was supposed to keep pursuing once the first protection-services packets were already complete.
