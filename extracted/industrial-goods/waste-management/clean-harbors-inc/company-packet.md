## Annual report takeaways

- Clean Harbors is the strongest current candidate to sharpen the blind-spot split between broad route-density waste networks and more specialized hazardous and industrial environmental-service infrastructure.
- AnnualReports classifies the company as `Industrial Goods / Waste Management` and, as of Monday, August 10, 2026, already exposes a `2025 Annual Report and Form 10-K`, which makes Clean Harbors a cleaner current-year archive case than Waste Management's still-lagging AnnualReports page.
- Full-year `2025` revenue reached `$6.03 billion`, net income reached `$391.0 million`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$1.17 billion`, and adjusted free cash flow reached `$509.3 million`.
- The useful read is not simply trash or disposal. Clean Harbors combines hazardous waste disposal, incineration, recycling, field response, industrial cleaning, and the Safety-Kleen used-oil and re-refining system into one environmental-services platform.
- This makes it analytically useful for the blind-spot lane because it sits closer to hazardous, emergency, remediation, disposal, and industrial-compliance intensity than the more general route-density cleanup network represented by Waste Management.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue increased `12%` to `$1.74 billion`, income from operations increased to `$268.9 million`, net income increased `34%` to `$170.5 million`, diluted EPS reached `$3.22`, and adjusted EBITDA increased `22%` to `$409.0 million`.
- Guidance: on `July 29, 2026`, Clean Harbors raised the midpoint of `2026` adjusted EBITDA guidance by `$110 million` to `$1.38 billion` and raised the midpoint of adjusted free-cash-flow guidance by `$30 million` to `$550 million`.
- Management message: management emphasized strong disposal-and-recycling demand, remediation and PFAS-related work, high incineration utilization, favorable re-refined-product pricing, and a ten-year disposal contract tied to expanding U.S. manufacturing.
- Structural themes: Q2 reads as the clearest proof that this company belongs in the blind-spot lane as a specialized environmental-service and hazardous-waste infrastructure platform rather than as a generic waste-services packet.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue reached `$1.46 billion`, net income reached `$63.2 million`, diluted EPS reached `$1.19`, and adjusted EBITDA reached `$247.9 million`.
- Guidance: on `May 6, 2026`, Clean Harbors raised the midpoint of `2026` adjusted EBITDA guidance to `$1.27 billion` and the midpoint of adjusted free-cash-flow guidance to `$520 million`.
- Management message: management emphasized improving disposal-and-recycling demand, PFAS work, weather-resilient margin performance in Environmental Services, and stronger pricing plus rising demand inside Safety-Kleen Sustainability Solutions.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows the two-engine model clearly: Environmental Services benefits from hazardous-waste and project demand while Safety-Kleen benefits from charge-for-oil strategy and re-refining spread management.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue increased `5%` to `$1.50 billion`, net income reached `$86.6 million`, diluted EPS reached `$1.62`, and adjusted EBITDA reached `$278.7 million`. Full-year `2025` revenue reached `$6.03 billion`, net income reached `$391.0 million`, and adjusted EBITDA reached `$1.17 billion`.
- Guidance: on `February 18, 2026`, Clean Harbors introduced `2026` adjusted EBITDA guidance of `$1.20 billion` to `$1.26 billion` and adjusted free-cash-flow guidance of `$470 million` to `$530 million`, before later raising both outlooks in Q1 and Q2.
- Management message: management highlighted persistent Environmental Services margin expansion, PFAS work, disposal-and-recycling demand, Kimball incinerator ramp-up, Safety-Kleen pricing discipline, and a strategic fleet expansion to support vacuum-service growth.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` closes the year with Clean Harbors looking like a hazardous disposal, industrial emergency-response, remediation, re-refining, and compliance-infrastructure platform rather than a generic industrial-service company.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Clean Harbors broadens Industrial Goods beyond equipment, backlog, and power infrastructure by adding specialized environmental infrastructure, hazardous disposal, emergency response, and industrial cleanup intensity.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand matters less than the hazardous and regulated aftermath of manufacturing, refining, remediation, and waste handling, plus the recurring service logic inside used-oil collection and re-refining.
- Cultural: the broader cultural layer is industrial safety, regulatory compliance, PFAS response, and the physical costs of environmental cleanup rather than brand, identity, or direct consumer-facing demand.
- Regulatory: PFAS, disposal and incineration regulation, wastewater handling, emissions, hazardous-material transport, and industrial cleanup rules all matter directly.
- Industrial: reshoring and expanding North American manufacturing matter because they create more waste streams, more disposal demand, and more need for specialized environmental handling capacity.
- Technology: the relevant technology layer is not consumer software but disposal network control, incineration capacity, re-refining throughput, and operating coordination across response, collection, and treatment assets.
- Capital allocation: Clean Harbors is using capital for disposal and response capacity, Safety-Kleen profitability initiatives, tuck-in acquisitions, and direct manufacturing-linked contract wins.

## Why this company matters for the blind-spot lane

- `Waste Management` already anchors the general route-density and compliance-network layer.
- `Clean Harbors` is the best current extension because it can split that broad layer into a more specialized hazardous, emergency, remediation, and disposal-intensity layer.
- That makes the useful comparison `general route density` versus `specialized environmental-service intensity`, not simply one more waste-services company.
