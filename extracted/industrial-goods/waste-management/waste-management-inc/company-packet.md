## Annual report takeaways

- Waste Management adds the environmental-infrastructure layer to the industrial set. It broadens the archive beyond machinery, electrical gear, HVAC, defense, and rail into landfill economics, route density, recycling throughput, renewable natural gas, and compliance-heavy healthcare waste.
- AnnualReports classifies WM as `Industrial Goods / Waste Management`, but as of `2026-08-08` its company page still lags at the `2024` annual package. The `2025` annual chain therefore depends on SEC-hosted annual materials rather than AnnualReports-hosted PDFs.
- Full-year `2025` revenue was `$25.204 billion`, income from operations was `$4.308 billion` reported and `$4.719 billion` adjusted, and adjusted operating EBITDA was `$7.582 billion`. Free cash flow reached `$2.937 billion`, up from `$2.317 billion` in `2024`.
- The strategic message is not just trash hauling. WM is increasingly framing the business around a recurring Collection and Disposal base, sustainability growth projects in recycling and RNG, and Healthcare Solutions integration after the Stericycle acquisition.
- This is especially useful for the broader theme work because WM connects industrial infrastructure, local-service density, healthcare compliance, and circular-economy investment into one operating model.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$6.684 billion`, income from operations was `$1.253 billion` reported and `$1.290 billion` adjusted, operating EBITDA was `$2.030 billion` reported and `$2.067 billion` adjusted, and diluted EPS was `$1.95`.
- Guidance: on `July 28, 2026`, WM kept its full-year adjusted operating EBITDA target at `$8.15 billion` to `$8.25 billion` and free-cash-flow target at `$3.75 billion` to `$3.85 billion`, while trimming revenue guidance to `$26.275 billion` to `$26.475 billion`.
- Management message: management emphasized broad margin expansion, strong cash generation, and benefits from pricing discipline, automation, sustainability projects, and Healthcare Solutions integration.
- Structural themes: Q2 reads as a high-quality recurring-infrastructure quarter with strong price realization, resilient core margins, and mix lift from sustainability assets despite softer volume.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$6.227 billion`, income from operations was `$1.113 billion` reported and `$1.118 billion` adjusted, operating EBITDA was `$1.848 billion` reported and `$1.853 billion` adjusted, and diluted EPS was `$1.79`.
- Guidance: on `April 28, 2026`, WM reaffirmed the full-year outlook it had set with Q4 `2025`.
- Management message: management emphasized disciplined pricing, cost optimization, weather resilience, and cash conversion, while highlighting that free cash flow nearly doubled year over year.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows the model clearly: pricing, SG&A discipline, and project output matter more than pure waste-volume growth.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$6.313 billion`, income from operations was `$1.155 billion` reported and `$1.204 billion` adjusted, operating EBITDA was `$1.925 billion` reported and `$1.974 billion` adjusted, and diluted EPS was `$1.83`. Full-year `2025` diluted EPS was `$6.70`.
- Guidance: on `January 28, 2026`, WM entered `2026` expecting Collection and Disposal core price of `5.4%` to `5.8%`, yield of `3.2%` to `3.6%`, volumes of `0.2%` to `0.6%`, and nearly `30%` free-cash-flow growth at the midpoint.
- Management message: management framed `2025` as an operational-excellence year with best-ever operating expenses as a percentage of revenue and strong cash-flow growth.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` closes the year with WM looking like a pricing, route-optimization, and sustainability-project story rather than a generic defensive-services story.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: WM suggests industrial strength also comes from environmental infrastructure, municipal and commercial service density, and compliance-heavy recurring demand, not only from selling equipment into capex cycles.
- Consumer: direct consumer demand matters less than local commercial activity, municipal contracts, healthcare compliance, and the ability to price through route-based services without damaging retention.
- Cultural: the broader cultural layer is the circular economy and operating realism around waste, recycling, and landfill-to-energy conversion. This is a physical sustainability story, not a branding exercise.
- Regulatory: environmental regulation, landfill permitting, healthcare-waste handling rules, fuel costs, and local pricing regimes matter directly.
- Labor: frontline retention and local operating execution matter because route density, service quality, and cost control depend on labor availability and turnover.
- Technology: the relevant technology layer is routing, automation, cost optimization, recycling-facility productivity, and data-enabled operating control. WM is a good reminder that automation gains are showing up in physical service networks too.
- Capital allocation: WM is using cash flow to fund sustainability projects, integrate Stericycle, return capital to shareholders, and preserve balance-sheet flexibility.
