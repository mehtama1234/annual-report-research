## What changed

- Revenue: full-year `2025` revenue was `$25.204 billion`, up from `$22.063 billion` in `2024`. Q4 `2025` revenue was `$6.313 billion`, Q1 `2026` revenue was `$6.227 billion`, and Q2 `2026` revenue was `$6.684 billion`. This is a recurring-service and price-to-cost spread story more than a cyclical volume story.
- Earnings and margins: full-year `2025` income from operations was `$4.308 billion` reported and `$4.719 billion` adjusted. Full-year operating EBITDA was `$7.171 billion` reported and `$7.582 billion` adjusted, with margin of `28.5%` reported and `30.1%` adjusted. Q2 `2026` adjusted operating EBITDA was `$2.067 billion` with a `30.9%` margin.
- Cash flow: free cash flow was `$2.937 billion` in `2025`, `$823 million` in Q4 `2025`, `$920 million` in Q1 `2026`, and `$1.10 billion` in Q2 `2026`. WM is converting pricing and productivity into unusually strong cash generation.
- Segment mix: WM adds a different industrial read because the core engine is Collection and Disposal, with Recycling Processing and Sales, Renewable Energy, and Healthcare Solutions widening the model into circular-economy and compliance infrastructure. This is industrial infrastructure with recurring route density, landfill economics, recycling commodity exposure, and renewable natural gas buildout.
- Stericycle / Healthcare Solutions: `2025` and `2026` are partly an integration story. WM's materials explicitly frame Stericycle as Healthcare Solutions, and the early evidence says margin improvement is being driven by SG&A synergies, local operating integration, and cross-selling potential rather than by headline top-line acceleration alone.
- Sustainability growth: management repeatedly ties growth to completed recycling and renewable-natural-gas projects. On `2026-01-28` WM said it expected six additional RNG plants and four additional recycling projects in `2026`; by `2026-07-28` it said it had completed four sustainability growth projects during the year, including three RNG facilities and one Denver recycling facility in the second quarter.
- Pricing and volume: the operating model remains pricing-led. WM entered `2026` assuming Collection and Disposal core price of `5.4%` to `5.8%`, yield of `3.2%` to `3.6%`, and volumes of `0.2%` to `0.6%`. Q1 `2026` and Q2 `2026` both showed strong price and yield but weaker reported volume due to weather, wildfire-cleanup comparisons, and intentional shedding of lower-margin residential work.
- Technology and automation: the filings repeatedly tie cost optimization and margin expansion to technology and automation. This makes WM useful for the broader research set because it shows that automation is not only a factory or software theme; it also matters inside route-based environmental infrastructure.
- Management tone: disciplined and confident. The tone is less about macro demand and more about pricing, route economics, sustainability-project throughput, integration execution, and cost control.

## Open questions

- How much of the current earnings quality is durable core-price strength versus temporary benefits from wildfire comparisons, integration synergies, and project completions?
- Can Recycling Processing, Renewable Energy, and Healthcare Solutions keep compounding fast enough to meaningfully lift the mix beyond the mature Collection and Disposal base?
