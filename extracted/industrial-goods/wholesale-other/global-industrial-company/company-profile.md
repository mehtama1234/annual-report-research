## What changed

- Global Industrial adds a cleaner `Wholesale, Other` packet than many miscellaneous-bucket names because it is still visibly a scaled industrial and `MRO` distributor rather than a product manufacturer or retailer masquerading as wholesale.
- The `2025` annual filing says the company goes to market through branded e-commerce websites and relationship marketers, sells more than one million industrial and `MRO` products, and combines third-party assortment with white-label and private-brand offerings under Global Industrial Exclusive Brands and related trademarks.
- Full-year `2025` net sales were `$1.379B`, gross profit was `$490.2M`, operating income from continuing operations was `$97.6M`, and net income from continuing operations was `$72.0M`.
- The annual report also makes the channel logic explicit: private-brand economics, supplier breadth, e-procurement workflow, and relationship-marketer coverage matter more than ownership of the underlying manufactured goods.
- The quarter chain sharpens the operating read. Q4 `2025` showed accelerating average daily sales and better margins, Q1 `2026` extended that momentum through strategic-account and Canada strength, and Q2 `2026` exposed how tariff refunds can distort surface-level gross and operating margin trends in a procurement-sensitive distribution model.

## Working interpretation

- Global Industrial strengthens CLI 8 because it shows that a modern wholesale intermediary can own customer workflow through digital catalogs, e-procurement, account relationships, and private-brand assortment without needing a dense local branch footprint like Ferguson or Pool.
- It also improves the archive's read on procurement power and channel resilience because the business sits directly in the crosscurrents of supplier concentration, tariff exposure, inventory discipline, and customer demand from institutional and industrial buyers who still need a broad replenishment channel even when the macro backdrop is uneven.
