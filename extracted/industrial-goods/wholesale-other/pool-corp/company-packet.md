## Why this name matters

- Pool is the strongest next `Wholesale, Other` add because it widens CLI 8 beyond contractor plumbing, HVAC, MRO, and waterworks into a branch-based specialty distributor whose economics are built on installed-base maintenance demand and fragmented pro-customer relationships.
- It matters for CLI 8 because it shows channel control through local availability, seasonal inventory positioning, early-buy programs, receivables financing, and service-professional embeddedness rather than through ownership of a consumer brand.
- It also gives the frontier a cleaner read on how outdoor-living, Sun Belt migration, housing-linked discretionary demand, and recurring maintenance demand all move through a pro-customer wholesale layer before they reach the end user.

## Annual report takeaways

- AnnualReports confirms that Pool belongs in `Industrial Goods / Wholesale, Other`, but the site still lagged at `2024` on `2026-08-10`, so the packet is anchored to SEC and official IR materials for the required `2025` annual-plus-quarter chain.
- The `2025` annual filing says Pool was the world's largest wholesale distributor of swimming pool supplies, equipment, and related leisure products and one of the leading distributors of irrigation and landscape maintenance products in the United States.
- As of `2025-12-31`, Pool operated `456` sales centers across North America, Europe, and Australia and served pool builders, pool service companies, retail stores, commercial pool operators, and landscape contractors through five distribution networks.
- The annual report is unusually useful for CLI 8 because it states directly that Pool adds value by buying from a large number of manufacturers and distributing products on terms and service levels generally more favorable than customers could obtain on their own.
- The recurring installed-base economics are the core of the model. In `2025`, approximately `64%` of sales came from recurring maintenance and minor repair, `22%` from remodeling and upgrades, and only `14%` from new pool construction.
- Full-year `2025` net sales were `$5.289B`, operating income was `$580.2M`, and net income was `$406.4M`. Even with softer housing conditions, the installed base still supported a large profitable middle layer.
- The annual report also surfaces the broader societal lens: long-term migration toward warmer markets, higher spending on outdoor living spaces, increased bundling of pools with landscaping and irrigation, higher demand for automation and control products, and increased focus on sustainable and energy-efficient products.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: net sales increased `2%` to `$1.823B`, gross profit increased `1%` to `$540.8M`, operating income decreased `2%` to `$267.7M`, and net income decreased `3%` to `$188.1M`.
- Demand read: management tied the quarter to healthy maintenance activity from the installed base and continued building-materials improvement even while discretionary spending stayed muted.
- Margin read: gross margin declined `30` basis points, which shows that even a branch-dense installed-base distributor is still exposed to mix pressure and operating-cost timing.
- Operating read: the quarter also included visible CEO transition costs, which is useful because the underlying distribution system still produced stable first-half operating income even during leadership turnover.
- Cash-flow read: first-half `2026` net sales reached `$2.961B`, operating income was `$350.3M`, and the receivables facility remained central to seasonal working-capital support, with `$315.5M` outstanding as of `2026-06-30`.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: net sales increased `6%` to `$1.138B`, gross profit increased `$17.5M`, operating income increased `7%` to `$82.6M`, and net income was `$53.2M`.
- Demand read: the quarter was driven by solid maintenance-product demand, strong equipment sales, and some continued improvement in discretionary categories including building materials.
- Mix read: gross margin fell `20` basis points to `29.0%` because equipment and early-buy sales were stronger, which is an important reminder that channel strength does not eliminate seasonal or mix-related margin pressure.
- Structural read: management said base-business results closely approximated consolidated results, which reinforces that the branch network itself rather than acquisitions was carrying the quarter.
- Working-capital read: as of `2026-03-31`, Pool had `$300.0M` outstanding under the receivables facility, underscoring how seasonal inventory and receivables financing are part of the business model rather than incidental treasury details.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: quarterly net sales decreased `1%` to `$982.2M`, operating income decreased `14%` to `$52.0M`, and net income decreased to `$31.6M`.
- Full-year signal: despite a softer quarter, full-year `2025` net sales still reached `$5.289B`, operating income reached `$580.2M`, and net income reached `$406.4M`.
- Mix read: gross profit increased even though sales fell, which shows the resilience of maintenance-heavy product categories and the ongoing importance of margin discipline inside a seasonal distributor.
- Demand read: management framed the year around resilient maintenance demand and back-half growth in building materials rather than a broad rebound in new pool construction.
- Capital read: year-end `2025` net cash provided by operating activities was `$365.9M`, but product inventories rose sharply and receivables-facility borrowings were still meaningful, which makes working-capital timing central to how this channel operates.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Pool sits close enough to household demand that it captures real consumer and cultural shifts, but it does so through professional intermediaries rather than direct retail branding.
- The strongest cultural pattern is the continued prioritization of outdoor living. Pools, remodels, landscape upgrades, automation, lighting, and related backyard improvements remain part of household identity and status spending in warmer and higher-income markets.
- The stronger stabilizer, though, is not aspiration but upkeep. A large installed base of pools creates non-discretionary chemical, equipment, repair, and replacement demand that persists even when new construction and larger discretionary projects slow.
- The annual report also ties the lane to broader societal shifts such as migration toward the southern United States, higher home-equity-backed renovation behavior, and increasing demand for sustainable and energy-efficient products.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- This is a seasonal working-capital business. Inventory timing, customer early buys, receivables financing, and branch execution all materially shape reported cash flow and margin.
- The distributor is exposed to both recurring and discretionary demand at once. Maintenance and minor repair are resilient, but remodeling, upgrades, and new pool construction remain sensitive to housing conditions, financing costs, and consumer confidence.
- Product mix matters a great deal. Chemicals, equipment, building materials, and outdoor-living categories do not all carry the same margin structure, and that affects quarter-to-quarter profitability even when sales growth holds up.
- Supplier breadth is an advantage, but it also means Pool has to keep managing pricing, availability, and procurement relationships across a highly fragmented manufacturer base.
- The branch network and digital ecosystem matter because this is still a local-service and local-availability business. A customer whose jobsite needs chemicals, replacement pumps, tile, stone, or irrigation products on time is buying uptime and workflow reliability, not just cartons.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Installed base beats new demand.` Pool shows how a maintenance-heavy installed base can create recurring revenue even when new-construction demand is softer.
- `Channel ownership in fragmented markets is a moat.` Pool wins because it sits between many suppliers and many professional buyers and simplifies price, availability, and procurement friction.
- `Seasonal working capital is part of the product.` Inventory builds, early-buy activity, and receivables-facility usage are not side details; they are how the distributor keeps the channel stocked and responsive.
- `Local branch density turns lifestyle demand into B2B infrastructure.` Outdoor living may look consumer-driven from the surface, but the actual monetization runs through service pros, remodelers, and trade relationships using a dense distribution network.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Ferguson Enterprises Inc.` and `Watsco, Inc.`: those names show contractor-facing branch distribution in plumbing, HVAC, and waterworks, while Pool shows the same branch-density logic in a more seasonal outdoor-living and installed-base maintenance channel.
- Against `Builders FirstSource, Inc.`: Builders FirstSource is tied more directly to new housing starts and value-added building-product flow, while Pool shows a healthier mix of recurring installed-base maintenance and remodel-driven demand.
- Against `Fastenal Company` and `Applied Industrial Technologies, Inc.`: those names explain industrial procurement and uptime-support workflow, while Pool explains a more lifestyle- and climate-linked branch distributor whose moat still rests on procurement leverage and local execution.

## Working interpretation

- Pool materially strengthens CLI 8 because it shows that a middle layer can own procurement power and channel access in a market that still looks consumer-facing from the outside.
- The bigger pattern is that installed-base maintenance, pro-customer embeddedness, seasonal working-capital discipline, and local branch execution can produce a durable wholesale moat even when the end market carries obvious housing and discretionary risk.
