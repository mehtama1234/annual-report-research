## What changed

- Pool adds a different kind of wholesale-infrastructure packet than contractor-heavy waterworks or HVAC names because its moat rests on installed-base service demand, seasonal inventory timing, and deep pro-customer relationships in a fragmented outdoor-products channel.
- The `2025` annual report says Pool operated `456` sales centers at year-end across North America, Europe, and Australia and sold to pool builders, pool service companies, retail stores, commercial pool operators, and landscape contractors through five distribution networks.
- The annual filing makes the installed-base economics unusually explicit. In `2025`, approximately `64%` of sales came from recurring maintenance and minor repair, `22%` from remodeling, renovation, and upgrades, and only `14%` from new pool construction.
- Fiscal `2025` net sales were `$5.289B`, operating income was `$580.2M`, and net income was `$406.4M`. The business therefore remained large and profitable even while new construction stayed weaker than the installed-base service flow.
- The quarter chain shows why Pool matters analytically: maintenance chemicals, replacement equipment, building materials, early-buy programs, branch productivity, customer relationships, and receivables-facility financing all sit inside one branch-dense intermediary rather than a simple consumer discretionary brand.

## Working interpretation

- Pool strengthens CLI 8 because it captures a middle layer where fragmented suppliers, seasonal inventory, local branch execution, and recurring service-professional demand matter more than the branded end product.
- It also broadens the wholesale-and-distribution lane into outdoor-living and installed-base upkeep, which helps the frontier explain how maintenance-heavy demand can stay resilient even when new-build demand and discretionary spending are softer.
