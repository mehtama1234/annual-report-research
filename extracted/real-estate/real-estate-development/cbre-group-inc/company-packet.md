## What CBRE adds to the broader real-estate read

- A services-and-intermediation layer that sits between tenants, landlords, developers, lenders, and infrastructure operators rather than owning the assets outright
- Direct evidence that the real-estate cycle in 2025 and 2026 is being shaped by brokerage recovery, mortgage origination rebound, project-management demand, and outsourced facilities operations
- A strong bridge between classic commercial-property activity and AI-linked critical infrastructure, especially data-center services, power, cooling, and telecom support
- A more culturally useful office and workplace read than the REITs alone because CBRE sees leasing, occupier strategy, flexible workplace usage, building operations, and project demand in real time

## Structural themes

- Real estate is no longer only a landlord story: CBRE shows that services, brokerage, financing, project execution, and building operations are large and growing economic layers around the property base
- Critical infrastructure is becoming a sector-wide engine: management repeatedly tied growth to data centers, power, telecom, and related infrastructure work, not just to conventional office or retail recovery
- AI demand is shaping physical-world activity: the archive now has another explicit confirmation that AI is translating into land, power, cooling, construction, and maintenance demand
- The sector taxonomy is getting weaker: AnnualReports places CBRE in `Real Estate Development`, but the actual business is a mixed platform spanning brokerage, facilities, project management, investment management, and infrastructure services
