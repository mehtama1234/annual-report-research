## What changed

- Revenue: CBRE produced more than `$40.6 billion` of 2025 revenue, then grew revenue another `19%` in Q1 2026 and `16%` in Q2 2026, showing that this is not just a property owner read but a broad transaction-plus-services platform.
- Margin: Core EPS moved from `$2.73` in Q4 2025 to `$1.61` in Q1 2026 and `$1.56` in Q2 2026 on seasonal quarterly bases, while full-year 2025 Core EPS reached `$6.38` and 2026 guidance rose to `$7.80` to `$7.90` by July 29, 2026.
- Guidance: management introduced 2026 Core EPS guidance of `$7.30` to `$7.60` in February 2026, raised it to `$7.60` to `$7.80` in April 2026, and raised it again to `$7.80` to `$7.90` in July 2026.
- Capital allocation: CBRE deployed about `$2.7 billion` of capital in 2025, including the Pearce Services and Industrious transactions plus roughly `$1.0 billion` of repurchases, while still ending 2025 at only `1.24x` net leverage.
- Segment mix: the company now spans Advisory, Building Operations & Experience, Project Management, and Real Estate Investments, which makes it a better read on real-estate intermediation and infrastructure services than on simple rent collection.
- Operational issues: U.K. fire-safety remediation reserves and pension-related charges distorted GAAP profit, but the operating story remained strong underneath, especially in leasing, property sales, mortgage origination, facilities management, and infrastructure-linked project work.
- Consumer / cultural signals: CBRE is an indirect read on office utilization, capital-markets normalization, corporate workplace strategy, and the buildout of AI-linked data-center and power infrastructure rather than on household consumer demand.
- Management tone: management is assertive about the strategic pivot toward resilient service lines and critical infrastructure, with repeated emphasis on AI-driven demand, data-center services, and disciplined capital deployment.
