## What JLL adds to the broader real-estate read

- A second large real-estate-services intermediary that validates CBRE’s broader pattern rather than leaving it as a single-company story
- A sharper read on office leasing recovery, occupier decision-making, workplace outsourcing, and gateway-market demand for high-quality office product
- Additional confirmation that capital-markets activity, debt advisory, and investment sales were improving in 2025 and 2026, not just service revenues tied to recurring contracts
- A more explicit framing around data, AI, and research advantage as competitive tools inside brokerage, advisory, and client execution

## Structural themes

- Real estate services are becoming a real archive layer: JLL and CBRE together show that the sector cannot be understood only through REIT rent rolls and development pipelines
- Office is not dead, but it is selective: JLL’s 2026 materials point to better leasing momentum, especially in the U.S. and gateway markets, with quality and scarcity of top-tier space mattering
- Capital-markets normalization is broadening: investment sales, debt and equity advisory, and transaction volumes are recovering enough to materially lift earnings
- Data centers and AI demand matter even here: beyond owners and developers, JLL is seeing data-center-linked leasing and project demand, which broadens the AI physical-infrastructure thesis across the service stack
