## What changed

- Revenue: JLL produced `$26.1 billion` of 2025 revenue, then grew revenue another `9%` in local currency in Q1 2026 and `10%` in Q2 2026, confirming that transaction and services activity stayed healthy into 2026.
- Margin: diluted EPS moved from a record `$8.34` in Q4 2025 to `$3.33` in Q1 2026 and a record `$4.59` in Q2 2026, while adjusted diluted EPS reached `$18.80` for full-year 2025 and `$8.67` for the first half of 2026.
- Guidance: by July 30, 2026 management was explicitly raising its full-year adjusted EPS target range, citing `34%` year-over-year growth at the midpoint.
- Capital allocation: JLL generated nearly `$1.0 billion` of free cash flow in 2025 and accelerated repurchases in 2026, with `$410 million` bought back in the first half of 2026.
- Segment mix: JLL’s revenue base is dominated by Real Estate Management Services, with additional scale in Leasing Advisory, Capital Markets Services, and Investment Management.
- Operational issues: the strongest operating signals are in office, industrial, and data-center leasing plus debt and equity advisory; this makes JLL a sharper office-recovery and capital-markets barometer than the REIT owners alone.
- Consumer / cultural signals: JLL adds a corporate workplace and occupier-behavior read, including office return, demand for high-quality space, outsourcing of workplace operations, and the importance of trusted advisory relationships in uncertain markets.
- Management tone: management is confident and execution-focused, repeatedly emphasizing market-share gains, margin expansion, a `One JLL` approach, and an explicit data-and-AI advantage under the Accelerate 2030 framing.

## Open questions

- How much of JLL’s strength is cyclical recovery in office leasing and capital markets versus structurally durable gains in workplace management and project execution?
- Does JLL’s stronger office-leasing signal imply a broader normalization trend, or is it concentrated in high-quality assets and gateway markets?
