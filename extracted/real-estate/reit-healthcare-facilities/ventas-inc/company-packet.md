## Annual report takeaways

- Ventas gives CLI 6 the healthcare-property and longevity-economy case, where value creation depends on senior-housing demand, supply scarcity, operating-platform quality, and capital access rather than simple rent collection alone.
- The `4Q25` and full-year `2025` results chain showed attributable net income per share of about `$0.54`, Nareit FFO per share of about `$3.50`, and normalized FFO per share of about `$3.48`.
- Management framed `2025` around more than `15%` SHOP same-store cash NOI growth and about `$2.5B` of accretive investment activity, which makes the annual read less like a static healthcare REIT and more like a scaled allocator into an aging-population bottleneck.
- The deeper interpretation is that Ventas monetizes a societal shift, not just a property bucket: more seniors are aging into assisted and supported living needs while new supply remains historically tight.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: `2Q26`

- Results: Ventas reported attributable net income per share of about `$0.14` and normalized FFO per share of about `$0.97`, up about `9%` year over year.
- Operating detail: total company NOI grew about `17%` year over year, total same-store cash NOI grew about `10%`, and SHOP same-store cash NOI increased about `16%`.
- Capital and growth detail: year-to-date the company had closed about `$3.4B` of investments focused on senior housing, raised its `2026` investment volume expectations to about `$4.5B`, and reported about `$4.9B` of liquidity with net debt to further adjusted EBITDA of about `4.7x`.
- Strategic read: `2Q26` is the clearest proof in this lane that demographic demand plus low new supply can turn healthcare real estate into a multiyear growth system rather than a defensive yield vehicle only.

### Quarter minus 1: `1Q26`

- Results: Ventas reported attributable net income per share of about `$0.11` and normalized FFO per share of about `$0.94`, up about `9%` versus the prior year.
- Operating detail: total company NOI grew about `14%`, total same-store cash NOI grew about `9%`, and SHOP same-store cash NOI grew more than `15%`.
- Capital detail: the company had already closed about `$1.7B` of senior-housing investments and had about `$2.4B` of equity capital either settled or available through forward structures.
- Structural read: `1Q26` showed that this was already becoming a scale-and-supply-constraint story before the larger second-quarter acceleration.

### Quarter minus 2: `4Q25`

- Results: Ventas reported attributable net income per share of about `$0.15`, Nareit FFO per share of about `$0.91`, and normalized FFO per share of about `$0.89` for the quarter.
- Full-year readthrough: full-year `2025` attributable net income per share was about `$0.54`, Nareit FFO per share was about `$3.50`, and normalized FFO per share was about `$3.48`.
- Structural read: `4Q25` anchors the name as more than a healthcare-income wrapper; it is an operating and capital-allocation platform built to serve the aging-population demand wave.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and societal pattern: Ventas is one of the strongest direct proofs in the repo that aging is not just a demographic statistic. It is a demand engine for housing, care support, and senior-living infrastructure.
- Industrial pressure: new supply remains historically low, so operating execution and investment pacing matter more than speculative development volume.
- Cultural shift: older households and families are more willing to pay for safety, support, and socialization in supported environments, which makes senior housing look more like recurring necessity than optional real estate.
- Bigger-picture read: Ventas gives CLI 6 a real-property counterpart to insurers and allocators. It monetizes trust and duration, but through care-adjacent occupancy and demographic scarcity rather than through float, fees, or spreads.
