# Welltower Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Welltower Inc.
- Sector: Real Estate
- Industry: REIT - Healthcare Facilities
- Ticker: `WELL`
- Fiscal year-end: December 31
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: `2Q26`
  - quarter 2: `1Q26`
  - quarter 3: `4Q25`

## Annual report takeaways

- Welltower gives CLI 6 the property-and-care-delivery hybrid model: a healthcare-facility REIT whose economics increasingly depend on senior-housing operating leverage, occupancy, operator quality, capital access, and acquisition discipline rather than only on passive rent collection.
- The `2025` annual and late-`2025` quarter chain showed a company leaning hard into senior housing as an operating system, not just a property bucket, with heavy investment activity and a stronger growth profile than most plain-vanilla property vehicles.
- This matters for the batch because Welltower sits between pure balance-sheet finance and healthcare operations: it is exposed to property values and debt markets, but its earnings momentum also depends on care demand, resident affordability, labor, and operating execution.
- Compared with PNC and MetLife, Welltower shows a more visible demographic tailwind but also a much more tangible exposure to labor, occupancy, and real-estate capital costs.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: 2Q26

- Results: net income attributable to common stockholders was `$0.61` per diluted share and normalized FFO was `$1.60` per diluted share.
- Operating detail: total portfolio same-store NOI growth was `15.5%`, seniors housing operating portfolio same-store NOI growth was `20.5%`, and seniors housing operating same-store revenue growth was `9.2%`.
- Strategic read: `2Q26` reinforced the core Welltower thesis that aging demand, better occupancy, and sharp operator data discipline can produce much stronger REIT economics than generic real-estate labels imply.

### Quarter minus 1: 1Q26

- Results: net income attributable to common stockholders was `$1.02` per diluted share and normalized FFO was `$1.47` per diluted share.
- Operating detail: total portfolio same-store NOI growth was `16.4%`, seniors housing operating NOI growth was `22.1%`, and seniors housing operating same-store revenue growth was `9.5%`.
- Structural read: `1Q26` showed the same pattern as `2Q26`, which matters because it suggests the current momentum is not only a one-quarter bounce.

### Quarter minus 2: 4Q25

- Results: the late-`2025` chain pointed to full-year FFO per share growth of about `22%`, year-end net debt to adjusted EBITDA of about `3.0x`, and roughly `$11B` of full-year net investment activity.
- Operating detail: seniors housing operating same-store NOI grew `20.4%`, organic revenue growth was `9.6%`, and occupancy growth was about `400` basis points.
- Structural read: `4Q25` is where the batch's demographic signal becomes hard to ignore. Senior housing demand is not only recurring; it is becoming a high-participation operating platform with meaningful pricing and occupancy leverage.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and societal pattern: aging and the need for care-adjacent housing are proving more durable than many discretionary property narratives.
- Industrial pressure: labor, operator quality, financing costs, and acquisition discipline remain decisive.
- Cultural shift: more of later-life healthcare and housing consumption is moving into managed, branded, and data-optimized facility systems.
- Bigger-picture read: Welltower is one of the strongest examples in CLI 6 that a property vehicle can become an operating moat when the tenant / operator / resident loop gets tight enough.
