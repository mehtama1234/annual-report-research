## What changed

- Demographic demand is showing up as operating momentum, not just as a long-term story. The `4Q25`, `1Q26`, and `2Q26` chain all point to stronger occupancy, revenue, and NOI in seniors housing.
- The company is closer to operations than a plain-vanilla property landlord. The packet repeatedly shows that operator quality, data discipline, and care-adjacent execution matter alongside rent collection and capital access.
- Capital intensity is still real: Welltower still carries property and financing exposure, but the aging-demand tailwind and acquisition engine make the economics look stronger than a generic real-estate label suggests.
- Later-life consumption is becoming more managed and systematized. The company benefits when housing, care, and resident support move into branded, data-optimized facility networks rather than remaining fragmented.
- CLI 6 role: Welltower is one of the clearest proofs that a property vehicle can become infrastructure when it sits directly on a durable demographic bottleneck and pairs that with scale, operator selection, and investment capacity.

## Open questions

- How much of the current growth profile depends on unusually favorable supply conditions versus a durable structural edge in operator and asset selection?
- If labor or affordability pressure worsens, does the operating leverage remain strong enough to protect the current growth premium?
