# Apple Hospitality REIT, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Apple Hospitality REIT, Inc.
- Sector: `Real Estate`
- AnnualReports.com sector label: `Financial`
- Industry: `REIT - Hotel/Motel`
- Ticker: `APLE`
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: `2025`
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: `Q2 2026`
  - quarter 2: `Q1 2026`
  - quarter 3: `Q4 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- Apple Hospitality is the second lodging-owner REIT now added to the recreation lane, but it broadens the comparison differently than Host. Instead of concentrating on luxury and upper-upscale destination assets, Apple is a diversified, rooms-focused U.S. hotel owner tied primarily to Marriott and Hilton brands.
- As of `2025-12-31`, the company owned `217` hotels with `29,583` guest rooms across `37` states and the District of Columbia. The year-end `10-K` and quarter chain show a hotel base concentrated in Hilton Garden Inn, Courtyard, Hampton, Residence Inn, and Homewood Suites, with substantially all hotels operating under Marriott or Hilton brands.
- The `2025` annual and year-end release frame the company around broad portfolio diversification, outsourced operating management, capital discipline, distributions, and selective dispositions rather than around pipeline growth or loyalty-system expansion.
- Full-year `2025` results were mixed rather than booming. Net income was `$175.4M`, operating income was `$257.8M`, Adjusted EBITDAre was `$443.6M`, Comparable Hotels Adjusted Hotel EBITDA was `$474.2M`, and Comparable Hotels RevPAR was `$117.95`, down `1.6%` versus `2024`.
- The year-end management commentary matters because it shows a more burdened and cyclical version of hotel demand than the asset-light operators presented. Policy uncertainty, weaker government travel, and mix pressure weighed on results even though leisure demand remained supportive.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: Q2 `2026` net income was `$67.1M`, operating income was `$88.2M`, and Adjusted EBITDAre was `$144.5M`.
- Demand and growth: Comparable Hotels ADR was `$169.90`, up `3.5%`; Comparable Hotels occupancy was `80.1%`, up `1.6%`; and Comparable Hotels RevPAR was `$136.17`, up `5.3%`.
- Management message: management said broad-based business and leisure travel both improved, weekday occupancy strengthened, and July `2026` preliminary Comparable Hotels RevPAR was up more than `5.5%`. The company also said FIFA World Cup `2026` host-market demand helped but was not the only driver.
- Structural themes: this quarter reinforces Apple as a broad U.S. rooms-demand proxy. The economics improved when weekday business demand normalized, but the company still monetized that demand through asset ownership, operating-margin execution, and capital spending rather than through fee and loyalty control.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: Q1 `2026` net income was `$27.7M`, operating income was `$48.0M`, and Adjusted EBITDAre was `$100.6M`.
- Demand and growth: Comparable Hotels ADR was `$157.35`, up `0.1%`; Comparable Hotels occupancy was `72.8%`, up `2.1%`; and Comparable Hotels RevPAR was `$114.61`, up `2.2%`.
- Management message: management described the quarter as a stronger-than-expected start to `2026`, emphasized resilient demand for broadly diversified rooms-focused hotels, and cited improving occupancy and booking trends even after a difficult comparison against wildfire-recovery business and inauguration demand in early `2025`.
- Structural themes: Q1 is important because it marks the turn from a softer `2025` demand mix into a healthier `2026` environment. Apple still reads as a more stable, standardized hotel-owner model than Host, but it is still clearly dependent on occupancy and weekday mix recovery.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025 / full-year 2025

- Results: Q4 `2025` net income was `$29.6M`, operating income was `$49.6M`, and Adjusted EBITDAre was `$93.1M`; full-year `2025` net income was `$175.4M`, operating income was `$257.8M`, and Adjusted EBITDAre was `$443.6M`.
- Demand and growth: Q4 Comparable Hotels ADR was `$151.89`, down `0.9%`; Q4 Comparable Hotels occupancy was `70.4%`, down `1.7%`; and Q4 Comparable Hotels RevPAR was `$106.90`, down `2.6%`. Full-year Comparable Hotels ADR was `$159.09`, down `0.1%`; full-year occupancy was `74.1%`, down `1.6%`; and full-year Comparable Hotels RevPAR was `$117.95`, down `1.6%`.
- Management message: management cited policy uncertainty, a pullback in government travel, and disruption to midweek occupancy, while also saying leisure travel remained a source of support and that the company remained positioned for growth as the operating environment improved.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` makes Apple a useful contrast because it shows the lodging-owner layer absorbing a softer real-world travel mix without the cushion of franchise, management-fee, or loyalty monetization.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Apple broadens the recreation lane into a second lodging-owner REIT with a more standardized and diversified rooms mix than Host.
- Consumer: the company is a direct read on whether broad U.S. hotel demand, not just luxury destination demand, remains strong enough to support higher occupancy and ADR.
- Cultural: Apple supports the same experience-economy conclusion as the branded hotel names and Host, but with a more middle-market operating lens. Travel remains socially durable, yet not every owner captures that demand equally cleanly.
- Operating model: Apple is a good reminder that many hospitality owners do not control the guest relationship directly. The brands, loyalty systems, and much of the commercial power sit with Marriott and Hilton, while Apple owns the real estate and depends on third-party managers for execution.
- Industrial pressure: government and business-travel mix, operating-cost control, property-level margin management, dispositions, and capital expenditures all matter more here than in the asset-light operator packets.
- Capital allocation: Apple repeatedly frames value creation through disciplined capital allocation, monthly cash distributions, selective dispositions, and targeted development funding rather than through rapid system expansion.
- Cross-company pattern: Apple strengthens the lane's split between participation demand and profit capture. The same consumer willingness to travel can support the brand layer, the manager layer, and the property owner, but the cleanest economics still tend to sit above the hard-asset layer.

## Working interpretation

- Apple materially improves the recreation / participation lane because it adds a second lodging-owner packet that is not just a duplicate of Host. It captures a more diversified, rooms-focused, select-service and compact full-service hotel real-estate model.
- The demand evidence improved across the target chain. Full-year `2025` was soft, but Q1 `2026` and Q2 `2026` both showed renewed Comparable Hotels RevPAR growth, with Q2 accelerating to `5.3%`.
- The larger interpretive payoff is structural. Apple shows a hotel owner that benefits from Marriott and Hilton brand ecosystems without controlling them. That makes it a cleaner proof that participation demand and relationship control are not the same thing.
- Combined with Host and the asset-light hotel operators already in the archive, Apple makes the hospitality lane more realistic. It shows that broad travel demand can recover and still leave the owner layer carrying more of the physical, margin, and capital-allocation burden than the layers controlling the brand, loyalty program, or operating relationship.
