## What changed

- Apple Hospitality gives the recreation lane a second lodging-owner packet after Host, but with a different shape. This is a rooms-focused, diversified U.S. select-service and compact full-service hotel REIT rather than a luxury and upper-upscale trophy-asset owner.
- As of `2025-12-31`, the company owned `217` hotels with `29,583` guest rooms across `37` states and the District of Columbia. As of `2026-06-30`, it owned `216` hotels with `29,459` guest rooms after a held-for-sale property was sold in April `2026`.
- The portfolio matters strategically because substantially all hotels operate under Marriott or Hilton brands while management is outsourced across multiple third-party managers. That means Apple is exposed to branded travel demand without controlling the brand or loyalty layer itself.
- The year-end `2025` read was softer than the branded hotel operators and softer than the later `2026` quarter chain. Management cited policy uncertainty and a pullback in government travel as headwinds, with full-year Comparable Hotels RevPAR down `1.6%` to approximately `$118`.
- The `2026` quarter chain then improved materially. Q1 `2026` Comparable Hotels RevPAR rose `2.2%`, and Q2 `2026` Comparable Hotels RevPAR rose `5.3%`, with weekday occupancy improving and management describing demand as broad-based across business and leisure travel.
- This makes Apple useful as a middle-case lodging-owner contrast. It does not have Host's luxury concentration or trophy-asset intensity, but it still bears the physical-asset, margin, and capital-allocation burden that the asset-light brand operators avoid.

## Open questions

- How durable is the rebound in business and weekday demand after the government- and policy-linked softness visible in `2025`?
- Does Apple's more standardized, rooms-focused portfolio produce steadier risk-adjusted lodging-owner economics than higher-end hotel REITs, or does it simply cap upside while retaining the same structural burden?
- Over time, who captures the cleaner economics from this same demand system: the Marriott and Hilton brand layer, the third-party manager layer, or the REIT owner funding the real estate underneath them?
