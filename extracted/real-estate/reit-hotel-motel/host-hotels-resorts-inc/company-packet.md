Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Sector: `Real Estate`

- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: `2025`
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: `Q2 2026`
  - quarter 2: `Q1 2026`
  - quarter 3: `Q4 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- Host is the missing hospitality-owner packet the recreation lane needed because it captures the capital-heavy property-owner side of travel demand rather than the brand, loyalty, and fee side already represented by Hilton, Marriott, and Hyatt.
- AnnualReports.com was current here as of `2026-08-10`, showing `2025 Annual Report and Form 10K`, which makes Host a cleaner archive-confirmation case than several recent consumer and hospitality names in the repo.
- The `2025` annual materials and year-end release frame Host as the nation's largest lodging REIT and one of the largest owners of luxury and upper-upscale hotels. The strategic emphasis is not only on travel demand, but on capital recycling, renovation programs, resilient balance-sheet management, and disciplined shareholder returns.
- Full-year `2025` results were strong. Comparable hotel Total RevPAR increased `4.2%`, comparable hotel RevPAR increased `3.8%`, net income was `$776M`, Adjusted EBITDAre was `$1,757M`, diluted EPS was `$1.10`, and Adjusted FFO per diluted share was `$2.07`.
- Host also matters because it shows how much physical ownership still matters in the experience economy. In `2025`, the company sold `$237M` of hotel real estate during the year, returned large amounts of capital to stockholders, and advanced transformational capital programs with Hyatt and Marriott.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenues were `$1.64B`, net income was `$241M`, EBITDAre was `$519M`, and Adjusted EBITDAre was `$525M`.
- Demand and growth: comparable hotel Total RevPAR was `$417.58`, up `5.9%`; comparable hotel RevPAR was `$251.53`, up `7.0%`; and year-to-date comparable hotel RevPAR was up `5.7%`.
- Management message: Host framed the quarter around strong leisure and group demand, FIFA World Cup-related travel tailwinds, continued rate strength, and enough confidence to raise full-year `2026` comparable hotel Total RevPAR and RevPAR growth guidance ranges to `4.75%` to `5.25%`.
- Structural themes: this quarter reinforces Host as a direct read on affluent travel demand and out-of-room spending, but in a capital-heavy format where wage inflation, insurance outcomes, and portfolio dispositions still shape earnings conversion.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenues were `$1.645B`, net income was `$501M`, EBITDAre was `$537M`, and Adjusted EBITDAre was `$543M`.
- Demand and growth: comparable hotel Total RevPAR was `$418.20`, up `4.6%`; comparable hotel RevPAR was `$244.11`, up `4.4%`; and comparable hotel EBITDA was `$505M`, up `7.0%`.
- Management message: management emphasized that affluent consumers were still prioritizing travel, group demand remained steady, and the company used that strength alongside major asset sales to raise full-year guidance.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows how different the lodging-owner model is from the asset-light operators. Host monetized strong travel demand, but the quarter was also heavily shaped by asset-sale gains, a special dividend, and real-estate recycling.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025 / full-year 2025

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenues were `$1.603B`, net income was `$137M`, and Adjusted EBITDAre was `$428M`; full-year `2025` revenues were `$6.114B`, net income was `$776M`, and Adjusted EBITDAre was `$1,757M`.
- Demand and growth: Q4 comparable hotel Total RevPAR was `$380.71`, up `5.4%`; Q4 comparable hotel RevPAR was `$227.14`, up `4.6%`; and full-year comparable hotel Total RevPAR was `$382.83`, up `4.2%`.
- Management message: Host emphasized capital allocation, portfolio quality, travel demand resilience, real-estate sales, and progress on the Hyatt and Marriott transformational programs.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` makes Host a strong archive anchor because it ties travel demand, food-and-beverage and ancillary spending, asset sales, renovation strategy, balance-sheet discipline, and REIT shareholder distribution into one lodging-owner model.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Host broadens the archive beyond hotel operators into the property-owner economics underneath branded hospitality.
- Consumer: the company is a direct read on whether affluent consumers continue to prioritize travel and experiences, and whether that demand is strong enough to support both room-rate growth and out-of-room spending.
- Cultural: Host is useful because it shows that the experience economy does not only reward brands and loyalty systems. It also still rewards iconic physical destinations, but with a much heavier capital and operating burden.
- Capital heavy versus capital light: this is the key analytical reason to own the packet. Host provides the real-estate-backed lodging-owner contrast against Hilton, Marriott, and Hyatt, which mostly monetize the same demand through brand and fee economics.
- Industrial pressure: wage inflation, insurance claims, property damage, renovation disruption, capital expenditures, and real-estate recycling all matter much more here than in the asset-light lodging packets.
- Capital allocation: Host repeatedly frames value creation through asset sales, special dividends, repurchases, debt management, and targeted reinvestment rather than through pure system growth.
- Cross-company pattern: Host suggests that value inside travel keeps splitting between the brand controller and the asset owner. Demand can be strong for both, but the cleaner economics usually sit with the layer carrying less hard-asset intensity.

## Working interpretation

- Host materially improves the recreation / participation lane because it adds the missing lodging-owner layer beneath the branded travel systems already in the archive.
- The demand evidence is constructive through the entire target chain: comparable hotel Total RevPAR and RevPAR were positive in full-year `2025`, Q1 `2026`, and Q2 `2026`, and management repeatedly said affluent consumers were still prioritizing travel and experiences.
- The bigger interpretive payoff is structural. Host shows that the same travel demand can produce very different economics depending on which layer owns the control point. Brands and loyalty systems often capture cleaner economics, while the property owner absorbs more capex, insurance, balance-sheet, and renovation risk even when the underlying travel market looks healthy.
