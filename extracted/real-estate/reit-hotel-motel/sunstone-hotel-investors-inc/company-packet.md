## Annual report takeaways

- Sunstone is the missing third lodging-owner contrast in the recreation lane because it is neither Host's scale-heavy luxury REIT nor Apple's broad rooms-focused branded-hotel owner. It is a smaller, more concentrated, higher-end hotel owner with materially more event-driven upside and asset-specific volatility.
- AnnualReports.com was not current here as of `2026-08-10`, still showing `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`. That makes Sunstone a useful reminder that AnnualReports is a taxonomy and archive-check source, not the authoritative annual anchor when it lags.
- The `2025` annual materials frame the business around portfolio quality, capital returns, renovation-led value creation, and real-estate recycling rather than around system expansion. As of `2025-12-31`, Sunstone owned `14` hotels totaling `6,999` rooms across `7` states.
- Full-year `2025` operating performance was solid. Net income was `$24.6M`, or `$0.12` per diluted share; excluding the loss on sale of Hilton New Orleans St. Charles, net income would have been `$33.3M`. Total Portfolio RevPAR increased `3.8%` to `$225.12`, ADR was `$317.07`, occupancy was `71.0%`, Adjusted EBITDAre rose `3.0%` to `$236.6M`, and Adjusted FFO per diluted share rose `7.5%` to `$0.86`.
- The bigger strategic point is that Sunstone keeps pairing operating commentary with capital-allocation commentary. Management highlighted recycling out of a lower-growth hotel, the debut of Andaz Miami Beach, completion of capital investments, repurchases, and more than `$170M` returned through buybacks and dividends in `2025`.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: Q2 `2026` net income attributable to common stockholders was `$26.0M`, or `$0.14` per diluted share, and Adjusted EBITDAre was `$76.7M`.
- Demand and growth: RevPAR rose `9.3%` to `$263.61`; ADR was `$339.71`; occupancy was `77.6%`; Total RevPAR rose `7.7%` to `$434.00`; and RevPAR excluding Andaz Miami Beach still rose `4.3%`.
- Management message: even after the large Andaz swing factor, quarter commentary still showed healthy underlying demand. Management also kept the framing tied to capital realization rather than pure demand celebration.
- Structural themes: the quarter reinforces that Sunstone is a high-beta owner model. Growth can look strong, but interpretation requires separating normalized hotel demand from renovation ramp, property-specific recovery, and held-for-sale activity such as Hyatt Regency San Francisco.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: Q1 `2026` net income attributable to common stockholders was `$16.0M`, or `$0.08` per diluted share, and Adjusted EBITDAre was `$67.7M`.
- Demand and growth: RevPAR rose `14.6%` to `$255.04`; ADR was `$344.19`; occupancy was `74.1%`; Total RevPAR rose `13.4%` to `$411.28`; and RevPAR excluding Andaz Miami Beach still rose `5.7%`.
- Management message: management said results came in ahead of expectations despite weather-related headwinds, emphasized strong resort performance and a solid first quarter from Andaz Miami Beach, raised full-year outlook, and again discussed valuation discount and repurchases.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows why Sunstone matters in the archive. This is not a passive real-estate owner just waiting for room demand to show up. It is trying to convert consumer travel demand into shareholder value through repositioning, timing, and buybacks.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025 / full-year 2025

- Results: Q4 `2025` net income was `$7.2M`, Adjusted EBITDAre was `$56.6M`, and Adjusted FFO per diluted share was `$0.20`; full-year `2025` net income was `$24.6M` and Adjusted EBITDAre was `$236.6M`.
- Demand and growth: Q4 Total Portfolio RevPAR rose `9.6%` to `$220.12`; ADR was `$319.01`; occupancy was `69.0%`; and full-year Total Portfolio RevPAR rose `3.8%` to `$225.12`.
- Management message: management highlighted stronger performance at Andaz Miami Beach and Wailea Beach Resort, along with portfolio recycling, capital investments, and large shareholder returns.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` makes Sunstone an especially good owner-layer case study because operating performance, redevelopment execution, asset-sale timing, and shareholder-yield decisions are all intertwined.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Sunstone deepens the recreation lane's hospitality-owner coverage by adding a concentrated upper-upscale and luxury lodging owner rather than another fee-based hotel operator.
- Consumer: the company is a direct read on whether affluent travelers keep spending on resort and high-end urban lodging, but also on whether that demand is strong enough to offset the friction of renovation, repositioning, and property concentration.
- Cultural: Sunstone supports the same broader experience-economy conclusion visible across hotels, but with a sharper point. Consumers may still prioritize travel, destinations, and occasion spending, yet monetizing that demand through ownership of a small high-end portfolio is structurally less smooth than monetizing it through loyalty and fee control.
- Participation and occasion spend: properties such as Wailea Beach Resort, Andaz Miami Beach, Montage Healdsburg, and Hilton San Diego Bayfront tie the company to destination, event, and occasion-driven spending patterns rather than purely functional lodging demand.
- Industrial pressure: renovation execution, property ramp timing, insurance and operating-cost burden, asset-sale timing, and concentration risk matter more here than in both the asset-light brand packets and Apple's broader portfolio model.
- Capital allocation: Sunstone repeatedly frames value through capital recycling, discounted share repurchases, selective dispositions, and embedded real-estate value realization.
- Cross-company pattern: Sunstone strengthens the lane's larger pattern that strong participation demand does not automatically imply clean economics for every layer. The farther down the system the archive moves toward hard-asset ownership, the more demand resilience has to fight through capex, timing risk, and portfolio-specific execution.

## Working interpretation

- Sunstone materially improves the recreation / participation lane because it adds a third owner model rather than a redundant hotel packet. The lane now captures scale-heavy lodging ownership, diversified rooms-focused lodging ownership, and concentrated redevelopment-and-recycling lodging ownership.
- The demand evidence is constructive across the target chain. Full-year `2025`, Q1 `2026`, and Q2 `2026` all showed positive RevPAR growth, and the underlying resort and occasion-travel read appears healthy even after stripping out part of the Andaz effect.
- The larger interpretive payoff is structural. Sunstone shows how the experience economy can stay strong while the owner layer still depends on difficult execution choices around renovation timing, asset sales, buybacks, and valuation realization.
- Combined with Host, Apple, Hilton, Marriott, and Hyatt, Sunstone makes the hospitality lane more realistic. It shows that travel demand, participation demand, and occasion spending can be durable across society while the cleanest economics still tend to sit with the layer that controls relationships and fees instead of the layer that owns the most capital-intensive assets.
