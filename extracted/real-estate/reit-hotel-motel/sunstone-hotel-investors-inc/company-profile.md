## What changed

- Sunstone gives the recreation lane a third lodging-owner REIT, but its analytical use is different from both Host and Apple. This is a smaller, more concentrated, higher-end portfolio where renovation, repositioning, and sale timing visibly move the numbers.
- As of `2025-12-31`, the company owned `14` hotels with `6,999` rooms across `7` states. As of `2026-06-30`, it still owned `14` hotels, but one of them, Hyatt Regency San Francisco, was classified as held for sale.
- The portfolio description and quarter commentary make clear that Sunstone is not just harvesting stabilized room demand. It is actively trying to unlock value through redevelopment, property repositioning, capital recycling, and buybacks.
- The strongest example is `Andaz Miami Beach`, which is repeatedly carved out of quarter comparisons because the renovation and ramp materially affect growth optics. That is a different economic setup from a diversified rooms portfolio and a different burden from an asset-light brand system.
- Q4 `2025`, Q1 `2026`, and Q2 `2026` all showed strong reported RevPAR growth, but management also kept emphasizing valuation discount, repurchase activity, and embedded asset value. That means the equity story depends not only on demand resilience, but also on whether management can realize value through timing, redevelopment, and dispositions.
- This makes Sunstone especially useful for the archive's larger purpose. It shows that experience demand can be real and healthy while profit realization still depends on hard-asset execution, portfolio concentration, and capital-allocation judgment.

## Open questions

- How much of Sunstone's upside is durable operating momentum versus temporary benefit from renovation ramp, easy comparisons, and asset-specific recovery at properties like Andaz Miami Beach?
- Does a concentrated high-end lodging-owner model create better long-term value than a broader diversified rooms model, or does it mainly add volatility and execution risk?
- If travel demand remains constructive, who captures the cleaner economics: the brand, the manager, or the owner trying to crystallize value through redevelopment and asset sales?
