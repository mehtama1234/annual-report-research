## What changed

- Revenue: the key operating story is not top-line retail-like revenue growth but embedded rent growth, leasing strength, occupancy stability, and fee-bearing strategic-capital activity across the platform.
- Margin: Core FFO per diluted share moved from `$1.44` in Q4 2025 to `$1.50` in Q1 2026 and `$1.63` in Q2 2026, while GAAP net earnings per diluted share moved from `$1.49` to `$1.05` to `$1.13` across the same set.
- Guidance: Prologis entered 2026 confident in earnings growth, then raised 2026 guidance in Q1 and raised it again in Q2.
- Capital allocation: the company is actively rotating capital through development, acquisitions at discounts to replacement cost, dispositions, and contributions into Strategic Capital vehicles.
- Segment mix: Prologis is still fundamentally a logistics real-estate platform, but the 2025 annual and 2026 quarterlies show management deliberately widening the narrative toward digital infrastructure, power procurement, and data-center adjacency.
- Operational issues: occupancy remained in the mid-`95%` range while leasing volumes accelerated sharply, which suggests the company is benefiting from tight logistics supply in high-barrier markets rather than merely defending legacy occupancy.
- Consumer / cultural signals: this is an indirect consumer and commerce read because Prologis sits behind business-to-business distribution and retail or online fulfillment networks rather than directly selling to consumers.
- Management tone: management is notably confident and expansionary, stressing broadening customer demand, long-duration growth opportunities, capital access, and platform scale rather than balance-sheet repair.

## Open questions

- How much of the current strength comes from cyclical logistics recovery versus a more durable infrastructure shift toward data centers and power-linked development?
- Can Prologis sustain record leasing and high occupancy if broader industrial activity softens while development exposure rises?
