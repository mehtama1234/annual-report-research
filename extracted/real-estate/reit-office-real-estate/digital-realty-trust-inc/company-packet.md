# Digital Realty Trust Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Digital Realty Trust Inc.
- Sector: Real Estate
- Industry: REIT - Office Real Estate
- Ticker: DLR
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Why this name matters

- Digital Realty is the right second datacenter-property flagship because it tests whether the Equinix pattern is broader than one company and shows a different ownership model built more around hyperscale leasing, powered land, and private-capital-enabled expansion.
- It also sharpens the archive’s connectivity stack by adding another ownership layer above Vertiv’s power-and-cooling systems and beside Equinix’s interconnection-dense campus model.

## Annual report takeaways

- Digital Realty is one of the clearest taxonomy-breaker names in the real-estate archive. AnnualReports still classifies it as `REIT - Office Real Estate`, but the annual materials clearly describe a global datacenter, colocation, and connectivity platform.
- The `2025` annual evidence shows a business whose economics are now tied to hyperscale demand, cloud and enterprise data workloads, powered capacity, and private-capital structures rather than to legacy office-property logic.
- Full-year `2025` and Q4 metrics were strong. Q4 `2025` revenue was about `$1.6B`, FFO per share was `$1.89`, and Core FFO per share was `$1.86`.
- The key annual operating signals are bookings and backlog. Q4 `2025` total bookings reached `$400M` annualized GAAP rent at `100%` share, and backlog at Digital Realty’s share reached `$817M`.
- Digital Realty matters because it shows a second way AI and cloud demand are remaking property economics: not only through interconnection density as at Equinix, but also through hyperscale leasing, private capital, and large powered-capacity development.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: revenue reached about `$1.9B`, FFO per share reached `$2.73`, and Core FFO per share excluding net promote reached `$2.13`.
- Demand and operating indicators: Q2 bookings reached `$307M` annualized GAAP rent at `100%` share, July hyperscale leases added another `$410M`, and backlog at Digital Realty’s share rose to `$1.4B`.
- Management message: management raised `2026` Core FFO guidance again and emphasized record backlog, continued small-scale plus interconnection strength, and major hyperscale demand.
- Structural themes: this quarter shows that Digital Realty’s demand engine is not a normal occupancy story. Backlog, leasing velocity, and powered-capacity demand are now the cleaner forward indicators.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: revenue stayed around `$1.6B`, FFO per share reached `$1.99`, and Core FFO per share reached `$2.04`.
- Demand and operating indicators: total bookings reached `$707M` annualized GAAP rent at `100%` share, backlog at Digital Realty’s share climbed to `$1.0B`, and management cited the largest hyperscale lease in company history.
- Management message: management raised `2026` guidance and emphasized record `0-1 MW` plus interconnection leasing.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the clearest proof that Digital Realty is not only a hyperscale landlord. Smaller-scale leasing and connectivity-rich demand are important parts of the model as well.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was about `$1.6B`, FFO per share was `$1.89`, and Core FFO per share was `$1.86`.
- Demand and operating indicators: total bookings reached `$400M` annualized GAAP rent at `100%` share, and backlog reached `$817M` at Digital Realty’s share.
- Management message: management entered `2026` with a strong outlook tied to top-line growth, record small-scale plus interconnection leasing, and clear backlog visibility.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` is where Digital Realty becomes a strong archive bridge name. It demonstrates that one part of real estate is now effectively a digital-capacity and powered-land development business.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Digital Realty is not consumer-facing, but it sits below everyday habits that now feel essential: cloud usage, AI-driven services, enterprise digital operations, streaming, and low-latency application delivery.
- The broader social pattern is that more of the economy depends on large, power-intensive datacenter campuses even when end users do not see them directly.
- There is also a capital-markets and governance angle. Digital Realty shows that some real-estate companies are no longer mainly rent collectors; they are capital-allocation machines building and financing digital capacity in response to infrastructure demand.
- Culturally, the company helps explain why old property labels are increasingly misleading. A datacenter REIT with record hyperscale leasing and AI-driven backlog is not behaving like traditional office real estate in any economically useful sense.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Power access and development timing matter directly. The growth story depends on bringing powered capacity online fast enough to satisfy bookings and hyperscale demand.
- Private-capital structures help the model but also complicate quarter reading. Promote income, joint ventures, and selective ownership changes can make results less straightforward than a plain rental model.
- Backlog is strong, but it still has to be converted into delivered capacity and recurring revenue. A datacenter owner can have visible demand yet still be constrained by construction, energization, and market timing.
- Customer mix matters. Hyperscale leases are powerful, but the company also needs a balanced stream of interconnection-rich and smaller-scale demand to avoid becoming too one-dimensionally dependent on a few giant customers.
- Valuation and expectations risk are real in this lane. Once the market treats a property company as digital infrastructure, it has to keep proving that bookings, powered-land monetization, and FFO growth justify that re-rating.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Some real estate now behaves like digital capacity infrastructure.` Digital Realty is one of the strongest examples in the archive of property economics being driven by AI, cloud, and network demand rather than by classic office metrics.
- `Backlog can matter more than occupancy in AI-linked property lanes.` Digital Realty’s backlog growth from `$817M` to `$1.4B` is a clearer signal of demand than traditional property heuristics alone.
- `Private capital has become part of the datacenter operating model.` This name shows that capital structure and joint-venture design can be strategic growth levers, not merely financing details.
- `Hyperscale demand reshapes ownership economics.` Digital Realty helps show how large tenant demand waves alter development cadence, powered-land strategy, and the balance between recurring rent and development-led upside.
- `Taxonomy lag is analytically useful.` The gap between “office REIT” labeling and actual operating reality is itself evidence that old market categories are failing to describe modern infrastructure businesses cleanly.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Equinix, Inc.`: Equinix is the denser interconnection and ecosystem-control read, while Digital Realty is the clearer hyperscale, powered-land, and private-capital-enabled datacenter-property read.
- Against `Vertiv Holdings Co`: Vertiv supplies critical power and thermal systems, while Digital Realty owns the campuses and capacity where those systems are deployed.
- Against `Crown Castle Inc.`: Crown Castle would represent communications-infrastructure ownership across towers, small cells, and metro fiber, while Digital Realty represents datacenter-capacity ownership for compute and storage demand.
