## Annual report takeaways

- Realty Income gives CLI 6 the scaled net-lease REIT model, where reliability comes from occupancy, lease duration, tenant quality, and funding access rather than from destination retail concentration.
- The `2025` year-end read emphasized AFFO per share of about `$4.28`, occupancy of about `98.9%`, and large investment volume supported by low-friction rent collection across a broad portfolio.
- The annual interpretation is that Realty Income remains one of the lane's most utility-like property vehicles because its economics are less about merchandising or experiential traffic and more about long-duration contractual cash flow.
- Compared with Simon, Realty Income is the less glamorous but structurally steadier retail-property case: lower excitement, lower participation intensity, and a more bond-like rent stream.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: `2Q26`

- Results: Realty Income reported total revenue of about `$1.548B`, net income available to common stockholders of about `$344.0M`, or `$0.37` per share, and AFFO per share of about `$1.09`.
- Operating detail: portfolio occupancy was about `98.8%` at June 30, 2026, and management raised `2026` AFFO guidance.
- Strategic read: `2Q26` shows the value of the model in this lane: cash flow remains steady enough that the company can keep positioning itself as a monthly-income utility even while expanding into new structures such as private capital and data-center-related ventures.

### Quarter minus 1: `1Q26`

- Results: Realty Income reported total revenue of about `$1.549B`, net income available to common stockholders of about `$311.8M`, or `$0.33` per share, and AFFO per share of about `$1.13`.
- Operating detail: occupancy remained about `98.9%`, and the company kept leaning into disciplined investment activity rather than chasing growth for its own sake.
- Structural read: `1Q26` reinforces that the core moat here is duration and diversification, not consumer buzz.

### Quarter minus 2: `4Q25`

- Results: Realty Income reported AFFO per share of about `$1.08` for the quarter and about `$4.28` for full-year `2025`.
- Full-year readthrough: management framed the period around dependable monthly dividend support, high occupancy, and continued investment capacity rather than around aggressive rent spikes.
- Structural read: `4Q25` anchors Realty Income as the lane's purest net-lease rent collector, where financing cost and tenant durability matter more than participation-rich consumer demand.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and societal pattern: Realty Income monetizes the persistence of everyday physical commerce and service needs, but in a less discretionary and less identity-driven way than Simon.
- Industrial pressure: funding cost, acquisition spreads, tenant credit quality, and lease structure are the decisive operating variables.
- Cultural shift: investors still reward property vehicles that package long-duration rent streams into dependable income, especially when the underlying asset mix is diversified and boring in the right way.
- Bigger-picture read: Realty Income is the cleanest property-side rebuttal to the broker thesis, but it still remains more rate-sensitive and financing-dependent than the best advisory franchises.
