## What changed

- Cash-flow steadiness: Realty Income kept producing high occupancy and narrow quarter-to-quarter variation in AFFO, which is exactly what makes it read like a utility-style property vehicle.
- Exposure mix: unlike Simon, the core story is not destination traffic or premium asset excitement. It is broad tenant diversification, lease duration, rent collection consistency, and funding discipline.
- Rate and funding sensitivity: the business still depends heavily on acquisition spreads, debt cost, and investment pacing, so the steadiness comes with financing dependence rather than with a pure operating moat.
- Strategy evolution: the packet shows Realty Income leaning into new structures such as private capital and data-center-related ventures while still framing itself around dependable monthly income and contractual rent streams.
- CLI 6 role: Realty Income is the cleaner contrast to both Simon and Annaly because it sits between physical property ownership and bond-like cash-flow packaging without relying on extreme leverage or advisory economics.

## Open questions

- How much of Realty Income's apparent steadiness depends on continued access to attractive funding and acquisition spreads rather than on the underlying tenant base alone?
- Do newer ventures like private capital and data-center exposure strengthen the moat, or do they mainly compensate for tighter traditional net-lease economics?
