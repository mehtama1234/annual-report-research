## Annual report takeaways

- Simon brings the retail-property cash-flow leg into CLI 6, extending the lane from financing and advisory platforms into physical rent collection tied to tenant health, traffic, and destination quality.
- The `2025` annual and year-end release framed Simon around high occupancy, resilient Real Estate FFO, rising base rent, and strong liquidity rather than around raw consumer-volume excitement.
- The annual interpretation is that Simon still monetizes physical participation and retail presence best when its assets remain the highest-quality destinations in a pressured but still functioning mall and outlet ecosystem.
- Compared with mortgage REIT financing, Simon is far more operating-asset based; compared with brokers, it is more capital intensive and tenant-cycle exposed.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent reported quarter as of 2026-08-10: 1Q26

- Timing note: as of Monday, August 10, 2026, Simon had not yet reported `2Q26`; its earnings call was scheduled for later that same day, so the latest reported quarter in scope was still `1Q26`.
- Results: Simon reported Real Estate FFO of about `$1.28B`, or `$3.45` per diluted share, and domestic occupancy of about `95.8%`.
- Operating detail: the company increased full-year guidance and kept leaning on premium destination quality, leasing strength, and retailer demand rather than on simple cyclical hope.
- Strategic read: `1Q26` shows that high-quality retail real estate can still compound if the landlord owns the best physical nodes, but the model remains more tenant- and traffic-sensitive than brokerage.

### Quarter minus 1: 4Q25

- Results: Simon reported net income attributable to common stockholders of about `$1.4B`, or `$3.28` per diluted share, while domestic occupancy reached about `96.5%`.
- Operating detail: fourth-quarter Real Estate FFO per diluted share was about `$3.68`, and the company closed the year with strong liquidity and continued rent discipline.
- Structural read: `4Q25` anchors Simon as a landlord whose strongest moat is asset quality and tenant demand concentration, not light-balance-sheet flexibility.

### Quarter minus 2: 3Q25

- Results: Simon reported net income attributable to common stockholders of about `$606.2M`, or `$1.86` per diluted share, FFO of about `$1.228B`, or `$3.25` per diluted share, and domestic occupancy of about `96.4%`.
- Operating detail: domestic property NOI increased about `5.1%`, portfolio NOI increased about `5.2%`, and the quarterly dividend was raised to `$2.20` per share.
- Structural read: `3Q25` shows the lane's retail-property version of trust monetization: premium centers keep attracting tenants and shoppers, but the cash flow still depends on physical occupancy and retailer health.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and societal pattern: Simon monetizes destination retail, experiential traffic, and premium-location relevance rather than just generalized retail square footage.
- Industrial pressure: occupancy, leasing spreads, tenant credit quality, refinancing conditions, and consumer participation all matter more here than in the broker models.
- Cultural shift: strong physical retail still works when it behaves like curated experience and brand concentration, not commodity mall inventory.
- Bigger-picture read: Simon is the batch's best test of whether trust-and-duration economics can still hold in a property vehicle when the real moat is destination quality instead of advisory workflow.
