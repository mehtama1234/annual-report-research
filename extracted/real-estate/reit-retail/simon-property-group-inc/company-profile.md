## What changed

- Operating stability: the quarter chain shows that Simon kept producing high occupancy, strong leasing, and resilient FFO across `3Q25`, `4Q25`, and `1Q26`.
- Asset-quality advantage: the key differentiator is not low capital intensity. It is owning premium retail and outlet destinations that still attract tenants and shoppers even in a pressured physical-retail environment.
- Exposure profile: unlike brokers or asset managers, Simon remains directly exposed to tenant health, leasing spreads, traffic, and physical occupancy, which makes the cash flows sturdier than weak mall exposure but still more cyclical than advisory or fee platforms.
- Participation angle: the packet supports the idea that curated destination retail still works when it behaves like experience, brand concentration, and premium footfall rather than generic square footage.
- CLI 6 role: Simon is useful because it gives the lane a capital-heavy property owner whose returns depend on durable physical node quality rather than on funding carry or workflow control.

## Open questions

- How durable is Simon's premium-asset advantage if retailer rationalization or traffic softness broadens beyond lower-quality malls?
- Does the property moat stay strong enough to offset the heavier capital and tenant-cycle burden compared with lighter intermediary models elsewhere in CLI 6?
