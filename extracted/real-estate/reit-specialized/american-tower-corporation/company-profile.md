## What changed

- American Tower adds a clean landlord-style infrastructure-rent case to CLI 5. The `2025` annual anchor and Q4 `2025` through Q2 `2026` chain show a company monetizing communications-site tenancy and increasingly data-center-linked demand rather than only selling equipment, materials, or software control.
- The archive status is cleaner than some other recent CLI 5 additions. AnnualReports already reflected the `2025` annual package on `2026-08-10`.
- The operating narrative broadened. Management repeatedly emphasized towers plus highly interconnected data centers, not towers alone.
- The quarter chain showed steady scale rather than explosive step changes. Total revenue was `$2.738B` in Q4 `2025`, `$2.738B` in Q1 `2026`, and `$2.749B` in Q2 `2026`, while AFFO moved from `$1.230B` to `$1.324B` to `$1.264B`.
- The quality signal comes more from predictability and strategic positioning than from software-like acceleration. Management consistently pointed to robust leasing demand, strong mobile data growth, cloud adoption, and AI-driven workload expansion.
- The burden signal remains important. Net leverage stayed at `4.9x` across Q4 `2025`, Q1 `2026`, and Q2 `2026`, which reinforces that this is still a balance-sheet-heavy capture model even when the digital-infrastructure demand read is favorable.

## Open questions

- Does American Tower’s landlord-style rent model deserve to be read as a durable AI-era toll, or will the best economics still sit more clearly with data-center-heavy landlords such as Equinix and Digital Realty?
- How much incremental upside can CoreSite and data-center interconnection add relative to the more mature tower-rent base?
- Can American Tower keep improving earnings quality and reducing risk while maintaining enough capital flexibility for future digital-infrastructure demand waves?
