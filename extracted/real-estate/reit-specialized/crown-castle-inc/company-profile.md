## What changed

- Crown Castle adds a third landlord-style infrastructure case to CLI 5, but unlike American Tower or SBA the central story is asset simplification rather than just steady rent collection.
- The archive status is lagged on AnnualReports, making this another case where company IR and SEC have to control when the taxonomy source falls behind.
- The annual still reflected a mixed portfolio including approximately `40,000` towers and approximately `90,000` route miles of fiber, while the 2026 quarter chain showed management moving decisively away from that bundled model.
- The quarter chain changed the interpretation of the company more than the raw numbers alone. Q1 `2026` was the pre-close transition quarter, and Q2 `2026` became the post-close proof of the new pure-play U.S. tower direction.
- The core operating message across the period was not just demand durability. It was that tower economics looked more attractive once separated from fiber and small-cell complexity.
- The burden signal remains central. DISH terminations, Sprint cancellations, restructuring, and heavy capital-allocation moves show that even a durable infrastructure toll may need major portfolio surgery before the economics become visibly cleaner.

## Open questions

- Does Crown Castle's retreat to a simpler U.S. tower model produce better long-run economics than the more diversified landlord approaches at American Tower or the more tower-pure but narrower SBA model?
- How much of Crown Castle's prospective improvement comes from genuine business-quality change versus one-time cleanup from asset sales, debt repayment, and restructuring?
- Once the simplification work is complete, will the market reward the clearer tower-rent profile with a higher multiple, or will the category still be viewed as more burdened than the strongest software-control and technical chokepoint layers?
