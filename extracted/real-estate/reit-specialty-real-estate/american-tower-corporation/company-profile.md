## What changed

- Revenue: full-year `2025` revenue reached `$10.645B`; quarterly revenue was `$2.738B` in Q1 `2026` and `$2.749B` in Q2 `2026`.
- Property economics: total property revenue was `$10.305B` for full-year `2025`, `$2.670B` in Q1 `2026`, and `$2.688B` in Q2 `2026`, keeping towers and data centers as the dominant recurring engine.
- Cash earnings: AFFO attributable to AMT common stockholders was `$5.042B` in `2025`, `$1.324B` in Q1 `2026`, and `$1.264B` in Q2 `2026`.
- Demand quality: management repeatedly tied demand to rising mobile-data consumption, continued `5G` deployment, cloud adoption, and AI-related workloads; Q2 also highlighted record CoreSite leasing activity.
- Pressure points: organic tenant billings growth slowed to `1.7%` in both Q1 and Q2 `2026` because DISH churn moved fully into the reported churn base, with churn running at about `5%` of tenant billings in the 2026 10-Qs.
- Risk concentration: the `2025` 10-K disclosed DISH default under the Strategic Collocation Agreement, while the `2026` 10-Qs also documented the AT&T Mexico arbitration and related reserves.
- Segment mix: American Tower is a tower REIT first, but it also carries a U.S. data-center platform through CoreSite and a tower-services layer that supports customer deployments.
- Capital intensity: the model still benefits from high incremental margins on colocations, but it remains exposed to customer concentration, FX, legal disputes, and the timing of carrier and hyperscaler network expansion.

## Working interpretation

- American Tower is one of the clearest archive names for understanding how bandwidth demand becomes recurring infrastructure rent. The core engine is not consumer software itself, but the physical right to place radios, equipment, and cloud-adjacent traffic at scale.
- The more important shift is that AMT is no longer just a tower landlord. It increasingly reads as a hybrid ownership platform spanning mobile-network densification, interconnection-rich data centers, and the capital structure needed to finance both.

## Open questions

- How much of the gap between healthy revenue growth and muted organic tenant billings growth persists once the DISH churn reset annualizes?
- Can CoreSite and broader data-center leasing become large enough to materially change the market’s default view of AMT as only a tower REIT?
