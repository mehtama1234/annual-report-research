## What changed

- Business mix: the biggest structural change is not quarter-to-quarter growth but the portfolio reset. Crown Castle signed the strategic fiber sale in March `2025` and completed it on May 1, `2026`, receiving roughly `$8.4B` of net cash proceeds and becoming a pure-play tower company.
- Revenue base: full-year `2025` continuing-operations site rental revenue was `$4.049B`; quarterly continuing-operations site rental revenue was `$1.019B` in Q4 `2025`, `$961M` in Q1 `2026`, and `$967M` in Q2 `2026`.
- Cash earnings: full-year `2025` Adjusted EBITDA was `$2.863B` and AFFO was `$1.904B`; quarterly AFFO was `$489M` in Q4 `2025`, `$446M` in Q1 `2026`, and `$488M` in Q2 `2026`.
- Organic demand: tower demand remained positive, but reported growth was dragged by Sprint cancellations and DISH terminations. Organic Contribution to Site Rental Billings as adjusted for those items was `3.4%` in Q4 `2025`, `3.1%` in Q1 `2026`, and `3.9%` in Q2 `2026`.
- Operating simplification: management used the transition period to cut tower and corporate workforce by about `20%` and target roughly `$65M` of annualized operating cost savings.
- Portfolio profile: after the sale, Crown Castle owned and leased about `40,000` towers and related structures in the U.S., with about `2.0` average tenants per tower and roughly `$26B` of remaining contracted tenant receivables as of June 30, `2026`.
- Capital allocation: the post-sale model emphasizes dividends, share repurchases, debt reduction, and land-interest purchases rather than trying to support both tower and fiber/small-cell capital needs in one structure.

## Working interpretation

- Crown Castle is one of the clearest archive examples of a company choosing strategic simplification over adjacency sprawl. By exiting small cells and fiber, it is betting that the market will eventually reward a cleaner domestic tower cash-flow profile more than a harder-to-explain bundled communications platform.
- The bigger lesson is that not all connectivity infrastructure scales equally well inside one public company. Towers retain high-margin, contract-heavy recurring economics, while small cells and fiber can demand more bespoke execution, more capex, and more investor patience.

## Open questions

- Does Crown Castle’s tower-only reset create a cleaner long-term multiple story, or does it surrender part of the metro-edge and densification stack just as bandwidth demand keeps rising?
- How much of the `2026` recovery path depends on legal and billing resolution around DISH rather than on underlying tower demand alone?
