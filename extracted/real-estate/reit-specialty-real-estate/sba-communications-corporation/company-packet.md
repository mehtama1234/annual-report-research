## Why this name matters

- SBA is the cleanest pure-play tower-leasing name in this archive because it turns mobile-data growth and carrier densification into recurring site rent without also asking the reader to underwrite datacenters, metro fiber, or a broader communications bundle.
- It is the right comparison against `American Tower Corporation` and `Crown Castle Inc.` because all three monetize the same dependence on wireless connectivity, but they do so through different portfolio choices and different degrees of complexity.
- SBA also matters because it makes the physical layer behind software and AI usage legible in a simpler way: bandwidth demand becomes recurring tower rent before it becomes app, cloud, or platform revenue.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual evidence shows a company centered on a `46,328`-site portfolio, with `17,394` sites in the United States and its territories and `28,934` internationally. Site leasing contributed `97.9%` of total segment operating profit in `2025`, which makes the economics unusually clean.
- The `2025` 10-K also shows why tower ownership remains a compelling recurring-cash-flow model. SBA ended the year with an average of `1.8` tenants per site, and management emphasized that existing sites still have room for additional equipment and tenants at relatively low incremental cost.
- The annual materials frame the secular demand case explicitly. SBA cited estimated global mobile network traffic of about `197` exabytes per month by the end of `2025`, projected to rise to `482` exabytes per month by `2031`, alongside ongoing spectrum deployment, `5G`, and rising need for reliable interconnected wireless solutions.
- The company’s strategic language is broader than its current revenue mix. The `2025` 10-K notes exploration around edge data centers, fiber aggregation and regeneration huts, satellite ground stations, and private networks, but those are still adjacency options rather than the central earnings engine.
- The annual evidence also surfaces the core constraints. SBA depends on a relatively small number of major wireless customers, with domestic site-leasing revenue derived primarily from `T-Mobile`, `AT&T Wireless`, and `Verizon Wireless`, while management also called out the impact of prior and future carrier consolidation and EchoStar actions on churn.
- Land control is another key quality marker. As of December 31, `2025`, about `71%` of tower structures were on land SBA owned or controlled for more than `20` years, with an average remaining life of `35` years including renewal options under SBA’s control.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: site leasing revenue increased `5.1%` to `$663.9M`, Adjusted EBITDA increased `1.8%` to `$483.8M`, AFFO was `$324.4M`, and AFFO per share was `$3.05`. Net income attributable to SBA was `$198.8M`, or `$1.87` per share.
- Demand and operating indicators: domestic site-leasing revenue fell `3.7%` to `$452.5M`, while international site-leasing revenue rose `30.5%` to `$211.4M`. SBA built `109` towers in the quarter and ended June 30, `2026` with `46,390` sites.
- Management message: management described carrier activity as steady, with customers both upgrading sites and expanding through new colocations, while also tying the medium-term demand case to Auction `115`, future `6G`, and expanding Central America build activity.
- Structural themes: Q2 shows the appeal of the simple tower story, but it also shows where the pressure moves. Domestic growth remained softer while international expansion, FX, and Millicom-linked build activity did more of the visible work.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: site leasing revenue increased `6.5%` to `$656.1M`, Adjusted EBITDA increased `4.0%` to `$475.4M`, AFFO was `$321.7M`, and AFFO per share was `$3.03`. Net income attributable to SBA was `$184.8M`, or `$1.74` per share.
- Demand and operating indicators: management said domestic leasing backlogs increased during the quarter. SBA built `80` towers and ended March 31, `2026` with `46,358` sites.
- Management message: management raised full-year `2026` outlook across key metrics and explicitly said the quarter absorbed the full removal of EchoStar revenue from reported results as of the beginning of the year.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the cleanest quarter for understanding the reset. The company still produced stable site-leasing revenue and AFFO, but it had to prove that the business could absorb customer-specific churn and still sustain dividend growth and portfolio expansion.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: site leasing revenue increased `3.1%` to `$666.2M`, Adjusted EBITDA was `$486.0M`, AFFO was `$340.4M`, and AFFO per share was `$3.19`. Net income was `$370.4M`, or `$3.47` per share.
- Demand and operating indicators: SBA acquired `2,026` communication sites in the quarter, including `2,020` from the Millicom transaction, built `164` towers, and ended `2025` with `46,328` sites.
- Management message: management said carrier customers continued to densify and expand coverage, tied growth to new spectrum bands, regulatory requirements, and `5G` use cases such as fixed wireless access, and reset `2026` outlook to exclude EchoStar contracted revenue.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the bridge quarter between a cleaner reported base and the more explicit churn and concentration discussion that followed in `2026`. It also shows the international portfolio widening through Millicom before the new-year reporting reset.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- SBA sits below one of the most normalized behaviors in modern life: people now assume that video, messaging, maps, payments, work tools, and AI-assisted services will function everywhere with minimal latency or interruption.
- The social shift here is not novelty but dependence. Wireless connectivity has become invisible civic infrastructure, and SBA monetizes that dependence through rent on scarce locations rather than through consumer brand ownership.
- There is also a cultural expectation shift embedded in the quarter commentary. `5G`, fixed wireless access, and eventually `6G` are presented less as optional upgrades and more as table stakes for network quality, which means carriers keep paying for site amendments, new equipment, and new builds even when consumer enthusiasm is no longer explicitly about telecom.
- The tower layer also reflects a broader platform-dependence pattern. Consumers interact with apps, clouds, and AI services, but the first economic capture point often sits earlier in the stack at the physical site where coverage and capacity are actually created.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Customer concentration remains the central operating pressure. SBA’s recurring-rent profile is strong, but it still depends on a small number of large carriers whose strategic decisions can materially change churn, amendment timing, and backlog visibility.
- EchoStar is the clearest recent example. Q4 `2025` outlook was reset to exclude contracted EchoStar revenue, and Q1 `2026` explicitly referenced the full removal of EchoStar revenue from reported results.
- Higher rates still matter even when revenue is recurring. SBA’s filings note that higher interest rates have impacted, and are expected to continue to impact, the ability and willingness of wireless service providers to incur capital expenditures at prior levels.
- International expansion brings its own tradeoffs. Central America and other international markets can provide faster visible growth, but they also bring FX, local permitting, political, and land-control risks that do not exist in the same way in a purely domestic tower model.
- The capital structure is better than it was, but still material to the story. Net leverage remained in the middle of the `6.0x` to `7.0x` target range, and Q2 `2026` financing activity showed how much the equity story depends on disciplined debt access and refinancing execution.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Bandwidth demand becomes rent before it becomes app revenue.` SBA is one of the clearest archive examples of digital demand monetizing first at the tower site.
- `Infrastructure simplicity is itself a strategic asset.` Compared with peers that mix towers with datacenters or recently exited fiber and small cells, SBA offers a narrower but more legible recurring-cash-flow model.
- `Recurring revenue still depends on customer behavior.` Long leases and escalators help, but they do not eliminate carrier concentration, churn resets, or funding-cycle pressure.
- `Physical expansion follows social normalization.` The more consumers treat mobile connectivity as ambient and non-negotiable, the more value accrues to owners of scarce wireless locations and long-duration land rights.
- `AI and software still ride the old physical stack.` Even when markets focus on chips and applications, the supporting demand still shows up in towers, backhaul, test equipment, cooling, power systems, and other less visible physical infrastructure.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `American Tower Corporation`: American Tower is the broader global tower-plus-datacenter platform, while SBA is the narrower and cleaner pure-play tower rent model.
- Against `Crown Castle Inc.`: Crown Castle is the U.S. simplification story after exiting fiber and small cells, while SBA is the cleaner tower-only comparison without the same portfolio-reset noise.
- Against `Dycom Industries Inc.`: Dycom turns broadband and carrier network activity into project revenue, while SBA turns carrier network activity into recurring owned-site economics.
- Against `Equinix, Inc.` and `Digital Realty Trust, Inc.`: those names capture the compute and interconnection layer after traffic enters the network core, while SBA captures part of the wireless access layer where traffic first becomes physical network demand.

## Working interpretation

- SBA improves the archive because it gives the cleanest read on tower economics as a recurring infrastructure lane rather than as a telecom equipment or software adjacency.
- The larger pattern is that modern digital life keeps creating value for owners of invisible physical choke points. Towers are one of the simplest examples: the business captures mobile dependence, carrier capital allocation, and future wireless standards in rent form rather than in consumer narrative form.
