## What changed

- Portfolio scale: SBA ended `2025` with `46,328` owned or operated communications sites, grew to `46,358` by March 31, `2026`, and reached `46,390` by June 30, `2026`.
- Geographic mix: at year-end `2025`, `17,394` sites were in the United States and its territories and `28,934` were international; domestic sites generated `72.6%` of total site-leasing revenue in `2025`.
- Cash earnings: quarterly site leasing revenue held between `$656.1M` and `$666.2M` across Q4 `2025`, Q1 `2026`, and Q2 `2026`, while AFFO moved from `$340.4M` to `$321.7M` to `$324.4M`.
- Customer and churn reset: management excluded all contracted EchoStar revenue from its `2026` outlook and described the full removal of EchoStar revenue from reported results as of the beginning of `2026`, making carrier concentration and dispute risk much more visible.
- Capital structure: leverage moved from `6.4x` net debt to annualized Adjusted EBITDA at year-end `2025` to `6.6x` in Q1 `2026`, then back to `6.4x` in Q2 `2026` after stronger financing flexibility and the July `2026` unsecured notes issuance.
- Operating mix: site leasing generated `97.9%` of total segment operating profit in `2025`, making SBA one of the cleanest recurring-rent tower models in the archive.
- Asset quality: SBA reported an average of `1.8` tenants per site at December 31, `2025`, controlled about `71%` of tower structures on land owned or controlled for more than `20` years, and had an average remaining ground-lease life of `35` years including renewal options under its control.
- Strategic adjacency: the company still explores edge data centers, fiber aggregation huts, satellite ground stations, and private-network opportunities, but the core economics remain tower leasing first rather than a diversified digital-infrastructure bundle.

## Working interpretation

- SBA is the clearest pure-play tower-rent comparison in this archive. It monetizes normalized mobile dependence, carrier densification, and wireless capacity growth without the extra datacenter or fiber layers that complicate some peers.
- The more important insight is that “recurring infrastructure revenue” is not the same thing as “risk-free revenue.” The model is simple, but it is still exposed to carrier consolidation, customer disputes, FX, interest-rate-sensitive customer capex, and the timing of network upgrades.

## Open questions

- Once the EchoStar removal fully annualizes, does SBA return to visibly cleaner organic growth than its larger tower peers, or does carrier-spending concentration keep growth optics uneven?
- How much incremental value can SBA capture from Central America build-to-suit growth and tower adjacency experiments without diluting the simplicity that currently makes the story attractive?
