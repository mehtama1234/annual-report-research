## What changed

- Nike is a useful archive addition because it gives the research base a scaled global brand that is explicitly balancing wholesale reach against direct consumer control, product-margin discipline, and first-party relationship ownership.
- The fiscal `2026` annual package is current and filing-backed. Full-year revenues were `$46.4B`, NIKE Brand revenues were `$45.2B`, wholesale revenues were `$27.5B`, NIKE Direct revenues were `$17.7B`, and gross margin increased `20` basis points to `42.9%`.
- The direct relationship is economically material, but the packet also shows the tension in the model. NIKE Direct revenues declined in full-year `2026`, and the trailing quarter set repeatedly shows weaker NIKE Brand Digital performance even while wholesale reaccelerated.
- The quarter trail makes the operating tradeoff visible rather than theoretical. Q2 `2026` NIKE Direct revenues were `$4.6B`, Q3 were `$4.5B`, and Q4 were `$4.1B`, while wholesale revenues ran `$7.5B`, `$6.5B`, and `$6.6B` across the same window.
- The archive is especially useful here because it keeps the strategic claim honest. Nike is not a clean "direct wins everywhere" story. It is a real example of a global brand trying to rebuild growth while deciding how much of the consumer relationship to own directly versus through partners.

## Open questions

- How much of Nike's direct-to-consumer value comes from superior first-party data and brand control versus simple channel mix economics?
- Can Nike stabilize digital and owned-store traffic without giving back too much margin or brand control to wholesale partners?
- Is the most durable long-term lesson here that direct relationships matter, or that brands need a flexible balance between direct surfaces and third-party reach?
