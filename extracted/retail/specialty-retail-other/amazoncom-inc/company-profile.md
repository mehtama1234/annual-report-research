## What changed

- Amazon is a strong archive addition because it turns the broad Prime story into a filing-backed system view: subscriptions matter, but the real economics span retail traffic, third-party seller services, advertising, fulfillment speed, and cloud profit.
- The `2025` annual package was very strong. Full-year net sales increased `12%` to `$716.9B`, North America segment sales reached `$426.3B`, International segment sales reached `$161.9B`, AWS segment sales reached `$128.7B`, operating income increased to `$80.0B`, and net income reached `$77.7B`.
- The consumer-relationship point is explicit in the filings. Amazon defines subscription services to include Amazon Prime, and the `10-K` describes Prime as a membership program with fast shipping, video, live sports, and other benefits.
- The quarter trail also makes clear why Prime should not be read in isolation. In `Q2 2026`, advertising services were `$19.8B`, subscription services were `$13.7B`, and third-party seller services were `$46.8B`. In `Q1 2026`, the comparable figures were `$17.2B`, `$13.4B`, and `$41.6B`.
- The packet matters because it shows the bundle at work. Delivery speed, grocery and everyday essentials, sponsored ads, seller tools, media, and Prime membership all reinforce one another inside one ecosystem.

## Open questions

- How much of Prime's long-term economic value sits in subscription revenue itself versus the way it lifts frequency, advertising inventory, and third-party seller monetization?
- Does Amazon's growing advertising business make the consumer relationship structurally more valuable than the retail margin alone would suggest?
- As Amazon keeps pushing same-day delivery and grocery, how much more lock-in can it create before the cost of speed starts pressuring returns?
