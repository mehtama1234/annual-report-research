## Annual report takeaways

- Bath & Body Works is a useful archive addition because it gives Retail a weaker and more repair-oriented specialty retail case instead of another winner with obvious structural advantages.
- The local packet is filing-backed through the official annual report PDF, the `10-K`, and the full `Q3 2025` through `Q1 2026` SEC chain. Official IR pages were verified and current, even though direct scripted capture of the quarter release HTML timed out in this environment.
- The `2025` annual filing shows a brand with real scale but clear pressure. Full-year net sales were `$7.291B`, down `0.2%`; operating income was `$1.126B`, down `11%`; reported net income was `$649M`; and reported diluted EPS was `$3.11`.
- The more important structural point is that management is explicitly repositioning the business through the `Consumer First Formula`, a multi-year transformation launched in `Q3 2025` around disruptive product, brand reignition, broader marketplace reach, and operating speed.
- Bath & Body Works also matters because it still has meaningful direct-relationship assets: `1,927` company-operated stores in the U.S. and Canada, `573` international stores, `34` international e-commerce sites, an Amazon presence, and `40 million` active loyalty members.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` net sales were `$1.378B`, down `3%`; operating income was `$231M`; net income was `$183M`; reported EPS was `$0.90`; and adjusted EPS was `$0.32`.
- Operating posture: the `10-Q` shows stores-and-direct pressure in the core North American business, with stores at `$1.062B` versus `$1.110B` a year earlier and direct at `$246M` versus `$250M`, partly offset by international and other growth to `$70M`.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it is not a clean recovery. Reported earnings improved, but much of that came from an `$88M` pre-tax interchange-fee settlement benefit while sales were still down and adjusted earnings remained modest.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` net sales were `$2.724B`, down `2%`; EPS was `$1.99`; operating income was `$599M`; and net income was `$403M`.
- Operating posture: the quarter closed a softer year rather than reversing it. Management framed the period around innovation, brand elevation, and progress against the Consumer First Formula rather than obvious demand acceleration.
- Structural themes: this is the key quarter for the packet because it closes the `2025` year with a full retail-holiday read. Bath & Body Works still has large seasonal power, but it was not enough to stop the broader slowdown.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` net sales were `$1.594B`, down `1%`; operating income was `$161M`; net income was `$77M`; reported EPS was `$0.37`; and adjusted EPS was `$0.35`.
- Operating posture: the `10-Q` shows the mix pressure more clearly. Stores in the U.S. and Canada were stable at `$1.222B` versus `$1.220B`, but direct fell to `$299M` from `$321M`, while international rose to `$73M` from `$69M`.
- Structural themes: this quarter is where the transformation story really enters the archive. Management explicitly launched the Consumer First Formula because current trends and marketplace positioning were not good enough.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Bath & Body Works gives Retail a real middle-position specialty chain rather than another ecosystem or brand-superpower case.
- Consumer: this is a direct read on discretionary, giftable, fragrance-led spending where product novelty, average order size, and repeat purchase behavior matter more than pure essentials demand.
- Cultural: the business is trying to reclaim cultural relevance, not just run promotions harder. That makes it useful for understanding when an established specialty retailer has to rebuild emotional and generational relevance.
- Relationship and loyalty: the `40 million` active-member loyalty base matters, but the filings show that a big membership file alone does not guarantee growth if product, brand, and marketplace execution weaken.
- Channel structure: Bath & Body Works is also a good omnichannel and marketplace case. It still depends heavily on physical stores, but direct, international, and wholesale-style expansion all matter to the future model.
- Competitive positioning: this is the archive’s cleaner weaker-brand-led retail counterweight to Nike. Nike has unusual global brand power; Bath & Body Works shows what specialty brand retail looks like when it still has real scale and loyalty but needs reinvention.

## Working interpretation

- Bath & Body Works materially improves the Retail archive because it adds a specialty retailer where the main question is not scale advantage, but whether a large legacy brand can rebuild growth through better product, better brand work, broader access, and tighter execution.
- The important evidence is not only that sales were soft in `Q3 2025`, `Q4 2025`, and `Q1 2026`. It is that management responded with an explicit transformation architecture rather than pretending the problem was only macro.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Bath & Body Works is a strong archive addition for understanding when loyalty, omnichannel access, and specialty-brand recognition are helpful but still insufficient without product leadership and cultural relevance.
