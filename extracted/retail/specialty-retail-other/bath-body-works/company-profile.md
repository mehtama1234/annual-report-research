## What changed

- Bath & Body Works adds a direct specialty-retail operator to the archive that is neither a platform retailer like Amazon nor a global athletic brand like Nike.
- The `2025` annual package shows a business in transition rather than expansion. Full-year net sales were `$7.291B`, down `0.2%`; operating income was `$1.126B`, down `11%`; reported net income was `$649M`; and reported diluted EPS was `$3.11`.
- The structural point is that Bath & Body Works is trying to rebuild the model through the `Consumer First Formula`, a multi-year transformation launched in `Q3 2025` around product innovation, brand reinvigoration, marketplace expansion, and speed-and-efficiency work.
- The annual filing also makes the relationship and channel structure visible. Bath & Body Works says it had `1,927` company-operated stores in the U.S. and Canada, `573` international stores, `34` international e-commerce sites, and a customer loyalty program with `40 million` active members as of `2026-01-31`.
- The quarter trail shows why the packet matters now. `Q3 2025` and `Q4 2025` were both softer on sales and earnings, but `Q1 2026` showed a partial stabilization: sales were still down `3%`, yet reported operating income rose to `$231M` and reported EPS rose to `$0.90`, helped by litigation settlements while the underlying adjusted EPS remained only `$0.32`.

## Open questions

- Is Bath & Body Works a real brand-reinvigoration and specialty-retail recovery story, or mainly a cost and self-help story masking weaker consumer demand?
- How durable is the `40 million` member loyalty base if product novelty and broader brand relevance do not improve?
- Can the company widen beyond mall-era and legacy channel constraints fast enough for the `Consumer First Formula` to produce real growth by `2027`?
