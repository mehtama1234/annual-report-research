# Criteo Company Packet

## Why Criteo belongs in this frontier

Criteo is one of the clearest ways to connect advertising, retail media, shopper intent, and AI interfaces inside a single public-company packet. It gives the `attention and influence` lane a company whose economics sit close to transaction behavior rather than only to content inventory or brand campaigns.

That matters for this frontier because the archive is supposed to explain how attention gets bought, measured, packaged, and monetized as consumer interfaces change. Criteo shows that a large part of the next advertising fight is moving toward commerce surfaces, retailer-owned media, and AI-mediated product discovery.

## Business role

- Global commerce media operator
- Retail-media monetization partner for retailers and brands
- Performance-media infrastructure for demand capture and conversion
- Emerging advertising layer for AI and conversational shopping surfaces

## Annual report read: FY2025

The `2025` annual report and Q4/FY2025 release show a company trying to define the next version of ad tech around `commerce media` and `agentic commerce` rather than around legacy retargeting. Management framed the platform around the path from product discovery to purchase and leaned on differentiated commerce data, AI-driven decisioning, and global retailer/brand reach.

FY2025 operating scale remained material:

- revenue: `$1.945B`
- gross profit: `$1.049B`
- contribution ex-TAC: `$1.175B`
- adjusted EBITDA: `$407M`
- media spend: `$4.3B`
- free cash flow: `$211M`

The core tension in the year was that retail media remained strategically central while also becoming visibly concentrated. FY2025 retail-media contribution ex-TAC still grew in constant currency for the full year, but Q4 turned negative because of scope changes at two large retail-media clients. That makes Criteo a strong packet for studying how attractive new ad categories can still be vulnerable to customer concentration and retailer bargaining power.

## Quarter chain

### Q4 2025

Q4 `2025` closed the year with large operating scale and a sharper strategic story around AI-assisted commerce.

- revenue: `$541M`
- gross profit: `$297M`
- contribution ex-TAC: `$330M`
- adjusted EBITDA: `$120M`
- media spend: `$1.4B`
- net income: `$46M`

Key read:

- Management positioned the company as the commerce media platform connecting marketers and media owners from product discovery to purchase.
- Retail media was already showing concentration stress, with the same two client scope changes dragging the quarterly comparison.
- Performance media stayed steadier than the retail-media segment, which helps explain why Criteo is not just a retailer-tech vendor but a broader commerce-demand platform.

### Q1 2026

Q1 `2026` showed the concentration issue more explicitly while still indicating healthier underlying demand once the client-specific headwind is excluded.

- revenue: `$425M`
- contribution ex-TAC: `$250M`
- adjusted EBITDA: `$65M`
- media spend: more than `$1.0B`
- free cash flow: `$16M`

Key read:

- Retail-media revenue and contribution ex-TAC fell sharply, but management quantified a roughly `$27M` headwind from two client scope changes.
- Excluding that impact, management said the underlying client base still grew strongly, which implies the lane itself remained structurally attractive even while account concentration distorted the reported results.
- Criteo also kept extending retailer coverage, including additions such as DoorDash Canada and Hyundai Department Store.

### Q2 2026

Q2 `2026` made the frontier case especially strong because it tied retail media directly to AI shopping interfaces.

- revenue: `$428M`
- contribution ex-TAC: `$255M`
- adjusted EBITDA: `$73M`
- media spend: `$1.1B`
- net income: `$12M`
- free cash flow: `$(38)M`

Key read:

- Management again said the two retail-media client scope changes were masking strong underlying client-base expansion.
- The major strategic disclosure was that Criteo had become OpenAI's first advertising technology partner, with more than `2,000` brands advertising on ChatGPT across seven countries.
- This pushes the packet beyond standard ad-tech fragmentation and into a more important frontier question: who monetizes intent when consumers begin product discovery inside AI interfaces rather than on publisher pages, search results, or retailer sites alone?

## Frontier interpretation

### 1. Commerce intent is becoming a premium attention surface

Criteo shows that not all attention is equally valuable. Attention close to product discovery and purchase can carry much stronger monetization logic than generic media reach. That is why retail media, recommendation surfaces, and shopping-adjacent placements matter so much in this frontier.

### 2. Retail media is both a growth lane and a concentration risk

The packet is useful precisely because it is not a clean triumph narrative. Retail media looks structurally attractive, but large retailers can still change scope, hold bargaining power, and create abrupt reported volatility for infrastructure partners.

### 3. AI interfaces are becoming part of the ad stack

The OpenAI partnership is not just a company-specific novelty. It is a broader signal that conversational interfaces may become monetizable shopping and product-discovery surfaces. That puts Criteo in the same frontier conversation as publishers, broadcasters, measurement firms, and access operators who are all fighting to remain relevant as consumer interfaces shift.

### 4. Advertising infrastructure is moving closer to transaction rails

Compared with publishers or broadcasters, Criteo monetizes workflow and conversion infrastructure more than content itself. Compared with sell-side operators such as Magnite or trust operators such as DoubleVerify, it sits closer to shopper demand, retailer monetization, and purchase-adjacent activation.

## Why this packet improves the frontier

Criteo makes the `attention and influence` lane more defensible because it adds a retail-media and commerce-demand model that is materially different from:

- publisher or broadcaster monetization
- audience measurement
- sell-side programmatic infrastructure
- independent verification
- recommendation-widget distribution

It therefore broadens the frontier from media fragmentation alone into a sharper explanation of how commerce, customer ownership, and AI interfaces are changing the monetization of attention.
