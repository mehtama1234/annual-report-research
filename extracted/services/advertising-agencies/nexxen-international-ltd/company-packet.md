## Annual report takeaways

- Nexxen is one of the cleaner current packets for the frontier's fragmented-attention infrastructure layer because it sits across multiple monetization surfaces at once: DSP, SSP, data, CTV, and measurement or optimization workflow. It does not mainly own the audience or the content franchise. It tries to own enough of the transaction, data, and interface path to stay indispensable as viewing and ad buying splinter.
- The filed `2025` `20-F` and the `Q4 2025` / FY2025 results chain make the business mix clear. Full-year revenue was about `$364.8M`, essentially flat year over year, but programmatic revenue reached a record `$340.6M`, up `5%`, and contribution ex-TAC reached a record `$353.1M`, up `3%`. That shape matters because it shows the company deliberately concentrating the portfolio around the higher-control programmatic core while de-emphasizing weaker non-programmatic lines.
- The annual chain is especially useful for frontier interpretation because the disclosed strategy is not just "CTV growth." Management tied the business to exclusive `V` / `VIDAA` ACR data, smart-TV home-screen activation, enterprise DSP expansion, data licensing, and AI-powered workflow tools. The company is effectively arguing that fragmented attention can still be monetized if the operator controls enough proprietary data, enough buy-side workflow, and enough premium CTV access points.
- The `20-F` is also a strong pressure document. Nexxen repeatedly highlights exposure to privacy rules, CTV tracking constraints, data-provider dependence, third-party cloud and data-center infrastructure, and the risk that advertisers or platforms reduce access to the identifiers and data that make targeting possible. That makes the packet valuable not just as a growth story, but as a map of where the open ad ecosystem is structurally fragile.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent reported quarter on the baseline date

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was about `$86.8M`, up `11%`; programmatic revenue was about `$81.9M`, up `14%`; contribution ex-TAC was about `$84.5M`, up `13%`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$16.3M`; cash and cash equivalents were about `$94.6M`; long-term debt remained zero.
- Product and operating signals: management described record Q1 contribution, programmatic revenue, and CTV revenue, then raised full-year `2026` contribution ex-TAC and programmatic guidance. The company highlighted enterprise customer wins, mobile in-app momentum, `nexAI` workflow tools, and expanding adoption of `Nexxen TV Home Screen` across DSPs, agencies, and CTV OEMs.
- Interpretation: this is the quarter that makes Nexxen materially different from a pure sell-side or verification name. The company is trying to monetize fragmented viewing not only through software pipes, but through differentiated CTV surfaces and exclusive media-plus-data partnerships.
- Bigger-picture read: the ad market is rewarding infrastructure that shortens workflow, improves targeting quality, and owns scarce CTV entry points. Nexxen's latest reported quarter is a strong signal that the future of media-fragmentation monetization may sit in orchestrating data, activation, and interface access together rather than in any single ad-tech layer.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was about `$100.7M`, down `10%`; programmatic revenue was about `$94.3M`, down `4%`; contribution ex-TAC was about `$97.8M`, down `7%`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$33.9M`; cash and cash equivalents were about `$133.3M`; long-term debt remained zero.
- Full-year signals: FY`2025` revenue was about `$364.8M`; programmatic revenue was about `$340.6M`; contribution ex-TAC was about `$353.1M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$115.1M`; contribution ex-TAC retention fell to `92%` from `102%`.
- Strategic signals: management framed the quarter as distorted by lower spend from one DSP customer and by softer non-core activities, but it also emphasized the `V` partnership, data licensing with Yahoo DSP, programmatic smart-TV home-screen activation, curated marketplace rollout, and industry-specific solutions such as `Nexxen Sports` and `Nexxen Health`.
- Interpretation: Q4 is the quarter that makes the packet frontier-worthy rather than merely technical. It shows a company whose reported growth can wobble when partner behavior shifts, but whose strategic differentiation is moving toward more exclusive data and media assets, not less.
- Bigger-picture read: fragmented attention is still vulnerable to concentration at the workflow level. Even "independent" ad-stack operators can take a hit when a major DSP or buying channel changes behavior. The moat therefore has to come from exclusive data, CTV surfaces, and deeper enterprise embedding, not just open-market scale.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` revenue was about `$94.8M`, up `5%`; programmatic revenue was about `$89.6M`, up `10%`; contribution ex-TAC was about `$92.6M`, up `8%`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$28.2M`; cash and cash equivalents were about `$116.7M`; long-term debt remained zero.
- Guidance and mix signals: Nexxen lowered full-year `2025` guidance despite meeting Q3 expectations because of weaker-than-expected activity from certain third-party DSP partners and softness in non-core lines. Even so, programmatic revenue reached `94%` of revenue and management described broad-based omnichannel growth, enterprise DSP adoption, and rising data demand.
- Strategic signals: the company renewed and expanded the `VIDAA` partnership, extended exclusive global ACR-data access through at least `2029`, secured exclusive third-party video and display monetization rights on `VIDAA`'s North American media, and launched what it called the industry's first programmatic smart-TV home-screen activation solution.
- Interpretation: Q3 is the clearest quarter for understanding the tension inside the business. Nexxen had real underlying programmatic and data momentum, but it still had to confront how exposed parts of the SSP and open-market stack were to buyer concentration and shifting spend behavior.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter shows how media fragmentation creates both opportunity and fragility. The more ad inventory disperses, the more value can move to operators that unify it. But those operators are still exposed when upstream buying platforms change how they route demand.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Fragmented attention is being monetized through multi-layer control, not just one function: Nexxen combines buy-side software, sell-side inventory, proprietary data, and CTV interface access in one stack.
- Programmatic CTV is converging with device and interface control: the `VIDAA` / `V` relationship and smart-TV home-screen activation show that monetizable attention can be captured before a viewer even selects a streaming app.
- Exclusive data is becoming a substitute for scale where open-web economics are volatile: Nexxen repeatedly points to ACR data and direct media rights as differentiation against larger generic platforms.
- AI in ad tech is becoming workflow infrastructure rather than only targeting rhetoric: `nexAI`, automated troubleshooting, reporting compression, and DSP UI redesign are sold as labor-saving control points for agencies and enterprise buyers.
- Open-market independence still has concentration risk: the Q3 and Q4 chain shows that third-party DSP behavior can materially affect performance even when the company is growing elsewhere.
- Privacy and tracking pressure remain structural: Nexxen's `20-F` makes clear that access to cookies, mobile IDs, CTV tracking, consented data, and partner-provided identifiers is still a foundational operating dependency.

## Working interpretation

- Nexxen materially deepens the first sub-lane because it adds a distinct control model between `Comscore`, `Magnite`, `PubMatic`, and `DoubleVerify`. It is less about pure measurement or pure sell-side routing and more about stitching together demand, supply, data, and CTV interface inventory.
- The strongest repeated pattern is that fragmented attention is being rebuilt into monetizable systems through exclusive data and scarce surfaces. Nexxen's home-screen and `ACR` strategy is a strong signal that smart-TV operating layers are becoming ad-market choke points.
- The cultural and industrial signal underneath the numbers is that audiences may be scattered, but buyers still need simplification. The operators that can reduce workflow friction, prove differentiated data access, and package premium CTV entry points are trying to become the new coordinators of a splintered media world.
- For this frontier, Nexxen also makes the `Comscore`, `Magnite`, `PubMatic`, `DoubleVerify`, and `Roku` cluster more defensible. `Comscore` prices and validates audiences, `Magnite` monetizes fragmented premium supply, `PubMatic` pushes AI and open-internet execution, `DoubleVerify` proves quality and trust, `Roku` controls the household TV interface, and `Nexxen` sits between those worlds by combining data, workflow, and smart-TV activation into one platform.
