## What changed

- Taxonomy and lane fit: Omnicom is the cleanest direct `Advertising Agencies` flagship in the current frontier. It gives the archive a true attention-and-influence operator rather than another platform, publisher, or communications carrier.
- Structural shift: the `2025` annual report and `2026` quarters are dominated by the IPG merger and the resulting integration, disposition, and repositioning program. That matters because it turns Omnicom from a steady agency holding company into a live test of scale consolidation in marketing services.
- Operating model: Omnicom does not own the audience in the way a platform or broadcaster does. It sits one layer upstream as the allocator, strategist, buyer, and optimizer of client attention spend across media, commerce, PR, CRM, and specialty communications.
- Technology and workflow: management now explicitly frames the business around AI-powered Omni capabilities, identity and data infrastructure, connected capabilities, agentic marketing transformation, and AI-driven discovery. The technology story here is not product hype. It is workflow control over planning, targeting, measurement, and client execution.
- Quarter progression: Q4 `2025` was dominated by merger charges and portfolio repositioning. Q1 `2026` then shifted toward early integration and operating proof, while Q2 `2026` showed stronger organic growth, wider adjusted margins, and more explicit claims that clients were consolidating spend with larger integrated partners.

## Open questions

- How much of Omnicom’s improved `2026` operating picture is durable client demand versus temporary merger synergy and portfolio-reset effects?
- Does agency consolidation increase pricing and influence power, or does it mainly defend relevance against digital platforms and automated media tools?
- How much of the new Omnicom moat comes from scale in buying and data infrastructure versus real differentiation in strategy and creative judgment?
