# C.H. Robinson Worldwide Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-08

## Snapshot

- Company: C.H. Robinson Worldwide Inc.
- Sector: Services
- Industry: Air Delivery & Freight Services
- Ticker: CHRW
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- C.H. Robinson is the first direct `Services` sector evidence set in the archive and gives the project a freight-brokerage and forwarding read rather than another capital-goods or consumer-products read.
- The `2025` annual package shows a logistics company managing through weak freight demand by leaning on pricing discipline, productivity, and cost optimization rather than on end-market strength.
- Full-year `2025` total revenues were `$16.2 billion`, income from operations was `$795.0 million`, net income was `$587.1 million`, diluted EPS was `$4.83`, and adjusted diluted EPS was `$5.09`.
- The annual filing is especially useful because it lays out the operating structure clearly: `NAST` is the North American truckload-and-LTL engine, while `Global Forwarding` provides the ocean-air-customs read. That makes CHRW a practical bridge between domestic freight conditions and global shipping dislocation.
- The company also matters for theme work because it links AI language to actual workflow and margin improvement. Management is not talking about AI as a software valuation story; it is talking about quote-to-cash automation, shipment execution, pricing discipline, and lower cost to serve.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 2026 net income was `$186.8 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.56`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.61`, total revenues were `$4.9 billion`, and income from operations was `$255.7 million`.
- Guidance and setup: on `July 29, 2026`, management said the company had reached its mid-cycle operating-margin targets while freight demand was still in a trough.
- Management message: management emphasized `13` consecutive quarters of NAST market outgrowth versus the Cass Freight Shipment Index, strong pricing discipline, and more than `60%` productivity improvement since the end of `2022`.
- Structural themes: this quarter makes CHRW look like a freight-services company that can expand margins without needing a broad freight recovery, but it also shows how exposed cash generation is to working-capital swings when rates move quickly.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 2026 net income was `$147.2 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.22`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.35`, total revenues were `$4.0 billion`, and income from operations was `$175.7 million`.
- Guidance and setup: on `April 29, 2026`, management framed the truckload market as tightening on the supply side and argued that the new CHRW can still produce secular earnings growth through cycle changes.
- Management message: management stressed that NAST volume was flat while the Cass Freight Shipment Index declined `6.2%`, and it positioned the quarter as another proof point for disciplined pricing and `Lean AI`.
- Structural themes: this quarter is a good example of how broker economics can stay resilient even when freight volumes are soft, so long as the operator is taking share and protecting gross profit per shipment.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 2025 net income was `$136.3 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.12`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.23`, total revenues were `$3.9 billion`, and income from operations was `$181.4 million`. Full-year `2025` net income was `$587.1 million` and diluted EPS was `$4.83`.
- Guidance and setup: on `January 28, 2026`, management described the freight environment as difficult, with weak global freight demand, rising truckload spot costs, and falling ocean rates.
- Management message: despite those headwinds, management stressed market-share gains, better revenue management, and productivity improvement across both NAST and Global Forwarding.
- Structural themes: this quarter closes `2025` with CHRW looking less like a pure freight beta and more like a margin-and-execution story inside a weak macro backdrop.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: CHRW suggests that the first `Services` read should focus on freight brokerage, forwarding, and logistics workflow rather than on consumer-facing services.
- Consumer: consumer demand matters indirectly through freight activity, retail replenishment, foodservice flow, and shipping volumes, but the more useful lens is transport intensity and pricing.
- Cultural: the strongest cultural signal is operational discipline. The company is selling `Lean AI` as a workflow system and productivity culture rather than as a flashy product narrative.
- Technology: the technology layer is workflow automation, dynamic pricing, quoting, shipment orchestration, and visibility across a large freight network.
- Labor: labor matters because productivity gains are explicitly being generated through lower headcount, workflow redesign, and higher shipments per person.
- Capital allocation: capital expenditures are modest compared with industrial companies, but repurchases and dividends remain significant, which keeps the service model asset-light and cash-return oriented.
- Macro sensitivity: CHRW is a good cross-sector indicator because it sits close to truckload spot rates, LTL pricing, ocean demand, air demand, and working-capital conditions all at once.
