## What changed

- Revenue: full-year 2025 total revenues were `$16.2 billion`, down `8.4%`, with freight demand still weak and the Europe Surface Transportation divestiture creating an additional headwind. Q4 2025 revenues were `$3.9 billion`, down `6.5%`. Q1 2026 revenues were `$4.0 billion`, down `0.8%`. Q2 2026 revenues accelerated to `$4.9 billion`, up `19.3%`, largely because truckload, LTL, air, and ocean pricing moved up sharply.
- Earnings and margins: full-year 2025 income from operations was `$795.0 million`, net income was `$587.1 million`, diluted EPS was `$4.83`, and adjusted diluted EPS was `$5.09`. Q4 2025 income from operations was `$181.4 million`; Q1 2026 was `$175.7 million`; Q2 2026 rose to `$255.7 million`. This is a pricing-discipline and cost-to-serve story more than a simple freight-cycle rebound.
- Segment mix: the key operating engines are `North American Surface Transportation` (`NAST`) and `Global Forwarding`. In 2025, NAST revenue was `$11.6 billion` and segment operating income was `$621.8 million`, while Global Forwarding revenue was `$3.1 billion` and segment operating income was `$183.8 million`. NAST is the core U.S. truckload and LTL engine; Global Forwarding is the more global ocean-air-customs read.
- Productivity and AI: management repeatedly attributes outperformance to the Robinson operating model and its `Lean AI` strategy. Q4 2025 highlighted double-digit NAST productivity improvement for the full year and high-single-digit productivity improvement in Global Forwarding. Q2 2026 said productivity had improved by more than `60%` since the end of `2022` in both NAST and Global Forwarding.
- Market share versus the freight backdrop: CHRW is useful because it frames relative outgrowth against a weak freight market. Q4 2025 said NAST total volume increased about `1%` against a `7.6%` decline in the Cass Freight Shipment Index. Q1 2026 said NAST volume was flat against a `6.2%` Cass decline. Q2 2026 said NAST volume increased about `1.5%` against a `3.3%` Cass decline.
- Working capital and cash flow: the operating model is profitable, but cash conversion can swing hard with freight prices and working-capital timing. Q1 2026 cash from operations fell to `$68.6 million`, down `$37.9 million`. Q2 2026 cash from operations fell to `$35.9 million`, down `$191.2 million`, mainly because net operating working capital increased as prices moved higher.
- Network scale: the `2025` filing says CHRW served `75,000` customers worldwide and had more than `450,000` transportation providers on its platform, while managing approximately `37 million` shipments and `$23 billion` in freight. That makes it a useful service-sector bridge between freight demand, pricing, and enterprise workflow automation.
- Management tone: assertive and process-heavy. The company is explicitly arguing that the new CHRW can post secular earnings growth across freight cycles through disciplined pricing, workflow automation, and a lower cost to serve, rather than depending on a clean demand recovery.

## Open questions

- How durable are CHRW's margin gains if truckload spot costs stay elevated but end-demand remains soft?
- Does `Lean AI` remain a real service-productivity edge, or does it become easier for other brokers and forwarders to replicate once freight markets normalize?
