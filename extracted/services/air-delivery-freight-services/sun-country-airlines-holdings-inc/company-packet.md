# Sun Country Airlines Holdings, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-09

## Snapshot

- Company: Sun Country Airlines Holdings, Inc.
- Sector: Services
- Industry: Air Delivery & Freight Services
- Ticker: SNCY
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q1 2026
  - quarter 2: Q4 2025
  - quarter 3: Q3 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Sun Country is the right next travel packet because it is the sharpest local counterexample to the loyalty-heavy airline reading. Delta is the loyalty-currency case. United is the premium-network and reliability case. Southwest is the merchandising-reset case. Sun Country is the hybrid-utilization case.
- The `2025` annual filing describes Sun Country as a "new breed of hybrid low-cost air carrier" that dynamically shifts shared resources across scheduled service, charter, and cargo. That is not marketing filler. It is the operating model.
- Full-year `2025` numbers were respectable but not the main story: total operating revenues were about `$1.1268B`, operating income was about `$100.6M`, and net income was about `$52.8M`.
- The more important point is mix. Passenger revenue was about `$971.7M`, cargo revenue was about `$155.0M`, and the fleet configuration itself made the model visible: `47` passenger aircraft, `20` cargo aircraft flying for Amazon, and `3` aircraft on lease to unaffiliated airlines as of `2025-12-31`.
- Sun Country therefore expands the archive beyond the standard airline relationship question. This is not mainly an airline trying to deepen status or premium affinity. It is an operator trying to get higher returns out of a flexible fleet and labor pool across leisure demand, charter demand, and outsourced cargo flying.
- The annual packet also introduces a structural `2026` event that matters to the archive: Sun Country entered a merger agreement with Allegiant on `2026-01-11`, so the live reporting chain after the annual filing becomes partly a consolidation story instead of a clean next-quarter sequence.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` total operating revenues were about `$338.4M`, up from about `$326.6M` a year earlier; net income was about `$24.1M`; adjusted net income was about `$32.9M`; and adjusted EBITDA was about `$73.5M`.
- Mix and capacity: passenger revenue was essentially flat at about `$285.3M`, while cargo revenue rose `64%` year over year to about `$46.1M`.
- Operating shift: scheduled-service departures fell `14%` and passengers fell `12%`, while charter revenue still increased and cargo expanded. That matters because it shows the company intentionally shifting flying hours toward the Amazon cargo business rather than maximizing scheduled passenger volume.
- Merger frame: the filing says the Allegiant merger agreement signed on `2026-01-11` had already moved into the regulatory and proxy phase, with DOJ early termination on `2026-03-16`, DOT interim approval on `2026-04-15`, and a Sun Country special meeting scheduled for `2026-05-08`.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the clearest proof that Sun Country belongs in the archive as a hybrid allocation model. The company used weaker passenger capacity and stronger cargo economics to hold growth while the business was also being prepared for acquisition.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was about `$281.0M`, net income was about `$8.1M`, operating income was about `$18.2M`, operating margin was `6.5%`, adjusted operating income was about `$19.5M`, and adjusted operating margin was `7.0%`.
- Full-year frame: the Q4 release closed a fiscal `2025` year with about `$1.1268B` of operating revenue and about `$52.8M` of net income.
- Operating message: the important year-end operating development was not only earnings stability. It was that Sun Country had completed the move from `12` to `20` Amazon cargo aircraft during `2025`, materially widening the cargo side of the business.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` reads as the quarter where Sun Country most clearly becomes a hybrid airline-and-cargo packet rather than a smaller airline packet with some extra business lines attached.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` total operating revenues were about `$255.5M`; operating income was about `$9.9M`; operating margin was `3.9%`; net income was about `$1.6M`; adjusted operating income was about `$12.4M`; and adjusted operating margin was `4.8%`.
- Segment mix: passenger revenue was about `$201.1M`, cargo revenue was about `$44.0M`, and charter revenue within the passenger segment rose to about `$58.7M`.
- Capacity signal: the quarter showed the same direction that became clearer later. Passenger flying was being reduced while the company focused on cargo growth, with all eight additional Amazon cargo aircraft placed in service during the first nine months of `2025`.
- Cultural and operating read: management also described Q3 `2025` as the thirteenth consecutive profitable quarter, which matters because it shows the hybrid model was not being pitched as a rescue plan. It was already the core way the company thought about profitability.
- Structural themes: Q3 is the quarter where the archive first gets a clean view of Sun Country as an airline whose consumer interface is thinner than the legacy majors because more of the economics come from flexible asset use, charter customers, and a single large cargo partner.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Thin relationship layer: Sun Country is important because it tests how far an airline-adjacent operator can go without the thicker loyalty and status economics visible at Delta, United, or even Southwest.
- Hybrid transport logic: this is one of the clearest packets in the archive where the real moat is not pure customer love or points accumulation but the ability to redeploy aircraft and crew across scheduled service, charter, and cargo.
- Amazon concentration: cargo is not generic freight exposure. It is concentrated outsourced flying for Amazon, which turns part of the company into an embedded logistics service layer.
- Merger and consolidation: the Allegiant transaction makes Sun Country a good `2026` example of how sector structure, not only quarter-to-quarter demand, can interrupt the normal public-company reporting sequence.
- Cultural read: Sun Country still serves leisure and VFR travelers, but the filings make it harder to read the business as a consumer-brand story first. It looks more like a disciplined utilization and seasonal-allocation story with an airline brand attached.
- Taxonomy lesson: AnnualReports.com classified Sun Country as `Services / Air Delivery & Freight Services` and still showed only `2024` as the latest annual package on `2026-08-09`. That classification is unexpectedly useful because it captures the way cargo and transport-services economics are central to the business.

## Working interpretation

- Sun Country materially improves the travel lane because it shows that not every airline-like company should be read through loyalty, premium status, or direct consumer interface depth.
- The most important evidence is that the business was explicitly reallocating capacity toward cargo and charter while preserving profitability. That is a different operating skill from the relationship-deepening strategies visible at the larger carriers.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Sun Country is one of the clearest examples of a travel company whose most interesting economics sit behind the traveler-facing surface.
