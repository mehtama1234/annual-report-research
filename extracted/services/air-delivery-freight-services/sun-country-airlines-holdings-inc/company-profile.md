## What changed

- Sun Country is the useful contrast case in the airline lane because it is much less a loyalty machine and much more a utilization machine. The company calls itself a hybrid low-cost carrier and uses shared aircraft and crew resources across scheduled service, charter, and cargo.
- The `2025` annual filing makes the model concrete. Total operating revenues were about `$1.127B`; net income was about `$52.8M`; operating income was about `$100.6M`; passenger revenue was about `$971.7M`; and cargo revenue was about `$155.0M`.
- The fleet and segment disclosures matter more than the headline earnings alone. As of `2025-12-31`, Sun Country had `70` Boeing `737-NG` aircraft, including `47` in the passenger fleet, `20` cargo aircraft operated for Amazon, and `3` aircraft on lease to unaffiliated airlines.
- The quarter trail shows a business reallocating capacity rather than simply chasing airline volume. Q3 `2025` and Q1 `2026` both showed lower scheduled-service intensity and higher cargo contribution, with `2026` Q1 cargo revenue up `64%` year over year.
- The biggest structural shift in `2026` was not only operational. On `2026-01-11`, Sun Country entered a merger agreement under which Allegiant would acquire the company, and the `2026` Q1 filing shows that regulatory, proxy, and closing work quickly became a central part of the disclosure stack.

## Open questions
