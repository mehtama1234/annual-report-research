## What changed

- Revenue: full-year `2025` revenue was `$88.7 billion`, down from `$91.1 billion` in `2024`. Q4 2025 revenue was `$24.5 billion`, Q1 2026 revenue was `$21.2 billion`, and Q2 2026 revenue was `$22.8 billion`. UPS is not presenting a broad demand boom. It is presenting a revenue-quality and mix reset, especially in the U.S. domestic network.
- Earnings and margins: full-year `2025` operating profit was `$7.9 billion`, non-GAAP adjusted operating profit was `$8.7 billion`, operating margin was `8.9%`, and non-GAAP adjusted operating margin was `9.8%`. Diluted EPS was `$6.56`, while non-GAAP adjusted diluted EPS was `$7.16`. Q4 2025 diluted EPS was `$2.10`; Q1 2026 was `$1.02`; Q2 2026 was `$0.71`, but the adjusted Q2 2026 EPS was much stronger at `$1.76` because transformation charges were unusually large.
- Segment structure: UPS has two reportable package segments, `U.S. Domestic Package` and `International Package`, with the rest in `Supply Chain Solutions`. In `2025`, U.S. Domestic revenue was `$59.5 billion`, International was `$18.6 billion`, and consolidated revenue was `$88.7 billion`. This makes UPS a broader transport and parcel read than CHRW's broker-forwarder model.
- Package and customer scale: the `2025` filing says UPS delivered an average of `20.8 million` packages per day, totaling `5.2 billion` packages during the year. It served `1.6 million` shipping customers and more than `10.7 million` delivery recipients daily. That gives the Services archive a direct physical-delivery network with very different economics from a brokered freight platform.
- Amazon glide-down and network reset: the biggest strategic variable is the deliberate reduction of volume from Amazon. The `2025` filing says Amazon represented `10.6%` of consolidated revenue in `2025`, and UPS has been executing a strategy to reduce that customer's volume by more than `50%` by June `2026` from `2024` levels. Management repeatedly frames `2026` as the transition year after that glide-down.
- Transformation and restructuring: this is not a normal steady-state operating story. Q4 2025 referenced transformation programs, while Q2 2026 included after-tax transformation charges of `$891 million`, primarily tied to workforce reductions and the Driver Choice Program. UPS is using cost-out, facility consolidation, automation, and network redesign to defend margins while reshaping the package mix.
- Pricing and revenue-per-piece: across the in-scope quarters, revenue per piece was the main positive lever. Q4 2025 U.S. Domestic revenue per piece grew `8.3%`. Q1 2026 U.S. Domestic revenue per piece grew `6.5%`. Q2 2026 U.S. Domestic revenue per piece grew `9.3%`. International revenue per piece also stayed strong. UPS is effectively trading lower or lower-quality volume for better mix and pricing.
- Cash generation and capital allocation: Q4 2025 said cash from operations for the year was `$8.5 billion` and non-GAAP adjusted free cash flow was `$5.5 billion`. UPS returned `$6.4 billion` to shareowners in `2025` through dividends and repurchases. This is still a large cash-generating network even while it is being reconfigured.
- Management tone: direct and transitional. The message is that `2026` is a strategic handoff year: weaker near-term GAAP optics because of transformation charges, but better revenue quality, less Amazon dependence, and a path toward margin expansion once the network reset is absorbed.

## Open questions

- Does UPS get the margin lift it wants after the Amazon glide-down, or does the lost density create a more persistent domestic-network cost problem?
- How much of the current parcel story is true quality improvement, and how much is just aggressive cost removal that becomes harder to repeat after `2026`?
