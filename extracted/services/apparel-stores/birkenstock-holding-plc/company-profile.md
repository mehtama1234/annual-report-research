## What changed

- Revenue: Birkenstock moved from `EUR 526M` in `Q4 FY2025` to `EUR 402M` in `Q1 FY2026` and `EUR 618M` in `Q2 FY2026`, while still posting strong constant-currency growth each quarter.
- Mix: the annual and quarter chain shows a meaningful broadening beyond sandals, with closed-toe share rising to `38%` of annual business and management repeatedly highlighting clogs, boots, and closed-toe shoes.
- Pricing and demand: fiscal `2025` combined `12%` unit growth with `5%` constant-currency ASP growth, which is unusually strong evidence that the brand can carry both volume and price.
- Consumer and cultural signals: Birkenstock is a body-function and practical-wellness purchase that also operates as a lifestyle and identity purchase.
- Operating pressure: the `2026` quarter chain shows FX, tariffs, inflation, and Middle East disruption as real constraints, but not enough to break demand or the full-year growth target.

## Open questions

- How far can the closed-toe extension go before Birkenstock starts looking more like a broader footwear house than a distinct footbed-first brand?
- How durable is the current ASP strength if macro pressure weakens and wellness-adjacent footwear gets more crowded?
- Does the biggest long-run upside come from deeper DTC penetration, continued B2B partner strength, or product expansion beyond core footwear?
