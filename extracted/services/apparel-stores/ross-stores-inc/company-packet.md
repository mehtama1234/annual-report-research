# Ross Stores, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-09

- Company: Ross Stores, Inc.
- Sector: Services
- Industry: Apparel Stores
- Ticker: ROST
- Fiscal year-end: January
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q1 2026
  - quarter 2: Q4 2025
  - quarter 3: Q3 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Ross is a high-value archive addition because it gives the consumer-and-retail work a format that is neither premium-brand-led nor giant-ecosystem-led. It wins through value, traffic, merchandising, and disciplined off-price execution.
- The annual evidence chain is current and clean. AnnualReports already listed the `2025 Annual Report and Form 10K` on `2026-08-09`, and Ross's official IR plus SEC stack fully support the annual package for the year ended `2026-01-31`.
- The annual package was strong. Fiscal `2025` sales increased `8%` to a record `$22.8B`, comparable store sales increased `5%` on top of a `3%` gain in fiscal `2024`, net income was `$2.1B`, and earnings per share were `$6.61`.
- The strategically important point is that Ross is not describing a digital-relationship or membership story first. It is describing a value-and-execution story: compelling branded bargains, high customer engagement, off-price sourcing, clean inventory management, and a broad store base that can capture trade-down and market-share demand.
- That makes Ross especially useful for the archive. It shows that a physical retail format can still be very strong when the value proposition is clear and the buying model is structurally advantaged.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` sales increased `21%` to `$6.0B`; comparable store sales increased `17%`; net income was `$650M`; and earnings per share increased `37%` to `$2.02`.
- Operating posture: first-quarter operating margin reached `13.4%`, well above plan, as strong sales outperformance leveraged the model. Management also raised full-year guidance after the quarter.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows very strong traffic-led demand. Management said customer traffic was the primary driver, supported by compelling merchandise, better customer acquisition and engagement from marketing, and an improved in-store experience.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` sales increased `12%` to `$6.6B`; comparable store sales increased `9%`; net income rose to `$646M`; and earnings per share increased to `$2.00`.
- Operating posture: the company closed fiscal `2025` with strong momentum, completed `$1.05B` of share repurchases during the year, and approved a new two-year `$2.55B` repurchase authorization.
- Structural themes: this is the key quarter for the packet because it closes the year with the clearest market-share signal. Ross said holiday execution was strong, marketing increased customer engagement, and the value proposition continued to resonate across a pressured consumer environment.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` sales increased `10%` to `$5.6B`; comparable store sales increased `7%`; net income was `$512M`; and earnings per share increased to `$1.58`.
- Operating posture: management said the merchandise assortment of compelling brand-name values resonated with shoppers and that the new marketing campaign helped drive stronger customer engagement. Operating margin of `11.6%` was much better than expected.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because it shows momentum accelerating before the strong holiday finish. Ross was already taking share through broad-based merchandise and geographic strength, while keeping cost discipline tight enough to absorb tariff pressure better than expected.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Ross gives the archive a scaled off-price value retailer where physical-store traffic and branded-bargain merchandising still produce very strong economics.
- Consumer: this is a direct read on value-seeking behavior, trade-down dynamics, and what happens when consumers are pressured but still willing to spend if the deal quality is compelling.
- Cultural: Ross matters because it shows that not all consumer resilience depends on loyalty systems, subscriptions, or premium identity. Everyday bargain hunting and in-store discovery can still be a durable behavior at national scale.
- Loyalty and technology: Ross is useful partly because it is not a classic loyalty or interface-control story. The model is less about owned digital habit loops and more about merchandising execution, store convenience, and customer engagement through value and marketing.
- Business model: the key business-model signal is that off-price still has a strong second-order advantage when weaker retailers are over-inventoried or mis-executing. Ross can turn branded closeout availability, lean inventory discipline, and store traffic into profit growth.
- Competitive positioning: Ross is especially useful because it gives the archive a format winner that can outperform weaker middle-market retailers without needing Amazon-scale infrastructure or Nike-style brand ownership.

## Working interpretation

- Ross materially improves the archive because it gives the broader retail-and-services work a value-format winner that can be compared directly against Kohl's weak middle and Best Buy's cyclical hybrid model.
- The most important evidence here is not only the topline strength. It is that Ross repeatedly attributes performance to compelling bargains, customer traffic, stronger engagement, and disciplined execution, which points to a format-level advantage rather than a one-off promotional spike.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Ross is a strong archive addition for understanding how off-price retail can capture market share and drive profitable growth in an environment where many other consumer formats are still trying to stabilize.
