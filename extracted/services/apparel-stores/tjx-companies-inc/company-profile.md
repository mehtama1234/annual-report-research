## What changed

- TJX adds the clearest large-scale off-price and treasure-hunt retail case currently available for the blind-spot lane.
- The annual and quarter chain shows a business that is not primarily winning through premium identity, paid membership, or marketplace-layer monetization. It is winning through value perception, fast-changing branded assortment, customer traffic, store density, and flexible buying.
- The annual filing and official results pages repeatedly frame the business around delivering great value every day and offering branded merchandise at prices generally `20%` to `60%` below full-price retailers' regular prices on comparable merchandise.
- The scale is also structurally important. As of fiscal year-end `2026-01-31`, TJX described itself as operating over `5,200` stores, and the official overview page said it operated in ten countries across three continents.

## Open questions

- How much further can TJX keep taking share if the value-hunt consumer stays active but inventory availability at full-price peers tightens?
- Does the off-price system stay strongest as a physical-store discovery model, or does the e-commerce layer become more strategically relevant over time?
- How different is TJX's bargain-discovery relationship from Ross and Burlington once global scale, home exposure, and multiple banners are taken into account?
