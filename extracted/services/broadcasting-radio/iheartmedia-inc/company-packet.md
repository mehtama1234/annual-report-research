## Annual report takeaways

- iHeartMedia is a strong attention-and-influence packet for this frontier because it shows what happens when legacy radio tries to turn itself into a broader measurable-audio system. The business still depends on broadcast scale, but the strategic language and the better growth pockets are all about podcasts, digital audio, targeting, attribution, software, and cross-platform ad packaging.
- The `2025` annual report makes the structure explicit. iHeartMedia reports three segments: `Multiplatform Group`, which includes broadcast radio, networks, sponsorships, events, SmartAudio, Premiere Networks, Total Traffic and Weather Network, BIN, and national sales; `Digital Audio Group`, which includes digital audio and podcasting; and `Audio & Media Services Group`, which includes Katz Media and RCS software. That framing matters because it makes the packet about the full attention stack, from audience creation to ad sales and measurement.
- The annual filing also gives the frontier a concrete cultural shift. Management describes two sectors in audio: the music-collection business and the companionship business. iHeartMedia wants to win the second one, where consumers use radio, podcasts, streaming, personalities, social, and events as everyday presence rather than as a one-time media purchase. That is a very direct statement about how attention is changing.
- Scale still matters here. The annual package says the company had `860+` broadcast stations in around `160` markets, distribution across `500+` platforms and thousands of devices, and a social footprint of more than `345M` fans and followers. But the packet is more useful because of how that scale is monetized: management keeps stressing a one-of-a-kind cross-platform audio advertising solution with data targeting and attribution measurement.
- The financial shape is the key annual signal. Full-year `2025` segment revenue was about `$2.274B` for `Multiplatform Group`, about `$1.329B` for `Digital Audio Group`, and about `$272.5M` for `Audio & Media Services Group`. That means the company is still funded mainly by legacy-style audio distribution, but its cleaner growth story sits in digital audio and podcasts. The annual risk language also matters: management says macroeconomic uncertainty continued to affect advertising revenue, profit margins, cash flow, and liquidity during `2025`.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was about `$884M`; GAAP operating income was about `$1.5M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$93M`; cash used for operating activities was about `$93M`; free cash flow was about `$(114)M`.
- Segment signals: `Digital Audio Group` revenue was about `$327M`, up `18%`; podcast revenue was about `$147M`, up `27%`; `Multiplatform Group` revenue was about `$493M`, up `4%`; `Audio & Media Services Group` revenue was about `$66.6M`, up `12%`.
- Margin and liquidity signals: `Digital Audio Group` adjusted EBITDA was about `$86.8M`, essentially flat, while `Multiplatform Group` adjusted EBITDA fell about `33%` to roughly `$47.0M`; total available liquidity was about `$495M`.
- Management signal: iHeart kept its full-year `2026` adjusted EBITDA guide around `$800M`, free-cash-flow guide around `$200M`, added a new `$50M` annualized cost-savings program, and said programmatic revenue should reach about `$200M`.
- Interpretation: Q1 is the clearest quarter for the lane because it shows a business where audience monetization is shifting toward digital and measurable audio, but the economic drag from legacy broadcast, high expenses, and cash pressure has not gone away.
- Bigger-picture read: this is a frontier-quality signal for media fragmentation. Consumers keep listening, podcast revenue keeps growing, and advertisers still want measurable audio, but the old broadcast economics remain soft enough that the company has to rely on cost actions, liquidity management, and cross-platform packaging to defend the whole stack.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was about `$1.127B`; GAAP operating income was about `$86M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$220M`; cash provided by operating activities was about `$156M`; free cash flow was about `$138M`.
- Segment signals: `Digital Audio Group` revenue was about `$387M`, up `14%`; podcast revenue was about `$174M`, up `24%`; `Multiplatform Group` revenue was about `$665M`, down `3%`; `Audio & Media Services Group` revenue was about `$78.9M`, down `19%`.
- Political and guidance signals: excluding political revenue, Q4 consolidated revenue was up `7.7%` and `Multiplatform Group` revenue was up `2%`. Management guided to about `$800M` of full-year `2026` adjusted EBITDA, about `$200M` of free cash flow, about `$200M` of programmatic revenue, and year-end net leverage in the mid-fives.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY2025 is the quarter that makes iHeartMedia a useful flagship packet rather than a category filler. It shows that headline radio softness can hide underlying growth in digital audio and podcasts once political distortions are stripped out.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter makes the attention lane more defensible because it shows how audio monetization is being rebuilt around measurable formats. The old bundle was station reach plus ad inventory; the new bundle is station reach plus podcasting plus data-targeted digital audio plus attribution.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` revenue was about `$997M`; GAAP operating loss was about `$(116)M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$205M`; cash used for operating activities was about `$10M`; free cash flow was about `$(33)M`.
- Segment signals: `Digital Audio Group` revenue was about `$342M`, up `14%`; podcast revenue was about `$140M`, up `22%`; `Multiplatform Group` revenue was about `$591M`, down `5%`; `Audio & Media Services Group` revenue was about `$66.6M`, down `26%`.
- Operating-pressure signals: Q3 included about `$209M` of non-cash FCC-license impairment charges. Management said lower political revenue and continued uncertain market conditions hurt broadcast advertising, while modernization initiatives reduced some compensation costs.
- Interpretation: Q3 is the most stressed quarter in the chain and therefore one of the most useful. It shows the business taking a real GAAP hit from impairment at the same time that digital audio continues to grow and legacy broadcast stays under pressure.
- Bigger-picture read: the quarter shows that the radio lane is not dead, but it is being repriced. Audience ownership is still valuable, especially when it can be extended into podcasts and digital targeting, but local and spot-broadcast economics are weaker, more cyclical, and less defensible on their own.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Audio is becoming a measurable attention layer: iHeartMedia is one of the best current packets for showing that audio is no longer just a brand-awareness medium. Management keeps emphasizing targeting, attribution, programmatic, podcasting, and cross-platform audio sales.
- Media fragmentation does not eliminate scale; it changes where scale monetizes: the company still has national radio reach, but the cleaner growth comes from digital audio and podcasts where advertisers can buy more precisely and measure more credibly.
- Political advertising distorts the lane: both Q3 `2025` and Q4 `2025` show how comparisons to the prior election cycle can mask underlying organic trends. Reported revenue can look weak while ex-political underlying demand is still positive.
- Legacy broadcast is under structural pressure even when the audience franchise still matters: `Multiplatform Group` keeps facing lower spot revenue, weaker broadcast advertising, and margin compression even as the company remains a scaled audio network.
- Non-cash trade and strategic marketing initiatives matter to quality of earnings: repeated references to non-cash trade revenue and expense suggest that gross revenue growth does not always equal clean incremental monetization.
- Leverage and liquidity remain part of the operating story: total available liquidity moved from about `$510M` at Q3 `2025` to about `$640M` at Q4 `2025` and then down to about `$495M` at Q1 `2026`, while management continued to frame `2026` around cost savings and leverage reduction.

## Working interpretation

- iHeartMedia materially strengthens the first sub-lane because it gives the frontier a true `Broadcasting - Radio` flagship instead of leaving the attention stack concentrated in agencies, newspapers, and TV.
- The key societal pattern here is that audio attention has not disappeared; it has been redistributed across live radio, podcasts, streaming, social, events, and data-targeted ad products. The winning model is not one-format dominance. It is cross-platform presence plus measurement.
- The strongest repeating pattern is that measurable demand is migrating toward digital audio even when the cultural surface still includes legacy broadcast brands. Omnicom explains how marketers buy demand, NYT explains how trust becomes paid habit, Fox explains how real-time video still scales, and iHeart explains how audio is being rebuilt into a more targetable and attributable medium.
- For this frontier, iHeart also adds a harder operating truth: audience franchises can survive while legacy monetization erodes. The company still reaches consumers at scale, but broadcast softness, political volatility, leverage, and expense pressure mean that scale alone is not enough unless it can be turned into higher-quality digital revenue.
