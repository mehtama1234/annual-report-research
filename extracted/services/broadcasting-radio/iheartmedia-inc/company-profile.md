## What changed

- Business mix: the `2025` annual report presents three reportable segments: `Multiplatform Group`, `Digital Audio Group`, and `Audio & Media Services Group`. That matters because the packet is no longer only a terrestrial-radio story; it is a cross-platform audio, ad-tech, and media-services story.
- Scale and reach: management says iHeartMedia is the number one audio media company in the U.S. based on consumer reach, with `860+` broadcast radio stations in about `160` markets, a leading social footprint, and audio distribution across `500+` platforms and thousands of devices.
- Revenue mix: full-year `2025` segment revenue was about `$2.274B` for `Multiplatform Group`, about `$1.329B` for `Digital Audio Group`, and about `$272.5M` for `Audio & Media Services Group`, showing that legacy broadcast still pays the bills while digital audio provides the clearer growth engine.
- Strategy language: the annual filing and quarter exhibits repeatedly frame the growth case around podcasting, digital advertising, SmartAudio targeting and attribution, and being able to sell across broadcast radio, streaming radio, and podcasting.
- Quarter progression: Q3 `2025`, Q4 `2025`, and Q1 `2026` show a repeated pattern where reported revenue is pressured by political-comparison rolloff and soft broadcast demand, but digital audio and podcasting remain strong enough to keep the broader attention model economically relevant.

## Open questions

- How much of the recent revenue support from strategic marketing initiatives and non-cash trade revenue represents durable demand versus accounting-heavy gross-up?
- Can digital-audio growth offset continued softness in legacy broadcast advertising without requiring ongoing margin sacrifice?
- How much flexibility does the company really have if ad markets weaken again while leverage remains elevated and liquidity stays constrained?
