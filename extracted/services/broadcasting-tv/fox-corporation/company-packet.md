  - quarter 1: FY2026 Q4

## Annual report takeaways

- Fox is a high-value attention-and-influence packet because it shows that mass media is not dead so much as repriced around scarce formats. The company is built around live news, live sports, local broadcast distribution, and the ability to sell viewers that still show up in real time.
- The `2025` annual report says the business differentiates itself through premium programming focused on live and `appointment-based` content, a significant presence in major markets, and broad distribution across traditional and digital platforms. That is one of the clearest statements in the frontier that mass attention still matters when the content is time-sensitive enough.
- The annual package also makes the revenue logic explicit. Management says the quality of programming and the strength of the brands maximize value through a combination of affiliate fees and advertising sales. That matters because it shows broadcasting as a dual-monetization model, not a simple ad market bet.
- Tubi is the other critical annual signal. The `2025` filing describes Tubi as a leading AVOD service that reaches a young, diverse, and highly engaged audience across nearly `300,000` movies and television episodes. That gives Fox a real bridge from legacy linear television into digital video demand without abandoning ad-supported economics.
- The annual and Q4 FY2025 result chain makes the financial scale concrete. Q4 FY2025 revenue was about `$3.29B`, adjusted EBITDA was about `$939M`, and full-year fiscal `2025` revenue was about `$16.30B` with about `$3.62B` of adjusted EBITDA. Management called fiscal `2025` another outstanding year and explicitly tied the result to digital growth assets and the coming launch of `FOX One`.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: FY2026 Q4 revenue was about `$4.21B`; net income was about `$696M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$1.20B`. Full-year fiscal `2026` revenue reached about `$17.13B`; full-year adjusted EBITDA reached about `$3.91B`.
- Operating signals: distribution revenue increased `5%`, driven by `7%` growth in Cable Network Programming. Advertising revenue increased `78%`, primarily due to the current-year broadcast of the FIFA Men's World Cup and continued digital growth led by Tubi.
- Management signal: Lachlan Murdoch said fiscal `2026` was capped by the World Cup, that FOX One had launched, Tubi had strengthened its position, and the company had announced the Roku acquisition.
- Interpretation: Q4 shows what real-time attention still looks like when it is scarce enough. Fox can still produce an event-driven advertising surge when it owns national sports inventory that advertisers cannot easily replicate elsewhere.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter is a reminder that the future of TV broadcasting is not generic linear programming. It is live events, brand trust, and extension into digital viewing surfaces that keep the ad and distribution machine relevant.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: FY2026 Q3 revenue was about `$3.99B`; net income was about `$175M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$954M`.
- Operating signals: distribution revenue increased `3%`, driven by `5%` growth at Cable Network Programming. Advertising revenue fell to about `$1.56B` from about `$2.04B` in the prior-year quarter, primarily because the prior year included the Super Bowl, though the impact was partially offset by an additional NFL Wild Card game and continued Tubi growth. Content and other revenue increased `12%` on higher sports sublicensing revenue.
- Management signal: Murdoch said the quarter showed continued strength in live programming and Tubi while Fox prepared to bring the FIFA Men's World Cup to U.S. homes.
- Interpretation: Q3 is a useful counterweight to the Q4 spike. Broadcasting economics remain highly sensitive to sports-calendar timing, which means the business is strong but not smooth.
- Bigger-picture read: the quarter reinforces a core lane pattern. Scarce live rights preserve relevance, but they also make results lumpy. The payoff is high monetization power; the cost is calendar-driven volatility and rights inflation pressure.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: FY2026 Q2 revenue was about `$5.18B`; net income was about `$247M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$692M`.
- Operating signals: distribution revenue increased `4%`, driven by `5%` growth at Cable Network Programming. Advertising revenue increased `1%` despite a difficult comparison because higher sports and news pricing, continued Tubi growth, and additional MLB postseason games offset lower political advertising revenue and lower ratings.
- Management signal: Murdoch described the quarter as another period of broad-based contribution across news, sports, streaming, and entertainment.
- Interpretation: Q2 is one of the clearest quarters in the frontier for understanding advertising cyclicality. Fox still grew companywide advertising even after a strong prior-year political ad period, but the comparison pressure is visible and structural.
- Bigger-picture read: broadcasting is still a utility for national moments, but the monetization stack is increasingly hybrid. Political cycles, sports windows, retransmission fees, and AVOD growth all sit together in the same revenue engine.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Live attention is still economically scarce: Fox is a strong rebuttal to the idea that fragmented media makes broadcast-scale attention irrelevant. Live sports and high-intensity news still command time-sensitive viewing and premium ad demand.
- Affiliate fees still matter: the company keeps stressing affiliate fees and distribution growth, which shows that household and distributor relationships remain central even as digital video grows.
- AVOD is a real bridge, not a side project: Tubi matters because it lets Fox keep ad-supported economics while capturing younger digital viewing behavior rather than conceding that audience entirely to platform incumbents.
- Broadcasting volatility is increasingly event-driven: political cycles, Super Bowl timing, World Cup rights, MLB postseason inventory, and sports sublicensing all create quarter-to-quarter swings. That is not noise; it is how the lane now works.
- Direct audience ownership is expanding cautiously: `FOX One` suggests that even a distributor-and-affiliate-heavy broadcaster needs a direct subscription surface, but the company is trying to add that layer without abandoning the linear bundle and retrans structure.

## Working interpretation

- Fox materially strengthens the first sub-lane because it shows that attention at national scale still monetizes when it is tied to real-time consumption. In this archive, it is one of the best live-event and live-news counterpoints to the subscription-led logic in The New York Times Company.
- The most important societal pattern here is that audience fragmentation does not eliminate shared attention; it changes where it survives. It survives in sports finals, breaking news, political cycles, and a smaller set of high-intensity viewing moments.
- The strongest repeating pattern is hybrid monetization. Omnicom shows how attention is bought, NYT shows how trust becomes subscription habit, and Fox shows how broadcaster economics now depend on affiliate fees, retransmission, political cycles, sports rights, AVOD growth, and selected direct-to-consumer expansion all at once.
- For this frontier, Fox also makes clear that the erosion story around media is incomplete. Legacy distribution is under pressure, but the companies that still own appointment-based content, premium ad inventory, and nationally recognizable brands retain meaningful pricing and relevance.
