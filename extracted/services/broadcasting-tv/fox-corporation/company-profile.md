## What changed

- Lane fit: Fox is one of the strongest broadcasting anchors available for this frontier because the company is built around television-scale news, sports, local stations, Tubi, and the FOX broadcast network even though AnnualReports classifies it more broadly under `Entertainment - Diversified`.
- Business model: the `2025` annual filing frames Fox around live and `appointment-based` programming, broad distribution across traditional and digital platforms, and a revenue stack driven by affiliate fees plus advertising sales.
- Brand and platform mix: the annual report positions FOX News, FOX Sports, Tubi Media Group, FOX Entertainment, and FOX Television Stations as a portfolio designed to keep cultural relevance while monetizing both linear and digital viewing.
- Streaming and audience shift: the annual filing says Tubi attracts a young, diverse, and highly engaged audience to a library of nearly `300,000` movies and television episodes, while the company also prepared to launch the direct-to-consumer service `FOX One` by fall `2025`.
- Quarter progression: FY2026 Q2, Q3, and Q4 show how the same franchise can swing with political advertising comparisons, Super Bowl timing, the World Cup, and streaming growth while distribution fees remain the ballast underneath.

## Open questions

- How durable is the economics of live television scarcity as sports-rights costs keep rising and more viewing shifts toward digital distribution?
- Can Tubi and FOX One deepen direct audience ownership enough to offset long-term linear pressure without eroding the affiliate-fee stack?
- How much of Fox's earnings power should be treated as structurally resilient versus event-driven because of political advertising, major sports calendars, and sublicensing cycles?
