# Nexstar Company Packet

## Why Nexstar belongs in this frontier

Nexstar is one of the best exact-category `Broadcasting - TV` packets for this frontier because it explains a different part of the attention economy than `Fox`. Fox is stronger for national live-event scale, sports-rights leverage, and Tubi's AVOD bridge. Nexstar is stronger for local-station ownership, retransmission economics, political advertising cycles, and the way a broadcaster tries to defend relevance by combining local TV, cable news, multicast assets, The CW, and digital distribution.

That matters because this frontier is not just about whether media companies still monetize audience attention. It is also about how they do it when the audience is fragmented, local trust still matters, MVPD households are shrinking, and political cycles distort results.

## Business role

- Largest local broadcast television station owner in the U.S.
- Retransmission and distribution-fee collector across MVPD and vMVPD relationships
- Political and non-political advertising platform
- Owner of national media assets including `NewsNation`, an `80.8%` interest in `The CW`, multicast networks, and digital news properties
- Expanding consolidator through the `TEGNA` acquisition

## Annual report read: FY2025

FY2025 showed a broadcaster whose core economics still depend on distribution and advertising, but whose operating story is increasingly shaped by political-cycle comparisons, network-side restructuring, and asset-portfolio complexity.

Key full-year points:

- net revenue: `$4.949B`
- distribution revenue: `$2.924B`
- advertising revenue: `$1.959B`
- net income: `$83M`
- adjusted EBITDA: `$1.561B`

The annual report is especially useful because it shows how much more stable distribution revenue can be than advertising revenue. Distribution was almost flat year over year, while advertising fell sharply because `2025` was not an election year and political advertising rolled off. That makes Nexstar a strong packet for understanding how retransmission and carriage fees help broadcasters survive cyclical shocks in ad demand.

The `2025` annual report also makes the strategic shape of the company clearer. Nexstar was no longer just a station group. It was operating local TV stations, `NewsNation`, `The CW`, multicast networks, and digital properties while preparing for the `TEGNA` acquisition. This is a media company trying to preserve local and linear economics while extending into hybrid distribution and news brands that can still command attention in a fragmented environment.

## Quarter chain

### Q4 2025

Q4 `2025` is the clearest quarter for reading Nexstar's political-cycle exposure and the limits of broadcaster earnings when extraordinary ad demand disappears.

- net revenue: `$1.289B`
- distribution revenue: `$720M`
- advertising revenue: `$549M`
- net loss: `$(170)M`
- adjusted EBITDA: `$433M`

Key read:

- Non-political advertising improved, but the quarter still fell sharply year over year because political advertising dropped by `$233M` to `$21M`.
- The quarter was pushed into a net loss partly by weaker income from `TV Food Network` and one-time corporate costs linked to the pending `TEGNA` deal.
- The release still showed underlying strategic strengths: distribution agreements covering more than `60%` of the subscriber base were renewed, and `The CW` posted `19%` audience growth with sports-led momentum.

### Q1 2026

Q1 `2026` showed that Nexstar could return to growth before even getting a full quarter of `TEGNA`, while `NewsNation` and `The CW` both supported the broader media story.

- net revenue: `$1.396B`
- distribution revenue: `$837M`
- advertising revenue: `$548M`
- net income: `$160M`
- adjusted EBITDA: `$470M`

Key read:

- Management highlighted record net revenue and said `NewsNation` remained the fastest-growing ad-supported cable network.
- The quarter also showed `The CW` moving toward profitability with sports programming approaching half of total programming hours.
- This is a strong quarter for the frontier because it shows a broadcaster trying to offset structural pressure in linear TV by combining retransmission, sports-led network programming, local news credibility, and distribution breadth.

### Q2 2026

Q2 `2026` is the strongest quarter in the chain for understanding Nexstar's scale advantage because it includes a larger `TEGNA` contribution and a clearer read on how the combined business monetizes attention.

- net revenue: `$1.993B`
- distribution revenue: `$1.116B`
- advertising revenue: `$862M`
- net income: `$113M`
- adjusted EBITDA: `$633M`

Key read:

- Distribution revenue rose sharply and remained the stabilizing core, even while the company continued to face the same long-term pressure of MVPD subscriber attrition.
- Advertising also jumped as political activity accelerated and `TEGNA` added more scale to the combined footprint.
- `NewsNation` remained the fastest-growing ad-supported cable news network, while `The CW` expanded through deals with `ESPN` and `Roku`, making it clear that broadcast assets now need streaming-distribution extensions to stay relevant.
- The release also showed how Nexstar is trying to turn local-station ownership into a broader multi-surface attention system rather than leaving the stations as isolated local assets.

## Frontier interpretation

### 1. Broadcast attention still matters when it is attached to local trust and political urgency

Nexstar shows that TV broadcasting is not only a legacy national-entertainment format. It is also a local trust and civic-information system that can still monetize heavily when elections, local news, and community coverage drive demand.

### 2. Distribution fees are the ballast under volatile advertising

This packet is especially valuable because it makes the split between `distribution revenue` and `advertising revenue` very clear. Advertising is more cyclical and politically distorted. Distribution is steadier, though still pressured by subscriber attrition. The modern broadcaster survives by keeping the second leg strong enough to absorb volatility in the first.

### 3. Local broadcasters are becoming hybrid media bundles

Nexstar is not defending itself with stations alone. It is using `NewsNation`, `The CW`, multicast networks, digital properties, and now `TEGNA` assets to create a broader monetization system. That is a sign that legacy media no longer survives by one-format ownership alone.

### 4. Political advertising can hide or exaggerate underlying performance

Nexstar is a strong packet because it forces the frontier to separate structural broadcaster economics from election-year windfalls. The Q4 2025 collapse versus the 2026 rebound is not just a story of execution. It is also a story of the political-ad cycle and how much it still shapes the economics of television attention.

### 5. Broadcasting is trying to stay relevant by extending into streaming distribution without abandoning linear cash flows

The `Roku` and `ESPN` references around `The CW`, together with the retransmission base and vMVPD growth, show that the better framing is no longer `linear versus streaming`. It is whether a broadcaster can use existing content, affiliate, and local-news assets to remain monetizable across both.

## Why this packet improves the frontier

`Fox Corporation` already gave the frontier a strong TV packet centered on national scale, live rights, and AVOD. `Nexstar` materially improves the lane because it adds:

- an exact-category `Broadcasting - TV` packet
- local-station and retransmission economics
- political-advertising cyclicality
- a different hybrid model combining stations, cable news, a broadcast network, and digital assets
- a clearer read on how local trust and carriage relationships still monetize attention

That makes the `attention and influence` lane more comparative and less dependent on one national-media model.
