## What changed in this window

- Nexstar's TV model became less legible as a pure station portfolio and more legible as a mixed system of retransmission, local and national ads, cable news, a broadcast network, digital, multicast, and pending M&A scale.
- The biggest structural shift in the period was the March 19, 2026 TEGNA acquisition closing, which expanded station count, digital assets, and connected-TV ad exposure through Premion.
- Q4 2025 showed how exposed the business remains to political-cycle comparisons even when non-political advertising is healthier.
- Q1 and Q2 2026 made clear that retransmission and distribution revenue remain the stabilizing core even while subscriber attrition still pressures the bundle.
- The CW and NewsNation mattered as operating assets rather than just side properties: The CW was increasingly sports-led and distribution-focused, while NewsNation was repeatedly positioned as the fastest-growing ad-supported cable news network.

## Open questions

- How much of the recent step-up is durable once the one-time benefits and integration effects of TEGNA normalize?
- Can distribution-fee growth continue to outrun MVPD subscriber losses over a longer window?
- Does local-TV scale remain as valuable if political advertising weakens and streaming shifts more national demand toward different surfaces?
