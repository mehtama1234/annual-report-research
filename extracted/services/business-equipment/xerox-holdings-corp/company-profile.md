## What changed

- Business model mix: Xerox said `79%` of `2025` revenue was post-sale-based, anchored in managed print, supplies, service, financing, and related recurring streams rather than one-time box sales.
- Business repositioning: the annual report says the company is shifting from a print-first business toward a software-enabled, services-led company, with IT Solutions and digital workflow capability meant to expand the addressable market beyond legacy print.
- Revenue quality: the Q4 `2025`, Q1 `2026`, and Q2 `2026` chain shows a large gap between reported growth and pro forma underlying demand, which makes Xerox a useful packet for studying acquisition-supported stabilization rather than clean organic expansion.
- Pressure pattern: management repeatedly points to tariff uncertainty, government funding disruption, DRAM cost inflation, lower page volumes, and deferred or weaker equipment installs as operating headwinds.
- Balance-sheet and integration agenda: by mid-`2026`, the company was framing the story around Lexmark synergies, leverage reduction, and gross-margin repair as much as around demand recovery itself.

## Open questions

- Can Xerox stabilize underlying equipment and service demand without relying on acquisition optics to offset secular print decline?
- How durable is the margin improvement if tariff accounting benefits, synergy timing, or channel/product mix normalize?
- Does the IT Solutions and services-led repositioning create a true recurring growth engine, or does it mainly cushion the decline of the legacy print estate?
