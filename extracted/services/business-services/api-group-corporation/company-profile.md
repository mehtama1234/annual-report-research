## What changed

- Revenue: full-year `2025` net revenues were `$7.911 billion`, up `12.7%`, with organic net revenue growth of `7.9%`. Q4 2025 revenues were `$2.117 billion`, up `13.8%`, with organic growth of `11.1%`. Q1 2026 revenues were `$2.0 billion`, up `15.3%`, with organic growth of `10.4%`. Q2 2026 revenues were `$2.3 billion`, up `13.3%`, with organic growth of `10.1%`. This is one of the clearest service-sector examples of durable project and recurring-service demand rather than just cost control.
- Earnings and margins: full-year `2025` reported net income was `$302 million`, diluted EPS was `$(0.69)`, adjusted net income was `$627 million`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.48`, and adjusted EBITDA margin reached a record `13.2%`. Q4 2025 adjusted EBITDA was `$295 million` with a `13.9%` margin. Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was `$235 million` with an `11.9%` margin. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA was `$311 million` with a `13.8%` margin. The margin story is tied to recurring service density, gross-margin improvement, and SG&A leverage.
- Operating model: APi broadens the Services archive into business services tied to safety, compliance, and infrastructure. The `2025` filing describes the company as a global business-services provider of fire and life safety, security, elevator and escalator, and specialty services, with a substantial recurring revenue base and more than `500` locations worldwide.
- Segment structure: `Safety Services` is the larger recurring-service engine, covering fire protection, electronic security, and elevators and escalators across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific, including design, installation, inspection, service, and monitoring. `Specialty Services` covers specialty contracting, fabrication and distribution, and infrastructure and utility services across North America. This makes APi a better bridge between Services and infrastructure intensity than either CHRW or Rollins.
- Scale and recurrence: the `2025` filing says APi had approximately `29,000` team members and provides statutorily mandated and other contracted services to a long-standing customer base. That is analytically important because the service demand here is not discretionary in the same way as many consumer or project-based services.
- Acquisitions and capital deployment: APi is using M&A as a strategic growth lever. Q1 2026 highlighted the closing of CertaSite and signed deals for Wtech and Onyx, representing more than `$1 billion` of investment across those three transactions. The company is pairing organic growth with acquisition-driven expansion of the Safety Services footprint.
- Backlog and visibility: Q2 2026 said backlog exceeded `$5 billion`, which gives this service name more forward visibility than the transport names. APi therefore reads less like a near-term freight barometer and more like a multi-quarter safety-and-infrastructure services compounder.
- Cash flow and leverage: full-year `2025` adjusted free cash flow was a record `$836 million`, with adjusted free cash flow conversion of `80%` and a net leverage ratio of `1.6x`. Management guided to adjusted free-cash-flow conversion of about `115%` based on adjusted net income in `2026`. This is a strong cash-generating service model despite the company remaining active on acquisitions.
- Management tone: highly disciplined and target-driven. Management keeps returning to its `13/60/80` and then `10/16/60+` targets, framing APi as a recurring-revenue and margin-compounding operator rather than a generic contractor.

## Open questions

- How much of APi's current strength is truly secular recurring-service growth, and how much is still being helped by a favorable project cycle in data centers, healthcare, and critical infrastructure?
- Can APi keep margin expansion and free-cash-flow conversion at current levels while digesting a larger acquisition pipeline and preserving service quality across a highly decentralized operating footprint?
