  - quarter 1: FY2026 Q4
  - quarter 2: FY2026 Q3
  - quarter 3: FY2026 Q2

## Annual report takeaways

- Cintas gives the Services sector a fifth company and broadens the archive into route-based outsourced uniforms, facility services, first aid, safety, and fire protection rather than more transport or consumer-home service.
- The `2025` annual package showed revenue of `$10.340 billion`, operating income of `$2.360 billion`, net income of `$1.812 billion`, diluted EPS of `$4.40`, cash flow from operating activities of `$2.166 billion`, and free cash flow of `$1.757 billion`.
- The annual filing matters because it shows that roughly `95%` of revenue comes from route servicing at customer locations. This is a dense, recurring workplace-services network with pricing power, retention, and local execution as the key earnings levers.
- The AnnualReports taxonomy should be treated carefully here. The site tags Cintas as `Services / Apparel Stores`, but the actual filing evidence shows an outsourced workplace-services and safety-compliance platform.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: FY2026 Q4 revenue was `$2.91 billion`, operating income was `$673.0 million`, net income was `$511.0 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.26`, and adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.29`.
- Guidance: on `2026-07-15`, management introduced fiscal `2027` guidance for revenue of `$12.10 billion` to `$12.25 billion` and adjusted diluted EPS of `$5.36` to `$5.50`.
- Management message: management framed the quarter as the close of an outstanding year, highlighted record revenues and operating margins, and said the FTC second request on UniFirst was expected.
- Structural themes: this quarter reinforces Cintas as a service-density compounding machine. The model keeps producing growth through route economics, cross-sell, and pricing while still throwing off large free cash flow.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: FY2026 Q3 revenue was `$2.84 billion`, operating income was `$659.9 million`, net income was `$502.5 million`, and diluted EPS was `$1.24`.
- Guidance: on `2026-03-25`, management raised fiscal `2026` revenue guidance to `$11.21 billion` to `$11.24 billion` and adjusted diluted EPS guidance to `$4.86` to `$4.90`.
- Management message: management emphasized record revenues, all-time-high gross margins in each route-based business, and the value-creation case for the announced UniFirst acquisition.
- Structural themes: the quarter shows that route-based service density and recurring workplace demand can remain strong even without a big macro acceleration. It also introduces M&A risk into an otherwise highly disciplined operating model.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: FY2026 Q2 revenue was `$2.80 billion`, operating income was `$655.7 million`, net income was `$495.3 million`, and diluted EPS was `$1.21`.
- Guidance: on `2025-12-18`, management raised fiscal `2026` revenue guidance to `$11.15 billion` to `$11.22 billion` and diluted EPS guidance to `$4.81` to `$4.88`.
- Management message: management pointed to record revenue, all-time-high operating margin, strong segment growth, and robust cash generation, while keeping the focus on technology investments and execution.
- Structural themes: this quarter is the cleanest early read on the operating model. Cintas is not leaning on one-time demand spikes. It is leaning on route density, price, sales productivity, and recurring workplace-service relationships.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Cintas broadens Services into outsourced workplace operations, safety, hygiene, and uniform-management services.
- Consumer: the direct read-through is weaker to household demand and stronger to commercial occupancy, small-business formation, employment density, and workplace-normalization behavior.
- Cultural: the cultural layer is workplace readiness, safety compliance, clean-and-professional presentation, and outsourced operational reliability for employers.
- Technology: the technology layer is route planning, sales productivity, service density, processing efficiency, and customer cross-sell rather than flashy digital products.
- Labor: labor matters heavily because the model depends on route productivity, plant throughput, retention, and field-service execution across thousands of delivery routes.
- Capital allocation: Cintas is a strong cash generator with continuing buybacks and dividends, and now has a large strategic acquisition question through UniFirst.
- Macro sensitivity: Cintas is a useful indicator for commercial activity and employer operating discipline more than for freight cycles or discretionary consumer demand.
