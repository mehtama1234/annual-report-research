## What changed

- Revenue: fiscal `2025` revenue was `$10.340 billion`, up from `$9.597 billion`, with organic revenue growth of `8.0%`. Fiscal `2026` Q2 revenue was `$2.80 billion`, up `9.3%`, with organic growth of `8.6%`. Fiscal `2026` Q3 revenue was `$2.84 billion`, up `8.9%`, with organic growth of `8.2%`. Fiscal `2026` Q4 revenue was `$2.91 billion`, up `8.9%`, with organic growth of `8.4%`. This is a high-consistency route-based service growth story rather than a volatile demand snapback.
- Earnings and margins: fiscal `2025` operating income was `$2.360 billion`, net income was `$1.812 billion`, diluted EPS was `$4.40`, and gross margin reached `50.0%`. Fiscal `2026` Q2 operating income was `$655.7 million` with a `23.4%` operating margin. Fiscal `2026` Q3 operating income was `$659.9 million` with a `23.2%` operating margin. Fiscal `2026` Q4 operating income was `$673.0 million` with a `23.2%` operating margin, while full-year fiscal `2026` operating income reached `$2.607 billion` and diluted EPS reached `$4.91`.
- Operating model: Cintas is analytically much closer to a route-based outsourced workplace-services and safety-compliance platform than to a retailer, even though AnnualReports labels the industry as `Apparel Stores`. The core engine is `Uniform Rental and Facility Services`, supported by `First Aid and Safety Services`, with `Fire Protection Services` and `Uniform Direct Sale` in `All Other`.
- Segment structure: the fiscal `2025` filing shows segment revenue of `$7.976 billion` for `Uniform Rental and Facility Services`, `$1.218 billion` for `First Aid and Safety Services`, and `$1.146 billion` for `All Other`. The fiscal `2026` filing shows those rising to `$8.622 billion`, `$1.392 billion`, and `$1.251 billion`, respectively. That mix makes Cintas a useful bridge between facility operations, uniforms, hygiene, workplace safety, and fire protection.
- Route density and service recurrence: the `2025` filing says approximately `95%` of company revenue is derived from route servicing performed at customer locations. That makes route efficiency, retention, pricing, and service density central to the earnings model.
- Scale and operating footprint: the AnnualReports page lists `10,000+` employees, while the fiscal `2025` filing shows about `12,100` local delivery routes and `490` facilities. The fiscal `2026` filing shows about `12,500` local delivery routes and `496` facilities, reinforcing the scale of the route network.
- Capital allocation and cash flow: fiscal `2025` cash flow from operating activities was `$2.166 billion` and free cash flow was `$1.757 billion`. Fiscal `2026` Q2 free cash flow reached `$737.5 million` for the first six months, Q3 free cash flow reached `$1.268 billion` for the first nine months, and full-year fiscal `2026` free cash flow reached `$1.881 billion`.
- Management tone: the messaging is consistent: strong customer retention, route productivity, technology investment, and cross-sell penetration are doing more work than macro acceleration. By Q3 and Q4 fiscal `2026`, that tone was combined with UniFirst acquisition framing and still-rising guidance.

## Open questions

- If the UniFirst acquisition closes in the second half of calendar `2026`, will integration complexity dilute the simplicity and service-density advantages that currently make the model so clean?
