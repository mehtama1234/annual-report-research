## Annual report takeaways

- Comcast is the right next CLI 5 extension because it gives the lane a second fixed-access and cable-gateway operator next to Charter, while also adding the extra complexity of media, streaming, and theme-park ownership.
- The taxonomy stack is messy but useful. AnnualReports still places Comcast in `Technology / Entertainment - Diversified`, while the SEC still uses `Cable & Other Pay Television Services`. Neither label captures the full operating reality. Comcast is a connectivity system, an entertainment system, and now a simplification story as it sheds older conglomerate layers.
- The `2025` annual materials show the scale clearly. Full-year `2025` revenue was `$123.707B`, net income attributable to Comcast was `$19.998B`, Adjusted EBITDA was `$37.384B`, net cash provided by operating activities was `$33.643B`, and free cash flow was `$19.235B`.
- The annual filing keeps the burden stack visible. Comcast ended `2025` with `$9.5B` of cash and cash equivalents and `$98.9B` of debt. Connectivity & Platforms capital expenditures rose to `$8.7B` in `2025`, which is a useful reminder that even the broader Comcast system still depends on expensive physical-network upkeep and customer-equipment investment.
- The annual packet also shows the customer transition clearly. Comcast ended `2025` with `31.255M` total domestic broadband customers, `9.305M` domestic wireless lines, `11.270M` domestic video customers, and `64.983M` domestic homes and businesses passed. Broadband and video remain pressured, but wireless continues to scale as the main convergence lever.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$29.940B`, net income attributable to Comcast was `$3.526B`, Adjusted EBITDA was `$8.902B`, net cash provided by operating activities was `$8.092B`, and free cash flow was `$4.604B`.
- Operating read: domestic residential broadband customer net losses improved by `34K` year over year, but the category still shrank by `167K` in the quarter. Domestic wireless line net additions were `448K`, the best quarterly result on record, bringing total domestic wireless lines to `10.187M`.
- Capital and balance-sheet signal: capital expenditures were `$5.253B` for the first six months of `2026`; cash and cash equivalents were `$7.661B`; and total debt was `$90.4B` at `2026-06-30`.
- Capital-allocation signal: Comcast returned `$2.1B` to shareholders through `$1.2B` of dividend payments and `$900M` of share repurchases in Q2.
- Structural themes: Q2 is the strongest current signal that Comcast is trying to turn broadband pressure into a wider convergence story. Wireless is growing fast, Business Services remains strong, Peacock reached quarterly profitability, and the company announced its intention to separate NBCUniversal and Sky. That is a major clue that the old connectivity-plus-content conglomerate structure is under active strategic pressure.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$31.457B`, net income attributable to Comcast was `$2.174B`, Adjusted EBITDA was `$7.929B`, net cash provided by operating activities was `$6.891B`, and free cash flow was `$3.901B`.
- Operating read: domestic residential broadband customer net losses improved by `117K` year over year to `65K`, while domestic wireless line net additions reached `435K`, lifting total domestic wireless lines to `9.739M`.
- Capital and balance-sheet signal: capital expenditures were `$2.351B`; cash and cash equivalents were `$9.468B`; and total debt was `$94.6B` at `2026-03-31`.
- Capital-allocation signal: Comcast returned `$2.5B` to shareholders through `$1.2B` of dividends and `$1.3B` of share repurchases.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the cleanest quarter for seeing the post-Versant Comcast model. The company used major live events, Peacock growth, and an improved broadband go-to-market strategy to support a convergence narrative, but the underlying fixed-access pressure never fully went away.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$32.310B`, net income attributable to Comcast was `$2.168B`, Adjusted EBITDA was `$7.900B`, net cash provided by operating activities was `$8.841B`, and free cash flow was `$4.369B`.
- Full-year frame: full-year `2025` revenue was `$123.707B`, net income attributable to Comcast was `$19.998B`, Adjusted EBITDA was `$37.384B`, and free cash flow was `$19.235B`.
- Customer signal: Comcast ended `2025` with `50.766M` total Connectivity & Platforms customer relationships, `31.255M` total domestic broadband customers, `9.305M` domestic wireless lines, and `11.270M` domestic video customers.
- Capital and liquidity signal: year-end cash and cash equivalents were `$9.5B` and total debt was `$98.9B`. Q4 capital expenditures were `$3.7B`, and full-year Connectivity & Platforms capital expenditures reached `$8.7B`.
- Structural themes: the year-end release is the strongest single document for seeing the transition clearly. Comcast still owns a huge fixed-access base, but the old cable-and-content bundle is being reworked through wireless convergence, Peacock, major live-event leverage, and corporate separation.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- CLI 5 role: Comcast adds the second fixed last-mile and household-gateway operator to the lane, which makes the cable-access read more durable and less dependent on Charter alone.
- Consumer and household read: households increasingly treat broadband and wireless as the real core relationship, while video is less central and entertainment is being re-bundled through streaming, live events, and platform surfaces instead of the old cable package.
- Cultural and societal read: reliable connectivity remains a baseline household utility, but Comcast also shows that media and entertainment scale no longer automatically belong inside the same public-company structure as the access network.
- Industrial read: broadband competition, wireless convergence, streaming distribution, Peacock monetization, live-event leverage, and theme-park attendance all interact inside one operator, which makes Comcast a cleaner test of whether complexity helps or hurts access economics.
- Capital-structure read: this remains a large-debt, large-capex, high-cash-flow system. Comcast is less burdened than the weakest utility cases in the lane, but the need to simplify the portfolio suggests that complexity itself became a burden.
- Monetization-system read: Comcast is trying to turn a broadband gateway into a broader wallet through wireless, Business Services, streaming engagement, and pricing simplification, while also breaking apart business lines that may monetize better on their own.

## Working interpretation

- Comcast matters because it stops the archive from reading fixed-access economics through Charter alone. Both companies show the same broad pattern: broadband remains essential, video keeps shrinking, wireless is the main wallet-expansion tool, and the household gateway is still valuable but more pressured than it once looked.
- Comcast also adds a second and different structural signal: corporate simplification. The completed Versant separation and the newly announced NBCUniversal / Sky separation plan suggest that the market is no longer rewarding the older “own the pipe and the content empire” structure in the same way.
- Compared with Charter, Comcast is less of a pure access-and-bundle defense case and more of a complexity-unwind case. The physical network still matters, but the company is also discovering which adjacent assets belong inside the connectivity shell and which do not.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Comcast is strong evidence that one of the most important repeating infrastructure patterns is convergence without clean bundle restoration. The operator can still grow wireless, business connectivity, and selected streaming economics, but the old cable-video-centered structure does not come back. It gets reorganized.
