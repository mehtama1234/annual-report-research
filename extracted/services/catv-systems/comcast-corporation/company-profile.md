## What changed

- Lane fit: Comcast is the strongest current second communications-and-access comparison because it shows the same household-ownership problem as Charter from a larger, more diversified platform.
- Taxonomy reality: AnnualReports currently classifies Comcast as `Entertainment - Diversified`, so this packet is a frontier-fit communications anchor rather than an exact-category AnnualReports `CATV Systems` match.
- Operating reframing: the company now needs to be read through `Connectivity & Platforms`, wireless growth, broadband retention, Peacock, and post-Versant portfolio structure rather than through legacy cable television alone.
- Household account logic: Comcast remains a billing-relationship and network-control company first. Broadband, mobile, WiFi, and platform packaging still define the moat even as content and media assets get reorganized.
- Comparative value: Comcast gives the frontier a second large-scale access operator so the communications lane does not depend only on Charter and AT&T.

## Open questions

- Can Comcast keep broadband and wireless account ownership strong enough to offset mature cable-video erosion and structural media fragmentation?
- How durable is the post-Versant mix as a combined connectivity-plus-premium-content model rather than a legacy cable conglomerate?
- Does Peacock become a real relationship-enhancing platform asset, or mainly a strategic defense layer around sports and franchise content?
