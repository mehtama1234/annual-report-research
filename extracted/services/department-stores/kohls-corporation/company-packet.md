## Annual report takeaways

- Kohl's is a high-value archive addition because it gives the retail-and-services work a weaker middle-position department-store operator instead of another unusually strong ecosystem or brand franchise.
- The annual evidence chain is current even though AnnualReports is not. As of `2026-08-09`, AnnualReports still showed only the `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, while Kohl's official IR and SEC stack already supported the fiscal `2025` annual package for the year ended `2026-01-31`.
- The annual package shows a business that is still shrinking at the top line. Fiscal `2025` net sales decreased `4.0%` to `$14.8B`, comparable sales decreased `3.1%`, diluted EPS was `$2.38`, and adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.62`.
- The strategically important point is not growth. It is reset and stabilization. Kohl's describes itself as a leading omnichannel retailer operating `1,153` stores, and the annual filing shows a heavy effort to rebuild the proposition through value, omnichannel improvements, inventory cleanup, expense control, and the Sephora partnership.
- That makes Kohl's useful for the archive. It is a realistic case where loyalty, beauty adjacency, credit-related economics, and omnichannel work matter precisely because the core department-store model is under pressure.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` net sales decreased `1.7%` to `$3.0B`; comparable sales decreased `1.1%`; and diluted loss per share was `($0.13)`.
- Operating posture: gross margin increased `4` basis points; SG&A decreased `1.6%`; inventory declined `8%`; and borrowings under the revolving credit facility were `$0`, down `$545M` year over year.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because management framed it as the best comparable-sales performance in over four years, but the business still did not return to sales growth. That is the right Kohl's read: operational stabilization first, real growth still unproven.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` net sales decreased `3.9%` to `$5.0B`; comparable sales decreased `2.8%`; and diluted EPS was `$1.07`.
- Operating posture: gross margin increased `25` basis points; SG&A decreased `4.9%`; operating income increased to `$212M`; inventory fell `7%`; and full-year operating cash flow increased to `$1.4B`.
- Structural themes: this is the key quarter for the packet because it closes the year with the full reset logic visible. Full-year sales were still down, but management argued the company ended `2025` stronger than it started through discipline, earnings improvement, and balance-sheet repair.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` net sales decreased `2.8%` to `$3.4B`; comparable sales decreased `1.7%`; diluted EPS was `$0.07`; and adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.10`.
- Operating posture: gross margin increased `51` basis points; inventory decreased `5%`; and operating cash flow improved to a positive `$124M` from a prior-year use of cash.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because it shows Kohl's delivering better-than-expected results without actually fixing the demand problem. Sales were still down, but cost control and cleaner inventory kept the business from deteriorating more sharply.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Kohl's gives the archive a pressured department-store case where value positioning, omnichannel execution, and beauty adjacency are all being used to slow decline rather than to amplify obvious strength.
- Consumer: this is a direct read on the more fragile part of discretionary middle-income retail demand, where traffic is weaker, promotion matters more, and stabilization itself counts as progress.
- Cultural: Kohl's matters because it shows what happens when a retailer still has national scale and loyalty infrastructure but less identity power, less pricing power, and less platform leverage than the winners.
- Loyalty and technology: Kohl's is a good counterexample to simple loyalty narratives. The company has large-scale customer reach, rewards programs, credit-card economics, and omnichannel tools, but those assets do not automatically produce strong sales momentum.
- Business model: the key business-model signal is that Kohl's is trying to widen the proposition through Sephora, better value architecture, omnichannel convenience, and cleaner execution, but the economics are still dominated by a pressured legacy department-store base.
- Competitive positioning: Kohl's is especially useful because it is neither collapsing nor clearly winning. It is a middle case where better discipline can improve earnings and cash flow even while the core format remains structurally challenged.

## Working interpretation

- Kohl's materially improves the archive because it gives the broader retail-and-services work a case that is not driven by clear platform dominance, brand power, or premium experience scarcity.
- The most important evidence here is not only that sales are down. It is that management keeps emphasizing value, cleaner inventories, Sephora, omnichannel improvement, and operational discipline, which implies the company is trying to rebuild the customer relationship from a weaker starting point.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Kohl's is a strong archive addition for understanding how a middle-position retailer can improve earnings quality and balance-sheet flexibility without yet proving that it has solved the deeper demand and format problem.
