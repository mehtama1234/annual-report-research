## What changed

- Kohl's adds a weaker middle-position department-store operator to the archive, which matters because the current retail-service comparison set had too many structurally advantaged systems.
- The annual and quarter materials show a business under ongoing traffic and topline pressure, but one that has improved cost discipline and inventory management enough to partially stabilize earnings and cash flow.
- The packet is especially useful because management is not describing a clean growth engine. It is describing a reset: better value, better experience, cleaner inventory, stronger balance sheet, and process modernization.
- Sephora is one of the most important supporting details. As of the end of fiscal `2025`, Kohl's said it operated `1,153` stores and had a Sephora shop presence in over `1,100` stores, which shows how much of the relationship-refresh effort depends on beauty adjacency and a better in-store proposition.
- Kohl's is also a useful archive counterweight because it shows that loyalty, omnichannel, and brand-partner initiatives can help, but do not automatically overcome the weaker economics of a middle-market department-store core.

## Open questions

- Is Kohl's current improvement mainly cost discipline and easier comparisons, or the start of a durable customer recovery?
- How much of the store traffic and basket improvement can really be driven by Sephora and value messaging versus the broader pressure on department stores?
- Can Kohl's turn loyalty, credit, and omnichannel convenience into a stronger recurring relationship, or is the customer connection still too promotion-dependent?
