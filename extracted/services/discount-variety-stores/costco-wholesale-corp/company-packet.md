## Annual report takeaways

- Costco is a high-priority archive addition because it gives the research base a scaled consumer operator where loyalty is embedded in a paid membership model rather than in classic airline-style points or app-only rewards.
- AnnualReports was still behind as of `2026-08-09`, showing only the `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, while Costco's official investor-relations stack already exposed the fiscal `2025` annual report and the fiscal `2026` quarter trail.
- The fiscal `2025` package was very strong. Net sales increased `8.1%` to `$269.9B`, total revenue reached `$275.235B`, membership fees increased to `$5.323B`, net income reached `$8.099B`, and diluted EPS reached `$18.21`.
- The structural point is more important than the headline growth rate. Costco is not only a low-price retailer; it is a membership-funded warehouse system with unusually strong traffic, repeat behavior, and trust in the value proposition.
- The packet also matters because it keeps the thesis grounded. Costco's economic engine is not abstract "brand love." It is a disciplined loop among perceived price advantage, warehouse volume, membership fee growth, and increasingly strong digitally-enabled shopping behavior.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q3 `2026` net sales increased `11.6%` to `$69.15B`; total company comparable sales were `9.8%` or `6.6%` adjusted; digitally-enabled comparable sales were `21.5%`.
- Operating posture: net income was `$2.19B`, or `$4.93` per diluted share; first-36-weeks net sales reached `$203.37B`; quarter-end cash and cash equivalents were `$18.946B`.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows Costco's value-and-membership model can still accelerate even at very large scale, with digital growth staying strong while warehouse economics remain intact.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q2 `2026` net sales increased `9.1%` to `$68.24B`; first-24-weeks net sales increased `8.7%` to `$134.22B`; total company comparable sales were `7.4%` or `6.7%` adjusted; digitally-enabled comparable sales were `22.6%`.
- Operating posture: net income was `$2.035B`, or `$4.58` per diluted share; first-24-weeks net income was `$4.04B`, or `$9.08` per diluted share.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because it shows Costco's traffic and membership engine still translating into broad-based sales growth across geographies, not only in the U.S.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q1 `2026` net sales increased `8.2%` to `$65.98B`; total company comparable sales were `6.4%`; digitally-enabled comparable sales were `20.5%`.
- Operating posture: net income was `$2.001B`, or `$4.50` per diluted share; the company said it was operating `923` warehouses at quarter-end.
- Structural themes: this quarter sets the tone for the whole trailing window because it shows Costco entering fiscal `2026` with both sales momentum and digital strength already in place.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Costco is one of the strongest archive examples that a paid-membership retail model can create consumer lock-in without looking like a traditional subscription software business.
- Consumer: this is a direct read on value-seeking behavior, pantry-loading, and the willingness of households to pay upfront for trusted low-price access.
- Cultural: Costco matters because it is a rare brand where frugality, scale, and status can coexist. The warehouse trip is both practical and, for many consumers, identity-reinforcing.
- Loyalty and technology: Costco shows a loyalty system that does not depend on endless promotional gamification. The core habit is economic trust, while digital functionality increases convenience and frequency.
- Business model: the model compounds because membership fees add a high-quality revenue stream on top of already large merchandise volume, while digital growth makes the ecosystem more convenient without replacing the warehouse base.
- Competitive positioning: Costco is especially useful for the archive because it demonstrates that "owning the membership relationship" can be as defensible as owning an app surface, provided the value proposition is credible enough.

## Working interpretation

- Costco materially improves the archive because it lets the broader strategy work talk about loyalty in a more precise way. This is not only "customers like the brand." It is a membership-funded retail system where repeat behavior, value perception, and convenience reinforce one another.
- The most important evidence here is not that Costco has a famous brand or a lot of stores. It is that the company keeps converting member trust into growing merchandise sales, rising membership-fee revenue, and durable digital engagement at enormous scale.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Costco is a strong archive addition for understanding how value, membership, frequency, and convenience can combine into a consumer model that is both culturally sticky and financially powerful.
