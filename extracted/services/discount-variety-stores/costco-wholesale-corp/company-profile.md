## What changed

- Costco is a strong archive addition because it is one of the clearest scale retail cases where the business is not just "cheap goods," but a membership cash machine with unusually strong traffic, renewal behavior, and growing digital convenience layered on top.
- AnnualReports was still behind as of `2026-08-09`, showing only the `2024` annual package, while Costco's official investor-relations stack had already moved on to the fiscal `2025` annual report and the fiscal `2026` quarter trail.
- The fiscal `2025` package was very strong. Net sales increased `8.1%` to `$269.9B`, total revenue reached `$275.235B`, membership fees grew to `$5.323B`, net income reached `$8.099B`, and diluted EPS was `$18.21`.
- The quarter set shows acceleration rather than fatigue. Net sales growth ran from `8.2%` in fiscal `Q1 2026` to `9.1%` in `Q2` and `11.6%` in `Q3`, while digitally-enabled comparable sales stayed above `20%` in all three quarters.
- The operating model matters more than any single quarter. Costco keeps compounding through a simple loop: warehouse traffic, price leadership, membership economics, and digital convenience all reinforce each other.

## Open questions

- How much of Costco's recent strength is pure price-value positioning versus a deeper structural membership habit that would hold even if the consumer backdrop improves?
- Does the digital layer meaningfully raise lifetime member value, or is it still mostly a convenience extension around an already durable warehouse habit?
- As Costco keeps expanding internationally, which parts of the model travel best: price reputation, treasure-hunt behavior, executive membership economics, or operational discipline?
