# Dollar Tree, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-09

## Snapshot

- Company: Dollar Tree, Inc.
- Sector: Services
- Industry: Discount, Variety Stores

- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q1 2026
  - quarter 2: Q4 2025
  - quarter 3: Q3 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Dollar Tree is a high-value archive addition because it gives the consumer-and-services work a large discount-variety chain that is neither a membership ecosystem like Costco nor a full omnichannel-media machine like Walmart.
- The `2025` annual evidence chain is current and clean, but only through official sources. AnnualReports still showed `2024` as the most recent package on `2026-08-09`, while Dollar Tree's official IR pages already exposed the `2025 Annual Report` and the fiscal `2025` `10-K`.
- The annual package is really a post-separation reset. Dollar Tree completed the sale of Family Dollar on `2025-07-05`, and the annual materials say all continuing-operations discussion now reflects the Dollar Tree banner plus corporate, support, and other.
- Fiscal `2025` continuing-operations net sales increased `10.4%` to `$19.4B`, diluted EPS from continuing operations increased to `$5.94`, and operating income increased to `$1.7B`. At year-end, the company operated about `9,000` U.S. stores and about `275` Canadian stores.
- The strategically important point is that Dollar Tree's story is not mainly a digital-relationship story. It is a value-architecture and store-format story: the `1.25` opening price point remains the anchor, but management is deliberately expanding multi-price assortment, refreshing stores, modernizing the supply chain, and trying to widen relevance without losing value credibility.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` net sales increased `7.2%` to `$5.0B`; comparable store net sales increased `3.5%`; operating income increased `23%` to `$473.3M`; and diluted EPS from continuing operations was `$1.76`.
- Operating posture: gross margin improved by `120` basis points, adjusted operating income margin expanded by `110` basis points, and the company raised its full-year adjusted EPS outlook to `$6.70` to `$7.10`.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows the cleaner standalone Dollar Tree model starting to work. Growth was driven by a `4.5%` increase in average ticket, helped by targeted price changes and higher multi-price penetration, while traffic was still down `1.0%`.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` net sales increased `9.0%` to `$5.45B`; comparable store net sales increased `5.0%`; gross margin increased `150` basis points to `39.1%`; and diluted EPS from continuing operations was `$2.56`.
- Operating posture: full-year net sales from continuing operations increased `10.4%` to `$19.4B`; comparable store net sales increased `5.3%`; and the company introduced fiscal `2026` guidance for `3%` to `4%` comparable store net sales growth and adjusted EPS from continuing operations of `$6.50` to `$6.90`.
- Structural themes: this is the key quarter for the packet because it closes the year with the clearest message that the business is being rebuilt around the Dollar Tree banner rather than around a dual-banner compromise. Management said it ended the year with about `5,300` Dollar Tree 3.0 multi-price stores.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` net sales increased `9.4%` to `$4.7B`; same-store net sales increased `4.2%`; gross margin increased `40` basis points to `35.8%`; and diluted EPS from continuing operations was `$1.20`.
- Operating posture: the company said its multi-price strategy drove strong momentum, converted about `646` stores to the Dollar Tree 3.0 multi-price format during the quarter, and updated its full-year fiscal `2025` net sales outlook to `$19.35B` to `$19.45B`.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because it shows the underlying mechanism before the strong year-end finish. Average ticket increased `4.5%`, but traffic declined `0.3%`, which suggests the business was already leaning on pricing architecture, mix, and store-format change more than on broad-based trip acceleration.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Dollar Tree gives the archive a national discount-variety operator where the core strategy is value-engineering, multi-price assortment, and banner simplification rather than ecosystem monetization.
- Consumer: this is a direct read on lower-ticket discretionary and consumable demand, trade-down behavior, and how far a value chain can stretch pricing before it starts to erode traffic.
- Cultural: Dollar Tree matters because it shows that the value end of the market is not one thing. Some consumers want warehouse membership and bulk savings, some want off-price branded discovery, and some want a fast low-ticket treasure-hunt trip with visible price discipline.
- Loyalty and technology: Dollar Tree is useful partly because it is a weaker owned-interface story than Walmart, Costco, or Kroger. Technology still matters through supply chain modernization, store refresh, delivery access, and price-mix management, but the moat is not primarily a digital habit loop.
- Business model: the key signal is that Dollar Tree is trying to widen gross profit and average ticket through multi-price architecture while preserving a value reputation rooted in convenience and low absolute price points.
- Competitive positioning: Dollar Tree is especially useful because it sits between stronger traffic winners and weaker middle-market retailers. It is performing better than the structurally pressured middle, but it does not show the same effortless traffic strength as Ross or the same second-layer monetization power as Walmart.

## Working interpretation

- Dollar Tree materially improves the archive because it makes the discount-retail comparison more precise. Walmart shows the full ecosystem machine, Costco shows the membership cash engine, Ross shows the traffic-winning off-price model, and Dollar Tree shows a different value format built around low absolute price points, multi-price mix expansion, and post-separation focus.
- The most important evidence here is not only the topline growth. It is the mix of positive comps with weak traffic and stronger average ticket, plus management's repeated emphasis on multi-price rollout, store conditions, supply chain modernization, and a more connected customer experience. That points to an operator still redesigning the format rather than one simply riding broad demand.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Dollar Tree is a strong archive addition for understanding how the lower-ticket value end of U.S. retail is evolving after inflation, tariff pressure, and portfolio simplification.
