## What changed

- Dollar Tree gives the archive a discount-variety operator that sits between Walmart's ecosystem-scale retail machine and Ross's off-price treasure-hunt model.
- The annual and quarter materials show a cleaner post-separation business. Following the `2025-07-05` sale of Family Dollar, the continuing operations packet is primarily a Dollar Tree standalone-banner story focused on pricing architecture, store standards, supply chain modernization, and multi-price assortment expansion.
- The annual filing says Dollar Tree operated about `9,000` U.S. stores and about `275` Canadian stores at `2026-01-31`, carried its expanded multi-price assortment in the majority of stores, and had over `8,800` stores serviceable through Uber Eats. That gives the archive a large discount network without the same membership-ad-marketplace layer that dominates the Walmart read.
- The packet is especially useful because the traffic pattern is mixed. Dollar Tree is still comping positively, but the quarter trail shows growth coming more from average ticket and multi-price mix than from strong traffic, which makes it a good comparison against Ross's stronger traffic-led momentum.

## Open questions

- How much of Dollar Tree's recent growth is durable format improvement versus one-time pricing architecture gains from the multi-price rollout?
- Can the company keep protecting value perception while pushing average ticket higher through broader multi-price penetration?
- Does the post-Family-Dollar standalone strategy create a cleaner operating story, or does it reduce scale advantages that had been partially hidden inside the combined company?
