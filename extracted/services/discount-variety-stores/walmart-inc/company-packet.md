# Walmart Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-09

## Snapshot

- Company: Walmart Inc.
- Sector: Services
- Industry: Discount, Variety Stores
- Ticker: WMT
- Fiscal year-end: January
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q1 2026
  - quarter 2: Q4 2025
  - quarter 3: Q3 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Walmart is a high-priority archive addition because it broadens the retail-media and relationship-monetization thesis beyond Kroger into scaled mass retail.
- The `2025` annual filing is explicit about the architecture. Walmart says the business is expanding through `membership, advertising, marketplace and fulfillment services, and financial services`, and that these are `mutually reinforcing pieces of our omnichannel model`.
- That matters because it turns the theme from inference into management's own framing. Walmart is not describing advertising or marketplace as side projects. It is describing them as attached businesses that reinforce the full omnichannel engine.
- The official annual-report release adds the operating shape around that strategy. Walmart said fiscal `2026` delivered revenue growth of `5.1%` in constant currency, adjusted profit growth of `5.4%`, and global eCommerce growth of `24%`.
- The annual package is especially useful because it shows the company trying to do more than defend low-price retail. It is building a broader commerce ecosystem where customer traffic, data, fulfillment reach, membership, and ad inventory can reinforce one another.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` total revenues increased `7.3%`; operating income increased `5.0%`; adjusted operating income increased `5.1%`; and operating cash flow was `$4.7B`.
- Operating posture: global advertising grew `37%`; Walmart Connect in the U.S. grew `44%` excluding VIZIO; Walmart U.S. eCommerce grew `26%`; and global membership fee income grew `17.4%`.
- Structural themes: this is the most useful quarter for the downstream page because Walmart directly says growth in global platforms including advertising, membership and marketplace is improving business mix and reinforcing long-term strategy.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` total revenues increased `5.6%`; operating income increased `10.8%`; adjusted operating income increased `10.5%`; operating cash flow was `$41.6B`; and free cash flow was `$14.9B`.
- Operating posture: global advertising grew `37%`; Walmart Connect in the U.S. grew `41%`; Walmart U.S. eCommerce grew `27%`; and global membership fee revenue grew `15.1%`.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because Walmart is showing the relationship layer already contributing at scale through advertising income, membership fees, and strong digital engagement rather than only through core merchandise volume.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` total revenues increased `5.8%`; constant-currency total revenues increased `6.0%`; operating income declined `0.2%`; adjusted operating income increased `8.0%`; operating cash flow was `$27.5B`; and free cash flow was `$8.8B`.
- Operating posture: global advertising grew `53%`; Walmart Connect in the U.S. grew `33%`; Walmart U.S. eCommerce grew `28%`; and management said it was expanding the ecosystem in higher-margin areas such as digital advertising and marketplace.
- Structural themes: this quarter is the clean setup quarter because the `10-Q` explicitly frames digital advertising and marketplace as higher-margin ecosystem growth areas before the stronger `Q4 2025` and `Q1 2026` mix evidence arrives.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Walmart gives the archive a scaled mass-retail operator where omnichannel traffic is increasingly monetized through advertising, membership, marketplace, and service layers.
- Consumer: this is a useful read on how routine household spending and convenience behavior can support a much broader profit system than the weekly basket alone.
- Cultural: Walmart matters because high-frequency value shopping is one of the largest repeated consumer behaviors in the economy. That makes the owned relationship unusually valuable when paired with data, delivery speed, and platform reach.
- Loyalty and technology: Walmart is a strong filing-backed example that loyalty in modern retail does not need to look like classic points. It can look like convenience, fulfillment density, membership, and an ad network built on commerce behavior.
- Business model: the key signal is that Walmart is trying to make the commerce relationship more valuable by adding higher-return businesses that ride on top of the same retail traffic and infrastructure.
- Competitive positioning: Walmart is especially useful because the company repeatedly describes advertising, marketplace, and membership as ecosystem pieces rather than isolated line items.

## Working interpretation

- Walmart materially improves the archive because it lets the broader strategy work say something more precise than "retailers can sell ads." Walmart's own annual and quarter materials present advertising, membership, marketplace, fulfillment, and financial services as a reinforcing ecosystem around the retail relationship.
- The most important evidence here is not a loose analogy to tech platforms. It is Walmart's own language that these higher-return offerings are part of the omnichannel model, combined with quarter releases showing `37%` to `53%` advertising growth and strong membership and eCommerce growth inside the same window.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Walmart is a strong archive addition for understanding how a dominant value retailer can turn traffic, convenience, first-party commerce behavior, and fulfillment density into a second layer of profit beyond plain merchandise margin.
