## What changed

- Best Buy adds a cleaner electronics-retail operator to the archive, which was useful because the current retail read had a gap between Amazon's ecosystem scale and Nike's brand-channel tradeoffs.
- The annual and quarter materials show a mixed but improving demand picture rather than a clean growth story. FY26 revenue was `$41.691B`, the company returned to positive full-year comparable sales of `0.5%`, and Q1 FY27 then opened with `2.0%` comparable-sales growth.
- The packet is especially useful because the important operating logic is not only category demand. Best Buy keeps emphasizing omnichannel execution, services, Marketplace, and Best Buy Ads as the pieces that can make a mature electronics chain more resilient and more monetizable.
- The company also provides a useful counterweight because the economics are less structurally dominant than Amazon's. Category strength in computing, gaming, and mobile phones helped `Q3 FY26` and `Q1 FY27`, but holiday softness in appliances and home theater still showed up in `Q4 FY26`.
- Best Buy Health is also an important reality check. In `Q3 FY26`, the company recorded `$192M` of pre-tax non-cash Best Buy Health impairments, showing that not every adjacency has worked and that new profit streams still have to survive harder end-market and execution tests.

## Open questions

- Can Marketplace and Best Buy Ads become meaningful enough to offset the cyclicality and margin pressure of core electronics retail?
- How durable is the recent comparable-sales improvement once the strongest gaming and mobile upgrade cycles normalize?
- Does Best Buy's service layer and omnichannel support structure create a real moat, or mostly a softer buffer against category volatility?
