## What changed

- Business model: RH gives the lifestyle lane a luxury home-and-design operator that sells aspiration, hosted space, and aesthetic authority rather than only furniture units.
- Demand exposure: the packet makes RH one of the clearest reads in the repo on how luxury housing turnover, mortgage rates, and big-ticket financing sensitivity hit a supposedly premium consumer category.
- Participation layer: restaurants, wine bars, design galleries, and guesthouse ambitions make RH more like a destination and taste system than a conventional furnishings retailer.
- Cross-lane value: RH broadens the recreation and participation lane into home-as-identity spending, not just travel, gaming, or gifting.

## Open questions

- If housing and financing conditions stay restrictive, can RH's destination and hospitality framing meaningfully cushion the core big-ticket furnishing cycle?
- Is RH's cleaner long-term moat design authority, destination retail, or affluent-customer relationship ownership?
- Compared with Williams-Sonoma and Wayfair, how much of RH's upside really comes from category demand versus a narrower luxury share-capture story?
