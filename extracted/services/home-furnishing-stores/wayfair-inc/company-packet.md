- comparison quarter retained for trend continuity: `Q3 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- Wayfair is the strongest scaled e-commerce home comparison for this lane because it ties category demand to logistics, supplier enablement, and a high-frequency digital merchandising engine rather than to showroom-driven brand aura.
- The `2025` `10-K` says the platform serves `21M` active customers and connects them with more than `22,000` suppliers, many of them small or family-run operations without broad retail access.
- The annual report is especially valuable because it explains the real infrastructure behind online home retail. Wayfair repeatedly frames CastleGate, the Wayfair Delivery Network, and supplier services as the mechanisms that turn fragmented inventory into reliable customer experience.
- That makes the company a strong fit for the user's emphasis on industrial and operating pressures. The packet is not only about home spending. It is also about freight, fulfillment, mobile demand, category share capture, and platform economics.
- The annual report also shows the company broadening beyond one banner through specialty and luxury brands, plus a stated physical-retail and omnichannel strategy.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` net revenue was `$3.5B`, up `7.5%`, gross profit was `$1.054B` or `30.0%` of net revenue, non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA was `$242M`, and diluted loss per share was `$0.01` while adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.95`.
- Operating posture: active customers reached `21.7M`, orders delivered were `10.6M`, and management highlighted nearly `20%` growth in specialty retail brands and more than `35%` growth at Perigold.
- Structural themes: this quarter is important because it shows Wayfair recovering not through broad category boom alone, but through share capture, repeat-order density, specialty-brand expansion, and logistics leverage.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` net revenue was `$2.9B`, up `7.4%`, non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA was `$151M`, and active customers were `21.4M`, up `1.4%`.
- Operating posture: orders per customer rose to `1.88`, and management said the business outperformed the home-furnishings category by a high-single-digit spread.
- Structural themes: the quarter matters because it frames Wayfair as a market-share gainer in a still choppy home category. The company's own argument is that years of investment in logistics, assortment, and technology are now translating into faster growth than the category.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` net revenue was `$3.3B`, up `6.9%`, gross profit was `$1.0B`, non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA was `$224M`, and full-year `2025` net revenue was `$12.5B`, up `5.1%`.
- Operating posture: management said `2025` was the year Wayfair returned to growth and accelerated through the year, with new customer growth, repeat-order strength, and improving profitability all moving together.
- Structural themes: the year-end print is the best single release for seeing Wayfair's core pattern. The company is using supplier breadth, mobile usage, logistics density, and increasingly segmented brand architecture to recover category share without sacrificing margin.

### Quarter minus 3 for continuity

- Results: `Q3 2025` net revenue was `$3.1B`, up `8.1%`, gross profit was `$934M`, non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA was `$208M`, and active customers were `21.2M`.
- Operating posture: orders delivered rose `5.4%` to `9.8M`, repeat customers placed `80.1%` of orders, and LTM net revenue per active customer increased `6.1%` to `$578`.
- Structural themes: this quarter shows that the recovery was already underway before year-end, with order growth, repeat behavior, and profitability all strengthening together.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer trend: Wayfair is a strong read on consumers still spending into home categories when the operator can make selection, convenience, and delivery speed compelling enough.
- Cultural and societal shift: unlike RH or Williams-Sonoma, Wayfair monetizes home expression at scale through digital convenience, mobile usage, and assortment abundance rather than through curation alone.
- Industrial and operating pressure: logistics density, fulfillment efficiency, supplier economics, category contraction, tariffs, and capital structure all matter repeatedly.
- Bigger-picture pattern: Wayfair shows that even in a pressured category, scale and infrastructure can create share gain. The repeated signal is not only demand softness or strength, but the operator's ability to intermediate fragmented supply better than peers.
- Infrastructure theme: this company is especially valuable for the archive because it turns the home/lifestyle lane into a physical-systems story as well as a consumer story. CastleGate and the Wayfair Delivery Network are as central to the packet as merchandising itself.

## Working interpretation

- Wayfair is a strong counterweight to RH and Williams-Sonoma because it shows home participation demand mediated through infrastructure, assortment scale, and logistics rather than through pure curation or luxury signaling.
- The recurring pattern is that category recovery is being won by the operator that can create faster delivery, stronger supplier access, better repeat behavior, and better fixed-cost flow-through.
- For the archive's bigger-picture trend work, Wayfair is one of the clearest examples of a consumer category where physical infrastructure and technical operating systems are inseparable from the demand story.
