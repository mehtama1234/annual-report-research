Date baseline: 2026-08-09

- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q1 2026
  - quarter 2: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Home Depot is a high-value archive addition because it gives the research base a direct housing-upkeep and pro-trade system instead of another generic retail packet.
- AnnualReports was current for Home Depot as of `2026-08-09`, showing the `2025 Annual Report and Form 10K`, and the official Home Depot investor-relations and SEC stack cleanly exposed the fiscal `2025` annual package and the right trailing-quarter chain.
- The fiscal `2025` annual package showed resilient but pressured demand. Sales increased `3.2%` to `$164.7B`, comparable sales increased `0.3%`, net earnings were `$14.2B`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$14.69`, adjusted operating margin was `13.1%`, and adjusted ROIC was `25.7%`.
- The strategically important point is that Home Depot is no longer best read as only a big-box home-improvement chain. The `10-K` centers an interconnected physical-plus-digital customer experience, while the quarter trail and call transcripts show the company building a much broader pro-distribution system through `SRS` and `GMS`.
- That makes Home Depot especially useful for the archive. The economics are driven by repair, maintenance, storm work, professional trade distribution, trade credit, delivery density, and housing-linked project demand rather than only by the consumer basket.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q1 `2026` sales increased `4.8%` to `$41.8B`; comparable sales increased `0.6%`; U.S. comparable sales increased `0.4%`; and adjusted diluted EPS was `$3.43`.
- Operating posture: management said demand looked relatively similar to what it saw through fiscal `2025` despite greater consumer uncertainty and housing affordability pressure.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows the pro-distribution strategy getting bigger even while the core macro backdrop stays restrained. Management said the combined Home Depot plus SRS platform now includes over `1,300` SRS branches, about `16,000` delivery assets, and a cross-sell run rate of roughly `$400M`.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q4 `2025` sales were `$38.2B`; comparable sales increased `0.4%`; full-year fiscal `2025` sales reached `$164.7B`; and full-year comparable sales increased `0.3%`.
- Operating posture: management said results were largely in line with expectations and that, after adjusting for storms, underlying demand was relatively stable throughout the year.
- Structural themes: this is the key quarter for the packet because it closes the year with the clearest evidence that Home Depot is fighting housing pressure with a more durable maintenance-and-pro system rather than with a simple housing rebound thesis.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q3 `2025` sales increased `2.8%` to `$41.4B`, including about `$900M` from `GMS`; comparable sales increased `0.2%`; and adjusted diluted EPS was `$3.74`.
- Operating posture: management said the quarter missed expectations because storm demand did not materialize and housing pressure kept weighing on larger projects.
- Structural themes: this quarter is especially useful because it makes the hybrid model explicit. Demand was soft enough to pressure guidance, but the `GMS` contribution and pro-network expansion show why Home Depot is leaning harder into trade distribution and contractor relationships.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Home Depot gives the archive a service-retail hybrid where the real engine is not just store traffic but a large maintenance, delivery, trade-credit, and professional-distribution network.
- Consumer: this is a direct read on housing-affordability pressure, repair-versus-discretionary project behavior, storm-sensitive demand, and how households respond when large-ticket remodel activity weakens.
- Cultural: Home Depot matters because home upkeep is one of the most practical forms of consumer behavior in the archive. It is not aspirational consumption alone; it is maintenance, weather response, property stewardship, and skilled-trade dependency.
- Labor and industrial overlap: the packet sits close to Industrial Goods because pro customers, delivery assets, trade relationships, and skilled-trades capacity matter directly to outcomes.
- Business model: the key business-model signal is that Home Depot is trying to widen the moat through an interconnected channel system and a larger pro ecosystem rather than waiting for housing to normalize on its own.
- Competitive positioning: Home Depot is especially useful because it shows how a mature retailer can become more infrastructure-like. SRS and GMS push the company deeper into distribution, delivery, and contractor workflow rather than only consumer merchandising.

## Working interpretation

- Home Depot materially improves the archive because it gives the consumer-and-services work a direct bridge into the built environment. The filings show a business shaped by home maintenance, project deferral, storm repair, and pro-trade distribution rather than only by normal retail demand.
- The most important evidence here is not just sales growth. It is that management keeps describing underlying demand as stable but pressured by housing conditions while simultaneously broadening the pro system through `SRS`, `GMS`, delivery capacity, and contractor relationships.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Home Depot is a strong archive addition for understanding how housing-sensitive demand behaves when affordability is tight and the stronger operators respond by becoming more operational, more distribution-heavy, and more deeply embedded in professional workflow.
