## What changed

- Home Depot gives the archive a direct home-upkeep and pro-trade operator instead of another general retailer. That matters because the company sits between consumer repair-and-remodel demand, housing affordability pressure, storm-related demand, and a large professional distribution network.
- The fiscal `2025` annual package was stable rather than booming. Fiscal `2025` sales increased `3.2%` to `$164.7B`, comparable sales increased `0.3%`, net earnings were `$14.2B`, adjusted operating margin was `13.1%`, and adjusted ROIC was `25.7%`.
- The structural point is broader than store traffic. The annual filing says Home Depot is investing in an interconnected customer experience across physical and digital channels, while the quarter and transcript trail show the company widening its pro ecosystem through `SRS` and the `GMS` acquisition.
- The quarter set makes clear why this matters. `Q3 2025` included about `$900M` of GMS sales, and `Q1 2026` management said the combined Home Depot plus SRS platform now included over `1,300` SRS branches, roughly `16,000` delivery assets, over `5,000` sales associates, and a cross-sell run rate of about `$400M`.
- The packet matters because it shows home improvement as a hybrid system: part consumer discretionary, part nondiscretionary maintenance, part professional supply-chain and delivery network. That makes it a better read on the built environment than a plain retail label suggests.

## Open questions

- How much of Home Depot's medium-term growth will come from stable repair-and-maintenance demand versus a real recovery in larger discretionary projects?
- Does the SRS and GMS expansion make Home Depot meaningfully more like a specialty trade distributor than a classic big-box retailer?
- If housing affordability pressure stays high, can pro cross-sell and storm-related demand offset weaker large-ticket DIY behavior for long enough to keep margins steady?
