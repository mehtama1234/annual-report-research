## What changed

- Choice adds a more specific missing layer than Wyndham alone could provide. Both are asset-light hotel franchisors, but Choice is more overtly using extended stay, conversion activity, and segment mix improvement to move the economics upward rather than simply scaling broad economy-and-midscale exposure.
- The company is still a read on practical and lower-price-point lodging demand, but it is no longer just an economy-volume story. The packet repeatedly shows Choice leaning toward higher-revenue extended stay, midscale, and upscale brands, with Radisson, Country Inn, Cambria, and extended-stay growth carrying disproportionate strategic weight.
- Full-year `2025` already showed the pattern clearly. U.S. RevPAR was soft, especially after adjusting for prior-year hurricane benefit, yet franchise agreements awarded rose `22%`, the conversion pipeline expanded, international net rooms grew `12.5%`, and adjusted EBITDA still reached a record-like `$625.6M`.
- The `2026` quarter chain then pushed the thesis further. Q1 `2026` showed a sharp jump in awarded agreements and a first-quarter opening recovery, while Q2 `2026` added stronger U.S. room openings, better fee growth, and a much more explicit lower-capital-intensity posture.
- This makes Choice a strong archive anchor for the consumer and industrial trend work. It suggests that participation demand in lodging is still durable, but a growing share of the winning model comes from owner demand for conversions, franchisee demand for better unit economics, and consumer willingness to trade toward longer stays and practical branded reliability rather than toward luxury alone.
- Choice also sharpens the pressure map. Softer government and international inbound demand, capital-market discipline, pipeline quality, and the need to reduce hotel development and lending outlays all show that even asset-light lodging systems are being managed against a real cost-of-capital and demand-quality backdrop.

## Open questions

- How much of Choice's stronger room-growth trajectory is structural extended-stay demand, and how much is temporary benefit from conversion opportunities created by pressure on independent owners?
- If U.S. practical-travel demand softens again, does Choice's mix shift toward higher-revenue brands materially protect margins better than a broader economy-heavy franchise system would?
- Does the planned continuation of asset sales in `2027` mark a durable strategic shift toward a cleaner fee model, or is it mainly a temporary clean-up step after prior development and lending activity?
