## Annual report takeaways

- Hilton is the right next Services name because it closes the lodging and travel side of the archive's experience-economy gap. Live Nation gave the archive concerts and ticketing; Hilton adds hotel demand, brand-driven lodging, meetings, and travel-linked loyalty economics.
- AnnualReports still lagged here as of `2026-08-10`, showing only the `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, while Hilton IR already exposed the `2025` annual report. That makes Hilton another useful example of why the archive cannot depend on the aggregator alone.
- The `2025` annual materials frame Hilton as an asset-light hospitality compounder rather than a traditional hotel-owner story. The business ended `2025` with 9,158 properties and `1,351,351` rooms across 143 countries and territories, while the development pipeline reached `520,500` rooms.
- The full-year `2025` earnings release said diluted EPS was `$6.12`, net income was `$1,461M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$3,725M`, and system-wide comparable RevPAR increased `0.4%` on a currency-neutral basis.
- Hilton also matters because it translates consumer and business travel into fee growth, pipeline expansion, and capital return. This is a cleaner read on travel and lodging demand than the current archive had before.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` diluted EPS was `$2.10`, net income was `$482M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$1,054M`.
- Demand and growth: system-wide comparable RevPAR increased `3.9%` on a currency-neutral basis, the development pipeline reached `541,300` rooms, and net unit growth was `6.1%`.
- Management message: Hilton framed the quarter around steady travel demand, strong pipeline conversion, and continued brand expansion.
- Structural themes: this quarter reinforces Hilton as a fee-based travel demand read rather than a property-balance-sheet story. The key signals are RevPAR, room additions, pipeline depth, and owner demand to sign more Hilton brands.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` diluted EPS was `$1.66`, net income was `$383M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$901M`.
- Demand and growth: system-wide comparable RevPAR increased `3.6%` on a currency-neutral basis, the pipeline reached `527,000` rooms, and net unit growth was `6.3%`.
- Management message: Hilton raised full-year outlook and highlighted the launch of `Select by Hilton` plus the YOTEL-related brand arrangement as part of continued network expansion.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows that the travel-and-lodging read is not just room-night recovery. It is still about brand-system growth, owner demand, and the ability to compound fees off a larger global room base.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` full-year diluted EPS was `$6.12`, net income was `$1,461M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$3,725M`; fourth-quarter diluted EPS was `$1.27`, net income was `$298M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$946M`.
- Demand and growth: full-year comparable RevPAR increased `0.4%` on a currency-neutral basis, the pipeline reached a record `520,500` rooms, and full-year net unit growth was `6.7%`.
- Management message: Hilton emphasized record pipeline, unit growth, brand launches, and continued capital return.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` makes Hilton a strong archive anchor because it shows travel demand, owner confidence, development momentum, and shareholder distribution all in one fee-driven model.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Hilton broadens Services into lodging, global travel demand, meetings, and loyalty rather than only logistics, business services, and field operations.
- Consumer: this is a direct read on travel and stay behavior, especially premium and loyalty-linked demand rather than everyday household consumption.
- Cultural: Hilton extends the experience-economy theme from event attendance into destination travel, status travel, business travel, and branded hospitality.
- Technology: the technology angle is quieter than in software or ticketing, but still matters through digital booking, loyalty, revenue management, and system-wide distribution.
- Capital light versus capital heavy: Hilton is analytically useful because it shows how much value can be created from brands, fees, and pipeline growth without owning most of the physical real estate.
- International: with more than `1.35M` rooms at year-end `2025` and more than `1.38M` by Q2 `2026`, Hilton provides a global demand read rather than a single-country consumer snapshot.
- Capital allocation: buybacks, dividends, and brand expansion remain central. This is another compounder that converts a global operating network into shareholder returns without the same balance-sheet shape as a hotel REIT.

## Working interpretation

- Hilton materially improves the Services packet because it adds the travel-and-lodging side of the experience economy in a cleaner, more globally diversified way than the archive had before.
- It also helps the broader theme work. Live Nation says consumers still spend on scarce live moments. Hilton says they also still spend on travel, branded stays, and meetings-linked mobility, and that hotel owners still want to build against those expectations.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Hilton is a strong piece of evidence that experience demand remained durable across more than one format, with lodging demand, RevPAR, pipeline growth, and net unit growth all staying constructive into `2026`.
