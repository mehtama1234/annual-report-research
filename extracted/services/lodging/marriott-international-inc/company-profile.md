## What changed

- Services mix: Marriott gives Services a second scaled global lodging operator, which makes the travel lane less dependent on Hilton as the only hotel read.
- Business model: Marriott is a large asset-light fee and brand platform rather than a hotel-owning balance-sheet story.
- Demand read-through: Marriott posted worldwide RevPAR growth of `2.0%` for full-year `2025`, `4.2%` in Q1 `2026`, and `3.4%` in Q2 `2026`, which keeps the travel-demand read constructive even with regional disruption.
- Growth algorithm: Marriott expanded from `9,805` properties and `1,779,936` rooms at year-end `2025` to `10,082` properties and `1,813,698` rooms by Q2 `2026`, while the pipeline moved from nearly `610,000` rooms to approximately `629,000`.
- Loyalty and payments: Marriott Bonvoy is central to the model, with `75%` of U.S. room nights and `68%` of global room nights booked by loyalty members in `2025`, while co-branded credit card fees added `$60M` in Q1 `2026` and `$73M` in Q2 `2026`.
- Consumer / cultural angle: Marriott broadens the archive's experience-economy work into branded stays, meetings, status travel, and repeat guest behavior across a very large global network.

## Open questions

- How much of Marriott's current resilience is broad travel demand versus the specific strength of Marriott Bonvoy, co-brand economics, and branded booking preference?
- Does the pipeline stay this strong if international softness persists, or is the owner-signing engine now durable enough to power through regional shocks?
- Compared with Hilton and Delta, is Marriott more of a loyalty-and-wallet system, or more of a scale-and-distribution system with loyalty reinforcing it?
