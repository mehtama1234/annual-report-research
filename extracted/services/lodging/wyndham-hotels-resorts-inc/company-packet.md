# Wyndham Hotels & Resorts, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Company: Wyndham Hotels & Resorts, Inc.
- Sector: `Services`

- Ticker: `WH`

- Target annual report year: `2025`
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: `Q2 2026`
  - quarter 2: `Q1 2026`
  - quarter 3: `Q4 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- Wyndham is the missing lower-price-point lodging operator the recreation lane needed. Hilton, Marriott, and Hyatt already captured premium, upscale, and broader global brand-system dynamics. Wyndham adds a more economy-and-midscale, everyday-traveler, road-and-value-oriented demand read.
- AnnualReports.com was not current here as of `2026-08-10`, still showing `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`. Wyndham is therefore another clear example of why the archive has to use AnnualReports for taxonomy and archive confirmation, while relying on company IR and SEC for the current annual anchor.
- The `2025` annual and year-end materials frame Wyndham as an asset-light, fee-based hotel franchisor rather than a lodging-owner story. The emphasis is on system growth, franchisee demand, ancillary revenues, pipeline quality, owner returns, and RevPAR stabilization rather than on owned-asset operating margins.
- Full-year `2025` was strategically better than the headline GAAP outcome suggests. Full-year diluted EPS fell `31%` to `$2.50` and net income fell `33%` to `$193M`, but that mostly reflected impairment and other-related charges. Adjusted diluted EPS still rose `6%` to `$4.58`, adjusted EBITDA rose `3%` to `$718M`, system-wide rooms grew `4%`, ancillary revenues hit an all-time high, and the development pipeline reached a record `259,000` rooms.
- Wyndham also matters because it shows that experience and mobility demand are not only premium or aspirational phenomena. Even with softer U.S. RevPAR in `2025`, the brand-and-franchise layer still compounded via room growth, pipeline additions, ancillary revenue streams, and shareholder returns.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: Q2 `2026` net income was `$102M`, diluted EPS was `$1.36`, adjusted net income was `$111M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$212M`.
- Demand and growth: U.S. RevPAR grew `2%`; system-wide rooms grew `4%` excluding Revo rooms; and the development pipeline grew `4%` to a record approximately `261,000` rooms with a roughly `30%` FeePAR premium to existing systems.
- Management message: management framed the quarter around accelerating U.S. RevPAR, stronger ancillary revenues, fee-based business quality, and record openings skewed toward higher-FeePAR midscale-and-above hotels.
- Structural themes: this quarter reinforces Wyndham as a mass-market mobility read. The cleaner economics still sit in the franchise layer, but the demand signal is tied more to everyday domestic travel and value-oriented stay patterns than to luxury or upper-upscale travel.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: Q1 `2026` net income was `$61M`, diluted EPS was `$0.80`, adjusted net income was `$73M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$156M`.
- Demand and growth: system-wide rooms grew `4%`; the development pipeline grew `3%` to over `259,000` rooms and over `2,200` hotels; U.S. RevPAR was flat year-over-year but `250` basis points ahead of expectations; and ancillary revenues rose `21%`.
- Management message: management said U.S. RevPAR recovery in economy and midscale was ahead of expectations and tied that recovery to confidence entering the peak summer season. The company also highlighted FeePAR-accretive development, ancillary growth, and AI-enabled technology differentiation.
- Structural themes: Q1 is important because it shows Wyndham before the clearer Q2 U.S. RevPAR turn. The model still held up through room growth, ancillary revenue, and capital allocation even while domestic RevPAR was only stabilizing.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025 / full-year 2025

- Results: full-year `2025` net income was `$193M`, diluted EPS was `$2.50`, adjusted net income was `$353M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$718M`.
- Demand and growth: system-wide rooms grew `4%`; `870` development contracts were awarded globally in `2025`, up `18%`; the development pipeline grew `3%` to a record `259,000` rooms; and ancillary revenues increased `15%`.
- Management message: management emphasized record openings, room growth, pipeline expansion, free cash flow, and capital return despite continued negative U.S. RevPAR pressure.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` makes Wyndham a strong archive anchor because it shows how a franchisor can still compound when headline demand is uneven. The pressure point was U.S. RevPAR, but the offset came from system growth, owner demand, ancillary economics, and disciplined capital return.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Wyndham deepens the lodging lane by adding a franchise-heavy operator aimed more at the everyday traveler than the premium and upper-upscale hotel operators already in the archive.
- Consumer: the company is a cleaner read on domestic and value-conscious travel behavior than Hilton, Marriott, or Hyatt. It is useful for testing whether lower-price-point mobility demand stays durable even when premium travel narratives dominate headlines.
- Cultural: Wyndham broadens the experience-economy frame. Travel remains socially durable, but not only through premium experiences and status-heavy stays. The lane also has to account for ordinary road travel, family trips, regional mobility, and practical overnight demand.
- Participation and access: Wyndham is analytically useful because it monetizes participation through broad accessibility rather than through scarcity or luxury. That is a different way of capturing the same societal willingness to keep moving, traveling, and spending on stays.
- Industrial pressure: Revo-related fee deferrals, weather disruptions, cross-border softness, China exposure, marketing-fund variability, and lower other franchise fees all show that even the clean franchise layer is not frictionless.
- Capital allocation: Wyndham repeatedly frames value through share repurchases, dividends, free cash flow conversion, higher-FeePAR pipeline additions, and selective balance-sheet refinancing.
- Cross-company pattern: Wyndham strengthens the lane's larger point that relationship control generally earns cleaner economics than hard-asset ownership. It also adds a second pattern: even within brand-control models, the consumer mix matters. Premium lodging and everyday-traveler lodging can both work, but they expose different parts of the social and economic travel system.

## Working interpretation

- Wyndham materially improves the recreation / participation lane because it adds a lodging operator that is still asset-light and fee-based but exposed to a more everyday, economy-and-midscale travel customer than the premium-heavy operator set already in the archive.
- The demand evidence is mixed but improving, which is exactly why the packet matters. Full-year `2025` still reflected negative U.S. RevPAR pressure, but Q1 `2026` stabilized faster than expected and Q2 `2026` turned positive in the U.S. with `2%` RevPAR growth.
- The larger interpretive payoff is structural. Wyndham shows that broad travel participation can still be durable even outside the luxury and upper-upscale tiers, and that the franchise layer can keep compounding via room growth, pipeline quality, ancillary revenues, and capital return even when the consumer backdrop is only gradually improving.
- Combined with Hilton, Marriott, Hyatt, Host, Apple, and Sunstone, Wyndham makes the hospitality lane more complete. The archive now captures premium brand control, everyday-traveler brand control, and multiple forms of underlying lodging ownership rather than flattening all hotel economics into one travel narrative.
