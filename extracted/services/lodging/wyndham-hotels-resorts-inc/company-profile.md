## What changed

- Wyndham adds the missing lower-price-point, franchise-heavy lodging operator contrast to the recreation lane. Hilton, Marriott, and Hyatt lean more clearly into premium, upscale, and broader global business-travel narratives. Wyndham adds the economy-and-midscale everyday-traveler layer.
- The business model is analytically important because it is still asset-light and fee-based, but it sits much closer to ordinary road travel, domestic leisure mobility, and value-conscious stay behavior than the premium-heavy hotel operators do.
- Full-year `2025` showed a mixed but strategically useful picture. U.S. RevPAR was weak, global RevPAR was down, and GAAP earnings were hit by impairment and other-related charges, yet system-wide rooms still grew `4%`, ancillary revenues hit an all-time high, the pipeline reached a record `259,000` rooms, and adjusted EBITDA still rose to `$718M`.
- The `2026` quarter chain then showed improving everyday-travel conditions. Q1 `2026` U.S. RevPAR was flat but ahead of expectations, and Q2 `2026` U.S. RevPAR turned positive at `2%`, which management framed as evidence that economy and midscale demand was recovering.
- This makes Wyndham especially useful for the archive's consumer-trend work. It shows that the experience economy is not only about premium travel, aspirational resorts, or loyalty elites. It also runs through lower-price-point, more domestic, more utilitarian travel habits that still feed fee growth when the franchise system keeps expanding.
- Wyndham also sharpens the operating-pressure picture. Even in an asset-light model, softer U.S. RevPAR, hurricane effects, cross-border weakness, China exposure, Revo insolvency issues, and royalty-fee deferrals can all compress the clean narrative.

## Open questions

- How durable is the apparent `2026` recovery in U.S. economy-and-midscale travel after the weak U.S. RevPAR backdrop visible in `2025`?
- Does Wyndham's lower-price-point franchise model prove more resilient in a pressured consumer environment, or does it simply shift exposure toward weaker ADR and more domestically cyclical demand?
- Over time, does Wyndham's everyday-traveler focus create a stronger read on mass-market mobility than Hilton, Marriott, or Hyatt, or is it too affected by franchise-fee timing and one-off factors like Revo and marketing-fund variability?
