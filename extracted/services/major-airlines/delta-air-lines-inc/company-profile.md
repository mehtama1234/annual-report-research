## What changed

- Delta is a strong archive addition because it gives the loyalty-economy work a direct airline operator where the miles system is explicitly disclosed as a business inside the business.
- The `2025` annual filing is unusually useful. Delta says its award-winning SkyMiles program is designed to attract lifetime members, deepen engagement through a growing ecosystem of partnerships, and deliver high-margin revenue and resilient cash flows.
- The most important hard number is in the `10-K`: remuneration from American Express totaled `$8.2B` in `2025`, and Delta said it expects that to grow to `$10B` over the next few years.
- The annual filing also shows that the mechanism is not abstract. In `2025`, `12%` of revenue miles flown on Delta were from award travel, members redeemed miles for approximately `35M` award tickets, and Delta described the co-brand American Express contract as its most significant and valuable contract to sell miles.
- The quarter trail reinforces the point. In `Q1 2026`, American Express remuneration was over `$2.0B`, up `10%`; in `Q2 2026`, American Express remuneration was `$2.4B`, up `16%`, while loyalty and related revenue grew `19%`.

## Open questions

- How much of Delta's long-term earnings durability depends on co-brand economics staying rich enough to keep the loyalty engine expanding?
- Does SkyMiles become more valuable mainly through card spend and partner sales, or through how it reinforces premium travel demand and brand preference?
- If the airline cycle weakens, does the loyalty ecosystem protect margins enough to justify the stronger "business inside the business" framing?
