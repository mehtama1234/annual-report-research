# United Airlines Holdings, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-09

## Snapshot

- Company: United Airlines Holdings, Inc.
- Sector: Services
- Industry: Major Airlines
- Ticker: UAL
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- United is the right next airline packet because the travel lane already had Delta as the strongest loyalty-currency case, but it still needed a second major carrier to test whether the current travel-and-status conclusions were really industry-level or just Delta-specific.
- The `2025` annual materials show a business with scale and quality that is not mainly disclosed through one giant partner-remuneration number. Full-year `2025` total operating revenue was `$59.1B`, pre-tax earnings were `$4.3B`, adjusted pre-tax earnings were `$4.6B`, adjusted pre-tax margin was `7.8%`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$10.62`, and free cash flow was `$2.7B`.
- The operating message is different from Delta's. United consistently frames the business around winning "brand-loyal customers" through product, network, reliability, and technology rather than around one explicit loyalty-economics engine. The Q4 materials highlighted record quarterly revenue, record annual passenger volume, the airline's lowest seat cancellation rate in company history, and rapidly expanding Starlink Wi-Fi deployment.
- That makes United analytically useful. Delta is the cleaner example of a loyalty currency acting like a business inside the business. United is the cleaner example of a premium-network airline trying to earn loyalty through operational performance, widebody and international breadth, product segmentation, digital utility, and customer-experience upgrades.
- United also sharpens the archive's aggregator-gap lesson. As of `2026-08-09`, AnnualReports still showed only `2024` as the latest annual package, while United's own IR stack already exposed the `2025` annual report and the current `2026` quarter chain.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` total operating revenue was `$17.7B`, net income was `$805M`, diluted EPS was `$2.46`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.99`, pre-tax earnings were `$1.0B`, and adjusted pre-tax earnings were `$843M`.
- Growth and mix: TRASM was up `12.1%`, passenger revenue was up `16.4%`, premium revenue was up `16%`, loyalty revenue was up `11%`, revenue from Basic Economy was up `11%`, cargo revenue was up `23%`, and contracted business revenue was up `27%`.
- Strategic message: management raised full-year `2026` adjusted diluted EPS guidance to `$9.00` to `$11.00` despite nearly `$6B` of added expected fuel cost versus the year's starting assumption.
- Product and operational signal: Starlink had reached `450` installed aircraft with nearly `1,000` expected by year-end; customer satisfaction on Starlink flights was more than twice as high as other Wi-Fi-equipped aircraft; and Newark delivered its best-ever second-quarter on-time departure results.
- Structural themes: this quarter reinforces United as a premium and product-led airline relationship system where customer preference is being built through service reliability, network breadth, mobile and connectivity tools, and cabin segmentation rather than through one narrowly disclosed financial loyalty engine.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` total operating revenue was `$14.6B`, net income was `$699M`, diluted EPS was `$2.14`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.19`, pre-tax earnings were `$870M`, and adjusted pre-tax earnings were about `$500M`.
- Growth and mix: TRASM was up `6.9%`; premium revenue was up `14%`; loyalty revenue was up `13%`; Basic Economy revenue was up `7%`; and business revenue was up `14%`.
- Fuel and capacity discipline: United said first-quarter fuel expense increased by `$340M` year over year, reduced planned capacity by five points for the rest of `2026`, and targeted third- and fourth-quarter capacity to be flat to up roughly `2%`.
- Product and interface signal: management highlighted new premium and economy segmentation, MileagePlus program enhancements, accelerating Starlink rollout, and record app use and digital check-in.
- Structural themes: Q1 is useful because it shows United protecting profitability through network and revenue quality, not just headline traffic growth. The company was willing to trim capacity and still keep investing in brand, product, and customer tools.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` total operating revenue was `$15.4B`, net income was `$1.0B`, diluted EPS was `$3.19`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$3.10`, pre-tax earnings were `$1.3B`, and adjusted pre-tax earnings were also about `$1.3B`.
- Full-year frame: full-year `2025` revenue was `$59.1B`, pre-tax earnings were `$4.3B`, adjusted pre-tax earnings were `$4.6B`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$10.62`, operating cash flow was `$8.4B`, and free cash flow was `$2.7B`.
- Revenue composition: compared with the year-ago quarter, premium revenue was up `9%`, loyalty revenue was up `10%`, and Basic Economy revenue was up `7%`.
- Management message: United emphasized record quarterly revenue, record annual passenger volume of `181M`, strongest quarterly unit revenue for `2025`, rapid Starlink deployment, and brand-loyal-customer gains driven by new aircraft, infrastructure, and technology investments.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the quarter where United most clearly reads as a large-scale premium-network operator trying to turn operational quality, product upgrades, digital self-service, and international-network breadth into a durable customer-preference system.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Travel and status lane: United strengthens the archive because it gives the airline comparison set a second major network carrier where the customer relationship matters, but the mechanism is less "partner-funded points currency" and more "product, network, and reliability compounder."
- Loyalty and customer-control logic: United still uses the "brand-loyal customer" framing throughout the earnings materials, and the quarter trail shows loyalty revenue growing in both Q1 and Q2 `2026`. The difference is that the disclosures tie that loyalty more tightly to product and service execution than Delta does.
- Digital interface: United is one of the better travel packets for seeing the customer relationship expressed through app utility, touchless airport tools, Wi-Fi, digital check-in, bag and security-line visibility, and self-service problem resolution.
- Premium and segmentation: the company repeatedly frames growth around premium cabins, better economy segmentation, improved interiors, international-network breadth, and higher-value customer cohorts rather than only around raw capacity.
- Operational culture: Newark recovery, record or near-record NPS readings, best-on-time-departure streaks, and historically low seat-cancel rates matter because they show how travel preference can be won through reliability as much as through status perks.
- Capital and balance sheet: United is also a useful counterpoint to lighter-weight travel platforms because the filings keep balance-sheet management, aircraft deliveries, and capacity moderation tightly linked to the customer and earnings story.

## Working interpretation

- United materially improves the archive because it tests the airline relationship thesis against a carrier whose disclosures are less centered on one explicit co-brand cash engine and more centered on network quality, premium demand, operational reliability, and product improvements.
- The most important evidence is that the quarter chain consistently shows premium, loyalty, and business revenue holding up while management is also willing to moderate capacity in response to fuel volatility. That combination suggests a stronger operator rather than just a temporarily favorable demand environment.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, United is strong evidence that airline customer relationships can be built through operational trust, premium segmentation, connectivity, app utility, and international network breadth even when the filings are less explicit than Delta's about loyalty as a standalone financial engine.
