## What changed

- United is the right airline follow-on to Delta because it gives the archive a second large U.S. network carrier, but the disclosure emphasis is different. Delta is the cleaner loyalty-currency and co-brand-remuneration case, while United is the cleaner operational-product-and-network-execution case.
- The `2025` annual and Q4 materials show a business that still talks about brand-loyal customers, but frames the win through reliability, premium product, international network breadth, and technology-enabled experience improvements rather than through one dominant partner-economics disclosure.
- The full-year `2025` numbers are strong enough to matter on their own: total operating revenue reached `$59.1B`, pre-tax earnings were `$4.3B`, adjusted pre-tax earnings were `$4.6B`, adjusted pre-tax margin was `7.8%`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$10.62`, operating cash flow was `$8.4B`, and free cash flow was `$2.7B`.
- The quarter trail sharpens the strategic posture. Q1 `2026` showed United staying profitable even with a `$340M` year-over-year increase in fuel expense while cutting planned capacity and still growing premium, loyalty, and business revenue. Q2 `2026` then showed that those revenue initiatives were working: revenue rose `16.0%`, TRASM rose `12.1%`, and management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to `$9.00` to `$11.00` despite nearly `$6B` of added expected fuel cost for the year.
- United is especially useful because the operating message is not just about seats. The filings and earnings exhibits keep returning to premium-cabin segmentation, MileagePlus-linked product design, record or near-record customer satisfaction, Starlink rollout, mobile-app utility, and international-network differentiation.
- United also gives the archive another clean AnnualReports lag. As of `2026-08-09`, AnnualReports still showed only `2024` as the most recent annual package, while the official IR stack was already current at `2025` plus `Q2 2026`.

## Open questions

- How much of United's recent earnings resilience comes from true brand and product differentiation versus a favorable moment for international and premium network demand?
- Does United's customer-relationship layer become structurally comparable to Delta's loyalty-currency model, or is it better understood as an operational-and-product-led premium airline with a strong but less separately disclosed loyalty system?
- If fuel and macro volatility persist, will United's revenue-recovery discipline and capacity moderation prove enough to keep the "brand-loyal customer" framing defensible?
