  - quarter 1: Q2 2026

## Annual report takeaways

- Huron is one of the strongest current packets for the institutional-interface lane because it makes the case that organizations under operational, financial, and technological pressure increasingly buy execution capacity, not just advice. The company sits at the junction of consulting, digital transformation, and managed services, with heavy exposure to healthcare systems, educational institutions, and commercial clients facing cost, labor, and technology strain.
- The `2025` annual chain makes the operating model explicit. Full-year revenues before reimbursable expenses (`RBR`) reached a record about `$1.66B`, up `11.9%`, while adjusted EBITDA reached about `$237.5M`, up `18.1%`. That matters because it shows both top-line demand and operating leverage in a category that often gets treated as generic consulting clutter.
- The annual report and FY2025 release are especially useful for frontier interpretation because they show how the business is changing. Huron repeatedly points to consulting plus managed services plus digital capabilities across all three segments, with healthcare at about `50%` of `2025` RBR, education at about `30%`, and commercial at about `20%`. This is less a pure project-consulting model than an institutional adaptation platform.
- The pressure side is just as important. FY2025 results were shaped by acquisitions, healthcare digital softness offset elsewhere, the divestiture of Studer Education, and the need to keep utilization, hiring, and execution tight enough to preserve margin expansion. That makes Huron a good packet for understanding how institutional buyers are prioritizing outside help while still renegotiating what kind of help they actually want.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` RBR was about `$465.6M`, up `15.7%`; net income was about `$31.2M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$72.6M`; diluted EPS was about `$1.91`; adjusted diluted EPS was about `$2.46`.
- Operating signals: Huron described record Q2 RBR with strength across all three operating segments and across both Consulting plus Managed Services and Digital capabilities. Q2 organic RBR growth was about `10.8%` excluding acquisition contribution.
- Capital and labor signals: Huron returned about `$53.1M` to shareholders in the quarter and about `$208.6M` in the first half through repurchases. Managed-services professionals more than doubled year over year to `3,913`, including about `1,100` added through the `RelateCare` acquisition.
- Interpretation: Q2 is the clearest quarter for why Huron materially improves the frontier. It shows institutions paying for a broader execution stack that includes consulting, technology implementation, workflow redesign, and labor-heavy managed services rather than only strategic advice.
- Bigger-picture read: institutional adaptation is getting more operational and more recurring. The company is growing not just because clients want plans, but because they need outside operators to run or modernize mission-critical functions.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` RBR was about `$443.7M`, up `12.1%`; net income was about `$23.2M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$50.6M`; diluted EPS was about `$1.34`; adjusted diluted EPS was about `$1.73`.
- Demand signals: management highlighted growth across healthcare, education, and commercial segments, including record healthcare RBR, while affirming full-year `2026` guidance and emphasizing pipeline and backlog strength.
- Mix signals: the increase included about `$19.3M` of acquisition-driven RBR, but organic RBR still grew about `7.3%`. Managed-services professionals in healthcare rose sharply year over year, and management emphasized an increasingly AI-enabled client environment.
- Interpretation: Q1 is the cleanest proof that Huron's growth is not only a late-2025 artifact. It shows continued demand for transformation and operating support across multiple institutional end markets.
- Bigger-picture read: clients appear willing to keep spending when the outside provider combines industry expertise with digital and managed-services delivery. That is a strong signal that outsourced institutional capability remains in demand even in a tighter macro environment.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` RBR was about `$432.3M`, up `11.3%`; net income was about `$30.7M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$68.0M`; diluted EPS was about `$1.72`; adjusted diluted EPS was about `$2.17`.
- Full-year signals: FY`2025` RBR was about `$1.66B`, up `11.9%`; net income was about `$105.0M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$237.5M`; adjusted diluted EPS was about `$7.83`.
- Strategic signals: management emphasized record healthcare and commercial RBR in Q4, continued digital strength in commercial and education, and the idea that client complexity around AI, operations, and end-market pressure would keep creating long-term demand for Huron's services.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY2025 is the quarter that makes Huron a frontier-quality institutional packet rather than just another consulting name. It shows the company producing real scale, improving margins, and expanding recurring capability in markets where clients are under structural pressure to adapt.
- Bigger-picture read: the institutional-services lane is not just about selling advice to willing buyers. It is about selling execution into constrained, stressed, and technologically shifting systems that cannot move fast enough on their own.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Institutions are increasingly buying operating capacity, not just strategic framing: Huron's mix across consulting, digital, and managed services makes this explicit.
- Healthcare, education, and commercial transformation are converging around the same core need: outside partners who can modernize workflows, absorb complexity, and provide labor or delivery depth.
- Managed services are becoming a bigger part of the professional-services frontier: Huron shows how recurring workflow support can sit beside higher-margin advisory and digital work.
- AI appears here as an institutional pressure and implementation theme, not as hype language alone: management repeatedly frames AI as part of how clients are changing their business models and why Huron's expertise remains relevant.
- Acquisitions are helping scale the model, but Huron repeatedly discloses organic growth separately, which helps show that real market demand remains strong underneath the portfolio moves.
- Margin expansion in professional services increasingly depends on utilization discipline, mix improvement, and delivery leverage rather than only on top-line project growth.

## Working interpretation

- Huron materially deepens the third sub-lane because it adds a different management-services model from both `ICF International` and `Korn Ferry`. `ICF` is more implementation-heavy around regulated and public-sector programs; `Korn Ferry` is more talent and leadership infrastructure; Huron is more enterprise and institutional transformation plus managed-services execution.
- The strongest repeated pattern is that institutional stress is being monetized through hybrid service stacks. Huron wins when clients need strategy, workflow redesign, technology change, staffing depth, and recurring operations support together rather than one narrow service line.
- The cultural and industrial signal underneath the numbers is that institutions are not adapting in-house fast enough. Health systems, universities, and large commercial organizations are turning to external firms that can translate complexity into operating throughput.
- For this frontier, Huron also makes the `Adtalem`, `Grand Canyon Education`, `Forrester`, `Gartner`, `ICF`, and `Korn Ferry` cluster more defensible. Those packets together now show institutions buying workforce formation, interpreted judgment, execution infrastructure, talent access, and transformation labor through distinct but connected business models.
