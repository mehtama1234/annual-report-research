## What changed in this window

- Huron's mix kept moving toward a broader transformation platform spanning consulting, digital, and managed services rather than a narrower advisory model.
- Healthcare remained the largest segment, but the quarter chain repeatedly shows strength across healthcare, education, and commercial clients rather than dependence on one end market.
- Managed-services scale became more important to the frontier read, especially in healthcare revenue-cycle and education research-administration workflows.
- Acquisitions remained part of the growth story, but management repeatedly separated organic and acquired growth, which makes the packet useful for distinguishing true demand strength from portfolio expansion.
- AI showed up less as a standalone product story and more as a client-pressure and workflow-acceleration theme embedded in delivery, backlog, guidance, and competitive positioning.

## Open questions

- How durable is Huron's current margin expansion if utilization softens or if institutional clients delay large-scale transformation work?
- Can Huron keep converting acquisitions into higher recurring managed-services economics without diluting its consulting margins?
- Does AI deepen demand for outside execution partners, or does it eventually compress parts of the professional-services labor model Huron depends on?
