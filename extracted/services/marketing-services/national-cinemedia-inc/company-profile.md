## What changed

- Taxonomy and lane fit: National CineMedia is an exact-category `Marketing Services` packet, but its economics are closer to physical-world attention infrastructure than to generic agency or software categories.
- Revenue logic: the business monetizes cinema attendance, advertiser demand, and inventory utilization rather than household subscriptions or always-on digital engagement.
- Operating leverage: the quarter chain shows sharp fixed-cost sensitivity. Q4 produces strong profitability when advertiser demand and movie cadence cooperate, while Q1 reverts quickly to losses under seasonal and event-driven pressure.
- Audience thesis: management consistently frames the company as a premium-video and full-funnel platform built around young, diverse audiences, scaled inventory, and data-driven measurement.
- Demand signal quality: the Q3 and Q4 releases are especially useful because they show revenue and advertiser demand outperforming box-office or attendance trends, which means the company is not only a passive theater-volume proxy.
- Quarter cutoff: the correct latest reported quarter as of Monday, `2026-08-10` is Q1 `2026`, not Q2 `2026`, because the company had only scheduled the Q2 release for Tuesday, `2026-08-11`.

## Open questions

- How durable is advertiser demand if the film slate weakens or attendance becomes less predictable again?
- Can NCM keep increasing revenue per attendee fast enough to offset the underlying cyclicality of theatrical traffic?
- Does the AI and efficiency program mainly cut cost, or can it also improve sell-through, targeting, and category breadth?
- How much of the business is a true differentiated attention surface versus a niche media format competing for the same macro ad budgets as digital video and live events?
