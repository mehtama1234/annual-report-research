# Netflix, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Netflix, Inc.
- Sector: Services
- Industry: Music & Video Stores
- Ticker: NFLX
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Netflix is a high-value archive addition because it gives the repo a scaled recurring-access cultural operator where monetization comes from subscription habit, recommendation quality, advertising, and global entertainment relevance rather than physical retail or enterprise workflow.
- The evidence chain also matters operationally. On `2026-08-09`, direct shell access to both Netflix's main IR HTML pages and AnnualReports.com was unreliable, but the official static-file chain and SEC filings still supported a complete `2025` annual and trailing-quarter packet.
- The `2025` annual package shows a very large and profitable consumer-attention business. Full-year revenue was about `$45.2B`, operating margin was `29.5%`, ad revenue rose more than `2.5x` to over `$1.5B`, the service crossed `325M` paid memberships in Q4, and full-year free cash flow was about `$9.5B`.
- Netflix is especially useful for this archive because it is not only media exposure. It is a recurring consumer utility for entertainment and a clear case where culture, habit, interface quality, and monetization layers reinforce one another.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$12.560B`, operating income was `$4.193B`, operating margin was `33.4%`, net income was `$3.401B`, and diluted EPS was `$0.80`.
- Operating detail: free cash flow was `$1.525B`, EMEA revenue moved above `$4.0B`, LATAM and APAC each moved above `$1.5B`, and the company said members watched more than `97 billion` hours in the first half of `2026`.
- Management message: management emphasized durable double-digit growth, strong regional breadth, and a narrower `2026` revenue guide of `$51.0B-$51.4B` while maintaining operating-margin guidance of `31.5%`.
- Structural themes: Q2 is useful because it shows Netflix compounding without a single-region dependency and continuing to grow the ads layer alongside the core subscription engine.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$12.250B`, operating income was `$3.957B`, operating margin was `32.3%`, net income was `$5.283B`, and diluted EPS was `$1.23`.
- Operating detail: free cash flow was `$5.094B`, ad revenue remained on track to reach `$3B` in `2026`, and management said the service was still approaching a global audience of one billion people.
- Management message: the key message was that Netflix still saw large monetization headroom through ad growth, broad household penetration runway, and expansion of entertainment formats around the core service.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the cleanest bridge between the strong `2025` finish and the `2026` monetization plan because it combines continuing revenue growth with explicit confidence in the ad ramp.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$12.051B`, operating income was `$2.957B`, operating margin was `24.5%`, net income was `$2.419B`, diluted EPS was `$0.56`, and free cash flow was `$1.872B`.
- Operating detail: full-year `2025` revenue was `$45.2B`, full-year operating margin was `29.5%`, full-year free cash flow was `$9.5B`, and ad revenue rose to over `$1.5B`.
- Management message: management framed `2025` as a year of strong growth, margin expansion, and increasing monetization breadth, with the paid-membership base crossing `325M` during the quarter.
- Structural themes: this is the defining quarter for the packet because it closes the annual-report year with the clearest combination of consumer habit, pricing power, growing ad inventory, and scale-driven profitability.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Netflix broadens Services beyond logistics, field services, travel, and staffing into a direct recurring entertainment-and-attention operator.
- Consumer: Netflix is one of the clearest archive cases where consumer habit and subscription continuity become a durable monetization base.
- Cultural: the cultural signal is strong because Netflix sits at the center of global entertainment discovery, fandom, releases, live programming experiments, and shared viewing behavior.
- Advertising and monetization depth: Netflix matters because it shows a subscription business adding an increasingly meaningful ad layer rather than relying only on price increases or volume.
- Competitive frame: Netflix is useful for comparing how consumer attention is contested across streaming, social media, gaming, live experiences, and other leisure formats.
- Platform extension: the quarter letters suggest continued expansion into live, games, and adjacent entertainment formats, which makes Netflix relevant to broader questions about what a paid cultural platform becomes after streaming scale is already achieved.

## Working interpretation

- Netflix materially improves the archive because it adds a scaled consumer-attention operator where retention, recommendation, pricing, advertising, and cultural relevance all compound together inside one recurring product.
- The clearest lesson is not only that Netflix is large. It is that paid consumer habit can still broaden into ads, live programming, and other entertainment extensions without losing the coherence of the core subscription model.
- For the `2025` through `2026` collection window, Netflix is one of the strongest names for connecting consumer behavior, culture, monetization layering, and platform-like entertainment reach in a single packet.
