## What changed

- Revenue: full-year `2025` revenues were `$3.8 billion`, up `11.0%`, with organic revenues up `6.9%` and acquisition-related revenues up `4.1%`. Q4 2025 revenues were `$913 million`, up `9.7%`. Q1 2026 revenues were `$906 million`, up `10.2%`. Q2 2026 revenues were `$1.1 billion`, up `7.9%`. This is steady route-based service growth, but the growth cadence became more uneven by Q2 because consumer-initiated demand softened.
- Earnings and margins: full-year `2025` operating income was `$726 million`, net income was `$527 million`, diluted EPS was `$1.09`, and adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.12`. Q4 2025 operating income was `$160 million`; Q1 2026 was `$145 million`; Q2 2026 was `$201 million`. Margins remained solid, but they compressed in the in-scope quarters because insurance, claims, people costs, and selling investments ran ahead of the softer near-term demand profile.
- Business mix and recurrence: Rollins broadens the Services sample beyond transport. AnnualReports describes the company as a pest and termite control provider serving residential and commercial customers across North America and several international regions. Management said the recurring base of business plus ancillary service lines represented more than `80%` of total revenue and grew more than `7%` organically in both Q4 2025 and full-year 2025. That makes Rollins a useful read on recurring household and commercial field-service demand rather than freight flows.
- Demand channels: the quarter chain shows a services company whose demand depends heavily on channel quality. Q1 2026 improved sharply through March, with strong exit velocity. Q2 2026 then showed slower residential growth in brands more dependent on search, digital media, and inbound consumer leads, while home-builder and door-to-door channels held up better. This is a more consumer- and housing-adjacent demand picture than CHRW or UPS.
- Capital allocation and acquisitions: Rollins remains acquisitive and cash generative. Full-year `2025` operating cash flow was `$678 million`, with `$310 million` invested in acquisitions and `$328 million` paid in dividends. Q1 2026 included `$18 million` of acquisitions and Q2 2026 included `$117 million`, while management kept describing capital allocation as balanced and disciplined.
- Balance sheet and leverage: the quarterly filings show `$500 million` of long-term debt outstanding at both `2026-03-31` and `2026-06-30`, with leverage still modest at `0.9x` in Q1 and `1.0x` in Q2. This gives the Services archive a high-recurring, acquisition-supported compounder with a more conservative leverage profile than many consolidators.
- Weather and execution sensitivity: management explicitly called erratic weather a drag on one-time and seasonal work in Q4 2025, then pointed to insurance and claims pressure in Q1 2026 and weaker consumer lead volume in Q2 2026. Rollins therefore reads as a service operator where local execution, staffing, and channel quality matter as much as headline macro demand.
- Management tone: disciplined and operational. Unlike CHRW's freight-share narrative or UPS's network-reset story, Rollins is presenting a quieter compounder model built on recurring route density, pricing, modernization, acquisitions, and local execution.

## Open questions

- Does the Q2 2026 consumer-lead softness prove temporary, or is it the start of a more persistent residential demand slowdown for the more digitally sourced parts of the business?
- Can Rollins restore margin expansion while continuing to invest in demand generation, local staffing, and acquisitions if growth stays slower than the cost structure expected entering peak season?
