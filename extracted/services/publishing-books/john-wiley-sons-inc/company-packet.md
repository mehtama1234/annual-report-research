  - quarter 1: FY2026 Q4

## Annual report takeaways

- Wiley is a strong attention-and-influence packet for this frontier because it shows that publishing books is no longer just about selling units of content. The business is increasingly about controlling authoritative knowledge, institutional distribution, recurring research relationships, and the rights to license high-value intellectual property into AI and corporate-workflow systems.
- The `2025` annual report makes the mix shift clear. Research accounted for about `64%` of consolidated revenue in fiscal `2025`, while Learning accounted for about `35%`. Research generated about `96%` of its revenue from digital and online products and services, and Learning generated about `60%` from digital and online products and services. That means Wiley belongs in this frontier not as a nostalgic book packet, but as a knowledge-infrastructure and institutional-interface packet.
- The annual strategy language is the key signal. Wiley says its Research segment publishes over `1,800` academic journals and is built around journal subscriptions, transformational agreements, open access, licensing, and digital information services for researchers, authors, societies, and corporate customers. That is an unusually direct example of trust, curation, and domain authority becoming monetizable infrastructure.
- The annual package also shows the company simplifying itself around higher-value knowledge assets. University Services had already been sold, most of Wiley Edge had already been sold, and CrossKnowledge had been sold. That matters because management is deliberately exiting more labor-intensive or lower-conviction education-service activities and tightening around Research and a narrower Learning business.
- The broader frontier read is that Wiley sits at the intersection of information trust, institutional spending, and IP monetization. Management explicitly frames growth around open access, recurring multi-year licenses, content licensing for science and innovation applications, and high-value digital courseware and assessments. This is one of the clearest packets in the archive for how specialized knowledge gets repackaged into multiple monetization forms once the underlying content base is authoritative enough.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: FY2026 Q4 revenue was about `$448M`, up `1%` as reported and flat at constant currency; diluted EPS was about `$2.61`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$149M` with margin up to `33.2%`.
- Segment signals: `Research` revenue was about `$296M`, up `5%` as reported and `4%` at constant currency; `Learning` revenue was about `$152M`, down `6%` as reported and `7%` at constant currency.
- Full-year FY2026 signals: revenue was about `$1.677B`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$440M`; adjusted EPS was about `$4.19`; operating cash flow was about `$261M`; free cash flow was about `$195M`; AI revenue reached about `$49M`, up `23%`.
- Management signal: Matthew Kissner called fiscal `2026` Wiley’s breakout year, said the company was accelerating two reinforcing growth engines, `Research` and `AI and data analytics`, and highlighted the post-year-end Emerald Publishing acquisition as a way to increase scale and proprietary content advantage in the AI economy.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY2026 turns Wiley from a category placeholder into a flagship packet. It shows a publisher moving beyond content sales into recurring research contracts, open-access economics, AI licensing, and scale consolidation in specialized knowledge.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter belongs in the frontier because it shows franchise/IP monetization directly. Wiley is not just selling books and journals; it is licensing the corpus, the workflow position, and the credibility of the content stack into AI and institutional decision systems.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: FY2026 Q3 revenue was about `$410M`; adjusted operating income was about `$70M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$105M`; adjusted EPS was about `$0.97`.
- Segment signals: `Research` revenue was about `$274M`, up `2%` as reported and `1%` at constant currency; `Learning` revenue was about `$136M`, down `1%` as reported and `2%` at constant currency.
- AI and cash-flow signals: Wiley realized about `$7M` of AI revenue in the quarter and about `$42M` year-to-date. Year-to-date operating cash flow improved to about `$103M`, while free cash flow improved to about `$56M` from a prior-year use of cash.
- Management signal: the company said Research Publishing would have grown `4%` at constant currency excluding an unfavorable prior-year AI comparison and highlighted a recurring-revenue agreement with OpenEvidence after quarter close.
- Interpretation: Q3 is the cleanest quarter for seeing the split between institutional knowledge demand and softer end-market learning demand. Research remains resilient because universities, funders, and corporate R&D buyers still need access, publishing, and licensing infrastructure; Learning remains softer because it is exposed to retail and professional-spending conditions.
- Bigger-picture read: the quarter sharpens a central frontier theme. Not all information businesses are under the same pressure. Specialized, institutional, workflow-embedded knowledge can stay resilient even while more consumer-adjacent or discretionary learning formats remain sluggish.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: FY2026 Q2 revenue was about `$422M`; GAAP operating income was about `$73M`; adjusted operating income was about `$79M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$115M`; adjusted EPS was about `$1.10`.
- Segment signals: `Research` revenue was about `$279M`, up `6%` as reported and `5%` at constant currency; `Learning` revenue was about `$143M`, down `11%`.
- AI and operating signals: Wiley executed about `$6M` of content-licensing revenue for AI model training in the quarter and about `$35M` year-to-date. Research benefited from strong global demand to publish, read, and license, while Learning was hit by an online-retailer inventory drop, slower consumer and corporate spending, and print declines outweighing digital growth.
- Management signal: Kissner said the company was seeing strong performance in Research and accelerating momentum in AI, with defensible growth in recurring revenue and open-access models.
- Interpretation: Q2 is the quarter that best reveals the operating contrast inside Wiley. Research behaves like durable institutional infrastructure. Learning behaves more like a market-sensitive educational and professional-content business.
- Bigger-picture read: this is a valuable societal and industrial signal for the frontier because it shows which kinds of knowledge still command budget priority. Institutions keep paying for authoritative research access and publishing throughput; discretionary or channel-dependent learning demand is more fragile.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Authoritative knowledge is becoming licensable infrastructure: Wiley is one of the clearest current packets for showing that trusted domain content can be monetized not just through subscriptions and sales but through AI training, data partnerships, and application-layer licensing.
- Institutional demand is stronger than consumer-adjacent demand: the `Research` segment keeps growing through subscriptions, transformational agreements, open access, and licensing, while `Learning` remains more exposed to retail softness, corporate spending caution, and print pressure.
- Open access is not just a mission story; it is a revenue model: Wiley repeatedly links growth to author-funded open access alongside recurring subscriptions and multi-year agreements. That makes the company a useful case for how trust and access models are being repriced rather than abandoned.
- AI monetization is already material but still evolving: the quarter chain shows AI revenue moving from one-off licensing projects toward recurring arrangements and vertical partnerships. That gives the frontier a direct example of knowledge-IP monetization rather than only generic AI commentary.
- Portfolio focus matters: by selling University Services, Wiley Edge, and CrossKnowledge, Wiley has simplified into a sharper research-plus-learning structure. That suggests that not every education or knowledge business benefits equally from breadth; some benefit more from concentration around defensible IP and recurring institutional budgets.
- Margin expansion in mature content businesses can come from mix and simplification: Wiley keeps showing margin and cash-flow improvement even when total revenue is flat to slightly down, which matters for the frontier because it shows how slower-growth information businesses can still strengthen if their recurring, digital, and licensing mix improves.

## Working interpretation

- Wiley materially strengthens the first sub-lane because it gives the frontier a real `Publishing - Books` flagship instead of leaving the knowledge-publishing layer represented only by newspapers and broadcasters.
- The key societal pattern here is that high-trust, specialized knowledge remains budget-priority infrastructure even in a noisy digital environment. Universities, funders, R&D teams, and professional users still pay for curated access, publishing services, and rights-cleared content at scale.
- The strongest repeating pattern is that information trust is being monetized through multiple channels at once: subscriptions, transformational agreements, open access, platform access, and AI licensing. Omnicom explains how demand is bought, NYT explains how trust becomes subscriber habit, and Wiley explains how specialized knowledge franchises become reusable institutional and AI infrastructure.
- For this frontier, Wiley also adds a sharper version of the IP story the user asked for. This is a direct franchise/knowledge-rights monetization packet: the same content base supports journals, books, recurring institutional contracts, licensing into science and innovation workflows, and now AI model and application revenue.
