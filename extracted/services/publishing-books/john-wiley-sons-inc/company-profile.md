## What changed

- Business mix: the `2025` annual report shows Wiley increasingly operating as a research-intelligence and knowledge-rights platform rather than as a conventional print-book publisher. Research accounted for about `64%` of fiscal `2025` revenue and Learning for about `35%`, while non-core education-services businesses had already been sold or marked as held for sale.
- Digital tilt: the annual filing says about `96%` of Research revenue and about `60%` of Learning revenue in fiscal `2025` came from digital and online products and services.
- IP and recurring models: management frames Research around journal subscriptions, transformational agreements, open access, licensing, and digital products. It published over `1,800` journals in fiscal `2025`, and the strategic language repeatedly emphasizes recurring revenue, open access, and licensing content into science and innovation workflows.
- Portfolio simplification: the `2025` annual package makes clear that Wiley had already sold University Services, most of Wiley Edge, and CrossKnowledge, tightening the company around Research and Learning rather than broad education and talent-intermediation businesses.
- Quarter progression: FY2026 Q2 through Q4 shows Research and AI licensing strengthening while Learning stays under macro and channel pressure, which makes Wiley a strong packet for understanding how authoritative intellectual property and institutional knowledge budgets are holding up differently from consumer-adjacent learning demand.

## Open questions

- How durable is the recent AI-licensing surge once the first wave of model-training and strategic partnership deals matures?
- Can Learning stabilize without a broader consumer, retail, and corporate-training recovery, or does the segment remain structurally weaker than Research?
- Does Wiley’s AI monetization remain primarily episodic licensing revenue, or does management successfully convert it into a larger recurring data-and-application business?
