# Scholastic Corporation Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Scholastic Corporation
- Sector: Services
- Industry: Publishing - Books
- Frontier role: children's reading, school-channel distribution, family attention, and education-market interface
- Ticker: SCHL
- Fiscal year-end: May
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: FY2026 Q4
  - quarter 2: FY2026 Q3
  - quarter 3: FY2026 Q2

## Annual report takeaways

- Scholastic is useful for this frontier because it gives the books lane a materially different economic model from `Wiley`. This is not primarily institutional research publishing. It is a children's reading, school-channel, educator, family, and franchise-IP platform.
- The `2025` annual chain and the FY2026 quarter commentary show why the company belongs in both the attention-and-influence lane and the human-capital or institutional-interface lane. Scholastic reaches kids and families through `Book Fairs`, `Book Clubs`, classrooms, school and public libraries, retail, online channels, and entertainment formats, while also depending directly on school and district spending behavior.
- The strategic language around the quarter chain is the key signal. Management repeatedly emphasizes iconic children's franchises, proprietary school-based channels, trusted educator and family relationships, and the ability to reach kids both with books and on screens. That means the company monetizes attention through trusted distribution, not just through broad media reach.
- The `2025` annual window also matters because it anchors the business before the FY2026 sale-leaseback and capital-return actions dominate the reported numbers. The cleaner enduring story is that Scholastic is trying to turn a century-old children's brand into a multi-channel habit and franchise system that can monetize reading, school events, digital discovery, and entertainment extensions.
- For this frontier, Scholastic is one of the strongest packets for the overlap between consumer habits and institutional budgets. Families and children supply the demand habit, but schools, teachers, district budgets, and school-event participation still shape the monetization surface.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: FY2026 Q4 revenue was about `$476.1M`, down `6%`; operating income was about `$51.4M`, down `4%`; adjusted operating income was about `$58.3M`; and adjusted EBITDA was about `$84.7M`, or about `$83.7M` on a comparable basis in the prior year.
- Full-year frame: FY2026 revenue was about `$1.582B` versus `$1.626B` in FY2025; operating income was about `$15.2M`; adjusted operating income was about `$47.1M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$151.5M`, up `4%`; and pro forma adjusted EBITDA was about `$132.4M`, up `15%`.
- Segment signals: Children's Book Publishing and Distribution revenue in Q4 was about `$276.3M`, with `Book Fairs` up `5%` to about `$186.6M`; Education revenue was about `$109.2M`, down `13%`; Entertainment revenue was about `$21.0M`, up `42%`.
- Management signal: Scholastic said Book Fairs deepened reach with schools and families, Trade remained anchored by enduring children's franchises, Entertainment expanded discovery and engagement across platforms, and Education remained pressured by funding volatility and a difficult supplemental-curriculum market.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY2026 is the best quarter for seeing Scholastic as a hybrid reading-and-attention platform. It combines school-based habit formation, franchise monetization, and digital-distribution expansion, while showing how vulnerable the model still is to title timing and school spending conditions.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter belongs in the frontier because it shows trust and customer ownership operating through schools and families at once. Scholastic does not need to own a giant generic media audience if it owns repeated access to children through fairs, clubs, educators, and beloved franchises.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: FY2026 Q3 revenue was about `$329.1M`, down `2%`; operating loss was about `$(26.9)M`; adjusted operating loss was about `$(24.3)M`; and adjusted EBITDA was about `$0.0M` reported, or about `$6.7M` excluding sale-leaseback impact.
- Capital and cash signals: free cash flow was about `$407.0M`, versus a use of cash in the prior-year quarter, and net cash was about `$90.6M` versus net debt of about `$189.4M`, largely because the company closed sale-leaseback transactions and used the proceeds to repay debt and support shareholder returns.
- Segment signals: Children's Book Publishing and Distribution revenue was about `$197.6M`, down `3%`; `Book Fairs` rose `2%` to about `$113.3M`; Consolidated Trade fell `10%` to about `$69.7M`; Education revenue fell `2%` to about `$56.1M`; Entertainment revenue rose `25%` to about `$16.0M`.
- Management signal: Scholastic paired its operating commentary with a planned `$200M` modified Dutch auction tender offer and a long-term net-leverage target of `2.0x` to `2.5x` adjusted EBITDA, while stressing that children's publishing, entertainment, and distribution remained underpinned by iconic franchises and proprietary school-based channels.
- Interpretation: Q3 is the best quarter for seeing the distinction between operating franchise strength and reporting distortion. The underlying children's channel and franchise business is still functioning, but the quarter is dominated by sale-leaseback economics, Trade timing, and capital-allocation decisions.
- Bigger-picture read: the quarter shows that mature media-and-publishing businesses can still throw off significant strategic cash, but they increasingly use that flexibility for balance-sheet redesign and shareholder returns rather than only for conventional organic growth.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: FY2026 Q2 revenue was about `$551.1M`, up `1%`; operating income was about `$82.9M`, up `11%`; adjusted operating income was about `$95.0M`; diluted EPS was about `$2.17`; and adjusted EBITDA was about `$122.5M`, up `13%`.
- Segment signals: Children's Book Publishing and Distribution revenue rose `4%` to about `$380.9M`; `Book Fairs` revenue rose `5%` to about `$242.0M`; Trade revenue rose `7%` to about `$110.4M`; Education Solutions revenue fell `13%` to about `$62.2M`; Entertainment revenue fell `10%` to about `$15.1M`; International revenue rose `3%` to about `$89.5M`.
- Franchise and distribution signal: management highlighted `Dog Man: Big Jim Believes`, special editions in `The Hunger Games` and `Harry Potter`, more than `3.5 million` views on the new Scholastic-branded streaming app since launch, and three premium animated series in production with major media partners.
- Operating and capital signal: Scholastic said the sale-leaseback transactions unlocked more than `$400M` of net proceeds, expanded open-market repurchase authorization to `$150M`, and supported a multi-year reduction in share count.
- Interpretation: Q2 is the clearest quarter for understanding why Scholastic belongs in this frontier. It shows a company monetizing children's attention and reading habits through trusted school events, bestselling franchises, and emerging digital-video surfaces, while simultaneously taking direct pressure from volatile education funding.
- Bigger-picture read: this is a strong societal packet because it ties literacy habits, family brand trust, school-event participation, children's media franchises, and district-budget stress into one operating story. The monetization surface is cultural and institutional at the same time.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Children's attention is still monetizable when the distribution is trusted: Scholastic shows that attention does not have to be won only through social platforms or mass broadcast. School-based access, educator trust, family familiarity, and reading rituals still create valuable repeated demand.
- Publishing can become a relationship system rather than a unit-sales business: `Book Fairs`, `Book Clubs`, libraries, classrooms, retail, digital viewing, and franchise extensions all reinforce one another. That gives the books lane a habit-and-channel model rather than only a rights-and-subscription model.
- Franchise/IP monetization works differently here than at `Wiley`: Scholastic monetizes beloved children's franchises across print, school events, digital engagement, and entertainment production, while Wiley monetizes authoritative knowledge rights across journals, institutions, and AI workflows.
- Education funding volatility remains a real operating constraint: the quarter chain repeatedly links weak Education results to federal, state, school, and district budget pressure. That makes Scholastic valuable for the institutional-interface lane because it shows how public-sector or quasi-public spending conditions shape a consumer-facing brand.
- Reading and media habits are converging: Scholastic's streaming app, YouTube engagement, and animated-series development show that the company is trying to carry reading brands across screens rather than accept a hard divide between books and digital media.
- Capital allocation now sits inside the operating story: sale-leasebacks, tender offers, repurchases, leverage targets, and dividends are central to the FY2026 chain. This is a reminder that mature attention businesses often finance strategic repositioning through asset monetization and balance-sheet engineering as much as through topline growth.

## Working interpretation

- Scholastic materially strengthens the frontier because it gives the archive a second exact-category `Publishing - Books` packet with very different economics from `Wiley`.
- The recurring pattern here is that attention can still be owned through habit, trust, and repeated physical or school-based touchpoints. `Book Fairs` and educator relationships are doing some of the customer-ownership work that digital platforms or subscriptions do elsewhere in the frontier.
- The social pattern is that children's franchise power still matters when it can move across books, schools, family routines, streaming, YouTube, and animated production. This is a cleaner franchise/IP monetization packet than a generic publishing comp.
- Scholastic also sharpens one of the frontier's core tensions: a company can have strong cultural demand, trusted channels, and recognizable IP while still getting hit by institutional budget pressure, uneven title timing, and the need to finance transformation through asset sales and aggressive capital allocation.
