## Annual report takeaways

- This is one of the strongest remaining additions for the attention-and-influence frontier because it deepens the trust-and-information-distribution side of media without repeating `The New York Times Company`.
- The important distinction is not simply national versus local newspapers. `The New York Times` is a premium direct-subscription habit system. `Gannett` / `USA TODAY Co.` is a broader local-to-national reach operator trying to hold together a declining print base, a scaled digital audience, a local-news mission, and an advertising-services business for small and medium-sized clients.
- FY `2025` anchors that transition clearly. Total revenue was `$2.302B`; total digital revenue reached `46%` of total revenue, or about `$1.1B`; net income attributable to USA TODAY Co. was `$1.7M`; total adjusted EBITDA was `$263.0M`; and cash provided by operating activities was `$114.4M`.
- The annual report is especially important because it makes the cultural shift explicit: average `2025` unique visitors across the USA TODAY Media and Newsquest segments reached `186M`, and by `2025-12-31` digital-only subscriptions reached about `1.5M`, which management said outnumbered print subscriptions.
- That makes the packet frontier-significant because it shows a newspaper operator trying to survive not by denying print decline, but by converting trust, local reach, and audience habits into digital subscriptions, digital advertising, marketing services, AI licensing, and commerce-linked revenue.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$536.3M`, down `8.3%`; total digital revenue was `$254.3M`, or `47.4%` of total revenue; net income attributable to USA TODAY Co. was `$9.1M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$56.9M`; cash provided by operating activities was `$35.4M`; and free cash flow was `$19.6M`.
- Digital signals: management reported `158M` average monthly unique visitors, digital-only subscription revenue of `$45.6M`, and `LocaliQ` core-platform revenue of `$106.3M`.
- Interpretation: Q2 shows the current tension in the business. The company stayed profitable and digital metrics kept improving, but total revenue still fell materially because the legacy publishing base continues to contract faster than digital can fully offset it in a weak environment.
- Bigger-picture read: this is a high-signal quarter for the frontier because it shows information-trust businesses under modern distribution pressure. Audience still exists, but monetization has to be rebuilt across subscriptions, SMB marketing services, licensing, commerce, and AI-adjacent discovery shifts.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$548.5M`, down `4.0%`; total digital revenue was `$261.9M`, or `47.8%` of total revenue; net income attributable to USA TODAY Co. was `$19.9M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$73.1M`; cash provided by operating activities was `$19.3M`; and free cash flow was `$6.4M`.
- Digital and balance-sheet signals: same-store digital revenue grew `5.2%`; average monthly unique visitors reached `180M`; digital-only subscription revenue was `$45.9M`; `LocaliQ` core-platform revenue was `$99.3M`; cash and cash equivalents were `$85.2M`; total debt principal was `$988.3M`; and first-lien net leverage fell to `2.3x`.
- Interpretation: Q1 is the cleanest quarter in the chain because it shows the company closest to a defensible turnaround narrative. Digital mix approached `50%`, profitability improved sharply, and leverage kept falling.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter shows that the newspaper lane is not only about content trust. It is also about whether a local-news operator can become a hybrid subscription, ad-tech, and SMB-services platform quickly enough to outrun print decay.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: FY `2025` revenue was `$2.302B`; Q4 `2025` revenue was `$585.0M`; total digital revenue in Q4 was `$277.5M`, or `47.4%` of total revenue; total adjusted EBITDA was `$263.0M` for the year and `$91.1M` for the quarter; cash provided by operating activities was `$114.4M` for the year and `$43.4M` for the quarter; and free cash flow was `$64.2M` for the year and `$31.5M` for the quarter.
- Digital and strategic signals: management said Q4 total digital revenue returned to year-over-year growth on a same-store basis, hit an all-time high share of revenue, and was supported by `179M` average monthly unique visitors, digital advertising revenue of `$94.4M`, and digital-only subscription revenue of `$45.6M`.
- Structural signals: management said full-year `2025` produced the first positive annual net income since the `2019` merger, third consecutive year of free-cash-flow growth, several AI licensing deals expected to be accretive to EBITDA, and about `$136M` of debt repayment.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY2025 is the anchor quarter because it shows a legacy newspaper operator reaching a visible digital inflection without escaping the underlying structural pressure of print decline.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter is frontier-quality evidence that media trust can still monetize at scale, but the monetization stack is now much broader than circulation and print ads. It includes subscriptions, digital ad products, LocaliQ marketing services, licensing, commerce, and AI-linked content distribution.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Trust and distribution are separating: audience attention remains valuable, but the operator no longer controls discovery the way legacy newspapers once did.
- Local information still matters: Gannett is useful because it shows how local news, community identity, and civic relevance can still support real monetization, even under severe format pressure.
- Media fragmentation is changing what a newspaper company is: this is now part publisher, part subscription platform, part digital-ad business, and part SMB marketing-services operator.
- Print decline remains a hard industrial constraint: digital progress is real, but it is being measured against a shrinking legacy revenue base that still funds much of the system.
- AI creates both risk and opportunity: Gannett explicitly uses AI licensing and content partnerships as new monetization vectors, even while broader AI-driven discovery threatens traditional traffic flows.
- Audience scale is no longer enough by itself: the key challenge is converting reach into recurring, higher-quality revenue streams before distribution intermediaries capture most of the value.
- Frontier fit: this packet materially strengthens the first sub-lane because it gives the archive a second exact-category newspaper anchor with very different economics from `The New York Times` and a much clearer read on local-news strain, print erosion, and digital rebuilding.

## Working interpretation

- This packet materially improves the frontier because it gives the archive a direct window into the erosion and reconstruction of newspaper economics outside the premium-national model.
- The repeated cross-company pattern is that trust still monetizes, but not through the old bundle alone. Content now has to be paired with subscriptions, audience data, local advertiser tools, commerce, licensing, and distribution partnerships.
- The cultural signal underneath the numbers is that communities still need local information, but the businesses providing it are being forced to rebuild themselves around digital discovery systems they do not control.
- In combination with `The New York Times Company`, this packet makes the newspaper lane much more defensible because it separates premium direct-relationship success from the more difficult local-scale and hybrid-monetization struggle.
