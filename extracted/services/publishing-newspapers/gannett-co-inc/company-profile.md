## What changed

- Exact-category newspaper depth: this packet adds a second true `Publishing - Newspapers` anchor beside `The New York Times Company`, but with much more exposure to local-news economics, print decline, and digital-marketing services.
- Identity continuity: the archive now explicitly captures the transition from `Gannett` / `GCI` to `USA TODAY Co.` / `TDAY`, which matters because AnnualReports and newer filings use different labels for the same issuer.
- Digital share versus print decline: by FY `2025`, total digital revenues reached `46%` of total revenue, or about `$1.1B`, while print and commercial revenues remained the largest single bucket and kept declining.
- Subscription and audience signals: as of `2025-12-31`, digital-only subscriptions reached about `1.5M`, exceeding print subscriptions, while the company averaged `186M` unique visitors in `2025`.
- Local-news and SMB-advertising mix: the packet ties together USA TODAY Network audience reach, Newsquest's U.K. local-news presence, and LocaliQ's digital-marketing services for small and medium-sized businesses.
- AI and licensing as monetization extensions: management explicitly connected `2025` gains to AI licensing deals and broader digital content syndication, commerce, and partnership expansion.

## Open questions

- Can the company keep digital revenue near or above half of total revenue before print decline erodes the base faster than digital can offset it?
- How durable is local-news trust as a monetizable advantage when search, social, and AI intermediaries keep reshaping discovery?
- Does LocaliQ provide a sufficiently differentiated advertising-services engine, or does it mainly soften the decline of legacy publishing economics?
- How scalable are AI licensing, commerce, and digital-other revenues as repeatable profit pools rather than incremental supplements?
