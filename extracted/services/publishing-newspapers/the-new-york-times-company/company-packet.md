## Annual report takeaways

- The New York Times Company is one of the strongest possible attention-and-influence packets for this frontier because it shows how trust, habit, product breadth, and direct subscriber ownership can be turned into a scalable media business even inside a fragmented information environment.
- The `2025` annual report shows that this is no longer usefully read as only a newspaper company. Management presents a bundle built around news, The Athletic, Audio, Cooking, Games, Wirecutter, podcasts, and related Times-branded products. That matters because the company is monetizing repeated daily participation across adjacent intellectual-property and utility products, not just the front page.
- The annual numbers make the model concrete. For `2025`, total revenue was about `$2.825B`, including about `$1.951B` of subscription revenue, about `$566.0M` of advertising revenue, and about `$308.1M` of affiliate, licensing, and other revenue. The company exited the year with about `12.78M` total subscribers, generated about `$584.5M` of operating cash flow, and about `$550.5M` of free cash flow.
- The strategy language is the key frontier signal. Management says it is building valuable daily habits and lifelong direct relationships worth paying for, with a high-value bundle that gives subscribers multiple reasons to engage each day. That is a direct answer to media fragmentation: own the habit loop, own the billing relationship, and reduce dependence on third-party platforms for monetization.
- The annual package also sharpens the cultural and societal dimension of the lane. It explicitly ties audience size and engagement to public sentiment about independent journalism, the digital information ecosystem, search engines, social, video, AI and other platforms, and changing methods of consuming news. This makes NYT a trust-and-distribution packet, not only a content packet.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` total revenue was about `$762.5M`; operating profit was about `$118.0M`; adjusted operating profit was about `$155.3M`; diluted EPS was `$0.57`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.69`.
- Subscriber and advertising signals: total subscribers reached `13.35M`; digital-only subscribers reached `12.80M`; digital-only quarterly net additions were about `280,000`; digital-only ARPU rose to `$9.94`; digital advertising revenue rose `20.7%`.
- Management signal: Meredith Kopit Levien said the company was investing in independent, high-quality journalism and market-leading lifestyle products while leaning into video.
- Interpretation: Q2 is not just a healthy newspaper quarter. It is evidence that premium information distribution can compound when bundled with lifestyle utility and when the company controls the direct relationship to the user.
- Bigger-picture read: the emphasis on video matters because it shows that even a subscription-led publisher still has to adapt format and distribution to changing media behavior. Trust alone is not enough; it has to travel across the formats where attention is moving.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` total revenue was about `$712.2M`; operating profit was about `$90.6M`; adjusted operating profit was about `$117.9M`; diluted EPS was `$0.54`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.61`.
- Subscriber and advertising signals: total subscribers reached `13.08M`; digital-only subscribers reached `12.52M`; digital-only net adds were about `310,000`; digital-only ARPU rose to `$9.77`; digital advertising revenue rose `31.6%`.
- Cost and operating context: operating costs rose `7.7%` and adjusted operating costs rose `9.4%`, largely on higher compensation and benefits related to journalism.
- Interpretation: Q1 reinforces that the company is monetizing trust and habit at the same time. It can spend more on journalism and still expand profit because the audience relationship is direct, sticky, and increasingly diversified across products.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter is a good counterexample to the lazy view that legacy media can only cut its way to survival. NYT is investing into journalism and still showing revenue, margin, and subscriber momentum.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` total revenue was about `$802.3M`; operating profit was about `$161.6M`; adjusted operating profit was about `$192.3M`; diluted EPS was `$0.79`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.89`.
- Subscriber and advertising signals: total subscribers reached `12.78M`; digital-only subscribers reached `12.21M`; digital-only net adds were about `450,000`; digital-only ARPU was `$9.72`; digital advertising revenue rose `24.9%`.
- Full-year cash signal: full-year `2025` operating cash flow was about `$584.5M` and free cash flow was about `$550.5M`.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY2025 is the quarter that makes NYT a flagship packet rather than a category placeholder. It shows that a publisher can combine subscriber scale, ARPU improvement, premium ad demand, and strong cash generation without relying on mass-market legacy distribution economics.
- Bigger-picture read: the quarter makes clear that information trust is becoming a monetizable scarcity. In a noisier, more AI-saturated media environment, a credible brand can sell certainty, routine, and identity as much as articles.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Trust as a paid product: NYT is one of the clearest current archive examples of independent journalism itself becoming a premium good. Trust is not just reputational here; it is directly tied to subscriber growth, ARPU, and advertiser willingness to buy premium inventory.
- Bundle economics over single-product media: the company is broadening the monetization base through The Athletic, Audio, Cooking, Games, Wirecutter, podcasts, and video. That shows how media companies can stabilize demand by turning one franchise into multiple repeated-use surfaces.
- Direct audience ownership: management keeps framing the strategy around habits and direct relationships. This is the most important lane signal. The company wants to own the user relationship rather than lease attention from search, social, or platform distribution.
- Advertising cyclicality with quality insulation: advertising still matters and remains cyclical, but the recent quarter chain suggests premium inventory and stronger marketer demand can coexist with a subscription-led model instead of undermining it.
- Media fragmentation and format adaptation: the annual report explicitly warns that audience engagement depends on changes at search engines, social, video, AI and other platforms. The push into video shows that even trusted publishers must keep adapting to how attention moves.

## Working interpretation

- The New York Times Company materially strengthens the first sub-lane because it shows that attention can still be monetized at scale when it is wrapped in trust, routine, and premium product breadth rather than in pure reach.
- The most important societal shift here is that many consumers appear willing to pay directly for fewer, more trusted, higher-frequency information and utility products in a fragmented media environment. That is not a reversion to old newspaper economics; it is a modern participation-and-habit model.
- The strongest repeating pattern is that customer ownership matters more than raw distribution reach. Omnicom explains how attention is bought. Charter and AT&T explain who owns the connectivity relationship. NYT explains what happens when a media company owns the user relationship and can convert trust into a recurring subscription loop.
- For this frontier, NYT also sharpens a broader cultural point: as search, social, and AI flood the market with abundant content, scarcity shifts toward credibility, editorial identity, and the ability to become part of a person’s daily routine.
