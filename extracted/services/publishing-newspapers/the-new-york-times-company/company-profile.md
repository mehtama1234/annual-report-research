## What changed

- Scale and mix: the `2025` annual report shows a business increasingly defined by digital subscription scale rather than legacy newspaper economics, with about `$2.825B` in total revenue, about `$1.951B` from subscriptions, about `$566.0M` from advertising, and about `$308.1M` from affiliate, licensing, and other revenue.
- Product model: management describes a multi-product bundle spanning news, The Athletic, Audio, Cooking, Games, Wirecutter, podcasts, and related Times-branded products, showing that the company is monetizing habit breadth rather than a single newspaper SKU.
- Subscriber base: the annual filing says the company exited `2025` with about `12.78M` total subscribers, more than at any point in its history.
- Strategy language: the annual package frames growth around building valuable daily habits, lifelong direct relationships worth paying for, and a high-value subscription package that gives subscribers multiple reasons to engage each day.
- Quarter progression: Q4 `2025`, Q1 `2026`, and Q2 `2026` show sustained subscriber adds, rising ARPU, and unusually strong digital advertising growth at the same time, which makes NYT more useful as a frontier anchor than a standard newspaper cyclicality story.

## Open questions

- How durable is the current advertising strength if marketer demand softens or platform supply dynamics change?
- Can subscriber growth remain efficient as penetration rises and more of the growth case depends on habit depth, bundling, and pricing rather than simple new-user acquisition?
- How much of the current premium is tied to trust in independent journalism versus the broader lifestyle and utility products orbit around the core news franchise?
