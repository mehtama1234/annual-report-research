## What changed

- Revenue and earnings: Planet Fitness is visible here as a recurring membership and habit-loop operator rather than a simple gym chain. Full-year `2025` revenue increased `12.1%` to about `$1.3B`, net income attributable to Planet Fitness, Inc. reached `$219.1M`, and adjusted EBITDA reached `$551.6M`.
- Membership and network scale: the year-end chain showed about `20.8M` members and `2,896` clubs at `2025-12-31`, then about `21.5M` members and `2,909` clubs at `2026-03-31`, and `2,930` clubs at `2026-06-30`.
- Franchise architecture: the `10-K` showed `2,604` franchisee-owned clubs and `292` corporate-owned clubs at year-end `2025`, while the quarter releases repeatedly stated that about `90%` of clubs are owned and operated by independent business owners.
- Price and value posture: the `10-K` framed the standard Classic Card for new members at `$15` per month and the PF Black Card for new members at `$24.99` per month, while the `Q1 2026` release showed that management paused the planned national Black Card price increase pending a broader pricing review.
- Blind-spot fit: this is not a normal `Consumer Goods` story and it is not well captured by `--None--` industry labeling. The real logic is a low-friction, recurring-fee, body-confidence, and habit-conditioned service system operating through a franchise-heavy access network.

## Open questions

- Can Planet Fitness reignite stronger net member growth without giving up the low-friction value architecture that made the chain scale in the first place?
- How much pricing flexibility does the company really have before the high-value, low-price identity begins to weaken?
- Does the company become a thicker membership system over time through retention, pricing, and app usage, or does it remain more of a thin but highly routine fee-access habit?
