## What changed

- Caesars is now a direct archive addition for the gaming / loyalty-database claim that previously sat in the downstream packet without a company packet underneath it.
- The `2025` annual filing gives a much cleaner mechanism than the old generic casino argument. Caesars says Caesars Rewards helps it compete more effectively, capture a larger share of customer entertainment spending across regions and online wagering, and support its cross-market strategy.
- The filing is especially useful because it connects the physical and digital estate. In `2025`, Caesars added a universal digital wallet to access funds and Caesars Rewards in one wallet, and wagers on Caesars Sportsbook, Caesars Racebook, and iGaming apps can earn credits toward Caesars Rewards or free wagering credits.
- The loyalty system is not described as a soft marketing overlay. Caesars says Caesars Rewards is designed to cultivate a gratifying and frictionless relationship that motivates members to increase both visit frequency and spend.
- The annual numbers also show that the digital extension is already economically meaningful. Caesars Digital generated `2025` net revenues of `$1.408B` and Adjusted EBITDA of `$236M`, up from `$1.163B` and `$117M` in `2024`.
- The quarter trail reinforces the point. Q4 `2025` Caesars Digital Adjusted EBITDA reached `$85M`; Q1 `2026` was `$69M`; and Q2 `2026` was `$68M`, while consolidated quarterly revenue stayed around the `$2.9B` to `$3.0B` range.

## Open questions

- How much of Caesars' long-term differentiation comes from the cross-property relationship and the digital wallet versus the quality of the physical resort base itself?
- Does Caesars Digital become more important mainly as a growth segment, or mainly because it keeps the Caesars Rewards database active between in-person trips?
- The filing-backed mechanism is strong, but how durable is it if regional casino competition or promotional intensity pushes harder against margins?
