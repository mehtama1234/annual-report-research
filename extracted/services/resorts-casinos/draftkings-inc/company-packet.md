# DraftKings Inc. packet

Date baseline: `2026-08-10`

## Snapshot

- DraftKings is a software-mediated wagering and real-money participation platform built on sports attention, habit loops, product adjacency, and jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction legalization.
- The key `2025-2026` story is a move from scale-at-all-costs toward profitable scale, followed immediately by a willingness to reinvest that new earnings power into a fresh participation frontier:
  - `2025` delivered revenue above `$6 billion`, positive full-year GAAP net income, and much stronger adjusted EBITDA
  - `Q1 2026` reinforced that the core Sportsbook and iGaming engine was scaling efficiently
  - `Q2 2026` then showed how quickly hold volatility and promotional reinvestment can pressure reported revenue even when engagement keeps growing
- That makes DraftKings a strong archive case for how modern sports fandom, gambling, media, and software get fused into one repeat-spend participation system.

Covered scale:

- full-year `2025` revenue: `$6.055 billion`
- full-year `2025` adjusted EBITDA: `$620.0 million`
- `Q1 2026` revenue: `$1.646 billion`
- `Q2 2026` revenue: `$1.443 billion`

## Filing coverage

## Annual takeaways from `2025`

### 1. DraftKings is not just a sportsbook; it is trying to own the broader real-money fan relationship

- The `2025 10-K` describes DraftKings as a digital sports entertainment and gaming company spanning:
  - `Sportsbook`
  - `iGaming`
  - daily fantasy sports
  - digital lottery courier
  - prediction markets
  - digital media
- That matters because the company is not simply monetizing a bet.
- It is trying to build a multi-product account relationship with what it calls the `skin-in-the-game` sports fan.
- The strategic objective is to keep the same user moving across products, jurisdictions, and sports calendars rather than treating each wager as a standalone event.

### 2. The core economic model is hold plus promotions plus software-led user management

- The `10-K` says Sportsbook revenue comes from the built-in theoretical margin in fixed-odds propositions, while actual outcomes create short-term volatility in hold and profitability.
- It also says promotions and incentives reduce reported revenue across Sportsbook, iGaming, fantasy, lottery, and prediction markets.
- That means DraftKings' reported revenue is not just a demand number.
- It is the result of three interacting systems:
  - gross consumer volume
  - win-rate or hold outcomes
  - promotional reinvestment
- This is one of the most important archive lessons from the name:
  - user growth can be strong
  - handle can be strong
  - and revenue can still disappoint if outcomes and promotions move the wrong way

### 3. `2025` proved that the model can produce scale and profits at the same time

- Full-year `2025` revenue reached `$6.055 billion`, up from `$4.768 billion`.
- Full-year GAAP net income attributable to common stockholders was `$3.7 million`, versus a loss of `$507.3 million` in `2024`.
- Full-year adjusted EBITDA reached `$620.0 million`, up from `$181.3 million`.
- Annual KPIs in the `10-K` also showed:
  - average `MUPs` of `4.0 million`
  - `ARPMUP` of `$125`
  - Sportsbook handle of `$53.6 billion`
  - Sportsbook net revenue margin of `7.1%`
- Those numbers matter because they show a business that is no longer only a growth-and-acquisition story.
- It had become a scaled monetization engine by `2025`, even before the next investment push.

### 4. Prediction markets are the new strategic frontier

- The `10-K` says `DraftKings Predictions` launched on `2025-12-19` under `CFTC` oversight.
- Management used both the `Q4 2025` and `Q1/Q2 2026` chains to frame Predictions as a major incremental opportunity.
- In archive terms, this is important because it shows DraftKings trying to extend the same participation logic into adjacent regulated formats:
  - event-contract trading
  - sports-adjacent speculation
  - new engagement surfaces that resemble sportsbook behavior but travel through a different regulatory structure
- This is not extra product clutter.
- It is an attempt to widen the real-money participation surface around sports attention.

### 5. The company runs on state openings, regulatory permissions, and cross-product routing

- The `10-K` says DraftKings' priorities include:
  - launching offerings in new jurisdictions
  - creating replicable jurisdiction-level unit economics
  - expanding offerings
- It also says the technology is highly scalable and customizable to jurisdiction-specific regulations.
- This is a reminder that DraftKings is not only a consumer-internet company.
- It is also a regulatory-distribution company whose TAM expands when laws, taxes, and licenses move in its favor.

## Quarter-by-quarter read

### `Q4 2025`

- Revenue: `$1.989 billion` versus `$1.393 billion`
- Revenue growth: `43%`
- Net income attributable to common stockholders: `$136.4 million`
- Adjusted EBITDA: `$343.2 million`
- `MUPs`: `4.8 million`, flat year over year
- `ARPMUP`: `$139`, up `43%`

Additional management signals:

- management said `2025` closed on a high note with records for revenue and adjusted EBITDA
- it introduced `FY2026` guidance of `$6.5 billion-$6.9 billion` revenue and `$700 million-$900 million` adjusted EBITDA
- it said the company repurchased `16 million` shares during `2025`
- it framed Predictions as a `massive, incremental opportunity`
- mobile sports betting footprint stood at `26 states` plus `Washington, D.C.`, with iGaming live in `5 states` and Ontario

Interpretation:

- `Q4 2025` is the quarter where DraftKings stops looking like a speculative legalization trade and starts looking like a scaled, profitable sports-attention platform.
- The combination of strong revenue, positive quarterly net income, and a large EBITDA step-up is the cleanest evidence in the packet that the core engine had become structurally stronger.

### `Q1 2026`

- Revenue: `$1.646 billion` versus `$1.409 billion`
- Revenue growth: `17%`
- Net income attributable to common stockholders: `$21.1 million`
- Adjusted EBITDA: `$167.9 million`
- `MUPs`: `4.2 million`, down `4%`, but up `2%` excluding Lottery
- `ARPMUP`: `$131`, up `21%`
- Sportsbook handle: `$14.083 billion`, up `1.5%`

Additional operating detail:

- management said profitability was inflecting and positioned that cash generation as the reason it could press into Predictions
- the company highlighted the coming `Super App`, proprietary exchange, market-making, and combo capabilities
- guidance was maintained at `$6.5 billion-$6.9 billion` revenue and `$700 million-$900 million` adjusted EBITDA
- footprint expanded to `27 states`, `Washington, D.C.`, and `Puerto Rico` for mobile sports betting
- the quarter's MUP decline was explicitly linked to lower Lottery users after the `Texas` exit in `2025`

Interpretation:

- `Q1 2026` is important because it shows DraftKings' management moving from defense to offense.
- The business was profitable enough that leadership no longer talked only about efficiency.
- It talked about redeploying that efficiency into a new category while still keeping the core sportsbook and iGaming engine healthy.

### `Q2 2026`

- Revenue: `$1.443 billion`, down `5%`
- Six-month revenue: `$3.089 billion`, up `5.8%`
- Net loss attributable to common stockholders: `$(67.6) million`
- Six-month net loss attributable to common stockholders: `$(46.5) million`
- Adjusted EBITDA: `$114.6 million`
- Six-month adjusted EBITDA: `$282.5 million`
- Sports Consumer Volume: `$13.14 billion`, up `15%`
- `MUPs`: `3.6 million`, up about `9%`
- `ARPMUP`: `$132`, down about `13%`

Liquidity and capital-structure signals:

- as of `2026-06-30`, cash and cash equivalents were `$983.9 million`
- convertible notes, net of issuance costs, were `$1.260 billion`
- Term B loan, net of issuance costs, was `$574.6 million`
- purchase obligations totaled about `$2.0 billion`
- net cash provided by operating activities in the first half was `$63.0 million`

Additional management signals:

- revenue weakness was explicitly tied to customer-friendly sports outcomes and higher promotional reinvestment for Sportsbook and Predictions
- the company said the core business still looked on track to generate about `$1 billion` of adjusted EBITDA in `2026`, even while official guidance stayed at `$700 million-$900 million`
- management said the `Super App` was live nationwide
- it said Predictions was growing faster than expected
- DraftKings launched Sportsbook and iGaming in `Alberta`, lifting its Canadian live-footprint to provinces representing about `51%` of the Canadian population

Interpretation:

- `Q2 2026` is the best quarter in the packet for understanding the real economics of the model.
- Engagement was clearly strong:
  - volume up
  - users up
  - retention solid
- But the conversion of that engagement into revenue weakened because:
  - sports outcomes favored customers
  - DraftKings chose to spend harder on acquisition and reinvestment
- This is exactly the kind of quarter that makes the archive useful.
- It shows that participation businesses can have excellent demand signals and still report weaker near-term financial results if the monetization mechanics shift.

## Repeating operating signals

### Consumer behavior

- DraftKings monetizes the urge to make sports more participatory.
- The products function as:
  - wager interfaces
  - second-screen engagement systems
  - habit loops around live events and seasons
  - cross-sell surfaces between sports, casino, fantasy, lottery, and prediction formats
- The consumer is not only buying a chance to win money.
- The consumer is buying a more active role in the event itself.

### Cultural and societal read-through

- DraftKings is one of the clearest filing-backed examples of sports fandom becoming a real-money participation economy.
- Watching a game is no longer the whole product.
- The company is monetizing:
  - prediction
  - emotional investment
  - in-play attention
  - repeat checking behavior
  - account-based loyalty across multiple betting-adjacent formats
- This matters socially because it turns sports from a broadcast spectacle into an interactive financialized routine for a large share of users.
- The spread from Sportsbook into Predictions also matters.
- It suggests that the broader culture is getting more comfortable with software-mediated speculation across regulated categories, not just traditional casino-style formats.

### Industrial and operating pressures

- repeated hold volatility:
  - short-term results can move sharply with sports outcomes
  - management repeatedly calls out customer-friendly outcomes when they pressure revenue
- repeated promotional pressure:
  - revenue is directly reduced by free plays, bonuses, deposit matches, and related offers
  - new category pushes such as Predictions can raise reinvestment intensity
- repeated regulatory pressure:
  - every new state or province is subject to licensing, taxes, political timing, and local rules
  - the company is structurally exposed to policy change
- repeated legal and reputational pressure:
  - the `10-K` details litigation around promotions, addiction claims, and marketing practices
  - responsible-gaming posture is therefore not optional branding; it is a business defense layer
- repeated capital-allocation pressure:
  - buybacks show confidence, but the company also carries convertible notes and a Term B loan
  - future spending has to balance growth, advocacy, debt discipline, and customer acquisition
- repeated product-mix pressure:
  - Sportsbook can be highly volatile
  - iGaming is steadier
  - Lottery can shift with jurisdiction exits
  - Predictions is growing but still in heavier investment mode

## Cross-company interpretation

- Compared with `Caesars`, DraftKings is more software-native, more acquisition-driven, and more explicitly centered on online account behavior rather than a resort-and-loyalty base with digital extensions.
- Compared with `MGM`, it is more concentrated in the direct digital participation loop and less tied to destination-property economics.
- Compared with `Playtika`, it monetizes real-money regulated wagering rather than social-casino and casual-game habit loops, but the underlying engagement logic still overlaps:
  - repeated sessions
  - targeted offers
  - portfolio cross-sell
  - monetization of routine
- Compared with `The Trade Desk`, it is a demand endpoint rather than the infrastructure that routes attention across media.

That gives the archive another useful distinction:

- destination and loyalty gaming ecosystems: `Caesars`, `MGM`
- software-native wagering participation: `DraftKings`
- habitual mobile monetization loops: `Playtika`
- demand-routing infrastructure beneath participation: `The Trade Desk`, `AppLovin`

## Bottom line

- DraftKings is a strong archive case for how sports attention gets converted into software-mediated, repeat real-money participation.
- The most important signal is not just legalization growth.
- It is that the company now has a multi-product operating system for monetizing sports engagement across wagers, casino play, fantasy, lottery, and event contracts.
- The most important caution is equally clear:
  - strong engagement does not guarantee strong near-term revenue conversion
  - hold, promotions, regulation, and responsible-gaming pressure all sit between user activity and durable earnings
- That tension is exactly why the name belongs in the lane.
