## What changed

- Las Vegas Sands gives the recreation lane a cleaner international integrated-resort case than the U.S.-heavy gaming operators already in the repo.
- The `2025` annual materials frame the company less as a Las Vegas casino operator and more as a two-market luxury and tourism platform anchored by Marina Bay Sands in Singapore and a multi-property Macao system through Sands China.
- The annual filing shows `2025` total net revenues of `$13.017B` and operating income of `$2.818B`. The year-end earnings release adds that net income attributable to Las Vegas Sands was `$1.63B`, or `$2.35` per diluted share.
- The quarter chain shows a more uneven `2026` than the full-year `2025` picture. Q1 `2026` accelerated with revenue up `25.3%` to `$3.59B` and net income up `57.1%` to `$641M`, while Q2 `2026` softened to revenue of `$3.15B` and net income of `$373M` because unusually low rolling-play hold in Macao hurt reported results.
- The company is unusually explicit about capital return. It repurchased `$500M` of stock in Q4 `2025`, `$740M` in Q1 `2026`, and `$787M` in Q2 `2026`, then increased authorization to `$6.0B` on `2026-07-21`.
- The packet also sharpens the archive's operator-versus-owner distinction. Sands owns and develops the destination system itself, so the relationship, the asset base, and the capital burden all sit in the same structure.

## Open questions

- How much of Sands' forward earnings power depends on structurally rising Asian tourism and premium-gaming demand versus unusually favorable hold, VIP mix, and suite utilization?
- Does the strongest long-term return come from Macao volume recovery or from Marina Bay Sands becoming an even more exclusive Singapore luxury and convention platform through the expansion project?
- Compared with Caesars and MGM, is Sands best read as a gaming operator, a tourism-infrastructure owner, or a luxury-capital-allocation vehicle tied to two scarce destination licenses?
