## Annual report takeaways

- MGM is the right next gaming and destination packet because it gives the archive a second large resort-and-casino operator beside Caesars, which makes the gaming and loyalty work less dependent on one wallet-forward example.
- The classification stack is cleaner here than in some travel names, but the archive signal is still useful: AnnualReports places MGM in `Services / Resorts & Casinos`, while the SEC describes the business under `Hotels & Motels`. That combination helps because MGM is not just a casino operator or just a hotel operator. It is a destination-platform business spanning lodging, gaming, entertainment, meetings, regional repeat play, Macau, and digital gaming.
- The `2025` annual materials show a business with record revenue but uneven segment quality. Full-year `2025` consolidated net revenues reached `$17.5B`, consolidated Adjusted EBITDA reached `$2.4B`, and Adjusted EPS reached `$3.31`.
- The segment mix matters more than the headline. For full-year `2025`, Las Vegas Strip Resorts revenue was `$8.4B` and down `4%`, Regional Operations revenue was `$3.8B` and up `1%`, MGM China revenue was `$4.5B` and up `11%`, and MGM Digital revenue was `$654M` and up `19%`.
- That mix makes MGM analytically stronger than a generic casino label suggests. Las Vegas was softer, but regional gaming held up, Macau stayed strong, and digital activity kept expanding. The packet is useful because it shows a diversified destination-and-gaming portfolio rather than one narrow wagering engine.
- MGM also sharpens the archive's aggregator-gap lesson. As of `2026-08-09`, AnnualReports still showed only `2024` as the most recent annual package, while MGM's own IR stack already exposed the `2025` annual report and the current `2026` quarter chain.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` consolidated revenue was `$4.5B`, net income attributable to MGM Resorts was `$292M`, diluted EPS was `$1.11`, Adjusted EPS was `$0.59`, and consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was `$610M`.
- Growth and mix: management framed the quarter around record consolidated revenue, a second consecutive quarter of Las Vegas Strip year-over-year revenue growth, and all-time best Regional Operations same-store quarterly revenue.
- Segment signals: Las Vegas Strip revenue was `$2.2B` and up `3%`; Regional Operations same-store revenue was `$904M` and up `3%`; MGM China revenue was roughly `$1.1B` and flat; MGM Digital revenue was `$196M` and up `20%`.
- Strategic message: management said MGM Osaka remained on track for a `2030` opening and tied future growth to returns on digital businesses as well as to luxury-investment opportunities in Las Vegas.
- Structural themes: this quarter reinforces MGM as a destination-platform portfolio with several monetization surfaces at once: Strip resorts, regional gaming, Macau, and digital extensions.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` consolidated net revenues were `$4.5B`, net income attributable to MGM Resorts was `$125M`, diluted EPS was `$0.48`, Adjusted EPS was `$0.49`, and consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was `$580M`.
- Growth and mix: management described the quarter as record first-quarter consolidated net revenues driven primarily by MGM China, MGM Digital, and growth at the BetMGM North America venture.
- Segment signals: Las Vegas Strip revenue was about `$2.2B`; Regional Operations revenue was `$918M`; MGM China revenue was `$1.1B`; MGM Digital revenue was `$183M`; and the unconsolidated affiliates line for BetMGM and other swung to `$10.0M` of operating income from a prior-year loss.
- Capital-allocation and portfolio signal: MGM closed the sale of the operations of MGM Northfield Park for `$546M` in April `2026`, which management framed as incremental liquidity for balance-sheet support and share repurchases.
- Structural themes: Q1 is useful because it shows MGM reading less like a pure Las Vegas cycle and more like a diversified resort-and-gaming system with regional, Macau, and digital offsets.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` consolidated net revenues were `$4.6B`, net income attributable to MGM Resorts was `$294M`, diluted EPS was `$1.11`, Adjusted EPS was `$1.60`, and consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was `$635M`.
- Full-year frame: full-year `2025` consolidated net revenues were `$17.5B`, consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was `$2.4B`, and Adjusted EPS was `$3.31`.
- Segment signals: Q4 Las Vegas Strip revenue was `$2.2B`; Regional Operations revenue was `$950M`; MGM China revenue was `$1.2B`; and MGM Digital revenue was `$188M`, up `35%` year over year for the quarter.
- Management message: MGM highlighted diversified operating strategy, strong group and convention business, continued regional performance, premium-mass leadership at MGM China, double-digit BetMGM North America venture revenue growth, and the long-term pipeline around MGM Osaka.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the quarter where MGM most clearly reads as a diversified destination and gaming portfolio rather than a simple Strip recovery story.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Travel and destination lane: MGM strengthens the archive because it gives the travel-and-experience work another operator that monetizes destination demand through lodging, meetings, gaming, luxury positioning, and repeat property visits.
- Wallet and loyalty logic: MGM is less explicit than Caesars about one universal wallet, but it still shows a meaningful relationship system through MGM Rewards, BetMGM linkage, repeat resort visitation, and digital-gaming adjacency.
- Digital extension: the most useful distinction is that `MGM Digital` and `BetMGM` are not the same thing. MGM Digital captures LeoVegas and other consolidated subsidiaries, while BetMGM sits in the unconsolidated-affiliates line. That makes MGM a stronger comparison case for how digital exposure can exist in more than one accounting and operating form.
- Geographic diversification: MGM is a better cross-market comparison than Caesars for understanding how Las Vegas, regional U.S. gaming, and Macau can offset one another inside one company.
- Cultural: MGM is a status and experience business as much as a gaming business. Luxury stays, convention demand, destination entertainment, premium-mass play in Macau, and digital wagering all sit inside one branded consumption system.
- Capital intensity: MGM is a stronger capital-heavy counterweight to Booking, Marriott, and Hilton than Caesars is, because the story more visibly includes integrated resorts, international development, and large-scale physical destination assets.

## Working interpretation

- MGM materially improves the archive because it gives the gaming and travel work a second large destination operator whose economic logic is more diversified than a simple rewards-and-wallet case.
- Caesars remains the cleaner filing-backed example of one universal wallet and one cross-channel rewards loop. MGM is different and useful precisely because it shows a broader resort portfolio where Las Vegas, regional gaming, Macau, and digital exposure all interact.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, MGM is strong evidence that the better destination operators are trying to widen the customer relationship beyond a single visit by linking lodging, gaming, regional repeat behavior, premium entertainment, and digital extensions into one operating system.
