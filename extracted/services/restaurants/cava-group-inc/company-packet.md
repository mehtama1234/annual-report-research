## Annual report takeaways

- CAVA describes Mediterranean cuisine as a growing cultural and wellness
  category and reports 367 locations after 58 net new openings (p. 3).
- The report frames restaurants as social gathering spaces and describes
  eat-in, take-out, catering, loyalty, and relationship-building as multiple
  guest occasions (pp. 4-5).
- CAVA says it raised prices approximately 3% in 2024, below inflation, while
  emphasizing value, operational excellence, and reinvestment (p. 6).
- The report describes culinary innovation, loyalty, hospitality, team
  development, and disciplined growth as central operating themes (pp. 3-6).

## Seven research themes

### Premium-value bifurcation

Reported evidence: CAVA describes value, below-inflation pricing, consumer
friction, and multiple dining occasions (pp. 5-6). It does not isolate price,
traffic, income cohort, or affordability effects.

### Restaurant margin vise

The report emphasizes operational excellence, reinvestment, disciplined growth,
and team performance (p. 6), but this shareholder narrative does not provide a
complete restaurant-margin or labor-cost table. Margin transmission remains
open.

### Health-conscious consumption

Reported evidence: CAVA explicitly presents Mediterranean cuisine as a wellness
and nutrition-oriented category and describes bold, satisfying food without
compromise (p. 3). This establishes positioning and narrative, not measured
health-driven demand or causality.

### Labor and automation

Reported evidence: the report emphasizes high-performing teams, career
development, hospitality, loyalty, and operational execution (pp. 5-6). It does
not quantify wages, labor hours, throughput, service time, or automation.

### Supply-chain and geopolitical volatility

The report's bounded narrative does not provide a dated realized supply-chain
or geopolitical shock. Any broader exposure remains absent from this packet and
must not be inferred.

### Regulatory fragmentation

The report does not provide a bounded regulatory-cost or jurisdictional
fragmentation observation. Regulatory exposure remains an open evidence field.

### Capital concentration and ownership

Reported evidence: CAVA describes disciplined growth, 58 net new openings,
reinvestment, and expansion to 25 states and Washington, D.C. (p. 3). The
report does not establish capital efficiency, ownership concentration, or a
causal growth outcome.

## Open evidence boundary

CAVA's annual-report narrative is useful cultural and consumer context, but it
is not a substitute for the primary operating observations already preserved in
the observatory. Traffic/mix decomposition, labor, margin, cash conversion,
exposure, and falsifier evidence remain open.
