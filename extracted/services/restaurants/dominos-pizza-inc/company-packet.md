## Annual report takeaways

- Domino's is a high-priority archive addition because the local strategy packet has been using it as shorthand for direct ordering, habit, and tech-enabled repeat behavior without a real source packet underneath it.
- AnnualReports was still behind as of `2026-08-09`, showing only the `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, while the official Domino's investor-relations stack already exposed the `2025` annual report and the `2026` quarter materials.
- The `2025` annual package was strong on both scale and operating performance. Total revenues were `$4.94B`, income from operations was `$954.0M`, net income was `$601.7M`, diluted EPS was `$17.57`, and global net store growth reached `776`, ending the year with `22,142` stores.
- The most useful structural disclosure is that Domino's is not just selling pizza; it is explicitly optimizing a delivery-and-carryout system around convenience, transaction counts, franchise royalties, supply-chain throughput, and technology.
- The digital relationship is now central enough to be filing material. Domino's said more than `85%` of U.S. retail sales in `2025` came from digital channels, described technology as vital to long-term success, and disclosed a `$0.375` per-digital-transaction technology fee in fiscal `2025`.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` global retail sales growth excluding foreign currency impact was `3.0%`; U.S. same-store sales growth was `0.1%`; international same-store sales declined `0.1%`; global net store growth was `209`.
- Operating posture: income from operations increased `3.1%`, net income increased `3.6%`, and diluted EPS increased `6.8%` to `$4.07`.
- Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows softer same-store momentum than late `2025`, but the model still converted modest sales growth into operating and EPS growth through franchise royalties, supply-chain gross margin, and repurchases.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` global retail sales growth excluding foreign currency impact was `3.4%`; U.S. same-store sales growth was `0.9%`; international same-store sales declined `0.4%`; global net store growth was `180`.
- Operating posture: revenues increased `3.5%`, income from operations increased `9.6%`, but net income decreased `6.6%` and diluted EPS fell to `$4.13` because of an unfavorable change in remeasurement gains and losses tied to DPC Dash.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because it shows the business can still grow orders, royalties, and operating income even when the reported bottom line is noisier.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` global retail sales growth excluding foreign currency impact was `4.9%`; U.S. same-store sales growth was `3.7%`; international same-store sales growth excluding foreign currency impact was `0.7%`; fourth-quarter global net store growth was `392`.
- Operating posture: income from operations increased `8.0%` in the quarter and `8.5%` for full-year `2025`; full-year net income reached `$601.7M`.
- Structural themes: this is the most important quarter for the packet because it closes the year with a strong operating and market-share story while linking that performance directly to value, convenience, technology, and order growth.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Domino's gives the archive a scaled restaurant operator where the relationship surface is ordering flow, convenience, and repeat purchase rather than classic points-heavy loyalty alone.
- Consumer: this company is a useful read on affordable routine spending, especially in a pressured consumer backdrop where value and convenience matter.
- Cultural: Domino's helps show that consumer habit can be built through operational speed and ordering ease, not only through premium status or aspirational branding.
- Loyalty and technology: Domino's is one of the cleaner filing-backed cases that "owning the ordering flow" is economically meaningful. More than `85%` of U.S. retail sales came from digital channels in `2025`, and digital transactions carry explicit fee economics.
- Business model: the packet makes clear that Domino's is really a franchise-royalty and supply-chain engine whose economics improve when transaction counts, same-store sales, and store growth all move together.
- Competitive positioning: Domino's is especially useful because it now straddles both direct channels and aggregators. The filings show that technology and convenience still matter, but they also show the company is adapting rather than pretending aggregators do not exist.

## Working interpretation

- Domino's materially improves the archive because it lets the loyalty-economy work say something more precise than "apps matter." The filings show that digital ordering, transaction frequency, carryout and delivery leadership, and franchise economics are tightly linked.
- The most important evidence here is not a vague claim that Domino's is "really a tech company." It is that the company explicitly discloses digital penetration, technology-fee economics, a convenience-and-order-flow strategy, and an operating model where same-store sales and net store growth directly feed royalties, supply-chain revenues, and profits.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Domino's is a strong archive addition for understanding how direct ordering, routine consumer habit, value positioning, and asset-light franchise economics reinforce each other in a mass-market restaurant business.
