## What changed

- Domino's materially strengthens the archive because it turns the "direct ordering and habit loop" thesis into a filing-backed operator packet rather than just a clever consumer-internet analogy.
- The `2025` annual package shows a strong financial year: total revenues were `$4.94B`, income from operations was `$954.0M`, net income was `$601.7M`, diluted EPS was `$17.57`, and global net store growth was `776`, ending the year at `22,142` stores.
- The strategic point is more important than the raw restaurant numbers. Domino's explicitly frames its model around delivery, carryout, convenience, technology, and transaction count, not just menu items.
- The filing set shows how much digital behavior now matters. Domino's said more than `85%` of U.S. retail sales in `2025` came from digital channels, and the annual filing also discloses a per-digital-transaction technology fee of `$0.375` in fiscal `2025`.
- Domino's is also useful because it is not a pure app-only story. The annual filing ties digital ordering to an asset-light franchise system, supply-chain economics, carryout and delivery dominance, fortressing, and a value proposition built to keep transaction counts moving.

## Open questions

- How durable is the digital and convenience edge now that aggregators are stronger and Domino's is listed on both DoorDash and Uber Eats marketplaces?
- Does fortressing keep driving order growth and service quality, or does it eventually pressure unit economics at the store level?
- Is Domino's best understood as a technology-enabled restaurant operator, or as a franchise-and-supply-chain machine that happens to use digital ordering as the main customer-relationship surface?
