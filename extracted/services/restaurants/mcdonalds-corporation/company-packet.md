  - quarter 1: Q2 2026

## Annual report takeaways

- McDonald's is a high-priority archive addition because the loyalty-economy packet explicitly grew out of a McDonald's annual-report thread, yet the local archive previously had no direct McDonald's source packet.
- AnnualReports was still lagging here as of `2026-08-09`, showing only the `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, while the official McDonald's investor stack already exposed the `2025` annual report and the `2026` quarter materials.
- The `2025` annual filing shows a still-powerful franchise-and-brand machine: global comparable sales increased `3.1%`, consolidated revenues increased `4%` to `$26.9B`, net income increased `4%` to `$8.563B`, and diluted EPS increased `5%` to `$11.95`.
- The more important thematic change is disclosure style. McDonald's now reports loyalty metrics prominently enough that they belong in the archive's consumer, cultural, and loyalty-economy analysis rather than being treated as a side note.
- This company matters because it bridges classic physical retail, franchise economics, global scale, digital ordering, value positioning, and loyalty behavior in one operator.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` global comparable sales increased `1.3%`, with U.S. comparable sales up `0.8%`; systemwide sales increased `5%` to `$37B`; consolidated revenues increased `4%` (`2%` in constant currencies).
- Loyalty and engagement: across `70` loyalty markets, trailing-twelve-month systemwide sales to loyalty members increased over `20%` to `$40B`, with nearly `220M` 90-day active loyalty users as of quarter-end.
- Management message: the quarter still leaned heavily on value, affordability, and digital / personalized offers, but it also suggested that U.S. execution and consumer caution had become more visible.
- Structural themes: McDonald's is useful here because even in a softer quarter it still reported loyalty scale directly, which makes the company a stronger operator proof point for the archive than broader loyalty rhetoric alone.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` global comparable sales increased `3.8%`; systemwide sales increased `11%` (`6%` in constant currencies) to over `$34B`; consolidated revenues increased `9%` (`4%` in constant currencies).
- Loyalty and engagement: across `70` loyalty markets, systemwide sales to loyalty members were over `$38B` for the trailing twelve months and over `$9B` for the quarter.
- Management message: management framed the quarter around value leadership, marketing, and menu innovation, with loyalty and scale helping support traffic.
- Structural themes: this quarter is the stronger read for the McDonald's loyalty thesis, because it shows comp growth, systemwide sales growth, and loyalty engagement all moving in the right direction together.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` global comparable sales increased `5.7%`; full-year `2025` global comparable sales increased `3.1%`; full-year consolidated revenues increased `4%` to `$26.9B`; full-year diluted EPS reached `$11.95`.
- Loyalty and engagement: across `70` loyalty markets, full-year systemwide sales to loyalty members increased `20%` to nearly `$37B`, with nearly `210M` 90-day active loyalty users as of year-end.
- Management message: management tied the year-end result to value leadership, improved traffic, and stronger affordability scores, but the loyalty metrics made clear that the digital customer relationship is now part of the operating scorecard.
- Structural themes: this is the most important quarter for the packet because it closes the year with both traditional financial strength and explicit loyalty-scale metrics.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: McDonald's adds a major branded restaurant and franchise operator to Services, broadening the archive beyond lodging, live events, logistics, and business services.
- Consumer: the company is a useful read on value-sensitive consumer behavior rather than only affluent or premium-spend behavior.
- Cultural: McDonald's helps test whether loyalty and habit-forming digital ecosystems matter not just in travel and payments, but in global everyday food occasions.
- Loyalty and technology: the archive now has direct evidence that McDonald's tracks loyalty-member sales and 90-day active users at enormous scale, which makes the loyalty-economy packet more defensible.
- Business model: the loyalty story matters even more inside a franchise-heavy system because digital engagement can increase traffic and mix while the operator still benefits from rent, royalties, and systemwide sales growth.
- Competitive positioning: McDonald's gives the archive a cleaner example of how value, marketing, digital offers, and loyalty can work together when lower-income consumers are more pressured.

## Working interpretation

- McDonald's materially improves the archive because it finally gives the loyalty-economy work a direct large-scale restaurant operator instead of forcing that packet to borrow examples from other sectors.
- The most important current evidence is not a clever one-liner about apps. It is that McDonald's now discloses loyalty-member sales and active-user counts as core metrics, alongside the usual comparable-sales and revenue numbers.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, McDonald's is a strong addition for understanding how value, loyalty, digital ordering, and franchise economics interact in a mass-market consumer business.
