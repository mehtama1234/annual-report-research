## Annual report takeaways

- RBI reported nearly $47 billion of annual system-wide sales, more than
  33,000 restaurants in more than 120 countries and territories, and more than
  95% of system-wide restaurants franchised at year-end (pp. 4-5).
- The four-brand system reported 2025 global system-wide sales of $46.762
  billion: Tim Hortons $8.248 billion, Burger King $29.368 billion, Popeyes
  $7.789 billion, and Firehouse Subs $1.357 billion (p. 4).
- Management describes quality, service, and convenience as strategic pillars,
  and explicitly links new products and guest feedback to traffic and guest
  base expansion (p. 5).
- The report describes mobile/web ordering, kiosks, third-party delivery,
  digital loyalty programs, and technology including AI for drive-through and
  ordering/payment choices (p. 9).
- Company restaurant sales were $2.348 billion and company restaurant expenses
  were $1.968 billion in 2025; consolidated revenue was $9.434 billion and
  operating income was $2.202 billion (p. 67). These are consolidated/company
  reporting measures, not a clean franchisee unit-margin read-through.
- The report explicitly connects consumer discretionary spending, value
  shifts, labor costs, geopolitical conflict, supply chains, and guest traffic
  to restaurant sales and profitability risks (pp. 12-15).

## Seven research themes

### Premium-value bifurcation

Reported evidence: RBI says restaurant sales, guest traffic, and profitability
are correlated with discretionary spending, inflation, unemployment, income,
confidence, and shifts toward affordability and value menus (p. 12). The
report also identifies product quality, affordability, and service as bases of
competition (p. 9). This establishes a value-sensitive exposure, not a dated
income-cohort or price-elasticity estimate.

### Restaurant margin vise

Reported evidence: 2025 company restaurant sales were $2.348 billion and
company restaurant expenses were $1.968 billion; consolidated supply-chain,
company-restaurant, franchise/property, advertising, and G&A costs are shown
separately (p. 67). This provides a bounded expense surface but does not
isolate labor, commodity, price, mix, or franchisee margin transmission.

### Health-conscious consumption

The report describes food quality, nutrition, transparency, food safety, and
wellness trends as strategic or risk considerations (pp. 10, 13). It does not
provide a dated health-oriented demand measure, menu mix shift, or occasion
response. Theme presence is contextual and the mechanism remains absent.

### Labor and automation

Reported evidence: RBI identifies labor costs, staffing difficulties, and
franchisee margins as risks, while describing digital ordering, kiosks,
loyalty, AI, and technology-enhanced guest experience (pp. 9, 14). The report
does not establish labor-hour savings, service-time improvement, or causal
automation impact.

### Supply-chain and geopolitical volatility

Reported evidence: RBI operates supply and distribution networks, identifies
commodity prices and supply-chain operations as risks, and describes Ukraine,
Middle East, Latin America, and East Asia conflicts as possible sources of
cost, traffic, and development disruption (pp. 8, 14-15). These are exposure
and risk disclosures, not realized company-specific shock estimates.

### Regulatory fragmentation

Reported evidence: the report discusses federal, state, local, Canadian, and
international regulatory, delivery, food-safety, privacy, labor, tax, and
franchise-law exposures (pp. 3, 9, 13-14, 58-59). The packet does not quantify
the resulting cost or establish a mechanism to a particular company outcome.

### Capital concentration and ownership

Reported evidence: more than 95% of system-wide restaurants are franchised;
RBI owns or operates a smaller company-restaurant base, including restaurants
acquired in the Carrols transaction, and plans further refranchising (pp. 4,
6). The report also identifies debt, liquidity, franchisee profitability, and
capital commitments. Ownership structure is contextual and does not by itself
prove superior economics or causality.

## Open evidence boundary

The report supplies bounded source-owned context and consolidated/company
operating facts. It does not supply a complete like-period traffic/mix bridge,
franchisee labor-hour measure, clean system-wide margin denominator, or a
falsifier for the observatory's proposed signal-to-company mechanisms. Those
remain open for packet-bound LLM adjudication.
