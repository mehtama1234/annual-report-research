## What changed

- Chewy adds a recurring pet-care and autoship relationship to Services rather than another conventional brick-and-mortar format.
- The annual and quarter materials show a wider interface than "online pet store" implies. The company ties repeat behavior to websites, mobile apps, prescriptions, wellness, and service offerings that help pet parents manage care, not just purchase food.
- The economics improved materially through the target window. Full-year fiscal `2025` net sales reached `$12.60B`, active customers reached `21.3M`, and adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to `5.7%` for the year and `7.5%` in Q1 `2026`.
- The Q3 `2025` presentation signals are especially useful because they make the recurring behavior explicit: about `84%` Autoship mix, stronger net active-customer additions, and high net sales per active customer.
- That makes Chewy a useful bridge case between value and replenishment retail, consumer-health adjacency, and digital relationship platforms elsewhere in the archive.

## Open questions

- How durable is Chewy's recent margin expansion if the pet category remains more mixed and the consumer backdrop stays pressured?
- How much of the moat comes from Autoship and customer service versus pharmacy, prescriptions, and broader health-adjacent services?
- Should Chewy ultimately be grouped analytically with digital retail, consumer-health routine platforms, or subscription-like recurring consumer interfaces more than with generic Services taxonomy?
