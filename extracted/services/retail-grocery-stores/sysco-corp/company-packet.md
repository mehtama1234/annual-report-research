# Sysco Corporation Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Company: Sysco Corporation
- Sector: Consumer Goods
- Industry: Food Wholesale
- Ticker: SYY
- Fiscal year-end: June
- Assigned CLI 8 sub-lane: institutional supply and replenishment
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: FY2026 Q4
  - quarter 2: FY2026 Q3
  - quarter 3: FY2026 Q2

## Why Sysco matters in this frontier

- Sysco is a pure middle-layer company. It connects food producers to restaurants, hospitality venues, healthcare kitchens, education, and other institutional buyers that need dependable replenishment rather than one-off transactions.
- The company expresses the core economics this frontier is meant to explain: procurement scale, inventory positioning, route density, working-capital discipline, and customer embeddedness.
- Sysco also gives this frontier a bridge into service-layer physical demand. Its volumes reflect whether restaurants, travel, workplace dining, and institutional meal systems are actually functioning.

## Annual report takeaways

- Sysco’s 2025 annual report presents a business built around dependable foodservice distribution, not consumer branding. Management emphasizes customer relationships, culinary support, and a supply chain that delivers on time and in full.
- Fiscal `2025` revenue reached `$81.4 billion`, operating income reached `$3.1 billion`, adjusted operating income reached `$3.5 billion`, cash flow from operations reached `$2.5 billion`, and free cash flow reached `$1.8 billion`.
- The annual letter is unusually explicit about the operating blueprint:
  - International is described as a growth engine.
  - U.S. National is a contract, mix, and efficiency discipline business.
  - U.S. Local is the service-intensive branch-and-route business that needed performance repair.
  - Specialty is a faster-growing, higher-profit lane tied to differentiated assortment.
- That structure matters because it shows Sysco is not just “food wholesale.” It is a layered operating platform where contract economics, local service, specialty mix, and global purchasing all interact.
- Management also signals a cautious macro backdrop. The annual letter says Sysco expected a cautious end consumer entering fiscal `2026`, which is important because it shows the company already understood that downstream demand would stay pressured even as volumes recovered.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: FY2026 Q4 sales increased `4.7%` to `$22.1 billion`, gross profit increased `3.7%` to `$4.1 billion`, operating income increased `10.6%` to `$983 million`, and adjusted EPS increased `3.4%` to `$1.53`.
- Volume and channel signal: U.S. Foodservice volume increased `2.5%` and U.S. local volume increased `2.6%`, showing the branch-and-route model improved materially by year-end.
- Management message: leadership pointed to positive case growth across local, national, and international businesses plus continued supply-chain productivity gains.
- Structural meaning: this is what good middle-layer execution looks like. Procurement, routing, mix, and local sales execution align and let a distributor expand earnings faster than demand alone would suggest.
- Forward indicator: FY2027 guidance calls for `6%-7%` sales growth and `9%-11%` adjusted EPS growth on a 53-week basis, including about `$100 million` of AI-enabled efficiency improvements.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: FY2026 Q3 sales increased `4.7%`, gross profit increased `6.5%` to `$3.8 billion`, adjusted operating income was roughly stable at `$768 million`, and adjusted EPS declined `2.1%` to `$0.94`.
- Volume and channel signal: U.S. Foodservice volume increased `2.3%` and local volumes increased `3.3%`, which indicates healthy customer activity even though reported earnings stayed pressured.
- Management message: Sysco emphasized confidence in full-year guidance and highlighted improving cash generation.
- Structural meaning: this quarter shows the tension inside distribution businesses. Volumes can improve and working capital can get better while margins still absorb labor, mix, transformation, and cost noise.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: FY2026 Q2 sales increased `3.0%`, gross profit increased `3.9%` to `$3.8 billion`, adjusted operating income increased `3.1%` to `$807 million`, and adjusted EPS increased `6.5%` to `$0.99`.
- Volume and channel signal: U.S. Foodservice volume increased `0.8%` and local volumes increased `1.2%`, which is modest growth but important because it signals replenishment continuity in a cautious environment.
- Management message: Sysco still expected full-year results at the high end of guidance.
- Structural meaning: even a slower demand quarter can work if pricing discipline, sourcing efficiency, and route execution stay intact.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer: the end consumer remains cautious and value-sensitive, but away-from-home food demand has not broken. That keeps distributors relevant even when restaurant operators are pressured.
- Cultural: convenience, reliability, and labor-saving support matter more for downstream operators. Distributors that help kitchens stay open become more embedded than simple wholesalers.
- Societal: hospitals, schools, hotels, and restaurants rely on replenishment networks they do not control themselves. This creates dependence on scaled intermediaries with dense logistics footprints.
- Industrial: route density, sourcing leverage, inflation management, incentive compensation, and warehouse or sales-capacity investment all shape earnings more than abstract volume growth does.
- Labor: Sysco’s disclosures imply that labor availability and sales-force effectiveness still matter, but the current response is better tools, routing, forecasting, and process redesign rather than pure headcount expansion.
- Technology: AI appears here as operational plumbing. Forecasting, inventory management, routing optimization, and back-office automation are the real technology story in this frontier.
- Capital allocation: Sysco returns capital consistently, but it also continues to invest in supply-chain capacity, customer-facing systems, and targeted M&A. That combination reinforces its embedded middle-layer role.

## Frontier interpretation

- Sysco helps explain how food actually moves through the economy once it leaves producers. The company owns the logistical and customer relationship layer that lets institutional eating channels function.
- The repeat pattern is that distributors with scale do not just earn spread on goods. They earn through reliability, route density, procurement power, mix management, and by reducing the operational burden on customers.
- Sysco also shows that middle-layer businesses are sensitive to culture and society even without direct consumer brands. When households trade down, eat out less often, or demand more value, those shifts propagate into distributor volumes, margins, and route efficiency.
