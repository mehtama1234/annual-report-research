# US Foods Holding Corp. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: US Foods Holding Corp.
- Sector: Consumer Goods
- Industry: Food Wholesale
- Ticker: USFD
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Assigned CLI 8 sub-lane: institutional supply and replenishment
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Why this name matters

- US Foods is the strongest next non-healthcare add because it broadens institutional supply and replenishment through a second flagship foodservice distributor rather than deepening only one healthcare sub-branch.
- It matters for CLI 8 because it makes food procurement, route density, customer segmentation, case-volume mix, private label, and digital ordering visible inside the middle layer between producers and restaurants, hospitals, hotels, schools, and government kitchens.
- It also gives the frontier a clearer read on how consumer caution and hospitality demand propagate into B2B distribution economics without collapsing into a direct consumer-retail packet.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual package is explicit about the business model. US Foods supplies approximately `250,000` customer locations nationwide, including independent restaurants, chains, healthcare, hospitality, government, and education.
- Scale and physical reach are core to the story. The annual report describes over `70` distribution facilities, a fleet of over `6,500` trucks, and more than `90` cash-and-carry locations.
- This is not just a logistics spread business. Management frames the relationship around local sales support, chefs, category management, business solutions, private-label growth, and the `MOXe` digital platform that helps customers manage ordering and inventory.
- Fiscal `2025` results were strong enough to make the packet worth carrying. Net sales rose `4.1%` to `$39.4B`, gross profit rose `5.1%` to `$6.9B`, net income rose `36.8%` to `$676M`, adjusted EBITDA rose `11.0%` to `$1.93B`, and adjusted diluted EPS rose `26.3%` to `$3.98`.
- The annual report also shows why this is a real middle-layer frontier. US Foods believes it can grow through better customer retention, higher private-label penetration, stronger case mix, improved profitability, and deeper local execution rather than only through broad food inflation.
- Cash flow matters because this is a working-capital and route business. Fiscal `2025` cash flows from operating activities reached `$1.369B`, with free cash flow of `$965M`.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: net sales increased `4.5%` to `$10.5B`, gross profit increased `8.0%` to `$1.9B`, net income increased `22.8%` to `$275M`, adjusted EBITDA increased `10.2%` to a record `$604M`, and adjusted diluted EPS increased `21.0%` to `$1.44`.
- Volume and channel signal: total case volume increased `1.9%`, while independent restaurant case volume increased `5.1%`, healthcare volume increased `3.5%`, and hospitality volume increased `4.4%`.
- Structural read: this quarter is the cleanest proof that US Foods belongs in CLI 8. The company is monetizing broad institutional demand through food flow, sourcing, route efficiency, and customer mix rather than through end-consumer brand pull.
- Working-capital read: inventories were tightly controlled at `$1.703B` versus `$1.711B` at fiscal year-end `2025`, even as sales and gross profit grew.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: net sales increased `2.8%` to `$9.6B`, gross profit increased `2.4%` to `$1.7B`, net income increased `0.9%` to `$116M`, adjusted EBITDA increased `6.2%` to `$413M`, and adjusted diluted EPS increased `14.7%` to `$0.78`.
- Volume and channel signal: total case volume increased `1.4%`, while independent restaurant case volume increased `4.6%`, healthcare volume increased `3.7%`, and hospitality volume increased `5.0%`.
- Pressure read: management still described a deteriorating macro environment and weather-related disruptions, which makes the quarter useful for showing resilience rather than simple smooth growth.
- Structural read: even with a softer backdrop, the company kept growing through share gains in target customer types and steady local execution.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: Q4 net sales increased `3.3%` to `$9.8B`, gross profit increased `3.2%` to `$1.7B`, net income increased `178.8%` to `$184M`, adjusted EBITDA increased `11.1%` to `$490M`, and adjusted diluted EPS increased `23.8%` to `$1.04`.
- Full-year signal: full-year `2025` net sales reached `$39.4B`, net income reached `$676M`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$1.93B`, and adjusted EBITDA margin expanded `30` basis points to `4.9%`.
- Volume and channel signal: total case volume increased `0.8%` in Q4, while independent restaurant case volume increased `4.1%`, healthcare volume increased `2.9%`, and hospitality volume increased `3.1%`.
- Structural read: the year-end quarter shows how a food distributor can widen margins even when total case growth is not explosive, as long as customer mix, cost-of-goods execution, inventory management, and local share gains improve together.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- US Foods is not consumer-facing in the usual sense, but it is still one of the best ways to read consumer caution indirectly. Restaurant traffic, value sensitivity, menu pressure, and hospitality demand all show up first in case mix and route economics.
- The cultural pattern here is that food away from home remains socially durable, but downstream operators increasingly need distributors to act as procurement, menu, and operating partners rather than simple box movers.
- The societal pattern is institutional dependence. Hospitals, schools, hotels, and restaurants do not want to own broad inventory positions themselves. They rely on foodservice distributors to absorb complexity, timing, and assortment risk.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- This is a route-density and working-capital business. Procurement discipline, food inflation, private-label mix, LIFO effects, inventory control, and local case density all matter at once.
- The quarter chain also shows the balance between growth and exposure. Independent restaurant growth is strong, but chain volumes remain weaker, and the business still depends on an operating environment where restaurant and hospitality customers survive and order consistently.
- US Foods is also clearly trying to widen its moat through digital workflow. `MOXe` and broader customer-support tooling are not side details; they are part of the embeddedness strategy.
- Capital allocation remains active. Share repurchases stayed material in `2025`, `Q1 2026`, and `Q2 2026`, which reinforces that the model can throw off cash even while still investing in the operating network.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Institutional replenishment is not only healthcare.` US Foods makes the same recurring-demand logic visible through food, kitchens, hospitality, and public-institution meal systems.
- `Route density and customer segmentation matter more than product glamour.` The distributor wins by being in the workflow, not by owning the brand on the plate.
- `Independent operators still matter.` The repeated emphasis on independent restaurant case growth shows that fragmented customers can still create a powerful scaled distribution moat when the intermediary is strong enough.
- `Digital tools deepen traditional distribution.` Ordering, inventory management, and customer-service platforms widen what would otherwise look like a lower-multiple logistics business.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Sysco Corporation`: Sysco is the clearest category flagship, while US Foods provides the best like-for-like comparison to test whether the foodservice lane is a one-name story or a real frontier pattern.
- Against `Henry Schein, Inc.` and `Baxter International Inc.`: those names explain recurring healthcare replenishment, while US Foods shows the same replenishment logic in food, hospitality, and institutional kitchens.
- Against `Hyatt Hotels Corporation`, `Hilton Worldwide Holdings Inc.`, and `Marriott International, Inc.`: the lodging packets show end-demand and owner-network economics, while US Foods shows one of the upstream operational layers that helps hospitality venues actually function.

## Working interpretation

- US Foods materially strengthens CLI 8 because it proves that the institutional replenishment lane is not just a healthcare or one-company foodservice story. It is a broader B2B and institutional operating layer with its own recurring economics.
- The bigger pattern is that middle-layer power comes from helping downstream operators stay open, stocked, and efficient while absorbing sourcing, route, and inventory complexity on their behalf.
