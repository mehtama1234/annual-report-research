## What changed

- US Foods gives CLI 8 a second flagship foodservice-distribution packet next to Sysco, which makes the institutional food and hospitality replenishment lane more defensible than a one-name proof.
- The annual report makes the scale and operating model unusually explicit. US Foods supplies approximately `250,000` customer locations nationwide, operates an extensive network of over `70` distribution facilities, uses a fleet of over `6,500` trucks, and runs over `90` cash-and-carry locations.
- The customer set matters because it is exactly the middle layer the frontier is trying to explain: independent restaurants, chains, healthcare, hospitality, government, and education.
- Fiscal `2025` showed real operating improvement. Net sales rose `4.1%` to `$39.4B`, gross profit rose `5.1%` to `$6.9B`, net income rose `36.8%` to `$676M`, and adjusted EBITDA rose `11.0%` to `$1.93B`.
- The annual narrative also shows that US Foods is not only moving boxes. It is deepening customer ties through private-label penetration, chefs and sales support, and the `MOXe` digital platform for ordering and inventory management.

## Working interpretation

- US Foods strengthens CLI 8 because it captures the recurring procurement, routing, and inventory layer between producers and the operators that actually feed restaurants, hotels, hospitals, schools, and institutional kitchens.
- It also broadens the frontier beyond healthcare-heavy expansion by showing how consumer caution, hospitality demand, and institutional meal dependence all propagate through a scaled B2B food-distribution platform.
