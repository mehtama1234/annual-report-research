## What changed

- Services mix: Robert Half gives the Services sector its cleanest direct labor-intermediation and staffing-platform read. The archive now has freight, parcel, recurring local services, compliance-heavy business services, workplace-route services, live entertainment, lodging, and labor-market intermediation in the same sector set.
- Labor-market signal: unlike other service names where labor is an input cost, Robert Half sells labor matching and specialized talent access directly. That makes it a better anchor for `fractional-worker`, contingent-labor, white-collar hiring, and enterprise project-demand analysis.
- Business model: the `2025` `10-K` frames the company around three operating blocks: contract talent solutions, permanent placement talent solutions, and Protiviti consulting. This matters because it separates contingent staffing, permanent hiring demand, and consulting/project work inside one reporting stack.
- Demand read-through: the year-end and 2026 quarter chain points to sequential improvement before a clean year-over-year recovery. Q4 `2025` was still down `6%` reported and `7%` adjusted, Q1 `2026` was down `4%` reported and `6%` adjusted, and Q2 `2026` was down `2%` reported and `3%` adjusted, but management kept emphasizing sequential improvement and better hiring conditions.
- Labor-market nuance: Q2 `2026` is the strongest signal so far because management said permanent placement operations posted adjusted year-over-year growth of `2.5%` while broader hiring demand continued to improve. That makes Robert Half a useful bridge between soft labor-market caution and a possible white-collar hiring turn.
- Countercyclical balance: Protiviti gives the company a consulting, governance, risk, and internal-audit layer that can partially offset cyclical staffing weakness, which makes the packet useful for separating labor-market softness from enterprise project and compliance demand.

## Open questions

- Is Robert Half showing an early cyclical turn in white-collar hiring, or just a modest sequential improvement off a weak base?
- How durable is the permanent-placement rebound if broader business confidence or enterprise budgets soften again?
- Does Protiviti become the stabilizer that matters most in a weak hiring backdrop, or does the archive still need more pure-play staffing names to sharpen the labor-market read?
