## What changed

- Mistras adds the clearest literal `Technical Services` packet now in CLI 8, which matters because it turns inspection, compliance, testing, and uptime support into a direct middle-layer proof rather than an inferred adjacency from facilities or safety-service names.
- The `2025` annual filing says the company delivers asset protection solutions that combine inspection services, products, data management, analytics, and engineering expertise, often on a recurring "run and maintain" basis at customer facilities.
- Full-year `2025` revenue was `$724.0M`, gross profit was `$204.5M`, gross margin was `28.2%`, income from operations was `$40.6M`, and net income attributable to Mistras was `$16.8M`.
- The annual filing also shows the right customer logic for CLI 8: customers use Mistras to inspect mission-critical assets without shutting down the asset or entire facility, which means the service is really sold as uptime preservation, compliance, and risk reduction.
- The latest reported-quarter chain shows a company with better margins and stronger technical positioning but still visible working-capital strain, capital spending needs, and debt reduction pressure.

## Working interpretation

- Mistras materially improves CLI 8 because it makes the technical-services layer literal: instead of only showing recurring outsourced labor, safety monitoring, or route density, it shows how industrial customers buy inspection, non-destructive testing, data integrity, and engineering support to keep critical assets running.
- It also broadens the frontier beyond pure distribution and recurring field service by showing that the middle layer can monetize reliability, certification, and operational uptime in regulated industrial environments where shutdown avoidance is itself the product.
