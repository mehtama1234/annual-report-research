# AT&T Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: AT&T Inc.
- Sector: Services
- Industry: Telecom Services - Domestic
- Ticker: `T`
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: `2025`
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: `2Q 2026`
  - quarter 2: `1Q 2026`
  - quarter 3: `4Q 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- AT&T is the physical-access utility in this lane, and the `2025` annual package is best read as an effort to prove that scale telecom can still create better cash economics through converged fiber and wireless execution.
- The `2025` annual report centers on fiber expansion, `5G` network quality, customer retention, and long-range free-cash-flow improvement rather than on explosive top-line growth.
- That matters for the lane because AT&T is the clean counterweight to Alphabet, Keysight, and KLA. It owns a necessary layer of the stack, but it does not capture the same economics as the demand concentrator, the validation toll collector, or the precision bottleneck supplier.
- The annual framing also shows what telecom management has to optimize: household entrenchment, capital efficiency, leverage discipline, and steady payout support while network investment remains unavoidable.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `2Q 2026` revenue was `$31.6B`, adjusted EBITDA was `$12.3B`, free cash flow was `$4.7B`, and postpaid phone net adds were `432K`.
- Operating posture: management kept framing the quarter around disciplined execution in wireless and fiber rather than around a radical mix shift.
- Structural themes: the quarter reinforces the key lane contrast that usage growth and social indispensability do not automatically create the best profit-capture position.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `1Q 2026` revenue was `$31.5B`, adjusted EBITDA was `$11.6B`, free cash flow was `$2.5B`, and AT&T said it had reached more than `37M` fiber locations.
- Operating posture: fiber expansion and bundled connectivity remained the core operating thesis.
- Structural themes: AT&T is trying to deepen the household relationship and make its network utility harder to replace, but the path still runs through heavy capital discipline rather than software-like operating leverage.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `4Q 2025` revenue was `$33.5B`, adjusted EBITDA was `$11.2B`, adjusted EPS was `$0.52`, and free cash flow was `$4.2B`.
- Operating posture: management said it met or exceeded `2025` guidance and supported improved adjusted EBITDA growth, adjusted EPS growth, and higher free cash flow through `2028`.
- Structural themes: the year-end reset matters because it positions telecom as a discipline business. The moat is persistence, network reach, and household utility status, not velocity.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: AT&T is the batch's asset-heavy connectivity utility and last-mile access owner.
- Consumer: households increasingly want one provider to carry more of their connectivity stack through fiber, wireless, and bundle adjacency.
- Cultural: connectivity is treated as default infrastructure, not a premium feature, which raises social importance without guaranteeing superior economics.
- Regulatory: telecom oversight, spectrum policy, and network obligations remain live constraints in a way they do not for most other batch names.
- Labor: field operations, maintenance, and network deployment matter more here than at the platform or tool layers.
- Technology: fiber and `5G` are the operating rails, but the business model is still about execution density more than frontier novelty.
- Capital allocation: leverage discipline and free-cash-flow credibility matter alongside customer growth.

## Lane role and thematic interpretation

- Lane role: physical network owner and regulated access utility.
- What makes it distinctive in this batch: AT&T absorbs much more of the physical burden of the connectivity stack than the other names while converting that burden into slower but steadier cash generation.
- Main moat: last-mile network reach, bundled fiber-plus-wireless relationships, and the practical difficulty of replacing trusted household connectivity.
- Main fragility: capital intensity, price competition, and regulation constrain how much upside AT&T captures from the same data and AI demand that enriches other layers of the stack.
- Bigger-picture read: AT&T is the batch's clearest example of infrastructure necessity without control-point economics.
