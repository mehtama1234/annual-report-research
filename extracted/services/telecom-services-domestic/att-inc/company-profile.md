## What changed

- AT&T is still the asset-heavy utility layer in CLI 5. The quarter chain stays centered on fiber expansion, wireless retention, and free-cash-flow discipline rather than on software-like operating leverage.
- The company is trying to turn necessity into stickier household economics. Fiber and wireless are being sold as a converged relationship rather than as isolated products.
- The burden stack is still obvious. Network build, maintenance, spectrum, and debt discipline remain inseparable from the business model.
- Social indispensability does not automatically create the cleanest economics. AT&T carries a critical layer of modern life, but much of the best value capture still migrates to control layers, tooling layers, and bottleneck suppliers elsewhere in the lane.
- CLI 5 role: AT&T is the clearest current proof that owning the physical access layer can make a company necessary without giving it the strongest profit-capture position.

## Open questions

- Can AT&T deepen household control through fiber-plus-wireless convergence fast enough to offset the structural burden of capital intensity and pricing competition?
- Does the utility-like cash profile get stronger from here, or does network spending keep capping how much upside AT&T can retain from rising connectivity demand?
