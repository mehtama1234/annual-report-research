# Roku Company Packet

## Why Roku belongs in this frontier

Roku is one of the clearest bridge companies between the `attention and influence` lane and the `communications and access` lane. It owns a consumer interface at the household edge, but it monetizes that position through advertising, subscriptions, recommendations, billing relationships, and device distribution rather than through a classic cable or wireless model.

That makes Roku a strong frontier packet because this archive is supposed to explain how attention, access, and device infrastructure fit together. Roku shows that the most valuable communications layer in the living room may be the operating system and home screen that decide what viewers see first, which services get discovered, and which ad inventory and subscription offers are easiest to buy.

## Business role

- Streaming operating-system and TV-interface owner
- Household attention gateway through the Roku home screen and content-discovery layer
- CTV advertising platform with open DSP and SSP integrations
- Subscription, billing, and merchandising intermediary across streaming services
- Low-margin device layer supporting a higher-value recurring platform model

## Annual report read: FY2025

The `2025` annual report and FY2025 shareholder letter show Roku maturing into a platform-monetization business rather than a pure streaming-device vendor.

FY2025 operating scale was substantial:

- total net revenue: `$4.737B`
- platform revenue: `$4.145B`
- gross profit: `$2.074B`
- streaming hours: `145.6B`, up `15%`

Management framed `2025` around the differentiation of its TV streaming platform, expansion of platform monetization, positive net income, higher adjusted EBITDA margin, and record trailing free cash flow. The key interpretive point is that Roku's economic center of gravity is now the interface and billing layer, not the box itself.

That matters for this frontier because Roku sits directly on the line between access ownership and attention monetization. It connects users, publishers, and advertisers on the TV screen, and it increasingly gets paid not only for hardware distribution but for discovery, advertising, subscription conversion, and usage inside the operating environment.

## Quarter chain

### Q4 2025

Q4 `2025` closed the year with record platform scale and confirmed that Roku had become a profitable CTV interface operator rather than just a device brand.

- total net revenue: `$1.395B`
- platform revenue: `$1.224B`
- total gross profit: `$607M`
- net income: `$81M`
- adjusted EBITDA: `$169M`
- free cash flow (TTM): `$484M`

Key read:

- Roku ended `2025` with positive net income and materially larger platform monetization.
- The Roku Channel was already the `#2` app on the platform by engagement in the U.S., which means Roku was not only distributing third-party media but increasingly benefiting from owned-and-operated attention as well.
- The quarter is useful for this frontier because it shows the device layer becoming strategically subordinate to platform monetization, billing, and interface control.

### Q1 2026

Q1 `2026` made the business model clearer by breaking Platform into `Advertising` and `Subscriptions`.

- total net revenue: `$1.249B`
- platform revenue: `$1.131B`
- total gross profit: `$565M`
- net income: `$86M`
- adjusted EBITDA: `$148M`
- streaming hours: `38.7B`, up `8%`
- free cash flow (TTM): `$539M`

Key read:

- Management said Platform revenue grew `28%` year over year, driven by strength in both Advertising and Subscriptions.
- Roku explicitly framed the Roku Experience, including the home screen, content discovery, and AI-powered recommendations, as a competitive advantage that drives engagement, advertising, and subscriptions.
- This is a high-signal quarter for the frontier because it shows the household interface becoming a multi-product monetization layer rather than a single ad-supported screen.

### Q2 2026

Q2 `2026` is the strongest quarter in the chain for understanding Roku's frontier relevance because it ties household interface control directly to ad performance, subscription conversion, sports discovery, and AI-enabled navigation.

- total net revenue: `$1.355B`
- platform revenue: `$1.221B`
- total gross profit: `$674M`
- net income: `$164M`
- adjusted EBITDA: `$254M`
- streaming hours: `37.9B`, up `7%`
- free cash flow (TTM): `$704M`

Key read:

- Advertising revenue grew to `$673M`, subscriptions revenue grew to `$548M`, and both were described as drivers of monetization diversity.
- Roku called the Home Screen one of the most valuable pieces of real estate in TV and said it was used by more than half of U.S. broadband households before viewing decisions are made.
- Management said the new Home Screen improved household retention in the U.S., which is important because interface retention directly expands the base of users Roku can monetize.
- Roku also described AI-enhanced search and recommendations as future monetization drivers because they can improve subscription conversion, retention, streaming hours, advertising performance, and merchandising.
- The company emphasized open integrations with major DSPs and SSPs, showing that Roku is trying to be the operating environment where fragmented TV attention becomes buyable and measurable at scale.

## Frontier interpretation

### 1. The consumer interface is becoming the real communications choke point

Roku shows that in modern TV distribution, the most strategic control point may not be the physical network alone. It may be the software layer that viewers open first, search through, and transact inside. That turns the home screen into a communications asset, an ad surface, and a subscription funnel all at once.

### 2. Attention monetization is moving into operating-system real estate

Roku's business makes clear that the TV operating system is now monetizable media inventory. Discovery modules, sponsored placements, content zones, and recommendations are not secondary UI details. They are premium attention surfaces at the point of intent formation.

### 3. The best access businesses now mix ads, subscriptions, and billing

Roku is valuable in this frontier because it does not rely on only one monetization engine. It combines advertising, subscriptions, billing relationships, content-discovery control, and devices. That makes it a better bridge packet than a pure broadcaster, a pure telecom carrier, or a pure device maker.

### 4. AI in media is becoming an interface economics story

Roku's repeated focus on AI-powered recommendations and better search is important because it shows AI entering the living room as a discovery and conversion layer, not just as a back-end efficiency tool. The monetization implication is straightforward: better interpretation of viewer intent can raise ad value, subscription acquisition, and retention.

### 5. Open interoperability is part of Roku's strategic defense

Unlike a more vertically closed ecosystem, Roku highlighted integrations with major DSPs and SSPs. That suggests one way fragmented streaming markets stay monetizable is through interface owners that remain interoperable enough to attract advertisers, publishers, and subscription partners at the same time.

## Why this packet improves the frontier

Roku materially improves the frontier because it adds a communications-and-access packet that is not just another broadband or wireless network comparison. It brings in:

- household TV operating-system ownership
- direct overlap between access control and attention monetization
- platform economics that sit above devices and below content brands
- a clearer consumer-interface read than a carrier-only packet can provide
- an important bridge between ad-tech fragmentation and household customer ownership

It therefore makes the frontier more coherent by showing how media monetization, interface control, and consumer access are converging inside the living room.
