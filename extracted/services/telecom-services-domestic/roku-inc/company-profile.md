## What changed in this window

- Roku looked less like a hardware company and more like a TV operating system that monetizes household entry points through advertising, subscriptions, merchandising, and billing.
- Management began disaggregating Platform into `Advertising` and `Subscriptions`, making clear that the streaming-interface business is not a single ad bucket but a two-engine monetization model.
- The Roku Experience, especially the Home Screen, was framed as high-value media real estate reaching more than half of U.S. broadband households before viewing decisions are made.
- The latest quarter tied interface control directly to AI-enabled discovery, with Roku explicitly describing AI-powered recommendations and improved search as future monetization levers.
- Open ad-tech interoperability remained central: Roku highlighted major DSP and SSP integrations rather than trying to present itself as a closed advertising stack.

## Open questions

- How durable is Roku's interface control if TV operating systems become more contested or if large content owners push users into more direct distribution paths?
- Can political and sports-driven advertising strength translate into steadier all-cycle ad growth?
- Does the device layer remain strategically useful if the platform increasingly earns its economics from software, distribution, and billing rather than from hardware volume itself?
