# Verizon Communications Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Verizon Communications Inc.
- Sector: Services
- Industry: Telecom Services - Domestic
- Ticker: `VZ`
- Fiscal year-end: December 31
- Target annual report year: `2025`
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: `2Q 2026`
  - quarter 2: `1Q 2026`
  - quarter 3: `4Q 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- Verizon's official annual-report page carries the `2025` annual report PDF, while the saved AnnualReports company page still shows `2024` as the latest archived annual package, so IR and SEC are the authoritative current-period sources here.
- The `2025` annual framing is a telecom-turnaround story rather than a pure defensive-utility story: Verizon said it delivered more than `1M` total mobility and broadband net additions, including `616K` postpaid phone net additions, while producing `$37.1B` of cash flow from operations and `$20.1B` of free cash flow.
- The annual packet also makes the balance-sheet burden explicit. Verizon ended `2025` with `$131.1B` of total unsecured debt and `$110.1B` of net unsecured debt while still spending `$17.0B` on capital expenditures.
- Frontier is the key strategic bridge between the annual and the `2026` quarter chain. Management uses the acquisition to argue that Verizon can move from mature-wireless utility economics toward a larger converged fiber-and-wireless household relationship.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `2Q 2026` mobility and broadband service revenue reached approximately `$23.4B`, up `2.8%` year over year; total operating revenue was `$34.3B`, down `0.7%`; postpaid phone net additions were `184K`; broadband net additions were `348K`, including `193K` fixed wireless and `155K` fiber broadband additions.
- Guidance: Verizon raised full-year guidance for the second consecutive quarter and said mobility and broadband service revenue growth is expected to move toward approximately `4.0%` in `4Q 2026`.
- Management message: the saved earnings release frames the quarter as proof that lower churn, lower subsidy intensity, and simpler converged offers are creating a structural growth inflection.
- Structural themes: the telecom owner is trying to improve economics not by inventing a new product layer, but by tightening customer economics, expanding fiber density, and using AI infrastructure demand as an incremental growth tailwind.
- Interpretation: Verizon is still a capital-heavy network utility, but `2Q 2026` is stronger than a normal telecom quarter because it suggests the company may finally be improving the monetization of the same usage growth that historically leaked upstream to platforms and downstream to tooling vendors.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `1Q 2026` total operating revenue was `$34.4B`, up `2.9%`; consolidated net income was `$5.1B`; consolidated adjusted EBITDA was `$13.4B`; adjusted EPS was `$1.28`; cash flow from operations was `$8.0B`; and free cash flow was `$3.8B`.
- Guidance: Verizon raised adjusted EPS guidance and pushed expected retail postpaid phone net additions toward the upper half of its `750K` to `1M` range.
- Management message: the company emphasized that it posted its first positive first-quarter postpaid phone net additions since `2013`, which management uses as evidence that the turnaround is moving from marketing language into measurable subscriber performance.
- Structural themes: Frontier results were included beginning January 20, 2026, so the quarter also marks the beginning of the company's larger fiber-scale integration test.
- Interpretation: `1Q 2026` is the cleanest proof point that Verizon's operating model is being rebuilt around better churn, better unit economics, and more converged household relationships instead of relying only on industry-wide wireless maturity.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: the `4Q 2025` and full-year release said Verizon delivered `2025` total operating revenue of `$138.2B`, consolidated net income of `$17.6B`, consolidated adjusted EBITDA of `$50.0B`, cash flow from operations of `$37.1B`, free cash flow of `$20.1B`, and capital expenditures of `$17.0B`.
- Guidance: Verizon said it delivered on `2025` financial guidance and linked the Frontier acquisition to expanded fiber access across more than `30M` homes and businesses.
- Management message: the full-year story is that subscriber momentum, broadband additions, and stronger financial execution are restoring strategic flexibility.
- Structural themes: the quarter shows the legacy telecom constraint clearly. Stronger subscriber and broadband performance still has to coexist with a very large debt load and continuous network-spend requirements.
- Interpretation: `4Q 2025` matters because it sets the baseline for the `2026` turnaround narrative: Verizon is not trying to become a software-like margin story; it is trying to prove that a scaled network owner can earn better economics through density, convergence, and tighter execution.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: telecom network owner and connectivity utility
- Consumer: households increasingly respond to simpler bundled connectivity and value framing rather than endless promotional churn
- Cultural: connectivity is now a baseline household expectation, so service reliability and friction reduction matter more than novelty
- Regulatory: telecom oversight, spectrum, transaction review, and infrastructure obligations remain live economic constraints
- Labor: field deployment, customer care, and network operations still matter materially more here than at software or platform control points
- Technology: fiber expansion, fixed wireless access, and early AI infrastructure revenue are the important technical rails in the current quarter chain
- Capital allocation: leverage, buybacks, Frontier integration, and free-cash-flow conversion are inseparable from the growth story

## Lane role and thematic interpretation

- Lane role: physical network owner trying to improve monetization of recurring connectivity demand
- What makes it distinctive in this lane: Verizon is the cleanest current telecom-turnaround contrast to the Batch A names because it is trying to close part of the economics gap between traffic growth and telecom profit capture through convergence, fiber density, and lower subsidy intensity.
- Main moat: dense wireless and fiber infrastructure, trusted household relationships, and the practical replacement cost of national telecom scale
- Main fragility: debt load, capex requirements, regulatory exposure, and the historical tendency for traffic growth to benefit other layers of the stack more than the telecom owner itself
- Bigger-picture read: Verizon is the best current test of whether a telecom utility can materially improve its economics in an AI-and-broadband-heavy environment without ever gaining the control-point margins of platform, software, networking, or semiconductor bottleneck businesses
