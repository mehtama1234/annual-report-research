## What changed

- Verizon is trying to improve the economics of a mature telecom utility rather than pretending to become a software company. The quarter chain is about better churn, better unit economics, and more converged household relationships.
- Frontier is the strategic bridge between the annual and quarterly story. The acquisition gives Verizon a larger fiber footprint and a more credible wireline-plus-wireless convergence case.
- The company is still capital-heavy and debt-heavy, but the current evidence says it is trying to monetize connectivity demand better than telecom carriers historically have.
- Household simplification matters. Simpler bundled offers, lower churn, and premium-network positioning are being used as economic tools, not just marketing language.
- CLI 5 role: Verizon is one of the best current tests of whether a network utility can materially tighten profit capture without ever gaining the cleaner margins of control-layer software or bottleneck hardware names.

## Open questions

- Can Verizon hold onto improved subscriber economics once competitors push back harder on pricing and promotions?
- Does the larger fiber footprint create a durable convergence edge, or does it mainly add integration complexity and more capital burden?
