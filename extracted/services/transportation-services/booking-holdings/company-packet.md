## Annual report takeaways

- Booking is the right next Services name because it fills the missing travel-booking and distribution-platform layer. Delta gives the archive an airline loyalty-currency business, Hilton and Marriott give it branded lodging networks, and Caesars gives it a rewards-and-wallet entertainment loop. Booking adds the digital marketplace that sits upstream of much of that travel demand.
- AnnualReports is only partially useful here. As of `2026-08-09`, it listed the current `2025 Annual Report and Form 10K`, but it left sector and industry as `--None--`. That makes Booking a clean example of why the archive needs a fallback taxonomy and cannot rely on the aggregator as the sole organizing authority.
- The `2025` annual materials frame Booking as a scaled online travel reservation and payments platform rather than a conventional travel operator. Full-year `2025` revenue reached `$26,917M`, segment Adjusted EBITDA less Capex reached `$9,852M`, and the company remained overwhelmingly international with `$24,338M` of revenue outside the U.S. against `$2,579M` in the U.S.
- The annual filing also shows the business mix shift that matters for the broader project. Merchant revenue reached `$17,755M` in `2025` versus `$14,142M` in `2024`, while agency revenue declined to `$7,968M` from `$8,524M`, which reflects the ongoing move from agency to merchant bookings at Booking.com.
- Booking's travel-stack breadth is analytically useful. In `2025`, flight gross bookings increased `29%`, rental car gross bookings increased `9%`, and management kept pushing the Connected Trip and payments logic that sits on top of the core accommodation engine.
- The official IR framing makes the strategic surface unusually explicit: make it easier for everyone to experience the world, put AI at the center of the Connected Trip vision, keep increasing direct mix, widen payments and fintech capabilities, and use loyalty, apps, and localization to deepen traveler behavior.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` total revenue was `$7,352M` and segment Adjusted EBITDA less Capex was `$2,631M`.
- Demand and growth: total gross bookings were `$50,957M`, room nights were `325M`, merchant gross bookings were `$36,996M`, and the mix of gross bookings generated on a merchant basis rose to `73%` from `69%` a year earlier.
- Operating and product signals: Booking.com's platform had approximately `4.7M` properties as of `2026-06-30`; the mix of Booking.com room nights coming from alternative accommodations was approximately `37%`; and the official IR overview tied the quarter to high-`50%` mobile app mix, mid-`60%` B2C direct mix, high-`50%` Genius mix, and Connected Trip transaction mix in the low double digits.
- Management message: the quarter still showed resilient growth, but the filing also made clear that the Middle East conflict raised fuel costs, pressured long-haul international demand, and weighed on some travel corridors.
- Structural themes: this quarter reinforces Booking as the consumer-travel interface layer. The important signals are merchant mix, direct behavior, app usage, loyalty participation, payments attachment, cross-vertical travel transactions, and supply density rather than only classic operator metrics.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` total revenue was `$5,532M` and segment Adjusted EBITDA less Capex was `$1,262M`.
- Demand and growth: total gross bookings were `$53,758M`, room nights were `338M`, merchant gross bookings were `$38,736M`, and merchant mix rose to `72%` from `67%` in the prior-year quarter.
- Product and channel signals: the trailing-twelve-month mobile-app room-night mix was in the high `50%` range, direct mix on total room nights stayed in the mid `50%` range, and Booking.com had approximately `4.0M` alternative-accommodation properties plus `500,000` hotels, motels, and resorts at `2026-03-31`.
- Management message: Booking highlighted strong room-night and flight-booking growth, but it also said the Middle East conflict hit March trends and made gross bookings more pressured than revenue because the company recognizes revenue at time of travel.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows why Booking is not just a hotel-booking site. The core motion is increasingly merchant-led, app-mediated, direct-repeat, and cross-vertical, with flights, alternative accommodations, and payments all reinforcing the surface.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: the year-end `2025` evidence chain showed full-year revenue of `$26,917M` and segment Adjusted EBITDA less Capex of `$9,852M`.
- Demand and growth: `2025` global room nights increased `8%`, flight gross bookings increased `29%`, rental car gross bookings increased `9%`, and the business kept shifting toward merchant bookings.
- Management message: Booking continued to frame the company around global travel demand, supply diversification, direct behavior, and reinvestment through the transformation program.
- Structural themes: year-end `2025` makes Booking a strong archive anchor because it ties online travel distribution, payments-linked monetization, mobile and direct consumer behavior, and marketplace scale into one business model.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Booking broadens Services into travel distribution, online reservation flows, payments-linked travel commerce, and marketplace-like demand aggregation.
- Consumer: this is one of the strongest direct reads on how people plan, compare, and book travel rather than simply how often they fly or stay.
- Cultural: Booking adds a major travel-intent and trip-planning interface where convenience, trust, loyalty, and price transparency shape behavior across borders.
- Technology: this packet is a clean bridge between consumer internet and travel. AI, app engagement, Connected Trip, direct acquisition, search dependence, and marketplace density all matter.
- Payments and wallet logic: Booking is not a stored-value wallet in the Caesars sense, but the merchant shift and payments facilitation make the economics much closer to a transaction orchestration platform than to a pure referral marketplace.
- Capital light versus capital heavy: Booking is analytically useful because it owns demand capture, traveler intent, and transaction flow without owning the hotel room, airline seat, or rental car fleet.
- International: Booking is one of the most globally exposed consumer-interface names in the repo, which makes it a particularly useful read on cross-border travel patterns and geopolitical disruption.

## Working interpretation

- Booking materially improves the Services packet because it adds the distribution and interface layer that sits on top of travel demand rather than another travel asset owner.
- It also sharpens the broader travel theme. Delta shows a loyalty currency, Hilton and Marriott show branded lodging systems, Caesars shows a rewards-and-wallet entertainment loop, and Booking shows the digital planning, booking, and merchant-payments surface that can route demand across all of them.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Booking is strong evidence that the better travel businesses are trying to own not just the trip itself, but the surrounding workflow: discovery, booking, cross-sell, loyalty, payments, mobile engagement, and in-trip continuity.
