## What changed

- Services mix: Booking gives the Services sector the travel-distribution and online-reservation layer rather than another owner-operator of planes, hotels, or casinos.
- Business model: the core value is not physical capacity ownership. It is traffic, direct demand capture, merchant payments rails, alternative-accommodations supply, flight distribution, and cross-vertical travel orchestration.
- Travel-platform read-through: the annual and quarter materials show a business still compounding through room-night growth, merchant-mix expansion, direct and app behavior, and increasing supply breadth despite geopolitical noise.
- Interface logic: Booking is one of the clearest consumer-interface names in the repo because it is explicitly trying to make travel planning, booking, payments, and in-trip engagement feel like one connected surface rather than a series of disconnected transactions.
- Strategic stack: the official IR framing makes the priorities unusually explicit: Connected Trip, AI-powered offerings, payments and fintech, localization, loyalty, mobile apps, and increasing direct mix.
- Taxonomy exception: AnnualReports did not provide a sector or industry label here, so the archive uses the SEC's `Transportation Services` classification to keep the folder structure stable and defensible.

## Open questions

- Does Booking's strongest long-term moat come from app and direct-repeat behavior, from supply density, or from becoming the default travel orchestration layer across more trip categories?
- How much margin upside remains if the merchant shift, transformation program, and direct mix keep widening together?
- If travel demand cools, does Booking hold up better than airlines and hotels because it sits in the distribution layer, or does that simply change where pressure appears inside the model?
