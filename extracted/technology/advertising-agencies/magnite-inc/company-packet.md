## Annual report takeaways

- Magnite is one of the strongest remaining attention-and-influence packets because it explains how fragmented media gets monetized after the archive moves past publishers, broadcasters, and agencies into the transaction infrastructure itself.
- The `2025` annual chain is especially useful for this frontier because Magnite is not selling content ownership or direct audience trust in the same way that `The New York Times`, `Fox`, or `iHeartMedia` do. It sells the connective tissue that allows publishers to monetize inventory and allows agencies and brands to access brand-safe supply across CTV, online video, display, and audio.
- That makes the company's recurring strategic claim frontier-relevant: Magnite repeatedly describes itself as the world's largest independent sell-side advertising company. Independence matters here because the packet becomes a read on whether media fragmentation creates room for transaction-layer operators outside the largest walled gardens.
- The annual-plus-year-end chain also shows that the modern ad stack should be read through `Contribution ex-TAC`, not reported revenue alone. Magnite's preferred measure strips out traffic-acquisition pass-throughs and highlights the economics of the platform's core monetization layer.
- For this frontier, Magnite materially strengthens the archive because it gives the attention lane a direct packet on how fragmentation itself becomes monetizable infrastructure. The business benefits when audiences splinter across streaming and digital surfaces so long as buyers and sellers still need an independent route between them.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` contribution ex-TAC was `$189.6M`, up `17%`; CTV contribution ex-TAC was `$97.1M`, up `36%`; DV+ contribution ex-TAC was `$92.5M`, up `2%`; net income was `$19.4M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$70.6M`, up `30%`, with a `37%` margin; and operating cash flow was `$57.4M`.
- Guidance signal: management guided Q3 `2026` contribution ex-TAC to `$188M-$192M`, including `$98M-$100M` from CTV and `$90M-$92M` from DV+.
- Full-year signal: Magnite raised FY2026 contribution ex-TAC growth to `13%-14%`, raised adjusted EBITDA growth to above `20%`, raised adjusted EBITDA margin to at least `37%`, and raised free-cash-flow growth to the high `40%` range.
- Interpretation: the quarter is the clearest proof that CTV is no longer just the growth adjacency. It is the organizing center of the business, while DV+ has improved enough to stop acting as a pure drag.
- Bigger-picture read: the Q2 release is frontier-significant because it frames Magnite as infrastructure positioned between supply and demand, with SpringServe and agentic tools supporting the idea that the value in fragmented media increasingly sits in routing, curation, and workflow rather than in owning any single endpoint.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` contribution ex-TAC was `$160.9M`, up `10%`; CTV contribution ex-TAC was `$82.3M`, up `30%`; DV+ contribution ex-TAC was `$78.6M`, down `5%`; adjusted EBITDA was `$42.9M`, up `16%`, with a `27%` margin; and operating cash flow was `$23.3M`.
- Mix and market signals: CTV moved above `50%` of total contribution ex-TAC, while management said CTV momentum was broad-based across publishers, agencies, DSPs, buyer marketplaces, ClearLine, live sports, and SMB demand.
- Technology and regulatory signal: management said AI was becoming foundational across agentic buying, creative development, inventory curation, and workflow, while also noting that pending Google remedies could matter for the DV+ side of the market.
- Interpretation: Q1 shows a business shifting from one digital-ad bucket to another. Magnite is not simply participating in overall ad growth; it is riding a mix migration from broader digital surfaces into streaming inventory and more structured supply-path relationships.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter is especially valuable because it links media fragmentation, AI workflow tooling, and competitive antitrust dynamics into one operating picture. The transaction layer is becoming more important precisely because the buying environment is more complex.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` contribution ex-TAC was `$195.1M`, up `8%`, or `16%` excluding political; CTV contribution ex-TAC was `$93.6M`, up `20%`, or `32%` excluding political; DV+ contribution ex-TAC was `$101.5M`, down `1%`, or up `4%` excluding political; net income was `$123.1M`, helped by a `$90M` one-time tax benefit; adjusted EBITDA was `$83.8M` with a `43%` margin; and operating cash flow was `$61.0M`.
- Full-year signals: full-year `2025` CTV contribution ex-TAC was `$304.2M`, up `17%`, or `22%` excluding political, and represented `45%` of total contribution ex-TAC; full-year adjusted EBITDA was `$232.1M`, up `18%`.
- Capital-allocation signal: during `2025`, Magnite repurchased or withheld about `5.2M` shares for `$79.2M`, and on `2026-02-23` the board authorized a new `$200M` repurchase program through `2028-02-29`.
- Interpretation: the quarter shows how noisy ad-market comparisons can be when political spending distorts the baseline. Magnite's management repeatedly used excluding-political comparisons to show that the deeper story was still strong CTV migration rather than a simple cyclical swing.
- Bigger-picture read: Q4/FY2025 is the best quarter for seeing the frontier pattern clearly. Money is shifting into CTV from other digital surfaces, and the operator that wins does not need to own the content so long as it owns enough of the transaction flow and supply-path logic.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Media fragmentation is not only a content problem: Magnite shows that fragmentation creates an investable infrastructure layer where publishers need monetization tooling and buyers need scalable access to dispersed inventory.
- CTV is becoming the premium growth surface: the quarter chain repeatedly shows CTV outgrowing DV+ and crossing from important segment to majority share of the business.
- Trust shifts from editorial trust to transaction trust: Magnite is not monetizing information credibility in the publisher sense. It is monetizing trust in inventory quality, interoperability, execution, and commercial neutrality.
- Political and event-driven volatility still matters: the Q4 `2025` quarter demonstrates that ad cyclicality can distort the apparent rate of change, which means normalized growth framing is essential for interpreting media infrastructure operators.
- AI is moving into ad workflows now: the Q1 and Q2 `2026` releases treat AI and agentic products as current operating tools for buying, curation, and productivity rather than as speculative strategy language.
- Frontier fit: Magnite materially improves the archive because it complements `Comscore` well. `Comscore` explains how fragmented attention gets measured and priced as currency; `Magnite` explains how that fragmented inventory actually gets routed and monetized at scale.

## Working interpretation

- Magnite materially strengthens the frontier because it gives the archive a direct sell-side infrastructure operator in the media-fragmentation stack rather than another company that sits one step away from the transaction itself.
- The recurring pattern is share migration, not just cyclical ad recovery. CTV keeps taking a larger role in the business while DV+ stabilizes more slowly, which mirrors how attention and monetization are moving toward streaming environments.
- The social and industrial pattern is that fragmented media still needs coordination. As audiences spread across more surfaces, independent infrastructure providers can gain importance by reducing complexity for both publishers and buyers.
- Magnite also sharpens a useful frontier-wide distinction: some operators monetize trust in content and identity, while others monetize trust in routing, execution, and market access. Both matter, but they sit at different layers of the same attention economy.
