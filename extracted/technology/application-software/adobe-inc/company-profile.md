## What changed

- Revenue: FY2025 revenue reached `$23.77B`; the trailing three reported quarters rose from `$6.19B` in Q4 FY2025 to `$6.40B` in Q1 FY2026 and `$6.62B` in Q2 FY2026.
- Margin: FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was `$16.70` and non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$20.94`; the trailing three quarters delivered GAAP diluted EPS of `$4.45`, `$4.60`, and `$4.25`, with the Q2 FY2026 GAAP figure including a non-cash goodwill impairment charge tied to Publishing and Advertising.
- Guidance: Adobe raised FY2026 total revenue and non-GAAP EPS targets in Q2 FY2026 and kept emphasizing double-digit ARR growth.
- Capital allocation: Adobe generated `$10.03B` of operating cash flow in FY2025 and repurchased approximately `30.8M` shares during the year.
- Segment mix: the economic center remains subscription software spanning creative tools, document workflows, and customer-experience software, with FY2025 total customer-group subscription revenue of `$22.80B`.
- Operational issues: Adobe is pushing AI monetization through Firefly, Acrobat, GenStudio, and broader workflow integration, while also carrying execution risk around sustaining AI upsell and absorbing Semrush-related contribution.
- Consumer / cultural signals: Adobe sits at the intersection of creator workflows, knowledge-work document flows, and enterprise marketing orchestration, which makes it a useful read on how generative AI gets monetized inside existing software habits rather than only through infrastructure.
- Management tone: management is assertive about Adobe's role in the AI ecosystem, repeatedly framing the business around creativity, productivity, and customer experience with commercially deployable AI tools.

## Open questions

- Can Adobe keep converting AI usage into durable pricing power and ARR growth without taking on hyperscaler-style capex?
- How much of the recent acceleration reflects broad workflow demand versus early-cycle AI feature adoption and acquisition contribution?
