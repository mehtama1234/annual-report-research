Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Company: Affirm Holdings, Inc.
- Sector: Technology

- Target quarter window as of 2026-08-10:
  - quarter 1: Q3 2026
  - quarter 2: Q2 2026
  - quarter 3: Q1 2026

## Annual report takeaways

- Affirm adds another taxonomy-breaking `Technology / Application Software` case that behaves much more like an embedded-installment, underwriting, and merchant-conversion payment network than like ordinary seat-based software.
- Unlike several other taxonomy-break cases in the archive, AnnualReports was current here on `2026-08-10` and already exposed the `2025 Annual Report and Form 10K`. That makes this a cleaner example where the source label itself, not only the source lag, is the important mismatch.
- The annual scale is large enough to matter as a real consumer-finance and commerce system. Fiscal `2025` gross merchandise volume reached about `$36.7B`, total revenue reached about `$3.2B`, operating loss narrowed to about `$87M`, and transactions per active consumer reached `5.8`.
- The product mix makes the operating lane clearer. Interest-bearing monthly installment loans represented `72%` of fiscal `2025` GMV, while `Pay-in-X` and `0% APR` installment loans represented `14%` and `13%`, respectively. This is not only a classic pay-later niche. It is a broader financing surface spanning merchant-funded offers, consumer-paid installment credit, and card-linked usage.
- The annual materials also show why the company matters for the broader archive. Affirm facilitates the `Affirm Card`, merchant point-of-sale integrations, virtual-card usage, and merchant-funded promotions, which means it sits at the intersection of checkout conversion, affordability management, underwriting, and software-mediated distribution.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent reported quarter: Q3 2026

- Results: revenue reached about `$1.039B`, revenue less transaction costs reached about `$498M`, GMV reached about `$11.6B`, net income reached about `$100M`, adjusted operating income reached `27.0%` of revenue, and operating income reached `8.5%` of revenue.
- Consumer and merchant behavior read: active consumers increased `22%` to `26.8M`, active merchants increased `44%` to `515K`, and transactions per active consumer increased `20%` to `6.7`.
- Mix read: direct-to-consumer GMV grew `48%` to about `$3.7B`, primarily driven by Affirm Card GMV growth of `146%` to about `$2.1B`, while active cardholders more than doubled to `4.4M`.
- Funding and operating read: management said this was the tenth consecutive quarter of above-`30%` GMV growth, highlighted strong capital-markets demand, and said average annualized cost of funds reached the lowest level in three and a half years.
- Strategic read: Q3 is the clearest proof that Affirm is maturing from a narrow BNPL story into a broader payment-network and merchant-conversion layer with improving profitability, stronger repeat usage, and deeper card attachment.

### Quarter minus 1: Q2 2026

- Results: revenue reached about `$1.123B`, revenue less transaction costs reached about `$543M`, GMV reached about `$13.8B`, net income reached about `$49M`, adjusted operating income reached `30.0%` of revenue, and operating income reached `10.5%` of revenue.
- Consumer and merchant behavior read: active consumers increased `23%` to `25.8M`, transactions per active consumer increased `20%` to `6.4`, and active merchants increased `42%` to `478K`.
- Affordability and merchant-conversion read: management's `Big Nothing` `0% APR` event produced about a `5%` GMV lift for participating merchants overall and about a `27%` lift when promoted on Affirm surfaces, showing that financing design itself can move demand rather than just facilitate a pre-existing purchase.
- Product read: over `65%` of approved U.S. loan applications were presented with a `0% APR` offer, and `39%` of quarter transactions were `0% APR` either through `Pay in X` or monthly installments.
- Technology read: management explicitly highlighted `AdaptAI` and `BoostAI` as internal optimization systems for financing-offer selection, checkout conversion, and merchant program design.

### Quarter minus 2: Q1 2026

- Results: revenue reached about `$933M`, revenue less transaction costs reached about `$455M`, GMV reached about `$10.8B`, net income reached about `$181M`, adjusted operating income reached `28%` of revenue, and operating income reached `7%` of revenue.
- Consumer and merchant behavior read: active consumers increased `24%` to `24.1M`, active merchants increased `30%` to `419K`, and active cardholders reached `2.8M`.
- Mix read: about half of GMV growth came from direct merchant point-of-sale integrations, about a third came from direct-to-consumer activity led by Affirm Card, and the remainder came from wallet partnerships.
- Merchant-financing read: GMV from `0% APR` products inclusive of `Pay in X` grew `64%`, while GMV from `0% APR` monthly installment loans grew `74%` and the number of merchants funding `0% APR` offers more than tripled to over `40K`.
- Strategic read: management explicitly framed Affirm as a multi-sided payment network and argued that richer transaction data improves credit, fraud, conversion, and program-selection models in a way that strengthens merchant dependence on the same surface.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector and taxonomy: Affirm is a strong reminder that a `Technology / Application Software` label can hide a consumer-finance and commerce system whose real control point is payment choice, approval logic, and merchant conversion rather than software seats.
- Consumer behavior: the packet is a direct read on normalized installment behavior, merchant-funded affordability offers, growing card-linked repeat usage, and the idea that consumers increasingly treat financing choice as part of checkout rather than as a separate banking decision.
- Merchant behavior: the stronger story is not only loan growth. It is merchant demand for conversion tools, `0% APR` promotion design, wallet distribution, and software-mediated program optimization at the purchase moment.
- Business model: the key insight is that Affirm monetizes a mix of merchant fees, interest-bearing installment economics, card-linked flows, and capital-markets-funded loan infrastructure rather than relying on one narrow consumer-credit spread story.
- Technology and AI: `AdaptAI`, `BoostAI`, underwriting, fraud, and routing logic matter because the moat increasingly sits in data-driven approval and offer optimization rather than in commodity loan origination alone.
- Watch item: the next useful same-neighborhood comparison after Affirm is not another generic bank. It is another issuer-backed installment, card-linked financing, or purchase-moment affordability operator that can pressure-test how durable this payment-network framing is versus both `Synchrony` and the wallet or PSP models.
