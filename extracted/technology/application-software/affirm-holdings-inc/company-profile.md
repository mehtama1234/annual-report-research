## What changed

- Affirm adds a new technology lane that is better understood as embedded installment finance and merchant-conversion infrastructure than as generic application software.
- The company now gives the archive a cleaner comparison between `Synchrony`'s partner and private-label model, `Block`'s software-plus-wallet model, `PayPal`'s wallet surface, and `Adyen`'s enterprise PSP layer.
- Fiscal `2025` GMV reached about `$36.7B`, revenue reached about `$3.2B`, operating loss narrowed to about `$87M`, and transactions per active consumer reached `5.8`, which makes the platform economically central rather than niche.
- The quarter chain shows repeated growth in active consumers, merchants, transactions per consumer, cardholders, and merchant-funded `0% APR` activity, which means the model is widening on both the consumer and merchant sides.
- `AdaptAI`, `BoostAI`, wallet partnerships, and the financing-offer mix show that underwriting logic and offer optimization are central product features, not only back-end risk controls.

## Open questions

- Does Affirm ultimately behave more like a payment network, a software-mediated underwriting layer, or a consumer-finance platform with better merchant distribution?
- How durable is the current combination of merchant-funded `0% APR` demand, card attachment, and capital-markets funding if consumer credit conditions worsen?
- Does the company’s `Technology / Application Software` taxonomy remain stable, or does the archive eventually need to treat this as one of the clearest cross-sector interface breaks in the repo?
