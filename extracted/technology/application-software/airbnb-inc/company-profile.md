## What changed

- Source taxonomy versus operating reality: AnnualReports classifies Airbnb as `Technology / Application Software`, but the business clearly behaves like a global travel marketplace and host-guest network. That mismatch is one of the most useful findings in the packet.
- Revenue and bookings: FY`2025` revenue reached `$12.2B` on gross booking value of `$91.3B`, then the next two quarters ran at `$2.7B` and `$3.6B` of revenue with double-digit gross-booking-value growth.
- Product and payments surface: Airbnb is widening the relationship through `Reserve Now, Pay Later`, `Pay Less Upfront`, hotel pilots, services, experiences, and smoother checkout rather than through a classic points-based loyalty program.
- App and repeat behavior: Q4 `2025` app bookings accounted for `64%` of total nights booked, and Q1 `2026` app-booked nights accounted for `63%` of total nights booked, which makes the consumer-app surface materially important to the business model.
- AI and efficiency: management now frames Airbnb as increasingly AI-native, with customer-support automation resolving roughly a third of issues in Q4 `2025`, over `40%` in Q1 `2026`, and nearly `45%` in Q2 `2026`, alongside declining support cost per booking.
- Travel and community signals: Airbnb is one of the clearest businesses in the repo where travel demand, local supply creation, event-driven network effects, host economics, and consumer trust all sit inside the same platform.

## Open questions

- Is Airbnb best understood as a travel intermediary, a consumer marketplace, or a software-enabled community network with travel attached?
- How durable is the current mix of product-led growth, payment flexibility, and AI-driven efficiency if travel demand cools or regulation tightens in key cities?
- Do hotels, services, and experiences strengthen the core host-home network, or do they gradually turn Airbnb into something closer to a broader travel super-app?
