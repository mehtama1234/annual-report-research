# AppLovin Corp. packet

Date baseline: `2026-08-10`

## Snapshot

- AppLovin has become a cleaner pure-play on advertising infrastructure after selling its Apps Business in June 2025.
- The central claim in the filings is that `Axon AI` keeps improving ad targeting, auction efficiency, and monetization outcomes across a widening advertiser base.
- That model produced very strong `2025` and first-half `2026` growth:
  - full-year `2025` revenue: `$5.481 billion`
  - full-year `2025` net income: `$3.334 billion`
  - full-year `2025` adjusted EBITDA: `$4.512 billion`
  - `Q1 2026` revenue: `$1.842 billion`
  - `Q2 2026` revenue: `$1.924 billion`
- The company is pairing that growth with aggressive capital return:
  - `2025` share repurchases and withholdings: `$2.58 billion`
  - `Q1 2026` repurchases and withholdings: about `$1.0 billion`
  - `Q2 2026` repurchases and withholdings: `$551.3 million`

## Filing coverage

- `2025 10-K` filed `2026-02-19`
- `Q4 2025` earnings `8-K` and `EX-99.1` filed `2026-02-11`
- `Q1 2026 10-Q` and earnings `8-K` filed `2026-05-06`
- `Q2 2026 10-Q` and earnings `8-K` filed `2026-08-05`

## Annual takeaways from `2025`

### 1. The company completed a strategic reset toward pure advertising infrastructure

- The `2025 10-K` says AppLovin sold the Apps Business on `2025-06-30`.
- Consideration totaled about `$715.6 million`:
  - `$430.6 million` cash
  - `596.9 million` Tripledot ordinary shares
- After the divestiture, AppLovin says it no longer generates Apps revenue and now operates as a single segment built around:
  - `Axon Ads Manager`
  - `MAX`
  - `Adjust`
  - `Wurl`

### 2. Revenue is now overwhelmingly tied to the ad-recommendation and auction engine

- The filing says revenue from `Axon Ads Manager` comprises substantially all revenue.
- AppLovin describes Axon as the core recommendation engine matching advertiser demand with publisher supply through auctions at scale.
- The broader business stack matters, but mostly as support to the core engine:
  - `MAX` helps publishers optimize ad inventory
  - `Adjust` handles attribution and analytics
  - `Wurl` extends the company into connected TV distribution and advertising

### 3. AppLovin is trying to move from mobile-gaming concentration into broader commerce budgets

- The company says it has been expanding since `2024` into web-based e-commerce advertisers.
- This is strategically important because the company also warns that its revenue is still concentrated in the mobile-app ecosystem and specifically mobile gaming.
- The annual filing keeps both ideas in tension:
  - concentration is still real
  - diversification into e-commerce and CTV is a major growth ambition

### 4. Financial output in `2025` was exceptional

- `Q4 2025` results:
  - revenue: `$1.658 billion`
  - net income: `$1.102 billion`
  - adjusted EBITDA: `$1.399 billion`
  - net cash from operating activities: `$1.31 billion`
  - free cash flow: `$1.31 billion`
- Full-year `2025` results:
  - revenue: `$5.481 billion`
  - net income: `$3.334 billion`
  - adjusted EBITDA: `$4.512 billion`
  - net cash from operating activities: `$3.97 billion`
  - free cash flow: `$3.95 billion`

### 5. Capital structure is still a real operating condition, even if cash generation is strong

- As of `2025-12-31`, the company had:
  - `$3.6 billion` aggregate principal amount of senior unsecured notes outstanding
  - a `$1.0 billion` unsecured revolving credit facility with no borrowings outstanding at year-end
- The filing also notes that AppLovin borrowed `$200 million` under the revolver in March 2025 to fund share repurchases and repaid it by May 2025.
- This matters because the company is simultaneously:
  - generating huge cash flow
  - carrying large debt
  - committing major capital to repurchases

## Quarter-by-quarter read

### `Q4 2025`

- Revenue grew `66%` year over year to `$1.658 billion`.
- Net income grew `84%` to `$1.102 billion`.
- Adjusted EBITDA grew `82%` to `$1.399 billion`.
- Management also highlighted:
  - `$1.31 billion` in operating cash flow
  - `$1.31 billion` in free cash flow
  - `0.8 million` shares repurchased or withheld in the quarter for `$481.7 million`

Interpretation:

- By the end of `2025`, AppLovin was showing that its post-games identity was not a defensive retrenchment.
- It was becoming a higher-conviction bet on AI-driven ad economics and auction leverage.

### `Q1 2026`

- Revenue: `$1.842 billion`, up `59%`
- Net income: `$1.206 billion`, up `109%`
- Net income from continuing operations: `$1.206 billion`, up `67%`
- Adjusted EBITDA: `$1.557 billion`, up `66%`
- Operating cash flow: `$1.3 billion`
- Free cash flow: `$1.3 billion`
- Repurchases and withholdings: about `$1.0 billion`

Additional `10-Q` context:

- The company recognized `Q1 2026` revenue related to Tripledot advertiser spend on Axon Ads Manager.
- It still carried `$3.6 billion` of senior notes and no borrowings on the revolver at `2026-03-31`.
- As of `2026-03-31`, about `$2.3 billion` remained authorized for repurchases.

Interpretation:

- `Q1 2026` shows the business behaving less like a game publisher and more like a highly cash-generative optimization engine.
- The Tripledot relationship is notable because AppLovin sold game ownership but still monetizes demand flowing through former game-adjacent relationships.

Interpretation:

- AppLovin is still compounding revenue and EBITDA at levels that suggest strong advertiser demand and strong pricing power in its optimization layer.

## Repeating operating signals

### Consumer and advertiser behavior

- AppLovin benefits from a world where performance marketers want measurable returns rather than broad awareness spend alone.
- The company is positioned for a consumer economy where purchases, installs, and subscriptions are increasingly mediated by algorithmic recommendation and auction systems.
- Its move into e-commerce suggests the same optimization logic that worked in mobile games is being applied to broader consumer-intent budgets.

### Cultural and societal read-through

- The broader participation economy is increasingly monetized before a user ever buys a toy, downloads a game, or joins a creator platform.
- AppLovin sits in that hidden but powerful layer:
  - it helps decide which app, offer, or product gets surfaced
  - it monetizes the auction behind consumer attention
  - it turns behavior data into capital allocation signals
- That makes it culturally less visible than Disney, Roblox, or Etsy, but economically crucial to how modern consumer demand gets routed.

### Industrial and operating pressures

- repeated concentration pressure:
  - mobile-app ecosystem dependence
  - especially mobile-gaming dependence
- repeated platform pressure:
  - Apple App Store
  - Google Play
  - Meta
- repeated expansion pressure:
  - proving e-commerce can be a durable growth vertical
  - proving `Wurl` and connected TV can scale economically
- repeated technology pressure:
  - continued investment in `Axon AI`
  - risk that model improvements slow or become less differentiated
- repeated capital-allocation pressure:
  - large share repurchases reduce cash flexibility
  - debt service and ratings still matter
- repeated regulatory pressure:
  - privacy
  - consumer protection
  - ad targeting
  - AI scrutiny

## Cross-company interpretation

- Compared with `Roblox`, AppLovin does not own the participation venue or social world.
- Compared with `Unity`, AppLovin does not own the creation workflow.
- Compared with `Take-Two` or `Electronic Arts`, AppLovin does not own the game canon.
- Instead, AppLovin owns a high-value control point in the monetization chain:
  - recommendation
  - auctioning
  - measurement
  - optimization

That gives the archive a cleaner distinction across the lane:

- owned worlds: Disney, Take-Two, Electronic Arts, Hasbro, Mattel, Spin Master
- participation platforms: Roblox
- creator and monetization tooling: Unity
- monetization and demand-routing infrastructure: AppLovin
- ritual and taste marketplace layer: Etsy

## Bottom line

- AppLovin is one of the strongest examples in the repo of a company monetizing consumer participation indirectly but powerfully.
- It does not need to own the brand, character, or world if it can own the system that allocates demand and spend around those worlds.
- The biggest open question is not whether the business is profitable now.
- It is whether the company can keep extending this optimization engine beyond mobile gaming into a broader consumer-attention economy without losing the margin structure that currently makes the model so strong.
