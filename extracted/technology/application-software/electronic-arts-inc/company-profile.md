## What changed

- Revenue and cash generation: Electronic Arts is visible here as a scaled digital participation and franchise business rather than a plain software vendor. FY`2026` net revenue was `$7.531B`, FY`2026` net income was `$887M`, and operating cash flow was `$2.553B`.
- Mix quality: the FY`2026` `10-K` said digital live services and other net revenue represented `71%` of total annual net revenue.
- Latest-quarter posture: `FY2027 Q1` revenue was about `$1.986B`, net income was `$397M`, and operating cash flow swung to negative `$242M` from positive `$17M` in the prior-year quarter.
- Franchise and live-service structure: the annual and quarterly materials repeatedly center the model on global football, American football, `Apex Legends`, `Battlefield`, and `The Sims`, with `Ultimate Team` remaining one of the clearest recurring live-service engines in the business.
- Quarter-chain signal: `FY2026 Q3` was explicitly framed around `Battlefield 6` launch momentum, while `FY2027 Q1` still showed net bookings up `4%` year over year because `Apex Legends` extra content and `Battlefield 6` sales offset softness elsewhere.
- Blind-spot fit: this is a useful correction to source taxonomy because the business behaves more like a live digital franchise and participation system than a generic `Technology / Multimedia & Graphics Software` issuer.

## Open questions

- How durable is `Battlefield 6` as a recurring participation engine after the launch window fades?
- How much of the model’s resilience still depends on sports live services and `Ultimate Team` rather than a broader spread of durable franchises?
- Does the unusual latest-quarter source chain signal a temporary disclosure-structure issue around corporate events, or a more persistent reduction in clean public IR artifacts?
