# F5, Inc. Company Packet

## Why this company belongs in CLI 5

F5 belongs in CLI 5 because it fills a category that sits between broad connectivity-cloud platforms and pure trust-governance software. It monetizes application delivery, API security, hybrid multicloud control, and runtime protection at an enterprise infrastructure layer that is more embedded than narrow edge-delivery specialists and less balance-sheet-heavy than telecom or landlord infrastructure. That makes it one of the cleanest tests of whether the next AI-era infrastructure toll sits in broad internet-edge platforms, narrow programmable-edge players, or enterprise application-delivery and security control.

## Annual anchor: year ended `2025-09-30`

The annual filing shows F5 operating from a stronger position than the lane's burdened infrastructure owners while still carrying more physical and services ballast than the cleanest software-control names. Fiscal `2025` revenue reached roughly `$3.088B`. Gross profit reached about `$2.514B`, implying gross margin of roughly `81.4%`. Operating income reached about `$765.9M`, net income reached about `$692.4M`, and net cash provided by operating activities reached about `$949.7M`. Cash and cash equivalents ended the year at roughly `$1.344B`, while remaining performance obligations were about `$2.0B`.

The revenue mix is important because it explains why F5 is not simply another software subscription company. Systems revenue was about `$705.6M`, software revenue was about `$803.1M`, and implied services revenue was about `$1.579B`. In other words, F5 is already a high-margin control layer, but it still monetizes that control through a three-part stack of systems, software, and services rather than through one pure recurring software line.

The business-model language makes the strategic role explicit. The `2025` filing describes F5 as a `multicloud application delivery and security provider` that helps customers deliver and secure every application and API across on-premises, cloud, and edge environments. The portfolio spans `F5 BIG-IP`, `F5 NGINX`, and `F5 Distributed Cloud Services`. The annual also ties the strategy to AI-era infrastructure through the `2025-09-26` acquisition of CalypsoAI, which management positioned as strengthening AI inference security inside the F5 Application Delivery and Security Platform.

The annual package also shows that F5 is not a frictionless control winner. It carries cyber-risk disclosure, litigation tied to alleged representations around cybersecurity capabilities, and the normal installed-base execution burden that comes with running a large application-delivery and security platform across hybrid environments. That is exactly why it matters in CLI 5: it shows a cleaner control toll than access utilities or narrow traffic layers, but not a costless one.

## What this company helps prove

- Sector role: F5 is an enterprise application-delivery and security control layer that sits between edge traffic/runtime platforms and the lighter pure-software governance layer.
- Consumer signal: end users increasingly expect applications to work instantly and securely, but the profit pool can migrate to the hidden control layer that protects and orchestrates the application rather than to the broad access owner.
- Cultural and societal signal: digital life is normalizing always-on app and API dependence, which makes invisible runtime protection and delivery control more valuable.
- Institutional signal: enterprises increasingly want fewer platforms spanning legacy and modern application delivery, API security, runtime protection, and hybrid multicloud governance.
- Technology signal: AI is increasing both inference demand and attacker capability, which makes F5's app/API security and delivery position more relevant than a simple traffic-optimization story.
- Industrial signal: F5 shows that a company can earn very strong margins from enterprise-control infrastructure while still carrying hardware mix, services ballast, and installed-base execution burden.
- Capital-allocation signal: F5 is far cleaner than telecom or tower ownership and cleaner than narrow delivery platforms, but not as light as the best recurring software-control vendors because some of the burden still lives inside systems and services.

## Working interpretation

F5 improves CLI 5 because it adds a category that the lane needed: enterprise application-delivery and security control. Cloudflare shows a broader connectivity-cloud ambition spanning routing, Zero Trust, developer runtime, and agent traffic. Fastly shows a narrower programmable-edge layer with more usage and concentration exposure. F5 shows a more enterprise-installed, hybrid multicloud, app/API control platform with stronger profitability and less obvious traffic exposure than Fastly, but also more systems-and-services burden than the cleanest software layers.

That position matters because it helps locate where value is actually settling. The `2025` annual and `2026` quarter chain show a company benefiting from structural pressures that are likely to persist: hybrid multicloud architectures remain messy, enterprise AI adds inference and security complexity, and the time between vulnerability discovery and exploitation is shrinking. Those pressures reward the platform that can sit in front of the app and API estate, standardize delivery and protection, and reduce operational fragmentation.

F5 therefore looks like a strong toll collector in the lane, but not an entirely frictionless one. The annual mix still includes significant systems and services revenue. The filings still show cyber, litigation, and execution burden. And compared with the very cleanest trust-governance or workflow-governance software names, F5 still carries more infrastructure reality.

The bigger pattern is that enterprises increasingly pay for control over the application surface itself. In the AI era, that control is no longer just load balancing or appliance replacement. It is a broader app/API defense and delivery layer across legacy, cloud, and edge environments. F5 is one of the clearest public proofs that this layer can generate strong margins and cash flow. It also suggests that one of the next strong CLI 5 profit pools sits in enterprise application-delivery and security control, between raw connectivity ownership and the narrowest edge-runtime specialists.
