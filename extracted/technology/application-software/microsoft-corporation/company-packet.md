## Annual report takeaways

- 2025 was a record year: revenue reached `$281.7 billion`, operating income reached `$128.5 billion`, and Azure surpassed `$75 billion` of revenue for the first time.
- The 2025 annual report is explicit that Microsoft sees itself in the middle of an AI platform shift, with security, quality, and AI innovation named as the three operating priorities.
- The annual letter frames infrastructure scale as a moat: more than `400` datacenters in `70` regions, over `2 gigawatts` of new capacity added during the year, and every Azure region described as AI-first.
- Copilot and agents moved from product concept to commercial platform signal in 2025: Microsoft says it surpassed `100 million` monthly active users across commercial and consumer Copilot usage, while GitHub Copilot exceeded `20 million` users.
- The annual report links the AI buildout to sector and policy themes beyond software, including sovereign cloud demand, responsible-AI governance, labor skilling, and digital-stability commitments in Europe.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: FY2026 Q4 revenue was `$90.0 billion`, operating income was `$40.6 billion`, net income was `$35.8 billion` GAAP, and diluted EPS was `$4.81` GAAP.
- Guidance: Microsoft said discrete items added `$0.27` to diluted EPS versus the April 29, 2026 guidance framework, including a `$3.2 billion` Anthropic investment gain and lower-than-expected Voluntary Retirement Program expense, partly offset by severance and Xbox impairment charges.
- Management message: management emphasized that Azure surpassed `$100 billion` annual revenue for the first time and Microsoft 365 Copilot exceeded `30 million` paid seats.
- Structural themes: enterprise AI monetization is now visible at scale, Microsoft Cloud revenue reached `$59.3 billion`, and the business is balancing hyperscale growth with restructuring and gaming cleanup.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: FY2026 Q3 revenue was `$82.9 billion`, operating income was `$38.4 billion`, net income was `$31.8 billion`, and diluted EPS was `$4.27`.
- Guidance: no numeric long-range guidance was saved in the local materials, but management said results exceeded expectations across revenue, operating income, and EPS.
- Management message: Satya Nadella said Microsoft’s AI business surpassed an annual revenue run rate of `$37 billion`, up `123%` year over year.
- Structural themes: Microsoft Cloud revenue was `$54.5 billion`, commercial remaining performance obligation reached `$627 billion`, Azure grew `40%`, and More Personal Computing was the relative laggard.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: FY2026 Q2 revenue was `$81.3 billion`, operating income was `$38.3 billion`, GAAP net income was `$38.5 billion`, and GAAP diluted EPS was `$5.16`; non-GAAP net income was `$30.9 billion` and non-GAAP EPS was `$4.14`.
- Guidance: the release again stressed results above expectations, with no detailed full-year guide captured in the saved page itself.
- Management message: management argued that Microsoft is still in the early phases of AI diffusion and highlighted Microsoft Cloud revenue crossing `$50 billion` in a single quarter.
- Structural themes: Microsoft Cloud revenue hit `$51.5 billion`, commercial remaining performance obligation rose to `$625 billion`, Azure grew `39%`, and shareholder returns remained heavy at `$12.7 billion`.

Note: for many companies this third slot will be a late-2025 quarter needed to complete the trailing three-quarter set as of 2026-08-08.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Microsoft is a clean read on software moving into capital-intensive cloud-and-AI utility economics, with scale infrastructure and booked backlog becoming as important as license mix.
- Consumer: consumer exposure still matters through Windows, gaming, search, and Copilot, but the strongest demand signal is enterprise willingness to pay for AI productivity and cloud capacity.
- Cultural: Microsoft is repositioning from legacy productivity incumbent to default enterprise AI operating layer, which has implications for how knowledge work, coding, and office workflows are changing.
- Regulatory: the annual report and quarterly releases keep legal, privacy, cybersecurity, and evolving AI regulation as live operating constraints; sovereign cloud and European resilience commitments are also notable.
- Labor: Microsoft is pairing AI automation rhetoric with skilling and workforce language, but Q4 also shows restructuring and retirement-program activity that fits a broader reallocation of labor toward AI priorities.
- Technology: Azure, Copilot, GitHub Copilot, Fabric, Azure AI Foundry, and in-house model development all point to Microsoft trying to own every profitable layer of the AI stack.
- Capital allocation: shareholder returns remain large, but the more important capital story is the willingness to keep funding massive datacenter and AI infrastructure expansion.
