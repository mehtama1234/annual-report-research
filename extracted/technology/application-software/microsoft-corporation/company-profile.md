## What changed

- Revenue: 2025 revenue was `$281.7 billion`, up `15%`; FY2026 Q2, Q3, and Q4 revenue rose to `$81.3 billion`, `$82.9 billion`, and `$90.0 billion`, respectively.
- Margin: 2025 operating income reached `$128.5 billion`; FY2026 quarterly operating income remained above `$38 billion` each quarter and hit `$40.6 billion` in Q4.
- Guidance: management consistently framed cloud and AI demand as still early-cycle, with earnings calls used for forward-looking guidance rather than detailed guidance in the saved release pages.
- Capital allocation: Microsoft returned `$10.2 billion` to shareholders in both FY2026 Q3 and FY2026 Q4, and `$12.7 billion` in FY2026 Q2.
- Segment mix: cloud remains the center of gravity, with Microsoft Cloud revenue of `$51.5 billion` in FY2026 Q2, `$54.5 billion` in FY2026 Q3, and `$59.3 billion` in FY2026 Q4.
- Operational issues: Microsoft continues to carry large investment and execution risk around AI infrastructure, OpenAI-related mark-to-market noise, and Q4 restructuring/Xbox impairment offsets to otherwise strong results.
- Consumer / cultural signals: Copilot, LinkedIn, and gaming remain important reach vectors, but the economic engine is increasingly enterprise AI adoption rather than classic desktop software alone.
- Management tone: management is highly assertive, framing Microsoft as a leading platform owner in the AI stack while also stressing security, quality, and responsible deployment.

## Open questions

- How much of FY2026 growth is sustainable underlying demand versus the front-loaded AI infrastructure build cycle?
- Does More Personal Computing remain structurally weaker while enterprise cloud and AI absorb most of the growth and capital intensity?
