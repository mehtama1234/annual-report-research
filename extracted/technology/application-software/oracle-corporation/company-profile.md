## What changed

- Revenue: FY2025 total revenue reached `$57.4B`; FY2026 then stepped up through `$16.1B`, `$17.2B`, and `$19.2B` quarterly revenue in `Q2`, `Q3`, and `Q4`
- Margin: FY2025 GAAP operating income reached `$17.7B`; FY2026 Q4 GAAP operating income reached `$6.1B` with non-GAAP operating income at `$8.6B`
- Guidance: FY2025 year-end framed FY2026 as a materially faster growth year; FY2026 Q3 then held FY2026 revenue guidance at about `$67B`, set FY2026 capex at `$50B`, and raised FY2027 revenue guidance to `$90B`
- Capital allocation: FY2026 became meaningfully more infrastructure heavy; Oracle said FY2026 operating cash flow reached `$32.0B`, free cash flow was negative `$23.7B`, and the company raised large amounts of debt and equity to fund AI cloud expansion
- Segment mix: cloud revenues and cloud infrastructure are the center of the story; on-premise software continues to decline while cloud applications, OCI, multicloud, and Cloud@Customer carry the growth narrative
- Operational issues: datacenter capacity, financing intensity, very large AI contracts, chip-supply flexibility, and the execution burden of moving from software vendor to scaled infrastructure owner now sit at the center of the operating model
- Lane role: Oracle is a hybrid application-software and cloud-infrastructure control layer inside CLI 5
- Consumer / cultural signals: enterprise AI and multicloud adoption are normalizing the idea that mission-critical workflows, databases, and AI workloads will be routed through a handful of giant cloud-control platforms
- Management tone: aggressive and scale-seeking; management repeatedly frames OCI and applications as both growth engines and strategic control points, not as adjuncts to the legacy license base
