# Roblox Corporation Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Roblox Corporation
- Sector: Technology
- Industry: Application Software
- Ticker: `RBLX`
- Fiscal year-end: December 31
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Roblox is a strong blind-spot fit because `Technology / Application Software` is a weak description of the operating reality. The `2025` annual package and trailing-quarter materials show a digital participation platform built on creator supply, game discovery, virtual-currency bookings, recurring engagement, and widening monetization layers rather than a plain software-seat or app-license model.
- AnnualReports.com still lagged here. On `2026-08-10`, the saved company page still showed `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, so the usable `2025` annual package had to come from the official IR metadata trail and the SEC chain.
- Full-year `2025` revenue was `$4.891B`, up `36%`, full-year bookings were `$6.788B`, up `55%`, and operating cash flow was about `$1.8B`.
- Management framed `2025` as a `banner year`, said Roblox added approximately `60 million` DAUs from `Q4 2024` to `Q4 2025`, and positioned the business around a flywheel linking user growth, engagement, creator opportunity, and expanding monetization.
- The annual and quarterly materials make the main distinction clear: Roblox is not most useful as a standard software issuer. It is more useful as a creator-economy, digital-play, and cultural-participation platform where advertising, communication, commerce, discovery, and AI tools widen the same relationship.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` revenue was `$1.469B`, up `36%`, and bookings were `$1.557B`, up `8%`.
- Engagement and scale: Roblox said it reached over `120 million` DAUs in the quarter who spent over `29 billion` hours on the platform.
- Cash generation: operating cash flow was `$318M`, up `60%`, and free cash flow was `$294M`, up `66%`.
- Operating posture: management said bookings landed at the low end of guidance because hourly monetization weakened, especially in younger cohorts in the U.S. and Canada, as engagement shifted away from high-monetizing `2025` viral games toward newer and evergreen titles with lower hourly monetization.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because it shows the real system risk and strength at the same time. Roblox behaves like a participatory media-and-gaming network where discovery algorithms, age-check friction, content mix, and monetization per hour matter as much as headline top-line growth.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` revenue was `$1.442B`, up `39%`, and bookings were `$1.731B`, up `43%`.
- Engagement and scale: average daily active users increased `35%` to `132 million`, and hours engaged rose `43%` to `31 billion`.
- Cash generation: operating cash flow was `$629M`, up `42%`, and free cash flow was `$596M`, up `40%`.
- Operating posture: management said the company was still pursuing `10%` of the global gaming content market and explicitly tied the platform to older-user expansion, age-appropriate communication systems, recommendations, subscriptions, marketplace features, and the broader `Roblox Reality` ambition.
- Structural themes: this quarter shows why Roblox belongs in the blind-spot lane. The growth engine is not just game consumption. It is a combined creator, social, communication, and discovery platform where safety systems and age-checking also affect growth and monetization.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: the `Q4 2025` shareholder letter showed quarterly revenue of `$1.415B`, up `43%`, quarterly bookings of `$2.222B`, up `63%`, and full-year `2025` revenue of `$4.891B` with full-year bookings of `$6.788B`.
- Engagement and scale: Roblox said `Q4 2025` DAUs grew `69%` and hours grew `88%` year over year, and that the business added about `60 million` DAUs from `Q4 2024` to `Q4 2025`.
- Cash generation: `Q4 2025` operating cash flow was `$607M` and free cash flow was `$307M`.
- Operating posture: management explicitly widened the strategic frame from games into communication, entertainment, commerce, and advertising.
- Structural themes: this closes the annual story by showing Roblox as a digital-world and creator marketplace whose core currency is participation. Users, creators, virtual currency, advertising, and AI-enabled creation tools sit inside the same system.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Roblox strengthens Technology, but the better read is as a digital participation and creator-economy platform that source taxonomy flattens into `Application Software`.
- Consumer: the demand is not just software usage. It is daily or recurring digital play, self-expression, social interaction, and franchise-world participation.
- Cultural: Roblox is filing-backed proof that fandom and participation now extend beyond toys and traditional gaming into creator-built digital worlds with their own discovery, monetization, and advertising layers.
- Societal: the company reflects the normalization of persistent digital worlds, youth and young-adult online identity formation, creator labor, age-gating and safety as platform design, and AI-assisted creation as a widening layer.
- Operating model: the important move is not selling a product one time. It is owning an engagement surface where bookings, revenue recognition, virtual currency, creators, ads, communication tools, and safety infrastructure all reinforce one another.
- Competitive positioning: Roblox is most useful next to `Hasbro`, `Mattel`, `Spotify`, `Netflix`, `Airbnb`, or other platforms where participation and relationship depth matter more than formal sector labels.

## Working interpretation

- Roblox materially strengthens the blind-spot lane because it extends fandom and participation from physical brands into a digital-native world where creators, players, virtual currency, communication, and ads all compound.
- `Hasbro` and `Mattel` prove that franchise systems can span toys, licensing, and digital extensions. `Roblox` proves that the same broader lane also includes a creator-built digital-world platform whose recurring value depends on engagement loops, content discovery, safety systems, and monetization layers rather than only on branded IP ownership.
- Together, `Hasbro`, `Mattel`, and `Roblox` show why the source taxonomy is too blunt. `Toys & Games` and `Application Software` are different labels, but the better comparison is inside a larger participation-and-franchise system:
  - a ruleset and collectible-fandom engine through `Hasbro`
  - a brand-universe and entertainment-extension engine through `Mattel`
  - a digital participation and creator-economy engine through `Roblox`
