# Samsara Inc. Company Packet

## Why this company belongs in CLI 5

Samsara adds a missing bridge between software-control tolls and physical infrastructure. It is classified as application software, but its economic role is to capture recurring value from the connected-device, telemetry, workflow, and AI-automation layer that sits on top of real-world fleets, sites, equipment, and frontline labor. That makes it a better CLI 5 fit than a generic SaaS name because it monetizes the software control of physical operations rather than only digital-office workflows.

## Annual anchor: fiscal year ended `2026-01-31`

The annual filing and year-end release show Samsara becoming a stronger proof that some of the next AI-era tolls sit in the physical economy’s workflow layer rather than only in cloud, cybersecurity, or semicap. Fiscal year `2026` revenue reached `$1.619B`, ending ARR reached `$1.890B`, GAAP gross margin reached `77%`, non-GAAP operating margin reached `17%`, net cash from operations reached `$236.2M`, and adjusted free cash flow reached `$208.7M`. Management described fiscal `2026` as a year of durable and efficient growth, with `$432M` of net new ARR and a data asset exceeding `25 trillion` annual data points.

The annual filing makes the business model more explicit. Samsara repeatedly describes its `Connected Operations Platform` as an open system that connects people, assets, and systems in complex physical operations. The filing emphasizes that physical-operations industries remain fragmented, data-poor, labor-constrained, and dependent on legacy point solutions. The company’s platform is meant to consolidate IoT devices, applications, third-party systems, and workflows into one operating layer. The practical consequence is that Samsara is not just selling dashboards. It is trying to become the software layer that coordinates fleets, equipment, frontline workers, safety, maintenance, and site operations in industries where labor, compliance, and asset utilization still do most of the economic work.

## What this company helps prove

- Sector role: Samsara is a software-control layer for the physical economy rather than for office productivity alone.
- Consumer signal: the end customer is usually indirect, but households increasingly depend on logistics, field services, fleet reliability, utilities, food distribution, and site operations that are becoming more data-coordinated.
- Cultural and societal signal: the economy keeps normalizing AI-assisted coordination and always-on operational visibility, not just AI inside office software.
- Institutional signal: enterprises in transportation, construction, field services, manufacturing, wholesale, logistics, and utilities are paying to reduce labor scarcity, compliance friction, safety incidents, and idle capacity.
- Technology signal: Samsara shows that AI monetization is not limited to model builders or datacenter owners. There is also a valuable layer in collecting real-time physical-world data, turning it into workflow control, and then automating actions on top of that data.
- Industrial signal: this is a software model built on messy physical operations where safety, maintenance, routing, compliance, and frontline execution matter more than abstract digital convenience.
- Capital-allocation signal: Samsara still looks much lighter than tower owners, semicap vendors, or telecom operators, but its economics are tied to proving ROI inside labor- and asset-intensive industries that care about utilization, uptime, and worker productivity.

## Working interpretation

Samsara strengthens CLI 5 because it shows that one of the next hidden tolls after connectivity and cloud may sit in operational coordination for the physical economy. Broadcom proves that scaled silicon and infrastructure software can capture extraordinary value inside the deployed AI stack. Samsara proves a different adjacent layer: once data is generated from fleets, equipment, cameras, sites, and frontline workflows, there is a recurring software toll in turning that data into a governable, automatable operating system.

Compared with ServiceNow, Samsara is less about digital-office workflow governance and more about machine-linked, field-linked, and site-linked operational control. Compared with telecom access owners, it avoids the heaviest network capex and household-billing burden. Compared with pure device or telematics vendors, it is trying to own the integrated workflow and agent layer rather than only the endpoint. That makes it a useful bridge between software-control names and physical chokepoint names in CLI 5.

The main caution is that this is still a hybrid operating environment. Samsara benefits from software-like margins, but the company still depends on customers whose budgets are tied to the physical economy, implementation success, hardware attachment, and proof of measurable operational ROI. Even so, it adds a major missing category to CLI 5: the software-control of physical operations layer.
