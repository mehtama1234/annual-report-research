## What changed

- Revenue and bookings: Take-Two is now visible in the archive as a scaled gaming and franchise system rather than a generic software issuer. FY`2026` GAAP net revenue reached `$6.656B`, FY`2026` net bookings reached `$6.721B`, FY`2026 Q4` net bookings were `$1.58B`, FY`2026 Q3` net bookings were `$1.76B`, and FY`2027 Q1` net bookings were `$1.39B`.
- Mix: the May `2026` investor presentation shows a diversified platform mix with FY`2026` net bookings at `49%` mobile, `40%` console, and `11%` PC and other.
- Recurrence quality: recurrent consumer spending accounted for `76%` of FY`2026 Q3` bookings, `82%` of FY`2026 Q4` bookings, and `84%` of FY`2027 Q1` bookings. This is a much more durable and service-like revenue engine than the `Application Software` label suggests.
- IP structure: the investor presentation makes clear that the business is organized around long-lived franchises like `Grand Theft Auto`, `NBA 2K`, `Red Dead Redemption`, and large mobile titles from the Zynga portfolio.
- Cultural timing: the quarter chain explicitly shows how large release windows, especially `Grand Theft Auto VI`, reshape management framing, guidance, and investor expectations well before launch.
- Consumer / cultural signals: this is one of the archive's clearest proofs that fandom, anticipation, and repeat digital participation can support a very large public company without fitting cleanly into toy, entertainment, or software buckets.

## Open questions

- How much of the expected FY`2027` step-up is truly additive versus pull-forward risk around `Grand Theft Auto VI`?
- Can Take-Two sustain very high recurrent-spending mix while still delivering enough new-title energy to prevent franchise fatigue?
- Does the mobile-plus-console-plus-PC balance make the model more resilient than legacy premium publishers, or more exposed to changing player-acquisition and platform economics?
