## What changed

- Buy-side ad infrastructure: The Trade Desk is a flagship buy-side programmatic operator rather than a publisher, broadcaster, or sell-side ad exchange, which makes it a clean complement to `Comscore`, `Magnite`, `PubMatic`, `DoubleVerify`, `Taboola`, and `Nexxen`.
- Open-internet positioning: management repeatedly frames the company around objective, transparent, data-driven buying on the open internet rather than around closed-platform dependence.
- CTV and identity infrastructure: the quarter chain emphasizes CTV inventory access, `UID2`, retail and commerce data, and integrations with counterparties such as `Databricks`, `Netflix`, and `Samsung Ads`.
- Customer-retention durability: management repeatedly reports customer retention above `95%`, which matters because the business depends on recurring allocation from sophisticated advertisers rather than one-off campaign traffic.
- Execution wobble in `2026`: the latest quarter is still profitable, but the slowdown from `12%` growth in Q1 to `3%` growth in Q2 shows that even a flagship open-internet platform remains exposed to macro pressure and execution complexity.
- Attention-stack role: the packet strengthens the frontier by showing how fragmented media budgets get allocated, optimized, and measured before they ever reach a publisher or broadcaster.

## Open questions

- Can The Trade Desk preserve premium growth while the open internet becomes more complex and macro conditions remain uneven?
- How durable is its independent DSP positioning if larger platforms and retailers internalize more identity, commerce, and decisioning tools?
- Does the current `2026` slowdown reflect temporary execution friction around platform upgrades or a deeper maturity shift in buy-side ad-tech growth?
- How much additional value can it capture from CTV, retail data, and identity standards before those layers become more contested or commoditized?
