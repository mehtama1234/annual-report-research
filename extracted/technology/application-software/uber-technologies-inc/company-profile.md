## What changed

- Source taxonomy versus operating reality: AnnualReports classifies Uber as `Technology / Application Software`, but the business clearly behaves like a mobility, delivery, and local-commerce coordination platform. That mismatch is one of the most useful findings in the packet.
- Scale and cash generation: the FY`2025` chain closes the year with `$193B` in gross bookings, about `$10.1B` of operating cash flow, and about `$9.8B` of free cash flow, then the next two quarters continue at very large scale.
- Membership and relationship depth: Q1 `2026` highlighted `50M` Uber One members, and management said those members drove roughly half of gross bookings across Mobility and Delivery.
- Cross-platform behavior: the company is clearly trying to connect rides, delivery, membership, and local demand inside one recurring consumer relationship rather than running separate single-use apps.
- Platform expansion: the quarter releases repeatedly frame autonomous vehicles and broader cross-platform strategy as extensions of the coordination layer, not as detached speculative technology bets.
- Demand normalization: the trailing quarters show that app-mediated local transport and app-mediated local fulfillment are now normalized recurring behavior at global scale.

## Open questions

- Is Uber best understood as a transportation company, a delivery company, or a broader consumer-coordination platform with multiple demand surfaces?
- How durable is the current mix of membership, mobility, delivery, and advertising-like monetization if local demand weakens or regulatory pressure rises?
- If AV supply scales materially, does it deepen Uber's platform moat or compress value capture by shifting bargaining power to fleet and technology partners?
