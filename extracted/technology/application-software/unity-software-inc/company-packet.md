## Annual report takeaways

- Unity is one of the clearest infrastructure names for the participation frontier because it does not primarily monetize a single world or fandom. It monetizes the software, runtime support, monetization stack, and AI-enhanced growth infrastructure that many other creators use to build and operate worlds.
- The `2025` annual filing shows a business that is still financially imperfect but strategically much clearer than in earlier periods. Revenue rose to `$1.85B` from `$1.81B`, gross profit rose to `$1.37B`, loss from operations improved to `$(479)M` from `$(755)M`, and net loss attributable to Unity improved to `$(403)M` from `$(664)M`. That is not a finished turnaround, but it is material progress.
- The deeper annual signal is portfolio concentration around the more durable infrastructure layer. The filing says Unity reset product and service offerings to focus on its strategic portfolio, mainly the Unity Engine and related consumption services on the Create side, plus monetization solutions on the Grow side. Management then used the year-end release to tie that strategy directly to Vector growth and unusually fast Unity 6 adoption.
- The operating model matters. Unity still reports one reportable segment, but its real economic split is between `Create Solutions` and `Grow Solutions`. Create is the development-and-runtime tool stack; Grow is the ads, mediation, and monetization layer. That combination makes Unity more than a game engine vendor. It is trying to become an end-to-end operating layer for interactive content creation and commercialization.
- The annual filing also makes AI a core strategic input rather than an optional overlay. Unity explicitly describes AI-driven content creation, workflow enhancements, Unity Vector, Unity AI tools, and embedded inference capabilities as part of the product roadmap. The bigger-picture implication is that Unity wants to own more of the intelligence layer underneath both creation and monetization, not just the editor.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: revenue was `$546M`, up `24%`; Create Solutions revenue was `$158M`; Grow Solutions revenue was `$389M`; GAAP net loss was `$23M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$160M`; adjusted EPS was `$0.28`; net cash provided by operating activities was `$206M`; and free cash flow was `$202M`.
- Mix and demand: strategic revenue was `$486M`, up `38%`, while the company guided to only `$20M` of non-strategic revenue for `Q3 2026`. That is the clearest signal yet that the business is being cleaned up around the parts management actually wants to own.
- Management message: Matt Bromberg called it arguably the best quarter in Unity’s history as a public company and tied the performance to ongoing success from `Unity Vector AI` plus the strongest product roadmap in the company’s history. That is exactly the language of a platform company trying to re-establish itself as indispensable infrastructure.
- Structural read: the `10-Q` shows that the ironSource Ads Network sunset was substantially completed in `Q2 2026`, and the Supersonic exit path advanced to held-for-sale treatment before closing on `2026-08-04`. That means the quarter is important not just for growth, but for reducing strategic clutter.
- Balance-sheet signal: cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash rose to `$2.357B`, while the filing still showed roughly `$2.2B` of convertible notes outstanding. Unity is generating enough cash to look more credible heading into the November `2026` notes maturity, but the capital structure still matters.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: revenue was `$508M`, up `17%`; Create Solutions revenue was `$157M`; Grow Solutions revenue was `$352M`; GAAP net loss was `$(347)M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$138M`; adjusted EPS was `$0.23`; net cash provided by operating activities was `$71M`; and free cash flow was `$66M`.
- The key interpretive point is that `Q1 2026` looked much worse on GAAP than on the underlying operating trajectory. The filing says Unity recorded `$279M` of impairment charges related to the ironSource Ads Network sunset and the planned Supersonic divestiture, including `$227M` in cost of revenue and `$47M` in sales and marketing.
- Strategic versus non-strategic framing is central here. The filing says revenue from those businesses is now included in non-strategic revenue for all periods presented, and that non-strategic revenue in the quarter was about `$76M` versus `$115M` in the prior year. This is one of the clearest examples in the repo of a company deliberately shrinking part of the reported business to improve the quality of the remaining business.
- Demand signal: Grow revenue still rose on strength in the Unity Ad Network, driven by `Unity Vector`, while Create revenue rose on subscription growth even as cloud and hosting revenue declined after the portfolio reset. That suggests Unity’s core demand is improving even while it is amputating weaker or less strategic parts.
- Bigger picture: Unity in `Q1 2026` was not simply a software company posting a bad GAAP quarter. It was a creator-infrastructure platform paying an accounting and restructuring price to simplify the future economic model.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025 / full-year 2025

- Results: fourth-quarter revenue was `$503M`, up from `$457M`; Create Solutions revenue was `$165M`; Grow Solutions revenue was `$338M`; GAAP net loss was `$(89)M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$125M`; operating cash flow was `$121M`; and free cash flow was `$119M`.
- Full-year context: the `10-K` shows full-year revenue of `$1.85B`, with `Create Solutions` at `$621M` and `Grow Solutions` at `$1.23B`. Grow is still the bigger economic engine, which matters because it means Unity’s creator-infrastructure story still relies heavily on monetization economics rather than pure software subscriptions.
- Management message: Bromberg said Vector delivered its third straight quarter of mid-teen sequential growth and that Unity 6 adoption was moving at the fastest pace the company had ever seen. That is a major cross-company signal because it shows infrastructure adoption and monetization performance reinforcing each other.
- Cultural and industry read: Unity’s products sit underneath mobile, PC, console, XR, and non-gaming use cases. The annual filing also emphasizes automotive, retail, manufacturing, healthcare, public sector, robotics, and construction. This matters because it suggests the participation economy’s infrastructure can spread into adjacent industrial and simulation markets, not just game fandom.
- Strategic read: `Q4 2025` is where Unity’s story becomes legible again. It is trying to be the creation-and-growth operating layer for interactive experiences, and the company increasingly frames Vector, AI tools, and engine adoption as one coordinated infrastructure strategy.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and creator trend: Unity supports the idea that participation economies increasingly depend on upstream creator infrastructure, not only downstream fandom. More worlds are being built, optimized, and monetized on shared technical rails.
- Cultural and societal shift: creation itself is becoming more industrialized. The same company now wants to help customers build content, run live interactive experiences, embed AI, acquire users, and monetize engagement. That points to a world where software infrastructure increasingly shapes what kinds of experiences culture can produce at scale.
- Industrial and operating pressure: Unity is exposed to platform-policy shifts, ad-market volatility, publisher behavior, debt maturities, restructuring charges, and AI-investment demands. It is not a frictionless software story.
- Bigger-picture pattern: Roblox owns a user participation economy. Unity sells the picks, shovels, and monetization plumbing for many worlds outside a Unity-owned social network. That is a different but equally important control point in the same broader system.
- Cross-company pattern: compared with Roblox, Unity has less direct control over users and community but broader exposure to creator tooling and cross-industry deployment. Compared with Take-Two or EA, Unity sits one layer lower in the stack: it is not mainly selling access to specific worlds, but access to the capability to build and monetize worlds.

## Working interpretation

- Unity strengthens the archive because it turns the play-and-participation frontier into a true systems map. After Hasbro, Mattel, Spin Master, JAKKS, Roblox, EA, and Take-Two, Unity adds the creator-infrastructure layer that sits underneath many other operators’ worlds.
- The social read is that culture is no longer only about who owns the characters or the players. It is also about who makes it easier for creators to build, improve, target, and monetize interactive experiences continuously.
- The industrial read is more demanding than it first appears. Unity has to manage product transitions, ad-network quality, AI investment, customer trust, balance-sheet obligations, and platform dependency all at once. That is infrastructure work, not just software distribution.
- In lane terms, Unity is the strongest comparison name against Roblox for the question of whether it is better to own the participation economy directly or to sell the infrastructure stack to everyone trying to participate in it.
