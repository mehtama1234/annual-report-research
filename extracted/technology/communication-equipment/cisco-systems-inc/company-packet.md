# Cisco Systems, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Cisco Systems, Inc.
- Sector: Technology
- Industry: Communication Equipment
- Ticker: `CSCO`
- Fiscal year-end: July
- Target annual report year: `2025`
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: `FY2026 Q3`
  - quarter 2: `FY2026 Q2`
  - quarter 3: `FY2026 Q1`

## Annual report takeaways

- Cisco's official annual-reports page and saved annual-report PDF cover the fiscal year ended `2025-07-26`, while the saved AnnualReports verification note confirms the company fits the `Communication Equipment` taxonomy and that the `2025` annual package is the right annual anchor for this research window.
- Fiscal `2025` revenue reached `$56.7B`, and Cisco said recurring revenue was more than half of total revenue. That matters because Cisco sits on the control-layer side of infrastructure economics rather than the raw access-utility side.
- The annual materials highlighted more than `$2B` of AI infrastructure orders in fiscal `2025`, plus Splunk integration, Cisco Silicon One, Hypershield, and AI-ready campus and datacenter demand.
- The core annual story is that Cisco is using AI buildout to refresh an installed-base networking and security franchise rather than to create a brand-new demand category.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `FY2026 Q3` revenue was `$15.8B`; GAAP EPS was `$0.85`; non-GAAP EPS was `$1.06`; and Cisco said year-to-date AI infrastructure orders had reached `$5.3B`.
- Guidance: management raised expected fiscal `2026` AI infrastructure orders to `$9B`.
- Management message: AI demand is broadening into networking, security, and infrastructure-control layers rather than staying concentrated only in chips and hyperscale compute.
- Structural themes: Cisco is monetizing the second ring of AI capex through network architecture, observability, and enterprise control rather than by owning the underlying compute silicon.
- Interpretation: `FY2026 Q3` is strong because it suggests the control-layer infrastructure vendors can capture real AI-era demand without taking on telecom-style asset intensity or hyperscaler-style capex.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `FY2026 Q2` revenue was `$15.3B`; GAAP EPS was `$0.80`; non-GAAP EPS was `$1.04`; and Cisco said hyperscaler AI infrastructure orders were already above `$2.1B`.
- Guidance: the quarter reinforced that Cisco was still in an AI order-acceleration phase rather than only defending a mature installed base.
- Management message: the company kept stressing AI infrastructure, enterprise networking refresh, and the strategic relevance of the Splunk integration.
- Structural themes: this is a higher-quality control point than a generic hardware vendor because Cisco combines installed base, software, security, and observability with the hardware layer.
- Interpretation: `FY2026 Q2` helps show why Cisco belongs in this lane: it owns orchestration and trust layers that become more valuable as traffic, workloads, and enterprise complexity rise.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `FY2026 Q1` revenue was `$14.9B`; GAAP EPS was `$0.72`; non-GAAP EPS was `$1.00`; and Cisco said AI infrastructure orders reached `$1.3B` in the quarter.
- Guidance: management's quarter framing suggested the AI demand wave was moving into Cisco's portfolio faster than a mature-networking narrative alone would imply.
- Management message: recurring revenue, AI networking demand, and the breadth of Cisco's control stack remain the key talking points.
- Structural themes: the quarter provides the starting proof that AI buildout is diffusing into network, security, and observability control points.
- Interpretation: `FY2026 Q1` is the beginning of the Batch B Cisco story, where enterprise control infrastructure starts to look like a more scalable profit-capture layer than telecom access ownership.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: enterprise networking, security, and observability control layer
- Consumer: indirect, through enterprise and hyperscale systems that support end-user digital participation
- Cultural: AI adoption is forcing enterprises to treat network resilience, control, and observability as baseline operating requirements
- Regulatory: lower direct consumer regulation than telecom, but still exposed to cyber, trust, and infrastructure-security demands
- Labor: technical sales, architecture, product depth, and enterprise integration matter more than field-network operations
- Technology: AI infrastructure orders, Silicon One, security, observability, and Splunk integration are the main technical levers
- Capital allocation: Cisco benefits from AI infrastructure demand without carrying telecom-style balance-sheet or capex burden

## Lane role and thematic interpretation

- Lane role: enterprise networking and systems-control toll collector
- What makes it distinctive in this lane: Cisco is the clearest direct contrast to Verizon because it monetizes connectivity complexity from the control layer rather than from the national access network itself.
- Main moat: installed base, enterprise relationships, recurring revenue, security and observability breadth, and network-architecture relevance
- Main fragility: Cisco still depends on enterprise and hyperscaler spending cycles, and it does not own the narrowest pure-play AI-networking or semiconductor bottleneck in the stack
- Bigger-picture read: Cisco is the Batch B evidence that better economics in the connectivity layer often sit above the telecom utility, in the software-plus-systems-control layer that enterprises cannot easily remove once complexity rises
