## What changed

- Iridium remains a hardware-linked satellite operator, but the economic core is recurring service revenue from a growing subscriber base. Full-year `2025` service revenue reached `$634.0 million`, and the quarter chain shows service revenue of `$158.9 million` in Q4 `2025`, `$158.0 million` in Q1 `2026`, and `$161.3 million` in Q2 `2026`.
- The company is broadening from narrowband voice and data toward a wider resilient-connectivity platform. The annual materials and transcripts emphasize IoT, standards-based NTN Direct, assured PNT, aviation safety, and national-security missions rather than only legacy satphone use cases.
- Subscriber growth is still the clearest recurring-demand signal. Total billable subscribers rose from `2.537 million` at year-end `2025` to `2.555 million` in Q1 `2026` and `2.627 million` in Q2 `2026`, driven primarily by commercial IoT growth.
- Government remains structurally important even when commercial service is the largest category. The U.S. government is still Iridium’s largest single customer, represented `17%` of service revenue in Q2 `2026`, and drives nearly all engineering and support revenue through EMSS and related national-security work.
- The business is in strategic transition, not just reporting transition. The Q2 `2026` release ties the Rocket Lab acquisition agreement to a halt in routine conference calls and guidance updates, while the same release expands the growth story through the Aireon acquisition and the four initiatives announced in February `2026`.
- Margin optics need context. OEBITDA was solid but pressured in both Q1 and Q2 `2026` by the shift to paying annual incentive compensation entirely in cash, and Q2 net income was further depressed by transaction costs tied to the pending acquisition.

## Working interpretation

- Iridium is one of the archive’s clearest examples of a communications company whose most important asset is not an app or a consumer platform, but a global coverage layer that becomes more valuable as logistics, defense, infrastructure, and autonomous systems need reliable connectivity outside terrestrial footprints.
- The most useful recurring pattern is that the company turns a fixed-cost global satellite network into subscription-heavy recurring revenue, then extends that base into adjacent services like PNT, direct-to-device IoT, aviation data, and national-security support.

## Open questions

- How much of Iridium’s next-stage upside will come from genuine new service adoption in PNT, NTN Direct, and aviation versus simple extension of its existing IoT and government franchise?
- Does the pending Rocket Lab transaction improve long-term vertical integration and satellite refresh economics enough to outweigh the loss of stand-alone capital-allocation flexibility and public-market visibility?
