## What changed

- Asset mix and story quality: Viasat is not just a satellite operator. It is a combined communications-services plus defense-and-advanced-technologies platform whose economics now depend on whether multi-orbit, multi-band assets can be translated into durable service revenue and lower leverage.
- Revenue quality: FY2025 revenue was about `$4.52B`, but the business still posted a FY2025 net loss of about `$575M`. The scale is real; the question is whether scale converts into attractive cash generation after launch, integration, and financing burdens.
- Demand mix: the strongest recurring demand signals are in aviation connectivity, government satcom, tactical networking, cyber, and other mission-oriented workloads. The weaker zones are fixed residential broadband and more mature commercial connectivity pockets facing heavier competition.
- Capital intensity: this is a bandwidth-demand story where the real bottlenecks are satellites, spectrum, launches, ground infrastructure, and debt, not just customer acquisition or software attachment.
- Physical-layer control: Viasat owns orbital, spectrum, and service-layer positions that matter for sovereign resilience, off-grid coverage, safety, mobility, and defense modernization. That makes it a cleaner physical-infrastructure name than many application-layer technology companies.
- Operating pressure: the filings and shareholder letters repeatedly show the tradeoff between backlog growth and execution burden. Launch timing, service-entry milestones, integration complexity, and capital expenditures can dominate the near-term economics even when demand is healthy.
- Societal and institutional pattern: connectivity demand is shifting toward resilient, mobile, sovereign, and mission-critical use cases. That favors providers with defense exposure and global mobility footprints, not just consumer broadband brands.

## Open questions

- Can Viasat keep shifting the mix toward aviation, government satcom, and defense technologies fast enough to outrun the structural drag from fixed broadband and more commodity-like connectivity?
- Does the strategic value of spectrum, sovereign-access positioning, and orbital infrastructure ultimately translate into a stable sub-`3x` leverage recurring-services model, or does it remain a capital-hungry ambition story?
