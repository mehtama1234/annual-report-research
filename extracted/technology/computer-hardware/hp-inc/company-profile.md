# HP Inc.

- Sector: Technology
- Industry: Computer Hardware
- Ticker: HPQ
- Exchange: NYSE
- Fiscal year end: October
- Annual scope year: 2025 annual report
- Quarter scope window as of 2026-08-09: Q2 FY2026, Q1 FY2026, Q4 FY2025

## What the company is

HP is the most useful current technology-sector evidence set for a smaller, less AI-direct incumbent centered on commercial PCs, consumer PCs, printers, supplies, and the future-of-work hardware stack rather than cloud, chips, or platform moats.

## Why HP matters in this archive

- It fills the technology-sector gap for a more conventional end-device and printing incumbent.
- It adds direct exposure to PC demand, print economics, channel inventory, tariffs, and office-workflow hardware replacement.
- It helps test whether technology spending remains healthy below the AI-infrastructure layer and outside the most advantaged platform and semiconductor names.

## 2025 annual highlights

- Full-year net revenue: `$53.6B`
- Full-year diluted net EPS: `$2.82`
- Full-year non-GAAP diluted net EPS: `$3.39`
- Personal Systems full-year net revenue: `$36.8B`
- Printing full-year net revenue: `$16.8B`
- The annual materials framed HP around future-of-work devices, commercial demand, gaming and premium-device mix, print profitability, and tariff and supply-chain exposure.

## Early read

- HP is the clearest current proof in this archive that technology demand below the hyperscale and semiconductor layer still depends on ordinary hardware-cycle issues such as commercial refresh timing, tariffs, pricing, and print-mix pressure.
- Unlike Dell, HP is less tied to AI-server backlog and more tied to client devices and print economics, which makes it a cleaner read on whether enterprise and consumer endpoint spending remains healthy.
- HP is also a useful contrast with Apple: Apple monetizes a premium ecosystem with stronger brand and services support, while HP is more exposed to replacement cycles, channel conditions, and cost/tariff management.
