## What changed

- Taxonomy and lane fit: Zebra is not a consumer-gadget or office-supplies story. It is a strong enterprise edge-hardware and workflow-infrastructure packet under `Technology / Computer Peripherals`.
- Revenue model: the company sells rugged mobile computers, barcode scanners, card and label printers, RFID and RTLS products, machine vision, workflow software, and services rather than one narrow endpoint category.
- Segment structure: Zebra changed its reporting to `Connected Frontline` and `Asset Visibility and Automation`, which makes the frontier fit much cleaner than the older product-line framing.
- Acquisition mix: the `Elo` acquisition expands customer-facing and enterprise-interface surfaces, while `Photoneo` adds machine vision and robotic intelligence to the automation stack.
- Installed-base logic: recurring economics sit in services, software, workflow optimization, supplies, and enterprise-standardized hardware fleets rather than in discretionary one-time consumer upgrades.
- Quarter progression: FY `2025` established the new operating map and acquisition base. Q1 `2026` showed steadier growth across both operating segments. Q2 `2026` showed a sharper step-up in revenue, margin, and segment operating income.

## Open questions

- How durable is current demand if retail, logistics, and healthcare customers slow frontline-capex deployments after the recent rebound?
- Can Zebra keep turning acquisitions like `Elo` and `Photoneo` into higher-value workflow and software attach rather than just broader hardware catalog breadth?
- Does the segment shift toward frontline and automation workflows make the business more resilient, or simply more exposed to enterprise digital-transformation budget cycles?
- How much of the current acceleration is structural installed-base modernization versus catch-up demand after earlier customer caution?
