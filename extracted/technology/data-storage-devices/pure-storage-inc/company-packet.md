# Pure Storage / Everpure Company Packet

## Why this company belongs in CLI 5

Pure Storage strengthens CLI 5 because it sits in a more interesting place than either a commodity hardware vendor or a clean software-control name. It monetizes the governed flash-storage and enterprise-data-management layer that enterprises need before AI workloads, cyber-resilience systems, cloud portability, and always-available operations can work cleanly. In other words, it is not just selling arrays. It is trying to own the persistence, modernization, subscription, and control logic around enterprise data itself.

That makes it a useful bridge company inside the lane. Seagate and Western Digital prove that retained-capacity economics matter. NetApp proves that governed data infrastructure matters. Pure / Everpure shows a third version of the same theme: the company tries to turn storage into a service-heavy, control-plane-enhanced, AI-ready platform rather than a one-time hardware shipment business.

## Annual anchor: fiscal year ended `2026-02-01`

The issuer's late-January fiscal year means the freshest annual available by `2026-08-10` is fiscal `2026`, filed on `2026-03-25`, with the annual-report package filed on `2026-05-01`. The annual chain shows a company that is growing faster than many legacy infrastructure peers while steadily shifting more of the economics toward subscriptions, recurring commitments, and platform control. Fiscal `2026` revenue reached roughly `$3.663B`. Gross profit reached about `$2.578B`, operating income reached about `$114.8M`, net income reached about `$188.2M`, and net cash provided by operating activities reached about `$880.1M`. Cash and cash equivalents ended the year at about `$854.9M`, while total cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities stayed around `$1.5B`.

The annual package makes the strategic shift explicit. The company describes itself as evolving from a flash-based storage provider into an integrated storage and data-management platform that virtualizes data across on-premises, hybrid cloud, public cloud, and edge environments into a unified storage layer with consistent control, built-in automation, and continuous modernization. The filing says customers increasingly need to store, manage, govern, and derive value from data as AI becomes more deeply embedded in operations, and it frames the `Enterprise Data Cloud` as the architectural answer.

That annual language matters because it shows where the company thinks the next toll sits. The pitch is no longer just `all flash is better than disk`. It is that enterprises want fewer silos, more hardware-independent subscriptions, cleaner data mobility, stronger cyber resilience, and AI-ready data infrastructure. The filing repeatedly ties the opportunity to:

- all-flash modernization versus disk
- cloud-native and hybrid-cloud operations
- subscription and as-a-service delivery, especially `Evergreen//One`
- hyperscale flash deployments
- AI-driven demand for higher-performance, more manageable, more power-efficient data infrastructure

The annual evidence therefore places Pure / Everpure in the CLI 5 stack as a persistence-and-control layer: more physical than the cleanest software names, but more governed and recurring than lower-level storage media suppliers.

## What this company helps prove

- Sector role: Pure / Everpure is a governed storage-and-data-management platform sitting between raw flash hardware and higher-level enterprise workflow software.
- Consumer signal: the consumer linkage is indirect, but growing digital activity, richer media, machine output, and always-on service expectations create more enterprise data to retain, govern, and serve back.
- Cultural and societal signal: organizations increasingly expect data to stay available, portable, secure, and usable across environments instead of being trapped inside hardware or application silos.
- Institutional signal: enterprise buyers increasingly prefer fewer infrastructure layers that combine storage, modernization, subscriptions, protection, and control rather than piecing those functions together across many vendors.
- Technology signal: AI raises the value of the layer that can make enterprise data accessible, governable, and continuously modernized before models, analytics, and workflows consume it.
- Industrial signal: this is still a real hardware-and-supply-chain business, but it is trying to capture cleaner economics by shifting the customer relationship toward subscriptions, control software, and outcome-based service levels.
- Capital-allocation signal: the company is converting recurring growth into stronger operating cash flow and continued buybacks while still funding product and platform expansion.

## Working interpretation

Pure / Everpure deepens the storage-persistence argument inside CLI 5, but in a more strategic way than Seagate or Western Digital. Those companies prove that mass retained capacity matters once data exists. Pure proves that another profit pool can sit one level higher, in the platform that governs how enterprise data is stored, updated, accessed, protected, and sold back to customers as a service.

That distinction matters because AI-era infrastructure will not be captured by one winner class. There are at least three separate layers:

- media and retained-capacity economics
- governed storage and data-management platforms
- upper-layer workflow, observability, and security control

Pure / Everpure belongs in the middle layer. It is still physical enough to feel supply, component, and hyperscale timing pressure, but it is also recurring enough to build ARR, RPO, and service-level commitments that look cleaner than classic hardware cycles. The filings therefore suggest a company trying to turn data persistence from an installed product into an ongoing operating relationship.

The larger thematic readthrough is strong. As enterprises absorb more AI, more cyber risk, more hybrid-cloud complexity, and more permanent data obligations, the value increasingly accrues to whichever layer reduces infrastructure friction without forcing the customer to rebuild everything from scratch. Pure's `Enterprise Data Cloud`, `Evergreen//One`, unified control-plane language, and `1touch` acquisition all point to the same idea: the next toll is not only in owning storage, but in owning the policy, service, and modernization layer around storage.

The main caution is that this is not yet a pure software-quality business. The company still depends on execution in flash economics, hyperscale demand, product cadence, rebrand integration, and customer willingness to standardize around its platform. But relative to lower-level storage suppliers, it looks like a structurally more ambitious attempt to capture the cleaner layer of enterprise data infrastructure economics.
