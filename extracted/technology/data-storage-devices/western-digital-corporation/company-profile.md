## What the company is

Western Digital is a storage-infrastructure company built around hard disk drives after the February 21, 2025 separation of its Flash business into Sandisk. In this archive it adds a second flagship `Data Storage Devices` packet, but with a different strategic profile from Seagate: a cleaner post-spin HDD pure-play whose public-market story is now more tightly tied to cloud mass-capacity demand, margin expansion, and the economics of persistent data retention.

## Why Western Digital matters in this archive

- It deepens the storage-device lane rather than treating Seagate as a one-off read.
- It shows how AI infrastructure demand can strengthen legacy storage media when the core question is cost-efficient retention of large and growing data volumes.
- It adds a corporate-structure angle to the physical AI stack: after the Sandisk separation, Western Digital is easier to read as a direct HDD and cloud-capacity vehicle rather than a blended storage portfolio.

## Early read

- Western Digital is one of the clearest examples of AI demand showing up as persistent-storage demand rather than as pure compute demand.
- The post-Sandisk structure sharpens the story: this is now a more direct bet on HDD economics, cloud storage demand, and high-capacity drive adoption.
- The upside is real, but the filings still show classic infrastructure-hardware fragility around demand forecasting, oversupply, supplier concentration, pricing pressure, and tariff or trade-policy shocks.
