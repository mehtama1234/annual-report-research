## What changed

- Connectivity mix: Charter is still one of the largest recurring-connectivity revenue bases in the U.S., but the growth engine is no longer pure broadband subscriber expansion. The quarter chain shows recurring pressure in residential Internet and video while mobile keeps gaining scale and strategic importance.
- Customer-relationship framing: management increasingly describes the business around customer relationships, connectivity customers, mobile lines, WiFi quality, and bundled value rather than around a single cable-product count. That is a meaningful change in how the carrier tries to defend the household wallet.
- Revenue quality: full-year `2025` revenue was `$54.8B` and adjusted EBITDA was `$22.7B`, but by Q1 and Q2 `2026` the composition of that revenue was diverging. Internet and video were pressured, while mobile service, commercial, advertising, and device-related activity helped offset part of the drag.
- Network and subsidy buildout: Q1 `2026` capex included `$812M` of line extensions, and Q2 `2026` added `127,000` activated subsidized rural passings. Charter remains a real physical-network expansion story, not just a billing platform.
- Competition response: product messaging around `Invincible WiFi`, a `$1,000` savings guarantee, simplified pricing and packaging, and inclusion of programmers' streaming applications shows the company moving from selling access alone toward selling managed coordination across home broadband, wireless, and streaming fragmentation.

## Open questions

- Can mobile attachment and bundle redesign stabilize the broader connectivity relationship before Internet and video losses overwhelm the pricing and retention gains?
- Does Charter's network-evolution and rural-build spending create durable share and ARPU advantages, or does it mainly raise the cost of defending a mature household franchise?
