## What changed

- Revenue and scale pressure: consolidated revenue fell to `$15.005B` in FY2025, then to `$3.667B` in Q1 2026 and `$3.576B` in Q2 2026. The company remained large, but the operating base kept shrinking.
- Household relationship erosion: pay-TV subscribers fell from `6.998M` at FY2025 to `6.632M` in Q1 2026 and `6.391M` in Q2 2026, while wireless subscribers slipped from `7.511M` to `7.527M` and then down to `7.375M`.
- Liquidity deterioration before reset: cash and cash equivalents fell from `$1.883B` at FY2025 to `$1.344B` in Q1 2026 and `$440M` in Q2 2026 before later closings and deconsolidation actions changed the balance-sheet presentation.
- Balance-sheet optics versus operating reality: current debt and finance obligations were `$7.321B` at FY2025, `$6.237B` at Q1 2026, and `$1.446B` at Q2 2026, but the sharp Q2 improvement reflected deconsolidation and transaction activity rather than a clean underlying operating recovery.
- Strategic pivot away from the original wireless build thesis: the company had already moved traffic off its 5G network by November 15, 2025, shifted into a hybrid MNO model using AT&T, and tied part of its spectrum monetization and future customer proposition to SpaceX transactions.

## Open questions

- Does EchoStar still have a durable consumer-connectivity franchise after pay-TV runoff, prepaid-wireless pressure, and the retreat from the original facilities-based wireless buildout?
- Are spectrum, satellite fleet, and Hughes enterprise/government assets enough to create long-term value once restructuring, carrier dependence, and transaction-driven balance-sheet repair are stripped out?
