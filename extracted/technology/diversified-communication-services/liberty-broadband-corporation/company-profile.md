## What changed

- Operating profile simplification: after the July 14, 2025 GCI divestiture, Liberty Broadband became much more obviously a public-market wrapper around Charter rather than a mixed holding company with a direct Alaska operating business still inside the perimeter.
- Capital-structure intensity: year-end `2025` debt was already meaningful at `$1.746B`, including a `$956M` current exchangeable component and a `$790M` noncurrent margin-loan component. Q1 `2026` debt then rose to `$2.565B` as the company prepared to retire the `3.125%` debentures due 2053 in April 2026.
- Debt reset in Q2: by `2026-06-30`, total debt had fallen to `$1.223B`, split between `$864M` current and `$359M` noncurrent, after the April debenture settlement and a May term loan from Charter that replaced part of the margin-loan exposure.
- Capital-allocation constraint: both Q1 and Q2 `2026` explicitly stated that Liberty Broadband made no share repurchases because repurchases were restricted by the merger agreement with Charter.
- Exposure concentration: the company still controlled `25.01%` of Charter's aggregate voting power, but the packet shows that exposure is shaped as much by voting caps, tax agreements, lender waivers, and transaction timing as by the underlying recurring connectivity economics.

## Open questions

- How much of the remaining Liberty Broadband value proposition is still an operating or capital-allocation choice, versus simply the timing and tax wrapper around the pending Charter combination?
- If recurring connectivity assets increasingly sit inside simplified operating companies, will public holding-company wrappers like Liberty Broadband continue to trade as useful strategic instruments or mainly as temporary transaction vehicles?
