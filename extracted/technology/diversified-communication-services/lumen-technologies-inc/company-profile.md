## What changed

- Business mix: full-year 2025 operating revenue was `$12.402B`, down `5%`, while the company posted a net loss of `$(1.739)B`. The important change is not top-line growth but which revenue is left after Lumen continues exiting or shrinking lower-quality legacy and consumer-heavy lines.
- Strategic-versus-legacy shift: by Q1 2026 strategic revenue had become `51%` of total business revenue, and by Q2 2026 it reached `53%`. That is the cleanest near-term indicator that Lumen is slowly becoming more of a fiber, wavelength, networking, and orchestration platform rather than a legacy telco runoff vehicle.
- Enterprise-quality split: the business is no longer just "enterprise." Management now discloses Large Enterprise, Mid-Market Enterprise, Public Sector, and Wholesale separately. That matters because AI, cloud, and public-institution modernization do not hit each channel the same way.
- Mass-market shrink and monetization: Lumen completed the sale of its Mass Markets Fiber-to-the-Home business in 11 states to AT&T on February 2, 2026 for pre-tax cash proceeds of `$5.75B`. That sale improves deleveraging capacity but also makes Lumen less of a broad consumer broadband story and more of a carrier-and-enterprise infrastructure story.
- Cash and leverage profile: the annual filing showed `2025` net cash provided by operating activities of `$4.738B`. Q4 2025 materials showed debt moving from `$17.6B` toward `$12.9B` after the related paydown path, and 2026 guidance depends heavily on that balance-sheet reset holding.
- Infrastructure density: the 2025 `10-K` said Lumen had about `163,000` fiber on-net buildings and over `340,000` route miles of fiber globally as of December 31, 2025. That makes it a genuine physical-backbone case, not just a services reseller.

## Open questions

- Can Lumen turn AI-backbone, PCF, and orchestration demand into durable business-revenue growth before legacy decline and pricing pressure consume the benefit?
- Does the FTTH divestiture truly simplify Lumen into a higher-quality enterprise and wholesale infrastructure platform, or does it mainly remove one growth option while leaving the core revenue base still in decline?
