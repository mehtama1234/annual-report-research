# Coursera, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Coursera, Inc.
- Sector: Technology
- Industry: Education & Training Services
- Ticker: COUR
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- Coursera is a strong human-capital and institutional-interface packet because it turns learning into a software-and-marketplace system rather than a campus, publisher, or district-contractor model.
- The `2025` annual materials show a company monetizing three overlapping relationships at once: direct learners, enterprise or government workforce buyers, and university or degree partners.
- That matters for this frontier because the lane is not only about education demand in the abstract. It is about who gets paid when learners and institutions both need faster skill formation, wider distribution, and more modular credentialing than legacy systems provide.
- Full-year `2025` revenue reached `$757.5M`, gross profit reached `$413.4M`, gross margin reached `54.6%`, net cash provided by operating activities reached `$108.7M`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$63.5M`, and free cash flow reached `$78.5M`.
- The annual packet is especially useful because it captures the pre-close logic of the Udemy combination: Coursera was already framing itself as an AI-enabled, global workforce-learning platform before the `2026` combined-company reporting step-change.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$298.6M`; gross profit was `$173.4M`; gross margin was `58.1%`; GAAP net loss was `($80.4M)`; non-GAAP net income was `$40.4M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$42.7M`; and free cash flow was `($32.6M)`.
- Segment and customer signals: Enterprise revenue was `$140.0M`; Consumer revenue was `$158.6M`; enterprise net retention was `91%`; enterprise customers were `12,107`; and paid subscribers were `1.655M`.
- Strategic signal: the quarter was the first major combined-company read after the Udemy transaction closed on `2026-05-11`; management said it now expected at least `$85M` of annual run-rate net synergies by the end of `2026`.
- Capital-allocation signal: Coursera also announced a `$100M` strategic investment in `LearnVector` and repurchased `$90.3M` of shares in the quarter, with more than `$140M` repurchased through `2026-07-28`.
- Interpretation: Q2 is not just a growth quarter. It is a structure-change quarter. Reported scale jumps sharply because the company is now trying to unify direct learning demand, enterprise-skilling contracts, and AI-native product ambition inside one larger platform.
- Bigger-picture read: this is one of the clearest packets in the frontier for seeing education become a data-rich consumer-and-enterprise interface business. Skills discovery, recommendation, retention, and measurement now matter as much as content itself.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$195.7M`; gross profit was `$108.6M`; gross margin was `55.5%`; GAAP net loss was `($20.5M)`; non-GAAP net income was `$12.4M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$13.5M`; and free cash flow was `$3.0M`.
- Learner and enterprise signals: the company added `7.6M` new registered learners, reached `205M` total registered learners, had `1,729` paid enterprise customers, and reported `90%` paid-enterprise net retention.
- Mix signal: Consumer revenue was `$129.5M`, while Enterprise revenue was `$66.2M`, making the pre-close business still visibly dual-sided rather than purely B2C or purely enterprise.
- Interpretation: Q1 is the cleanest quarter in the packet for seeing Coursera before the Udemy step-change. The company already had global learner scale, measurable consumer growth, and enterprise monetization, but it was still seeking a larger platform footprint and broader skills catalog.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter makes the social pattern visible. Workers, students, and employers increasingly want ongoing access to changing skills rather than one-time educational milestones. Coursera monetizes that pressure by operating a persistent learning marketplace rather than a bounded institution.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$196.9M`; gross profit was `$106.8M`; gross margin was `54.2%`; GAAP net loss was `($26.8M)`; adjusted EBITDA was `$11.2M`; and free cash flow was `($2.0M)`.
- Full-year and segment signals: full-year `2025` revenue was `$757.5M`; full-year adjusted EBITDA was `$63.5M`; Q4 Consumer revenue was `$131.5M`; Q4 Enterprise revenue was `$65.4M`; full-year Consumer revenue was `$502.2M`; and full-year Enterprise revenue was `$255.3M`.
- Strategic signal: management explicitly framed the announced Udemy combination as a way to broaden reach, strengthen complementary capabilities, and transform how the global workforce discovers and masters new skills.
- Interpretation: Q4 is important because it shows Coursera as a viable standalone before the merger logic changed the reporting frame. It already had operating leverage, positive annual free cash flow, and evidence that direct learner demand and enterprise contracts could coexist.
- Bigger-picture read: the quarter shows that online education is no longer just a pandemic-era or college-adjacent category. It is becoming a persistent consumer and workforce utility where distribution, catalog depth, recommendation quality, and credential relevance all affect monetization.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Workforce training and upskilling: Coursera is one of the frontier's clearest reads on modular skill formation for workers, enterprises, universities, and governments at global scale.
- Institutional outsourcing: the platform lets institutions buy learning infrastructure, catalog breadth, and delivery capability without building all content or product systems internally.
- Human-capital bottlenecks: the learner and enterprise metrics suggest ongoing demand for outside systems that compress reskilling, onboarding, and professional adaptation.
- AI and skills intelligence: the Udemy combination, `LearnVector`, and management's repeated AI framing all suggest that the next competitive layer is not just course inventory but better matching, personalization, and measurable learning outcomes.
- Consumer and enterprise convergence: Coursera is especially useful because it monetizes both sides. It sits between household self-improvement demand and institutional workforce spending rather than serving only one.
- Frontier fit: Coursera materially strengthens the human-capital and institutional-interface lane because it adds a platform-and-marketplace model distinct from healthcare credentialing, university enablement, or K-12 and career-learning delivery.

## Working interpretation

- Coursera improves the frontier because it turns the education-and-training lane into a true platform economy rather than a set of institution-specific service models.
- The social pattern is persistent skills intermediation. Learners do not stop after school, employers do not want to rebuild every training stack internally, and credentialing is becoming more modular, searchable, and software-mediated.
- The strongest repeating pattern is that scale is now strategic in learning markets, but scale alone is not enough. The monetization edge is moving toward AI-guided discovery, skills intelligence, retention systems, and the ability to serve both direct learners and institutions from one underlying platform.
- Coursera also sharpens a frontier-wide link between interface control and institutional adaptation: once learning demand is fragmented across careers, geographies, and employers, the operator that organizes the interface between content, credentials, and outcomes can capture disproportionate value.
