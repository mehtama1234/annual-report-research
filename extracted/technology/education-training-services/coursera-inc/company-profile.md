## What changed

- Exact-category value: Coursera gives the frontier a fourth distinct `Education & Training Services` model after Adtalem, Grand Canyon Education, and Stride.
- Operating model clarity: the company monetizes a three-sided learning system that spans consumer subscriptions and transactions, enterprise upskilling contracts, and degree or credential marketplace economics.
- Platform shift: the quarter chain shows the lane moving from pure online-course scale toward skills intelligence, AI-native personalization, and combined-platform economics after the Udemy transaction.
- Institutional dependence: enterprises, universities, governments, and workforce programs still matter materially even though the platform also has direct consumer demand.
- Capital allocation: by Q2 `2026` the company was pairing merger integration and AI investment with large-scale share repurchases and higher full-year revenue expectations.

## Open questions

- Can Coursera integrate Udemy fast enough to turn scale into durable margin expansion rather than just bigger combined revenue?
- Does the platform become more valuable because it spans direct learners and institutions, or more operationally complex because it has to serve both?
- How much of the next growth wave comes from AI-native product improvement versus simple post-merger revenue consolidation?
