## Why this name matters

- Arrow is one of the cleanest possible CLI 8 additions because it is a literal global distribution and channel-control company in electronic components and enterprise computing solutions.
- It materially broadens the frontier away from contractor-heavy physical-distribution names and into the technology supply chain, where procurement power, inventory availability, design support, logistics, and solutions attachment determine how semiconductors, embedded systems, and enterprise infrastructure actually move.
- It also gives CLI 8 a useful comparison against `WESCO International Inc.`, `Watsco, Inc.`, `Resideo Technologies, Inc.`, and `Fastenal Company`. Those names show electrical infrastructure, HVAC, security channels, and industrial procurement. Arrow shows the enterprise-electronics and component-distribution layer that sits under industrial automation, cloud infrastructure, transportation electronics, and connected-device production.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual chain makes clear that Arrow is not a thin reseller. The company describes itself as a technology-centric go-to-market and supply-chain-services company that aggregates electronic components, infrastructure software, and IT hardware for customers on behalf of suppliers.
- Arrow’s scale is central to the packet. The `10-K` says the company maintained over `140` sales facilities and `39` distribution and value-added centers, serving more than `85` countries. That is exactly the kind of middle-layer infrastructure the archive needs.
- The segment mix matters. In `2025`, approximately `70%` of sales came from global components and approximately `30%` came from global `ECS`. That split makes Arrow a strong proof that the middle layer can monetize both physical component flow and higher-service enterprise-technology routing.
- Full-year `2025` consolidated sales were `$30.853B`, up `10.5%` year over year. Global components sales were `$21.501B`, up `7.6%`, and global `ECS` sales were `$9.352B`, up `17.8%`.
- Profitability improved in `GAAP` terms but still showed margin pressure from mix and operating conditions. Operating income was `$822M`, up `7.0%`, but operating margin slipped `10` basis points to `2.7%`, while non-`GAAP` operating income fell `5.3%` to `$948M`.
- The annual liquidity and working-capital read is especially useful for CLI 8. At `2025-12-31`, working capital was `$7.437B`, cash and cash equivalents were `$306M`, and working capital as a percentage of sales fell to `21.3%` from `23.0%` because higher sales improved turns.
- Supplier relationships also visibly shape the risk profile. The `10-K` says supplier protections covered about `56%` of consolidated inventories for price reductions and about `59%` for repurchase arrangements as of `2025-12-31`, which is a direct example of how channel operators try to defend inventory economics.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Second-quarter `2026` consolidated sales were `$9.992B`, up `32%` year over year, while net income attributable to shareholders rose `45%` to `$273M` and diluted `EPS` rose `47%` to `$5.26`.
- The core signal is that the strongest acceleration remained in components. Global components sales were `$7.366B`, up `39%`, while global `ECS` sales were `$2.627B`, up `14%`.
- Regional detail matters because it shows broad technology demand rather than one narrow pocket. Americas components sales rose `44%`, EMEA components rose `36%` or `32%` in constant currency, and Asia-Pacific components rose `38%`.
- `ECS` still grew, but the margin story was weaker. Global `ECS` operating income and non-`GAAP` operating income both fell `12%` year over year even though sales and billings rose `14%`.
- Cash generation stayed strong but normalized from the prior quarter. Arrow generated `$318M` of operating cash flow and repurchased `$43M` of shares.
- The `10-Q` also shows the working-capital intensity directly. At `2026-07-04`, receivables were about `$28.0B`, inventories were about `$5.94B`, and cash was about `$245M`.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- First-quarter `2026` consolidated sales were `$9.474B`, up `39%` year over year, while net income attributable to shareholders rose to `$235M` and diluted `EPS` rose to `$4.55`.
- Both major segments accelerated sharply. Global components sales were `$6.640B`, up `39%`, and global `ECS` sales were `$2.833B`, also up `39%`.
- The geographic split reinforces the breadth of the recovery. Americas components rose `47%`, EMEA components rose `32%` or `19%` constant currency, Asia-Pacific components rose `37%`, EMEA `ECS` rose `46%`, and Americas `ECS` rose `30%`.
- The biggest operating signal was cash flow. Arrow generated `$700M` of operating cash flow, which management tied partly to the timing of cash flows within the supply-chain-services offering.
- That is analytically important for CLI 8 because it shows the company is not simply passing through product demand; it is also financing, coordinating, and timing customer and supplier flows in ways that reshape working capital quarter to quarter.
- The first-quarter outlook for `Q2 2026` also showed management expected growth to stay elevated, with consolidated sales guided to `$9.15B` to `$9.75B`, which the company then exceeded.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Fourth-quarter `2025` consolidated sales were `$8.746B`, up `20%` year over year, while net income attributable to shareholders almost doubled to `$195M` and diluted `EPS` rose to `$3.75`.
- Global components sales were `$5.882B`, up `22%`, and global `ECS` sales were `$2.864B`, up `16%`, showing broad-based improvement across both sides of the platform.
- The quarter also gives useful regional detail. Americas components rose `22%`, EMEA components rose `16%`, Asia-Pacific components rose `26%`, EMEA `ECS` rose `24%`, and Americas `ECS` rose `7%`.
- Other financial information matters here because it shows the operating core. Arrow generated `$200M` of fourth-quarter operating cash flow and repurchased `$50M` of shares.
- Full-year `2025` therefore ends up looking like a strong throughput year with moderate margin compression rather than a simple profit-collapse or boom story.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Arrow is far from end-consumer branding, but it sits under several of the most important current social and industrial shifts: electrification, connected devices, industrial automation, cloud infrastructure, transportation electronics, and enterprise digitization.
- The company also captures a cultural shift inside B2B technology markets. Customers increasingly need not just parts, but a distributor that can shorten time to market, bundle technologies, reduce sourcing friction, and provide engineering and supply-chain support.
- This packet also helps explain why modern technology supply chains feel fragile and strategic at the same time. Many downstream companies do not want to own every procurement and logistics problem directly, so they rely more heavily on a scaled intermediary like Arrow.
- In that sense, Arrow is a strong proof that the middle layer in technology has become more important as devices, infrastructure, and embedded systems have become more complex.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Working capital is central to Arrow’s economics. The quarter chain shows very large swings in receivables, inventories, and operating cash flow, which means execution quality is tied closely to timing, credit, and inventory discipline.
- Segment mix also matters. Global components is the larger and faster-growing engine in the current period, while `ECS` is a more service-heavy and enterprise-relationship-driven platform with weaker latest-quarter operating-income flow-through.
- Foreign exchange still affects the optics meaningfully. The quarter releases repeatedly quantify positive or negative sales and `EPS` effects from currency changes.
- Supplier and customer concentration risk is implicit in the model. The annual filing repeatedly frames the business around relationships with key suppliers, exposure to changing demand conditions, and the need to manage price protection and obsolescence.
- The company is also exposed to the broader industrial and enterprise spending cycle. Industrial, transportation, cloud, and enterprise-IT conditions all flow through the packet faster than they would in a consumer-facing technology name.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Technology distribution is still physical.` Even in semiconductors, embedded systems, and enterprise infrastructure, the middle layer still depends on inventory, financing, logistics, and value-added centers.
- `Channel control matters more when systems get more complex.` Arrow becomes more valuable as customers need engineering support, supply-chain services, and bundled access to many suppliers rather than isolated product transactions.
- `Working capital is part of the product.` The swings in receivables, inventories, and operating cash flow show that Arrow is partly monetizing coordination and timing, not just gross merchandise movement.
- `The technology economy still runs through intermediaries.` The archive often overweights branded software or device makers; Arrow shows how much of the real economic system depends on scaled unseen distributors.
- `Service attach widens the moat.` Enterprise computing solutions, supply-chain services, integration, and design support all deepen dependence beyond box movement alone.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `WESCO International Inc.`: WESCO is broader electrical, utility, communications, and project-heavy infrastructure distribution; Arrow is the enterprise-electronics and component-distribution analogue with deeper direct exposure to OEM, EMS, `VAR`, and `MSP` channels.
- Against `Watsco, Inc.`: Watsco monetizes HVAC replacement cycles, branch density, and contractor workflow; Arrow monetizes technology supply-chain complexity, electronic-component flow, and enterprise computing channel attachment.
- Against `Resideo Technologies, Inc.`: Resideo shows low-voltage security and building-controls channel ownership; Arrow shows a much broader global technology channel that sits under industrial automation, cloud, transportation, and embedded-device production.
- Against `Fastenal Company`: Fastenal embeds itself in industrial procurement workflow through onsite service and vending; Arrow does something analogous in technology markets through supply-chain services, design support, and multi-supplier aggregation.

## Working interpretation

- Arrow is one of the strongest proofs in CLI 8 that the middle layer is not just about trucks, branches, and contractors. It is also about global technology distribution, supplier aggregation, and embedded procurement relationships.
- The bigger pattern is that industrial and digital complexity both increase the value of intermediaries. As products, systems, and end markets become harder to coordinate, channel-control operators like Arrow become more structurally important rather than less.
