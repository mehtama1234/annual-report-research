## What changed

- Arrow materially broadens CLI 8 beyond contractor-heavy building and security channels by adding a true enterprise-electronics and technology-distribution packet with direct exposure to semiconductors, embedded systems, cloud infrastructure, and IT channel routing.
- The `2025` annual chain shows a business where approximately `70%` of sales came from global components and approximately `30%` from global `ECS`, which makes the product-flow versus solutions-flow mix explicit.
- The company maintained over `140` sales facilities and `39` distribution and value-added centers serving more than `85` countries, which makes this one of the most globally scaled channel-control businesses in the frontier.
- Full-year `2025` sales were `$30.853B`, with global components sales of `$21.501B` and global `ECS` sales of `$9.352B`.
- `Q4 2025` sales were `$8.746B`, with global components up `22%` and global `ECS` up `16%`; Arrow generated `$200M` of fourth-quarter operating cash flow.
- `Q1 2026` sales were `$9.474B`, with global components up `39%`, global `ECS` up `39%`, and operating cash flow at `$700M`, partly due to supply-chain-services timing.
- `Q2 2026` sales were `$9.992B`, with global components up `39%` and global `ECS` up `14%`; operating cash flow was `$318M`, inventories rose to about `$5.94B`, and receivables rose to about `$28.0B`.

## Open questions

- How much of the `2026` growth surge is cyclical normalization in components versus a structurally stronger supplier-and-customer position for Arrow itself?
- Can `ECS` keep compounding as a higher-service channel even when its quarterly margin profile lags the component rebound?
- Does Arrow’s supply-chain-services model create durable customer lock-in, or does it mainly amplify quarter-to-quarter working-capital noise?
- How vulnerable is this model if industrial, transportation, or enterprise-IT demand weakens after the current recovery burst?
