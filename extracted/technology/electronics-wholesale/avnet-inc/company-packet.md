# Avnet Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Target annual report year: `2025`
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: `Q4 FY2026`
  - quarter 2: `Q3 FY2026`
  - quarter 3: `Q2 FY2026`

## Why this name matters

- Avnet is one of the cleanest possible CLI 8 additions because it is a literal global electronics wholesaler and supply-chain intermediary with direct exposure to semiconductors, embedded components, industrial electronics, and business-customer procurement workflow.
- It materially strengthens the frontier’s technology-distribution lane by giving the archive a second flagship comparison beside `Arrow Electronics, Inc.` rather than relying on one electronics-channel operator to represent the category.
- Avnet also adds a somewhat different operating emphasis from Arrow. The packet is more explicit about the split between `Electronic Components` and `Farnell`, about inventory days and return on working capital, and about how a scaled distributor monetizes recovery while still managing a financing-heavy balance sheet.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual chain makes clear that Avnet is not just a pass-through reseller. The company distributes semiconductors, `IP&E`, and embedded products while also providing design support, point-of-use replenishment, labeling, testing, assembly, programming, supply-chain management, and materials-management services.
- The company operates through two distinct segments: `Electronic Components` and `Farnell`. That matters for CLI 8 because it shows both the higher-volume supply-chain intermediary and the more service-attached engineering-and-catalog side of the same channel system.
- Full-year `2025` sales were `$22.2008B`, down `6.6%` year over year. `Electronic Components` generated `$20.7550B`, or about `93.5%` of total sales, while `Farnell` generated `$1.4458B`, or about `6.5%`.
- Fiscal `2025` margin performance was weaker, which makes the rebound story more useful. Gross margin fell to `10.7%` from `11.6%`, operating income margin fell to `2.3%` from `3.6%`, adjusted operating income margin fell to `2.8%` from `3.8%`, and diluted `EPS` fell to `$2.75` from `$5.43`.
- Even in a softer year, the annual filing shows why scaled intermediaries matter. Avnet’s franchised distribution agreements provide inventory return privileges and some protection against obsolescence and price erosion, which is a direct example of how channel operators defend working-capital risk.
- The annual packet also broadens the end-market interpretation. Avnet’s component business sits under industrial, automotive, defense and aerospace, data-center and communications infrastructure, and a wider electronics manufacturing ecosystem rather than only one narrow consumer-tech category.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q4 FY2026

- `Q4 FY2026` sales reached a record `$8.2954B`, up `47.7%` year over year and `16.5%` sequentially, which is unusually strong proof that a channel intermediary can show accelerating recovery before many downstream producers fully translate it into their own results.
- Diluted `EPS` was `$1.49` versus `$0.07` in the prior-year quarter, while adjusted diluted `EPS` rose `182%` year over year to `$2.28`.
- Operating income margin rose to `2.8%`, up `147` basis points year over year, and adjusted operating income margin rose to `3.8%`, up `129` basis points.
- `Electronic Components` operating margin reached `4.1%` on record segment sales of roughly `$7.8B`, while `Farnell` operating margin reached `9.0%` on record sales of roughly `$500M`.
- Working-capital discipline stayed central even during the surge. Inventory days fell by `6` days sequentially to `71`, with `Electronic Components` inventory days under `65` and `Farnell` inventory days under `200`.
- The same release also gave the fiscal `2026` full-year summary: sales of `$27.6B`, up `25%`; diluted `EPS` of `$4.01`; adjusted diluted `EPS` of `$5.67`; and full-year inventory days of `81`, down `18` days year over year.

### Quarter minus 1: Q3 FY2026

- `Q3 FY2026` sales were `$7.1198B`, up `34.0%` year over year and `12.7%` sequentially, while diluted `EPS` was `$1.14`, up `12.9%` year over year.
- Adjusted diluted `EPS` reached `$1.48`, up `76.2%` year over year, and operating income margin improved to `2.9%`, up `58` basis points sequentially.
- The working-capital read is especially important here. Management said return on working capital expanded by more than `300` basis points sequentially while days of inventory improved to `77`.
- Segment detail shows that the growth was broad rather than isolated. `Electronic Components` sales were `$6.6651B`, up `34.7%`, while `Farnell` sales were `$454.7M`, up `24.0%`.
- Geographic performance was also broad. Americas sales were `$1.6150B`, EMEA sales were `$2.0463B`, and Asia sales were `$3.4585B`, with the strongest year-over-year growth again coming from Asia.
- The company used `$54M` of operating cash flow to support about `$800M` of sales growth, which reinforces the point that financing and coordination are part of the product in this lane.

### Quarter minus 2: Q2 FY2026

- `Q2 FY2026` sales were `$6.3190B`, up `11.6%` year over year and `7.1%` sequentially, which marked the clearer turn from the weaker `2025` period into a broadening rebound.
- Asia recorded its sixth consecutive year-over-year growth quarter and reached a record revenue level of about `$3.2B`, which makes this packet useful as a global technology-supply-chain indicator rather than only a U.S. distribution case.
- Diluted `EPS` was `$0.75` and adjusted diluted `EPS` was `$1.05`, while operating income margin was `2.3%` and adjusted operating income margin was `2.7%`.
- Operating cash flow was `$208M`, and inventories were reduced by `$126M`, which is analytically important because it shows Avnet was already improving working-capital efficiency while growth was reaccelerating.
- `Electronic Components` sales were `$5.8919B`, up `10.8%`, while `Farnell` sales were `$427.1M`, up `23.6%`.
- The quarter-end balance sheet also makes the economics visible. At `2025-12-27`, inventories were about `$5.2945B` and receivables were about `$5.2428B`.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Avnet is far from consumer branding, but it sits underneath several of the most important current social and industrial shifts: AI and data-center build-out, industrial automation, automotive electronics growth, electrification, defense-system demand, and the wider digitization of physical infrastructure.
- The packet also reflects a broader cultural shift inside business procurement. Customers increasingly do not want to solve sourcing, engineering support, availability risk, and logistics complexity supplier by supplier, so more value accrues to scaled intermediaries that reduce coordination burden.
- Avnet helps explain why the technology economy is still deeply physical. Even when the end story is software, cloud, automation, or smart systems, someone still has to finance inventory, aggregate suppliers, manage allocation, and move components through the channel.
- This is also a strong globalization-and-resilience story. Regional breadth, especially in Asia, matters because many downstream industries are trying to diversify sourcing risk without fully disintermediating the distribution layer.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Working capital is a first-order economic variable here, not a footnote. Inventory days, receivables, cash conversion, and return on working capital recur throughout the packet because the business is partly selling timing, availability, and financing capacity.
- The company is exposed to the electronics cycle, which means margins can compress sharply in weaker demand periods and then expand quickly when volume recovers.
- Segment mix matters. `Electronic Components` is the larger engine and carries the main scale logic, while `Farnell` adds a more service-attached and higher-margin catalog and engineering-oriented layer.
- Supplier relationships remain central. Avnet’s economics depend on franchise relationships, product access, price discipline, and the ability to manage obsolescence or price-erosion risk across a large inventory base.
- The company is also tied to industrial and geopolitical pressures that are broader than consumer demand: regional manufacturing cadence, semiconductor supply normalization, defense and aerospace demand, automotive electronics, and global capital-spending cycles.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `Technology distribution is still physical.` Avnet shows that semiconductors and embedded systems still move through an inventory-heavy, financing-heavy, and logistics-heavy middle layer.
- `Inventory discipline is part of the moat.` The repeated focus on inventory days and return on working capital shows that the intermediary’s advantage is not just customer relationships but also superior control of balance-sheet velocity.
- `Recovery hits the distributor early.` Avnet’s quarter chain suggests the channel can show broadening demand before the more visible downstream brands tell the same story.
- `Service attachment deepens the role.` Design support, programming, labeling, testing, replenishment, and supply-chain management widen the moat beyond product throughput alone.
- `The middle layer gains importance when sourcing gets harder.` As electronics systems become more global, specialized, and timing-sensitive, the scaled intermediary becomes more rather than less important.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Arrow Electronics, Inc.`: Arrow is stronger in enterprise-computing solutions and a somewhat broader downstream enterprise-channel read; Avnet is more explicit about component economics, `Farnell`, inventory days, and return on working capital.
- Against `WESCO International Inc.`: WESCO makes electrification, utility, communications, and data-center infrastructure visible through a project-heavy electrical channel; Avnet is the closer semiconductors-and-components analogue inside the technology supply chain.
- Against `Fastenal Company`: Fastenal embeds procurement inside industrial customer workflow through onsite service and vending; Avnet does something analogous in electronics through supplier aggregation, design support, and supply-chain-services attachment.
- Against `Resideo Technologies, Inc.`: Resideo shows low-voltage contractor-channel and security-controls distribution; Avnet shows a broader electronics and component-intermediation layer tied to OEMs, industrial buyers, and global manufacturing channels.

## Working interpretation

- Avnet is one of the strongest proofs in CLI 8 that the technology supply chain itself is a middle-layer frontier built on inventory, financing, supplier relationships, and service attachment rather than only branded product innovation.
- The bigger pattern is that electronics complexity strengthens the intermediary. As sourcing, design cycles, and regional production networks get harder to coordinate, companies like Avnet become more structurally important to how the economy actually functions.
