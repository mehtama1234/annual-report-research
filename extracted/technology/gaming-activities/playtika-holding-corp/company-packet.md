# Playtika Holding Corp. packet

Date baseline: `2026-08-10`

## Snapshot

- Playtika is a mobile-gaming operator built around repeat monetization, live-ops discipline, and increasingly direct billing control rather than tentpole console releases.
- The central operating story in the filings is a portfolio mix shift:
  - more direct-to-consumer revenue
  - more contribution from SuperPlay
  - less dependence on pure slot-style monetization
- That shift improved some core economics but also created new pressure through:
  - heavy SuperPlay-linked marketing
  - contingent-consideration volatility
  - a still-large debt stack
  - signs of softer consumer spending by `Q2 2026`
- Reported scale across the covered period:
  - full-year `2025` revenue: `$2.755 billion`
  - full-year `2025` adjusted EBITDA: `$753.2 million`
  - `Q1 2026` revenue: `$744.7 million`
  - `Q2 2026` revenue: `$731.1 million`

## Filing coverage

- `2025 10-K` filed `2026-02-26`
- `Q4 2025` earnings `8-K` and `EX-99.1` filed `2026-02-26`
- `Q1 2026 10-Q` and earnings `8-K` filed `2026-05-08`
- `Q2 2026 10-Q` and earnings `8-K` filed `2026-08-06`

## Annual takeaways from `2025`

### 1. Playtika is trying to turn habitual play into a lower-friction payments machine

- The `2025 10-K` and subsequent quarters show a clear push toward direct-to-consumer payment flows through Playtika webstore and related payment tooling.
- The annual filing says the company uses payment-page optimization tools for direct-to-consumer and Playtika webstore transactions.
- Full-year `2025` direct-to-consumer revenue reached `$814.5 million`, up from `$694.2 million`.
- This matters structurally because the later `10-Q`s say higher direct-to-consumer mix lowered platform-fee pressure versus third-party channels.

### 2. The company is no longer just a legacy social-casino story

- The `2025 10-K` says casual games generated `70.8%` of `2025` revenue, with social casino-themed games at `29.2%`.
- Management highlighted a healthier and more balanced mix by the end of `2025`.
- The fastest-growing title in `2025` was `Disney Solitaire`, described in the filing as a licensed collaboration between Disney and Pixar Games and SuperPlay based on Disney intellectual property.
- This is an important archive signal:
  - the company is still built on repeat spending loops
  - but the mix is moving toward broader casual and licensed participation rather than only casino-adjacent habit systems

### 3. SuperPlay became both the growth engine and the main accounting noise source

- The filings repeatedly frame SuperPlay as the major source of incremental revenue and marketing investment.
- But they also show that the SuperPlay earnout drives major swings in reported GAAP earnings.
- In `Q4 2025`, the change in estimated value of contingent consideration was `$394.1 million`.
- For full-year `2025`, that figure was `$398.8 million`.
- The `2025 10-K` says contingent consideration tied to SuperPlay was valued with a Monte Carlo model using significant unobservable inputs and was a Level 3 fair value measurement.
- This means one of Playtika's most important growth assets is also a major driver of earnings volatility and capital-allocation pressure.

### 4. The company still carries real balance-sheet weight

- The `2025 10-K` and `2026` quarterlies show a large financing structure:
  - `$1.9 billion` senior secured first lien term loan
  - `$600.0 million` of `4.250%` senior notes due `2029`
  - a revolving credit facility sized at `$550 million`
- The revolver maturity ultimately reset to `March 6, 2027`, not the longer extension once contemplated through the earlier amendment process.
- The filings also state that the company has significant indebtedness and is subject to restrictive covenants.
- So even with strong cash generation, Playtika is not a light-balance-sheet participation story.

### 5. Revenue concentration and platform dependence remain core risks

- The `2025 10-K` says the top ten games represented `90.2%` of revenue in `2025`.
- It also states the company relies on third-party platforms such as the iOS App Store and Google Play Store to distribute games and collect revenue.
- Apple and Google are described as significant distribution and payment platforms, with accounts receivable derived mainly from those two platforms.
- This leaves Playtika exposed to:
  - title concentration
  - platform policy changes
  - billing economics outside its direct-to-consumer channels

## Quarter-by-quarter read

### `Q4 2025`

- Revenue: `$678.8 million`
- Direct-to-consumer revenue: `$250.1 million`
- Net loss: `$(309.3) million`
- Adjusted net income: `$89.0 million`
- Adjusted EBITDA: `$201.4 million`
- Cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments at `2025-12-31`: `$820.2 million`

Additional operating detail:

- direct-to-consumer revenue increased `19.5%` sequentially and `43.2%` year over year
- `Bingo Blitz` revenue: `$158.5 million`
- `Disney Solitaire` revenue: `$71.6 million`
- `June's Journey` revenue: `$70.0 million`
- average daily paying users: `357 thousand`
- payer conversion: `4.5%`

Interpretation:

- `Q4 2025` is the clearest proof point that Playtika's growth model is no longer just about keeping older franchises alive.
- It is about re-routing monetization:
  - into direct billing
  - into new casual titles
  - into licensed IP overlays
- But the huge gap between GAAP net loss and adjusted profitability also shows how acquisition structure can swamp the simple operating read.

### `Q1 2026`

- Revenue: `$744.7 million`, up `5.5%` year over year
- Direct-to-consumer revenue: `$291.8 million`, up `62.8%` year over year
- Net loss: `$(57.5) million`
- Adjusted net income: `$13.6 million`
- Adjusted EBITDA: `$125.2 million`
- Cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments at `2026-03-31`: `$779.2 million`

Additional `10-Q` context:

- revenue growth was driven primarily by incremental revenue from certain SuperPlay titles, offsetting reduced monetization mainly in slot-themed games
- cost of revenue fell partly because more revenue came through direct-to-consumer platforms with lower platform fees
- sales and marketing expense increased by `$88.8 million`, largely from increased media buy related to SuperPlay
- general and administrative expense rose primarily because of the `$95 million` contingent-consideration adjustment for the SuperPlay earnout
- the company ended the quarter with `$550 million` of additional revolver capacity

Interpretation:

- `Q1 2026` shows the real economic trade:
  - better revenue mix
  - better billing mix
  - but much heavier growth spend
- This is a participation business that increasingly wins by reactivating and converting users efficiently, not by keeping costs static.

### `Q2 2026`

- Revenue: `$731.1 million`, up `5.0%` year over year
- Direct-to-consumer revenue: `$286.9 million`, up `63.1%` year over year
- Net income: `$48.0 million`
- Adjusted net income: `$53.6 million`
- Adjusted EBITDA: `$206.1 million`
- Cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments at `2026-06-30`: `$438.5 million`

Additional `10-Q` context:

- six-month revenue increased by `$73.8 million`, primarily due to incremental revenues from SuperPlay titles
- cost of revenue again benefited from a higher direct-to-consumer mix and lower platform fees
- six-month sales and marketing expense increased by `$83.7 million`, largely from increased media buy related to SuperPlay
- six-month operating cash flow was only `$51.5 million`, down from `$164.9 million` in the prior-year period
- the filing says the six-month period included `$111.0 million` of cash outflow for payment of the SuperPlay earnout in excess of acquisition-date fair value
- management said it expected the year to finish toward the lower end of guidance due to a more cautious consumer-spending view and a planned second-half marketing step-down

Interpretation:

- `Q2 2026` is the most useful quarter for thematic reading because it shows both sides of the Playtika model at once.
- The company can still drive:
  - strong DTC growth
  - high-margin quarter-to-quarter recovery
  - rapid contribution from acquired titles
- But it is not insulated from weaker consumer tone, and its cash profile can tighten quickly when earnouts and acquisition obligations come due.

## Repeating operating signals

### Consumer behavior

- Playtika's core user is not buying a one-time premium product.
- The model depends on repeated micro-commitments:
  - returning to the game
  - converting into a payer
  - staying in the habit loop long enough for lifetime value to justify media spend
- Rising payer conversion alongside direct billing growth suggests the company is getting better at extracting value from engaged users, not just growing raw reach.
- The `Q2 2026` language about more cautious consumer spending matters because this model is highly sensitive to how willing users are to keep making small recurring purchases.

### Cultural and societal read-through

- Playtika sits inside the same broader participation economy as Roblox, EA, and Take-Two, but at a more habitual and lower-ticket layer.
- It monetizes:
  - routine engagement
  - digital companionship with familiar game systems
  - repeat check-in behavior
  - limited-time offers
  - IP overlays that refresh attention without requiring a new entertainment universe
- `Disney Solitaire` is especially important here because it shows how licensed IP can be used as a monetization accelerant inside an already-optimized habit engine.
- That is a different cultural mechanism from owning a whole franchise world:
  - less mythic
  - more continuous
  - more transactionally optimized

### Industrial and operating pressures

- repeated platform pressure:
  - Apple and Google remain major distribution and payment bottlenecks
- repeated portfolio pressure:
  - the top ten titles still dominate revenue
  - older titles can weaken even while the overall portfolio grows
- repeated acquisition pressure:
  - SuperPlay drives growth but also marketing intensity, earnout cash obligations, and accounting volatility
- repeated capital-structure pressure:
  - large debt balances and a near-dated revolver maturity require ongoing discipline
- repeated legal and regulatory pressure:
  - the `2025 10-K` discloses litigation around `Dice Dreams` in Washington and social-casino monetization claims in Australia
  - this is a reminder that some participation loops can attract gambling, consumer-protection, and offer-design scrutiny

## Cross-company interpretation

- Compared with `Take-Two`, Playtika is less dependent on giant prestige-world launches and more dependent on continuous mobile monetization discipline.
- Compared with `Electronic Arts`, it has less sports-franchise insulation and more direct exposure to payer behavior in habitual mobile loops.
- Compared with `Roblox`, it does not own a user-generated social economy.
- Compared with `AppLovin`, it sits one layer closer to the player and one layer farther from the ad-auction infrastructure.

That gives the archive a useful additional distinction:

- owned prestige and sports worlds: `Take-Two`, `Electronic Arts`
- user-generated participation platform: `Roblox`
- creator and monetization tooling: `Unity`
- ad-routing and optimization infrastructure: `AppLovin`
- habitual live-ops and DTC monetization engine: `Playtika`

## Bottom line

- Playtika is one of the clearest examples in the repo of a company monetizing participation as repeated habit rather than one-time launch excitement.
- The strongest signal is not simply that revenue grew.
- It is that the company is trying to move the economics of mobile participation in its favor by:
  - increasing direct billing
  - improving payer conversion
  - using acquisition and licensed IP to refresh the habit loop
- The main open question is whether that mix shift can keep compounding if consumer spending weakens and SuperPlay-driven investment stops being so accretive.
