## Annual report takeaways

- Waystar gives CLI 5 the missing pure-play healthcare-information and hospital-workflow control anchor, which matters because it turns the healthcare-information bridge from an adjacent inference into a real named subcluster.
- Fiscal `2025` revenue was about `$1.099B`; fourth-quarter revenue was about `$303.5M`; fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA was about `$129.1M`; and the year closed with double-digit growth plus roughly `43%` adjusted EBITDA margin in `Q4`.
- The annual read is that Waystar is not just a narrow claims tool. It is a cloud-native revenue-cycle and payment-workflow system that sits inside eligibility checks, prior authorization, coding and documentation accuracy, claims monitoring, denial recovery, patient payments, and workflow analytics.
- The `2025` `10-K` makes the operating role concrete: Waystar said it served more than `30,000` clients representing over `1M` distinct providers, processed over `7.5B` healthcare-payment transactions in `2025`, and handled over `$2.4T` in gross claims volume spanning roughly `60%` of U.S. patients and one in three U.S. hospital discharges.
- Compared with Oracle and ServiceNow, Waystar looks less like generic enterprise workflow and more like a purpose-built hospital and provider operating layer. Compared with Quest and Labcorp, it is less about the accepted clinical answer and more about the administrative and financial workflow that determines whether providers actually get paid.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results: revenue was about `$319.7M`, net income was about `$40.9M` or `$0.21` per diluted share, and adjusted EBITDA was about `$136.7M` with about `43%` adjusted EBITDA margin.
- Operating detail: subscription revenue was about `$176.3M`, up `34%`; volume-based revenue was about `$142.1M`, up `3%`; provider revenue was about `$231.8M`, up `23%`; and patient revenue was about `$87.9M`, up `7%`.
- Structural read: `Q2 2026` is the clearest proof that healthcare workflow control can produce software-quality economics when the company owns mission-critical payment routing, denial prevention, patient financial workflows, and AI-enabled automation inside provider systems.
- Demand signal: the company said `1,453` clients contributed more than `$100,000` in LTM revenue, up `15%`, while net revenue retention was `108%`.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results: revenue was about `$313.9M`, net income was about `$43.3M` or `$0.22` per diluted share, and adjusted EBITDA was about `$135.4M` with about `43%` adjusted EBITDA margin.
- Operating detail: subscription revenue was about `$172.2M`, up `38%`; volume-based revenue was about `$139.5M`, up `7%`; net revenue retention was `111%`; and `1,433` clients contributed more than `$100,000` in LTM revenue, up `15%`.
- Strategic read: `Q1 2026` showed that provider workflow-control demand is not just tied to patient volume. It is tied to clients adopting more software categories and wanting fewer manual steps in revenue-cycle operations.
- Guidance signal: management guided `2026` total revenue to about `$1.274B` to `$1.294B` and adjusted EBITDA to about `$530M` to `$540M`, reinforcing that this is a scaling control layer rather than a marginal niche tool.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results: fourth-quarter revenue was about `$303.5M`, net income was about `$20.0M` or `$0.10` per diluted share, and adjusted EBITDA was about `$129.1M` with about `43%` adjusted EBITDA margin.
- Full-year readthrough: fiscal `2025` revenue was about `$1.099B`, and the year closed with strong growth plus sustained high-margin conversion.
- Strategic read: `Q4 2025` anchors Waystar as the pure-play healthcare-information and hospital-workflow control comparison because it shows providers paying recurring software tolls to make the revenue cycle, prior-authorization chain, and payment workflow more governable.
- Expansion signal: the `2025` annual filing also emphasizes over `500` channel-partner integrations including ERP, PM, and EHR systems, which makes the platform more embedded and harder to replace.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer and societal pattern: Waystar monetizes the fact that patients, providers, and payers all need healthcare payments, approvals, and collections to move with less friction and fewer manual errors.
- Industrial pressure: reimbursement complexity, prior authorization, denial management, staffing strain, EHR integration, and provider cash-flow sensitivity all create recurring demand for workflow-control software.
- Cultural shift: the platform reflects a healthcare system that increasingly accepts AI, automation, and software governance as necessary operating infrastructure rather than optional add-ons.
- Bigger-picture read: Waystar matters because it turns the CLI 5 healthcare-information bridge into a true software-control layer with direct healthcare exposure, not just an analogy borrowed from broader enterprise software.
