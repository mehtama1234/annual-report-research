## What changed

- Revenue: full-year FY2025 revenue reached `$69.67B`; the latest three reported quarters then moved from `$18.7B` in FY2026 Q1 to `$18.0B` in FY2026 Q2 and back to `$18.7B` in FY2026 Q3.
- Margin: full-year FY2025 GAAP operating margin reached `14.7%` and adjusted operating margin reached `15.6%`; the trailing quarter set then showed GAAP operating margins of `15.3%`, `13.8%`, and `17.0%` with diluted EPS of `$3.54`, `$2.93`, and `$3.80`.
- Cash flow: full-year FY2025 free cash flow reached `$10.9B`; FY2026 Q2 and Q3 still produced robust quarterly free cash flow of `$3.7B` and `$3.6B`.
- Bookings and pipeline: full-year FY2025 new bookings reached `$80.6B`, including `$5.9B` of generative-AI bookings; FY2026 Q1, Q2, and Q3 bookings then came in at `$20.9B`, `$22.1B`, and `$19.3B`.
- Segment mix: Accenture stays split between consulting and managed services, with quarterly commentary repeatedly showing slower consulting-style discretionary work and steadier managed-services demand.
- Operational issues: management continues to frame the business around enterprise reinvention, data-and-AI transformation, and productivity programs, while also acknowledging client caution on smaller projects and pressure from reduced U.S. federal work in FY2026 Q3 guidance.
- Consumer / cultural signals: this is not a direct household-demand read; it is an enterprise-spending and labor-arbitrage read showing how large organizations are buying implementation, workflow redesign, and AI adoption support rather than only software seats or infrastructure.
- Management tone: management is measured and execution-oriented, emphasizing bookings quality, large-scale transformation demand, disciplined profitability, and AI monetization through services rather than ownership of the underlying platform.

## Open questions

- Can Accenture keep converting strong AI bookings into durable revenue growth without leaning too heavily on lower-margin implementation work?
- Does the managed-services mix remain resilient if consulting budgets stay shorter-cycle and U.S. federal pressure continues?
