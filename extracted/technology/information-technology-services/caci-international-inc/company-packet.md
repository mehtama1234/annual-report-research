# CACI International Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Company: CACI International Inc.
- Sector: Technology
- Industry: Technical Services
- Ticker: CACI
- Fiscal year-end: June
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Fiscal Q4 2026
  - quarter 2: Fiscal Q3 2026
  - quarter 3: Fiscal Q2 2026

## Why this name matters

- CACI is the right next flagship because it adds the mission-software, intelligence-processing, electronic-warfare, space-sensing, and government-information-services layer that sits above raw connectivity and hardware but below the consumer-facing software narrative.
- It also helps the archive close an important gap in the physical-and-digital infrastructure stack. Towers, satellites, test gear, power systems, and datacenters explain where capacity lives, but CACI shows how national-security institutions convert that capacity into software-defined mission systems, classified workflows, and recurring federal technology spending.

## Annual report takeaways

- CACI’s `2025` annual report does not read like a legacy beltway-services story. The cover, shareholder letter, and business highlights frame the company around national security, software-enabled modernization, electronic warfare, counter-unmanned systems, network modernization, and mission knowledge rather than generic IT outsourcing.
- Fiscal `2025` was already strong before the later FY26 acceleration. Annual revenue reached `$8.63 billion`, up `12.6%`, net income reached `$499.8 million`, diluted EPS reached `$22.32`, adjusted diluted EPS reached `$26.48`, EBITDA reached `$966.8 million`, and free cash flow reached about `$442.5 million`.
- Contract awards matter because they are the clearest forward-demand signal in this lane. The annual report says CACI won `$10 billion` in contract awards in fiscal `2025`, giving the business visibility that looks different from classical enterprise software but can still produce durable revenue and margin growth.
- The company’s own positioning is useful for theme work. Management said it is focused on enduring and well-funded national-security priorities, investing ahead of customer need, leveraging software to deliver capabilities faster, and building differentiated bid and mission proximity.
- CACI’s technical mix is moving toward higher-value mission systems. The annual report highlights electronic warfare, software-defined modernization, counter-unmanned systems, classified-network tools, and enterprise automation at NASA. This points to a business that is trying to be more productized and technically embedded, not just more labor-intensive.
- The annual materials also point toward a broader technology thesis: AI and software are being deployed in security and defense contexts where latency, resilience, sensing, and trusted execution matter more than consumer scale or ad monetization.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Fiscal Q4 2026

- Results: fourth-quarter revenue was `$2.71 billion`, up `17.6%`, including `11.6%` organic growth. Net income was `$156.8 million`, adjusted net income was `$198.1 million`, and annual revenue reached `$9.6 billion` with annual EBITDA margin of `12.3%`.
- Demand read: annual contract awards reached `$10.2 billion` with book-to-bill of `1.1x`. Total backlog ended fiscal `2026` at `$32.0 billion`, and funded backlog reached `$5.4 billion`.
- Management message: management described CACI as a technology-first national-security company and said the FY27 outlook keeps the company on track to meet or exceed its three-year financial targets.
- Structural themes: fiscal `Q4 2026` is where CACI most clearly looks like a modern defense-tech and mission-software platform rather than a traditional contractor. It paired double-digit annual growth, margin expansion, and cash-flow improvement with explicit emphasis on AI, optical communications, space-domain buildout, and software-defined electronic warfare.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: fiscal `Q3 2026` revenue was `$2.4 billion`, up `8.5%`, driven by `6.8%` organic growth. EBITDA was `$289.7 million`, and EBITDA margin was `12.3%`, including `$17.4 million` of ARKA-related transaction expense.
- Demand read: Q3 contract awards were `$2.2 billion`. Total backlog reached `$33.4 billion`, up `6.4%`, and funded backlog reached `$5.0 billion`, up `19.0%`.
- Management message: management raised fiscal `2026` revenue and EBITDA-margin guidance while reaffirming free-cash-flow guidance even after absorbing ARKA transaction costs.
- Structural themes: Q3 shows how acquisitions are being used to sharpen technical relevance, not just to add scale. ARKA was framed as a way to expand space-based sensing, EO/IR and hyperspectral imaging, and Agentic-AI-enabled geospatial intelligence for national-security customers.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: fiscal `Q2 2026` revenue was `$2.2 billion`, up `5.7%`. Net income was `$123.9 million`, adjusted net income was `$150.7 million`, and EBITDA was `$262.6 million` with EBITDA margin of `11.8%`.
- Demand read: Q2 contract awards were `$1.4 billion`. Total backlog reached `$32.8 billion`, up `3.1%`, and funded backlog reached `$4.4 billion`, up `7.3%`.
- Management message: management raised fiscal `2026` guidance across all metrics and used the quarter to frame electronic warfare, Agile software development, and the planned ARKA acquisition as central to future value creation.
- Structural themes: Q2 is where the company’s barbell becomes easiest to see. One side is a recurring, budget-driven government-contractor revenue base; the other is a higher-technology push into space, software-defined systems, cyber, and AI-enabled mission tools.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- CACI is not a consumer-facing name, but it sits directly under several important societal shifts: the normalization of persistent geopolitical tension, growing comfort with software-mediated defense operations, and the expectation that AI, sensing, and data-processing capabilities should be fused into national-security decision loops.
- The cultural pattern here is not app adoption. It is the migration of software prestige from the consumer internet into the security state, critical government workflows, and classified or semi-classified mission systems. In that world, “platform dependence” means dependence on federal budgets, contract vehicles, clearances, and technical relevance to priority missions.
- There is also a legitimacy and sovereignty angle. Programs tied to electronic warfare, counter-drone systems, geospatial intelligence, secure network modernization, and military business systems reflect a broader societal willingness to treat digital capability as a national-security necessity rather than as optional modernization.
- CACI also reflects a shift in how AI is being marketed. Instead of general-purpose productivity or consumer engagement, AI here is presented as mission acceleration, sensing enhancement, software-defined decision support, and battlefield or intelligence advantage.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Government concentration is the core structural dependency. CACI can grow well inside that structure, but procurement timing, appropriations, protest risk, and shifts in federal priority still shape the revenue path.
- Technical density raises the execution bar. Electronic warfare, optical communications, secure networks, and space sensing can carry better margins and stronger mission relevance, but they also increase program complexity and delivery risk.
- ARKA deepens the growth story while raising integration risk. The acquisition clearly expands the company’s space and sensing stack, but it also increases leverage, interest expense, and the need for smooth organizational integration.
- Working-capital and cash discipline matter more than the top line alone. The quarter chain repeatedly highlights strong working-capital management and cash conversion, which implies this is a business where execution quality shows up in cash flow as much as in revenue.
- Cleared labor and technical talent remain quiet but critical constraints. The disclosures repeatedly reference competition for specialized talent and contract-performance obligations that are not entirely within management’s control.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `AI does not only monetize through consumer software or hyperscaler capex.` CACI shows a different AI pathway: mission systems, geospatial intelligence, software-defined electronic warfare, and secure government workflows.
- `Government dependence can still produce recurring infrastructure-like economics.` Backlog, multi-year awards, funded backlog, and book-to-bill give CACI a different kind of recurring visibility than subscription software, but the archive should treat it as a real recurring-demand model all the same.
- `Mission proximity is a moat.` CACI repeatedly argues that being close to the mission drives better product fit, faster iteration, and stronger competitive positioning. That is a useful pattern for understanding why some government-tech names can outgrow slower consulting peers.
- `Defense modernization is becoming a software-and-sensing story as much as a hardware story.` Space-based sensing, optical communications, Agile modernization, and agentic AI all point to a more software-defined defense stack.
- `National-security urgency can justify investment-heavy capital allocation.` CACI is using acquisitions, technical hiring, and portfolio expansion to press into areas where management believes customer need is durable and well funded.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `Motorola Solutions, Inc.`: Motorola monetizes mission-critical communications and institutional safety workflows, while CACI monetizes classified mission systems, intelligence, software-defined defense capabilities, and federal modernization.
- Against `Iridium Communications Inc.` and `American Tower Corporation`: those names own or monetize parts of the communications and coverage layer, while CACI monetizes what national-security users do with data, communications, sensing, and mission software once the network exists.
- Against `Teledyne Technologies Incorporated`: Teledyne is broader in sensing and instrumentation, while CACI is more directly tied to government program execution, mission software, and national-security operational workflows.

## Working interpretation

- CACI materially improves the archive because it adds the national-security information-services and mission-software layer that sits between physical connectivity infrastructure and the institutions that act on intelligence, signals, and operational data.
- It also sharpens the broader conclusion that software and AI demand are not only cultural or consumer stories. They are also defense, sovereignty, and mission-execution stories, monetized through contracts, backlog, technical adjacency, and long-cycle federal modernization budgets.
