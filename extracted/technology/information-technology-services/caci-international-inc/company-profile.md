## What changed

- CACI is increasingly presenting itself as a technology-first national-security platform rather than as a generic government-services contractor. The annual report and the FY26 quarter chain repeatedly emphasize software-defined capabilities, electronic warfare, space sensing, optical communications, cyber, and mission-focused AI.
- Growth and margins improved together. Fiscal `2025` revenue reached `$8.63 billion` with EBITDA margin of `11.2%`, then fiscal `2026` revenue reached `$9.6 billion` with EBITDA margin of `12.3%`. Quarterly revenue also stepped from `$2.2 billion` in fiscal `Q2 2026` to `$2.4 billion` in fiscal `Q3 2026` to `$2.7 billion` in fiscal `Q4 2026`.
- Backlog and awards remained the cleanest visibility read. Annual contract awards were `$10.0 billion` in fiscal `2025` and `$10.2 billion` in fiscal `2026`. Total backlog reached `$32.8 billion` in fiscal `Q2 2026`, `$33.4 billion` in fiscal `Q3 2026`, and `$32.0 billion` with funded backlog of `$5.4 billion` at fiscal year-end `2026`.
- ARKA changed the shape of the story. By fiscal `Q2 2026`, ARKA was still a planned acquisition framed around space-based sensing and ground-based software processing. By fiscal `Q3 2026`, it had closed and was already being used to deepen CACI’s position in national-security space, electro-optical and hyperspectral imaging, and Agentic-AI-enabled geospatial intelligence.
- Free-cash-flow quality improved materially. The fiscal `2026` full-year release says free cash flow grew `66%`, reinforcing that this is not only a backlog and budget story but also a cash-conversion story when working capital is managed tightly.

## Working interpretation

- CACI is one of the archive’s clearest examples of how AI, software, and information dominance are being monetized through national-security infrastructure rather than through consumer platforms. It sells mission systems, software-defined capabilities, intelligence processing, and program execution into long-cycle federal budgets.
- The most useful recurring pattern is that recurring government dependence can still produce a growth-and-margin compounder when the company sits close to priority missions, cleared workflows, and technically dense modernization programs. In CACI’s case, mission proximity matters as much as code.

## Open questions

- How much of the current growth and margin expansion is tied to a durable upgrade in CACI’s technical mix versus a favorable phase of federal budget and procurement timing?
- Does the ARKA acquisition deepen CACI’s space and sensing moat enough to outweigh the higher leverage, interest burden, and integration complexity that now sit inside the story?
