Date baseline: 2026-08-10

- Sector: Technology

- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026

## Why this name matters

- Leidos is the right next flagship because it adds the government-technology, digital-modernization, mission-software, cyber, and managed-services layer that sits between physical communications infrastructure and the institutions that actually use it.
- It is also a useful counterweight to CACI. CACI leans more explicitly into intelligence, software-defined defense, and national-security technology identity, while Leidos is broader across defense, homeland, health, civil, aviation, and enterprise modernization workflows.

## Annual report takeaways

- The `2025` annual report frames Leidos around digital modernization and cyber, mission software, and managed health services rather than around a low-level staffing story. Management describes a business built to run critical infrastructure, classified networks, government workflows, and operational decision systems.
- The company said `87%` of fiscal `2025` revenue came from U.S. government contracts. That single number is the clearest structural fact in the packet. Leidos is not primarily exposed to consumer demand or classic enterprise IT budgets. It is exposed to federal priorities, appropriations, contract vehicles, and program continuity.
- Segment structure matters. As of the annual filing, Leidos was aligned into `National Security & Digital`, `Health & Civil`, `Commercial & International`, and `Defense Systems`. That mix shows a company trying to own multiple government modernization lanes at once: secure digital systems, federal health workflows, aviation and transport operations, and defense hardware-software integration.
- The annual report’s capability language is unusually revealing for theme work. The company highlights digital support for critical infrastructure, cloud and network modernization, AI tools for defense and intelligence customers, multi-domain coordination, cyber operations, and managed health and biomedical capabilities. This is a platform built around mission continuity and data movement, not just labor hours.
- The risk section also sharpens the business model. Management repeatedly flags appropriations delays, continuing resolutions, shutdown risk, clearance needs, fixed-price estimation, cybersecurity threats, and procurement competition. That means Leidos has recurring demand, but it is recurring inside a politically mediated procurement system.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q2 2026

- Results context: the `2026-08-04` `10-Q` kept the core story intact. Revenue concentration remained `87%` U.S.-government-linked, cash and cash equivalents rose to `$748 million`, and outstanding debt stood at `$6.0 billion`.
- Demand read: total backlog was `$48.7 billion`, with `Intelligence & Digital` at `$18.4 billion`, `Health` at `$6.6 billion`, `Homeland` at `$9.9 billion`, and `Defense` at `$13.8 billion`. Defense and Intelligence & Digital were the clearest forward-demand anchors.
- Structural themes: the quarter shows how bandwidth, cloud, and AI demand can monetize through government mission networks rather than through consumer or hyperscaler stories alone. The filings keep emphasizing cyber capabilities, advanced technical solutions at scale, and resilient operations across distributed and classified environments.

### Quarter minus 1: Q1 2026

- Results context: the `2026-05-05` `10-Q` showed cash at `$457 million` and outstanding debt at `$6.3 billion` after the Entrust-related financing step-up.
- Demand read: total backlog was `$48.4 billion`, including `Intelligence & Digital` at `$19.3 billion`, `Health` at `$6.6 billion`, `Homeland` at `$9.9 billion`, and `Defense` at `$12.6 billion`. The Homeland figure included about `$371 million` of backlog acquired through Entrust.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the cleanest quarter for seeing capability expansion plus financing pressure together. Management used the balance sheet to add trusted-identity and infrastructure-related capability, which supports the recurring theme that higher-trust government workflows increasingly depend on integrated software, data, and security stacks.

### Quarter minus 2: Q4 2025

- Results context: the `2025` annual filing anchored the year-end state. Total backlog was about `$49.0 billion`, including roughly `$9.7 billion` of funded backlog.
- Demand read: the annual report repeatedly links growth to digital modernization and cyber, mission software, AI-enabled operations, and managed health services. This confirms that the business is leaning into priority modernization budgets rather than purely maintaining older contracts.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is where the company most clearly reads as a government infrastructure software and services platform. The business spans cloud and network modernization, secure data handling, cyber operations, mission software, transport and aviation systems, and federal health operations.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal interpretation

- Leidos is not consumer-facing, but it sits under several major societal shifts: a world of persistent geopolitical tension, rising dependence on software-mediated public systems, and the normalization of AI and data tooling inside defense, health, aviation, and homeland operations.
- The cultural shift here is that “software” is no longer only a workplace-productivity or consumer-platform story. In Leidos’s world, software is increasingly the operating layer for government logistics, benefits administration, health workflows, security networks, transportation management, and mission command.
- There is also a trust and sovereignty pattern. Institutions are spending not just for functionality but for controlled, secure, compliant execution inside sensitive systems. That makes clearances, procurement standing, cyber credibility, and mission embeddedness into real economic assets.
- Managed health services matter in this frame because they show that even healthcare infrastructure is being treated as a recurring operational platform problem: data movement, claims or workflow complexity, federal program execution, and service continuity rather than just one-time product sales.

## Industrial and operating pressures

- Federal-budget dependence is the central pressure. Appropriations delays, continuing resolutions, and potential shutdowns can affect award timing, new starts, and payment cadence even when long-run demand remains healthy.
- Contract mix matters. The filings repeatedly call out cost-reimbursement, fixed-price-incentive, firm-fixed-price, and time-and-materials structures. That means margin quality depends on estimating cost, labor, and scope correctly, not just on winning work.
- Cleared and specialized labor remains a real bottleneck. Leidos explicitly flags security clearances, specialized talent, and competition for technically capable labor. That constraint is easy to miss if the company is read too abstractly as “IT services.”
- Cyber and compliance burden are part of the product. The company is selling into environments where data protection, classified execution, and procurement compliance are prerequisites, which raises the operating bar but also reinforces moat-like characteristics.
- Acquisition and leverage discipline matter. The quarter chain shows debt stepping up around Entrust. Capability expansion can strengthen the platform, but it also creates integration and balance-sheet risk if growth or procurement timing slips.

## Repeated higher-order patterns

- `AI and software demand do not only monetize through consumer apps or hyperscaler capex.` Leidos shows a federal pathway where AI, cyber, and digital modernization monetize through mission systems, public infrastructure, and trusted workflow operations.
- `Recurring service revenue can hide inside contract economics.` Leidos does not look like subscription software on the surface, but its program entrenchment, backlog, and long-cycle government relationships create a different kind of recurring revenue base.
- `Platform dependence can mean procurement dependence.` In this lane, being embedded in the right contract vehicles, budget priorities, and agency workflows matters as much as traditional product-market fit.
- `Bandwidth and cloud demand ultimately require orchestration layers.` Towers, satellites, datacenters, and backup power provide capacity; Leidos provides part of the operational layer that turns capacity into intelligence, health, homeland, and defense execution.
- `The physical layer behind software often includes people, compliance, and trusted operational process.` The packet is a good reminder that infrastructure is not only hardware. It can also be institutionally embedded software and managed execution.

## Best comparison uses

- Against `CACI International Inc.`: CACI is more explicitly mission-tech and intelligence-forward, while Leidos is broader and more institutionally diversified across defense, homeland, health, civil, and transportation workflows.
- Against `Motorola Solutions, Inc.`: Motorola monetizes mission-critical communications and public-safety workflow systems, while Leidos monetizes the broader government-enterprise operating stack behind cyber, digital modernization, defense integration, and managed health.
- Against `Iridium Communications Inc.` and `American Tower Corporation`: those names own or monetize communications capacity, while Leidos monetizes what governments do with secure networks, data movement, and mission execution once the connectivity exists.
