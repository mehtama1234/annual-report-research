## Annual report takeaways

- This is one of the strongest remaining additions for the attention-and-influence frontier because it shows the infrastructure layer that sits between advertisers, publishers, and screens rather than a content owner, agency network, or pure measurement vendor.
- The continuity story matters. AnnualReports still preserves `Outbrain Inc.`, but the current issuer is `Teads Holding Co.` after the Teads combination and name transition. That makes the packet useful not only for ad-tech economics, but also for understanding how the open-internet monetization stack is being reorganized after consolidation.
- FY `2025` is the clean anchor year for that transition. Full-year revenue was `$1.300B`, gross profit was `$429.1M`, ex-TAC gross profit was `$529.7M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$93.4M`, and adjusted free cash flow was `$6.0M`.
- The annual and year-end materials show the basic shape of the business clearly: a scaled omnichannel advertising platform trying to connect more than `10,000` publishers and `20,000` advertisers across screens with predictive AI, addressability, measurement, and workflow tools.
- The important frontier signal is that this is not merely an ad-placement intermediary. It is a company trying to become a full-funnel open-internet operating layer where branding, performance, `CTV`, publisher yield optimization, and measurement are sold together.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$284.6M`, down `17%`; gross profit was `$95.6M`; ex-TAC gross profit was `$123.4M`; net loss was `$42.5M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$7.0M`; net cash provided by operating activities was `$9.2M`; and adjusted free cash flow was `$3.2M`.
- Operating signals: management reported `67%` year-over-year `CTV` revenue growth, said `CTV` reached `13%` of Q2 revenue versus `7%` in Q2 `2025`, and said branding customers using omnichannel campaigns represented `16%` of `CTV` spend versus `9%` a year earlier.
- Strategic signals: Teads launched `CTV Ensemble`, expanded attention measurement with `Lumen Research`, renewed the `LG` partnership, integrated `TiVo Ads` home-screen placements, and launched `Teads EngageOS` for publishers.
- Interpretation: Q2 shows the split inside modern open-internet ad infrastructure. The enterprise, `CTV`, and omnichannel parts of the platform are growing in strategic importance, while direct-response and SME demand remain under pressure.
- Bigger-picture read: this is frontier-quality because it shows media fragmentation being monetized through infrastructure rather than content ownership. The company is trying to win by coordinating inventory, workflow, and measurement across scattered surfaces.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$266.0M`, down `7%`; gross profit was `$83.6M`; ex-TAC gross profit was `$107.9M`, up `5%`; net loss was `$38.8M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$0.8M`; net cash used in operating activities was `$34.9M`; and adjusted free cash flow was `($41.1M)`.
- Business highlights: management said `CTV` revenue grew more than `50%`, omnichannel branding customers rose to `13%` of `CTV` spend from `8%`, and roughly `16%` of spend from enterprise brand advertisers was directed toward performance-based goals.
- Balance-sheet signals: cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities were `$98.7M`, while total debt obligations were `$623.4M`.
- Interpretation: Q1 is the cleanest articulation of the strategic thesis. Teads argues that the line between branding and conversion is collapsing, and that its value lies in turning `CTV` plus open-internet inventory into one measurable full-funnel workflow.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter is especially useful because it makes the cultural and commercial shift explicit. Advertisers are no longer just buying broad reach or last-click performance. They are buying systems that can move budget across attention surfaces while preserving attribution and control.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$352.2M`; gross profit was `$120.4M`; ex-TAC gross profit was `$151.8M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$36.5M`; net cash provided by operating activities was `$7.3M`; and adjusted free cash flow was `$2.6M`.
- Full-year `2025` anchors: revenue was `$1.300B`; gross profit was `$429.1M`; ex-TAC gross profit was `$529.7M`; adjusted EBITDA was `$93.4M`; net cash provided by operating activities was `$7.6M`; and adjusted free cash flow was `$6.0M`.
- Structural signals: the quarter and year were heavily shaped by the Teads acquisition. Management highlighted scale gains and higher-margin profile, but FY `2025` net loss of `$517.1M` also reflected a `$352.1M` non-cash goodwill impairment, `$15.6M` impairment of intangible assets, `$28.9M` of acquisition and integration costs, `$12.0M` of bridge facility costs, and `$15.3M` of restructuring charges.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY2025 is the anchor period because it shows both the promise and the cost of building a larger open-internet outcomes platform. The platform got bigger, broader, and more strategic, but it also carried impairment, financing, and integration burden.
- Bigger-picture read: this is a high-signal quarter for the frontier because it shows ad-tech consolidation as a response to fragmentation. Scale alone is not the point; the point is to own more of the path between publisher supply, advertiser demand, `CTV`, measurement, and workflow.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Attention monetization is moving into middleware: value can accrue to the operator that coordinates inventory, targeting, and measurement even if it owns neither the audience franchise nor the agency relationship outright.
- Brand and performance are converging: Teads repeatedly describes the future as full-funnel, which means the old split between brand-ad budgets and direct-response budgets is weakening.
- Media fragmentation creates both opportunity and stress: the company benefits when advertisers need help navigating a fragmented open internet, but it also suffers when weaker SME and direct-response demand exposes how cyclical that same fragmented market can be.
- `CTV` is becoming a bridge format: it carries television-like attention, but it is being sold with digital-style measurement, audience logic, and workflow flexibility.
- Trust and proof are no longer enough on their own: advertisers increasingly want measurable outcomes, while publishers increasingly want yield tools that preserve audience engagement. That pushes infrastructure vendors to serve both sides at once.
- Publisher monetization is becoming operationalized: `EngageOS` is a signal that publishers are being sold not just ad demand, but AI-shaped operating systems for total session revenue.
- Frontier fit: this packet materially strengthens the first sub-lane because it gives the archive a direct open-internet and `CTV` outcomes-platform operator that sits between demand, supply, and measurement.

## Working interpretation

- This packet materially improves the frontier because it gives the archive an operator that explains how fragmented attention is turned back into something buyable and measurable.
- The repeated cross-company pattern is that distribution is no longer the same as ownership. Teads does not own the main content franchises or the consumer billing loop, but it still tries to capture value by coordinating advertiser demand, publisher inventory, and screen-level measurement.
- The cultural signal under the numbers is that the open internet is trying to defend itself against both walled gardens and AI-era disintermediation by becoming more measurable, more outcomes-oriented, and more operationally integrated.
- In combination with `Comscore`, `Magnite`, `PubMatic`, `DoubleVerify`, `Taboola`, and `The Trade Desk`, this packet makes the infrastructure side of the attention lane much more defensible because it shows where monetization, workflow control, and publisher economics meet.
