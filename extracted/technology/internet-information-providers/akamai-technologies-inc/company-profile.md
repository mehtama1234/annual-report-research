## What changed

- Revenue: full-year `2025` revenue reached `$4.208 billion`; quarterly revenue then rose from `$1.074 billion` in Q1 `2026` to `$1.100 billion` in Q2 `2026`.
- Mix shift: in full-year `2025`, Security revenue reached `$2.243 billion`, Delivery revenue fell to `$1.257 billion`, and Compute revenue reached `$708 million`, including `$314 million` from Cloud Infrastructure Services. Q1 and Q2 `2026` continued that pattern with security and CIS growth offsetting delivery decline.
- Profitability: full-year `2025` GAAP income from operations reached `$567 million`, while Q1 `2026` GAAP operating income fell to `$114 million` and Q2 `2026` to `$80 million`, showing that the mix shift and infrastructure buildout have not produced simple near-term margin expansion.
- Earnings: full-year `2025` GAAP net income reached `$452 million`; Q1 `2026` GAAP net income was `$106 million`, and Q2 `2026` was `$79 million`.
- Cash generation: full-year `2025` cash from operations reached `$1.519 billion`; Q1 `2026` cash from operations reached `$313 million`, and Q2 `2026` reached `$326 million`.
- Capital intensity: capex reached about `$507.8 million` in `2025`, then about `$101.7 million` in Q1 `2026` and about `$246.6 million` in the first half of `2026`, showing a much more infrastructure-heavy model than legacy CDN stereotypes imply.
- Balance-sheet step-change: cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities rose from `$1.920 billion` at `2025-12-31` to `$4.616 billion` at `2026-06-30`, reflecting the large financing move that accompanied Akamai's accelerating cloud-infrastructure ambitions.
- Strategic signal: across Q4 `2025`, Q1 `2026`, and Q2 `2026`, management increasingly framed Akamai around security, cloud computing, inference, and AI infrastructure rather than around delivery alone.

## Working interpretation

- Akamai is one of the clearest names in the archive for showing how a mature internet infrastructure franchise tries to re-rate itself by shifting from traffic delivery toward security, cloud infrastructure, and AI-adjacent compute.
- The company still sits on the internet's performance and delivery plumbing, but the growth engine has moved toward protecting applications and serving new compute demand rather than simply accelerating static web traffic.

## Open questions

- Can Akamai make the cloud-infrastructure and AI-inference story large enough to offset the long-running erosion in delivery economics without permanently sacrificing margins?
- How defensible is Akamai's AI-infrastructure position against hyperscalers, Cloudflare, and other edge or compute platforms once the early contract wins move from announcement phase into deployment and usage reality?
