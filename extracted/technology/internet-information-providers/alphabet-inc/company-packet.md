# Alphabet Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-08

## Snapshot

- Company: Alphabet Inc.
- Sector: Technology
- Industry: Internet Information Providers
- Ticker: GOOGL
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: 2025
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: Q2 2026
  - quarter 2: Q1 2026
  - quarter 3: Q4 2025

## Annual report takeaways

- 2025 was a threshold year for scale: Alphabet delivered its first-ever `$100 billion` quarter in Q3 and full-year revenue exceeded `$400 billion` for the first time.
- The annual report frames Alphabet as an AI-first company with a full-stack model: technical infrastructure, TPUs and partner GPUs, first-party models, Cloud, Search, YouTube, and consumer AI distribution all reinforce each other.
- Google Cloud ended 2025 at an annual run rate above `$70 billion`, YouTube exceeded `$60 billion` of annual revenue across ads and subscriptions, and consumer subscriptions exceeded `325 million`.
- Alphabet’s annual report is unusually explicit on capital intensity: `2026` capex was expected around `$180 billion`, double the prior year’s `$90 billion`, as infrastructure scales for AI demand.
- The report also highlights consumer and enterprise AI adoption as live business drivers, not future optionality: Gemini, AI Overviews, AI Mode, paid Gemini seats, and Cloud AI usage are all already affecting demand.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 2026 revenue was `$119.8 billion`, operating income was `$40.8 billion`, net income available to common stockholders was `$112.1 billion`, and diluted EPS was `$9.11`.
- Guidance: management raised full-year 2026 capex guidance to `$195-$205 billion` and said capex should increase significantly again in 2027.
- Management message: Alphabet said AI investments are delivering measurable value across Search, Cloud, YouTube, and enterprise products; Cloud backlog reached `$514 billion`, and nearly `90%` of the Fortune 100 are using Gemini Enterprise.
- Structural themes: Cloud revenue accelerated to `82%` growth, TPU system sales began to be recognized, and technical infrastructure spending is now heavy enough to push quarterly free cash flow negative.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 2026 revenue was `$109.9 billion`, operating income was `$39.7 billion`, net income was `$62.6 billion`, and diluted EPS was `$5.11`.
- Guidance: Alphabet raised its quarterly cash dividend by `5%` to `$0.22` and continued leaning into AI-led demand growth.
- Management message: management highlighted 19% Search growth, Google Cloud revenue above `$20 billion`, backlog above `$460 billion`, `350 million` paid subscriptions, and strong Gemini Enterprise adoption.
- Structural themes: this is the clearest quarter showing Alphabet as both a consumer AI distributor and an enterprise AI infrastructure provider.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 2025 revenue was `$113.8 billion`, operating income was `$35.9 billion`, full-year revenue was `$402.8 billion`, full-year operating income was `$129.0 billion`, and full-year diluted EPS was `$10.81`.
- Guidance: management said 2026 capex investments were expected in the range of `$175-$185 billion`.
- Management message: Alphabet emphasized that annual revenue exceeded `$400 billion` for the first time, YouTube topped `$60 billion` of annual revenue, Cloud exited the year above a `$70 billion` run rate, and paid subscriptions surpassed `325 million`.
- Structural themes: Q4 2025 is the bridge quarter where Alphabet’s AI-first strategy stops looking experimental and starts looking like the operating center of the business.

Note: for many companies this third slot will be a late-2025 quarter needed to complete the trailing three-quarter set as of 2026-08-08.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Alphabet is a strong read on technology incumbents using AI to reinforce existing profit pools rather than replacing them outright.
- Consumer: Search, YouTube, Google One, and Gemini app adoption show how consumer usage can still compound even while the company becomes more enterprise- and infrastructure-heavy.
- Cultural: Alphabet is trying to make Gemini a default interface layer for knowledge work, search, media consumption, and mobile computing, which is a major cultural positioning shift.
- Regulatory: antitrust, privacy, AI governance, and data-usage scrutiny remain central background risks even while performance is very strong.
- Labor: Alphabet is still investing heavily in AI talent and R&D, and the operating model suggests premium technical talent remains strategically important despite automation rhetoric.
- Technology: TPUs, partner GPUs, Gemini models, Search AI, Cloud AI solutions, and enterprise tooling together make Alphabet a vertically integrated AI stack owner.
- Capital allocation: the company is willing to let capex surge to exceptional levels while still maintaining dividends, indicating confidence that AI infrastructure returns justify the spend.
