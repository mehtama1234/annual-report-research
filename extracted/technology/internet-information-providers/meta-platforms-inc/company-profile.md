## What changed

- Aggregator gap: Meta is another direct example of why the archive cannot stop at AnnualReports.com. As of `2026-08-09`, AnnualReports still showed `2024` as the most recent annual report, while Meta's official and SEC stack already supported the `2025` annual-report year and the full `Q4 2025` through `Q2 2026` quarter trail.
- Consumer-attention platform at scale: Meta gives the archive a direct global consumer and advertising platform rather than a streaming subscription platform, retailer, or software vendor.
- AI is already inside the business, not only in the narrative: the quarter trail repeatedly ties AI to ad performance, recommendation quality, creator and business tooling, and future product surfaces.
- The `2025` annual package was very large even before the newer AI buildout. Full-year revenue was `$200.966B`, net income was `$60.458B`, diluted EPS was `$23.49`, capital expenditures were `$72.22B`, and DAP averaged `3.58B` in December 2025.
- The quarter trail shows both continued growth and rising investment intensity. Q1 `2026` and Q2 `2026` both showed strong top-line growth and higher ad monetization signals, but also materially higher capital spending and a lower Q2 operating margin due to legal charges, severance, and heavier infrastructure investment.
- Cultural and commercial signal: Meta is useful because it connects daily social and messaging behavior, advertiser demand, AI recommendation systems, business messaging, and wearable ambitions in one consumer platform.

## Open questions

- How durable is Meta's current ad and engagement growth if infrastructure spending and legal or regulatory pressure stay elevated?
- Does the strongest long-run value sit in ad targeting and measurement, messaging commerce, consumer AI interfaces, or a future wearable platform?
- How much of the current consumer moat is network effects and habit, and how much is shifting toward AI-driven relevance and performance marketing?
