## What changed in this period

- The Trade Desk entered `2026` still looking like one of the strongest open-internet demand platforms, with `2025` revenue of `$2.9 billion`, customer retention above `95%`, heavy profitability, and a widening product set around `Kokai`, `OpenPath`, `PubDesk`, `OpenSincera`, and `UID2`.
- `Q1 2026` still showed healthy revenue growth and active product expansion into AI agents, retail media, and identity-linked activation.
- `Q2 2026` then marked a real slowdown:
  - revenue growth fell to `3%`
  - adjusted EBITDA margin compressed to `34%`
  - management said the quarter did not meet its own standard
  - `Q3 2026` guidance reset lower to at least `$650 million` in revenue

## Why this name matters in the archive

- The Trade Desk adds a control-point layer beneath digital participation and media consumption.
- It does not primarily own the content world, the game world, or the consumer relationship itself.
- It owns a large part of the decisioning, measurement, identity, and media-buying infrastructure that determines how advertiser demand gets routed across the open internet.
- That makes it a strong comparison point for:
  - `AppLovin` as an adjacent demand-routing and optimization layer
  - `Unity` as a creator-and-monetization software layer
  - `Roblox` as a participation platform
  - broader consumer and media names whose growth increasingly depends on measurable, privacy-aware, cross-channel customer acquisition
