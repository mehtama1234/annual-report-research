## What changed

- Revenue: full-year `2025` revenue reached `$1.6566 billion`; quarterly revenue then rose from the Q1 `2026` reported level of `$428.9 million` to `$434.6 million` in Q2 `2026`.
- Margin: full-year `2025` operating income reached `$1.121 billion`, implying very high operating leverage for an infrastructure-control business with limited physical capex.
- Cash generation: full-year `2025` operating cash flow reached `$1.0911 billion`; with only `$22.8 million` of capex, the model remains extremely cash generative.
- Deferred revenue: deferred revenue rose from about `$1.3845 billion` at year-end `2025` to about `$1.445 billion` by `2026-06-30`, reinforcing the recurring prepaid character of the registry base.
- Capital allocation: Q1 and Q2 `2026` continued the pattern of aggressive buybacks plus a quarterly dividend of `$0.81` per share.
- Pricing: the `Q1 2026` release disclosed a `.com` wholesale price increase from `$10.26` to `$10.97` effective `2026-11-01`, showing how regulated or contract-shaped price power still matters inside core internet plumbing.
- Platform expansion: the `Q2 2026` release added the `.web` delegation event, giving VeriSign a fresh optionality point beyond the legacy `.com` and `.net` cash engine.

## Working interpretation

- VeriSign is one of the cleanest names in the archive for showing that internet infrastructure can behave like a regulated toll bridge rather than like a consumer platform or a telecom carrier.
- The company monetizes a narrow but critical control point: naming, resolution, registry operations, and DNS reliability. That makes it useful as the layer between bandwidth-heavy physical infrastructure and consumer-facing web franchises.

## Open questions

- How durable is domain-base growth if more activity shifts into app ecosystems, AI assistants, marketplaces, and other environments where the end user is less directly aware of the open web?
- How much of the long-run economics rest on stable governance, contract renewals, and registrar-channel alignment rather than on direct product innovation?
