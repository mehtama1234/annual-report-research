## Annual report takeaways

- DoorDash is a strong blind-spot fit because `Technology / Internet Service Providers` is a weak description of the operating reality. The annual and trailing-quarter materials show a local-commerce marketplace and delivery-coordination system built on recurring convenience behavior, merchant aggregation, membership, advertising, and physical fulfillment density rather than a classic connectivity or access-provider model.
- The source mismatch is useful on its own. As of `2026-08-10`, AnnualReports.com still only showed a `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, while the local official IR verification and SEC chain already supported the `2025` annual anchor and the in-scope `Q2 2026`, `Q1 2026`, and `Q4 2025` set.
- Full-year `2025` revenue reached `$13.717B`, full-year net income reached `$935M`, operating income reached `$723M`, and cash and cash equivalents at year-end reached `$4.378B`.
- The Q4 `2025` results and shareholder-letter chain make the operating system especially clear: quarterly revenue reached `$3.955B`, orders reached `903M`, Marketplace GOV reached `$29.7B`, DoorDash exited the year with over `56M` monthly active users and over `35M` DashPass, Wolt+, and Deliveroo Plus members, and the company said it generated nearly `$75B` in sales for local merchants across over `40` countries in `2025`.
- Management's language is also the analytical clue. The official overview page described DoorDash as one of the world's leading local-commerce platforms, and the shareholder letter said the company was building infrastructure for local commerce plus new systems inside its Commerce Platform for merchants. That is economically and culturally different from the `Internet Service Providers` label.
- DoorDash therefore belongs in the same blind-spot lane as other coordination and interface businesses like `Airbnb`, `eBay`, and `Amazon`, but with a more physically embedded delivery layer sitting between digital demand and local merchant fulfillment.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` revenue was `$4.454B`, up `36%` year over year, and Marketplace GOV was `$33.1B`, also up `36%`.
- Activity: orders increased `27%` year over year to `970M`.
- Profitability: GAAP net income attributable to common stockholders was `$200M`, while adjusted EBITDA increased `40%` to `$914M`.
- Operating posture: the quarter release said U.S. restaurant Marketplace GOV growth accelerated slightly, supported by strong U.S. DashPass membership, while grocery and retail also delivered strong growth with better unit economics. The quarter also highlighted improved cohort order rates at Wolt and accelerating Deliveroo Marketplace GOV growth excluding the acquisition effect.
- Structural themes: this quarter is useful because it shows DoorDash less as a single-category food-delivery app and more as a scaled convenience and logistics platform widening across restaurants, grocery, retail, international markets, and membership behavior.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` revenue was `$4.036B`, up `33%` year over year, and Marketplace GOV was `$31.6B`, up `37%`.
- Activity: orders increased `27%` year over year to `933M`.
- Profitability: GAAP net income attributable to common stockholders was `$184M`, and adjusted EBITDA increased `28%` to `$754M`.
- Operating posture: management said DashPass-member growth accelerated in the quarter and that growth in international membership programs also accelerated. The release also emphasized ongoing investment in subscriptions, merchant tooling, logistics, and live production traffic ramping across the company's global marketplace brands.
- Structural themes: this quarter shows why DoorDash belongs in the blind-spot lane. The growth engine is not only more orders. It is a thickening relationship system where memberships, local merchant density, consumer habit, logistics tooling, and cross-brand platform integration all matter at once.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: the `Q4 2025` earnings release showed quarterly revenue of `$3.955B`, up `38%`, quarterly Marketplace GOV of `$29.7B`, up `39%`, and quarterly orders of `903M`, up `32%`.
- Full-year anchor: the same release and the `2025` `10-K` closed the year with full-year revenue of `$13.717B` and full-year net income of `$935M`.
- Relationship and scale: DoorDash ended `2025` with over `56M` monthly active users and over `35M` DashPass, Wolt+, and Deliveroo Plus members.
- Management posture: the shareholder letter described DoorDash as the largest local-commerce platform outside China, said it generated nearly `$75B` in merchant sales and over `$20B` in Dasher earnings across over `40` countries during `2025`, and emphasized technology unification and systems-building across DoorDash, Wolt, and Deliveroo.
- Structural themes: this closes the annual story by showing a company whose core asset is no longer just a digital storefront. It is a high-frequency coordination layer between consumers, local merchants, couriers, memberships, and merchant-enablement systems.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: DoorDash strengthens Technology, but the better read is as a local-commerce marketplace and convenience infrastructure platform that source taxonomy flattens into `Internet Service Providers`.
- Consumer: the demand is not just app usage. It is recurring convenience behavior, fill-in trips, meal ordering, grocery and retail add-on behavior, and subscription-shaped local fulfillment habit.
- Cultural: DoorDash is filing-backed proof that low-friction local-commerce ordering has become normalized consumer behavior at scale, not just a temporary delivery spike.
- Societal: the company reflects the wider embedding of app-mediated local commerce into everyday life, plus the growing dependence of many merchants on third-party demand aggregation, logistics infrastructure, and platform visibility.
- Business model: the important move is not only collecting a transaction fee. It is combining memberships, marketplace density, logistics, merchant tooling, advertising, and international brand integration inside one operating system.
- Competitive positioning: DoorDash is most useful next to `Amazon`, `Uber`, `Instacart`, `Airbnb`, and `eBay`, not next to legacy internet-access or generic software issuers.

## Working interpretation

- DoorDash materially strengthens the blind-spot lane because it adds the local-commerce and delivery-infrastructure case where the company sits between digital demand and physical fulfillment rather than merely providing software or connectivity.
- `Airbnb` shows a travel marketplace and trust network. `eBay` shows an enthusiast and recommerce marketplace. DoorDash shows a local-frequency and merchant-logistics marketplace where consumer habit, membership, and operational density matter more than classical sector labels.
- Together they show why the source taxonomy is too blunt. `Application Software`, `Internet Service Providers`, and older retail labels describe where the companies were filed, but not the more useful comparison set:
  - trust and travel coordination through `Airbnb`
  - seller supply and category-led marketplace discovery through `eBay`
  - local commerce, convenience frequency, and delivery infrastructure through `DoorDash`
