## What changed

- Revenue and scale: DoorDash is now visible in the archive as a local-commerce and logistics-coordination platform rather than a generic internet-service name. Full-year `2025` revenue reached `$13.717B`, full-year net income reached `$935M`, and Q2 `2026` quarterly revenue reached `$4.454B`.
- Marketplace intensity: the quarter chain shows Marketplace GOV at `$29.7B` in Q4 `2025`, `$31.6B` in Q1 `2026`, and `$33.1B` in Q2 `2026`, while orders rose from `903M` to `933M` to `970M`.
- Relationship depth: the Q4 `2025` materials said DoorDash exited the year with over `56M` monthly active users and over `35M` DashPass, Wolt+, and Deliveroo Plus members, which is much closer to a utility-and-membership platform than to a plain transaction website.
- Platform widening: the filings and exhibits repeatedly frame the business around local commerce, merchant tools, memberships, advertising, grocery and retail expansion, international execution, and technology unification across DoorDash, Wolt, and Deliveroo.
- Consumer / cultural signals: this is one of the archive's clearest proofs that convenience frequency and app-mediated local fulfillment have become a recurring demand system, not just a pandemic residue.

## Open questions

- How durable are DoorDash's unit-economics gains if grocery and retail mix keep rising inside the same platform?
- Does the platform become meaningfully thicker as membership, advertising, merchant tooling, and local-commerce infrastructure widen together?
- Should DoorDash be compared more directly with `Amazon`, `Uber`, `Instacart`, and `eBay` as different coordination systems rather than with older internet-service labels?
