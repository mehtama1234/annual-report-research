## Annual report takeaways

- Instacart is one of the cleanest blind-spot cases in the repo because the issue is not only bad labeling. As of `2026-08-10`, no discoverable `AnnualReports.com` issuer page was found for `Instacart` or `Maplebear Inc.` through the tested direct company-path patterns.
- The annual and trailing-quarter materials show a business that is much more useful than a generic internet or app label would suggest. Instacart is a grocery-demand aggregation layer, a retailer enablement platform, a retail-media network, and an increasingly embedded in-store and AI technology vendor.
- The full-year `2025` evidence chain closes the year at real scale: GTV reached `$37.224B`, orders reached `338.8M`, total revenue reached `$3.742B`, advertising and other revenue reached `$1.065B`, GAAP net income reached `$447M`, and adjusted EBITDA reached `$1.087B`.
- The year-end shareholder letter also makes the operating system explicit. Instacart said more than `26M` customers trusted the platform during `2025`, about `10M` customers placed at least one order in `December`, the marketplace spanned more than `2,200` retail banners and nearly `100,000` locations, and `Storefront` technology powered more than `380` grocers' ecommerce sites.
- Instacart therefore matters to the broader project because it shows how online grocery, retailer software, first-party data, and advertising monetization can sit inside one coordinated platform. This is the retail-media and grocery-demand blind spot that the normal AnnualReports industry tree does not surface well.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` GTV was `$10.351B`, orders were `90.3M`, total revenue was `$1.043B`, advertising and other revenue was `$297M`, GAAP net income was `$111M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$313M`, operating cash flow was `$493M`, and free cash flow was `$480M`.
- Demand and growth: GTV grew `14%` year over year, orders grew `9%`, total revenue grew `14%`, and advertising and other revenue grew `16%`.
- Operating signals: management said the business had accelerated growth over the prior three quarters, with net-new customer activation at the fastest year-over-year growth rates since `2022`.
- Product and infrastructure posture: the quarter highlighted `Arpalus` acquisition activity in inventory intelligence, more no-markup retailer launches, further `Storefront Pro` traction, expanded in-store technologies, a Google `Gemini` integration, and ad-partner expansion with `Pinterest`.
- Structural themes: Q2 is strong evidence that Instacart belongs in the grocery-demand aggregation and retailer-enablement blind spot. The consumer app matters, but the deeper asset is the embedded platform around retailers, ads, inventory, and AI.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` GTV was `$10.288B`, orders were `91.2M`, total revenue was `$1.019B`, advertising and other revenue was `$286M`, GAAP net income was `$144M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$300M`, operating cash flow was `$268M`, and free cash flow was `$253M`.
- Demand and growth: GTV grew `13%` year over year, orders grew `10%`, total revenue grew `14%`, and advertising and other revenue grew `16%`.
- Milestone signal: management said Q1 was the first quarter in company history to surpass `$10B` in GTV and `$1B` in total revenue.
- Strategic posture: the quarter also highlighted price-parity progress, deeper `ALDI` integration through `Storefront Pro`, the `Instaleap` acquisition for international enterprise expansion, and early retailer adoption of the company's `AI Solutions` suite.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows why Instacart is not just a grocery app. The same company is trying to own demand aggregation, white-label retailer software, and high-margin ads and data monetization at once.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` GTV was `$9.852B`, orders were `89.5M`, total revenue was `$992M`, advertising and other revenue was `$294M`, GAAP net income was `$81M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$303M`, and operating cash flow was `$184M`.
- Full-year anchor: FY`2025` GTV reached `$37.224B`, orders reached `338.8M`, total revenue reached `$3.742B`, advertising and other revenue reached `$1.065B`, GAAP net income reached `$447M`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$1.087B`, and operating cash flow reached `$971M`.
- Relationship and platform depth: the same annual letter said more than `26M` customers used Instacart in `2025`, with about `10M` placing at least one order in `December`, and emphasized more than `310` Carrot Ads partners plus more than `9,000` active brands advertising in Q4.
- Strategic read: the year-end letter described retailers increasingly coming to Instacart first as a technology partner rather than just a demand channel, and it framed `Storefront`, in-store technologies, AI solutions, data products, and off-platform media partnerships as mutually reinforcing.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` is the bridge quarter that makes Instacart analytically important. The company was not only delivering grocery orders. It was functioning as a retailer operating layer and media-enablement layer behind the grocery interface.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- AnnualReports omission as signal: Instacart is one of the strongest blind-spot examples in the archive because the source issue appears to be omission, not merely awkward classification.
- Grocery-demand aggregation: Instacart strengthens a lane the archive still needed. This is not only ecommerce. It is grocery-specific demand routing, basket assembly, substitution logic, parity management, and retailer-customer relationship intermediation.
- Retailer enablement: `Storefront`, `Storefront Pro`, `Caper`, `FoodStorm`, `Carrot Tags`, and AI solutions matter because they turn Instacart from a marketplace intermediary into infrastructure embedded inside retailers' own channels and stores.
- Retail media and data: crossing `$1B` in advertising and other revenue in `2025` is the critical proof that first-party grocery data and retail media are already a scaled second monetization layer.
- Affordability and parity: the repeated emphasis on price parity, no markups, and loyalty integration is a direct filing-backed sign that value perception remains a key gating factor for grocery digitization.
- Competitive positioning: Instacart is most useful next to `Amazon`, `DoorDash`, `Uber`, `Kroger`, `Walmart`, and retail-media platform work, not next to generic software or connectivity names.

## Working interpretation

- Instacart materially strengthens the blind-spot lane because it adds the grocery-specific demand-aggregation and retailer-enablement case that the source taxonomy does not organize cleanly.
- `DoorDash` shows local merchant and delivery coordination. `Uber` shows mobility plus fulfillment coordination. Instacart adds the grocery and retail-media version, where the platform simultaneously mediates demand, powers retailer-owned ecommerce, and monetizes brands through advertising and data.
- Together they show why the AnnualReports tree is too blunt for this research objective. The important comparison set is not legacy software or internet-service labels. It is:
  - local-commerce coordination through `DoorDash`
  - movement-and-fulfillment coordination through `Uber`
  - grocery-demand aggregation, retailer enablement, and retail-media monetization through `Instacart`
