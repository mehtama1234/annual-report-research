## What changed

- AnnualReports blind spot: this is not just a weak classification case. No discoverable AnnualReports issuer page was found for `Instacart` or `Maplebear Inc.` in the tested direct company-path patterns.
- Marketplace plus enterprise plus ads: Instacart is no longer just an online grocery marketplace. The filings and IR materials show a three-layer operating system built on demand aggregation, white-label retailer enablement, and retail-media plus data monetization.
- Scale and frequency: FY`2025` reached `$37.224B` in GTV and `338.8M` orders, then Q1 and Q2 `2026` both stayed above `$10B` in quarterly GTV.
- Retailer enablement depth: the annual and trailing-quarter materials repeatedly emphasize `Storefront`, `Storefront Pro`, `Caper`, `FoodStorm`, `Carrot Ads`, and AI solutions, which makes Instacart useful as a retailer-infrastructure case rather than just a consumer-app case.
- Affordability and parity: management repeatedly framed price parity, loyalty integration, and no-markup expansion as key to online grocery adoption and customer retention.
- Advertising and data relevance: Instacart crossed `$1B` in advertising and other revenue in `2025`, which makes the ads and first-party-data layer economically material, not experimental.

## Open questions

- Is Instacart best understood as a consumer marketplace, a retailer operating system, or a retail-media platform that happens to be anchored in grocery?
- How durable is current GTV and orders growth if grocery demand weakens or retailers push harder for direct-channel control?
- Does Instacart's long-term moat come more from consumer demand aggregation, enterprise embedment inside retailer systems, or first-party advertising and data?
