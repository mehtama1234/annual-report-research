# Fastly, Inc. Company Packet

## Why this company belongs in CLI 5

Fastly fills an important comparison slot inside CLI 5 because it is neither a pure trust-software vendor nor a classic telecom or communications-hardware owner. It is a narrower programmable edge and application-delivery control layer spanning network services, security, compute, and observability. That makes it one of the cleanest tests of whether internet-edge economics accrue to the broad connectivity-cloud model that Cloudflare is pursuing, to Akamai's transition model, or to a more focused programmable-edge platform that still carries meaningful delivery exposure and customer concentration.

## Annual anchor: year ended `2025-12-31`

The annual filing and year-end earnings materials show Fastly improving the quality of its model without escaping its underlying tension. Fiscal `2025` revenue reached roughly `$624.0M`, up `15%`. Gross profit reached about `$356.2M`, or `57.1%` gross margin. Remaining performance obligations reached roughly `$353.8M`. Net cash provided by operating activities reached about `$94.4M`, and free cash flow reached about `$45.8M`. GAAP net loss improved to about `$121.7M`, while full-year non-GAAP net income turned positive at roughly `$19.7M`.

The revenue-mix detail matters. Network services still dominated at roughly `$477.8M`, but security reached about `$125.1M`, up `21%`, and other revenue, including Compute and Observability, reached about `$21.1M`, up `64%`. The annual filing also states that the company derives primary revenue from usage-based fees on the platform, while security revenue is more subscription-like and billed in advance. In other words, Fastly is trying to climb from a more volatile delivery-and-usage base toward a cleaner security and developer-platform mix without abandoning the edge-runtime identity that made the company relevant in the first place.

The strategy language makes the same point in more ambitious terms. Fastly's `2025` annual filing describes the edge cloud as the convergence of CDN capability with functions traditionally delivered by hardware-centric appliances such as `ADC`, `WAF`, `API Management`, `Bot Detection`, `DDoS`, `WAAP`, and infrastructure protection. It also argues that processing at the edge is becoming more important for `agentic AI workloads`, AI traffic management, and safe machine interaction. The practical read is that Fastly wants to be more than a traffic-optimization vendor, but it still has to prove that broader edge relevance can produce durable control-point economics rather than just a better delivery business.

## What this company helps prove

- Sector role: Fastly is a narrower edge-runtime and application-delivery control layer, not a full connectivity cloud and not a pure security-governance platform.
- Consumer signal: users increasingly expect fast, safe, invisible digital performance as a baseline, but the monetization of that expectation does not automatically flow to every delivery layer.
- Cultural and societal signal: the internet is normalizing more machine traffic, more API exposure, more rights-sensitive media distribution, and more pressure to secure automated interactions at low latency.
- Institutional signal: enterprises increasingly want fewer edge vendors to handle performance, API security, bot management, DDoS, compute, and observability together, but they also still price these workloads through usage and traffic patterns that can keep the economics messier than classic subscription software.
- Technology signal: Fastly shows that AI-related traffic and agent interaction can matter at the edge, but the cleaner AI-era toll may still depend on whether the platform owns broad control, narrow security policy, or only a more focused programmable-edge runtime.
- Industrial signal: the company is shifting mix toward higher-value security and compute while still carrying visible delivery exposure, usage volatility, and customer concentration.
- Capital-allocation signal: Fastly is lighter than telecom, towers, and datacenter landlords, but it is not as clean as the best software-control layers because network footprint, bandwidth, and edge execution still matter directly.

## Working interpretation

Fastly improves CLI 5 because it sharpens the distinction between three adjacent internet-edge categories. Akamai represents a legacy delivery network trying to upgrade itself into a stronger security and cloud-infrastructure layer. Cloudflare represents a broader connectivity cloud trying to combine traffic control, Zero Trust, developer runtime, and AI-agent mediation into one platform. Fastly sits between them as a narrower programmable-edge and application-security platform whose results still reveal the delivery-era burden stack more clearly.

That tension is precisely why the company matters. The `2025` annual and `2026` quarter chain show clear quality improvement: gross margins are reaching record levels, non-GAAP profitability has turned positive, security growth is materially faster than network-services growth, and LTM net retention has climbed from `110%` in `Q4 2025` to `117%` in `Q2 2026`. The filings also show that Compute and Observability are growing from a smaller base, which means Fastly is not standing still as a CDN-like platform.

But the same evidence also shows the limits of the model. Fastly still derives primary revenue from usage-based platform fees. Top-ten customers still account for roughly one-third of revenue. Network services still remain the largest line by far. That means Fastly is proving that value does exist in programmable edge control, but not yet proving that it can own the broadest or cleanest profit pool in the category.

The bigger pattern is that enterprises are increasingly paying to secure, optimize, and govern internet-facing workloads before those workloads hit the user. Fastly shows one version of that pattern: a company can monetize that edge layer through performance, protection, and programmable execution. But compared with Cloudflare, the evidence here suggests the cleaner economics may increasingly sit with the platform that broadens from edge services into a fuller control plane, rather than with the narrower vendor still more exposed to traffic mix and concentration. Fastly therefore looks less like the final winner of the edge-control category and more like one of the clearest proof points for how valuable, but also how contested, that layer has become.
