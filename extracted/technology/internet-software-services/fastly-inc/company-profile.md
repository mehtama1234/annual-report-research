## What changed

- Fastly gives CLI 5 a narrower edge-runtime and application-delivery control layer than Cloudflare. It still spans delivery, security, compute, and observability, but the filing chain shows a more concentrated business with more visible revenue-mix and customer-concentration exposure.
- The `2025` annual package shows why Fastly matters despite its smaller scale. Revenue reached `$624.0M`, GAAP gross margin improved to `57.1%`, full-year operating cash flow reached `$94.4M`, and full-year free cash flow reached `$45.8M`. Revenue mix also shifted in a useful way: network services still dominated at `$477.8M`, but security grew to `$125.1M` and other revenue, including Compute and Observability, grew `64%` to `$21.1M`.
- The quarter chain then shows the quality improvement. `Q1 2026` and `Q2 2026` both delivered record revenue and record gross margins, while security revenue growth stayed above `40%` in each quarter and LTM net retention improved from `110%` in `Q4 2025` to `117%` by `Q2 2026`.
- The filings also show the tension. Fastly remains usage-driven, still relies heavily on top-ten customers, and still sits closer to delivery economics than the cleanest software-control names. That tension is what makes it valuable as a comparison case inside CLI 5 rather than as a perfect stand-alone winner.

## Open questions

- Can Fastly keep moving mix toward security, compute, and observability fast enough to avoid being valued mainly as a delivery platform with better gross margins?
- Does Fastly belong closer to Akamai's modernization path or to Cloudflare's newer edge-native connectivity-cloud story?
- How durable is the AI and agent-traffic tailwind if the cleanest monetization keeps accruing to broader control platforms rather than to narrower programmable-edge specialists?
