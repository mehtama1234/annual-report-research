## What changed

- Scale and durability: FY2025 revenue rose to `$2.673B`, with FY2026 quarterly revenue then stepping up from `$788.1M` to `$815.8M` to roughly `$850.5M`. This remains a high-growth infrastructure-software curve rather than a mature-security holding pattern.
- Demand quality: The live IR page showed `Q3 2026` ARR of `$3.525B`, RPO of roughly `$6.459B`, `748` customers with `$1M+` ARR, and `4,003` customers with `$100K+` ARR. That makes the business look less like seat-based software and more like a recurring policy-and-traffic control layer embedded deep in enterprise operations.
- Cash generation: FY2025 operating cash flow reached `$972.5M`. Quarterly free cash flow stayed strong at about `$413.3M` in FY2026 Q1, `$169.1M` in Q2, and `$136.0M` in Q3 even as the company kept expanding through acquisitions and platform build-out.
- Platform assembly: The FY2026 quarter chain added SPLX, Red Canary, SquareX, and the planned Symmetry Systems deal. The pattern is not random M&A. It is an effort to widen from secure access into AI discovery, browser isolation, managed detection context, and identity-plus-data governance.
- Ecosystem leverage: The packet shows dependence on broader cloud and software ecosystems rather than consumer brands. Zscaler highlighted more than `$1B` of cumulative AWS Marketplace sales, FedRAMP expansion outside the U.S., direct browser-extension security in Chrome and Edge, and AI-agent governance tied to modern enterprise workflows.
- Industrial role shift: The company increasingly describes the internet as a machine-governance problem. The important unit is not the human user session alone, but the interaction among users, devices, workloads, applications, APIs, data, and now agents.

## Open questions

- Can Zscaler keep broadening into AI-security governance without becoming too acquisition-led or too diffuse for customers who originally bought secure access?
- Does the company sustain its premium growth profile if AI demand shifts from broad experimentation toward a smaller set of concentrated enterprise winners with heavier cloud-native bundling power?
