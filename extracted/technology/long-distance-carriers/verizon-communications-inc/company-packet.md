## Annual report takeaways

- Verizon is a core communications-and-access packet because it shows the mobile-first side of household and enterprise connectivity more directly than Charter or Comcast while still carrying the scale, regulation, and capital intensity of national telecom infrastructure.
- The `2025` annual package and year-end release show a business trying to turn a legacy carrier into a broader mobility-and-broadband relationship engine. The strategic language is less about old telecom category boundaries and more about convergence, loyalty, value, and healthier customer economics.
- That matters for this frontier because the key question is no longer just who owns a network. It is who owns the recurring access relationship across mobile service, home broadband, fiber, fixed wireless, billing, and retention loops.
- The annual and year-end materials also reinforce the industrial reality under the consumer story. Verizon remains a debt-heavy, infrastructure-intensive operator that has to balance network spending, fiber expansion, Frontier integration, subscriber quality, and device-promotion discipline at the same time.
- For this frontier, Verizon matters because it sharpens the communications lane into a real comparison set: Charter shows cable rebundling, Comcast shows a platform-and-content-heavy access model, AT&T shows fiber-led telecom reinvention, and Verizon shows a wireless-led access utility trying to regain customer momentum while extending deeper into broadband.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$34.3B`; adjusted EBITDA was `$13.7B`; adjusted EBITDA margin was `40.1%`; EPS was `$0.92`; adjusted EPS was `$1.30`; cash flow from operations was `$10.4B`; and free cash flow was `$6.4B`.
- Customer and product signals: Verizon reported `184,000` postpaid phone net additions, more than `550,000` total mobility and broadband net additions in the quarter, and `348,000` broadband net additions made up of `193,000` fixed-wireless and `155,000` fiber adds.
- Operating pressures: total revenue still fell `0.7%` year over year because lower equipment revenue, longer device replacement cycles, and lower subsidies reduced reported top-line support even as service economics improved.
- Interpretation: Q2 shows a cleaner modern carrier model than the old telecom narrative. Verizon is explicitly leaning on lower churn, better unit economics, more disciplined promotions, and convergence between broadband and wireless instead of trying to force growth through subsidy-heavy handset volume.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter is one of the frontier's best proofs that connectivity has become background infrastructure. Customers still need the network every day, but operator economics increasingly depend on retention, bundle quality, and capital discipline rather than on rapid device-upgrade churn.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$34.4B`; net income was `$5.1B`; adjusted EBITDA was `$13.4B`; diluted EPS was `$1.20`; adjusted EPS was `$1.28`; cash flow from operations was `$8.0B`; capital expenditures were `$4.2B`; and free cash flow was `$3.8B`.
- Customer and strategic signals: Verizon reported `55,000` total postpaid phone net additions, its first positive first-quarter total postpaid phone net additions since `2013`, and framed the quarter around healthier customer economics, lower churn, operational efficiency, and reduced promotional friction.
- Broadband and integration signals: mobility and broadband service revenue reached about `$22.9B`, up `1.6%`; Frontier results were included beginning on `January 20, 2026`; and total unsecured debt rose to `$142.5B` from `$131.1B` at the end of Q4 `2025`.
- Interpretation: Q1 matters because it shows the turnaround logic before the stronger Q2 print. Verizon was already telling investors that the route back to growth ran through churn repair, less subsidy-heavy acquisition, and tighter broadband-wireless relationship management rather than through legacy carrier habits.
- Bigger-picture read: the quarter also captures the core tradeoff of this lane. A modern access operator can improve service economics and still see complexity rise because acquisitions, network outages, debt, and capital intensity remain part of the business model.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was `$36.4B`; net income was `$2.4B`; adjusted EBITDA was `$11.9B`; EPS was `$0.55`; adjusted EPS was `$1.09`; full-year `2025` revenue was `$138.2B`; full-year adjusted EBITDA was `$50.0B`; and full-year free cash flow was `$20.1B`.
- Customer metrics: Verizon reported `616,000` postpaid phone net additions, its highest quarterly level since `2019`, plus `372,000` broadband net additions including `319,000` fixed-wireless adds and `67,000` Fios internet net additions.
- Strategic signals: management leaned harder into the turnaround frame, highlighted more than `5.7M` fixed-wireless subscribers, and said the Frontier closing would push combined fixed-wireless and fiber broadband connections above `16.3M`.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY `2025` is the clearest annual handoff point in the packet. Verizon was no longer presenting growth as a pure wireless market-share story; it was recoding the company around one broadband-plus-mobility account relationship with stronger value retention and lower subsidy intensity.
- Bigger-picture read: the quarter also helps connect the access lane to the everyday-device lane. The growth story remains tied to connectivity, but the monetization still depends on the cadence of handset replacement, promotional intensity, and whether customers accept slower upgrade cycles.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Connectivity as infrastructure: Verizon is one of the frontier's strongest examples of communications access behaving like a household utility even when marketed as a consumer brand. The core system is broadband, wireless, fiber, and retention infrastructure wrapped in retail language.
- Customer ownership and subscriptions: the most important commercial pattern is convergence. Mobility, fixed wireless, fiber, loyalty programs, and lower churn all point to one aim: own the recurring customer account and make switching feel less attractive.
- Replacement cycles and durable-device demand: Verizon materially improves the frontier because it links the access lane to the hardware lane. Q2 `2026` makes explicit that longer device replacement cycles and lower subsidies can hold back reported revenue even while service quality improves.
- Legacy model under pressure: the old carrier identity is not gone, but it is strategically secondary. The packet shows a telecom operator trying to become a broader customer-relationship business while older communications labels lose explanatory power.
- Capital allocation and industrial pressure: large unsecured debt, Frontier integration, network spending, and share repurchases show that access operators are still capital-allocation stories as much as customer-growth stories.
- Frontier fit: Verizon materially strengthens the communications-and-access lane because it gives the archive a stronger mobile-first counterweight to Charter and Comcast and a useful wireless-led comparison against AT&T's more fiber-forward reinvention.

## Working interpretation

- Verizon materially improves the frontier because it makes the communications lane less cable-weighted and less dependent on a fiber-heavy telecom read alone.
- The social pattern is simplification under infrastructure dependence. Consumers may not care about telecom taxonomy, but they care about one reliable provider covering mobile service, home broadband, and bill continuity while reducing friction and churn.
- The strongest repeating pattern is convergence with restraint. Verizon is not chasing growth mainly through subsidy warfare; it is trying to improve the customer base, extend broadband ownership, and keep cash generation strong even when device-upgrade activity slows.
- Verizon also sharpens a frontier-wide pressure point already visible in Whirlpool, Xerox, and GoPro: slower replacement cycles are not only a hardware problem. They now feed back into access-operator revenue quality because the carrier no longer gets the same reported boost from frequent subsidized device turnover.
