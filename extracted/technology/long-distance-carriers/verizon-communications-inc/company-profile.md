## What changed

- Taxonomy reality: Verizon is the clearest mobile-first communications comp in this frontier, but the saved AnnualReports page labels it `Telecom Services - Domestic` rather than the older `Long Distance Carriers` wording used elsewhere in the archive.
- Commercial logic: the company is increasingly run around one mobility-and-broadband relationship rather than around legacy carrier product silos.
- Customer ownership: lower churn, postpaid phone recovery, fixed-wireless and fiber adds, and loyalty-value language all point to a bundle-retention model rather than pure line-selling.
- Replacement-cycle pressure: Q2 `2026` explicitly tied revenue softness to lower equipment revenue, longer device replacement cycles, and lower subsidies, which makes Verizon a useful bridge between the access lane and the mature-device lane.
- Capital intensity: the packet shows the same utility-like infrastructure burden as AT&T, but with a distinct mix of fixed wireless, Frontier-enabled fiber scale, share repurchases, and debt paydown.

## Open questions

- Can Verizon keep turning improved churn and disciplined promotions into durable pricing power, or does access remain structurally utility-like despite better execution?
- How much of the recent improvement is cyclical handset and promotional normalization versus a durable shift in customer economics?
- Does the Frontier integration meaningfully strengthen long-run broadband ownership, or mostly add near-term debt and execution complexity?
