## Annual report takeaways

- Take-Two is one of the clearest proofs in the repo that the work is not just about filing collection. The fiscal `2026` annual report and the latest-quarter chain read as a record of recurring participation economics, franchise concentration, mobile habit formation, and cultural demand for repeatable digital worlds rather than a narrow story about one-time game launches.
- The annual materials make the operating logic explicit. This is a company built around labels that own worlds directly: Rockstar, 2K, and Zynga. The portfolio spans blockbuster narrative franchises, annualized sports titles, and mobile live-service loops, but the connecting economic abstraction is recurrent consumer spending. That is the key bridge that makes Take-Two comparable not only to other game publishers, but also to other archive names monetizing repeat participation.
- Full-year `FY2026` shows the scale of the system. Net bookings rose `19%` to `$6.72B`, GAAP net revenue rose `18%` to `$6.66B`, and recurrent consumer spending rose `17%` to represent `78%` of total net bookings. Mobile alone contributed about half of bookings. Those figures matter because they show that Take-Two is not mainly waiting for tentpole launches; it is already monetizing large recurring loops across sports, mobile, and catalog engagement.
- The annual-plus-quarter chain also shows the hierarchy inside the business. NBA 2K and mobile provide the steadier participation engine. Grand Theft Auto provides both catalog monetization and the dominant future launch catalyst. As a result, Take-Two is a hybrid of repeat-spend infrastructure and launch-event concentration. That tension is one of the main reasons the packet matters.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter: Q1 FY2027

- Results: net bookings were `$1.39B`, GAAP net revenue was `$1.53B`, and net loss was `$34.1M`.
- Demand and growth: total net bookings declined `3%`, but recurrent consumer spending accounted for `84%` of net bookings and `84%` of GAAP net revenue. Mobile represented `$762.3M`, or roughly `50%`, of platform bookings.
- Management message: management said fiscal `2027` was off to an excellent start, reiterated the `$8.0B-$8.2B` full-year net bookings outlook, and framed the `November 19, 2026` launch of GTA VI as the major growth inflection for the year.
- Structural themes: this quarter is especially useful because it shows how much of the business is already recurring even while the market narrative is dominated by GTA VI. The quarter also highlights the portfolio balance problem: better-than-expected NBA 2K and GTA catalog performance offset pressure from lapping elevated mobile performance in mature Zynga titles.

### Quarter minus 1: Q4 FY2026 / full-year FY2026

- Results: fourth-quarter net bookings were `$1.58B`, full-year net bookings were a record `$6.72B`, and full-year GAAP net revenue was `$6.66B`. Full-year net loss was `$(298.2)M`.
- Demand and growth: fourth-quarter recurrent consumer spending rose `7%` and represented `82%` of bookings. For the full year, recurrent consumer spending rose `17%` and represented `78%` of bookings. Mobile contributed roughly `$3.3B`, or about half of full-year bookings.
- Management message: management called `FY2026` an exceptional year with record bookings, said NBA 2K delivered record net bookings and recurrent consumer spending, said Zynga had its strongest bookings since the `2022` acquisition, and positioned `FY2027` as a breakout or milestone year.
- Structural themes: year-end `FY2026` is where Take-Two stops looking like a business that merely bridges between launches. It looks more like a multi-loop participation system with three important engines: sports recurrence, mobile habit, and evergreen/catalog franchise monetization. But it also makes clear that GTA VI remains the event around which expectations, spending intensity, and valuation psychology are concentrated.

### Quarter minus 2: Q3 FY2026

- Results: net bookings rose `28%` to `$1.76B`, GAAP net revenue rose to `$1.70B`, and quarterly net loss was `$(92.9)M`.
- Demand and growth: recurrent consumer spending rose `23%` and represented `76%` of net bookings. Mobile bookings were about `$866M`, again around half of the platform mix. Management said the quarter was driven by stronger-than-expected NBA 2K, GTA, and mobile performance.
- Management message: management raised `FY2026` net bookings guidance to `$6.65B-$6.7B`, said NBA 2K26 was on track for the highest annual net bookings and recurrent consumer spending in franchise history, and reinforced the idea that GTA VI would establish a new baseline for the business.
- Structural themes: `Q3 FY2026` matters because it shows the compounding engine before the year-end celebration and before the official GTA VI launch-date framing. The filing story was already visible: repeat-spend loops in sports, mobile, and online catalog engagement were carrying the model forward between tentpole releases.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Consumer trend: Take-Two is a direct read on consumers spending repeatedly inside owned digital worlds rather than only paying once at launch. Sports players, GTA participants, and mobile users all re-enter the system through recurring spend loops.
- Cultural and societal shift: this packet shows how leisure demand keeps moving toward persistent participation, not just content consumption. The valuable object is not just a title. It is an ongoing world, season, community, or competitive loop that users revisit and pay into over time.
- Industrial and operating pressure: launch concentration, mobile user-acquisition efficiency, higher marketing and R&D around tentpole releases, and the challenge of balancing mature Zynga titles against newer mobile launches all remain central frictions. This is important because it shows that digital-world economics are cleaner than physical-goods systems, but not frictionless.
- Bigger-picture pattern: Take-Two suggests that owned game worlds can capture many of the same repeat-participation economics that platforms capture, but with more direct IP control and more event concentration. Recurrent consumer spending is the key concept that lets the archive compare Take-Two with Roblox, Hasbro, and other world or fandom owners on common ground.
- Cross-company pattern: compared with Roblox, Take-Two owns the canon instead of the participation rails. Compared with Hasbro and Mattel, it monetizes repeat fandom with far less physical-goods friction. Compared with Disney, it is narrower in surface area but unusually concentrated in interactive repeat-use worlds where spending is closer to the point of engagement.

## Working interpretation

- Take-Two materially strengthens the lane because it lets the archive compare two different digital participation models that are often blurred together. Roblox owns the participation infrastructure across many user-generated worlds. Take-Two owns specific worlds and monetizes repeat spending inside them.
- The broader cultural read is that valuable leisure systems increasingly look like places users revisit rather than products they finish. GTA, NBA 2K, and Zynga each express that in a different way: prestige world, annualized sports loop, and mobile daily habit.
- The industrial read is that direct world ownership produces cleaner monetization than many physical-IP systems, but still carries heavy concentration around release timing and capitalized development bets. That is precisely why Take-Two is useful in the archive: it sits between pure platform participation and pure franchise merchandising, while exposing the economics of repeat digital fandom very clearly.
