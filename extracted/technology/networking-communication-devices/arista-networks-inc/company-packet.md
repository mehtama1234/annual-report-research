# Arista Networks, Inc. Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Arista Networks, Inc.
- Sector: Technology
- Industry: Networking & Communication Devices
- Ticker: `ANET`
- Fiscal year-end: December
- Target annual report year: `2025`
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: `Q2 2026`
  - quarter 2: `Q1 2026`
  - quarter 3: `Q4 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- Arista's saved annual report PDF and AnnualReports verification note anchor the company cleanly in the `2025` annual window, while the official IR source file preserves the verified results-page chain despite the site's Cloudflare gating for direct shell HTML fetches.
- Full-year `2025` revenue reached `$9.006B`, GAAP net income reached `$3.511B`, and diluted EPS reached `$2.75`, which is an unusually strong profit profile for a networking company in this lane.
- Management framed `2025` as a validation year for `Arista 2.0`, with cumulative shipments reaching `150M` ports. The annual materials kept emphasizing AI networking, CloudVision, observability, campus expansion, and a `client-to-cloud` platform spanning AI centers, data centers, campuses, and WAN environments.
- The annual read is that Arista is not simply participating in networking demand; it is capturing a higher-quality AI-fabric and control-plane layer where network architecture itself becomes a bottleneck.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `Q2 2026` revenue was `$3.036B`; GAAP EPS was `$0.95`; non-GAAP EPS was `$1.02`; this was Arista's first `$3B` quarter; revenue grew `37.7%` year over year; and non-GAAP operating margin was `49.9%`.
- Guidance: the quarter reinforced that AI-networking and broader `client-to-cloud` demand remained strong enough to sustain both growth and very high margins.
- Management message: Arista keeps framing itself as a critical control plane for AI fabrics, large-scale data centers, campus networks, and observability-heavy operations.
- Structural themes: this is the cleanest direct sign in Batch B that AI-era infrastructure economics can concentrate in the network fabric and architecture layer rather than only in compute silicon or enterprise installed-base control.
- Interpretation: `Q2 2026` is the strongest Batch B quarter so far on pure networking economics, because Arista combines hyperscale-linked growth with unusually high margins and no telecom-style access burden.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `Q1 2026` revenue was `$2.709B`; GAAP EPS was `$0.80`; non-GAAP EPS was `$0.87`; and operating cash flow reached `$1.69B`.
- Guidance: the quarter maintained the high-growth networking-fabric story rather than showing any meaningful pause in AI-linked demand.
- Management message: the saved profile and results materials highlight AI-spine demand, liquid-cooled optics, and customer-satisfaction strength.
- Structural themes: Arista's quarter makes clear that AI-network buildout is not just a hyperscaler server and GPU issue; the network fabric and operations layer can become a monetizable control point in its own right.
- Interpretation: `Q1 2026` helps separate Arista from Cisco. Cisco benefits from AI through installed-base breadth, while Arista benefits more directly from the AI-fabric growth engine itself.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `Q4 2025` revenue was `$2.488B`; GAAP EPS was `$0.75`; non-GAAP EPS was `$0.82`; and Arista closed the year with nearly `29%` year-over-year Q4 revenue growth and more than `$1B` of quarterly net income.
- Guidance: the year-end release kept linking growth to AI networking, observability, and the wider `client-to-cloud` architecture story.
- Management message: `Q4 2025` was used to argue that Arista had moved beyond a narrower hyperscale switching story into a broader AI-and-enterprise networking platform narrative.
- Structural themes: the year-end quarter shows why Arista matters in this lane: it monetizes the speed, density, and architecture requirements created by AI workloads more directly than the access network and more purely than a broad enterprise incumbent.
- Interpretation: `Q4 2025` is the baseline proof that AI networking had already become a real profit pool before the even stronger `2026` quarters arrived.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: AI-network fabric, routing, observability, and control-plane infrastructure
- Consumer: indirect, through the AI and cloud systems that support end-user digital participation
- Cultural: enterprises and hyperscalers increasingly treat AI-ready network architectures as baseline infrastructure rather than optional performance upgrades
- Regulatory: less direct consumer regulation than telecom, though still exposed to infrastructure-security and concentration questions
- Labor: deep engineering, optical and switching expertise, and architecture execution matter more than field-network operations
- Technology: AI fabrics, Ethernet switching, routing, CloudVision, observability, and liquid-cooled optics are the main technical levers
- Capital allocation: Arista captures AI infrastructure growth without carrying the leverage and capex burden of telecom access ownership

## Lane role and thematic interpretation

- Lane role: pure-play AI-network fabric and architecture control point
- What makes it distinctive in this lane: Arista is the strongest current Batch B test of whether the highest-quality networking economics sit at the fabric and control-plane layer rather than the telecom access layer or the broader enterprise installed-base layer.
- Main moat: network-architecture relevance, AI-fabric positioning, switching and routing depth, observability software, and unusually strong operating execution
- Main fragility: Arista remains more concentrated in networking-fabric demand than Cisco and is still exposed to hyperscaler and large-customer build cycles
- Bigger-picture read: Arista is the clearest Batch B evidence that AI-era infrastructure profits can sit in the network fabric itself, where speed, topology, and control become bottlenecks rather than generic connectivity inputs
