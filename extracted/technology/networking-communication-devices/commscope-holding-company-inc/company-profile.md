## What changed

- Identity and asset scope: this is no longer a straightforward CommScope packet. On 2026-01-09, the company sold the CCS segment, including the CommScope name and brand, to Amphenol; on 2026-07-01, it sold RUCKUS to Belden; by Q2 2026 the continuing business was essentially Aurora plus corporate costs.
- Revenue base: full-year 2025 net sales were `$1.93B`; the latest three reported quarters then moved from `$514.5M` in Q4 2025 to `$471.8M` in Q1 2026 and `$319.6M` in Q2 2026 as the reporting perimeter narrowed and Aurora faced product-cost pressure.
- Profitability: full-year 2025 GAAP income from continuing operations was `$324.3M`, but that included tax and portfolio effects; quarter profitability moved from a Q4 2025 GAAP loss from continuing operations of `$50.3M` to Q1 2026 income of `$231.7M` and Q2 2026 income of `$26.1M`, while non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA fell from `$64.7M` in Q4 2025 to `$87.3M` in Q1 2026 and then `$35.8M` in Q2 2026.
- Core economics: management repeatedly uses Core adjusted EBITDA to isolate the remaining operating businesses from stranded corporate costs. Core adjusted EBITDA was `$379.4M` for full-year 2025, `$99.1M` in Q4 2025, `$87.3M` in Q1 2026, and `$45.5M` in Q2 2026.
- Cash and capital structure: the CCS sale produced roughly `$10B` of net proceeds, which were used to repay all outstanding debt and redeem preferred equity. Management distributed `$10.00` per share in April 2026, announced another `$5.00` per share for August 2026, and said the company should end 2026 with `$700M` to `$750M` of cash and no outstanding debt.
- Operational issues: Q2 2026 made clear that the remaining Aurora business is exposed to memory-chip pricing and availability, stranded costs from divestitures, and the challenge of converting demand into clean standalone margins.
- Customer and channel pattern: the filings repeatedly warn about dependence on customer capital spending in data, communication, and entertainment equipment, concentration among a limited number of customers, and sales through channel partners. This is a carrier-and-broadband capex read, not a software-subscription read.
- Industrial and geographic pattern: 2025 growth was U.S.-heavy, and the 2025 annual said non-U.S. sales fell to `28.5%` of total from `33.3%` in 2024. That fits a broader pattern where North American infrastructure spending can mask a weaker international or carrier backdrop.
- Cultural and societal signal: this packet is a reminder that bandwidth demand does not automatically create durable value for every network vendor. Once the cleaner brands and more advantaged assets are sold, what remains can be a narrower equipment business still exposed to operator budget cycles, supply constraints, and restructuring drag.
- Management tone: management is now talking like a portfolio-reset and capital-allocation story, emphasizing debt elimination, special distributions, optionality for acquisitions, and reinvestment discipline more than simple top-line growth.

## Open questions

- Can Aurora become a stable standalone broadband-and-access platform, or is it still too exposed to component volatility and operator spending pauses?
- After the asset sales and cash distributions, will Vistance use its balance-sheet flexibility to build a new portfolio, or will it remain a shrinking residual infrastructure platform?
