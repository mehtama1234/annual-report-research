## What changed

- Taxonomy and lane fit: TTM is a clean direct `Printed Circuit Boards` flagship, which gives the frontier a hardware-substrate operator rather than another utility or downstream device brand.
- Demand mix: the `2025` annual report and the `2026` quarters show PCB and advanced interconnect demand getting pulled by AI infrastructure, aerospace and defense, medical, and industrial programs rather than by a generic consumer-electronics rebound.
- Operating structure: TTM reported `2025` net sales of about `$2.9B`, about `18,200` employees, approximately `1,300` customers, and `24` specialized facilities across North America and Asia. This is important because it frames TTM as industrial infrastructure with meaningful scale, not as a niche fab story.
- Segment and capacity logic: TTM now reports three segments: `A&D`, `Commercial`, and `RF&S Components`. The company is positioning itself as a strategic manufacturing bridge between defense systems, data-center networking, and the broader interconnect stack needed for electronics scaling.
- Quarter progression: Q4 `2025` already showed AI and data-center-computing strength. Q1 `2026` then accelerated further on the same themes, and Q2 `2026` moved into record sales, record non-GAAP EPS, higher book-to-bill, and explicit acquisition-driven geographic expansion into Europe.

## Open questions

- How durable is the current AI-driven demand burst for advanced boards and substrates once the first wave of capacity buildout normalizes?
- Does TTM gain structural pricing power from complex manufacturing and defense relevance, or is it still exposed to sharp swings in end-market mix and customer concentration?
- How much of the current margin improvement is product mix and execution, and how much depends on unusually strong infrastructure demand conditions?
