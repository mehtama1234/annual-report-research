## Annual report takeaways

- Forrester is the strongest exact-category `Research Services` flagship for this frontier because it monetizes interpreted judgment rather than only media impressions, connectivity, or hardware. It sells research memberships, consulting, events, and guidance into the same institutional adaptation problem that runs across the frontier.
- AnnualReports classifies the company directly under `Technology / Research Services`, so this packet closes an exact-category hole rather than leaning on an adjacent advisory or information-services proxy.
- The `2025` annual materials are especially useful because they show both sides of the research-services business at once: enduring demand for outside interpretation in an AI-disrupted operating environment, and sharp pressure on legacy contract value, bookings, and goodwill.
- The annual read makes the restructuring pressure plain. `2025` total revenue fell to about `$396.9M` from about `$432.5M`, contract value fell `6%` to about `$292.4M`, and the company recorded total goodwill impairment charges of about `$110.7M`.
- That tension matters for the frontier. It shows that knowledge intermediaries are still essential, but they are not immune to the same trust, workflow, AI, and budget pressures reshaping media, enterprise software, and professional services.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was about `$100.2M`, compared with about `$111.7M` in the prior-year quarter; contract value was about `$283.2M`, down `3%`; GAAP net income was about `$15.3M`; adjusted net income was about `$7.7M`.
- Management tone: management said it delivered revenue, margin, and EPS above consensus, cited accelerated adoption of `Forrester AI`, and maintained `2026` guidance.
- Interpretation: Q2 is the clearest current evidence that research services are trying to become AI workflow infrastructure rather than only periodic analyst content. The product has to live inside client systems and decision cycles, not only inside PDF reading habits.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was about `$85.5M`, compared with about `$89.9M` in the prior-year quarter; contract value was about `$285.3M`, down `3%`; GAAP net loss was about `$21.8M`; adjusted net loss was about `$0.7M`.
- Management tone: management highlighted continued metric stabilization, improved AI capabilities that embed Forrester research into client workflows, and raised the low-to-midpoint range of revenue guidance for `2026`.
- Distortion: the quarter still included a non-cash goodwill impairment charge of about `$10.8M`.
- Interpretation: Q1 shows a business in transition. Clients still want the judgment layer, but the old research-subscription model is under pressure, so Forrester is pushing toward workflow integration, retention, and AI-enabled delivery.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was about `$101.1M`, compared with about `$108.0M` in the prior-year quarter; full-year revenue was about `$396.9M`; contract value was about `$292.4M`, down `6%`; Q4 GAAP net loss was about `$33.9M`; full-year GAAP net loss was about `$119.4M`.
- Management tone: management pointed to momentum in cash flow, client retention, and client count, partly fueled by the launch of `Forrester AI Access`, but admitted that fourth-quarter and full-year results fell short of expectations.
- Strategic shift: management said it would focus on cost efficiency, contract-value growth, and AI integration across the product portfolio.
- Interpretation: Q4 is the strongest historical anchor for the packet because it shows both the vulnerability and the relevance of the research-services layer. Demand for outside interpretation persists, but buyers are forcing research firms to prove workflow value, retention value, and AI relevance under tighter budgets.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Institutional spending behavior: Forrester is a direct read on whether enterprises still pay for external judgment when uncertainty rises. The answer is yes, but only if the product feels operationally useful rather than merely informative.
- Workforce training and outsourced expertise: the packet shows that knowledge work is being unbundled into research, advisory, consulting, and event formats. Institutions are buying help where they cannot internally absorb fast-moving technology and customer-experience change.
- Trust and information distribution: Forrester monetizes trusted interpretation rather than broad distribution. In a world flooded with AI-generated and low-friction information, curated judgment itself becomes a billable product.
- AI operating pressure: AI is both the opportunity and the threat. Forrester is trying to embed its research into client workflows and position itself as a guide to AI adoption, while also defending against the possibility that AI reduces the perceived value of legacy analyst content.
- Industrial pressure: declining contract value, restructuring, strategy-consulting exit, and repeated goodwill impairment all show that the research-services model is under real commercial pressure even when the thematic demand case looks strong.
- Frontier bridge value: Forrester sits between the attention lane and the institutional-interface lane. It does not own audiences like a publisher or network, but it does shape how leaders interpret technology, customer behavior, and market shifts.

## Working interpretation

- Forrester helps the frontier explain that modern institutions do not only buy software, networks, or media. They also buy a layer of translated judgment that helps leaders decide what to do when markets, customers, and technology all move faster than internal consensus can.
- The quarter chain also makes a broader cultural point: information abundance does not eliminate the market for experts. It changes the standard. Research firms now need to deliver trusted interpretation inside working systems, with AI-enabled access and workflow relevance, rather than only sell periodic reports.
- That is why this packet matters beyond one company. It shows a knowledge intermediary under pressure to reinvent itself at the same time that demand for interpretation should, in theory, be rising. The gap between those two facts is one of the most important patterns in this frontier.
- The company is effectively being squeezed from both sides: buyers want help with AI, customer experience, and digital change, but they also expect lower-friction access, clearer ROI, and tighter budgets. That pressure shows up in falling contract value, restructuring, goodwill impairment, and portfolio pruning.
- Forrester therefore makes the frontier's human-capital and institutional-interface lane much more defensible. It turns `Research Services` from a vague label into a concrete business model: the monetization of trusted, workflow-relevant judgment in an environment where ordinary information is abundant but actionable interpretation is scarce.
