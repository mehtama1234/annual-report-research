## Annual report takeaways

- Gartner is a strong human-capital and institutional-interface packet because it sits where executive uncertainty gets turned into recurring spending. The company sells not labor in the narrow staffing sense, but interpreted judgment, institutional confidence, and decision support.
- The `2025` annual report says Gartner delivers actionable, objective business and technology insights that help organizations perform on mission-critical priorities. It serves more than `13,000` enterprises across about `90` countries and territories and organizes the business around `Business and Technology Insights`, `Conferences`, and `Consulting`.
- That framing matters for the frontier because it shows how institutions buy adaptation. Gartner is not just publishing information. It is packaging proprietary interpretation, workflow influence, executive access, event-based community, and consulting help into a recurring enterprise product.
- The annual numbers make the model concrete. Gartner reported about `$6.5B` of total revenue in `2025`, with about `$5.1B` from Insights, about `$644.7M` from Conferences, and about `$552.5M` from Consulting. Year-end contract value was about `$5.2B`, up `1%` on a foreign-currency-neutral basis.
- The annual package also sharpens the operating lens for this frontier. Gartner explicitly tracks contract value, consulting backlog growth, client retention, wallet retention, consulting utilization, and conference attendance and exhibitor levels. The filing says Insights client retention was `85%` in `2025`, up from `84%` in `2024`.
- Gartner is also a good read on AI-era pressure. The annual filing says the company expects more competition from increased adoption of AI services. That makes Gartner a useful test of whether enterprises will still pay a premium for curated, trusted, and applied insight even as generic information becomes cheaper.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was about `$1.7B`; net income was about `$275M`; adjusted EBITDA excluding the divested operation was about `$466M`; free cash flow was about `$378M`.
- Operating signals: contract value reached about `$5.3B`, up `0.3%` sequentially and `1.7%` year over year on an FX-neutral basis. Reported revenue slipped `0.6%`, but adjusted revenue rose `2.8%` as reported and `1.8%` FX neutral.
- Management signal: Gene Hall said contract-value growth accelerated again and that revenues, adjusted EBITDA excluding the divested operation, adjusted EPS, and free cash flow were ahead of expectations.
- Interpretation: Q2 shows the most important lane signal. Institutional demand does not have to look explosive in reported revenue to be healthy. The stronger indicator is contract value, which measures future committed spending for insight and advisory access.
- Bigger-picture read: this is what knowledge-work intermediation looks like when clients are trying to navigate technology shifts, cost pressure, and AI disruption. They are still paying for interpretation, prioritization, and confidence.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was about `$1.5B`; net income was about `$222M`; adjusted EBITDA excluding the divested operation was about `$395M`; free cash flow was about `$371M`.
- Operating signals: contract value reached about `$5.3B`, up `1.0%` year over year on an FX-neutral basis. Reported revenue declined `1.5%`, but adjusted revenue increased `1.6%` as reported.
- Management signal: Gene Hall said contract value accelerated in the quarter and that Insights revenue, adjusted EBITDA excluding the divested operation, adjusted EPS, and free cash flow were ahead of expectations.
- Interpretation: Q1 reinforces that Gartner should be read through forward indicators and enterprise commitment rather than just through quarterly top-line optics. Institutions appear willing to keep spending when the value proposition is tied to prioritization and execution.
- Bigger-picture read: as organizations confront AI and technology uncertainty, outsourced judgment becomes a spending category of its own. Gartner benefits when internal teams need external validation and cross-market pattern recognition.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` revenue was about `$1.8B`; net income was about `$242M`; adjusted EBITDA was about `$436M`; free cash flow was about `$271M`.
- Full-year signals: full-year `2025` revenue reached about `$6.5B`; adjusted EBITDA reached about `$1.6B`; free cash flow reached about `$1.2B`; Gartner repurchased about `$2.0B` of stock during the year.
- Operating signals: contract value was about `$5.2B`, up `1%` year over year on an FX-neutral basis.
- Management signal: Gene Hall said fourth-quarter results were ahead of expectations, highlighted the first investment-grade bond issuance and the pending Digital Markets divestiture, and said contract value was expected to accelerate through `2026`.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY2025 makes Gartner a flagship packet rather than a generic consultant. It shows a large subscription-like institutional insight business with meaningful cash generation, strong capital allocation, and operating metrics that point to sticky client relationships.
- Bigger-picture read: the quarter suggests a broader institutional shift. Enterprises are not only buying software and consultants; they are also buying market interpretation and executive decision infrastructure.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Institutions are paying for interpreted judgment: Gartner is one of the clearest current archive examples of enterprises spending recurring dollars on decision support, not only on execution labor.
- Contract value matters more than the quarter alone: the business should be read through forward commitments, retention, backlog, and utilization. That fits the frontier's focus on institutional behavior under uncertainty.
- Research, events, and consulting reinforce one another: Gartner's mix shows that institutional influence can be monetized across subscription insight, conference-based network effects, and targeted consulting follow-through.
- AI raises both threat and demand: the company openly says AI services increase competition, but the same environment can also increase demand for trusted filtering, prioritization, and executive framing.
- Institutional adaptation is a spend category: Gartner benefits when organizations face faster-moving technology, vendor complexity, and pressure to justify choices to boards, CIOs, and business leaders.

## Working interpretation

- Gartner materially strengthens the third sub-lane because it shows that institutional interface businesses can monetize uncertainty directly. When executives do not trust internal conviction alone, they buy objective framing, peer benchmarks, and advisory structure.
- The most important cultural and organizational shift here is that knowledge abundance does not eliminate the market for experts. It often increases the value of filters, synthesis, and decision accountability.
- The strongest repeating pattern is that modern institutions pay recurring fees to reduce ambiguity. Adtalem shows adaptation through workforce throughput, Accenture shows adaptation through outsourced execution, and Gartner shows adaptation through interpreted insight and executive prioritization.
- For this frontier, Gartner also clarifies that the media and information question is not only consumer-facing. Trust, credibility, and information overload matter inside enterprises too, where they affect budgets, vendor choices, technology roadmaps, and how fast organizations can respond to disruption.
