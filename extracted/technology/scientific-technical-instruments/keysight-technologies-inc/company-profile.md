## What changed

- Revenue scale: fiscal `2025` revenue reached `$5.375B`; quarterly revenue then moved from `$1.42B` in fiscal Q4 `2025` to `$1.60B` in fiscal Q1 `2026` and `$1.72B` in fiscal Q2 `2026`.
- Earnings quality: fiscal `2025` GAAP EPS was `$4.89` and non-GAAP EPS was `$7.16`; quarterly non-GAAP EPS was `$1.91` in Q4 `2025`, `$2.17` in Q1 `2026`, and `$2.87` in Q2 `2026`.
- Cash generation: full-year fiscal `2025` cash flow from operations was `$1.41B` and free cash flow was `$1.28B`; quarterly operating and free cash flow were `$225M` and `$188M` in Q4 `2025`, `$441M` and `$407M` in Q1 `2026`, and `$501M` and `$472M` in Q2 `2026`.
- Segment mix: Communications Solutions Group was the main accelerator, with revenue of `$990M` in Q4 `2025`, `$1.124B` in Q1 `2026`, and `$1.231B` in Q2 `2026`, while Electronic Industrial Solutions Group grew from `$429M` to `$476M` to `$486M`.
- End-market tone: the filings tied the recent strength primarily to commercial communications, high-speed networks supporting AI, and aerospace and defense demand rather than to a generic broad macro rebound.
- Acquisition expansion: the Spirent acquisition broadened the communications-and-network-validation stack by adding wireless network test, assurance, and positioning technology, reinforcing Keysight’s role across the communications, networking, and datacenter ecosystem.
- Guidance path: Keysight guided fiscal Q1 `2026` revenue to `$1.53B` to `$1.55B`, then guided Q2 `2026` revenue to `$1.690B` to `$1.710B`, then guided Q3 `2026` revenue to `$1.730B` to `$1.750B`, showing a steadily strengthening demand setup before the then-upcoming `2026-08-18` Q3 report date.

## Working interpretation

- Keysight is one of the clearest ways to capture the measurement, validation, and test layer behind AI, networking, semiconductor, defense, automotive, and industrial-electronics complexity.
- The bigger lesson is that infrastructure booms do not only create demand for compute, fiber, power, and components. They also create recurring demand for engineers to prove that faster, denser, more failure-sensitive systems actually work before and after deployment.

## Open questions

- How much of the current communications acceleration is a durable test-intensity shift tied to AI and higher-speed networking, versus a cyclical rebound in carrier and infrastructure spending?
- Can Keysight preserve its high-value instrumentation and software positioning as more of the test stack shifts toward virtualized, automated, and software-defined validation environments?
