## Annual report takeaways

- Zscaler is the right next CLI 5 extension because it sharpens the trust-governance side of the lane with an even more access-and-policy-native model than Oracle, and a narrower architecture than the broader endpoint or platform-security stacks. The fiscal `2025` annual anchor plus the FY2026 quarter chain show a company monetizing Zero Trust standardization, AI-security governance, and data-control complexity with strong recurring-revenue quality.
- The annual source chain is straightforward in the SEC but lagged in the archives. On Monday, August 10, 2026, AnnualReports.com and Zscaler's own annual-reports page still pointed to `2024`, so the fiscal `2025` SEC `10-K` for the year ended `2025-07-31` is the controlling annual anchor.
- The strategic point is not just secure access. Zscaler increasingly sells a broader control plane for who and what can connect, what data can move, how AI agents can be governed, and how enterprises can keep distributed workflows compliant without reassembling fragmented legacy point tools.
- That matters for CLI 5 because Zscaler is a particularly clean example of a software-control toll. It monetizes the rising cost of governance failure, policy sprawl, AI-risk exposure, and data-security complexity without carrying the financing-heavy infrastructure burden that Oracle is increasingly accepting or the access-network burden carried by telecom operators.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: FY2026 Q3 revenue reached `$850.5M`, ARR reached `$3.525B`, net new ARR reached `$166M`, ARR excluding Red Canary reached `$3.398B`, non-GAAP income from operations reached `$195.8M`, non-GAAP operating margin reached `23%`, operating cash flow reached `$198.0M`, free cash flow reached `$136.0M`, and deferred revenue reached `$2.477B`.
- Operating read: this is the cleanest proof that Zscaler wants to be read as AI-era trust infrastructure rather than just network security. Management framed the company as the cybersecurity platform for the AI era and tied the quarter to AI-agent security, sovereignty, public-sector expansion, and policy control across distributed enterprise environments.
- Capital and model signal: the important contrast with Oracle is that Zscaler kept software-like operating quality while expanding its control layer. The important contrast with telecom is that the model captures rising dependency on trusted digital workflows without funding the whole physical access layer.
- Structural themes: the quarter shows enterprises paying for governability as AI spreads. Symmetry Systems, Anthropic Project Glasswing, the OpenAI DayBreak partnership, and Project AI-Guardian all point toward a business turning AI adoption itself into demand for access governance, identity governance, and secure policy enforcement.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: FY2026 Q2 revenue reached `$815.8M`, ARR reached `$3.359B`, operating cash flow reached `$204.1M`, free cash flow reached `$169.1M`, non-GAAP income from operations reached `$181.0M`, non-GAAP EPS reached `$1.01`, deferred revenue reached `$2.355B`, and cash plus short-term investments reached `$3.513B`.
- Management message: this quarter is the strongest direct statement that Zscaler sees AI as a security-governance and data-governance wave, not just an adjacent feature opportunity. Management highlighted AI Security, Zero Trust Everywhere, Data Security, AI Protect, Microsoft Entra Agent ID integration, and the `91%` year-over-year surge in enterprise AI usage from the ThreatLabz 2026 AI Threat Report.
- Business-model signal: Q2 shows Zscaler preserving strong cash generation while widening the trust perimeter into browsers, unmanaged devices, and non-human identities. The SquareX acquisition reinforces that the company wants to control the trusted path itself, not just inspect traffic around it.
- Structural themes: the quarter shows that always-on digital life and AI adoption are increasing the value of the policy-enforcement and identity-governance layer beneath enterprise workflows.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: FY2026 Q1 revenue reached `$788.1M`, ARR reached `$3.204B`, deferred revenue reached `$2.351B`, GAAP net loss was `$11.6M`, non-GAAP net income was `$159.5M`, non-GAAP income from operations reached `$171.9M`, operating cash flow reached `$448.3M`, free cash flow reached `$413.3M`, and cash plus short-term investments reached `$3.321B`.
- Management message: Q1 is the clearest early proof that Zscaler wanted to frame Zero Trust as the linchpin for AI security. Management tied the quarter to strong demand for the Zero Trust and AI Security platform, Rule-of-78 performance, and the SPLX acquisition's AI red-teaming and AI-asset-discovery expansion.
- Capital and scope signal: the Red Canary and SPLX acquisitions for an aggregate purchase price of `$692.0M` show the company using cash generation to broaden the platform into MDR and AI-security depth rather than accepting Oracle-style capacity burden.
- Structural themes: the quarter shows the trust-governance side of CLI 5 getting paid when customers want lower complexity, better policy control, and safer AI adoption without adding more legacy infrastructure sprawl.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Zscaler is a security-governance and access-governance platform sitting inside the expanded security-software-services branch of CLI 5.
- Consumer: indirect, through enterprises, governments, and institutions that increasingly need secure, low-friction, always-on digital systems even if end users never directly see the control plane.
- Cultural: AI adoption is normalizing the idea that identity, access, data movement, browser activity, and AI-agent behavior must be governed in one policy fabric rather than through scattered legacy tools.
- Regulatory: the platform directly monetizes sovereignty, data-residency, compliance, and public-sector trust burden as digital systems become more regulated and more agentic.
- Labor: the scaling advantage is that Zscaler monetizes software policy enforcement, identity mapping, and cloud-native control rather than national field operations, datacenter ownership at Oracle's scale, or large service fleets.
- Technology: Zero Trust Exchange, AI Security, Data Security, SquareX browser security, Red Canary MDR, SPLX red teaming, AI-agent identity control, and sovereignty capabilities are the main technical levers.
- Capital allocation: Zscaler is one of the clearest CLI 5 examples of a light-burden control platform where rising AI and security complexity can drive ARR, RPO, and cash generation without dragging the company into a telecom or hyperscale-style asset stack.

## Working interpretation

- Zscaler materially improves CLI 5 because it gives the lane a third strong trust-governance example and a particularly clean one. Compared with CrowdStrike, the model is more network-access and policy-governance oriented and less endpoint-first. Compared with Palo Alto, the model looks narrower, lighter, and more Zero Trust-native rather than broad platform-consolidation-heavy.
- Compared with Oracle, Zscaler is a much cleaner proof that enterprises will pay software-like toll economics for control without the provider needing to own the next wave of capacity itself. Oracle still matters as a control point, but it increasingly captures that control by accepting capex and financing burden. Zscaler captures a similar dependence on mission-critical digital workflows without inheriting the same physical stack.
- Compared with telecom operators, Zscaler shows how the AI and digital-dependency wave can push profit to the policy layer rather than to the traffic owner. Enterprises are paying for trusted access, identity governance, and AI-agent security because the cost of getting those wrong is rising faster than willingness to pay more for raw carriage alone.
- The packet also preserves a useful caution. Zscaler is still integrating acquisitions, still reports GAAP losses, and could overextend into adjacent categories if AI-security expansion becomes more marketing-heavy than durable. The current signal is strong, but part of the next-lane question is whether this cleaner architecture remains as strong when more public- and private-market security bundles compete for the same budget.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Zscaler is strong evidence that AI is not only enriching compute, networking, and endpoint platforms. It is also raising the value of the access-governance and policy-control platforms that make broader AI deployment governable for enterprises and regulated institutions.
