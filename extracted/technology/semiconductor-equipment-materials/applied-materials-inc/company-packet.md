## Annual report takeaways

- Applied Materials is the right next CLI 5 extension because it deepens the physical-chokepoint side of the lane after CrowdStrike and Oracle sharpened the software-control side. The fiscal `2025` annual and the latest quarter chain show a business monetizing materials engineering, process intensity, and advanced packaging complexity before value reaches the network or application layers.
- The source chain is clean. On Monday, August 10, 2026, AnnualReports.com already exposed the `2025 Annual Report and Form 10K`, Applied Materials IR exposed the official annual-report PDF and fiscal calendar, and the SEC chain confirmed the annual-plus-quarter filing sequence.
- The fiscal `2025` annual package already showed infrastructure-scale economics. Record annual revenue reached `$28.37B`, annual GAAP EPS reached `$8.66`, and annual non-GAAP EPS reached `$9.42`.
- The strategic point is not just more wafer-fab equipment. Applied keeps framing AI as a process-complexity and commercialization-speed story. Leading-edge logic, DRAM, HBM, advanced packaging, and the EPIC Center all point to one theme: the company is selling the manufacturing transitions required for AI-era compute performance and energy efficiency.
- That matters for CLI 5 because Applied sits at a different chokepoint than KLA or Lam. KLA is narrower around inspection and process control. Lam is stronger in deposition, etch, and installed-base chambers. Applied is broader, more systems-spanning, and increasingly tied to advanced packaging and co-development ecosystems that shape the commercialization curve itself.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `FY2026 Q2` revenue reached a record `$7.91B`; GAAP gross margin was `49.9%`; non-GAAP gross margin was `50.0%`; GAAP EPS reached a record `$3.51`; and non-GAAP EPS reached a record `$2.86`.
- Operating read: this quarter is the clearest current proof that Applied is benefiting from AI not just as a demand tailwind but as a technology-intensity multiplier. Management said semiconductor-equipment growth should exceed `30%` in calendar `2026`.
- Capital and model signal: Applied generated `$845M` in cash from operations and returned `$765M` to shareholders through `$400M` of repurchases and `$365M` of dividends while still increasing the quarterly dividend by `15%`.
- Structural themes: Q2 turns Applied into a stronger lane signal than a generic semicap name because the company tied growth directly to EPIC Center engagements with TSMC, SK hynix, Micron, Samsung, and research partners, as well as to large-area advanced-packaging expansion through the NEXX acquisition agreement. That is commercialization infrastructure, not just point-tool demand.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `FY2026 Q1` revenue reached `$7.01B`; GAAP gross margin was `49.0%`; non-GAAP gross margin was `49.1%`; GAAP EPS reached `$2.54`; and non-GAAP EPS reached `$2.38`.
- Operating read: management said the quarter was fueled by accelerating investment in AI computing and highlighted record DRAM revenue plus record services and spares revenue from Applied Global Services.
- Capital and model signal: Applied generated `$1.69B` in cash from operations and returned `$702M` to shareholders through `$337M` of repurchases and `$365M` of dividends.
- Structural themes: Q1 is the cleanest early proof that the physical chokepoint here is not only tool shipment volume. Applied is also monetizing recurring services, spares, and installed-base support while customers push harder into AI-related DRAM, HBM, and advanced packaging transitions.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `FY2025 Q4` quarterly revenue reached `$6.80B`; quarterly GAAP EPS reached `$2.38`; quarterly non-GAAP EPS reached `$2.17`; and full-year revenue reached a record `$28.37B`.
- Operating read: management said AI adoption drove substantial investment in advanced semiconductors and wafer-fab equipment, making fiscal `2025` the sixth consecutive year of growth.
- Management message: the release emphasized leadership in leading-edge logic, DRAM, and advanced packaging and argued those are the fastest-growing and highest-value technology inflections in the market.
- Structural themes: Q4 closes the annual anchor by showing that Applied entered fiscal `2026` with a strong AI-led manufacturing narrative already in place. This is useful for CLI 5 because it shows the AI buildout enriching upstream fabrication and packaging bottlenecks before later beneficiaries monetize traffic, software control, or trust layers.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Applied Materials is a broad semiconductor-equipment and commercialization-enablement chokepoint.
- Consumer: indirect, through AI compute, cloud, device, and memory systems that depend on more advanced semiconductors and packaging.
- Cultural: AI is becoming not only a software and datacenter expectation, but also a baseline reason for more energy-efficient chips, HBM scaling, and advanced packaging complexity.
- Regulatory: China exposure, export controls, tariffs, and customer concentration matter more here than they do for software-control names.
- Labor: the moat depends on materials science, process integration, co-development, field support, and customer-engineering depth rather than broad distribution or household relationships.
- Technology: leading-edge logic, DRAM, HBM, advanced packaging, EPIC Center collaboration, Applied Global Services, and commercialization speed are the main technical levers.
- Capital allocation: Applied pairs chokepoint economics with meaningful shareholder return, but the lane interpretation still depends more on technical indispensability than on balance-sheet leverage.

## Lane role and thematic interpretation

- Lane role: broad physical chokepoint and commercialization-speed enabler inside semiconductor equipment.
- What makes it distinctive in this lane: Applied is a stronger systems-level physical chokepoint than a narrow inspection or one-process specialist. It sits closer to the full chain of materials engineering, packaging transition, and customer co-development.
- Main moat: breadth across semiconductor systems, advanced packaging relevance, services and spares scale, and close customer collaboration on commercialization.
- Main fragility: customer concentration, China and export-control exposure, tariff pressure, and semicap-cycle timing remain real.
- Bigger-picture read: Applied strengthens the thesis that some of the best AI-era infrastructure economics sit in upstream technical bottlenecks where complexity, yield, packaging, and commercialization speed are non-negotiable.

## Working interpretation

- Applied Materials materially improves CLI 5 because it makes the physical-chokepoint side more complete. Oracle and CrowdStrike show where software control and trust governance capture value. Applied shows how AI also concentrates economics in the manufacturing transitions needed to make advanced compute hardware real.
- Compared with KLA, Applied is broader and more commercialization-oriented. KLA owns a narrower process-control bottleneck. Applied owns more of the materials-engineering and packaging transition stack that lets new node and AI architectures move from research into volume production.
- Compared with Lam, Applied looks less concentrated in deposition and etch but more system-wide in how it benefits from logic, DRAM, HBM, packaging, and services. That gives the lane a better read on whether the highest-value physical bottleneck is narrow precision or broader commercialization control.
- The packet also preserves a useful warning for CLI 5. This part of the lane remains more exposed to geography, tariffs, export controls, and customer capex timing than the cleanest software-control names. That does not erase the moat, but it does make the physical-chokepoint layer more cyclically and politically exposed.
- For the `2025` through `2026` window, Applied is strong evidence that AI is not only enriching software governance, security trust, and network architecture. It is also pushing more value into the materials and packaging bottlenecks required for the next generation of compute systems.
