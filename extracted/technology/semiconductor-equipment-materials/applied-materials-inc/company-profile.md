## What changed

- Applied Materials is the next clean CLI 5 physical-chokepoint addition because it sits upstream of both software-control platforms and network owners. The annual and quarter chain show that AI demand is translating into process-intensity and materials-engineering budgets before chips ever reach the network or software layers.
- The fiscal `2025` annual package already showed infrastructure-scale economics. Full-year revenue reached a record `$28.37B`, annual GAAP EPS reached a record `$8.66`, and annual non-GAAP EPS reached a record `$9.42`.
- The strategic clue is that Applied is not just selling generic wafer-fab capacity. Management repeatedly points to leading-edge logic, DRAM, HBM, and advanced packaging, and the `Q2 2026` quarter extends that into EPIC Center collaborations with TSMC, SK hynix, Micron, Samsung, and research partners.
- The quarter chain also shows that Applied is not only a cyclical tool vendor. `Q1 2026` delivered record DRAM revenue and record services and spares revenue, while `Q2 2026` said semiconductor-equipment growth should exceed `30%` in calendar `2026`. That creates a stronger installed-base and recurring-support story than a simple capex-cycle read would suggest.
- The packet also preserves a real lane risk signal: China exposure, trade restrictions, tariffs, and customer concentration still matter materially in this part of CLI 5 even when the underlying bottleneck economics are strong.

## Open questions

- Does Applied Materials ultimately look stronger than KLA and Lam as the broadest physical chokepoint in CLI 5, or is it more exposed to cycle and geography because of its wider product span?
- How much of the current AI-driven demand wave is truly structural versus a front-loaded buildout in HBM, advanced packaging, and leading-edge logic?
- Will the EPIC Center and packaging push make Applied more like a long-duration platform enabler than a conventional semicap supplier?
