# Lam Research Corporation Company Packet

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Snapshot

- Company: Lam Research Corporation
- Sector: Technology
- Industry: Semiconductor Equipment & Materials
- Ticker: `LRCX`
- Fiscal year-end: June
- Target annual report year: `2025`
- Target quarter window:
  - quarter 1: `June 2026`
  - quarter 2: `March 2026`
  - quarter 3: `December 2025`

## Annual report takeaways

- AnnualReports still listed the `2024` annual package on `2026-08-10`, so the authoritative annual source set for Lam is the official IR annual-report PDF plus the filed `2025` `10-K`.
- Fiscal `2025` revenue was `$18.436B`, net income was `$5.358B`, diluted EPS was `$4.15`, and cash flow from operating activities was `$6.2B`. That is already an infrastructure-scale profit pool before the even stronger `2026` quarter chain arrives.
- The annual materials frame AI not as a generic demand tailwind but as a semiconductor-process-intensity story. Lam keeps stressing that deposition and etch content rises as NAND moves beyond `200` layers, DRAM verticalizes, and foundry and logic shift toward gate-all-around and more complex packaging architectures.
- Management also argues that wafer fabrication equipment spending should remain structurally large and that Lam can expand its served available market from the low `30%` range of WFE toward the high `30%` range. That is a direct statement that the bottleneck is widening, not just cycling.
- Lam's customer-support business also matters in a way that fits the lane. The annual report says the Customer Support Business Group delivered record revenue in fiscal `2025`, with close to `100,000` chambers in the installed base. That makes Lam a recurring installed-base monetizer as well as a cyclical capital-equipment supplier.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: `June 2026` revenue was `$6.72B`; GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.81`; non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.82`; GAAP gross margin was `51.7%`; and GAAP operating margin was `37.4%`.
- Mix: systems revenue was `$4.250B`; customer support-related revenue and other was `$2.472B`; and China was `26%` of revenue.
- Management message: Lam said the quarter delivered record revenue, operating margin, and EPS as AI-driven demand continued to reshape semiconductor manufacturing.
- Structural themes: the quarter shows why Lam belongs in Batch B. AI demand is not only paying the network or systems-control layers. It is also raising the process complexity that deposition, etch, packaging, and installed-base service providers monetize.
- Interpretation: `June 2026` is the strongest current proof in the lane that the highest-value toll may sit upstream of both Arista and Cisco, in the process equipment and chamber-intensity layer required before the chips and memory exist.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: `March 2026` revenue was `$5.841B`; GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.45`; non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.47`; GAAP gross margin was `49.8%`; and GAAP operating margin was `35.0%`.
- Mix: systems revenue was `$3.731B`; customer support-related revenue and other was `$2.111B`; and China was `34%` of revenue.
- Management message: Lam called `March 2026` a record revenue and EPS quarter and kept emphasizing that AI is expanding Lam's opportunity across NAND, DRAM, foundry and logic, and advanced packaging.
- Structural themes: the March-quarter materials are useful because they show the semicap thesis broadening beyond one device category. The story is no longer only memory recovery or China demand. It is AI pushing harder process requirements across multiple semiconductor architectures.
- Interpretation: `March 2026` is the quarter that makes Lam's batch role hard to dismiss. It suggests the process-enablement layer can compound before the largest `June 2026` print arrives.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: `December 2025` revenue was `$5.345B`; GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.26`; non-GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.27`; GAAP gross margin was `49.6%`; and GAAP operating margin was `33.9%`.
- Mix: systems revenue was `$3.357B`; customer support-related revenue and other was `$1.987B`; and China was `35%` of revenue.
- Management message: Lam framed the start of calendar `2026` around an expanding product-and-services portfolio for smaller, more complex three-dimensional device architectures.
- Structural themes: this quarter shows the earlier stage of the same ramp. China was still a larger share of revenue, but the operational story was already about process intensity, installed-base monetization, and the shift toward more advanced memory and packaging complexity.
- Interpretation: `December 2025` is the baseline proof that Lam entered `2026` with both equipment and customer-support leverage already working in its favor.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: semiconductor process equipment, chamber-intensity, and installed-base support
- Consumer: indirect, through the AI, cloud, handset, storage, and compute devices that depend on higher-end semiconductor manufacturing
- Cultural: AI is no longer only a software or datacenter story; it is now a baseline manufacturing-complexity story inside memory, logic, and packaging
- Regulatory: export controls, tariffs, and China exposure matter more here than they do for the telecom or enterprise-network layers
- Labor: the moat depends on precision process engineering, tool design, field service, and deep customer co-development rather than broad consumer distribution
- Technology: deposition, etch, clean, advanced packaging, HBM, gate-all-around, and high-aspect-ratio memory structures are the main technical levers
- Capital allocation: Lam generates very large cash flows and returns capital, but its economics still depend more on technical chokepoint ownership than on network leverage or customer-relationship bundling

## Lane role and thematic interpretation

- Lane role: semiconductor process-enablement bottleneck and installed-base monetizer
- What makes it distinctive in this lane: Lam is the upstream contrast case that tests whether the best economics in AI-era infrastructure sit even before the network fabric, at the wafer-fabrication and process-complexity layer.
- Main moat: deposition and etch process depth, broad installed base, customer support scale, and relevance to NAND, DRAM, foundry and logic, and advanced packaging transitions
- Main fragility: semiconductor cycle timing, customer concentration, export-control risk, tariff pressure, and the possibility of abrupt shifts in geography or device mix
- Bigger-picture read: Lam is the Batch B evidence that infrastructure bottlenecks do not stop at the network. AI-driven growth is also concentrating value in the physics-heavy tool chain needed to produce more complex semiconductors and memory stacks.
