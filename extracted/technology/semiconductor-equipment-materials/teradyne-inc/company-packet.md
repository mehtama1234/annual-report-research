# Teradyne, Inc. Company Packet

## Why this company belongs in CLI 5

Teradyne adds a different kind of physical chokepoint to CLI 5. KLA, Lam, and Applied Materials already show where value accrues in inspection, process control, deposition, etch, and broader semiconductor commercialization. Teradyne pushes the lane farther downstream into semiconductor test, product test, high-speed interconnect test, and robotics that touch both chip complexity and AI data-center equipment buildout.

## Annual anchor: fiscal year ended `2025-12-31`

The `2025` annual materials show a major shift toward AI-linked semiconductor-test demand. Fiscal year `2025` revenue reached `$3.19B`, GAAP EPS reached `$3.47`, and non-GAAP EPS reached `$3.96`. The annual filing says Semiconductor Test growth was driven by AI applications in networking and vertically integrated producer compute solutions, with AI-related customer demand driving the majority of revenue in the second half of `2025`. It also highlights share gains in high-bandwidth memory and DRAM final test.

The broader takeaway is that AI does not only enrich wafer-fabrication tools. It also enriches the test and validation layer required before advanced compute, memory, and interconnect systems can be deployed. Teradyne also widens the lane because it spans Semiconductor Test, Product Test, and Robotics, which means the company sits partly inside semiconductor complexity and partly inside industrial automation and AI-data-center-equipment testing.

## What this company helps prove

- Sector role: Teradyne is part of the test, validation, and deployment-readiness layer inside the physical AI and semiconductor infrastructure stack.
- Consumer signal: the consumer read is indirect, because end users mostly experience the reliability of devices, networks, and AI systems rather than Teradyne itself, but that reliability increasingly depends on more advanced test coverage.
- Cultural and societal signal: AI becomes socially normal only if complex hardware, memory, interconnect, and system layers can be tested fast enough and thoroughly enough to support industrial-scale deployment.
- Institutional signal: chipmakers, vertically integrated compute players, memory producers, and AI-data-center-equipment builders are all under more pressure to validate increasingly complex systems.
- Technology signal: Teradyne shows that AI-era profit capture also lands in the post-fabrication precision layer, not only in manufacturing tools or software control.
- Industrial signal: this is still a cyclical hardware-exposed business with concentrated customers, but the AI cycle is broadening the strategic importance of test, memory validation, and high-speed interconnect assurance.
- Capital-allocation signal: the company is using AI-linked growth to strengthen earnings power while still carrying exposure to robotics restructuring, customer concentration, and cycle timing.

## Working interpretation

Teradyne strengthens CLI 5 by showing that one of the cleaner physical bottlenecks can sit after fabrication but before deployment. If Applied and Lam help explain how advanced chips get made, Teradyne helps explain how advanced chips, memory, and AI-data-center equipment get validated before customers can trust them at scale.

This also widens the lane beyond a simple semicap bucket. Teradyne’s test and robotics exposure means the company helps bridge semiconductors, data-center equipment, and industrial automation. The recurring pattern is that AI increases not only production complexity but also validation complexity, and customers are willing to pay for whichever layer reduces the risk of expensive system failure.

The main caution is that this remains a cyclical capital-equipment business with concentrated end demand. Teradyne can look structurally stronger during an AI-led buildout than it may look in a flatter capex environment. Even so, it adds a meaningful missing category to CLI 5: the validation and test bottleneck beneath AI infrastructure.
