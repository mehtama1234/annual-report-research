## What the company is

Micron is a memory and storage semiconductor company spanning DRAM, NAND, HBM, server memory, and data-center SSDs. In the current archive it is the clearest missing memory-layer addition to the technology infrastructure set because it ties AI demand not just to compute and networking, but to bandwidth, capacity, and storage architecture.

## Why Micron matters in this archive

- It closes the main open gap in the technology framework by adding a memory-and-storage infrastructure winner to the existing `NVIDIA`, `Arista`, `Dell`, and `Intel` set.
- It helps show that the AI buildout is widening into HBM, LP server DRAM, high-capacity memory modules, and data-center SSDs rather than staying confined to GPUs.
- It gives the archive a stronger off-calendar semiconductor case where the correct annual anchor is fiscal `2025`, not calendar `2025`.

## 2025 annual highlights

- Full-year 2025 revenue: `$37.378B`
- Full-year 2025 gross margin: `$14.873B`, or roughly `40%`
- Revenue increased `49%` year over year from `$25.111B`
- The annual package showed the business shifting sharply toward higher-value AI and data-center memory and storage demand

## Trailing reported quarter highlights

### Q3 2026

- Revenue: `$41.5B`
- Non-GAAP diluted EPS: `$25.11`
- Free cash flow: `$18.3B`
- Data center revenue exceeded `$25B`, data center SSD revenue exceeded `$5B`, and Micron said it had signed `16` strategic customer agreements

### Q2 2026

- Revenue: `$23.9B`
- Non-GAAP diluted EPS: `$12.20`
- Free cash flow: `$6.9B`
- Management highlighted high-volume production of G9 NAND-based PCIe Gen6 data-center SSDs, adoption of `122TB` SSDs, and a sharp sequential rise in data-center NAND demand

### Q1 2026

- Revenue: `$13.6B`
- Non-GAAP diluted EPS: `$4.78`
- Free cash flow: `$3.9B`
- Management said calendar `2026` HBM supply was fully covered by price-and-volume agreements and framed memory as a strategic AI asset rather than a commodity component

## Early read

- Micron materially improves the technology archive because it is the cleanest proof that AI infrastructure economics extend into memory bandwidth, storage density, and supply assurance.
- The quarter sequence from `$13.6B` to `$23.9B` to `$41.5B` is extreme even relative to the rest of the technology set and supports the view that memory and storage are becoming direct bottlenecks in the AI stack.
- The `Q3 2026` strategic-customer-agreement discussion is especially important because it suggests Micron is trying to turn a historically cyclical memory business into a more contracted, visibility-driven infrastructure supplier.
