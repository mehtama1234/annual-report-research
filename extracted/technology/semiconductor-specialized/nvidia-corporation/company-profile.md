## What changed

- Revenue: the annual package shows FY2026 revenue at `$215.9 billion`, up `65%`; the trailing quarter set then moved from `$57.0 billion` in FY2026 Q3 to `$68.1 billion` in FY2026 Q4 and `$81.6 billion` in FY2027 Q1.
- Margin: GAAP gross margin ran at `73.4%` in FY2026 Q3, `75.0%` in FY2026 Q4, and `74.9%` in FY2027 Q1; operating income rose from `$36.0 billion` to `$44.3 billion` to `$53.5 billion`.
- Guidance: management’s framing is not a conventional demand guide so much as an infrastructure thesis, with AI-factory buildouts, sovereign demand, and inference demand all emphasized.
- Capital allocation: FY2027 Q1 included a record `$20.2 billion` of share repurchases in the quarter and a new `$80 billion` repurchase authorization; the dividend was raised from `$0.01` to `$0.25` per share.
- Segment mix: Data Center is the economic center of gravity by a wide margin, reaching `$193.7 billion` in FY2026 and `$75.2 billion` in FY2027 Q1 alone.
- Operational issues: growth still depends on supply-chain execution, customer concentration at hyperscale scale, export-policy uncertainty around China, and the economics of very large AI-factory deployments.
- Consumer / cultural signals: NVIDIA remains culturally tied to gaming and developer ecosystems, but the current business engine is clearly enterprise and sovereign AI infrastructure rather than consumer graphics alone.
- Management tone: management is highly assertive and increasingly industrial in tone, describing AI as national and enterprise infrastructure rather than a normal semiconductor cycle.

## Open questions

- How much of current demand is durable end-demand versus front-loaded capacity reservation across hyperscalers, sovereign projects, and model builders?
- Can margins stay at current levels as Blackwell-class systems, networking, cooling, and factory-scale deployments become a larger share of the mix?
