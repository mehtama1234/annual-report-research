## Annual report takeaways

- Sunrun is one of the strongest physical-infrastructure and energy-transition additions for this frontier because it adds the residential side of the solar-and-storage system that `First Solar` does not cover. It is not a utility-scale module manufacturer; it is a household-energy platform built around batteries, subscriptions, financing, installation, and ongoing service rights.
- The `2025` annual package and year-end materials make clear that management wants the company read less like a rooftop contractor and more like a residential distributed power plant operator. The language keeps returning to storage-first offerings, home-to-grid power plants, grid reliability, and the idea that the company is helping meet a national power-capacity shortfall.
- That matters for this frontier because it turns the solar lane into two distinct operating systems. `First Solar` shows utility-scale industrial execution and manufacturing scale. Sunrun shows household energy ownership, recurring customer economics, financing complexity, and the monetization of distributed storage at the grid edge.
- The annual and year-end materials also sharpen a more consumer-facing pressure set than the one visible at utility-scale operators. Sunrun still has to manage tax equity, non-recourse debt, safe harbor timing, affiliate and direct-sales mix, customer experience, and the credit quality of the households it serves.
- For this frontier, Sunrun matters because it ties the physical-infrastructure lane back to the consumer interface. The household energy system becomes another recurring relationship business, not just a hardware shipment category.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` total revenue was about `$870.0M`; Aggregate Subscriber Value was about `$1.175B`; Aggregate Contracted Subscriber Value was about `$1.089B`; Cash Generation was `$23M`, or about `$45M` excluding net safe harbor investments; and Contracted Net Earning Assets were about `$3.677B`.
- Customer and product signals: Sunrun reported a record storage attachment rate of `74%`, about `20,979` customer additions, about `19,793` subscriber additions, about `4.647 GWh` of networked storage capacity, and about `1.206M` customers at quarter end.
- Operating pressures: management reduced full-year `2026` Aggregate Subscriber Value guidance to `$4.6B` to `$4.9B` from `$4.8B` to `$5.2B`, and lowered full-year cash-generation guidance to `$200M` to `$375M` from `$250M` to `$450M`, citing reduced affiliate-channel volumes, a delayed direct-sales ramp, and modestly higher costs.
- Interpretation: Q2 is useful because it shows the exact tension inside this lane. Household-energy infrastructure can still grow fast and attach more batteries, but that does not make the economics frictionless. Customer-acquisition channels, financing timing, and safe harbor decisions still matter enough to move the full-year guide.
- Bigger-picture read: this quarter is one of the frontier's best proofs that energy transition and consumer interface are overlapping systems. The same company is selling peace-of-mind energy ownership to households and trying to monetize a growing distributed grid asset at the same time.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` total revenue was `$722.2M`; Aggregate Subscriber Value was `$1.082B`; Contracted Net Value Creation was `$108M`; Cash Generation was `-$59M`, or about `-$31M` excluding safe harbor investments; and Contracted Net Earning Assets were about `$3.701B`.
- Customer and strategic signals: Sunrun reported a record storage attachment rate of `73%`, about `18,948` customer additions, about `17,665` subscriber additions, and about `1.185M` customers at quarter end.
- Financing and capital signals: management said project-finance timing shifted from Q1 into Q2 and that the company paid down `$92M` of recourse debt with excess cash.
- Interpretation: Q1 matters because it shows how heavily the model depends on funding cadence and capital structure, not only on installation demand. The business can still create subscriber value while reported cash generation temporarily dips.
- Bigger-picture read: the strongest social and commercial signal here is that Sunrun is trying to turn residential solar and storage into a subscription-like infrastructure relationship. The company keeps emphasizing customer lifetime value, recurring service relevance, and the strategic value of dispatchable household capacity rather than just panel sales.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` total revenue was `$1.159B`; full-year `2025` total revenue was `$2.957B`; Q4 Contracted Net Value Creation was `$176M`; full-year cash generation was `$377M`; and Contracted Net Earning Assets were `$3.571B`, or `$15.28` per share, including about `$1.2B` in total cash at year end.
- Customer metrics: Sunrun reported Aggregate Subscriber Value of about `$1.278B` in Q4, a storage attachment rate of `71%`, about `27,773` customer additions, about `25,475` subscriber additions, about `4.033 GWh` of networked storage capacity, and about `1.166M` customers at year end.
- Strategic signals: management said Sunrun was delivering storage-first offerings, protecting families from rising utility costs and an increasingly unreliable grid, and building a distributed power plant that it believed was critical to meeting the nation's urgent demand for more power.
- Interpretation: Q4/FY `2025` is the clean handoff point for the packet because it shows Sunrun crossing from "interesting thematic solar company" into a more defensible frontier packet with explicit household-energy, grid-services, and cash-generation claims.
- Bigger-picture read: the quarter also helps connect the physical-infrastructure lane to the communications and everyday-interface lanes. A battery-backed home energy relationship starts to look like another utility-like subscription surface with switching friction, financing complexity, and account ownership dynamics.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Energy transition as household interface: Sunrun materially improves this frontier because it shows that electrification is not only a factory, module, or regulated-utility story. It is also a household recurring-relationship story built around financing, service, batteries, and perceived reliability.
- Distributed power plants and grid-edge monetization: management is explicitly trying to monetize the installed base not only through customer contracts but through grid services, distributed power plant programs, and newer grid-edge or data-center-adjacent applications.
- Storage-first demand: rising storage attachment rates from `71%` in Q4 `2025` to `74%` in Q2 `2026` show that batteries are becoming central to the value proposition rather than an optional add-on.
- Subscription and customer ownership: Sunrun's subscriber-value framework, service relationship retention, and cross-sell rights make it a useful bridge between the energy lane and the broader frontier theme of owning the recurring customer account.
- Financing and industrial pressure: safe harbor timing, tax equity, affiliate volumes, direct-sales ramp, and recourse-debt reduction show that this business still depends heavily on capital-market and channel execution, not only on customer appetite.
- Frontier fit: Sunrun materially strengthens the physical-infrastructure and energy-transition lane because it gives the archive a residential solar-and-storage flagship to complement `First Solar`'s utility-scale industrial model.

## Working interpretation

- Sunrun materially improves the frontier because it turns the solar lane from a pure manufacturing and utility-scale buildout story into a household-energy-interface story with real recurring economics.
- The social pattern is reliability under volatility. Customers are not only buying lower electric bills; they are buying backup power, predictable energy exposure, and more control over an increasingly stressed grid.
- The strongest repeating pattern is installed-base monetization. Sunrun is trying to make its existing customer network more valuable through batteries, distributed power plant participation, grid services, and future cross-sell or upsell opportunities rather than through one-time hardware revenue alone.
- Sunrun also sharpens a frontier-wide theme already visible in `Apple`, `Roku`, `T-Mobile`, and `Verizon`: the company that owns the ongoing customer relationship can keep adding monetization layers around the same installed base. In Sunrun's case, that installed base is household energy hardware and dispatchable storage.
