## What changed

- Taxonomy reality: Sunrun gives this frontier a residential solar-and-storage packet even though AnnualReports does not classify it under a clean `Solar` bucket.
- Commercial logic: the company is no longer best read as a simple rooftop installer. It is explicitly selling storage-first energy offerings, financing structures, recurring service relationships, and distributed power plant capacity.
- Customer ownership: Sunrun's subscription model, customer-service relationship, and cross-sell or grid-services rights make the company a household-energy-interface operator as much as a hardware seller.
- Grid-edge monetization: by Q2 `2026`, management was explicitly tying future cash generation not only to customer growth but also to distributed power plant programs, grid-edge applications, and even emerging data-center-adjacent power needs.
- Operating pressure: the quarter chain also shows that this model remains sensitive to safe harbor timing, channel mix, affiliate volume, financing execution, and industry dislocation rather than only to panel demand.

## Open questions

- Can Sunrun keep expanding storage attachment and distributed-power-plant monetization fast enough to offset a lower 2026 cash-generation guide?
- How much of the economics ultimately belongs to the subscription relationship versus financing timing and tax-equity execution?
- Does residential solar and storage become a true infrastructure-like household utility category, or does it remain too policy- and channel-sensitive to compound cleanly?
