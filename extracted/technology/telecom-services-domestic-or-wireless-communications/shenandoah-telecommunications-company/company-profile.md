## What changed

- Growth mix kept shifting toward fiber: total revenue rose to `$357.9M` in FY2025, then to `$92.2M` in Q1 2026 and `$93.5M` in Q2 2026, with Glo Fiber expansion markets remaining the core growth driver.
- Regional fiber scale kept building: the 2025 annual said Glo Fiber passed about `427,000` homes and businesses, Q1 2026 highlighted roughly `6,000` Glo Fiber net additions, and Q2 2026 highlighted a record `6,200` net additions and the `100,000th` Glo Fiber customer milestone.
- Capital intensity remained high but started easing: capex totaled `$358.9M` in FY2025, then `$75.8M` in Q1 2026 versus `$83.2M` in the prior-year quarter as management pushed toward the stated goal of positive free cash flow in `2027`.
- Financing structure shifted toward fiber-backed funding: FY2025 included an inaugural `$567.4M` secured fiber-network revenue term-note deal, while debt rose from `$642.4M` at FY2025 to about `$707.4M` by Q1 2026 and roughly `$715.0M` noncurrent by Q2 2026.
- Incumbent decline and carrier churn still mattered: the annual filing tied a meaningful revenue decline to T-Mobile backhaul churn from former Sprint-network decommissioning, while the quarter chain continued to show incumbent video and DSL erosion and softer-demand pricing pressure in some lower-demographic markets.

## Open questions

- Can Shentel complete the Glo Fiber build and convert passings into enough durable penetration and ARPU stability to reach positive free cash flow on schedule in `2027`?
- Once the expansion build moderates, does the regional fiber-and-commercial-fiber model produce cleaner recurring economics than the national cable and wireless peers, or does local competition keep margins structurally constrained?
