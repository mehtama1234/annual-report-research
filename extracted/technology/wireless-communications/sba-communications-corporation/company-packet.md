## Annual report takeaways

- SBA is the right follow-on CLI 5 tower name because it sharpens the physical-landlord side of the lane without drifting into cable, telecom retail, or pure data-center economics. It is more tower-pure than American Tower and therefore a cleaner test of how much AI, mobile-data growth, and spectrum densification can support landlord-style rent collection.
- The `2025` annual anchor is not clean at the AnnualReports layer. On Monday, August 10, 2026, AnnualReports still showed only `2024`, so the packet has to rely on company IR and SEC as the controlling authorities.
- The business model remains overwhelmingly site-leasing driven. The `2025` annual stated that site leasing generated `97.4%` or more of total segment operating profit in each of the last three fiscal years, which makes SBA an unusually direct read on communications-site rent economics rather than a mixed-service model.
- Management's annual framing tied demand to wireless data traffic growth, 5G and new spectrum deployment, network-quality competition, and increasingly generative-AI-era traffic intensity. That matters because SBA is not selling AI software or semicap tools, but it is still trying to collect rent from the physical footprint those systems require.
- The annual also showed where the burden sits. SBA ended `2025` with `46,328` communication sites, heavy leverage, meaningful customer concentration, and land-control discipline as a core operating variable. Approximately `71%` of tower structures sat on land owned or controlled for more than twenty years, average remaining life on that controlled land was `35` years, and `14.5%` of tower structures were on ground interests maturing within the next ten years.
- The most useful annual strategic detail is that management is not treating towers as a frozen category. The `2025` annual discussed edge data centers, fiber aggregation and regeneration huts, satellite ground stations, and private networks, while also disclosing a seven-year exclusivity right to build up to `2,500` build-to-suit sites in Central America as part of the Millicom transaction. The bigger pattern is that even a tower landlord is being pulled toward a broader digital-infrastructure adjacency set.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` site leasing revenue was `$663.9M`, site development revenue was `$51.4M`, net income attributable to SBA was `$198.8M`, diluted EPS was `$1.87`, tower cash flow was `$524.9M`, Adjusted EBITDA was `$483.8M`, and AFFO was `$324.4M`, or `$3.05` per share.
- Operating read: the latest quarter shows a company still monetizing steady wireless-network densification rather than a single-step AI spike. Management described "another solid quarter" and emphasized continued portfolio investment, dividend growth, and shareholder returns while keeping an investment-grade balance-sheet posture.
- Capital and model signal: SBA acquired `6` communication sites for `$10.5M`, built `109` towers in the quarter, and ended June 30, 2026 with `46,390` sites. This is the most tower-pure version of the landlord toll now in CLI 5: modest growth, persistent build activity, and a financing-heavy structure that has to keep converting network dependence into steady AFFO.
- Structural themes: the latest quarter reinforces that physical-site rent can still capture upside from data growth and coverage expansion, but the economics are much more constrained by leverage, refinancing, and customer health than the lighter software-control names.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` site leasing revenue was `$656.1M`, site development revenue was `$47.3M`, net income attributable to SBA was `$184.8M`, diluted EPS was `$1.74`, tower cash flow was `$519.0M`, Adjusted EBITDA was `$475.4M`, and AFFO was `$321.7M`, or `$3.03` per share.
- Management message: Q1 is the cleanest quarter for the current operating story. Management said carrier activity remained steady globally, customers were expanding and densifying their networks while upgrading sites with new spectrum bands and technologies, and domestic leasing backlogs increased during the quarter.
- Business-model signal: the quarter also showed the model's vulnerability and resilience at once. SBA fully removed EchoStar revenue at the beginning of the year, yet still described solid results, a strong balance sheet, and increasing build-to-suit production under the Central America Millicom agreement.
- Structural themes: Q1 shows that tower landlords are less exposed to household churn than telecom operators, but still highly exposed to carrier capex rhythm, consolidation fallout, and the quality of a few large customer relationships.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` site leasing revenue was `$666.2M`, site development revenue was `$53.4M`, net income was `$370.4M`, diluted EPS was `$3.47`, tower cash flow was `$532.2M`, Adjusted EBITDA was `$486.0M`, and AFFO was `$340.4M`, or `$3.19` per share.
- Full-year `2025` read: the year closed with management highlighting industry-leading AFFO per share, dividend growth, continued network densification by carrier customers, and a `2026` outlook that fully removed all contracted EchoStar revenue.
- Management message: Q4 `2025` is where management most explicitly linked demand to new spectrum deployments, coverage obligations, and elevated activity from major U.S. customers. The signal is that coverage quality and capacity density are still forcing spend even after the first 5G buildout wave.
- Structural themes: Q4 `2025` strengthens the case that the tower layer remains a durable toll collector, but one whose upside depends more on recurring network upgrades and spectrum complexity than on software-like adoption curves.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: SBA sits inside CLI 5 as a communications-site landlord rather than as a telecom operator, component supplier, or software-control platform.
- Consumer: indirect, through always-on mobile usage, video and data consumption, app dependence, and the expectation that connectivity works everywhere without users thinking about the physical site footprint.
- Cultural: always-on participation and mobile-first digital behavior keep normalizing denser network footprints. The consumer does not pay SBA directly, but SBA benefits when constant connectivity becomes a baseline social expectation.
- Regulatory: local siting, zoning, land control, international market exposure, and spectrum-driven customer deployment obligations remain central realities.
- Labor: this is less field-service-intensive than retail telecom access but still a real-asset operating business where build pace, maintenance, and site development matter.
- Technology: the model rests on multitenant tower capacity, colocation economics, land control, and adjacent communications-site uses such as fiber huts, edge facilities, satellite-ground infrastructure, and private-network support.
- Capital allocation: SBA is one of the clearest examples in CLI 5 of a balance-sheet-heavy rent model trying to hold a middle ground between portfolio growth, share repurchases, dividends, and leverage discipline.

## Working interpretation

- SBA materially strengthens CLI 5 because it isolates a purer version of tower-rent economics than American Tower. Where American Tower increasingly blends towers with CoreSite and broader digital-infrastructure language, SBA is still more directly a bet on carrier densification, tower tenancy, and land-control quality.
- Compared with American Tower, SBA looks more exposed to the narrow logic of the wireless-site landlord: stronger purity, fewer adjacency cushions, and a more direct read on whether mobile data growth, spectrum upgrades, and coverage competition are enough to support durable AFFO growth.
- Compared with software-control names, SBA is almost the opposite burden stack. The software-control companies monetize trust, workflow, and policy with lighter asset burden; SBA monetizes unavoidable physical occupancy while carrying leverage, refinancing exposure, customer concentration, and site-control complexity.
- Compared with Corning, Amphenol, Arista, or semicap names, SBA is not upstream technical precision. It is downstream occupancy rent after the equipment and network architecture have already been chosen. That makes it a useful reminder that some AI and communications economics still settle in long-lived real estate rather than only in code or components.
- The packet also preserves the main caution. SBA's economics are durable, but not clean. The company still has to manage customer concentration, land-control duration, debt cost, site-development execution, and the aftereffects of customer churn events such as EchoStar. The tower toll can be attractive, but it remains far more balance-sheet-heavy and less structurally scalable than the strongest software-control layers in CLI 5.
