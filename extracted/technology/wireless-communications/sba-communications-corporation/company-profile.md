## What changed

- SBA adds a second landlord-style communications-infrastructure case to CLI 5, but with a more tower-pure profile than American Tower.
- The archive status is lagged on AnnualReports, which makes SBA a good example of the repo rule that company IR and SEC have to control when the archive taxonomy source falls behind.
- The annual showed the site base reaching `46,328` at year-end `2025`, with site leasing contributing nearly all segment operating profit and land-control duration remaining a core strategic variable.
- The quarter chain stayed operationally steady rather than explosive: site leasing revenue moved from `$666.2M` in Q4 `2025` to `$656.1M` in Q1 `2026` to `$663.9M` in Q2 `2026`, while AFFO moved from `$340.4M` to `$321.7M` to `$324.4M`.
- The core operating message remained consistent. Carrier customers continued densifying and expanding networks, adding new spectrum bands and technologies, and keeping demand for tower space intact even after the first 5G deployment cycle.
- The burden signal remains central. Net Debt to Annualized Adjusted EBITDA was `6.4x` in Q4 `2025`, `6.6x` in Q1 `2026`, and `6.4x` in Q2 `2026`, reinforcing that this is a financing-heavy toll with meaningful customer and asset-duration dependencies.

## Open questions

- Does SBA deserve to be read as the cleaner pure-play tower-rent comp inside CLI 5, or does American Tower's broader adjacency into data centers make its toll more durable even if less pure?
- How much durable upside remains in tower landlords once the easy first-wave 5G buildout is behind the industry and future growth depends more on densification, spectrum layering, and selected adjacency uses?
- Can SBA keep growing dividends, repurchasing stock, and funding build-to-suit activity without meaningfully raising the risk embedded in its leverage-heavy structure?
