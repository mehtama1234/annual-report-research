# American Tower Versus Cogent Versus Viasat Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo sharpens the hidden-connectivity and access-layer blind spot by adding the missing terrestrial route-density branch.

It focuses on the most useful three-way split now available in the archive:

- `American Tower`
- `Cogent`
- `Viasat`

The useful question is not whether all three are communications infrastructure names.

The useful question is which hidden-connectivity ownership structure each one most clearly represents:

- wireless-site access ownership
- terrestrial route-density and on-net private-connectivity ownership
- orbital broadband mobility and sovereign-access ownership

## Executive read

All three companies monetize ambient connectivity dependence before it becomes visible app or cloud revenue, but they do it through different ownership bottlenecks.

- `American Tower` is the clearest wireless-site and amendment-ready access ownership case
- `Cogent` is the clearest terrestrial route-density and on-net private-connectivity ownership case
- `Viasat` is the clearest orbital broadband mobility and sovereign-access case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `American Tower`: global tower ownership and datacenter-bridged access infrastructure
2. `Cogent`: route-density and on-net private-connectivity infrastructure
3. `Viasat`: satellite-broadband mobility and sovereign-access infrastructure

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by which physical hidden-connectivity layer each company most clearly owns, moving from scarce terrestrial sites to route-based terrestrial carriage and then to orbit-heavy mobility and sovereign-access capacity.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `American Tower` is the clearest access-site ownership case

`American Tower` is strongest when the question is who owns scarce wireless locations before connectivity becomes carrier revenue, cloud demand, or app usage.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows:

- `149,686` communications sites globally at year-end `2025`
- `30` operating data-center facilities across `11` U.S. markets at year-end `2025`
- full-year `2025` revenue of `$10.645B`
- `Q2 2026` property revenue of `$2.688B`

That keeps `American Tower` as the cleanest proof that one of the earliest monetization choke points is still the physical site itself.

### 2. `Cogent` is the clearest terrestrial route-density counterpoint

`Cogent` matters because it opens the missing branch between tower rent and orbital coverage.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows:

- full-year `2025` service revenue of `$975.8M`
- operations in `57` countries
- two network layers explicitly described in the filings: an IP Network and an Optical Wave Network
- on-net speeds ranging from `100` Mbps to `400` Gbps
- `Q2 2026` on-net revenue including wavelengths of `$150.2M`
- `Q2 2026` IP traffic growth of `16%` year over year

That makes `Cogent` the live public-market proof that hidden connectivity is also monetized through route placement, on-net building access, wavelength, and private-network economics rather than only through towers or satellites.

### 3. `Viasat` is the clearest orbit-heavy mobility and sovereign-access case

`Viasat` differs from both because its bottleneck is not a tower site and not a terrestrial route base.

Its economic choke point is orbit-heavy, spectrum-heavy, terminal-linked connectivity where terrestrial infrastructure is incomplete, capacity constrained, or strategically insufficient.

That keeps the right side of the comparison anchored in mobility, sovereign, and remote-bandwidth economics rather than in terrestrial site or route ownership.

## The most useful boundary split

### `American Tower`: access-site ownership first

`American Tower` matters most when the question is who owns the scarce places where wireless coverage and carrier equipment can physically exist.

The distinguishing asset is:

- tower-site breadth
- carrier amendments and colocations
- land control
- a partial datacenter bridge

### `Cogent`: route-density and on-net carriage first

`Cogent` matters most when the question is who owns enough route, building, and optical-network placement to monetize private connectivity and data movement across terrestrial networks.

The distinguishing asset is:

- on-net building economics
- IP and optical-network routes
- wavelength and private-network services
- route-density dependence rather than tower-site rent

### `Viasat`: orbital mobility and sovereign access first

`Viasat` matters most when the question is who provides high-bandwidth connectivity where terrestrial routes are absent, constrained, or strategically insufficient.

The distinguishing asset is:

- orbital capacity
- spectrum rights
- mobility terminals
- sovereign and mission-linked access economics

## What each company proves

### `American Tower`

`American Tower` proves that connectivity dependence can be monetized through scarce site ownership before users ever see a service interface.

### `Cogent`

`Cogent` proves that terrestrial hidden connectivity is also a route-density and building-access business, not only a tower business.

### `Viasat`

`Viasat` proves that the same dependence can clear through a very different ownership bottleneck when bandwidth must be delivered beyond terrestrial reach or under sovereign and mission-sensitive constraints.

## Why the taxonomy fails here

`AnnualReports` can scatter these companies across real estate and communications buckets.

That hides the real split.

The real split is:

- site ownership
- route-density terrestrial carriage
- orbital mobility and sovereign access

Those are different hidden-connectivity ownership structures inside one broader access-layer system.
