# American Tower Versus Crown Castle Versus SBA Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the direct comparison built from the hidden-connectivity and access-layer blind spot inside the broader `AnnualReports` taxonomy problem.

It focuses on the cleanest three-way split currently available in the archive:

- `American Tower`
- `Crown Castle`
- `SBA Communications`

The useful question is not whether all three own tower assets.

The useful question is what access-layer ownership structure each one actually represents:

- global tower ownership widened by a datacenter bridge
- domestic tower simplification after exiting more execution-heavy edge assets
- pure-play recurring tower rent with minimal portfolio complexity

## Executive read

All three companies monetize wireless dependence through scarce physical sites, but they do not package that dependence in the same way.

- `American Tower` is the global tower-ownership and datacenter-bridge case
- `Crown Castle` is the domestic tower simplification and portfolio-reset case
- `SBA Communications` is the pure-play tower-rent case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `American Tower`: global tower ownership and datacenter-bridged access infrastructure
2. `Crown Castle`: domestic tower simplification and portfolio-reset infrastructure
3. `SBA Communications`: pure-play tower-rent infrastructure

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by access-layer structure, moving from the broadest and most layered ownership model into the cleaner domestic simplification model and then to the narrowest recurring-rent tower model.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `American Tower` is the clearest global and datacenter-bridged ownership case

`American Tower` is not most useful as a generic `REIT - Specialty Real Estate` packet.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows the broadest access-layer ownership model in this set:

- `149,686` communications sites globally at year-end `2025`
- `30` operating data-center facilities across `11` U.S. markets at year-end `2025`
- full-year `2025` revenue of `$10.645B`
- full-year `2025` total property revenue of `$10.305B`
- full-year `2025` Adjusted EBITDA of `$7.130B`
- full-year `2025` AFFO attributable to common stockholders of `$5.042B`
- `Q2 2026` revenue of `$2.749B`
- `Q2 2026` property revenue of `$2.688B`

That is why `American Tower` is first in this comparison.

It is the clearest proof that access-layer ownership can widen beyond towers alone into a broader digital-capacity bridge. The same mobile-data and cloud or AI demand wave that raises tower amendments also supports CoreSite leasing, which makes `American Tower` the broadest hidden-connectivity ownership model rather than the cleanest pure-play one.

### 2. `Crown Castle` is the clearest domestic simplification case

`Crown Castle` differs because its current meaning is not just tower ownership. It is tower ownership being simplified for cleaner public-market legibility after disposing of fiber and small cells.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows that clearly:

- March `2025` Strategic Fiber Agreement to sell the fiber solutions business to Zayo and the small-cell business to EQT for aggregate consideration of `$8.5B`, subject to closing adjustments
- full-year `2025` continuing-operations site rental revenue of `$4.049B`
- full-year `2025` income from continuing operations of `$1.103B`
- full-year `2025` Adjusted EBITDA of `$2.863B`
- full-year `2025` AFFO of `$1.904B`
- `Q2 2026` continuing-operations site rental revenue of `$967M`
- `Q2 2026` Adjusted EBITDA of `$675M`
- `Q2 2026` AFFO of `$488M`

`Crown Castle` is second because it is the clearest proof that the public market often prefers the recurring tower story to a more complicated connectivity bundle, even when fiber and small cells remain strategically relevant.

The key return loop is not global breadth or a datacenter bridge. It is domestic tower cash flow being made simpler, lower-complexity, and easier to underwrite.

### 3. `SBA Communications` is the clearest pure-play tower-rent case

`SBA Communications` differs from both because it gives the narrowest and most legible version of the same broad wireless-dependence economics.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows a business centered on recurring site rent:

- `46,328` sites at year-end `2025`
- `17,394` sites in the United States and territories and `28,934` internationally at year-end `2025`
- site leasing contributing `97.9%` of total segment operating profit in `2025`
- average of `1.8` tenants per site at year-end `2025`
- about `71%` of tower structures on land SBA owned or controlled for more than `20` years, with an average remaining life of `35` years including renewal options under SBA's control
- `Q2 2026` site leasing revenue of `$663.9M`
- `Q2 2026` Adjusted EBITDA of `$483.8M`
- `Q2 2026` AFFO of `$324.4M`

`SBA Communications` is third because this ordering moves from the broadest and most layered ownership model toward the cleanest recurring-rent structure.

It remains a strong blind-spot case because `REIT - Specialty Real Estate` does not explain that this is one of the clearest ways to own mobile dependence before it shows up in visible software, cloud, or app revenue.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `American Tower`: broad ownership bridge first

`American Tower` matters most when the question is who owns global wireless sites at scale while also bridging into datacenter and interconnection-adjacent infrastructure.

The distinguishing asset is:

- global tower breadth
- carrier amendments and colocations
- CoreSite datacenter bridge
- participation in both wireless-edge and digital-capacity demand

### `Crown Castle`: simplification first

`Crown Castle` matters most when the question is how a company strips away complexity to present the cleanest domestic tower story to public markets.

The distinguishing asset is:

- U.S.-focused tower base
- site-rental cash-flow clarity
- exit from fiber and small cells
- simplification and operating-cost reset

### `SBA Communications`: pure recurring tower rent first

`SBA Communications` matters most when the question is who offers the narrowest recurring-rent exposure to the wireless access layer without the same degree of datacenter bridge or restructuring complexity.

The distinguishing asset is:

- tower-only leasing orientation
- long-duration land control
- low-incremental-cost colocations
- cleaner recurring-rent legibility

## What each company proves

### `American Tower`

`American Tower` proves that access-layer ownership can widen into a broader digital-capacity and datacenter bridge rather than remaining a simple tower multiple story.

### `Crown Castle`

`Crown Castle` proves that infrastructure simplification can itself be a strategy when capital markets prefer clearer recurring tower economics to a more complicated communications-asset mix.

### `SBA Communications`

`SBA Communications` proves that wireless dependence can be monetized in its cleanest rent form through a narrow tower-leasing model with limited portfolio complexity.

## Why the taxonomy fails here

`AnnualReports` can place all three companies under `REIT - Specialty Real Estate`.

That hides the real distinction.

The real distinction is:

- global tower ownership with a datacenter bridge versus
- domestic tower simplification versus
- pure-play tower rent

Those are different access-layer ownership structures inside the same broad hidden-connectivity lane, and the blind-spot thread is stronger when they are separated directly rather than flattened into generic specialty real estate.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `American Tower` is the clearest global and datacenter-bridged access-layer ownership case, not just another specialty REIT
- `Crown Castle` is the clearest domestic simplification and tower-reset case, not just another tower landlord
- `SBA Communications` is the clearest pure-play recurring tower-rent case, not just another communications-property label
- the practical comparison is not REIT versus REIT versus REIT, but broad global access ownership versus domestic simplification versus pure recurring tower rent
- the right current order is `American Tower` first on breadth and bridge structure, `Crown Castle` second on simplification clarity, and `SBA Communications` third on pure-play rent clarity
