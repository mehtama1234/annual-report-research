## Executive read

`AnnualReports` is useful as a filing locator and a legacy issuer taxonomy.

It is not a sufficient organizing system for the archive's bigger-picture work.

The blind spot is not simply missing categories. The deeper problem is that the site classifies issuers by legacy market labels instead of by:

- behavior systems
- relationship ownership
- cultural demand patterns
- business-model structure
- hidden infrastructure below the visible product

## What the taxonomy misses

### 1. Retail as a unified operating system

The live browse tree still has no clean standalone retail system. Retail-like operators are scattered across `Services`, `Consumer Goods`, and `Technology`.

That weakens comparison across:

- mass retail
- specialty retail
- grocery
- off-price
- beauty retail
- marketplace commerce
- membership retail

### 2. Business-model reality

The browse tree does not cleanly separate:

- marketplace versus owned inventory
- DTC versus wholesale or distributor dependence
- subscription versus transaction
- loyalty or membership economics
- advertising, seller-services, or retail-media layers
- IP licensing and franchise monetization
- ecosystem control and multi-layer monetization

### 3. Consumer and cultural behavior systems

The site categorizes companies, but not the recurring behavior systems underneath demand.

The archive needs separate lanes for:

- comfort and casualization
- value-hunt and bargain discovery
- routine-use trust and replenishment
- beauty, fit, and self-presentation
- ritual, occasion, and stimulation
- fandom, play, and participation
- recurring attention and cultural-distribution

### 4. Relationship ownership

The taxonomy does not distinguish companies that own the customer relationship directly from those that mostly rent access through wholesale, distributors, or third-party platforms.

That distinction drives:

- pricing power
- gross-margin quality
- loyalty depth
- data control
- media and loyalty monetization
- resilience under channel stress

### 5. Real-estate and infrastructure logic across old sector walls

The source taxonomy splits many coherent systems across `Financial`, `Real Estate`, `Services`, `Industrial Goods`, and `Technology`.

That obscures practical systems such as:

- logistics property
- data-center infrastructure
- tower ownership and connectivity access
- builder and project workflow infrastructure
- packaging, waste, and embedded environmental support

### 6. Taxonomy-breaker names

Some companies expose the limitation directly because their source bucket is much weaker than their operating reality.

Examples already visible in the archive include:

- `Airbnb`
- `eBay`
- `Birkenstock`
- `Kenvue`
- `YETI`
- `Planet Fitness`

## Practical routing rule

Ordinary uncovered industries are not automatically this thread's problem.

This thread should own the systems that still have no clean home on the site even if other threads finish the normal sector tree.

The right question is not "is the industry uncovered?"

The right question is "does the taxonomy hide the real system here?"

## Best current blind-spot lanes

The strongest current blind-spot lanes are:

- retail systems
- marketplace, platform, and ecosystem systems
- DTC control and relationship ownership
- loyalty, wallet, and membership systems
- consumer cultural pattern systems
- behind-the-shelf consumer infrastructure
- control-layer connectivity infrastructure
- hidden connectivity and access-layer ownership
- physical execution and embedded workflow systems
