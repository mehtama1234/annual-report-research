## Executive read

All three companies monetize hidden operating trust inside physical systems, but they do not govern the same embedded layer.

- `CECO` is the engineered plant-environment and process-reliability case
- `Veralto` is the embedded quality, water, and product-control workflow case
- `Ecolab` is the embedded hygiene, contamination-control, and compliance-service case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `CECO`: environmental and process-reliability infrastructure
2. `Veralto`: embedded quality, water, and product-control infrastructure
3. `Ecolab`: embedded hygiene, water, and compliance-service infrastructure

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by how deeply each company moves from engineered plant hardware into recurring workflow control and then into service-led site embedment.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `CECO` is the clearest engineered plant-environment case

CECO is the cleanest proof that the hidden consumer-infrastructure lane extends into hard industrial environmental-control systems, not just packaging, cleanup routes, or measurement workflows.

The FY `2025` through Q2 `2026` chain shows the operating shape clearly:

- FY `2025` revenue of `$774.4M`
- FY `2025` adjusted EBITDA of `$90.3M`
- Q1 `2026` orders of `$449.5M` and backlog of `$1.035B`
- Q2 `2026` orders of `$798.5M`, backlog of `$1.819B`, revenue of `$285.0M`, and adjusted EBITDA of `$40.2M`

The useful read is not "pollution controls."

The useful read is engineered infrastructure inside plants and utility-adjacent systems where customers are paying for:

- air and water control
- thermal and emissions management
- industrial process reliability
- capacity support for reshoring, semiconductors, data centers, and power demand

That is why `CECO` is first in this comparison.

### 2. `Veralto` is the clearest quality-and-water workflow case

Veralto matters because it shows that the same plant-embedded branch widens away from heavier engineered systems into recurring trust workflows built around water quality, inspection, coding, analytics, and product integrity.

The FY `2025` through Q2 `2026` chain shows that clearly:

- FY `2025` sales of `$5.503B`
- FY `2025` operating cash flow of `$1.077B`
- FY `2025` non-GAAP free cash flow of `$1.014B`
- Q1 `2026` sales of `$1.422B`
- Q2 `2026` sales of `$1.474B`

The management framing is also structurally different from CECO.

Veralto is not mainly selling plant hardware tied to one project cycle. It is embedding itself in:

- water analytics and environmental monitoring
- product inspection, coding, and marking
- quality assurance and traceability
- recurring essential-goods and industrial workflow trust

That makes Veralto second because it sits between harder engineered environmental systems and the more service-heavy embedment model visible at Ecolab.

### 3. `Ecolab` is the clearest service-led hygiene and compliance case

Ecolab matters because it shows that the hidden operating layer also lives in recurring onsite service, daily sanitation discipline, pest prevention, water management, and contamination-control execution.

The FY `2025` through Q2 `2026` chain shows the scale and operating breadth clearly:

- FY `2025` sales of `$16.1B`
- FY `2025` adjusted diluted EPS of `$7.53`
- FY `2025` operating cash flow of `$2.95B`
- Q1 `2026` sales of `$4.066B`
- Q2 `2026` sales of `$4.415B`

The useful read is not commodity chemistry.

The useful read is a service-and-compliance operating layer embedded across:

- hospitality sanitation
- food and beverage cleaning and safety
- hospitals and infection prevention
- pest elimination
- industrial and commercial water management
- microelectronics, life-sciences, and bioprocessing contamination control

That makes Ecolab third because this ordering moves from harder engineered plant systems into workflow control and then into the most service-intensive daily embedment model.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `CECO`: engineered environmental hardware first

CECO matters most when the question is who keeps industrial plants, regulated facilities, utilities, and power-adjacent systems within environmental and operating constraints.

The distinguishing asset is:

- engineered air and water systems
- thermal and emissions control
- process-reliability equipment
- project and backlog conversion inside regulated physical infrastructure

### `Veralto`: quality-and-water workflow control first

Veralto matters most when the question is who turns water quality, inspection, coding, traceability, and measurement into recurring embedded workflows inside essential-goods and industrial systems.

The distinguishing asset is:

- water analytics and monitoring
- coding, marking, and inspection
- product-control and traceability workflows
- recurring essential-goods quality trust

### `Ecolab`: service-led hygiene and compliance first

Ecolab matters most when the question is who becomes part of the customer's daily operating routine through sanitation, water treatment, pest control, contamination prevention, and embedded field execution.

The distinguishing asset is:

- onsite service intensity
- hygiene and food-safety workflows
- water and contamination-control discipline
- recurring compliance and site-performance relationships

## What each company proves

### `CECO`

`CECO` proves that the hidden infrastructure behind consumption and industrial throughput still includes hard engineered environmental hardware tied to power, heat, air, water, and process control.

### `Veralto`

`Veralto` proves that the same hidden layer can become a higher-trust quality-and-water workflow system built on monitoring, coding, inspection, and traceability rather than heavier project hardware alone.

### `Ecolab`

`Ecolab` proves that the hidden infrastructure layer can move one step further into recurring onsite service and compliance embedment, where daily hygiene, contamination control, pest prevention, and water stewardship become the real control point.

## Why the taxonomy fails here

`AnnualReports` can place these companies into labels such as:

- `Pollution & Treatment Controls`
- `Specialty Chemicals`

That is enough for source collection.

It is weak for analysis.

The stronger distinction is:

- engineered plant-environment hardware versus
- embedded quality-and-water workflows versus
- service-led hygiene-and-compliance embedment

Those are different monetization choke points inside the same hidden operating layer.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `CECO` remains the clearest heavy engineered plant-environment anchor in the behind-the-shelf lane
- `Veralto` is the clearest embedded quality, water, monitoring, and product-control counterpoint to CECO
- `Ecolab` is the clearest service-led hygiene, contamination-control, pest, and compliance counterpoint to both
- the useful comparison is not one environmental or chemistry company versus another, but engineered environmental hardware versus embedded workflow control versus recurring onsite service embedment
- this three-way split is the cleanest current proof that the plant-embedded branch of the behind-the-shelf lane can be broken into subtypes instead of left as one generic treatment or compliance bucket
