- `C.H. Robinson`

## Executive read

All three companies sit behind visible consumer demand, but they do not solve the same routing problem.

- `C.H. Robinson` is the freight-brokerage and shipment-orchestration case
- `UPS` is the dense parcel-network and package-flow infrastructure case
- `DoorDash` is the local merchant-demand and last-mile convenience-coordination case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `C.H. Robinson`: freight-brokerage and shipment-orchestration infrastructure
2. `UPS`: dense parcel-network and package-flow infrastructure
3. `DoorDash`: local merchant-demand and last-mile convenience coordination

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by position inside the delivery stack, moving from upstream shipment orchestration to scaled parcel execution to app-mediated local-demand routing.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `C.H. Robinson` is the clearest upstream shipment-orchestration case

`C.H. Robinson` is not most useful as a generic `Air Delivery & Freight Services` packet.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows a business whose core value is routing freight without owning the full physical network:

- full-year `2025` total revenues of `$16.2B`
- full-year `2025` income from operations of `$795.0M`
- `Q2 2026` total revenues of `$4.9B`
- `Q2 2026` income from operations of `$255.7M`
- `13` consecutive quarters of NAST market outgrowth versus the Cass Freight Shipment Index as discussed in `Q2 2026`

That is why `C.H. Robinson` is first in this comparison.

The filings show that the core asset is routing intelligence, pricing discipline, workflow automation, and carrier-shipper coordination rather than direct parcel-density ownership or local consumer habit.

### 2. `UPS` is the clearest dense parcel-network case

`UPS` differs because it owns the physical parcel network rather than mainly brokering freight across third-party carriers or app-routing local merchants.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows that structure clearly:

- full-year `2025` revenue of `$88.7B`
- full-year `2025` operating profit of `$7.9B`
- `5.2B` packages delivered in `2025`
- `1.6M` shipping customers served in `2025`
- `Q2 2026` revenue of `$22.8B`
- `Q2 2026` non-GAAP adjusted operating profit of `$2.1B`

`UPS` is second because it is the clearest proof that the delivery layer can itself be a capital-intensive infrastructure system shaped by route density, automation, labor, mix, and package economics.

The key return loop is not marketplace demand aggregation. It is dense package-flow execution with enough network control to deliberately trade lower-quality volume for better revenue quality.

### 3. `DoorDash` is the clearest local-demand and last-mile convenience case

`DoorDash` differs from both because it starts with consumer app behavior and local merchant visibility, then embeds delivery and fulfillment around that demand.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows that clearly:

- full-year `2025` revenue of `$13.717B`
- full-year `2025` net income of `$935M`
- quarterly Marketplace GOV of `$29.7B` in `Q4 2025`
- over `56M` monthly active users at year-end `2025`
- over `35M` DashPass, Wolt+, and Deliveroo Plus members at year-end `2025`
- `Q2 2026` revenue of `$4.454B`
- `Q2 2026` orders of `970M`
- `Q2 2026` Marketplace GOV of `$33.1B`

`DoorDash` is third because this ordering moves from freight orchestration and parcel-network density into the local, consumer-facing, last-mile coordination layer where convenience habit and merchant dependence are the product surface.

It remains a strong blind-spot case because `Internet Service Providers` does not describe a local-commerce logistics system well at all.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `C.H. Robinson`: shipment orchestration first

`C.H. Robinson` matters most when the question is who coordinates freight movement, pricing, and execution across a broad shipper-carrier network without needing to own the package network itself.

The distinguishing asset is:

- brokered carrier access
- pricing and procurement discipline
- forwarding workflow
- automation of the shipment process

### `UPS`: parcel infrastructure first

`UPS` matters most when the question is who owns a dense, capital-heavy package network tied to route density, sorting, automation, and revenue-quality management.

The distinguishing asset is:

- parcel-network density
- route economics
- sorting and automation infrastructure
- customer and package mix control

### `DoorDash`: local-demand routing first

`DoorDash` matters most when the question is who owns recurring convenience demand, merchant visibility, and last-mile local coordination between consumers, merchants, and couriers.

The distinguishing asset is:

- merchant density
- consumer habit and memberships
- last-mile coordination
- merchant tooling and platform visibility

## What each company proves

### `C.H. Robinson`

`C.H. Robinson` proves that a meaningful part of commerce routing is governed by orchestration, quoting, and shipment workflow rather than by direct asset ownership.

### `UPS`

`UPS` proves that commerce routing can also be governed by dense physical infrastructure where route economics, package mix, labor, and automation are the core moat.

### `DoorDash`

`DoorDash` proves that the delivery layer can be consumer-interface-led, where local demand aggregation, membership habit, merchant dependence, and last-mile coordination create a new kind of infrastructure around everyday commerce.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `C.H. Robinson` is the clearest freight-brokerage and shipment-orchestration case, not just another transport-services name
- `UPS` is the clearest dense parcel-network and package-flow infrastructure case, not just another delivery company inside `Air Delivery & Freight Services`
- `DoorDash` is the clearest local merchant-demand and last-mile convenience-coordination case, not a useful fit for a shallow internet label
- the practical comparison is not freight versus parcel versus software, but orchestration versus network density versus consumer-interface-led last-mile routing
- the right current order is `C.H. Robinson` first on brokerage-orchestration clarity, `UPS` second on parcel-infrastructure clarity, and `DoorDash` third on local-demand and merchant-coordination clarity
