# Cogent Versus Lumen Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the direct comparison built from the hidden-connectivity and access-layer blind spot inside the broader `AnnualReports` taxonomy problem.

It focuses on the cleanest two-way split now available inside the terrestrial route branch:

- `Cogent`
- `Lumen Technologies`

The useful question is not whether both are telecom or communications-services names.

The useful question is which terrestrial hidden-connectivity ownership model each one most clearly represents:

- lean route-density and on-net private-connectivity ownership
- broader fiber-backbone, enterprise-network, and portfolio-surgery ownership

## Executive read

Both companies monetize terrestrial data movement before value reaches cloud, software, or consumer applications, but they do not own the same kind of route business.

- `Cogent` is the clearest lean public-market route-density and on-net private-connectivity ownership case
- `Lumen Technologies` is the clearest broader fiber-backbone, enterprise-network, public-sector, and wholesale ownership case with visible portfolio surgery and legacy runoff

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `Cogent`: route-density and on-net private-connectivity infrastructure
2. `Lumen Technologies`: fiber-backbone and enterprise-network ownership infrastructure

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by how cleanly each company isolates a terrestrial route-ownership model, moving from the narrower route-density and on-net economics case into the broader backbone-and-portfolio-reset case.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `Cogent` is the clearest lean route-density and on-net case

`Cogent` matters most when the question is who owns a comparatively clean public-market route-density and on-net building model without the same consumer-access residue or portfolio-surgery burden.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows:

- full-year `2025` service revenue of `$975.8M`
- operations in `57` countries
- two network layers explicitly described in the filings: an IP Network and an Optical Wave Network
- on-net speeds ranging from `100` Mbps to `400` Gbps
- `Q2 2026` on-net revenue including wavelengths of `$150.2M`
- `Q2 2026` IP traffic growth of `16%` year over year

That keeps `Cogent` as the cleanest proof that terrestrial hidden connectivity can be monetized through route placement, building access, and wavelength economics without needing tower rent or orbital access.

### 2. `Lumen Technologies` is the clearest broader fiber-backbone counterpoint

`Lumen Technologies` differs because it is not mainly a lean on-net economics story.

It is a broader backbone, enterprise-network, public-sector, and wholesale route-ownership story where the key question is whether higher-value transport and orchestration can overcome legacy drag.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows:

- full-year `2025` operating revenue of `$12.402B`
- about `163,000` fiber on-net buildings as of `2025-12-31`
- more than `340,000` route miles of fiber globally as of `2025-12-31`
- nearly `$13B` of PCF deals signed to date in `Q4 2025` materials
- `17M` total intercity fiber miles highlighted in `Q4 2025`
- `Q2 2026` revenue of `$2.805B`
- strategic revenue reaching `53%` of total business revenue in `Q2 2026`
- sale of the Mass Markets FTTH business in `11` states to AT&T completed on `2026-02-02` for pre-tax cash proceeds of `$5.75B`

That makes `Lumen` the clearest public-market proof that another terrestrial route model exists beyond lean on-net carriage:

a larger fiber-backbone and network-orchestration system that is strategically important but burdened by debt, legacy runoff, and portfolio simplification.

## The most useful boundary split

### `Cogent`: lean route-density and on-net carriage first

`Cogent` matters most when the question is who owns enough route placement, optical capacity, and on-net building access to monetize private connectivity efficiently and with comparatively clear route economics.

The distinguishing asset is:

- on-net building economics
- IP and optical-network routes
- wavelength and private-network services
- leaner terrestrial route-density ownership

### `Lumen Technologies`: broader backbone and portfolio-surgery route ownership first

`Lumen` matters most when the question is who owns a much broader fiber and enterprise-network backbone where the opportunity is real but the economics are still being repaired through divestitures, mix shift, and debt reduction.

The distinguishing asset is:

- route-mile and on-net-building scale
- wavelength, PCF, RapidRoutes, and NaaS positioning
- enterprise, public-sector, and wholesale channel depth
- portfolio surgery and strategic-versus-legacy migration

## What each company proves

### `Cogent`

`Cogent` proves that terrestrial hidden connectivity can be a relatively pure route-density, building-access, and optical-carriage business rather than a broad telecom restructuring case.

### `Lumen Technologies`

`Lumen` proves that the same dependence can also be monetized through a much broader long-haul and metro fiber backbone where AI and cloud traffic matter, but where value capture still depends on legacy runoff management, balance-sheet repair, and portfolio simplification.

## Why the taxonomy fails here

`AnnualReports` can place both companies into `Diversified Communication Services`.

That hides the real distinction.

The real distinction is:

- lean route-density and on-net private connectivity versus
- broader fiber-backbone and enterprise-network ownership

Those are different terrestrial hidden-connectivity ownership structures inside one route branch, and the blind-spot lane is stronger when they are separated directly rather than flattened into one generic telecom bucket.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `Cogent` is the clearest live public-market route-density and on-net private-connectivity case
- `Lumen Technologies` is the clearest live public-market fiber-backbone and portfolio-surgery counterpoint
- the practical comparison is not one telecom versus another, but lean on-net route ownership versus broader backbone ownership with legacy drag
