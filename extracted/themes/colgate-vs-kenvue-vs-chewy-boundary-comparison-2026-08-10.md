# Colgate Versus Kenvue Versus Chewy Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the next direct comparison built from the routine-use trust and everyday-care blind-spot lane.

It focuses on the cleanest three-way split inside recurring care and replenishment systems:

- `Colgate`
- `Kenvue`
- `Chewy`

The useful question is not whether all three benefit from repeat behavior.

The useful question is what kind of trust-and-repeat system each company actually organizes:

- science-and-efficacy routine care
- consumer-health and self-care trust
- digital-first replenishment and care management

## Executive read

The three companies sit in the same broad repeat-care family, but they do not own the same kind of trust relationship.

- `Colgate` is the narrow, efficacy-led daily-use habit case
- `Kenvue` is the wider consumer-health and self-care trust case
- `Chewy` is the digital-native care-management and replenishment case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `Colgate`: science-and-efficacy routine-care system
2. `Kenvue`: consumer-health and self-care trust system
3. `Chewy`: digital-first replenishment and care-management system

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by how the repeat relationship is formed, moving from habitual product efficacy to healthcare-adjacent self-care trust to digital account-managed replenishment.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `Colgate` is the clearest efficacy-and-habit case

Colgate is the narrowest but most classical routine-use system in the trio.

Its filings repeatedly frame the business around:

- oral-care habit
- science-based innovation
- advertising support
- category leadership
- pet-health adjacency

The `Q4 2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows that clearly:

- full-year `2025` net sales of `$20.382B`
- record operating cash flow of `$4.198B`
- base business EPS of `$3.69` in `2025`
- `Q1 2026` net sales of `$5.324B`
- `Q2 2026` net sales of `$5.361B`
- organic sales growth of `2.9%` in Q1 and `2.4%` in Q2 `2026`

That is why Colgate is first in this comparison.

It is the clearest proof that science-supported daily-use trust can remain a durable consumer system without needing a heavy service layer or a broad wellness narrative.

### 2. `Kenvue` is the clearest consumer-health bridge case

Kenvue differs because the trust loop is wider and more healthcare-adjacent than Colgate’s.

The customer is returning not only for everyday hygiene, but also for pain, allergy, wound care, skin health, baby care, and oral care under a global self-care frame.

The FY`2025` through `Q2 2026` chain makes that clear:

- `2025` net sales of `$15.1bn`
- operations in more than `165` countries
- service to about `1.2 billion` people globally
- FY`2025` net cash flows from operating activities of `$2.2bn`
- `Q1 2026` net sales growth of `4.5%`
- `Q2 2026` net sales growth of `3.0%`
- third consecutive quarter of net and organic sales growth by `Q2 2026`

Kenvue is second because it is the clearest proof that the routine-use lane extends beyond classic staples into consumer-health and essential self-care trust.

It also remains one of the archive’s clearest taxonomy-breaker cases because `Technology / Biotechnology` is materially weaker than the operating reality.

### 3. `Chewy` is the clearest digital care-management case

Chewy differs from both shelf-based systems because the relationship is managed through an account, not just through branded package repetition.

The repeat behavior is organized through:

- Autoship
- prescriptions
- pharmacy
- mobile and website account management
- recurring pet-parent care

The `Q3 2025` through `Q1 2026` chain shows that clearly:

- full-year `2025` net sales of `$12.60B`
- adjusted EBITDA of `$719.2M`
- record free cash flow of `$562M`
- `21.3M` active customers at year-end `2025`
- about `84%` Autoship mix in `Q3 2025`
- `Q1 2026` net sales of `$3.36B`
- `Q1 2026` adjusted EBITDA of `$253.1M`

Chewy is third because this ordering moves from the tightest efficacy-led shelf habit to the broader self-care bridge to the fullest digital-native care-management model.

It is still a strong blind-spot case because `Retail-Catalog & Mail-Order Houses` does not explain a recurring pet-care interface with prescriptions, pharmacy, and account-managed replenishment.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `Colgate`: product efficacy and habit first

Colgate matters most when the question is who owns a narrow, trusted, low-consideration care habit with strong category leadership.

The distinguishing asset is:

- oral-care habit
- science-based efficacy
- repeat hygiene need
- advertising-backed category trust

### `Kenvue`: healthcare-adjacent self-care first

Kenvue matters most when the question is who owns a wider essential-care relationship spanning multiple self-care and consumer-health need states.

The distinguishing asset is:

- efficacy trust
- self-care breadth
- essential-health routines
- global consumer-health reach

### `Chewy`: account-managed replenishment first

Chewy matters most when the question is who owns a digital-native repeat-care relationship through subscription-like replenishment and service attachment.

The distinguishing asset is:

- Autoship
- prescriptions
- pharmacy
- account management
- pet-parent trust

## What each company proves

### `Colgate`

`Colgate` proves that daily-use efficacy and science-backed habit remain one of the cleanest recurring consumer demand systems in the archive.

### `Kenvue`

`Kenvue` proves that routine care can widen into self-care and consumer-health trust without becoming a biotechnology business in any useful analytical sense.

### `Chewy`

`Chewy` proves that digital-native replenishment and care management can become a durable consumer interface when trust, prescriptions, and convenience are attached to the same account relationship.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `Colgate` is the clearest science-and-efficacy routine-care case, not just another personal-products issuer
- `Kenvue` is the clearest consumer-health and self-care trust case, not a meaningful fit for `Technology / Biotechnology`
- `Chewy` is the clearest digital-first replenishment and care-management case, not a useful fit for a shallow mail-order retail label
- the practical comparison is not personal products versus biotechnology versus catalog retail, but efficacy-led routine care versus healthcare-adjacent self-care trust versus digital account-managed replenishment
- the right current order is `Colgate` first on efficacy-and-habit clarity, `Kenvue` second on self-care trust clarity, and `Chewy` third on digital replenishment-management clarity
