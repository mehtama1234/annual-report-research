Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

It covers one of the main structural blind spots in `AnnualReports`: the source taxonomy can classify companies, but it does not explain the deeper recurring consumer and cultural patterns underneath the results.

## Executive read

The archive already supports a stronger cultural reading than ordinary sector work can provide.

The consumer economy is splitting by behavior system, not just by product category.

The clearest current systems are:

- comfort and casualization
- value-hunt and bargain discovery
- routine-use trust and replenishment
- ritual, occasion, and stimulation
- beauty, fit, and self-presentation
- health and practical-wellness signaling
- direct relationship and identity control
- platform-led utility and convenience
- fandom, play, collectible identity, and digital participation

## What these patterns imply

### 1. Consumer demand is increasingly system-shaped

The archive shows that many durable demand patterns are better explained by repeated behavior and identity systems than by old product categories.

### 2. Comfort is a real demand system

`Crocs`, `Deckers`, `Birkenstock`, `lululemon`, `Planet Fitness`, and `Warby Parker` all support the idea that lower-friction body management has become culturally legible and commercially durable.

### 3. Value is not one thing

The archive should keep separating branded bargain discovery from necessity-led value and fill-in convenience.

### 4. Participation matters beyond media labels

The Hasbro, Mattel, Roblox, Take-Two, and EA lane matters because the consumer is often buying identity, participation, and repeat engagement, not just a toy, game, or software product.

### 5. Relationship control is a hidden economic divider

Two companies can look similar by product category and still have very different economics depending on whether they own demand directly or mostly rent it through third-party channels.
