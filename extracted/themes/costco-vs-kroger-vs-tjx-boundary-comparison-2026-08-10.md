# Costco Versus Kroger Versus TJX Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the next direct comparison built from the retail-system blind-spot lane.

It focuses on the cleanest non-platform store-based three-way split currently available in the archive:

- `Costco`
- `Kroger`
- `TJX`

The useful question is not whether all three are retailers.

The useful question is what kind of repeat retail system each company actually owns:

- paid utility membership retail
- replenishment and basket-data retail
- bargain-discovery and treasure-hunt retail

## Executive read

The three companies are all retail systems, but the repeat behavior and second layer are not the same.

- `Costco` is the paid utility-membership case
- `Kroger` is the replenishment and basket-data case
- `TJX` is the bargain-discovery and treasure-hunt case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `Costco`: paid utility-membership retail system
2. `Kroger`: replenishment and basket-data retail system
3. `TJX`: bargain-discovery and treasure-hunt retail system

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by what the customer is actually coming back for, moving from prepaid trusted access to habitual household basket formation to recurring bargain discovery.

## The core comparison

| Company | What the customer mainly returns for | What the company mainly controls | What widens the model beyond the base sale | Best current read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Costco` | trusted low-price access, stock-up behavior, and warehouse utility | paid membership, value trust, warehouse frequency, and increasingly strong digital convenience | membership fees and digitally enabled shopping on top of large-volume merchanting | paid utility-membership retail system |
| `Kroger` | recurring household basket formation, grocery replenishment, and personalized value | grocery basket, loyalty-linked household data, eCommerce habit, and everyday food-retail frequency | retail media, first-party data, and alternative profit businesses on top of the grocery relationship | replenishment and basket-data retail system |
| `TJX` | branded bargains, surprise assortment, and repeated treasure-hunt trips | off-price sourcing flexibility, dense store traffic, and branded-value perception | merchandise turn, traffic-led share capture, and global sourcing leverage rather than formal membership or media-first economics | bargain-discovery and treasure-hunt retail system |

## What the filings now show

### 1. `Costco` is the clearest paid utility-membership case

Costco is not just a low-price retailer with strong traffic.

The fiscal `2025` through `Q3 2026` chain shows a formal prepaid access system:

- fiscal `2025` total revenue of `$275.235B`
- fiscal `2025` membership fees of `$5.323B`
- fiscal `2025` net sales of `$269.9B`
- `Q1 2026` digitally enabled comparable sales up `20.5%`
- `Q2 2026` digitally enabled comparable sales up `22.6%`
- `Q3 2026` net sales up `11.6%` to `$69.15B`

That is why Costco is first in this comparison.

The customer is not mainly returning for promotional discovery or a one-time grocery fill-in. The core loop is trusted low-price access that is formalized through membership and reinforced by digital convenience.

### 2. `Kroger` is the clearest replenishment and basket-data case

Kroger differs because the repeat behavior is not prepaid access and not treasure-hunt discovery.

It is recurring household basket formation with a monetizable data layer.

The `2025` through `Q1 2026` chain shows that clearly:

- more than `$16B` in fiscal `2025` eCommerce sales
- `$1.5B` in fiscal `2025` operating profit from alternative profit businesses
- `Q3 2025` eCommerce sales up `17%`
- `Q4 2025` operating profit of `$1.246B`
- `Q1 2026` total company sales of `$46.1B`
- `Q1 2026` Kroger Precision Marketing profit up over `20%`

Kroger is second because it is the clearest proof that a grocery relationship can widen into retail media and alternative profit streams without ceasing to be a replenishment-first retail system.

### 3. `TJX` is the clearest bargain-discovery and treasure-hunt case

TJX differs because the customer is not mainly paying for access and not mainly repeating a pantry basket.

The customer is returning for branded value and new finds.

The fiscal `2026` through `Q1 FY27` chain shows that clearly:

- fiscal `2026` net sales of `$60.4B`
- fiscal `2026` comparable sales up `5%`
- `Q3 FY26` net sales up `7%` to `$15.1B`
- `Q4 FY26` net sales up `9%` to `$17.7B`
- `Q1 FY27` net sales up `9%` to `$14.3B`
- management repeatedly emphasizing customer transactions and outstanding branded merchandise availability

TJX is third because this ordering moves from prepaid trusted access to recurring necessity baskets to discretionary bargain discovery.

It remains a strong blind-spot case because `Apparel Stores` does not explain a scaled treasure-hunt system at all well.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `Costco`: access trust first

Costco matters most when the question is who directly monetizes trusted utility access.

The distinguishing asset is:

- membership fees
- prepaid belonging
- stock-up behavior
- large-scale warehouse trust

### `Kroger`: basket data first

Kroger matters most when the question is who owns one of the highest-frequency household baskets and can widen it into media and data economics.

The distinguishing asset is:

- grocery basket repetition
- loyalty-linked household data
- eCommerce habit
- alternative profit businesses tied to routine food demand

### `TJX`: discovery draw first

TJX matters most when the question is who turns branded bargains and changing assortment into recurring traffic without a membership engine.

The distinguishing asset is:

- treasure-hunt behavior
- branded-value perception
- sourcing flexibility
- dense physical discovery traffic

## What each company proves

### `Costco`

`Costco` proves that a retailer can formalize value trust into a paid relationship and still keep enormous physical-volume momentum.

### `Kroger`

`Kroger` proves that the everyday grocery basket can become a data-and-media asset without leaving the replenishment retail system.

### `TJX`

`TJX` proves that bargain discovery is a durable retail system in its own right, not just a trade-down side effect or a clearance channel.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `Costco` is the clearest paid utility-membership retail case, not just a strong discount retailer
- `Kroger` is the clearest replenishment and basket-data retail case, not just a grocery operator
- `TJX` is the clearest scaled treasure-hunt retail case, not just another apparel-store packet
- the practical comparison is not retailer versus retailer versus retailer, but prepaid access retail versus habitual basket retail versus recurring discovery retail
- the right current order is `Costco` first on paid access and utility trust, `Kroger` second on replenishment and household-data economics, and `TJX` third on branded-bargain discovery
