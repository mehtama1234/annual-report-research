# Costco Versus Ulta Versus Ross Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the direct comparison built from the relationship-thickness blind-spot lane.

It focuses on the cleanest retail-facing three-way split currently supported by the archive:

- `Costco`
- `Ulta`
- `Ross`

The useful question is not whether all three have repeat customers.

The useful question is how thick the relationship layer actually is:

- paid membership and explicit fee-funded relationship economics
- loyalty-data and discovery-driven interface economics
- value-and-traffic repeat behavior with a thinner formal relationship layer

## Executive read

The three companies all have repeat consumer behavior, but the economics do not sit in the same place.

- `Costco` is the thick paid-membership case
- `Ulta` is the mid-thickness discovery-and-data interface case
- `Ross` is the thinner bargain-traffic and format-execution case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `Costco`: thick membership-funded retail relationship system
2. `Ulta`: mid-thickness beauty-discovery and loyalty-data interface system
3. `Ross`: thinner value-and-traffic off-price relationship system

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by how visible, monetized, and structurally central the relationship layer is.

## The core comparison

| Company | What the customer mainly returns for | What the company mainly controls | What widens the model beyond the base retail trip | Best current read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Costco` | trusted low-price access, stock-up behavior, and warehouse routine | paid membership, value perception, high-frequency warehouse traffic, and increasingly strong digital convenience | membership-fee revenue, digitally enabled shopping, and trusted access economics | thick membership-funded retail relationship system |
| `Ulta` | beauty discovery, self-care replenishment, trend-aware assortment, and salon-linked repeat behavior | loyalty-member data, curated beauty discovery, app and e-commerce usage, stores, salons, marketplace assortment, and media inventory | `UB Marketplace`, `UB Media`, salons, app behavior, and international beauty-interface widening | mid-thickness beauty-discovery and loyalty-data interface system |
| `Ross` | branded bargains, in-store discovery, and repeat value-seeking trips | off-price buying discipline, store traffic, merchandise appeal, and value trust | marketing-led engagement, inventory turn, and opportunistic sourcing rather than a formal membership or wallet layer | thinner value-and-traffic off-price relationship system |

## What the filings now show

### 1. `Costco` is the clearest thick retail relationship case

Costco is not just a low-price retailer with loyal shoppers.

The `2025` through `Q3 2026` chain shows a formal membership-funded relationship system:

- fiscal `2025` total revenue of `$275.235B`
- fiscal `2025` membership fees of `$5.323B`
- fiscal `2025` net income of `$8.099B`
- `Q1 2026` digitally enabled comparable sales up `20.5%`
- `Q2 2026` digitally enabled comparable sales up `22.6%`
- `Q3 2026` net sales up `11.6%` to `$69.15B`, with digitally enabled comparable sales up `21.5%`

That is why Costco is first in this comparison.

The customer relationship is explicit, prepaid, and economically central. The filings do not force the analyst to infer relationship depth indirectly because the membership layer is directly disclosed.

### 2. `Ulta` is the clearest mid-thickness discovery-and-data retail case

Ulta differs because the customer relationship is meaningful and widening, but not through one dominant fee-funded membership or wallet line.

The `2025` through `Q1 2026` chain shows a thicker interface than a normal specialty chain:

- full-year `2025` net sales up `9.7%` to `$12.4B`
- full-year `2025` comparable sales up `5.4%`
- `Q4 2025` net sales up `11.8%` to `$3.9B`
- `Q1 2026` net sales up `11.1%` to `$3.2B`
- `UB Marketplace` reaching more than `200` brands and `5,000` SKUs by fiscal `2025` year-end
- `UB Media`, salons, app and e-commerce behavior, and Space NK integration widening the relationship

Ulta is second because it is one of the clearest cases where relationship thickness comes through loyalty-member data, discovery, services, and media monetization rather than through a plainly financialized membership fee stream.

### 3. `Ross` is the clearest thinner traffic-and-value retail case

Ross differs because repeat behavior is strong, but the disclosed economics still live mainly in traffic, bargains, and execution.

The `2025` through `Q1 2026` chain shows that structure clearly:

- fiscal `2025` sales up `8%` to `$22.8B`
- fiscal `2025` comparable sales up `5%`
- `Q3 2025` sales up `10%` to `$5.6B`
- `Q4 2025` sales up `12%` to `$6.6B`
- `Q1 2026` sales up `21%` to `$6.0B`, with comparable sales up `17%`
- management repeatedly tying performance to compelling bargains, customer traffic, stronger engagement, and merchandising execution

Ross is third because this ordering moves from explicit membership economics to data-and-discovery interface widening to a thinner but still powerful format where repeat demand is driven mostly by value and in-store discovery.

It remains a strong blind-spot case because `Apparel Stores` does not explain a bargain-traffic relationship model at all well.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `Costco`: relationship funding first

Costco matters most when the question is who directly monetizes trusted repeat access through an explicit paid relationship.

The distinguishing asset is:

- membership fees
- prepaid belonging
- trusted value access
- digital convenience widening a paid retail relationship

### `Ulta`: interface and data first

Ulta matters most when the question is who owns a discovery-rich retail relationship without relying mainly on a classic membership-fee engine.

The distinguishing asset is:

- loyalty-member data
- beauty discovery
- salons and services
- marketplace and media layers

### `Ross`: format and traffic first

Ross matters most when the question is who wins on repeat value-seeking behavior without a thick formal wallet or member engine.

The distinguishing asset is:

- branded bargains
- in-store discovery
- traffic and marketing engagement
- opportunistic buying discipline

## What each company proves

### `Costco`

`Costco` proves that a physical retailer can own one of the thickest disclosed consumer relationships in the archive through trusted fee-funded access rather than through a software-like subscription model.

### `Ulta`

`Ulta` proves that specialty retail can become meaningfully relationship-owned through discovery, loyalty-member data, services, media, and assortment control even without a plainly dominant paid-wallet layer.

### `Ross`

`Ross` proves that strong repeat demand can still be monetized through format advantage, bargain trust, and store traffic rather than through explicit membership economics.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `Costco` is the clearest thick retail relationship case, not just a strong discount retailer
- `Ulta` is the clearest mid-thickness discovery-and-data retail relationship case, not just a beauty specialty chain
- `Ross` is the clearest thinner off-price traffic case, not just another apparel-store packet
- the practical comparison is not retailer versus retailer versus retailer, but fee-funded membership thickness versus discovery-interface thickness versus value-and-traffic repeat behavior
- the right current order is `Costco` first on explicit relationship monetization, `Ulta` second on interface-led thickening, and `Ross` third on thinner but durable format-led repeat demand
