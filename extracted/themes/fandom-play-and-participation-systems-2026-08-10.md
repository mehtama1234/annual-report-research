# Fandom, Play, and Participation Systems

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo turns another `AnnualReports` blind spot into a dedicated cross-sector lane:

consumer systems where recurring demand is driven by fandom, organized play, collectible behavior, franchise attachment, digital participation, or recurrent entertainment spending rather than by plain product ownership or generic software usage.

## Executive read

The source taxonomy can say `Toys & Games`, `Application Software`, or `Multimedia & Graphics Software`.

That is not the useful comparison.

The useful comparison is which participation system each company sits inside:

- collectible ruleset and organized-play fandom
- brand-universe and entertainment-extension play
- creator-built digital-world participation
- sequel-cadence and recurrent-spending franchise play
- sports-franchise and live-services wallet participation

This is why the lane needs its own memo.

The archive now supports a cleaner split between:

- physical-plus-digital collectible fandom
- brand-portfolio and entertainment extension
- creator-economy digital participation
- large game-franchise recurrent monetization
- sports-identity live-services loops

## Working fandom-system map

| Participation-system subtype | Core anchor | What the relationship is built on | Main second layer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Collectible ruleset and organized-play fandom system | `Hasbro` | repeat players, crossover sets, collectibles, licensing, and physical-plus-digital franchise engagement | digital gaming, licensing, and screen-linked extension |
| Brand-universe and entertainment-extension play system | `Mattel` | iconic toy brands, theatrical releases, licensing, digital games, and adult-fan extension | movies, mobile games, DTC or first-party data, and licensing |
| Creator-built digital-world participation system | `Roblox` | creators, discovery, virtual currency, engagement loops, communication, and digital identity | advertising, commerce, AI-assisted creation, and social features |
| Sequel-cadence and recurrent-spending franchise system | `Take-Two` | major owned IP, release anticipation, live services, mobile scale, and recurrent consumer spending | mobile distribution, live operations, and launch-event monetization |
| Sports-franchise and live-services wallet system | `Electronic Arts` | sports identity, annual refresh cycles, live-service retention, and extra-content spending | `Ultimate Team`, digital add-on spending, and live-content persistence |

## What each anchor adds

### 1. Hasbro is the clearest physical-plus-digital fandom flywheel

`Hasbro` shows that this lane is not just about toys.

The packet makes clear that the economic engine runs through:

- `Magic: The Gathering`
- organized play
- crossover releases
- collectible and backlist demand
- digital gaming
- licensing

That makes it a fandom system with recurring engagement, not a plain seasonal product seller.

### 2. Mattel adds the brand-portfolio and entertainment-extension version

`Mattel` is structurally different from Hasbro.

It leans more on:

- iconic character and toy universes
- theatrical releases
- mobile games
- licensing
- adult-fan broadening

This makes it the cleanest proof that the play lane includes wider franchise worlds, not just organized-play communities.

### 3. Roblox extends the lane into creator-built digital participation

`Roblox` proves that the same broad lane can exist without traditional owned-IP control.

Its engine runs through:

- creator supply
- discovery
- engagement hours
- virtual-currency bookings
- communication
- advertising and commerce expansion

This matters because it widens the lane from brand ownership into participatory world-building.

### 4. Take-Two adds the premium publisher and launch-cadence version

`Take-Two` shows a different participation system:

- blockbuster franchises
- live services
- mobile scale
- recurrent consumer spending
- release anticipation

The filings make clear that the economics are not driven by one-time software sales.

They are driven by a franchise portfolio that keeps players spending between big launches.

### 5. Electronic Arts adds the sports-wallet and annual-refresh version

`Electronic Arts` keeps the lane from collapsing into open-world or creator-economy models.

Its structure is more heavily tied to:

- sports identity
- annual refresh cycles
- live-service retention
- recurring digital wallet spending

That makes it the clearest sports-franchise participation system in the archive.

## What this lane now says

### 1. Play demand is a participation system, not just a product category

The archive now supports several distinct participation structures:

- collectible and ruleset fandom
- brand-universe play and entertainment extension
- creator-economy digital worlds
- large franchise recurrent spending
- sports live-services wallet loops

That is exactly what `AnnualReports` does not classify well.

### 2. Physical and digital play are now economically connected

This lane becomes more useful when it treats the anchors as one broader system with different expressions:

- `Hasbro` and `Mattel` on the physical-IP and licensing side
- `Roblox`, `Take-Two`, and `Electronic Arts` on the digital-participation side

Those source-taxonomy labels differ, but the recurring fan behavior is part of the same broader map.

### 3. Recurrence matters more than unit sales

The strongest common theme is not one-time purchase volume.

It is:

- repeat participation
- content cadence
- collectible behavior
- community persistence
- digital add-on or virtual-currency spending

That makes this one of the archive's clearest lanes where the recurring relationship matters more than the original product form.

## Why this is a blind spot in `AnnualReports`

The taxonomy does not explain:

- why `Hasbro` is a fandom and organized-play system rather than just a toy issuer
- why `Mattel` is a brand-universe and entertainment-extension system rather than a generic toy portfolio
- why `Roblox` belongs in a play-and-participation lane rather than plain application software
- why `Take-Two` and `Electronic Arts` are recurrent franchise-economy systems rather than ordinary software publishers

The site can place the companies in legacy buckets.

It cannot explain the actual cultural and monetization logic.
