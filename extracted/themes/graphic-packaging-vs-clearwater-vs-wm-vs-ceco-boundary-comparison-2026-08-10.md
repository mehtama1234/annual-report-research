# Graphic Packaging Versus Clearwater Versus WM Versus CECO Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the next direct comparison built from the behind-the-shelf consumer infrastructure blind-spot lane.

It focuses on the cleanest four-way split inside the physical support systems behind visible consumer demand:

- `Graphic Packaging`
- `Clearwater Paper`
- `Waste Management`
- `CECO`

The useful question is not whether these companies are industrial, materials, or service names.

The useful question is which hidden infrastructure layer each one actually controls:

- packaging conversion
- packaging substrate supply
- route-density environmental services
- plant-embedded environmental and process systems

## Executive read

The four companies sit in the same broad hidden-infrastructure family, but they do not occupy the same layer.

- `Graphic Packaging` is the packaging-conversion middleware case
- `Clearwater Paper` is the paperboard substrate and mill-execution case
- `Waste Management` is the route-density cleanup and compliance network case
- `CECO` is the plant-embedded environmental and process-reliability case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `Graphic Packaging`: consumer-packaging middleware
2. `Clearwater Paper`: paperboard substrate and packaging-materials infrastructure
3. `Waste Management`: environmental route-density and compliance infrastructure
4. `CECO`: environmental and process-reliability infrastructure

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by physical position inside the behind-the-shelf consumer system, moving from the package itself outward into post-use and plant-level support layers.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `Graphic Packaging` is the clearest conversion-layer case

Graphic Packaging is the nearest of the four to the visible consumer package.

Its role is built on converting paperboard into branded formats that reach the shelf, the refrigerator, the pantry, and foodservice environments.

The FY `2025` to Q2 `2026` chain shows the structure clearly:

- FY `2025` net sales of `$8.617B`
- FY `2025` net income of `$444M`
- FY `2025` total debt of `$5.592B`
- Q2 `2026` net sales of `$2.188B`
- Q2 `2026` adjusted EBITDA of `$247M`

The important analytical point is that Graphic Packaging is not a brand owner.

It is packaging middleware between branded demand and raw-material inputs, and the recent story is dominated by affordability pressure, footprint optimization, and post-capital-cycle cash discipline.

### 2. `Clearwater Paper` is the clearest substrate-layer case

Clearwater sits one layer further upstream.

It is less about the final converted package and more about the paperboard substrate feeding converters and carton demand.

The FY `2025` to Q2 `2026` chain makes that distinction clear:

- FY `2025` net sales from continuing operations of `$1.6B`
- FY `2025` adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations of `$107M`
- Q2 `2026` net sales of `$375M`
- Q2 `2026` adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations of `($8)M`
- Q2 `2026` sales volumes up `8%` while average net selling price fell `9%`

That is why Clearwater belongs second.

It proves the hidden infrastructure stack is not only about converted packaging.

It also depends on mill execution, outage timing, industry operating rates, and substrate pricing that most consumers never see.

### 3. `Waste Management` is the route-density cleanup and compliance case

WM widens the lane beyond making packaging or industrial inputs.

Its system starts after consumption and commercial use, where physical waste streams, route density, disposal economics, recycling, RNG, and healthcare compliance become the real infrastructure layer.

The FY `2025` to Q2 `2026` chain shows the scale and recurring quality:

- FY `2025` revenue of `$25.204B`
- FY `2025` adjusted operating EBITDA of `$7.582B`
- FY `2025` free cash flow of `$2.937B`
- Q2 `2026` revenue of `$6.684B`
- Q2 `2026` adjusted operating EBITDA of `$2.067B`

WM is third in this ordering because it is physically further from the package itself, but it is still one of the clearest consumer-adjacent support systems in the archive.

It shows that the consumer economy also depends on recurring removal, processing, compliance, and local-service density.

### 4. `CECO` is the plant-embedded environmental-control case

CECO sits deeper inside industrial and utility-adjacent facilities than the other three.

Its importance comes from operating inside the plants that need environmental control, process reliability, water handling, thermal management, and emissions systems.

The FY `2025` to Q2 `2026` chain shows the growth logic clearly:

- FY `2025` revenue of `$774.4M`
- FY `2025` adjusted EBITDA of `$90.3M`
- Q1 `2026` orders of `$449.5M`
- Q2 `2026` orders of `$798.5M`
- Q2 `2026` backlog of `$1.819B`
- Q2 `2026` revenue of `$285.0M`
- Q2 `2026` adjusted EBITDA of `$40.2M`

CECO is fourth in this ordering because it is the deepest plant-level layer in the set.

It matters less as a direct package or route-density name and more as the environmental-control infrastructure that supports industrial throughput, reshoring, power demand, and data-center-linked capacity.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the four is:

### `Graphic Packaging`: conversion between material and branded demand

Graphic Packaging matters most when the question is who shapes the final paperboard format that touches the consumer-facing brand.

The distinguishing asset is:

- converted packaging architecture
- customer packaging mix
- format innovation
- capital-intensive plant footprint

### `Clearwater Paper`: substrate economics and mill execution

Clearwater matters most when the question is who supplies the packaging material before conversion.

The distinguishing asset is:

- SBS paperboard supply
- outage execution
- operating-rate exposure
- converter-facing substrate economics

### `Waste Management`: recurring cleanup and compliance density

WM matters most when the question is who owns the removal, recycling, and compliance layer after use.

The distinguishing asset is:

- route density
- disposal and recycling infrastructure
- healthcare-waste compliance
- pricing discipline in recurring service networks

### `CECO`: process reliability and environmental control inside facilities

CECO matters most when the question is who keeps plants and utility-adjacent systems within environmental and operating constraints.

The distinguishing asset is:

- embedded air and water systems
- emissions and thermal control
- backlog-driven engineered projects
- environmental infrastructure tied to reshoring and data-center power needs

## What each company proves

### `Graphic Packaging`

`Graphic Packaging` proves that a large part of consumer demand depends on packaging conversion businesses that sit between brands and materials rather than in either category cleanly.

### `Clearwater Paper`

`Clearwater Paper` proves that the hidden packaging stack starts earlier than the carton and depends on substrate supply, mill uptime, and industry operating discipline.

### `Waste Management`

`Waste Management` proves that consumer-adjacent infrastructure is not only about making things. It is also about cleaning up, processing, and regulating the physical aftermath of consumption.

### `CECO`

`CECO` proves that modern consumer and digital demand still rely on plant-level environmental hardware and process systems that remain mostly invisible in surface-level sector taxonomies.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `Graphic Packaging` is the clearest conversion-layer anchor in the behind-the-shelf lane, not a normal consumer-brand case despite sitting in `Consumer Goods`
- `Clearwater Paper` is the clearest substrate-layer anchor, showing why material supply and outage execution belong in the same broader packaging stack as converted packaging
- `Waste Management` is the clearest route-density and compliance-network anchor, not just a defensive industrial-service bucket
- `CECO` is the clearest plant-embedded environmental-control anchor, especially because it ties environmental hardware to reshoring, power demand, and data-center-linked industrial capacity
- the practical comparison is not materials versus industrials versus services, but conversion versus substrate versus cleanup-network versus embedded environmental-control infrastructure
