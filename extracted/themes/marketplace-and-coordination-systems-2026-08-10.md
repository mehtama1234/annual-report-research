# Marketplace And Coordination Systems

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo covers one of the clearest `AnnualReports` blind spots: the source taxonomy can locate marketplace-like companies, but it does not organize them by the operating system that actually matters.

`Amazon`, `eBay`, `Etsy`, `Instacart`, `DoorDash`, `Uber`, `Airbnb`, `Booking`, and `Expedia` are not one category.

They are different coordination systems with different trust structures, frequencies, monetization layers, and physical-world dependencies.

## Executive read

The useful question is not whether a company is a marketplace.

The useful questions are:

- what kind of demand is being coordinated
- how often that demand repeats
- whether trust sits in the seller, the platform, the merchant network, or the logistics layer
- whether the platform owns inventory, fulfillment, payments, or only traffic
- what second monetization layer sits on top of the base transaction

The current archive now supports at least six distinct marketplace and coordination types:

- commerce flywheel marketplaces
- creator and enthusiast marketplaces
- grocery and local-retail aggregation platforms
- local delivery and mobility coordination platforms
- travel trust marketplaces
- travel intermediation and trip-orchestration marketplaces

That is the core blind-spot correction.

`AnnualReports` can call these businesses `Retail`, `Consumer Goods`, `Technology`, or `Services`.

What it does not show is that they are all coordination businesses, but not the same coordination business.

## Why this lane matters

The source taxonomy is weak here for three different reasons:

1. It mixes together very different platform structures under generic labels like `Application Software`, `Internet Service Providers`, or specialty retail.
2. It does not distinguish trust-heavy discovery marketplaces from convenience-heavy logistics marketplaces.
3. It does not organize around second monetization layers such as ads, seller tools, merchant services, payments, memberships, and fulfillment.

That means standard sector packetization can collect the filings but still miss the operating comparison that matters most.

## What each marketplace type really means

### 1. Commerce flywheel marketplace

`Amazon` is the thickest marketplace case in the archive because the relationship is not only buyer-to-seller matching.

It is an ecosystem where:

- shopping frequency
- delivery speed
- Prime
- third-party seller services
- advertising
- and fulfillment scale

all reinforce the same account relationship.

The Q2 `2026` evidence is the cleanest proof:

- advertising services at `$19.8B`
- subscription services at `$13.7B`
- third-party seller services at `$46.8B`

This is not just a marketplace with listings.

It is a commerce flywheel where logistics density and merchant monetization are as important as consumer traffic.

### 2. Creator and enthusiast marketplaces

`eBay` and `Etsy` belong together, but not because they are interchangeable.

They are both thinner than `Amazon`, but their thinness is structural rather than weak.

They coordinate demand through taste, uniqueness, trust, and seller supply rather than through owned-fulfillment density.

`eBay` is the stronger recommerce and enthusiast case:

- FY `2025` GMV of `$79.6B`
- Q2 `2026` advertising revenue of `$596M`
- explicit emphasis on collectibles, pre-loved fashion, live shopping, and focus categories

`Etsy` is the stronger creator-merchant and identity-commerce case:

- FY `2025` GMS of `$11.917B`
- FY `2025` revenue of `$2.884B`
- Q2 `2026` revenue take rate of `25.9%`
- repeated emphasis on unique goods, human connection, repeat buyers, app behavior, and Etsy Ads

The important distinction is:

- `eBay` organizes around enthusiast discovery, resale, and community supply
- `Etsy` organizes around uniqueness, self-expression, gifting, and seller identity

Both prove that marketplace durability does not require owned inventory if trust and supply quality are strong enough.

### 3. Grocery and retail-demand aggregation

`Instacart` is one of the most important blind-spot names because it is not just a consumer app.

It coordinates:

- household grocery demand
- retailer ecommerce
- store-level data
- retail-media spend
- white-label digital infrastructure

The `2025` to `2026` chain makes that explicit:

- FY `2025` GTV of `$37.224B`
- FY `2025` advertising and other revenue of `$1.065B`
- more than `2,200` retail banners and nearly `100,000` locations in `2025`
- more than `380` grocers on `Storefront` technology at year-end `2025`

That means Instacart is not best compared with generic internet companies.

It is better understood as a grocery-demand routing and retailer-enablement layer where ads, data, software, and fulfillment coordination sit on top of the base basket.

### 4. Local-commerce and fulfillment coordination

`DoorDash` is the clearest high-frequency local-commerce coordination system in the archive.

Its marketplace is not mainly about unique inventory or broad product search.

It is about:

- convenience habit
- merchant density
- subscription-like frequency
- local delivery coordination
- widening from restaurants into grocery and retail

The core evidence is straightforward:

- FY `2025` revenue of `$13.717B`
- Q2 `2026` Marketplace GOV of `$33.1B`
- Q2 `2026` orders of `970M`
- more than `35M` DashPass, Wolt+, and Deliveroo Plus members at year-end `2025`

This makes DoorDash structurally different from both `Amazon` and `eBay`.

The moat is not broader assortment or enthusiast trust.

It is app habit plus local logistics density plus merchant dependence on demand routing and tooling.

### 5. Mobility and local-demand coordination

`Uber` belongs in the same broad blind-spot lane as `DoorDash`, but it widens the system further.

Its real coordination layer spans:

- rides
- delivery
- membership
- local-demand wallet behavior
- driver and courier supply
- future autonomous-vehicle supply

The membership signal matters most:

- Q1 `2026` Uber One members reached `50M`
- those members drove roughly half of gross bookings across Mobility and Delivery

Alongside that:

- FY `2025` gross bookings reached `$193B`
- Q2 `2026` MAPCs reached `208M`
- Q2 `2026` trips reached `3.9B`

This is not a standard software company.

It is a recurring movement-and-fulfillment interface trying to own more of the consumer's local-demand life.

### 6. Travel marketplaces are no longer one subgroup

`Airbnb`, `Booking`, and `Expedia` all intermediate travel demand, but they are running different coordination systems.

`Airbnb` is the stronger trust-and-supply marketplace:

- over `5M` hosts by year-end `2025`
- more than `2.5B` guest arrivals by year-end `2025`
- FY `2025` gross booking value of `$91.3B`
- Q1 `2026` roughly `20%` of global GBV from `Reserve Now, Pay Later`

The engine is:

- host supply
- guest trust
- app usage
- flexible payments
- and widening into services, experiences, and hotel inventory

`Booking` is the stronger trip-orchestration and transaction-flow marketplace:

- FY `2025` revenue of `$26,917M`
- Q2 `2026` gross bookings of `$50,957M`
- Q2 `2026` merchant gross bookings of `$36,996M`
- merchant mix at `73%` in Q2 `2026`

The engine is:

- search and booking flow
- direct mix
- app behavior
- merchant payments
- flights, lodging, rental cars, and Connected Trip widening

`Expedia` is the stronger loyalty-thickened and partner-enabled travel intermediation marketplace:

- FY `2025` revenue of about `$14.733B`
- FY `2025` gross bookings of about `$119.590B`
- FY `2025` B2B gross bookings of about `$35.723B`
- FY `2025` Expedia Group advertising revenue of about `$758M`
- Q2 `2026` revenue of `$4.315B`
- Q2 `2026` B2B gross bookings growth of `21%`

The engine is:

- brand-portfolio travel demand
- `One Key` loyalty
- B2B partner distribution
- advertising around travel intent
- cross-brand traveler reuse

So the real split is:

- `Airbnb`: trust-heavy host-guest network plus trip-surface expansion
- `Booking`: transaction-heavy travel orchestration plus merchant and payments control
- `Expedia`: loyalty-thickened, B2B-enabled, and ad-network travel intermediation

## The most important distinctions inside the lane

The archive now supports several high-confidence splits that the source taxonomy does not express well.

### Trust discovery versus convenience density

- `Etsy`, `eBay`, and `Airbnb` depend more on trust, uniqueness, community supply, or identity-led discovery.
- `DoorDash`, `Instacart`, and `Uber` depend more on speed, convenience, routing density, and repeat local behavior.

### Asset-light coordination versus embedded physical execution

- `Booking`, `Airbnb`, `eBay`, and `Etsy` are relatively lighter on direct fulfillment embedding.
- `Amazon`, `DoorDash`, `Instacart`, and `Uber` are much more physically embedded in fulfillment, routing, or delivery infrastructure.

### Merchant monetization versus traveler or buyer monetization

- `Amazon`, `Instacart`, `eBay`, and `Etsy` have more visible seller, merchant, or advertising monetization layers.
- `Booking` and `Airbnb` monetize more directly through booking and payments orchestration.
- `DoorDash` and `Uber` sit in between, with both consumer memberships and merchant-side dependence becoming material.

### High-frequency everyday loops versus episodic trip behavior

- `Amazon`, `Instacart`, `DoorDash`, and `Uber` are closer to daily or weekly habit systems.
- `Airbnb` and `Booking` are more episodic, but can still be strong because the dollar value per transaction and trust surface are large.

## Blind-spot conclusion

The marketplace lane is not one lane.

It is a family of coordination systems that differ by:

- what demand is being routed
- how trust is created
- how deeply the platform enters the physical world
- and where the second monetization layer sits

That is exactly what `AnnualReports` does not classify cleanly.

The site can tell you where the filing was placed.

It usually cannot tell you whether the real business is:

- a commerce flywheel
- a creator-merchant trust network
- a grocery-demand and retailer-enablement layer
- a local-logistics coordination platform
- a movement-and-fulfillment wallet
- a trust-led travel marketplace
- a loyalty-thickened travel intermediary
- or a trip-orchestration marketplace

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- marketplace businesses should be compared by coordination structure, not by legacy sector label
- `Amazon`, `eBay`, `Etsy`, `Instacart`, `DoorDash`, `Uber`, `Airbnb`, `Booking`, and `Expedia` are all useful blind-spot anchors, but they are useful for different reasons
- the second monetization layer remains the best shorthand for marketplace quality once the base transaction is established
- physical embedding is one of the key dividing lines between lighter trust marketplaces and denser convenience or logistics marketplaces
- the best cross-sector read is no longer "software versus retail versus services"
- the better read is trust marketplace versus convenience marketplace versus merchant-enablement platform versus travel-trust system versus loyalty-thickened travel intermediation versus trip-orchestration system
