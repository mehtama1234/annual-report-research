# Mid-Thickness Relationship Systems

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Executive read

The archive now needs a middle category.

Some companies are clearly thick relationship systems:

- membership fees
- stored value
- co-brand economics
- explicit wallet continuity
- directly disclosed loyalty concentration

Some are clearly thinner:

- traffic-led
- price-led
- assortment-led
- discovery-led
- ancillary-led

But a meaningful group sits in between.

These companies do have owned or semi-owned relationship systems.

They often show:

- direct behavior
- app usage
- loyalty participation
- first-party demand capture
- service trust
- repeat workflow ownership
- channel or distribution control

What they usually do not show, at least not yet, is the same level of explicit financialization as the thickest systems.

That is what makes them mid-thickness.

## What mid-thickness means

### 1. The relationship matters, but the monetization is more indirect

Mid-thickness systems are not just brand stories and not just traffic stories.

They already shape behavior through:

- trust
- repeat digital use
- direct booking or ordering
- loyalty participation
- service quality
- network breadth
- workflow convenience

But the extra economics are often expressed more through:

- better mix
- better conversion
- higher direct share
- stronger repeat usage
- broader distribution power
- service attachment
- operational leverage

rather than through one giant disclosed member-fee, wallet-balance, or partner-remuneration line.

### 2. These systems are often easier to feel than to measure

The filings usually make the relationship visible, but less cleanly than in a Delta, Costco, or Caesars style case.

Examples:

- `United` repeatedly talks about brand-loyal customers, loyalty revenue growth, app usage, connectivity, and service reliability, but the main disclosed story still runs through premium mix, unit revenue, and network quality rather than one giant loyalty-economics number.
- `Hilton` clearly benefits from repeat branded-stay behavior and loyalty-linked distribution, but the headline operating frame is still RevPAR, fees, pipeline growth, and owner demand.
- `Booking` is explicit about direct mix, mobile app share, merchant shift, Genius participation, and Connected Trip transactions, but the relationship is monetized through marketplace and payments orchestration more than through a classical wallet currency.
- `Airbnb` clearly owns repeat app behavior and trust, but the widening shows up through payments flexibility, hotels, services, and conversion rather than through a formal points program.
- `Ulta` clearly uses loyalty-member data, marketplace assortment, salons, and media, but the disclosed results still flow primarily through category breadth, comps, store growth, and discovery-driven retail economics.
- `Lowe's` clearly wants deeper Pro, digital, services, and loyalty relationships, but the operating scorecard still centers on project spend, Pro penetration, online growth, and home-services attachment rather than on a thick formal membership loop.
- `Coca-Cola` clearly owns routine behavior, cultural activation, and increasingly first-party data capture, but the relationship layer is still mediated through pack-price-channel architecture and bottler execution more than through a visible app-wallet layer.

## What the archive now says

### 1. Mid-thickness systems often own workflow rather than wallet

The thickest systems often own the member or wallet directly.

The middle band more often owns a workflow:

- `United`: premium and operational air-travel workflow
- `Hilton`: branded-stay and owner-distribution workflow
- `Booking`: planning, booking, and payments workflow
- `Airbnb`: host-guest discovery and trip-flexibility workflow
- `Ulta`: beauty discovery and repeat self-care workflow
- `Lowe's`: project, Pro, and home-services workflow
- `Coca-Cola`: routine beverage occasion and channel-architecture workflow

That is a strong form of relationship ownership, but it is different from direct fee-funded membership or stored-value economics.

### 2. Mid-thickness systems usually widen through behavior quality, not only through explicit fees

The key signals are:

- direct mix
- app share
- loyalty participation
- service attachment
- merchant or payments shift
- media layer growth
- Pro penetration
- first-party data capture
- repeat-use trust

Examples:

- `Booking` shifts toward merchant revenue and keeps deepening direct mix, app usage, Genius participation, and Connected Trip behavior.
- `Airbnb` widens through app-booked nights, `Reserve Now, Pay Later`, hotel cross-use, services, and experiences.
- `Ulta` widens through UB Marketplace, UB Media, loyalty-member behavior, salons, app and e-commerce usage, and curated brand discovery.
- `Lowe's` widens through Pro, online, home services, loyalty, and distribution assets.
- `Coca-Cola` widens through global brand activation, pack-price-channel design, connected packaging, and first-party data capture.

These are real second layers, but they are not always as directly monetized or separately disclosed as thick wallet systems.

### 3. Mid-thickness systems are often the most important transition cases

This middle band matters because it shows where categories may be thickening.

They are not as explicit as the strongest membership cases, but they are no longer plain transactional or plain shelf-presence businesses either.

That makes them important for judging category direction:

- Is travel becoming more workflow-owned and app-mediated?
- Is specialty retail becoming more media-, loyalty-, and data-driven?
- Is home improvement becoming more Pro-, service-, and infrastructure-like?
- Are legacy beverage systems becoming more data-aware and directly activatable?

Those are bigger-picture questions that a standard industry taxonomy does not answer.

### 4. Mid-thickness is not one thing

The middle band contains several distinct subtypes:

- product-and-service trust relationship: `United`, `Hilton`
- marketplace and trip-workflow relationship: `Booking`, `Airbnb`
- discovery-and-data retail relationship: `Ulta`
- project-and-service relationship: `Lowe's`
- routine-use and cultural-distribution relationship: `Coca-Cola`

That means the archive should not treat mid-thickness as a vague in-between bucket.

It is a real category of models where the relationship is meaningful, but still mediated through workflow, channel architecture, or service trust rather than through a visibly financialized member engine.

### 5. Mid-thickness systems can become thick, stay mid, or fall back toward thin

This is one of the best analytical uses of the category.

Some mid-thickness systems may thicken over time:

- more direct monetization
- more explicit membership economics
- more partner-funded behavior layers
- more first-party data monetization

Others may remain mid-thickness because their best economics come from:

- service quality
- network breadth
- distribution scale
- workflow ownership
- cultural activation

That distinction matters because not every strong company should be expected to become a classic loyalty wallet system.

## What this means for the broader research project

- The archive now has a more usable three-level structure:
- thick systems
- mid-thickness systems
- thin systems

- This improves the blind-spot lane because it replaces a blunt loyalty versus non-loyalty split with a more realistic spectrum of relationship ownership.
- It also helps explain why very different companies can all be strategically strong without having the same disclosed economic surface.
- Most importantly, it gives the project a way to track which categories are becoming more relationship-owned over time even when they do not yet disclose a classic wallet or membership engine.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- Mid-thickness systems are a real and important category across travel, retail, home improvement, and routine consumer goods.
- These companies often own behavior through workflow, trust, direct engagement, or distribution control rather than through the thickest fee-funded or wallet-funded mechanics.
- The best comparison question is not simply "is this a loyalty company?" but "how directly is the company monetizing the relationship, and through what mechanism?"
- `AnnualReports` taxonomy does not reveal this middle band at all, which makes it a genuine blind spot for cross-company analysis.
