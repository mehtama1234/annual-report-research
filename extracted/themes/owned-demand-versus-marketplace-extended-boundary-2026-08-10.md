# Owned-Demand Versus Marketplace-Extended Boundary

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo separates another key `AnnualReports` blind spot: the boundary between true owned-demand systems and marketplace-extended or interface-control systems.

The source taxonomy can place `Levi Strauss`, `Crocs`, `YETI`, `Warby Parker`, `Ulta`, `Amazon`, and `eBay` into familiar industries.

It does not explain when demand is really owned by a brand or service system, and when the stronger economic reality is traffic, discovery, assortment, or interface control layered across many brands or sellers.

## Executive read

The useful distinction is not simply:

- direct-to-consumer versus not direct-to-consumer

The useful distinction is:

- who the customer thinks they are coming back to
- whether the demand is anchored in one brand or category authority
- whether the company mainly controls product demand or interface demand
- whether the second layer widens a brand relationship or replaces it with traffic and assortment control

That creates three main cases in the archive:

- true owned-demand systems
- hybrid owned-demand systems with marketplace-assisted reach
- marketplace-extended or interface-control systems

## Working boundary map

| Boundary type | Core names | What the customer mainly returns for | What the company mainly controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| True owned-demand system | `Levi Strauss`, `Crocs`, `Warby Parker` | one brand, one fit system, one identity or service relationship | brand presentation, first-party demand, merchandising, pricing, and customer-file depth |
| Hybrid owned-demand system with marketplace-assisted reach | `YETI`, `Nike`, `Deckers`, `Birkenstock`, `Victoria's Secret`, `Tapestry` | a brand or category authority that still clearly belongs to the company even when partners help widen reach | brand heat, product authority, channel balance, and selective partner or marketplace assistance |
| Marketplace-extended or interface-control system | `Ulta`, `Amazon`, `eBay` | assortment breadth, discovery, traffic, loyalty, marketplace participation, or interface utility across many brands or sellers | traffic, category discovery, seller or assortment access, services, media, and interface economics |

## The main decision test

When deciding whether a company belongs mainly in the owned-demand lane or the marketplace-extended lane, ask:

what exactly is owned?

### Mostly owned demand

Keep the company mainly in the owned-demand lane if the repeated relationship depends mostly on:

- one brand or house of brands
- fit, identity, service, or product authority owned by the company
- category control that would still matter even without a broader marketplace layer
- customer-file depth tied directly to the company's own brand promise

### Mostly marketplace-extended or interface control

Keep the company mainly in the marketplace-extended lane if the repeated relationship depends mostly on:

- assortment breadth across many brands
- seller or supply participation
- discovery and traffic
- media, marketplace, or service layers attached to an interface
- loyalty or utility that is broader than one owned brand

### Mixed cases

Use both lanes when the company clearly owns demand but also widens it through third-party assortment, marketplace reach, or platform features that materially shape the economics.

That is the right read for `YETI`, and to a lesser extent for `Ulta`.

## What the current names reveal

### 1. `Levi Strauss`, `Crocs`, and `Warby Parker` are clean owned-demand cases

These companies differ by category, but not by the core logic of the relationship.

The customer is returning mainly for:

- the company's own brand
- its fit or comfort promise
- its direct service or merchandising environment

`Levi Strauss` is returning through heritage, fit, and wardrobe authority.

`Crocs` is returning through ease, comfort, and recognizable brand identity.

`Warby Parker` is returning through eyewear fit, exams, contacts, and a direct vision-care relationship.

In all three cases, the second layer strengthens owned demand rather than replacing it with a broader interface economy.

### 2. `YETI` is the most useful hybrid case

`YETI` is not pure brand-store DTC.

The packet explicitly shows direct control across:

- direct digital surfaces
- retail stores
- corporate sales
- `YETI Authorized` on the Amazon Marketplace

That does not make the relationship marketplace-first.

It means marketplace reach is helping a clearly owned premium-utility demand system travel further.

So the right read is:

- owned demand with marketplace-assisted reach

not:

- interface-control business

### 3. `Ulta` is not a brand-owned demand case in the same way

`Ulta` has a strong relationship.

But the customer is not mainly returning for one owned brand system.

The repeated behavior is anchored in:

- assortment authority
- discovery
- replenishment
- services
- loyalty
- marketplace assortment
- media monetization

That makes `Ulta` stronger than a plain retailer, but different from a true owned-demand brand-control operator.

The relationship is powerful precisely because it sits above many brands rather than inside one.

### 4. `Amazon` and `eBay` are clearly interface-control systems

`Amazon` and `eBay` both touch product demand, but the return loop is not one-brand attachment.

`Amazon` owns:

- search
- utility
- Prime
- fulfillment
- marketplace participation
- ads

`eBay` owns:

- search
- listing
- discovery
- trust
- seller tools
- category culture

That means both are better treated as marketplace-extended or interface-control systems than as owned-demand businesses.

### 5. `Nike`, `Deckers`, `Birkenstock`, `Victoria's Secret`, and `Tapestry` remain brand-control hybrids

These names are not as DTC-pure as the cleanest owned-demand cases.

But the customer is still coming back mainly because of:

- the brand
- the fit system
- the performance or comfort promise
- visible status or emotional connection

Partner distribution and wholesale matter, but they widen a company-owned demand system rather than replacing it.

The archive now supports these practical distinctions:

### Owned demand first

## Why this matters for thread ownership

This is another place where a normal sector thread will usually miss the best comparison.

A standard packet can describe:

- an apparel company
- a beauty retailer
- an internet marketplace

But it will usually miss the deeper split between:

- owning product demand
- owning category or fit authority
- owning interface demand
- widening demand through marketplace or service layers

That is exactly why the blind-spot thread should keep first claim here.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- true owned demand is strongest when the customer is returning mainly for one brand, one fit system, or one service relationship
- marketplace reach can widen owned demand without replacing it, which is why `YETI` is such a useful hybrid case
- `Ulta` is powerful, but it is better understood as marketplace-extended or interface control than as pure brand-owned demand
- `Amazon` and `eBay` are traffic, discovery, and interface-control systems rather than owned-demand brand systems
- the most useful comparison is not brand versus retailer in the abstract, but owned demand versus interface demand
