# Platform And Ecosystem Map

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo covers another major `AnnualReports` blind spot: the source taxonomy does not describe platform and ecosystem businesses very well.

Some of the archive's most important companies are not best understood as simple retailers, media companies, software companies, or travel operators.

They are better understood as ecosystems that widen the same user relationship across multiple monetization layers.

## Executive read

The archive now supports a clearer platform conclusion.

The strongest ecosystem businesses tend to do three things at once:

- own a repeated interface
- widen that interface into adjacent services or monetization layers
- make the relationship more valuable than the original transaction

The important thing is that not all platforms are the same.

The current archive already contains at least four different ecosystem types:

- commerce and merchant flywheels
- trip and marketplace networks
- device and operating-system ecosystems
- recurring cultural-distribution and attention platforms

## Working platform map

| Ecosystem type | Core companies | Main owned interface | Main expansion layers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commerce and merchant flywheel | `Amazon`, `eBay`, `Walmart`, `Target`, `Ulta` | shopping, search, account, seller access, loyalty or membership | ads, marketplace or seller services, subscriptions, services, fulfillment, assortment expansion |
| Trip and marketplace network | `Airbnb`, `Booking`, `Expedia` | booking flow, app behavior, trust and discovery | payments flexibility, advertising, B2B distribution, services, experiences, hotels, ancillary trip surfaces |
| Device and operating-system ecosystem | `Apple` | device base, identity, software, app and services attachment | services monetization, subscriptions, payments, ecosystem lock-in, developer distribution |
| Attention, media, and cultural-distribution platform | `Netflix`, `Spotify`, `Alphabet`, `Meta` | recurring attention, discovery, entertainment, search, recommendation | ads, subscriptions, creator or partner distribution, AI assistance, enterprise or consumer extensions |

## What each ecosystem type means

### 1. Commerce and merchant flywheel

`Amazon` is the thickest ecosystem case in the current archive.

The company is not just monetizing a shopping trip.

It is widening the same relationship across:

- Prime
- delivery speed
- third-party seller services
- advertising
- grocery and everyday essentials
- AWS-supported investment capacity

The key evidence is visible in the trailing quarter chain:

- Q2 `2026` advertising services were `$19.8B`
- subscription services were `$13.7B`
- third-party seller services were `$46.8B`

That means the customer relationship is not only a retail relationship. It is also a merchant and ad-inventory relationship.

`eBay` shows a thinner but still durable marketplace version.

It does not rely on Prime-like membership or owned fulfillment density.

Instead it compounds through:

- seller supply
- enthusiast discovery
- collectibles and recommerce
- ad monetization
- trust features

This is why the archive should not treat all marketplaces as Amazon-lite.

`Walmart`, `Target`, and `Ulta` add adjacent versions of the same general logic.

- `Walmart` is widening through advertising, membership, marketplace, and fulfillment services
- `Target` is doing the same through Roundel, Target Circle 360, same-day delivery, and Target+
- `Ulta` widens a beauty relationship through marketplace assortment, media, salons, and discovery

The shared structure is clear:

the platform becomes stronger when traffic or loyalty can be monetized more than once.

### 2. Trip and marketplace network

`Airbnb` is one of the strongest taxonomy-breaker names in the entire repo.

AnnualReports classifies it under `Technology / Application Software`, but the operating reality is a travel, trust, and marketplace network.

Its ecosystem is built on:

- over `5M` hosts
- more than `2.5B` guest arrivals by year-end `2025`
- app-led booking behavior
- trust systems
- flexible-payments features like `Reserve Now, Pay Later`
- expansion into services, experiences, and hotels

This is not just a lodging marketplace.

It is a trip-surface ecosystem where the company is trying to own more of the journey without owning the underlying assets.

`Booking` and `Expedia` are structurally similar but monetize a different version of the same behavior:

- `Booking` emphasizes merchant mix, alternative accommodations, Connected Trip, and payments-linked orchestration
- `Expedia` adds One Key loyalty, B2B travel distribution, and an advertising network on top of flagship consumer brands

The useful split is:

- `Airbnb`: network trust, app behavior, community supply, flexible payments
- `Booking` / `Expedia`: travel intermediation, branded trip planning, loyalty, B2B and ad surfaces

### 3. Device and operating-system ecosystem

`Apple` is the clearest device-ecosystem case in the archive.

The main asset is not only the hardware sale.

It is the installed base:

- more than `2.5B` active devices

That installed base then widens into:

- Services growth
- a Services gross margin of `75.4%` in `2025`
- software and platform lock-in
- repeat hardware refresh behavior
- developer and distribution leverage

This is one of the strongest examples in the archive where the physical product is only the access point.

The real value sits in the ongoing operating system around the user.

This is why Apple belongs in the same blind-spot lane as other ecosystems even though the surface product is a device.

### 4. Attention, media, and cultural-distribution platform

This is the broadest ecosystem lane in the current archive.

`Netflix`, `Spotify`, `Alphabet`, and `Meta` all monetize repeated attention, but they do it through different mechanisms.

`Netflix` is the cleanest recurring-access entertainment platform.

- paid memberships crossed `325M` in Q4 `2025`
- ad revenue rose more than `2.5x` to over `$1.5B` in `2025`

The key point is that paid habit broadened into advertising without breaking the core subscription model.

`Spotify` shows a different version of the same ecosystem pattern:

- `777M` MAUs
- `300M` Premium subscribers in Q2 `2026`
- ad-supported MAUs at `494M`

Spotify matters because the same relationship is monetized through:

- subscriptions
- advertising
- creator distribution
- podcasts
- audiobooks
- personalization

`Alphabet` is the largest and most vertically integrated platform in the current set.

Its ecosystem spans:

- Search
- YouTube
- subscriptions above `325M`
- Cloud
- Gemini distribution
- TPUs and AI infrastructure

This is important because Alphabet shows a platform where consumer attention and enterprise infrastructure sit inside the same broader stack.

`Meta` is the thinner version of attention ownership in the repo's current framework, but it remains useful because it shows how daily attention can still support very large ad economics even when the monetization mix is narrower than Alphabet's.

## The most important platform split

The strongest split is not consumer versus enterprise.

It is:

- platforms that monetize traffic and merchants
- platforms that monetize trust and trip orchestration
- platforms that monetize installed base and ecosystem lock-in
- platforms that monetize recurring attention and cultural discovery

This is a better way to compare these companies than the source sector labels.

## Second monetization layer by platform type

| Ecosystem type | Main second layer |
|---|---|
| Commerce and merchant flywheel | ads, seller services, subscriptions, marketplace economics, fulfillment |
| Trip and marketplace network | payments flexibility, B2B distribution, advertising, experiences, adjacent trip services |
| Device and operating-system ecosystem | services, app and software monetization, subscriptions, payments, developer leverage |
| Attention, media, and cultural-distribution platform | ads, subscriptions, creator tools, AI assistance, enterprise or consumer extensions |

## Cultural and societal read

The platform and ecosystem layer is also a behavioral layer.

These businesses matter because they increasingly become part of daily or recurring life:

- `Amazon`, `Walmart`, `Target`: ambient utility and household infrastructure
- `Airbnb`, `Booking`, `Expedia`: trip planning and travel behavior
- `Apple`: device-mediated digital life
- `Netflix`, `Spotify`, `Alphabet`, `Meta`: discovery, entertainment, communication, search, and attention allocation
- `eBay`: enthusiast culture, resale, and category-specific discovery
- `Ulta`: beauty discovery, loyalty, and service-mediated assortment

This means ecosystems do not just capture demand.

They reorganize how consumers move through categories.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- the archive already supports a strong platform-and-ecosystem layer that cuts across retail, travel, media, and technology
- the best ecosystems widen the same user relationship into multiple monetization layers rather than depending on one revenue line
- not all platforms are the same; commerce flywheels, trip networks, device ecosystems, and cultural-distribution platforms should not be flattened into one category
- `Airbnb` is one of the clearest live examples of source taxonomy failing to describe economic reality
- `Amazon` remains the clearest commerce flywheel in the archive because Prime, merchants, ads, and delivery density reinforce one another
- `Apple` is the clearest installed-base ecosystem case because devices are the entry point and Services is the widening layer
- `Netflix`, `Spotify`, and `Alphabet` show that recurring attention can be monetized through different mixes of subscriptions, ads, discovery, and infrastructure
