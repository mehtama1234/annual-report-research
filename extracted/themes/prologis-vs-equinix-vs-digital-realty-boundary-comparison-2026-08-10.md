# Prologis Versus Equinix Versus Digital Realty Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the next direct comparison built from the modern real-estate and infrastructure blind-spot lane.

It focuses on the cleanest three-way split inside infrastructure-like property ownership:

- `Prologis`
- `Equinix`
- `Digital Realty`

The useful question is not whether all three are REITs.

The useful question is what kind of bottleneck-bearing property system each one actually owns:

- logistics land and warehouse infrastructure
- interconnection-dense digital campuses
- hyperscale and powered-capacity datacenter infrastructure

## Executive read

The three companies sit in the same broad infrastructure-like ownership family, but they do not own the same kind of property economics.

- `Prologis` is the logistics land and occupancy-durable distribution case
- `Equinix` is the recurring interconnection and ecosystem-density campus case
- `Digital Realty` is the powered-capacity, hyperscale-leasing, and backlog-conversion case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `Prologis`: logistics land and warehouse infrastructure
2. `Equinix`: interconnection-dense digital-campus infrastructure
3. `Digital Realty`: hyperscale and powered-land digital-capacity infrastructure

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by what the owned property most directly bottlenecks in the built environment, moving from physical goods flow to dense digital exchange to large-scale powered compute capacity.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `Prologis` is the clearest logistics and strategic-land case

Prologis is the least ambiguous infrastructure-property name in the trio.

Its filings remain anchored in:

- lease commencements
- occupancy durability
- rent growth
- strategic capital
- scarce logistics land

The `Q4 2025` through `Q2 2026` chain makes that system clear:

- Q4 `2025` Core FFO per diluted share of `$1.44`
- Q1 `2026` Core FFO per diluted share of `$1.50`
- Q2 `2026` Core FFO per diluted share of `$1.63`
- occupancy remaining in the `95.3%` to `95.8%` range across the window
- more than `64M` to `67M` square feet of record leasing highlighted in Q1 and Q2 `2026`
- data-center power pipeline rising from `5.7 GW` in Q4 `2025` to `5.8 GW` in Q2 `2026`

That is why Prologis is first in this comparison.

It is the cleanest proof that some property behaves like scarce logistics and infrastructure land rather than like a generic real-estate beta bucket.

### 2. `Equinix` is the clearest ecosystem-density and interconnection case

Equinix differs because the core asset is not only powered space.

It is a dense ecosystem where networks, clouds, enterprises, and AI workloads already meet.

The FY `2025` through Q2 `2026` chain makes that distinct model visible:

- FY `2025` revenue of `$9.217B`
- FY `2025` operating income of `$1.848B`
- FY `2025` AFFO per share of `$38.33`
- more than `500,000` interconnections by year-end `2025`
- Q2 `2026` revenue of `$2.625B`
- Q2 `2026` adjusted EBITDA of `$1.396B`
- Q2 `2026` AFFO per share of `$11.78`
- record `9,700` net interconnections added in Q2 `2026`

Equinix is second because it is the clearest digital-campus ownership case where the moat is ecosystem density and switching friction, not only raw capacity or standard occupancy.

### 3. `Digital Realty` is the clearest powered-capacity and backlog case

Digital Realty differs from Equinix because the forward signal is more visibly tied to:

- hyperscale leasing
- backlog growth
- powered land
- private-capital-enabled development

The FY `2025` through Q2 `2026` chain shows that clearly:

- Q4 `2025` revenue of about `$1.6B`
- Q4 `2025` Core FFO per share of `$1.86`
- Q4 `2025` total bookings of `$400M` annualized GAAP rent at `100%` share
- backlog at share of `$817M` in Q4 `2025`
- Q1 `2026` total bookings of `$707M` annualized GAAP rent at `100%` share
- backlog at share of `$1.0B` in Q1 `2026`
- Q2 `2026` revenue of about `$1.9B`
- Q2 `2026` total bookings of `$307M` annualized GAAP rent at `100%` share, plus another `$410M` of July hyperscale leases
- backlog at share of `$1.4B` in Q2 `2026`

Digital Realty is third because this ordering moves from goods-flow land to ecosystem-dense digital campuses to the broadest powered-capacity and hyperscale backlog model.

It is still a strong taxonomy-breaker because `REIT - Office Real Estate` is materially weaker than the actual operating reality.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `Prologis`: scarce logistics land first

Prologis matters most when the question is who owns the high-value physical land and warehouse layer needed to move goods efficiently.

The distinguishing asset is:

- logistics locations
- occupancy durability
- rent spreads
- development rights with growing power optionality

### `Equinix`: ecosystem-density campus ownership first

Equinix matters most when the question is who owns the neutral digital exchange hubs where customers pay for dense proximity and interconnection.

The distinguishing asset is:

- interconnections
- MRR
- booking velocity
- switching friction created by campus density

### `Digital Realty`: powered-capacity and hyperscale backlog first

Digital Realty matters most when the question is who owns large-scale capacity and monetizes it through backlog, hyperscale leasing, and private-capital-enabled development.

The distinguishing asset is:

- powered land
- hyperscale demand
- backlog conversion
- large-capacity development execution

## What each company proves

### `Prologis`

`Prologis` proves that logistics property can behave more like scarce infrastructure than like a generic REIT asset pool.

### `Equinix`

`Equinix` proves that some real estate is really a recurring digital-presence and interconnection platform with buildings as the asset base.

### `Digital Realty`

`Digital Realty` proves that a datacenter owner can be better understood through powered-capacity backlog and hyperscale development than through old office-property taxonomy.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `Prologis` is the clearest logistics-land and occupancy-durable infrastructure case in the built-environment lane
- `Equinix` is the clearest interconnection-density and ecosystem-campus case, not a generic specialty REIT
- `Digital Realty` is the clearest powered-capacity and hyperscale-backlog case, not a meaningful fit for `REIT - Office Real Estate`
- the practical comparison is not industrial REIT versus specialty REIT versus office REIT, but logistics bottleneck ownership versus interconnection campus ownership versus powered-capacity backlog ownership
- the right current order is `Prologis` first on logistics infrastructure clarity, `Equinix` second on interconnection-campus clarity, and `Digital Realty` third on powered-capacity backlog ownership
