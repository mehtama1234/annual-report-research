# Recurring Behavior Crosswalk

Date baseline: 2026-08-09

## Scope

This memo links the current consumer-interface and financial-interface work into one shared framework:

- [Consumer Interface Framework Across Sectors](/home/manishmehta/ui-projects/annual-report-research/extracted/themes/consumer-interface-framework-cross-sector-2026-08-09.md)
- [Financial Interface Framework](/home/manishmehta/ui-projects/annual-report-research/extracted/financial/themes/financial-interface-framework-2026-08-09.md)

It is based on the current `2025` annual reports plus late-`2025` and `2026` quarterlies already packetized in the archive.

## Executive read

The strongest repo-wide conclusion now is that the best businesses across both consumer sectors and financial sectors are increasingly organized around recurring behavior.

The behavior looks different by domain:

- consumer sectors capture shopping, replenishment, ordering, viewing, travel, and entertainment routines
- financial sectors capture deposits, spend, payments, asset allocation, underwriting, servicing, and benchmark usage

But the underlying structure is similar:

- a repeated behavior or workflow
- an owned interface that keeps the customer or client inside that behavior
- a second layer of economics built on top of the repeated interaction

That means the repo is now pointing to one general question that works across multiple sectors:

What recurring behavior does the company control, and how many monetization layers sit on top of that control?

## Shared structure across the archive

### 1. Repetition is the core asset in both domains

In consumer sectors, the repeat loop often looks like:

- stock-up trips
- essentials replenishment
- off-price discovery
- daily food or beverage occasions
- recurring subscription viewing
- repeat travel and entertainment engagement

In financial sectors, the repeat loop often looks like:

- deposits and payments
- card spend and merchant acceptance
- recurring risk transfer
- retirement and savings allocation
- custody and servicing workflows
- fundraising, origination, and capital deployment
- benchmark and clearing dependence

The common point is that the best businesses become part of the customer’s or client’s normal operating rhythm.

### 2. Interface ownership matters more than the raw product

The archive now has enough evidence to say this directly.

In consumer sectors:

- `Costco`, `Walmart`, and `Amazon` own the shopping or platform interface
- `Starbucks`, `McDonald's`, and `Domino's` own the food-ordering or visit interface
- `Netflix` owns a recurring entertainment interface
- `Delta`, `Hilton`, and `Caesars` own different travel, stay, status, or wallet-linked interfaces

In financial sectors:

- `JPMorgan`, `Bank of America`, and `Citigroup` own parts of the payments, treasury, and balance-sheet interface
- `American Express` owns a premium spend and merchant-network interface
- `BlackRock` and `State Street` own allocation, implementation, custody, and servicing interfaces
- `Apollo`, `KKR`, `Ares`, and peers own fundraising, origination, and liability-linked capital interfaces
- `CME` and `S&P Global` own benchmark, clearing, and financial-information interfaces

So in both domains, the product is increasingly less important than the position inside the repeated workflow.

### 3. The second monetization layer is the strongest quality marker

This is one of the most stable patterns in the repo.

Consumer-side examples:

- `Amazon`: subscriptions, ads, seller services
- `Walmart`, `Kroger`, `Best Buy`: media, marketplace, services
- `Starbucks`: stored value and rewards
- `McDonald's`: loyalty-member sales and active-user scale
- `Domino's`: digital-fee economics
- `Netflix`: advertising
- `Delta`: co-brand and miles economics
- `Caesars`: wallet-linked digital wagering
- `P&G`, `Kimberly-Clark`, `Colgate`, `PepsiCo`: mix and trust-based repeat-purchase resilience

Financial-side examples:

- `JPMorgan`, `Bank of America`, `Citigroup`: payments, markets, treasury, and wealth layered on top of scale
- `American Express`: merchant economics, fees, travel, and network data layered on top of spend
- `Chubb`: investment float layered on top of underwriting
- `BlackRock` and `State Street`: technology, data, servicing, and administration layered on top of assets
- `Apollo`, `KKR`, `Ares`, `Blackstone`, `Carlyle`: insurance-linked liabilities, fee-paying AUM, origination, wealth distribution, and spread economics layered on top of capital formation
- `CME` and `S&P Global`: licensing, analytics, clearing, and data layered on top of market usage

The consistent lesson is that better businesses widen the relationship until the base transaction is only the first layer.

### 4. Consumer routines and financial routines often reinforce one another

The crosswalk is especially useful because the consumer and financial sides are not isolated.

Examples:

- `Delta` and `American Express` both benefit from affluent travel and status-linked spend behavior.
- `Costco`, `Walmart`, `Kroger`, and `McDonald's` all sit on top of repeated household spending patterns that also flow through payment systems and card networks.
- `Starbucks` stored value and rewards mechanics are a consumer-facing example of how payment-linked habit can create working-capital and retention advantages.
- `Netflix` subscription continuity is a consumer-attention version of the same recurring-billing logic that makes financial autopay, card-on-file, and spend-network economics more durable.
- `BlackRock`, `State Street`, and public asset managers ultimately sit downstream of the same household savings, retirement, and wealth-allocation behavior that begins in the broader consumer economy.

So one benefit of the crosswalk is that it shows how consumer behavior often becomes financial behavior one layer later.

### 5. The main split is routine type, not only sector

The archive increasingly supports this as the more useful organizing frame.

Consumer-side routine types:

- membership and stock-up
- replenishment
- ordering flow
- bargain discovery
- subscription attention
- status travel
- wallet-linked entertainment
- trusted routine use

Financial-side routine types:

- deposits and payments
- premium spend and network usage
- underwriting and float
- public-markets distribution and servicing
- alternative capital manufacturing
- market and benchmark infrastructure

That lets the repo compare very different companies without flattening them into generic sectors.

## Working crosswalk

| Consumer-side interface | Financial-side analogue | Shared recurring logic | Example companies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Membership and utility interface | Deposit and payments interface | repeated high-frequency usage creates a durable base for additional economics | Costco, Walmart, Amazon / JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citigroup |
| Ordering-flow interface | Affluent spend and network interface | the active transaction flow is owned and monetized beyond the base purchase | Starbucks, McDonald's, Domino's / American Express |
| Subscription-attention interface | Public-markets distribution and servicing interface | recurring engagement supports layered monetization and higher switching costs | Netflix / BlackRock, State Street |
| Status-and-travel interface | Affluent spend and network interface | prestige, status, and premium behavior create stronger relationship economics | Delta, Hilton / American Express |
| Wallet-linked entertainment interface | Deposit and payments or market-infrastructure interface | one account or wallet surface keeps the user inside a multi-step value loop | Caesars / JPMorgan, Citi, CME |
| Trusted routine-use interface | Underwriting and float or public-markets platform interface | repeated trust and low-friction reuse underpin durable economics without flashy front-end software | P&G, Kimberly-Clark, Colgate / Chubb, T. Rowe, Franklin |
| Replenishment interface | Alternative capital manufacturing interface | repeat need and repeated allocation create scaling advantages for distribution and product architecture | Kroger, Dollar General, Dollar Tree, PepsiCo / Apollo, KKR, Ares, Blackstone |

## Most important takeaways for the broader project

- The repo now has a sector-agnostic way to compare businesses across consumer and financial categories.
- The strongest names in both domains tend to own a recurring behavior first and sell products second.
- The second monetization layer remains the cleanest marker of business-model quality across both consumer and financial sectors.
- This crosswalk should make future sector work more coherent because new names can be slotted into an interface or recurring-behavior type instead of only a formal industry bucket.
