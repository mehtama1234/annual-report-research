# Sonoco Versus Aptar Versus Silgan Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the next narrower extension built from the behind-the-shelf consumer infrastructure blind-spot lane after the broader:

- `Graphic Packaging`
- `Clearwater Paper`
- `Waste Management`
- `CECO`

comparison and the narrower plant-embedded and waste sub-branches were established.

The useful question is not whether all three companies sit somewhere inside `Packaging & Containers`.

The useful question is which packaging-control layer each one actually owns:

- broader multi-format packaging and container control across a wide portfolio of consumer and industrial end markets
- dispensing, dosing, sealing, protection, and drug-delivery interface control between a packaged product and the user's actual behavior
- rigid-container, metal-can, and closure-system concentration tied to staple packaged-goods throughput

## Executive read

All three companies monetize packaging control, but they do not control the same layer.

- `Sonoco` is the broader packaging-format and container-control case
- `AptarGroup` is the dispensing, dosing, and product-use interface case
- `Silgan` is the rigid-container, metal-can, and closure-stack concentration case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `Sonoco`: broader packaging-format and container-control infrastructure
2. `AptarGroup`: packaging-interface and dispensing-component control infrastructure
3. `Silgan`: rigid-container and closure-control infrastructure

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by how broadly each company controls packaging formats before the comparison narrows into the user interface and then into staple rigid-container and closure concentration.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `Sonoco` is the clearest broader packaging-control case

Sonoco is the cleanest proof that the packaging branch cannot be reduced to cartons, paperboard substrate, or one narrow container type.

The FY `2025` through Q2 `2026` chain shows the operating shape clearly:

- FY `2025` GAAP net income attributable to Sonoco of `$1.003B`
- FY `2025` GAAP operating profit of `$1.018B`
- approximately `265` locations in `37` countries
- Q1 `2026` net sales of `$1.7B`
- Q2 `2026` net sales of `$1.9B`

The useful read is not "paper packaging."

The useful read is a broad packaging-control system spanning:

- metal packaging
- rigid paper containers and paper cans
- protective formats
- industrial paper packaging
- consumer packaging across food, beverage, household, and industrial end markets

That is why `Sonoco` is first in this comparison.

### 2. `AptarGroup` is the clearest product-use interface case

Aptar matters because it shows that packaging control can become much smaller, more engineered, more health-adjacent, and much closer to the actual use moment than broader packaging portfolios.

The FY `2025` through Q2 `2026` chain shows that clearly:

- FY `2025` net sales of `$3.777B`
- FY `2025` net income of `$392.5M`
- FY `2025` `Pharma` represented about `46%` of sales and about `69%` of adjusted EBITDA excluding non-allocated corporate costs
- Q1 `2026` sales of `$982.9M`
- Q2 `2026` sales of `$1.03B`

The management framing is also structurally different from Sonoco and Silgan.

Aptar is not mainly selling a broader format map or staple cans and containers. It is embedding itself in:

- drug and consumer-product dosing
- injectable and nasal-delivery components
- prestige-fragrance and skincare dispensing
- beverage and food closures
- active material science and product protection

That makes Aptar second because it sits between broader packaging portfolios and narrower rigid-container concentration, while controlling the highest-trust user interface in the set.

### 3. `Silgan` is the clearest rigid-container and closure-stack case

Silgan matters because it shows that packaging control can also narrow into a highly concentrated staple-goods system built around closures, cans, and rigid containers rather than broad portfolios or engineered use interfaces.

The FY `2025` through Q2 `2026` chain shows the structure clearly:

- FY `2025` net sales of `$6.5B`
- FY `2025` net income of `$288.4M`
- `Dispensing and Specialty Closures` generated about `$2.7B` of `2025` sales
- `Metal Containers` generated about `$3.1B` of `2025` sales
- Q1 `2026` net sales of `$1.561B`
- Q2 `2026` net sales of `$1.643B`

The useful read is not "another packaging company."

The useful read is concentration around:

- dispensing and specialty closures
- metal food containers
- custom rigid containers
- strong pet-food and staple packaged-goods throughput

That makes Silgan third because this ordering moves from broad format control into the interface layer and then into a staple-heavy rigid-container and closure stack.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `Sonoco`: broad packaging-control first

Sonoco matters most when the question is who owns a wide packaging and container map across multiple materials, formats, and end markets.

The distinguishing asset is:

- multi-format breadth
- consumer and industrial packaging reach
- portfolio reshaping and integration
- broad packaging-control relationships rather than one narrow container type

### `AptarGroup`: interface control first

Aptar matters most when the question is who controls the point where a consumer or patient actually doses, pumps, sprays, squeezes, seals, re-closes, or applies the product.

The distinguishing asset is:

- dispensing and dosing systems
- closures, pumps, sprays, and valves
- pharmaceutical and consumer-health qualification
- prestige, healthcare, and beverage interface trust

### `Silgan`: staple rigid-container concentration first

Silgan matters most when the question is who controls rigid packaging concentration across essential-goods closures, cans, and custom containers.

The distinguishing asset is:

- dispensing and specialty closures
- metal food containers
- custom rigid containers
- staple-goods throughput and preservation reliability

## What each company proves

### `Sonoco`

`Sonoco` proves that packaging control is a wide operating layer that can span formats, materials, and end markets rather than sitting only at the carton or paperboard stage.

### `AptarGroup`

`AptarGroup` proves that the packaging branch also has a high-trust interface layer where value is created by controlled dosing, sealing, delivery, and product-use performance rather than by broader format breadth alone.

### `Silgan`

`Silgan` proves that another distinct packaging-control layer exists between those two poles: a staple-heavy rigid-container and closure stack built on everyday throughput, preservation, and product integrity.

## Why the taxonomy fails here

`AnnualReports` can place all three companies in `Consumer Goods / Packaging & Containers`.

That is enough for source collection.

It is weak for analysis.

The stronger distinction is:

- broad packaging-format and container control versus
- packaging-interface and dispensing control versus
- rigid-container and closure concentration

Those are different monetization choke points inside the same hidden packaging layer.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `Sonoco` is the clearest broad packaging-format and container-control anchor in this branch
- `AptarGroup` is the clearest dispensing, dosing, and product-use interface counterpoint
- `Silgan` is the clearest staple rigid-container, metal-can, and closure-stack counterpoint to both
- the useful comparison is not one packaging company versus another, but broad format control versus interface control versus rigid-container concentration
- this three-way split is the cleanest current proof that the packaging branch of the behind-the-shelf lane can be broken into reusable subtypes instead of left as one generic `Packaging & Containers` bucket
