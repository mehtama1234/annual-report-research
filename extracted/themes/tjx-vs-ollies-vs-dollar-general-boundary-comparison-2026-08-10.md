# TJX Versus Ollie's Versus Dollar General Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the next direct comparison built from the value-hunt and bargain-discovery blind-spot lane.

It focuses on the cleanest three-way split inside consumer value behavior systems:

- `TJX`
- `Ollie's`
- `Dollar General`

The useful question is not whether all three sell at value prices.

The useful question is what kind of value behavior each company actually organizes:

- branded off-price treasure hunt
- closeout and excess-inventory discovery
- neighborhood essentials and fill-in convenience

## Executive read

The three companies sit in the same broad value-seeking family, but they do not monetize the same consumer draw.

- `TJX` is the scaled branded off-price and treasure-hunt case
- `Ollie's` is the lower-ticket closeout and opportunistic-discovery case
- `Dollar General` is the essentials-heavy fill-in convenience case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `TJX`: scaled off-price treasure-hunt system
2. `Ollie's`: closeout and excess-inventory discovery system
3. `Dollar General`: neighborhood essentials and fill-in convenience system

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by discovery intensity versus convenience intensity inside the broader value-behavior blind spot.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `TJX` is the clearest scaled off-price discovery case

TJX is the strongest large-scale proof in the archive that value behavior can be driven by discovery, not only by necessity.

Its filing set is explicit that the core draw is branded merchandise sold at prices generally `20%` to `60%` below comparable full-price retailers.

The fiscal `2026` through `Q1 FY27` chain shows how strong that system is:

- fiscal `2026` net sales of `$60.4B`
- fiscal `2026` comparable sales growth of `5%`
- fiscal `2026` net income of `$5.5B`
- fiscal `2026` diluted EPS of `$4.87`
- over `5,200` stores at fiscal year-end
- `Q1 FY27` net sales of `$14.3B`
- `Q1 FY27` comparable sales growth of `6%`
- `Q1 FY27` diluted EPS of `$1.19`

That is why TJX is first in this comparison.

It is the clearest case where bargain discovery is already a scaled primary retail system, not a weak substitute for other channels.

### 2. `Ollie's` is the clearest closeout and opportunistic-discovery case

Ollie's differs from TJX because the model is less apparel-and-home-fashion off-price and more closeout, excess inventory, and lower-ticket bargain excitement.

The fiscal `2025` through `Q1 FY2026` chain makes that structure explicit:

- fiscal `2025` net sales of `$2.649B`
- fiscal `2025` comparable store sales growth of `3.7%`
- fiscal `2025` adjusted EBITDA of `$366.0M`
- record `86` stores opened in fiscal `2025`
- `Ollie's Army` growing to about `17.0M` members at year-end and `17.5M` in `Q1 FY2026`
- `Q1 FY2026` net sales of `$658.9M`
- `Q1 FY2026` comparable store sales growth of `1.7%`
- `Q1 FY2026` adjusted EBITDA of `$87.9M`

Ollie's is second because it is the clearest proof that closeout-based and excess-inventory-based discovery is its own durable value system.

The customer is not mainly coming for fashion-off-price thrill or for pure essentials convenience.

The customer is coming for low-ticket deal discovery in a more opportunistic and flexible closeout environment.

### 3. `Dollar General` is the clearest essentials and proximity case

Dollar General differs from both because the relationship is built far more on convenience and consumables density than on surprise-driven discovery.

The fiscal `2025` through `Q1 2026` chain shows that clearly:

- fiscal `2025` net sales of `$42.7B`
- fiscal `2025` same-store sales growth of `3.0%`
- fiscal `2025` operating profit of `$2.2B`
- fiscal `2025` diluted EPS of `$6.85`
- `20,893` stores at fiscal year-end
- consumables at `81.0%` of fiscal `2025` sales
- `Q1 2026` net sales of `$10.8B`
- `Q1 2026` same-store sales growth of `2.0%`
- `Q1 2026` diluted EPS of `$2.00`

Dollar General is third because the system is least discovery-led.

It is still firmly part of the blind spot, but the core behavior is neighborhood fill-in, speed, and small-basket essentials control rather than branded off-price or closeout excitement.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `TJX`: branded bargain excitement first

TJX matters most when the question is who owns recurring traffic through recognizable brands, changing assortment, and a credible off-price treasure-hunt experience.

The distinguishing asset is:

- branded price gaps
- merchandise freshness
- dense store traffic
- global flexible buying

### `Ollie's`: closeout opportunism first

Ollie's matters most when the question is who turns excess inventory and closeout sourcing into repeat lower-ticket discovery behavior.

The distinguishing asset is:

- closeout buying
- opportunistic sourcing
- low-ticket deal excitement
- flexible merchandise mix across everyday categories

### `Dollar General`: proximity and essentials control first

Dollar General matters most when the question is who owns value behavior through location density, consumables mix, and fill-in convenience rather than through high-discovery treasure hunting.

The distinguishing asset is:

- store proximity
- consumables density
- small-basket utility
- format productivity through remodels and execution

## What each company proves

### `TJX`

`TJX` proves that off-price discovery and treasure-hunt behavior can scale into a massive mainstream retail system.

### `Ollie's`

`Ollie's` proves that closeout and excess-inventory discovery are not just clearance mechanics; they can form a durable repeat consumer draw on their own.

### `Dollar General`

`Dollar General` proves that value behavior can stay strong even with a thinner formal relationship when proximity, essentials, and quick low-ticket trips are credible enough.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `TJX` is the clearest scaled off-price treasure-hunt case, not a useful fit for plain `Apparel Stores`
- `Ollie's` is the clearest closeout and excess-inventory discovery case, not a generic discount-variety-store line item
- `Dollar General` is the clearest essentials and neighborhood-fill-in value case, not simply another discount bucket beside off-price or closeout names
- the practical comparison is not apparel store versus discount store versus discount store, but branded discovery versus closeout opportunism versus essentials convenience
- the right current order is `TJX` first on scaled off-price discovery clarity, `Ollie's` second on closeout-discovery clarity, and `Dollar General` third on proximity-led essentials value
