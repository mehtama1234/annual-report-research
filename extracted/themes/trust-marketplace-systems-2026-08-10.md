# Trust Marketplace Systems

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo covers a narrower blind spot inside the broader marketplace lane: marketplaces where trust, discovery, and community supply matter more than owned inventory, fulfillment density, or fee-membership lock-in.

`Etsy`, `eBay`, and `Airbnb` all fit this lane, but they do not fit it in the same way.

## Executive read

The useful question is not whether these companies are marketplaces.

The useful question is how trust is produced and what kind of discovery loop that trust supports.

The current archive supports a cleaner three-part split:

- `Etsy` is a creator-merchant and identity-commerce trust marketplace
- `eBay` is an enthusiast, recommerce, and category-discovery trust marketplace
- `Airbnb` is a host-guest and trip-flexibility trust marketplace

All three are lighter than `Amazon` on owned-fulfillment density and lighter than classic loyalty systems on formal wallet mechanics.

But they still create durable economics because trust itself is part of the moat.

## Why this lane matters

`AnnualReports` can place these names in old categories:

- `Etsy` under `Services / Specialty Retail, Other` on the site as reviewed in the packet
- `eBay` under `Consumer Goods / Internet Service Providers`
- `Airbnb` under `Technology / Application Software`

Those labels are not wrong as filing metadata.

They are weak as analytical categories.

They do not show:

- whether supply is driven by individual sellers, enthusiasts, or hosts
- whether trust sits in authenticity, uniqueness, community, or booking confidence
- whether repeat behavior is gifting, collecting, resale, or travel planning
- how ads, services, payments, and platform tools widen the model

## What each trust marketplace really means

### 1. Etsy: trust through uniqueness and seller identity

`Etsy` is the cleanest creator-merchant trust case in the archive.

The core promise is not lowest price or fastest fulfillment.

It is:

- unique and creative goods
- human connection
- seller identity
- taste and self-expression
- repeat buyer deepening

The FY `2025` chain makes the structure visible:

- consolidated GMS of `$11.917B`
- revenue of `$2.884B`
- revenue take rate of `24.2%`
- Etsy marketplace active buyers of `86.5M`
- Etsy marketplace active sellers of `5.6M`

The Q1 and Q2 `2026` signals matter because they show relationship repair rather than just traffic:

- active buyers growing sequentially again
- repeat and habitual cohorts stabilizing
- app GMS reaching about `47%` in Q1 `2026`
- Q2 `2026` revenue take rate at `25.9%`

This is not a commodity retail surface.

It is a marketplace where trust is partly emotional and identity-linked.

Buyers trust that they will find something distinctive, while sellers trust that creative supply and paid tools can still reach the right audience.

### 2. eBay: trust through recommerce and category culture

`eBay` is the stronger enthusiast and resale version of the same general lane.

Its trust system is less about personal creative identity and more about:

- category depth
- enthusiast supply
- collectibles culture
- pre-loved fashion
- resale confidence
- seller-buyer interaction density

The FY `2025` to Q2 `2026` chain is straightforward:

- FY `2025` GMV of `$79.6B`
- FY `2025` revenue of `$11.1B`
- Q2 `2026` advertising revenue of `$596M`
- Q1 `2026` advertising revenue of `$581M`

The more important signal is qualitative:

- management leaned into `Focus Categories`
- `C2C`
- recommerce
- collectibles
- live shopping
- the `Depop` acquisition closed on `2026-07-30`

That means eBay is not trying to become a generic everything store.

It is building a trust marketplace around category enthusiasm and resale liquidity.

The supply side matters as much as the demand side because richer, more specialized supply improves discovery and makes advertising inventory more valuable.

### 3. Airbnb: trust through hosts, identity, and flexibility

`Airbnb` is the strongest non-retail trust-marketplace case in the archive.

Its trust system is different from both `Etsy` and `eBay` because the buyer decision is higher-stakes and more episodic.

Trust is built through:

- host supply
- guest identity
- app behavior
- booking flexibility
- payments flexibility
- community scale

The FY `2025` chain establishes the scale:

- over `5M` hosts by year-end `2025`
- over `2.5B` guest arrivals by year-end `2025`
- FY `2025` gross booking value of `$91.3B`
- FY `2025` revenue of about `$12.2B`

The Q1 and Q2 `2026` evidence shows how the trust layer is widening:

- app-booked nights at `63%` of total in Q1 `2026`
- roughly `20%` of global GBV from `Reserve Now, Pay Later` in Q1 `2026`
- about `55%` of hotel bookers later returning to book a home
- services expansion, experience growth, and hotel broadening through Q2 `2026`

This is important because Airbnb proves that a marketplace can create repeat behavior without points, owned assets, or conventional loyalty funding.

The repeat mechanism is trust plus identity plus flexibility.

## The most important distinctions

### Uniqueness trust versus authenticity trust versus stay trust

- `Etsy` depends more on uniqueness, craft, and seller identity.
- `eBay` depends more on authenticity, resale confidence, and category expertise.
- `Airbnb` depends more on booking confidence, host credibility, and trip flexibility.

### Everyday browsing versus episodic planning

- `Etsy` and `eBay` can support more frequent browsing, search, and casual discovery loops.
- `Airbnb` is more episodic, but each decision is higher-consideration and more trust-sensitive.

### Emotional discovery versus supply liquidity

- `Etsy` is more emotional and identity-led.
- `eBay` is more liquidity- and category-led.
- `Airbnb` is more trust- and workflow-led around travel planning.

### Monetization widening

- `Etsy` widens through ads, payments, and seller services.
- `eBay` widens through advertising and seller tools tied to category discovery.
- `Airbnb` widens through services, experiences, hotels, and payments flexibility rather than through a large ad stack.

## Consumer, cultural, and societal read

This lane is useful because it shows that not all digital demand systems are built on convenience or hard utility.

Some are built on trust-heavy discovery.

- `Etsy` shows a consumer desire for uniqueness, self-expression, gifting, and human-scaled commerce.
- `eBay` shows the durability of recommerce, collecting, resale, and enthusiast participation.
- `Airbnb` shows that travel demand can be captured through identity, local feel, host trust, and flexibility rather than through status points or owned assets.

The societal implication is that large-scale public companies can still be built around decentralized supply and peer-like trust structures, not only around vertically controlled inventory systems.

## Blind-spot conclusion

The source taxonomy can locate these companies, but it does not classify the trust system they run.

That is the real blind spot.

The better frame is:

- `Etsy` as creator-merchant trust commerce
- `eBay` as enthusiast and recommerce trust commerce
- `Airbnb` as host-guest and trip-flexibility trust commerce

This is why the thread should treat trust marketplaces as their own analytical lane rather than as leftover software, retail, or travel pages.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- trust marketplaces should be compared by how trust is created, not by old sector labels
- `Etsy`, `eBay`, and `Airbnb` are all strong blind-spot anchors, but they solve different trust problems
- `Etsy` is the clearest seller-identity and uniqueness case
- `eBay` is the clearest recommerce and enthusiast-liquidity case
- `Airbnb` is the clearest higher-stakes host-guest and flexibility case
- ads are central to `Etsy` and `eBay`, while workflow and trip-surface widening are more central to `Airbnb`
