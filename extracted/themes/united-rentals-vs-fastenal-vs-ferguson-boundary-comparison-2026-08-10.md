# United Rentals Versus Fastenal Versus Ferguson Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the direct comparison built from the physical-execution and embedded-workflow blind spot inside the broader `AnnualReports` taxonomy problem.

It focuses on the cleanest three-way split currently available in the archive:

- `United Rentals`
- `Fastenal`
- `Ferguson`

The useful question is not whether all three support the physical economy.

The useful question is what execution and workflow layer each one actually controls:

- outsourced access to equipment, specialty capability, and fleet scale
- embedded procurement, replenishment, and onsite workflow control
- local branch density and contractor simplification across repair, maintenance, and project categories

## Executive read

All three companies monetize hidden operating dependence inside the physical economy, but they do not reduce the same kind of friction.

- `United Rentals` is the access-over-ownership and outsourced-equipment case
- `Fastenal` is the embedded procurement and replenishment-control case
- `Ferguson` is the branch-density and contractor-simplification case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `United Rentals`: access-over-ownership and outsourced-execution infrastructure
2. `Fastenal`: embedded procurement and replenishment-control infrastructure
3. `Ferguson`: branch-density and contractor-simplification infrastructure

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by workflow layer, moving from outsourced equipment access into embedded procurement control and then into local channel density and contractor simplification.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `United Rentals` is the clearest access-over-ownership case

`United Rentals` is not most useful as a generic `Rental & Leasing Services` packet.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows a company whose main value is outsourcing physical execution burden:

- full-year `2025` total revenue of `$16.1B`
- full-year `2025` net income of `$2.5B`
- full-year `2025` adjusted EBITDA of `$7.3B`
- full-year `2025` operating cash flow of `$5.2B`
- specialty rentals growth of `13.6%` in `2025`
- `Q2 2026` total revenue of `$4.410B`
- `Q2 2026` rental revenue of `$3.849B`
- `Q2 2026` adjusted EBITDA of `$2.056B`

That is why `United Rentals` is first in this comparison.

The filings repeatedly frame the business around one-stop-shop access, specialty capability, large projects, power exposure, and customer outsourcing. The company matters most when the question is who lets customers get complex work done without owning the full fleet themselves.

### 2. `Fastenal` is the clearest embedded-procurement case

`Fastenal` differs because it is not mainly renting capacity and it is not mainly winning through local contractor counter density in the same way as `Ferguson`.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows a company whose core asset is embedded workflow control:

- full-year `2025` net sales of `$8,200.5M`
- full-year `2025` gross profit of `$3,691.2M`
- full-year `2025` operating income of `$1,655.7M`
- full-year `2025` net income of `$1,258.4M`
- Digital Footprint representing `61.4%` of sales in `2025`
- `1,595` branch locations in `25` countries and `19` distribution centers globally at year-end `2025`
- `Q2 2026` net sales of `$2,386.9M`
- `Q2 2026` operating income of `$501.8M`

`Fastenal` is second because it is the clearest proof that a physical distributor can behave like embedded workflow infrastructure once it controls vending, replenishment logic, onsite staffing, and procurement flows.

The key return loop is not just selling parts. It is becoming part of the customer’s operating system for indirect spend and uptime.

### 3. `Ferguson` is the clearest branch-density and contractor-simplification case

`Ferguson` differs from both because its center of gravity is not fleet access and not plant-style replenishment control alone.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows a company whose core value is local channel density across fragmented contractor markets:

- calendar `2025` sales of `$31.3B`
- calendar `2025` gross margin of `31.0%`
- calendar `2025` adjusted operating margin of `9.6%`
- calendar `2025` operating cash flow of `$2.2B`
- about `1,729` branches and about `6,200` fleet vehicles as of `2025-12-31`
- `Q2 2026` sales of `$8.751B`
- `Q2 2026` adjusted operating profit of `$932M`
- `Q2 2026` adjusted operating margin of `10.7%`

`Ferguson` is third because this ordering moves from outsourced equipment access and embedded procurement control into the local service-density layer where contractors need fast availability, technical support, and recurring repair or project fulfillment.

It remains a strong blind-spot case because `Plumbing / HVAC Distribution` is too narrow a label for a company whose real operating role is simplifying fragmented contractor work across water, climate, PVF, residential repair, and non-residential capital projects.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `United Rentals`: access instead of ownership first

`United Rentals` matters most when the question is who reduces the burden of owning, maintaining, and scaling physical execution capability.

The distinguishing asset is:

- fleet scale
- specialty rental depth
- service and delivery logistics
- telematics and outsourced access

### `Fastenal`: procurement control first

`Fastenal` matters most when the question is who embeds itself into the customer workflow deeply enough to control replenishment, storeroom logic, and routine indirect procurement.

The distinguishing asset is:

- FMI and onsite presence
- eBusiness and digital footprint
- replenishment workflow control
- share gains through deeper customer embedment

### `Ferguson`: local contractor simplification first

`Ferguson` matters most when the question is who wins by being the closest, broadest, and most technically useful local channel for contractors facing recurring repair, maintenance, and project complexity.

The distinguishing asset is:

- branch and fleet density
- product breadth across contractor categories
- local inventory and technical support
- fragmented-channel simplification

## What each company proves

### `United Rentals`

`United Rentals` proves that access models can be structurally powerful in heavy physical categories, not only in software or consumer platforms.

### `Fastenal`

`Fastenal` proves that industrial distribution becomes much more valuable when the distributor controls procurement logic and embeds directly into customer operations.

### `Ferguson`

`Ferguson` proves that local availability, technical support, and branch density can be the real control layer in recurring contractor and repair demand.

## Why the taxonomy fails here

`AnnualReports` can place these companies into `Rental & Leasing Services`, `Industrial Supply`, and `Plumbing / HVAC Distribution`.

That hides the real distinction.

The real distinction is:

- outsourced equipment access versus
- embedded procurement control versus
- contractor-service density

Those are different workflow-control structures inside the same physical-execution lane, and the blind-spot thread is stronger when they are separated directly rather than flattened into old industrial buckets.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `United Rentals` is the clearest access-over-ownership and outsourced-execution case, not just another rental company
- `Fastenal` is the clearest embedded procurement and replenishment-control case, not just another industrial distributor
- `Ferguson` is the clearest branch-density and contractor-simplification case, not just another plumbing or HVAC channel name
- the practical comparison is not rental versus supply versus distribution, but outsourced access versus embedded procurement versus local workflow simplification
- the right current order is `United Rentals` first on access-model clarity, `Fastenal` second on procurement-control clarity, and `Ferguson` third on contractor-density clarity
