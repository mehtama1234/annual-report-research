# Warby Parker Versus Nike Versus Ulta Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the next direct comparison built from the owned-demand and channel-control blind-spot lane.

It focuses on the cleanest second three-way split currently available in the archive:

- `Warby Parker`
- `Nike`
- `Ulta`

The useful question is not whether all three have direct surfaces or strong customer relationships.

The useful question is what kind of relationship control each company actually owns:

- service-linked and fit-driven owned demand
- large-scale hybrid brand control across direct and wholesale
- assortment-led and discovery-led interface control

## Executive read

The three companies all touch identity and repeat consumer behavior, but the economic center of gravity is not the same.

- `Warby Parker` is the service-linked and fit-driven owned-demand case
- `Nike` is the large-scale hybrid brand-control case
- `Ulta` is the assortment-led and discovery-led interface-control case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `Warby Parker`: service-linked and fit-driven owned-demand system
2. `Nike`: large-scale hybrid brand-control system
3. `Ulta`: assortment-led and discovery-led interface-control system

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by how directly the company owns the demand relationship, moving from one owned fit-and-care relationship to a scaled hybrid brand system to a multi-brand category interface.

## What the filings now show

### 1. `Warby Parker` is the clearest service-linked owned-demand case

Warby Parker is not most useful as a medical-instruments supplier.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows a direct-demand system where fit, care, and identity all sit inside one owned relationship:

- full-year `2025` revenue of `$871.9M`
- full-year `2025` active customers of `2.689M`
- `323` stores at year-end `2025`, reaching `352` by `Q2 2026`
- contact lenses representing `11.1%` of `2025` revenue
- eye exams plus vision care representing `6.4%` of `2025` revenue
- `Q2 2026` average revenue per customer of `$336`

That is why Warby Parker is first in this comparison.

The customer is returning to one owned brand-and-service system. The relationship is deepened by exams, contacts, virtual tools, and insurance penetration rather than by partner assortment or marketplace traffic.

### 2. `Nike` is the clearest scaled hybrid brand-control case

Nike differs because the relationship is still clearly brand-owned, but the filings make the channel balance visible instead of presenting direct control as pure or frictionless.

The fiscal `2026` through `Q4 2026` chain shows that clearly:

- fiscal `2026` revenues of `$46.4B`
- fiscal `2026` wholesale revenues of `$27.5B`
- fiscal `2026` `NIKE Direct` revenues of `$17.7B`
- `Q2 2026` wholesale up `8%` while `NIKE Direct` was down `8%`
- `Q3 2026` wholesale up `5%` while `NIKE Direct` was down `4%`
- `Q4 2026` wholesale up `4%` while `NIKE Direct` was down `7%`

Nike is second because it is the clearest proof that brand control can remain the center of the relationship even when direct and wholesale do not behave the same way.

This is still owned demand, but it is owned demand operating at global hybrid scale rather than through one tightly integrated service relationship.

### 3. `Ulta` is the clearest interface-control case inside this trio

Ulta differs from both owned-demand cases because the customer is not mainly returning for one owned product brand or one owned fit system.

The `2025` through `Q1 2026` chain shows a category interface:

- full-year `2025` net sales of `$12.4B`
- full-year `2025` comparable sales up `5.4%`
- `Q4 2025` net sales of `$3.9B`
- `Q1 2026` net sales of `$3.2B`
- `UB Marketplace` offering more than `200` brands and `5,000` SKUs by fiscal `2025` year-end
- `UB Media`, salons, app behavior, e-commerce, and Space NK widening the model

Ulta is third because this ordering moves from owned brand-and-service demand to hybrid global brand control to a category interface where discovery, assortment, services, and media matter more than any one owned brand promise.

It remains a strong blind-spot case because `Specialty Retail, Other` does not explain an interface-control beauty system at all well.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `Warby Parker`: owned service relationship first

Warby Parker matters most when the question is who owns a fit-driven and care-linked consumer relationship directly.

The distinguishing asset is:

- one customer file
- fit and design authority
- exams and contacts
- direct pricing and service continuity

### `Nike`: brand control first

Nike matters most when the question is who owns global product and identity demand even when the channel mix is explicitly hybrid.

The distinguishing asset is:

- athletic brand authority
- global customer-file depth
- direct-plus-wholesale balance
- first-party storytelling at scale

### `Ulta`: category interface first

Ulta matters most when the question is who owns multi-brand discovery and beauty-category return rather than one owned product relationship.

The distinguishing asset is:

- assortment authority
- salons and services
- loyalty data
- marketplace and media layers

## What each company proves

### `Warby Parker`

`Warby Parker` proves that a consumer company can own demand through a fit-and-service relationship even when source taxonomy tries to frame it as regulated product supply.

### `Nike`

`Nike` proves that brand-owned demand can remain strategically central even when wholesale is still economically important and direct channels are uneven quarter to quarter.

### `Ulta`

`Ulta` proves that a customer can keep returning to a company-owned interface rather than to one owned brand promise, making interface control stronger than pure product ownership.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `Warby Parker` is the clearest service-linked owned-demand case, not a plain medical-instruments packet
- `Nike` is the clearest large-scale hybrid brand-control case, not a simple DTC-success story
- `Ulta` is the clearest assortment-led interface-control case, not an owned-brand demand system
- the practical comparison is not DTC brand versus retail chain versus athletic company, but fit-and-care owned demand versus hybrid brand control versus category-interface control
- the right current order is `Warby Parker` first on service-linked owned demand, `Nike` second on hybrid brand control, and `Ulta` third on interface-led category control
