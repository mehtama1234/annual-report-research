## Executive read

All three companies sit inside physical workflow control, but they do not intermediate the same operating dependency.

- `WESCO` is the broad electrical, communications, utility, and project-channel infrastructure case
- `Applied Industrial Technologies` is the engineering-heavy uptime, automation, and technical-service case
- `Core & Main` is the waterworks, fire-protection, and civic-failure-response distribution case

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `WESCO`: electrical, communications, utility, and project-channel infrastructure
2. `Applied Industrial Technologies`: engineering-heavy uptime, automation, and technical-MRO workflow infrastructure
3. `Core & Main`: waterworks, fire-protection, and civic-failure-response channel infrastructure

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an ordering by where each company sits inside the extended workflow stack, moving from broad infrastructure channel control into engineered uptime support and then into civic-flow replacement and utility-failure-response distribution.

## What the packets now show

### 1. `WESCO` is the clearest broad infrastructure-channel case

`WESCO` is not most useful as a generic `Electronics Wholesale` packet.

The `2025` through `Q2 2026` chain shows a company whose real role is coordinating physical infrastructure demand across electrical, communications, utility, broadband, and data-center projects:

- full-year `2025` net sales of `$23.5B`
- full-year `2025` organic sales growth of `9%`
- full-year `2025` data-center sales of `$4.3B`, up about `50%`
- `Q2 2026` net sales of `$6.665B`
- `Q2 2026` organic sales growth of `12.6%`
- `Q2 2026` data-center sales of `$1.5B`, up about `45%`
- `Q2 2026` backlog up about `60%` to a record level

That is why `WESCO` is first in this comparison.

The packet shows that the core asset is not only inventory breadth. It is the ability to sit between large infrastructure demand and actual project execution with enough scale, logistics depth, supplier access, and workflow coordination to become a physical-layer platform.

### 2. `Applied Industrial Technologies` is the clearest engineering-heavy uptime case

`Applied` differs because the center of gravity is not broad infrastructure channel breadth.

The `FY2025` through fiscal `Q3 2026` chain shows a company whose power comes from keeping industrial systems operating through technical service, automation, fluid power, calibration, and engineered MRO support:

- fiscal `2025` sales of `$4.6B`
- fiscal `2025` EBITDA margin of `12.3%`
- fiscal `2025` operating cash flow of `$492M`
- fiscal `Q3 2026` net sales of `$1.251B`
- fiscal `Q3 2026` organic sales growth of `6.0%`
- fiscal `Q3 2026` Engineered Solutions organic growth of `9.3%`
- Engineered Solutions approaching `40%` of total sales

`Applied` is second because it is the clearest proof that workflow control can become more technical, service-heavy, and uptime-sensitive than the broader stocked-inventory and project-channel model visible at `WESCO`.

### 3. `Core & Main` is the clearest civic-flow and waterworks channel case

`Core & Main` differs from both because the demand engine is tied to municipal replacement, utility resilience, and public-infrastructure failure response rather than to broad electrical projects or general industrial uptime.

The `2025` through `Q1 2026` chain shows that clearly:

- fiscal `2025` net sales of `$7.647B`
- fiscal `2025` gross margin of `26.9%`
- fiscal `2025` adjusted EBITDA of `$931M`
- `Q1 2026` net sales of `$1.910B`
- `Q1 2026` gross margin of `27.2%`
- continued emphasis on fire protection, smart utility, treatment-plant solutions, fusible HDPE, geosynthetics, and metering

`Core & Main` is third because this ordering moves from broad infrastructure channel control and engineering-heavy MRO support into a narrower but highly important civic-flow replacement system built around water reliability, wastewater handling, storm drainage, fire protection, and municipal infrastructure upkeep.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `WESCO`: broad project-complexity channel first

`WESCO` matters most when the question is who coordinates electrical, communications, utility, security, and data-center sourcing at scale.

The distinguishing asset is:

- broad category breadth
- procurement integration
- logistics density
- project-channel coordination
- supplier and customer workflow scale

### `Applied Industrial Technologies`: uptime and engineered support first

`Applied` matters most when the question is who solves technical maintenance, automation, fluid-power, motion, and engineered aftermarket problems that customers cannot manage efficiently on their own.

The distinguishing asset is:

- technical distribution depth
- engineered solutions
- automation and fluid-power capability
- repair complexity
- service and application expertise

### `Core & Main`: civic-failure-response and waterworks channel first

`Core & Main` matters most when the question is who controls the channel through which water, wastewater, storm-drainage, and fire-protection replacement demand flows.

The distinguishing asset is:

- specialty waterworks inventory
- municipal and contractor channel density
- project support
- local availability
- replacement and resilience demand

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `WESCO` is the clearest broad electrical, communications, utility, and project-channel case, not just another wholesaler
- `Applied Industrial Technologies` is the clearest engineering-heavy uptime and technical-MRO case, not just another industrial distributor
- `Core & Main` is the clearest waterworks and civic-failure-response channel case, not just another building-materials wholesaler
- the practical comparison is not wholesale versus distribution versus supply, but broad project-channel control versus technical uptime support versus civic-flow replacement-channel control
- the right current order is `WESCO` first on infrastructure-channel breadth, `Applied` second on engineering-heavy uptime depth, and `Core & Main` third on waterworks and public-infrastructure specificity
