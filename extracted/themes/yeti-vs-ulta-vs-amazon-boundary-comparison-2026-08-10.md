# YETI Versus Ulta Versus Amazon Boundary Comparison

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This memo is the first direct comparison built from the owned-demand versus marketplace-extended boundary lane.

It focuses on the cleanest unresolved three-way split in that lane:

- `YETI`
- `Ulta`
- `Amazon`

The useful question is not which one has the best channel mix in the abstract.

The useful question is what kind of demand each company actually owns:

- brand-owned demand
- assortment-and-service interface demand
- broad retail-interface and ecosystem demand

## Executive read

The three companies now sit on the same broad ownership map, but at clearly different points:

- `YETI` is owned-demand first, with marketplace reach assisting that demand
- `Ulta` is interface and assortment control first, even though the relationship is strong
- `Amazon` is the thickest interface-control case, where utility, fulfillment, seller participation, and advertising all sit on top of the same account relationship

That means the best current ordering is:

1. `YETI`: hybrid owned-demand system with marketplace-assisted reach
2. `Ulta`: marketplace-extended and service-led interface system
3. `Amazon`: full-scale interface and ecosystem-control system

| Company | What the customer mainly returns for | What the company mainly controls | What widens the model beyond the first sale | Best current read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `YETI` | premium utility, gifting, outdoor attachment, and direct brand trust | brand demand, product authority, direct reach, and category control | Amazon Marketplace reach, retail stores, and corporate sales | owned-demand system widened by marketplace-assisted reach |
| `Ulta` | beauty replenishment, discovery, services, assortment breadth, and loyalty | category environment, discovery interface, salons, loyalty data, and media | UB Marketplace, UB Media, and store-plus-app-plus-salon integration | interface-control system built on assortment, services, and discovery |
| `Amazon` | search, convenience, Prime, replenishment, and account-level utility | interface demand, fulfillment, seller services, advertising, and ecosystem utility | third-party seller services, ads, subscriptions, and fulfillment density | full ecosystem-control system |

## What the filings now show

### 1. `YETI` is still a brand-owned demand system

The important fact is not just that `DTC` is large.

It is that the customer is still returning mainly for:

- the `YETI` brand
- premium hardgoods trust
- outdoor utility
- gifting attachment

The packet shows:

- full-year `2025` sales of `$1.8685B`
- `DTC` channel sales of `$1.1278B`, or about `60%` of net sales
- explicit direct control across websites, retail stores, corporate sales, and `YETI Authorized` on the Amazon Marketplace

That does not make YETI interface-first.

It means marketplace reach helps a clearly owned premium-utility brand travel further.

### 2. `Ulta` owns the beauty interface more than one owned brand

Ulta has a powerful relationship, but not a one-brand owned-demand relationship in the YETI sense.

The filings show the customer is returning for:

- broad beauty assortment
- replenishment plus experimentation
- salon services
- loyalty
- marketplace discovery
- media-linked brand access

The `2025` annual and trailing-quarter chain makes that unusually explicit:

- full-year `2025` net sales of `$12.4B`
- `UB Marketplace` with more than `200` brands and `5,000` SKUs at fiscal `2025` year-end
- `UB Media` framed as a monetization layer built from loyalty-member behavior and brand-partner demand

That is why Ulta is better read as interface control than as true brand-owned demand.

The customer is returning to a company-owned beauty environment rather than to one owned brand promise.

### 3. `Amazon` has the broadest and thickest interface-control stack

Amazon is the far end of the same spectrum.

The customer is not mainly returning for one brand or one category environment.

The packet shows the relationship is monetized through:

- search
- convenience
- Prime
- seller services
- advertising
- fulfillment

By Q2 `2026`, Amazon reported:

- net sales of `$200.6B`
- advertising services of `$19.8B`
- subscription services of `$13.7B`
- third-party seller services of `$46.8B`

That means the relationship is no longer just retail or discovery.

It is a broad interface and ecosystem utility layer.

## The most useful boundary split

The cleanest way to separate the three is:

### `YETI`: product and brand demand first

Marketplace surfaces widen the company, but the customer is still coming back primarily for the company's own brand and product authority.

### `Ulta`: category interface first

Ulta is stronger than a standard retailer because it owns:

- beauty discovery
- loyalty
- service attachment
- assortment authority

But it still owns the category environment more than a singular owned brand demand loop.

### `Amazon`: account-level interface utility first

Amazon is broader still.

The relationship is not centered on one brand or one category.

It is centered on:

- account utility
- search and replenishment
- fulfillment
- merchant participation
- advertising surfaces

## What each company proves

### `YETI`

`YETI` proves that marketplace reach can assist owned demand without replacing it.

### `Ulta`

`Ulta` proves that a retailer can own a powerful interface and discovery system without becoming a pure owned-brand case.

### `Amazon`

`Amazon` proves that interface control can scale far beyond either brand-owned demand or category-specific discovery control.

## Highest-confidence conclusions

- `YETI` is still best understood as owned demand first, with marketplace reach acting as a widener
- `Ulta` is not a pure owned-demand brand system; it is a beauty-interface and assortment-control system
- `Amazon` is the thickest interface-control case in the archive, not a brand-owned demand case
- the practical comparison is not DTC versus non-DTC, but product-owned demand versus category-interface control versus ecosystem-interface control
- the right current order is `YETI` first on owned-demand purity, `Ulta` second on category-interface control, and `Amazon` third as the broadest interface-control system
