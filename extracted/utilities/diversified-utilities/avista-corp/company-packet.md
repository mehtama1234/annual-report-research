## Annual report takeaways

- Avista is the right next utility because it fills the archive’s remaining hydro-sensitive regional-utility gap. This is a Pacific Northwest mixed electric-and-gas utility where hydro generation, weather, wholesale power conditions, and customer concentration matter more than in the larger load-growth names.
- AnnualReports already exposes Avista’s `2025 Annual Report and Form 10K`, which makes Avista a cleaner aggregator-backed utility than several of the other current names.
- The `2025` year-end materials frame Avista as a smaller but still capital-intensive regulated utility. Avista Utilities invested `$553M` in capital expenditures during `2025`, and management framed a `2026` capital-expenditure assumption of about `$615M`.
- Avista also broadens the utility set geographically and structurally. It serves roughly `429,000` electric customers and `386,000` natural-gas customers across Washington, Idaho, and Oregon, while AEL&P adds a small Alaska electric business in Juneau.
- The year-end release makes the strategic constraint clear: Avista is still a growth utility, but part of the `2026` story is offset by a large industrial customer choosing to return to market procurement sooner than expected, reducing `2026` utility guidance by about `$0.12` per diluted share.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` GAAP EPS were `$0.43` and non-GAAP utility EPS were `$0.29`.
- Guidance and operating posture: management reaffirmed `2026` utility guidance of `$2.52` to `$2.72` and stressed disciplined cost management, balanced investment, and affordability.
- Interpretation: this is not a utility selling a pure hyperscaler-growth narrative. It is a smaller mixed utility where steady execution, weather-normal assumptions, and capital discipline keep guidance intact even while quarter-to-quarter utility EPS stays flat.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` GAAP EPS were `$1.11` and non-GAAP utility EPS were `$1.10`.
- Capital and strategy: management reaffirmed `2026` utility guidance, cited about `$147M` of first-quarter utility capex, and continued to point to `2026` capital expenditures of roughly `$615M`.
- Interpretation: Q1 shows the cleaner Avista model. Rate-case execution and utility margin still drive earnings, but the story is framed through fundamentals, not headline large-load conversion.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: full-year `2025` GAAP EPS were `$2.38` and non-GAAP utility EPS were `$2.55`; fourth-quarter `2025` utility EPS were `$0.89`.
- Stress and regulation: management initiated `2026` utility guidance of `$2.52` to `$2.72`, disclosed a `$0.12` headwind from the large industrial customer departure, and kept the long-term utility-growth framing at `4%` to `6%`.
- Interpretation: year-end `2025` is where Avista most clearly differs from the rest of the utility set. The company is still talking growth, but the real variables are hydro-normal assumptions, regional regulatory outcomes, and whether smaller-system customer churn changes the capital-return profile.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Avista gives the utility set a hydro-sensitive, mixed electric-and-gas Northwestern case rather than another larger pure electric utility.
- Industrial: the industrial read is less about explosive new load and more about the earnings sensitivity created when one large industrial customer leaves or joins a smaller system.
- Consumer: the consumer angle is affordability under ongoing utility investment, especially in a region where weather, wildfire, and hydro conditions still shape operating reality.
- Cultural: Avista carries a more local civic-utility tone than the larger growth names, with safety, reliability, and community service framing closer to the center.
- Regulatory: this is a useful example of ordinary rate-case execution still doing most of the earnings work even while management talks about transmission expansion and new large-load options.
- Climate and water: Avista gives the archive direct exposure to hydroelectric-generation sensitivity, water-resource dependence, wildfire risk, and climate-linked operating variability.
- Capital allocation: Avista is a smaller capital program than Duke, AEP, or Edison, but it is still a real buildout case once you include base capital, transmission optionality, and the possibility of incremental large-load spending.

## Working interpretation

- Avista is useful because it adds a different kind of regulated utility to the archive. It is not a giant wires platform, not a merchant counterexample, and not a wildfire-liability stress case like Edison. It is a smaller hydro-sensitive mixed utility whose economics are shaped by weather, water, customer concentration, and steady regulatory execution.
- That broadens the utility archive materially. It adds hydroelectric and regional-weather sensitivity to a set that otherwise risks reading too cleanly as a grid-buildout and load-growth basket.
- The remaining utility gap is now less about hydro and more about whether the archive wants another sharply different geographic-risk or ownership model next.
