## What changed

- Utility type: Avista adds the clearest Pacific Northwest hydro-sensitive mixed utility in the current archive. This is a smaller regulated utility where hydro conditions, regional wholesale markets, and weather matter more than in the larger southeastern and midwestern power names.
- Business mix: Avista Utilities provides electric service to about `429,000` customers and natural gas service to about `386,000` customers across Washington, Idaho, and Oregon, while AEL&P adds a small Alaska electric outpost in Juneau.
- Capital plan: Avista Utilities invested `$553M` in capital expenditures during `2025`, is assuming about `$615M` of capital expenditures in `2026`, and separately notes that new large-load integration could add up to `$350M` outside the base plan.
- Growth algorithm: management continues to frame the business around `4%` to `6%` long-term non-GAAP utility earnings growth from the midpoint of `2025`, with `2026` utility guidance of `$2.52` to `$2.72`.
- Distinctive risk: unlike the faster-growth data-center utilities, Avista’s archive now includes direct sensitivity to hydroelectric generation, river and reservoir conditions, wildfire and climate risk, wholesale power conditions, and the earnings effect of a single large industrial customer leaving earlier than expected.
- Quarter progression: the trailing-quarter set moved from full-year `2025` non-GAAP utility EPS of `$2.55` to Q1 `2026` utility EPS of `$1.10`, then Q2 `2026` utility EPS of `$0.29`, while guidance stayed intact and management kept stressing disciplined cost control and affordability.

## Open questions

- How durable is Avista’s `4%` to `6%` utility-growth algorithm if hydro conditions or wholesale power conditions turn less favorable?
- How much of the next capital cycle depends on winning replacement large-load demand after the announced industrial-customer departure?
- Does Avista’s smaller hydro-sensitive model prove more resilient than the archive’s larger load-growth utilities, or simply more exposed to weather and customer concentration?
