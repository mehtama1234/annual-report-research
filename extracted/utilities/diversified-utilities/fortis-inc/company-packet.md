## Annual report takeaways

- Fortis is the right next utility because it fills the archive's clearest remaining ownership-and-geography gap: a cross-border regulated holding company with electric and gas exposure across Canada, the U.S., and the Caribbean.
- AnnualReports is not current here. As of `2026-08-09`, it still shows the `2024 Annual Report`, which makes Fortis a good example of why the archive has to rely on official IR and SEC evidence rather than on the aggregator alone.
- The `2025` year-end disclosure materials frame Fortis as a large but steady regulated utility platform. The year-end release says Fortis generated `2025` revenue of `$12B`, had total assets of `$75B`, and employed `9,900` people serving customers in five Canadian provinces, ten U.S. states, and the Caribbean.
- The annual setup is fundamentally about regulated capital deployment. By Q1 `2026`, Fortis was already framing the business through a `$28.8B` five-year capital plan expected to grow midyear rate base from `$42.4B` in `2025` to `$57.9B` by `2030`, a `7%` CAGR.
- Fortis also adds a different kind of utility politics. The investment case is less about one headline hyperscaler pipeline and more about making a multi-jurisdiction regulated platform compound through repeated approvals, transmission opportunities, gas and electric system upgrades, and steady dividend growth support.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` net earnings were `$396M` or `$0.78` per common share, up from `$0.76` in Q2 `2025`.
- Guidance and operating posture: management said capital expenditures totaled `$2.7B` in the first half of `2026` and that the `$5.6B` annual capital plan remained on track.
- Interpretation: the quarter reinforces Fortis as a disciplined regulated compounding case. The notable updates were not dramatic earnings beats, but the continued movement of approved projects such as Tilbury Phase 1B in British Columbia and the Roadrunner Reserve II battery project in Arizona.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` net earnings were `$501M` or `$0.99` per common share.
- Capital and strategy: management reaffirmed the `$5.6B` annual capital plan and highlighted the broader `$28.8B` five-year capital plan, expected rate-base growth from `$42.4B` to `$57.9B`, and dividend-growth guidance of `4-6%` annually through `2030`.
- Interpretation: Q1 makes the Fortis model clear. This is a holding-company utility where the main engine is not merchant upside or one-off customer wins, but repeatable regulated capital formation across multiple subsidiaries and jurisdictions.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: the year-end `2025` disclosure package anchors Fortis as a large regulated electric-and-gas utility platform with `2025` revenue of `$12B` and total assets of `$75B`.
- Structure and scale: the same materials emphasize the geographic spread of the platform across five Canadian provinces, ten U.S. states, and the Caribbean, with shares listed on both the `TSX` and `NYSE` under `FTS`.
- Interpretation: year-end `2025` is where Fortis most clearly differs from the rest of the utility set. It is not a single-market utility and not a merchant counterexample. It is a geographically diversified regulated utility portfolio whose value comes from long-duration capital deployment under many regulators.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Fortis gives the utility set a cross-border holding-company case rather than another U.S.-only operating utility.
- Industrial: the industrial read is broader grid and energy-delivery capacity across many territories, including transmission expansion, battery storage, and system modernization tied to load growth opportunities.
- Consumer: the consumer angle is less rhetorical than in some utilities, but the same affordability compact exists across many regulated territories where Fortis has to earn approval for continued capital investment.
- Cultural: Fortis reads more like a long-duration civic-infrastructure allocator than a promotional growth utility. The tone is steady, utility-heavy, and governance-oriented.
- Regulatory: this is one of the archive's cleanest examples of regulatory diversification as a business model. The company is effectively a portfolio of regulated franchises rather than one monolithic utility.
- Geography and FX: Fortis adds direct Canada-U.S.-Caribbean exposure, which means the archive now includes a utility with currency translation and hurricane or storm exposure layered on top of ordinary rate-base growth.
- Capital allocation: Fortis broadens the capital-allocation story from pure demand-surge utilities into a steadier model where repeated approval, platform discipline, and long-term project execution matter more than headline load announcements.

## Working interpretation

- Fortis is useful because it broadens the utility set in a direction the archive did not yet have: a regulated holding-company platform spread across many jurisdictions rather than one utility with one regulator and one geographic narrative.
- That matters for the higher-level theme work. It adds FX sensitivity, hurricane and storm exposure, regulatory diversification, and portfolio-style capital allocation to a utility archive that already had cleaner wires, merchant generation, water, gas, hydro-sensitive, and wildfire-stressed cases.
- The utilities archive now has a stronger answer to the question of what utility growth looks like outside the pure U.S. load-growth story. In Fortis, it looks like patient rate-base compounding across a diversified regulated platform.
