## What changed

- Utility type: Fortis adds the clearest cross-border regulated holding-company model in the current utility archive. This is not a single-state or single-country utility. It is a portfolio of regulated electric and gas utilities spread across Canada, the U.S., and the Caribbean.
- Geographic mix: the `2025` year-end disclosure release says Fortis had `9,900` employees serving utility customers in five Canadian provinces, ten U.S. states, and the Caribbean. That gives the archive a multi-jurisdiction utility with currency, regulatory, weather, and storm exposure that is structurally different from the U.S.-only names.
- Capital plan: the Q1 `2026` release frames Fortis around a `$28.8B` five-year capital plan and expected midyear rate-base growth from `$42.4B` in `2025` to `$57.9B` by `2030`, a `7%` CAGR.
- Growth algorithm: Fortis ties that rate-base growth to dividend growth guidance of `4-6%` annually through `2030`, which makes it a steadier regulated compounding case than the archive's merchant or wildfire-stressed names.
- Distinctive risk: unlike the archive's pure wires and merchant names, Fortis carries explicit jurisdiction mix, foreign-exchange translation, storm exposure, and varied regulatory calendars across multiple subsidiaries.
- Quarter progression: the trailing-quarter set moved from the `2025` year-end setup into Q1 `2026` net earnings of `$501M`, then Q2 `2026` net earnings of `$396M`, while management kept stressing execution against the same annual capital plan and incremental growth options such as Tilbury Phase 1B and the Roadrunner Reserve II battery project.
