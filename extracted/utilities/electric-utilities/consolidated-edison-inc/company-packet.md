## Annual report takeaways

- Con Edison is the right sixth utility because it adds the dense urban regulated utility control case. It broadens the sample away from companies whose stories are increasingly dominated by hyperscaler load, generation buildout, or development-platform rhetoric.
- AnnualReports currently classifies Con Edison as `Utilities / Electric Utilities`, which fits the basic regulated electric framing, but the current company is better understood as a broader regulated urban energy-infrastructure system spanning electric, gas, steam, and transmission exposure.
- Official company materials describe Consolidated Edison, Inc. as a holding company whose main regulated businesses are Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Orange and Rockland Utilities, and Con Edison Transmission. The footprint is concentrated in New York City, Westchester, southeastern New York, and northern New Jersey.
- The annual and quarterly framing is less about blockbuster AI-power announcements and more about reliability, affordability, resilience, electrification, substations, and disciplined capital planning. That matters because it shows the utility build cycle can still be visible in a company whose messaging stays closer to public-service infrastructure than to growth-platform marketing.
- Con Edison’s strategic point is that a regulated utility serving one of the country’s most economically important urban regions can still deliver durable earnings growth through approved investment, system hardening, and electrification-led network expansion without depending on the loudest large-load narrative.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` net income for common stock was `$308 million` or `$0.83` per share, and adjusted earnings were also `$308 million` or `$0.83` per share.
- Guidance: management reaffirmed full-year `2026` adjusted EPS guidance of `$6.00` to `$6.20`.
- Management message: the Q2 release emphasized nation-leading electric reliability, targeted infrastructure investment, affordability, and support for New York’s clean-energy transition.
- Structural themes: management said year-to-date results were in line with expectations, pointed to electrification of buildings and transportation, and said it expects `28` new substations in service by `2035` alongside tens of billions of dollars of other capital investment.
- Operating themes: the quarter still reads like a regulated utility compounding story built on resilience and network expansion, not on step-change merchant or hyperscaler upside.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` net income for common stock was `$924 million` or `$2.55` per share, while adjusted earnings were `$790 million` or `$2.18` per share versus `$792 million` or `$2.26` per share a year earlier.
- Guidance: management reaffirmed `2026` adjusted EPS guidance of `$6.00` to `$6.20`.
- Management message: the Q1 release was the clearest articulation of Con Edison’s current investment case. Management emphasized the strength and durability of the regulated businesses, industry-leading reliability, electrification of heating and transportation, new substations, robust design standards, and affordability.
- Structural themes: management also noted a settled forward sale agreement for `7 million` shares and the completed sale of its interest in MVP for total consideration of `$357.5 million`, which helps separate the ongoing regulated core from legacy non-core items.
- Operating identity: Con Edison repeatedly ties growth to infrastructure investment across both utilities as demand grows, while keeping the narrative centered on execution quality and cost management.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` net income for common stock was `$297 million` or `$0.82` per share and adjusted earnings were `$320 million` or `$0.89` per share. Full-year `2025` net income for common stock was `$2,023 million` or `$5.66` per share, and adjusted earnings were `$2,038 million` or `$5.70` per share.
- Guidance: management introduced `2026` adjusted EPS guidance of `$6.00` to `$6.20` and a five-year compounded annual adjusted EPS growth target of `6%` to `7%`.
- Management message: year-end `2025` materials framed Con Edison as a regulated utility delivering top-end guidance performance through disciplined investment, cost management, and regional economic importance rather than through a more speculative growth posture.
- Structural themes: management tied the plan to approved investment programs, a three-year rate plan, and a multi-year capital program of `$6.595B` in `2026`, `$6.759B` in `2027`, and `$24.339B` in aggregate for `2028` through `2030`.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Con Edison is the cleaner weaker-growth and less-hyped control case the utilities set needed. It shows the current utility story does not require an explicit hyperscaler or merchant-power angle to remain relevant.
- Industrial: the industrial read is urban grid modernization, substations, resilience, and electrification infrastructure rather than greenfield generation or broad industrial-campus development.
- Consumer: Con Edison pushes affordability much harder than most peers. The company repeatedly frames investment through customer reliability, discounts for income-eligible customers, outage avoidance, and keeping the transition manageable in a politically sensitive service territory.
- Cultural: this is one of the clearest reminders in the archive that some infrastructure stories are still fundamentally civic stories. New York service reliability, heat resilience, and the social legitimacy of utility investment are central to the narrative.
- Regulatory: Con Edison is highly regulation-shaped. Three-year rate plans, approved investment programs, and authorized return support are part of the core earnings architecture rather than side issues.
- Technology: the technology angle is indirect. Electrification, cleaner-energy adoption, substations, and system hardening matter more than AI demand rhetoric. That contrast helps separate general grid-build economics from the market’s current AI-power enthusiasm.
- Capital allocation: the capital spend profile is still large enough to matter sector-wide. Con Edison supports the broader conclusion that utilities are being pulled into long-duration capital cycles even where management tone stays conservative.
