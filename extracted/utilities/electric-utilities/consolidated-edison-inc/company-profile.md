## What changed

- Business-model clarity: Con Edison is a dense urban regulated utility with a much narrower and more civic-infrastructure-heavy story than the louder utility names in the current set. The emphasis is reliability, resilience, affordability, and steady capital deployment across New York rather than a broad generation-development platform.
- Demand framing: management is still talking about growth, but it is framed through electrification of buildings and transportation, customer adoption of cleaner energy technologies, and the need for substations and network upgrades rather than through big hyperscaler announcements.
- Guidance posture: Con Edison introduced `2026` adjusted EPS guidance of `$6.00` to `$6.20` at Q4 `2025` and reaffirmed it in both Q1 and Q2 `2026`.
- Capital posture: the Q4 `2025` release laid out expected capital investments of `$6.595B` in `2026`, `$6.759B` in `2027`, and `$24.339B` in aggregate during `2028` through `2030`, showing that Con Edison is still part of the utility capex build cycle even if the rhetoric is less promotional.
- Operating identity: quarterlies repeatedly tie results to nation-leading reliability, robust design standards, extreme-weather resilience, and affordability. That makes Con Edison a useful counterweight to utilities whose growth framing is more overtly AI-power or merchant-generation adjacent.

## Open questions

- How much of Con Edison’s urban electrification narrative converts into upside beyond its current regulated capital plan, versus remaining a steady but lower-beta utility-growth case?
- Does the market continue rewarding reliability-and-affordability regulated utilities at similar multiples if peers keep posting more visible large-load conversion stories?
- How much of Con Edison’s next leg of growth depends on New York regulatory support for continued substation, network, and clean-energy-transition investment?
