## What changed

- Demand conversion: Duke moved from about `4.5 GW` of data-center load secured under electric service agreements in the annual-report framing to about `7.6 GW` secured by Q1 `2026`, with about `5 GW` already under construction and a high-confidence late-stage pipeline around `15.4 GW`.
- Capital intensity: year-end `2025` materials positioned a `$103B` capital plan for `2026` through `2030`, with earnings-base growth driven by generation investments in growing jurisdictions.
- Generation buildout: the company is openly in an “all-of-the-above” build cycle, targeting about `14 GW` of added capacity by `2031`, securing up to `20` gas turbines through the GE Vernova partnership, and pushing major gas, nuclear, hydro, solar, and storage execution.
- Guidance and earnings posture: Duke closed `2025` above the guidance midpoint at `$6.31` adjusted EPS, introduced `2026` guidance at `$6.55` to `$6.80`, and reaffirmed that range through Q1 and Q2 `2026`.
- Business mix: this is a more traditional regulated utility than NextEra and a broader multi-jurisdiction utility than AEP because the story spans electric plus gas utilities, large regulated service territories, and explicit load growth conversion through regulation and infrastructure recovery.
- Customer-affordability framing: Duke repeatedly couples growth with claims that large-load customers pay their fair share and that new infrastructure will help spread fixed costs while keeping rates below the national average.
- Regulatory architecture: unlike a merchant-power story, Duke’s earnings conversion still depends on rate cases, efficient recovery mechanisms, utility combinations, tax-credit monetization, and constructive regulatory outcomes.

## Open questions

- How much of the late-stage data-center and large-load pipeline converts into durable rate base versus slipping on permitting, generation timing, or customer build schedules?
- Can Duke sustain the affordability argument while the capital plan remains above `$100B` and generation intensity keeps rising?
- Does Duke’s gas-heavy capacity-addition program become an execution moat or a financing and permitting risk if load timing changes?
