## Annual report takeaways

- Edison is the right next utility because it fills the clearest remaining stress case in the archive: a major regulated utility whose story is inseparable from wildfire liability, catastrophe financing, resilience spending, and California regulatory structure.
- AnnualReports classifies Edison as `Utilities / Electric Utilities`, but still lags at `2024`, which itself is informative because it means the archive has to rely on official IR for the current annual package.
- The `2025` annual report frames Edison as a wires-focused utility investing in reliability, resiliency, and climate adaptation to enable the clean-energy transition.
- The annual materials are explicit that this is a high-capex, high-risk, but still regulated-growth utility. Edison reported `2025` rate base of `$48.206B`, capital expenditures of `$6.729B`, and core EPS of `$6.55`, while also carrying large wildfire-related claims and expense lines tied to legacy events and the newer Eaton Fire.
- Edison adds a different kind of utility politics to the set. The central issue is not only affordability or data-center demand. It is whether California can sustain the capital formation, liability protections, and recovery tools required for an electric utility to harden the grid in a climate-stressed service territory.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` GAAP EPS were `$1.39` and core EPS were `$1.54`.
- Guidance and operating posture: management reaffirmed `2026` core EPS guidance of `$5.90` to `$6.20` and kept emphasizing wildfire-mitigation execution, affordability, and resilience.
- Interpretation: this is not a clean “AI load growth” utility. It is a utility whose investability rests on disciplined execution under wildfire pressure, regulatory support, and the credibility of the state’s catastrophe-cost architecture.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` GAAP EPS were `$1.38` and core EPS were `$1.42`.
- Capital and strategy: the Q1 investor deck reaffirmed `5%` to `7%` core EPS CAGR from `2025` to `2030` and laid out a `2026`-`2030` capex plan of about `$38B` to `$41B`.
- Interpretation: Q1 makes the Edison model clear. Growth still exists, but it is earned through resilience and grid-hardening spend, not through a low-risk, plain-vanilla regulated playbook.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` GAAP EPS were `$4.80` and core EPS were `$1.86`; full-year `2025` GAAP EPS were `$11.58` and core EPS were `$6.55`.
- Stress and regulation: management introduced `2026` core EPS guidance of `$5.90` to `$6.20`, extended the `5%` to `7%` core EPS growth framing to `2030`, and explicitly tied the outlook to stronger wildfire-risk reduction and recent regulatory decisions.
- Interpretation: year-end `2025` is where Edison most clearly differs from the rest of the utility set. The company is trying to preserve a regulated-growth multiple while openly managing around possible ignition association, Wildfire Fund reimbursement, self-insurance layers, and securitization capacity.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Edison gives the utility set a California stress-test case rather than just another regulated-growth case.
- Industrial: the industrial read is grid hardening, transmission and distribution resilience, and catastrophe-system financing rather than incremental large-load conversion.
- Consumer: the consumer angle is politically charged because resilience and wildfire-mitigation spending have to coexist with affordability in a high-cost state.
- Cultural: Edison carries the most explicit climate-risk and public-safety tone in the current utility set. This is a utility that has to justify its role not just economically, but socially and politically after recurring wildfire trauma.
- Regulatory: this is one of the archive’s cleanest examples of regulation as both shield and vulnerability. The investment case depends on cost recovery, prudency treatment, Wildfire Fund mechanics, and securitization support.
- Climate adaptation: Edison turns climate adaptation from a side theme into the core operating model itself.
- Capital allocation: unlike utilities that sell growth through demand upside, Edison sells growth through resilience, climate adaptation, and risk-reduction investment under a dense regulatory framework.

## Working interpretation

- Edison is useful because it forces the utilities archive to deal with a harsher version of regulated growth. AEP and Exelon show cleaner wires and grid-expansion math. Duke and NextEra show regulated-growth plus demand upside. Atmos and American Water show essential-service compounding in gas and water. Edison shows what happens when the utility has to compound capital while also carrying a climate-disaster balance-sheet story.
- That broadens the utility archive materially. It adds climate-adaptation risk, wildfire-fund dependence, and California financial-stability politics to a set that otherwise risks reading too cleanly as a growth-infrastructure basket.
- The remaining utility gap is now less about stress and more about whether the archive wants a hydro-concentrated or otherwise structurally different utility model next.
