## Annual report takeaways

- Exelon is the right fifth utility because it adds the large-scale regulated transmission-and-distribution control case. That makes the utilities set less dependent on companies whose stories are currently dominated by generation buildout, development backlog, or outsized data-center demand.
- AnnualReports currently classifies Exelon as `Utilities / Diversified Utilities`, which fits the mixed electric-and-gas utility footprint but still understates how cleanly regulated the current Exelon model is after the Constellation separation.
- Official company materials describe Exelon as one of the nation’s largest utility companies, serving almost `11 million` customers through six fully regulated transmission and distribution utilities. The company also says it has more than `20,000` employees and a footprint concentrated in large urban and dense regional service territories.
- The annual and investor framing is less about blockbuster load announcements and more about reliability, affordability, regulatory recovery, infrastructure modernization, and disciplined capital deployment. That matters because it shows a different path to utility growth than the AEP, Duke, NextEra, and Alliant stories.
- Exelon’s core strategic point is that a large regulated wires platform with top-quartile operating performance can still support durable earnings growth through rate base and transmission without needing to be the loudest AI-power narrative in the sector.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` GAAP net income was `$0.39` per share and adjusted operating earnings were `$0.43` per share.
- Guidance: management reaffirmed full-year `2026` adjusted operating earnings guidance of `$2.81` to `$2.91` per share.
- Management message: the Q2 release framed results as in line with expectations and continued to emphasize steady execution, regulated recovery, and system investment rather than a change in the long-term plan.
- Structural themes: Exelon still reads as a regulated utility compounder whose main levers are rate base, transmission, distribution modernization, and reliability performance rather than a step-change large-load pipeline.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` GAAP net income was `$0.90` per share and adjusted operating earnings were `$0.91` per share, versus adjusted operating earnings of `$0.92` per share in Q1 `2025`.
- Guidance: management reaffirmed `2026` adjusted operating earnings guidance of `$2.81` to `$2.91` per share.
- Management message: the Q1 materials were the clearest statement of Exelon’s current investment case. Management emphasized a revised `$41.7B` four-year capital plan, expected rate-base growth of `7.9%`, and operating EPS growth near the top end of `5%` to `7%` from `2025` to `2029`.
- Structural themes: the same materials highlighted an additional `$12B` to `$17B` transmission opportunity beyond the base plan, making Exelon the most explicit transmission-upside utility in the current archive.
- Operating themes: all utilities were described as sustaining top-quartile reliability performance, with ComEd in top decile, which reinforces the idea that Exelon is selling grid quality and execution as much as growth.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` GAAP net income was `$0.58` per share and adjusted operating earnings were `$0.59` per share. Full-year `2025` GAAP net income was `$2.73` per share.
- Guidance: management initiated `2026` adjusted operating earnings guidance at `$2.81` to `$2.91` per share.
- Management message: year-end `2025` materials framed Exelon as a utility that can deliver above-expectation operating earnings while keeping the investment story centered on stable regulated execution.
- Structural themes: by late `2025`, Exelon was already arguing that close to `90%` of rate base was covered by established recovery mechanisms through the `2026` to `2027` window. That is a materially different risk posture from utilities that depend more on large project timing or market-linked development conversion.

Note: for many companies this third slot is a late-2025 quarter needed to complete the trailing three-quarter set as of 2026-08-09.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Exelon is the control case showing that utility growth does not need to look like a power-development franchise or a hyperscaler demand sprint. A large regulated wires platform can still support durable earnings growth through transmission, distribution, and rate recovery.
- Industrial: the industrial angle here is grid modernization, transmission expansion, and urban infrastructure resilience more than greenfield generation or new industrial campuses.
- Consumer: affordability and reliability sit closer to the center of the Exelon story than in most peers. The company repeatedly ties capital recovery to outage avoidance, restoration speed, and customer outcomes.
- Cultural: Exelon is a useful reminder that the utility story is also civic infrastructure. Dense metro footprints, restoration performance, and public trust still matter alongside AI and industrial-load narratives.
- Regulatory: this is one of the archive’s clearest examples of regulation as the earnings architecture itself. The logic is built around approved distribution and transmission rates, recovery mechanisms, multi-year plans, and transmission capital visibility.
- Technology: Exelon is less of a direct AI-power proxy than AEP, Duke, NextEra, or Alliant. That contrast is valuable because it helps separate broad utility infrastructure economics from the more market-exciting data-center load theme.
- Capital allocation: Exelon broadens the sector read by making transmission optionality more explicit. The `$41.7B` capital plan and additional `$12B` to `$17B` transmission opportunity push the archive toward a stronger grid-buildout interpretation.
