## What changed

- Business-model clarity: Exelon is the cleanest regulated transmission-and-distribution utility in the current set. It serves almost `11 million` customers through six fully regulated utilities and is not leaning on a merchant generation or development-platform story.
- Capital posture: by Q1 `2026`, management revised the four-year capital plan to `$41.7B`, tied to expected rate-base growth of `7.9%`, and highlighted an additional `$12B` to `$17B` of transmission opportunity beyond the base plan.
- Guidance posture: Exelon initiated `2026` adjusted operating earnings guidance at `$2.81` to `$2.91` per share at Q4 `2025`, then reaffirmed that range through Q1 and Q2 `2026`.
- Growth framing: management repeatedly points to operating EPS growth near the top end of `5%` to `7%` from `2025` to `2029`, but the growth engine is mainly rate recovery, transmission buildout, distribution modernization, and execution discipline rather than large-load customer conversion.
- Operating identity: Exelon’s messaging centers on top-quartile reliability, faster restoration, affordability, and the ability to keep investing across a large urban utility footprint.

## Open questions

- How much of Exelon’s stated `$12B` to `$17B` transmission opportunity beyond the base plan actually converts into filed and approved capital?
- Does Exelon remain the lower-drama regulated control case in utilities, or does that make it less exposed to the upside currently being priced into more data-center-levered peers?
- As more utility narratives tilt toward hyperscaler and industrial load, does Exelon benefit indirectly through baseline grid spending, or does it need more explicit large-load growth to sustain its valuation relative to peers?
