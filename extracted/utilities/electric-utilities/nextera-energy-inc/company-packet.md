## Annual report takeaways

- NextEra describes itself as the largest electric power and energy infrastructure company in North America. Its investor materials say Florida Power & Light serves approximately `12 million` people across Florida, while NextEra Energy Resources remains one of the largest energy infrastructure development businesses in the U.S.
- The `2025` annual framing is different from AEP's. NextEra is not only selling regulated-utility resilience; it is selling an integrated capital-compounding model built on FPL rate-base growth plus Energy Resources development backlog.
- Full-year `2025` adjusted earnings per share were `$3.71`, up more than `8%` from `2024`, and management said this exceeded the top end of the range it had communicated in December `2025`.
- The company reiterated an `8%+` adjusted EPS compound annual growth rate through `2032` and targeted the same from `2032` through `2035`, which is a much more explicitly long-duration growth posture than the average utility pitch.
- This makes NextEra a useful archive contrast: AEP is the cleaner regulated-grid and affordability story, while NextEra is a hybrid regulated-utility plus development-platform story built around renewables, storage, gas, transmission, and large-load infrastructure.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` GAAP EPS was `$1.50` and adjusted EPS was `$1.15`, up from adjusted EPS of `$1.05` in Q2 `2025`.
- Utility layer: FPL Q2 `2026` EPS was `$0.67` versus `$0.62` a year earlier, with regulatory capital employed growing from `$72.9B` to `$79.7B`, or about `9.3%`.
- Development layer: NextEra Energy Resources adjusted EPS was `$0.62` versus `$0.53` in Q2 `2025`, and the business added `3.6 GW` of new renewables and storage origination to backlog during the quarter.
- Strategic themes: management said power demand was accelerating, emphasized that the company is positioned to serve hyperscalers and large-load customers with a full suite of solutions, and advanced merger-approval filings for the proposed Dominion combination.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` GAAP EPS was `$1.04` and adjusted EPS was `$1.09`, compared with adjusted EPS of `$0.98` in Q1 `2025`.
- Utility layer: FPL Q1 `2026` EPS was `$0.70` versus `$0.64` a year earlier, with regulatory capital employed increasing from `$71.4B` to `$77.7B`, or about `8.8%`.
- Development layer: NextEra Energy Resources adjusted EPS was `$0.50` versus `$0.44` in Q1 `2025`, and the business added a record `4 GW` of new renewables and storage origination, including `1.3 GW` of battery storage.
- Strategic themes: management kept long-term expectations unchanged, continued to target the high end of the `2026` adjusted EPS range of `$3.92` to `$4.02`, and highlighted a growing data-center hub pipeline.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` GAAP EPS was `$0.73` and adjusted EPS was `$0.54`, compared with adjusted EPS of `$0.53` in Q4 `2024`. Full-year `2025` adjusted EPS was `$3.71`.
- Utility layer: FPL Q4 `2025` EPS was `$0.46` versus `$0.41` a year earlier, and full-year `2025` FPL EPS was `$2.42` versus `$2.21` in `2024`.
- Development layer: NextEra Energy Resources adjusted Q4 `2025` EPS was `$0.20`, and the business ended the year with backlog around `29.8 GW` after adding `13.5 GW` during `2025`, including `3.6 GW` in the quarter.
- Strategic themes: management framed `2025` as another year of strong operational and financial execution, emphasized low customer bills at FPL plus development scale at Energy Resources, and said the platform was built for America’s next wave of electricity demand.

Note: for many companies this third slot is a late-2025 quarter needed to complete the trailing three-quarter set as of 2026-08-09.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: NextEra broadens Utilities from pure regulated load-and-grid economics into a hybrid model where regulated earnings, renewable/storage backlog, and large-load development all matter.
- Industrial: the company is one of the clearest archive examples of how industrial and hyperscaler demand translate into generation, storage, transmission, and gas-development opportunity rather than only into utility-rate-base growth.
- Consumer: the consumer angle is mostly through FPL affordability and customer growth in Florida. Management continues to stress that bills are well below the national average even while investment keeps rising.
- Cultural: this is one of the clearest “America needs more electrons” narratives in the archive. The cultural frame is not utility defensiveness; it is infrastructure ambition and build capacity.
- Regulatory: FPL’s rate structure and capital recovery remain core, but regulation is only one part of the story. The strategic surface also includes transmission approvals, large-load tariffs, and the proposed Dominion merger.
- Technology: NextEra is a second-order AI and data-center infrastructure read. The company explicitly ties its hub strategy and large-load opportunity to hyperscaler and power-demand growth.
- Capital allocation: this is a compounding platform with high capital intensity and very explicit long-duration growth targets. FPL rate-base growth and Energy Resources backlog conversion are both capital-allocation stories.
