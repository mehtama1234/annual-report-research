## Annual report takeaways

- Atmos is the right tenth utility because it adds a cleaner gas-only and more purely regulated utility model than the current electric, merchant, nuclear, or retail-plus-wholesale names.
- AnnualReports classifies Atmos as `Utilities / Gas Utilities`, which broadens the utility archive beyond electric, diversified, and other-utility labels already in the set.
- The `2025` annual report frames Atmos as the country's largest fully regulated natural-gas-only distributor, serving roughly `3.4 million` customers across more than `1,400` communities in eight states, while also operating large intrastate pipeline and storage assets in Texas.
- The annual materials are explicit that the growth model is modernization plus regulation rather than commodity upside. The company points to about `$26 billion` of projected capital expenditures through fiscal `2030`, projected rate base growth from about `$21.4 billion` at fiscal `2025` year-end to about `$42 billion` by fiscal `2030`, and `6%` to `8%` forecasted EPS and dividend growth through fiscal `2030`.
- Atmos also gives the archive a different affordability story. Instead of promising customer offsets from hyperscaler load or merchant generation monetization, it argues that fast capital recovery, investment-grade financing, and safety-heavy spend can modernize the gas system while keeping bills relatively low.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: fiscal Q3 `2026` earnings per diluted share were `$7.33` on net income of about `$1.2 billion`.
- Capital and regulation: capital expenditures reached `$3.1 billion` year to date, over `85%` focused on safety and reliability, while annualized regulatory outcomes reached `$355.0 million`.
- Guidance: fiscal `2026` EPS guidance was reaffirmed at `$8.40` to `$8.50`.
- Interpretation: this is not a hyperscaler utility. It is a regulated infrastructure-compounding utility whose story is rate design, customer growth, pipeline and storage relevance, and disciplined system modernization.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: fiscal Q2 `2026` earnings per diluted share were `$5.92` on net income of `$984.9 million`.
- Capital and balance sheet: capital expenditures reached `$2.0 billion`, equity capitalization was `60.9%`, and available liquidity was about `$4.1 billion`.
- Guidance: management raised fiscal `2026` EPS guidance to `$8.40` to `$8.50` from `$8.15` to `$8.35`.
- Interpretation: the raise matters because it shows the regulated gas model still converting capital deployment and regulatory outcomes into earnings acceleration even without the louder electric-utility demand narratives.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: fiscal Q1 `2026` earnings per diluted share were `$2.44` on net income of `$403.0 million`.
- Capital and regulation: capital expenditures were about `$1.0 billion`, over `85%` focused on safety and reliability, with `$122.5 million` of annualized regulatory outcomes implemented.
- Guidance: management affirmed fiscal `2026` EPS guidance of `$8.15` to `$8.35`.
- Interpretation: Q1 set the baseline for the year by reinforcing that Atmos is a steady regulated-capital compounding case rather than a volume-spike or commodity-upside case.

Note: for off-calendar utilities like Atmos, the trailing-three-quarter set as of `2026-08-09` is fiscal Q3 `2026`, Q2 `2026`, and Q1 `2026`.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Atmos gives the utility set a cleaner gas-utility anchor and helps prevent the archive from reading like only an electric-grid and power-generation story.
- Industrial: the industrial read is pipeline modernization, storage, transmission-system integrity, and regulated energy delivery rather than power-market scarcity or dispatchable electricity monetization.
- Consumer: the company is still a household utility, but the consumer message is less about large-load conversion and more about low bills, reliability, safety, and population growth in served communities.
- Cultural: Atmos has a lower-drama narrative than the electric-demand names. The tone is steady modernization, bill affordability, and essential-service durability.
- Regulatory: this is one of the clearest examples in the utilities set of regulation as the earnings architecture itself. Annualized regulatory outcomes and lag reduction are central operating metrics, not side details.
- Energy transition: the company adds an important counterbalance to the electric-buildout story. It shows that gas-system capital, intrastate infrastructure, and natural-gas demand still matter in the `2025` to `2026` operating landscape.
- Capital allocation: the `2030` growth model is built around recurring safety and reliability capex, high credit quality, and constructive recovery, not around acquisitions or merchant optionality.

## Working interpretation

- Atmos is useful because it shows a different answer to infrastructure growth. AEP and Duke convert electric load into regulated electric capital. Vistra and Constellation convert power scarcity into merchant or contracting value. NRG converts large-load growth into customer-backed generation structures. Atmos converts service-territory growth and system modernization into gas-distribution and pipeline rate-base compounding.
- That broadens the utility archive materially. It keeps the set from collapsing into a single electric-grid narrative and makes the sector read more like a full energy-delivery map.
