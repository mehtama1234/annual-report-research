## What changed

- Utility type: Atmos is the cleanest gas-only regulated utility in the current archive. It is not an electric utility and not a merchant power platform. The earnings engine is rate-base growth, safety and reliability spending, and regulatory outcomes tied to natural-gas distribution plus pipeline and storage assets.
- Capital plan: the `2025` annual report frames a large but different infrastructure buildout from the electric utilities set, with about `$26 billion` of projected capital expenditures through fiscal `2030`, more than `85%` tied to safety and reliability, and projected rate base growth from about `$21.4 billion` at fiscal `2025` year-end to roughly `$42 billion` by fiscal `2030`.
- Growth algorithm: management continues to frame the business around `6%` to `8%` annual EPS and dividend growth through fiscal `2030`, backed by constructive regulatory mechanisms and relatively fast earnings recognition on capital spending.
- Demand tone: unlike the electric utilities that are explicitly selling hyperscaler and data-center conversion, Atmos frames demand around customer growth, community expansion, industrial additions, system modernization, and the continuing role of natural gas in growing Southern markets.
- Affordability and regulation: the company pushes a lower-bill and lower-cost-of-capital message, arguing that strong credit ratings, constructive jurisdictions, and safety-focused capital spending support affordability while modernizing the system.
- Quarter progression: fiscal `2026` moved from guidance affirmation in Q1 to a guidance raise in Q2 and then guidance reaffirmation in Q3, while annualized regulatory outcomes rose from `$122.5 million` to `$135.3 million` and then to `$355.0 million`.

## Open questions

- How durable is the fully regulated natural-gas-only model if electrification policy pressure intensifies over the next several years?
- Does Atmos keep winning customer and industrial growth in its jurisdictions fast enough to offset any long-horizon fuel-transition overhang?
- How much more upside remains from constructive regulatory lag reduction once the current large capital program is further into the base?
