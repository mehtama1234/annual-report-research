# ONE Gas Company Packet

## Why ONE Gas belongs in this frontier

ONE Gas gives the frontier an exact-category `Gas Utilities` packet built around pure-play natural-gas distribution rather than a mixed electric-water-gas utility structure. That matters because this frontier is supposed to show how physical infrastructure still shapes the wider system, and gas distribution remains one of the clearest examples of a capital-intensive, regulated network whose economics depend on reliability, customer ownership, rate recovery, and policy durability.

It also helps because ONE Gas is a cleaner comp against `Atmos Energy` and a cleaner contrast with `Essential Utilities`. `Atmos` emphasizes long-duration gas-network compounding at scale. `Essential` mixes water, wastewater, and gas plus merger dynamics. `ONE Gas` sharpens the specific gas-utility read: rate cases, weather normalization, system integrity capital, net margin, and customer growth in constructive jurisdictions.

## Business role

- Pure-play regulated natural-gas distribution utility
- Operator across Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas
- System-integrity and replacement capital allocator
- Infrastructure extension platform benefiting from customer and large-load growth

## Annual report read: FY2025

FY2025 showed a gas utility improving earnings through new rates, customer growth, and regulatory structure rather than through simple volume expansion alone.

Key full-year points:

- net income: `$264.2M`
- diluted EPS: `$4.37`
- adjusted diluted EPS: `$4.48`
- operating income: `$457.5M`
- full-year capital expenditures and asset removal costs: `$759.5M`
- forward-contract settlement proceeds in December: `$205.0M`

The big interpretive point is that ONE Gas wants investors to focus on `net margin` and regulatory recovery rather than headline revenue. As a fully regulated gas utility, fuel-cost pass-throughs do not define the economics. Rate relief, weather normalization, depreciation growth from capital investment, and the timing of regulatory returns do.

Management also explicitly framed `2025` as a year of strong operational execution with long-term opportunity in both residential and large-load growth. That matters because it shifts the gas-utility story away from pure stagnation and toward selective demand growth plus infrastructure reinvestment.

## Quarter chain

### Q4 2025

Q4 `2025` closed the year with stronger profitability and a clearer non-GAAP framework for handling regulatory-lag timing.

- net income: `$86.3M`
- diluted EPS: `$1.42`
- adjusted diluted EPS: `$1.48`
- operating income: `$139.7M`
- capital expenditures and asset removal costs: `$184.1M`

Key read:

- New rates were the biggest driver of operating-income growth.
- Net customer growth in Oklahoma and Texas still added incremental demand.
- Costs rose from labor, depreciation, and outside services, reinforcing that regulated growth still comes with real operating and capital burdens.
- The company introduced a more explicit adjusted framework to reflect the full impact of a regulatory mechanism intended to mitigate regulatory lag.

### Q1 2026

Q1 `2026` is the clearest quarter for understanding the underlying model because weather was unusually warm but earnings still improved.

- net income: `$128.7M`
- diluted EPS: `$2.04`
- adjusted diluted EPS: `$2.11`
- operating income: `$189.6M`
- capital expenditures and asset removal costs: `$169.6M`

Key read:

- Weather across the service territory was `20.5%` warmer than normal and `24.6%` warmer than the prior year.
- Even so, weather normalization mechanisms and new rates kept the quarter strong.
- Management highlighted workforce investment, outside services, and lower delivered volumes as offsets, which makes this a good packet for showing how regulated gas utilities defend earnings when weather and raw demand would otherwise work against them.

### Q2 2026

Q2 `2026` strengthened the same thesis and pushed guidance higher.

- net income: `$46.8M`
- diluted EPS: `$0.74`
- adjusted diluted EPS: `$0.82`
- operating income: `$82.7M`
- capital expenditures and asset removal costs: `$188.3M`

Key read:

- Management raised its `2026` adjusted earnings expectations to the upper half of prior ranges.
- New rates again drove the biggest contribution to operating-income growth.
- Weather was `42%` warmer than normal and `28%` warmer than the prior year, but normalization mechanisms again protected the economics.
- The quarter also showed line-extension revenue and customer growth in Oklahoma and Texas, which helps explain why management keeps emphasizing constructive jurisdictions and expansion potential.

## Frontier interpretation

### 1. Gas-utility economics are about regulation and recovery mechanics more than fuel demand alone

ONE Gas is a useful reminder that the gas network is not best understood as a commodity exposure. The economic engine is approved rate relief, recovery mechanisms, and the ability to keep earning on system integrity investment while smoothing weather volatility.

### 2. Physical resilience still requires heavy recurring capital

The company’s capex profile makes clear that this is a constant replacement and maintenance business. Aging infrastructure, safety, system integrity, and extension to new customers all require steady capital deployment.

### 3. Weather risk remains real, but policy design can mute it

This packet is especially good because the company repeatedly disclosed unusually warm conditions while still delivering stronger operating performance. Weather normalization mechanisms are therefore not a footnote. They are a core reason the model remains investable.

### 4. Energy-transition pressure does not eliminate gas-network relevance

ONE Gas is not an energy-transition winner in the same way as `First Solar`, but it shows why gas infrastructure remains economically relevant: millions of customers still depend on it, regulators still allow recovery on it, and certain regions still support load growth and network expansion.

## Why this packet improves the frontier

`Atmos Energy` already gave the frontier a gas-only regulated-capital compounding anchor. `ONE Gas` materially deepens the lane because it adds:

- a second exact-category `Gas Utilities` packet
- a sharper read on weather normalization as an earnings stabilizer
- a clearer non-GAAP regulatory-lag discussion
- a stronger emphasis on customer extensions and constructive-jurisdiction growth

That makes the `physical infrastructure and energy-transition edge` lane more comparative and less dependent on a single gas-utility model.
