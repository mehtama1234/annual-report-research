## What changed in this window

- ONE Gas sharpened the case that regulated gas utilities are not simple commodity bets; much of the economic story sits in net margin, weather normalization mechanisms, and approved rate recovery rather than raw fuel prices.
- Management leaned into a non-GAAP framing around regulatory-lag mitigation, signaling that timing differences between investment and rate recovery are central to how the business should be read.
- Customer growth and line-extension demand in Texas and Oklahoma reinforced that even mature utility infrastructure can still have meaningful expansion vectors.
- Capital intensity remained high, with full-year `2025` capital expenditures and asset removal costs at `$759.5M` and `2026` capital investment guidance at roughly `$800M` plus around `$230M` for new-customer extensions.
- The company’s public messaging also highlighted large-load growth, system integrity, and affordability, making the packet useful for reading how gas utilities defend relevance even as energy-transition narratives intensify elsewhere.
