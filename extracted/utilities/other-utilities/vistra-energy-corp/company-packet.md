## Annual report takeaways

- Vistra is the right seventh utility because it fills the business-model gap the current set still had. It adds an integrated retail-plus-merchant-power platform with competitive generation, hedging, and commodity exposure rather than another primarily regulated transmission-and-distribution utility.
- Official company materials describe Vistra as a leading integrated retail electricity and power generation company based in Irving, Texas, with a fleet spanning natural gas, nuclear, coal, solar, and battery energy storage. The investor-site framing emphasizes reliability, affordability, sustainability, and a customer-centric retail business.
- AnnualReports currently classifies Vistra only at the sector level under `Utilities` and leaves the industry as `--None--`. That taxonomy gap is actually informative: Vistra does not fit neatly into the same regulated-utility buckets as AEP, Duke, Exelon, or Con Edison.
- The annual and quarterly materials consistently frame Vistra as a company positioned for higher power demand through a dispatchable generation fleet, active hedging, selective acquisitions, retail scale, and long-dated power purchase agreements rather than through a traditional rate-base compounding story.
- Vistra's strategic point is that the current power buildout is not only a regulated-grid story. Merchant and competitive power platforms can also become direct beneficiaries when reliability, data-center demand, and long-term corporate PPAs increase the value of dispatchable generation and nuclear capacity.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` GAAP net income was `$305 million`, including an unrealized hedge loss expected to settle in future years of `$472 million`, and Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA was `$1,767 million`.
- Guidance: management reaffirmed full-year `2026` Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA guidance of `$6.8 billion` to `$7.6 billion` and Ongoing Operations Adjusted FCFbG guidance of `$3.925 billion` to `$4.725 billion`.
- Management message: the Q2 release emphasized strong execution, more than `30%` year-over-year EBITDA growth, continued progress on the pending Cogentrix acquisition, and Vistra's role as a preferred power provider through the Helix Digital Infrastructure venture with KKR KIA and NVIDIA.
- Structural themes: management also highlighted `97%` or greater commercial availability across the fleet during recent extreme heat in Texas and PJM, which underlines the central strategic claim that dispatchable generation is becoming more valuable as demand rises and reliability tightens.
- Filing note: SEC recent filings showed the Q2 `2026` `8-K` filed on `2026-08-07`, but no corresponding Q2 `2026` `10-Q` was visible in the recent submissions feed as of `2026-08-09`.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` GAAP net income was `$1,029 million`, including an unrealized hedge gain expected to settle in future years of `$723 million`, and Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA was `$1,494 million` versus `$1,240 million` a year earlier.
- Guidance: management reaffirmed `2026` guidance at `$6.8 billion` to `$7.6 billion` of Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA and `$3.925 billion` to `$4.725 billion` of Ongoing Operations Adjusted FCFbG.
- Management message: the Q1 release tied the story to strategic expansion and balance-sheet strengthening. Management emphasized the pending `5,500-MW` Cogentrix acquisition, signed long-term Meta PPAs at PJM nuclear sites, strong load growth across primary markets, and an investment-grade rating upgrade at a second major agency.
- Structural themes: year-over-year EBITDA growth was driven primarily by higher realized energy and capacity prices plus the first-quarter contribution from the Lotus acquisition, while the retail segment was pressured by mild weather.
- Operating identity: Vistra reads like a fleet-optimization and market-pricing company with retail integration, not like a standard regulated utility.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: full-year `2025` GAAP net income was `$944 million`, including an unrealized hedge loss expected to settle in future years of `$808 million`; cash flow from operations was `$4,070 million`; Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA was `$5,912 million`; and Ongoing Operations Adjusted FCFbG was `$3,592 million`.
- Guidance: management introduced `2026` guidance of `$6.8 billion` to `$7.6 billion` of Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA and `$3.925 billion` to `$4.725 billion` of Ongoing Operations Adjusted FCFbG, and said results exceeded the midpoint of original `2025` guidance by about `$112 million` of EBITDA and `$292 million` of FCFbG.
- Management message: year-end `2025` materials framed Vistra as a company reshaping itself around dispatchable generation, retail scale, and long-duration contracted upside. Management highlighted AWS and Meta nuclear PPAs, the closed Lotus acquisition, the pending Cogentrix acquisition, solar construction, and planned Permian Basin gas units.
- Structural themes: the quarter made explicit that merchant generation is being partially transformed into contracted infrastructure. As of `2026-02-18`, the company had hedged about `100%` of expected generation volumes for `2026`, `84%` for `2027`, and `58%` for `2028`.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: Vistra gives the utilities set the merchant and competitive-generation layer it was missing. The archive can now compare regulated wires and rate-base economics directly against a power-market and hedging-driven model.
- Industrial: the industrial read is dispatchable-power scarcity, gas and nuclear asset value, and acquisition-driven generation expansion rather than only regulated transmission and distribution buildout.
- Consumer: Vistra still matters on the consumer side because it is also a major retail electricity provider. That gives it a direct view into weather sensitivity, customer pricing, and affordability that sits alongside the generation story.
- Cultural: the narrative is less civic and more market-facing than Con Edison or Exelon. Reliability still matters, but the emphasis is on commercial execution, fleet availability, and using a broad generation fleet to meet rising demand profitably.
- Regulatory: the company is not free from regulation, but regulation is not the main earnings architecture. FERC approval for Cogentrix, nuclear-site PPAs, and market rules matter more than a classic multi-state rate-case cadence.
- Technology: Vistra is one of the clearest places where the utility archive intersects directly with AI and digital infrastructure. Nuclear PPAs with AWS and Meta and the Helix Digital Infrastructure announcement with NVIDIA show the power-system side of the compute buildout from the merchant-power angle.
- Capital allocation: the company combines acquisitions, project development, fleet investment, and large share repurchases. That makes it a more capital-markets-driven utility read than the traditional regulated names.

## Working interpretation

- Vistra is the cleanest proof so far that the archive's utility theme is not just regulated capex inflation and rate-base growth. It is also about who controls scarce dispatchable power, who can contract it intelligently, and who can absorb large-load demand through existing fleets plus selective buildout.
- The company also sharpens the merchant-versus-regulated contrast. AEP Duke Exelon and Con Edison convert demand into filings, rate structures, and approved investment. Vistra converts demand into realized prices, hedge books, PPAs, acquisitions, and fleet availability.
- That distinction matters for the broader archive because it separates the physical power bottleneck from the regulatory monetization path. Both are central. They just monetize in different ways.
