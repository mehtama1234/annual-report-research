## Annual report takeaways

- American Water is the right next utility because it fills the largest remaining gap in the current utility archive: a scaled, regulated water-and-wastewater utility rather than another electric, gas, merchant, or nuclear platform.
- AnnualReports classifies American Water as `Utilities / Water Utilities`, which broadens the utility archive beyond electric, diversified, gas, and other-utility labels already in the set.
- The `2025` annual report frames American Water as the largest regulated water and wastewater utility in the United States, serving approximately `14 million` people.
- The annual materials make the growth model explicit: the company invested `$3.2B` in capital in `2025`, added nearly `40,000` customer connections through acquisitions and organic growth, and plans to invest between `$46B` and `$48B` over the next decade in infrastructure renewal, water quality, resiliency, technology, and acquisitions.
- American Water also gives the archive a different affordability story. Instead of arguing that hyperscaler load offsets household bills, it emphasizes average residential water bills staying well under `1%` of median household income while still pursuing regulated capital recovery and acquisition-led system expansion.

## Quarter-by-quarter takeaways

### Most recent quarter

- Results: Q2 `2026` GAAP earnings were `$1.61` per share and adjusted earnings were also `$1.61` per share, with year-to-date adjusted earnings of `$2.62` per share.
- Capital and growth: the company said its `2026` capital investment plan of `$3.7B` remained on track and that acquisitions had added about `52,000` customer connections through `2026-06-30`, including Nexus Water systems.
- Guidance: management affirmed `2026` adjusted EPS guidance of `$6.02` to `$6.12` and reaffirmed long-term targets.
- Interpretation: this is a regulated consolidation-and-infrastructure compounding story. The operating logic is water-system investment, rate-case execution, and acquisition integration rather than power-market scarcity or data-center load conversion.

### Quarter minus 1

- Results: Q1 `2026` GAAP earnings were `$1.00` per share and adjusted earnings were `$1.01` per share.
- Capital and regulation: the company invested `$652M` in the first quarter, planned about `$3.7B` of full-year `2026` capital deployment, and said authorized annualized revenues since `2026-01-01` had increased by `$89M`.
- Guidance: management affirmed `2026` adjusted EPS guidance of `$6.02` to `$6.12` and reiterated `7%` to `9%` long-term EPS and dividend growth targets.
- Interpretation: Q1 reinforces that American Water converts rate cases, infrastructure surcharges, and acquisitions into earnings growth while trying to preserve the affordability and civic-utility framing that the sector requires.

### Quarter minus 2

- Results: Q4 `2025` adjusted EPS were `$1.24` and full-year adjusted EPS were `$5.64`; full-year GAAP EPS were `$5.69`.
- Capital and strategy: `2025` capital investment reached `$3.2B`; the year-end presentation showed about `$582M` of acquisitions under agreement representing about `104,000` customer connections, alongside six completed rate cases and seven active cases.
- Guidance: management affirmed `2026` adjusted EPS guidance of `$6.02` to `$6.12` and long-term EPS and dividend growth targets of `7%` to `9%`.
- Interpretation: year-end `2025` shows the water-utility version of utility growth infrastructure: steady regulated capital, acquisition-led connection growth, and affordability discipline instead of generation scarcity or merchant optionality.

## Signals to feed into higher-level analysis

- Sector: American Water gives the utility set a true water-and-wastewater anchor and keeps the archive from reading like only an electricity-and-gas system map.
- Industrial: the industrial read is aging-network replacement, water quality, resiliency, municipal-system roll-up, and permitting / regulatory execution rather than power-market volatility.
- Consumer: the consumer message is especially direct here because water bills and household affordability are central to the investment case and the political compact.
- Cultural: the tone is lower drama and more civic than the merchant and large-load power names. The language is reliability, affordability, water quality, and local infrastructure stewardship.
- Regulatory: this is one of the cleanest examples in the utility set of rates, infrastructure surcharges, and acquisition approvals acting as the earnings architecture itself.
- Consolidation: American Water adds a meaningful industry-consolidation angle because customer growth comes not only from organic expansion but also from municipal and system acquisitions.
- Capital allocation: the model is long-duration regulated compounding through a decade-scale water-infrastructure plan rather than through a generation buildout or commodity exposure.

## Working interpretation

- American Water is useful because it shows a different answer to infrastructure scarcity. AEP and Duke convert electric load into regulated electric capital. Vistra and Constellation convert power scarcity into merchant or contracting value. Atmos converts gas-distribution modernization into regulated rate-base compounding. American Water converts water quality, system resiliency, municipal fragmentation, and acquisition-led connection growth into a regulated infrastructure compounding model.
- That broadens the utility archive materially. It extends the sector from energy delivery into water delivery and makes the utility stack read more like an essential-network map than only a power map.
