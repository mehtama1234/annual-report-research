## What changed

- Utility type: American Water adds the clearest pure water-and-wastewater utility model in the current archive. It is a regulated infrastructure utility, but the core asset is not electric generation or gas delivery. It is water, wastewater, water quality, aging-pipe replacement, resiliency, and municipal-system consolidation.
- Capital plan: the `2025` annual report frames a long-duration infrastructure buildout around water systems, with `$3.2B` invested in `2025`, a `2026` capital plan of about `$3.7B`, and a decade plan of roughly `$46B` to `$48B`.
- Growth algorithm: the company continues to frame the model around `7%` to `9%` long-term EPS and dividend growth, supported by regulated capital recovery, acquisitions, and steady affordability discipline.
- Demand tone: unlike the electric utilities, the demand story is not hyperscaler load conversion. It is population growth, municipal-system acquisition, water-quality spend, resiliency, wastewater need, and essential-service infrastructure across regulated territories.
- Affordability and regulation: American Water makes affordability central, explicitly keeping average residential water bills under `1%` of median household income while still compounding regulated capital and acquisition-led customer growth.
- Quarter progression: the trailing-quarter set shows steady earnings conversion rather than a sharp guidance swing. The company moved from adjusted EPS of `$1.24` in Q4 `2025` to `$1.01` in Q1 `2026` and `$1.61` in Q2 `2026`, while keeping `2026` adjusted EPS guidance at `$6.02` to `$6.12`.
