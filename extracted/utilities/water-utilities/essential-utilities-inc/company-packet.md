# Essential Utilities Company Packet

## Why Essential Utilities belongs in this frontier

Essential Utilities gives the frontier a second water-utility comparison that is materially different from `American Water`. It is still a regulated essential-network operator, but it combines water, wastewater, and natural gas inside one balance sheet and then overlays acquisition-driven municipal consolidation, PFAS compliance, pipe replacement, and a pending mega-merger with `American Water`.

That makes it useful for this frontier because the assignment is not just to label utilities as defensive. It is to explain how essential-service infrastructure actually scales, how customer ownership works inside regulated systems, and how capital intensity, rate recovery, and resilience mandates reshape the economics of supposedly boring categories.

## Business role

- Regulated water and wastewater network operator
- Regulated natural-gas network operator through `Peoples`
- Municipal-system acquisition consolidator
- Large-scale infrastructure reinvestor exposed to PFAS, pipe replacement, and rate-case execution

## Annual report read: FY2025

FY2025 showed a company that still grew earnings and capital deployment, but with the economics driven by regulation, infrastructure, and system scale rather than consumer-like demand expansion.

Key full-year points:

- net income: `$616.4M`
- EPS: `$2.20`
- infrastructure investment: record `$1.4B`
- water segment rate awards or surcharges in `2025`: `$92.6M` of incremental annual revenue
- gas segment rate awards or surcharges in `2025`: `$8.9M` of incremental annual revenue

The annual read is that Essential is monetizing resilience itself. Pipe replacement, water-quality compliance, PFAS readiness, and acquired municipal-system integration are not background tasks. They are the engine of rate-base growth.

The second big annual theme is strategic scale. By early `2026`, shareholder approval had already been secured for the proposed merger with `American Water`, which moved the company from a pure regulated-growth story into a regulatory-combination and utility-consolidation story.

## Quarter chain

### Q4 2025

Q4 `2025` closed the year with decent earnings but made clear that even regulated utilities absorb cost pressure and weather-linked volatility.

- net income: `$132.7M`
- EPS: `$0.47`
- regulated water revenue: `$329.4M`
- regulated gas revenue: `$361.3M`

Key read:

- Water benefited from rate increases and wastewater pricing.
- Gas benefited from higher rates and volumes, but purchased-gas costs and program expenses remained meaningful.
- Operating costs rose in both segments, reminding the frontier that regulated resilience still has to be executed operationally, not just financed.

### Q1 2026

Q1 `2026` showed how a regulated utility can post solid underlying investment momentum while reported earnings still get distorted by weather, one-time items, and transaction costs.

- revenue: `$861.8M`
- GAAP net income: `$224.4M`
- EPS: `$0.79` GAAP / `$0.83` adjusted
- infrastructure investment in the quarter: `$269M`

Key read:

- The comparison versus Q1 `2025` looked weaker partly because the prior year had unusual favorable items.
- Water revenues still rose on recovery mechanisms and volume.
- Gas revenues rose on purchased-gas costs and regulatory recoveries, showing how this is not a clean water-only comp despite the AnnualReports taxonomy.
- Merger preparation costs and abnormal winter conditions added noise without changing the larger capital-growth thesis.

### Q2 2026

Q2 `2026` is the strongest frontier quarter because it clarifies the company’s current shape: a regulated-infrastructure compounder whose story is now partly merger-driven.

- revenue: `$530.9M`
- GAAP net income: `$105.7M`
- EPS: `$0.37` GAAP / `$0.38` adjusted
- first-half infrastructure investment: `$662.2M`
- 2026 infrastructure target: `$1.7B`

Key read:

- Water kept growing on regulatory recoveries and volume.
- Gas revenues declined year over year in the quarter because warm weather reduced volumes, underscoring that essential-service businesses still have weather sensitivity.
- The merger with `American Water` remained central, with additional regulatory approvals from `Kentucky`, `Ohio`, and `Virginia`.
- Essential also kept emphasizing acquisition pipeline depth, including pending systems serving more than `200,000` customer equivalents.

## Frontier interpretation

### 1. Utility resilience is a capital-allocation business, not just a defensive category

Essential shows that water resilience depends on persistent capital deployment, rate-case execution, and physical-system modernization. The story is not low drama. It is relentless infrastructure spending plus regulatory permission to recover it.

### 2. Water scarcity and water quality turn directly into regulated spending programs

PFAS compliance, main replacement, wastewater-system upgrades, and acquired municipal-system remediation all translate into real capital intensity. That makes water a direct physical-infrastructure lane, not a side category.

### 3. Customer ownership in utilities is mediated through regulation, not branding

Unlike media or device companies, Essential does not monetize discretionary engagement. It monetizes long-lived essential-service relationships whose economics are shaped by state commissions, approved surcharges, and network reliability obligations.

### 4. Consolidation is part of the resilience story

Essential’s municipal-acquisition pipeline and pending merger with `American Water` show that resilience increasingly scales through consolidation. Smaller systems often lack the capital base or operating scale to handle modern compliance and infrastructure burdens on their own.

## Why this packet improves the frontier

`American Water` already gave the frontier a large water-utility anchor around affordability, resilience, and regulated infrastructure renewal. `Essential Utilities` materially deepens the lane because it adds:

- a blended water-plus-gas utility model
- a more explicit acquisition-rollup engine
- a larger merger-driven strategic overlay
- a clearer view of PFAS, pipe replacement, and municipal-system integration as investable themes

That makes the `physical infrastructure and energy-transition edge` lane less dependent on one water-utility read and better connected to the broader frontier theme that physical systems, not just digital interfaces, determine what can actually scale.
