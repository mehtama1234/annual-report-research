# AnnualReports Browse-Tree Blind-Spot Mandate

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

## Purpose

This file is the clearest operating answer to the instruction:

"cover the areas not really covered by the `AnnualReports` website because other threads are covering the ordinary sectors."

It is not a claim that `AnnualReports` has no categories for these companies.

It is a claim that the live [Browse by Industry](https://www.annualreports.com/Browse/Industry) tree does not organize these areas in a way that is good enough for cross-company research on consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, and control-layer systems.

## The short answer

Other threads should cover the normal `AnnualReports` sector and industry tree.

This thread should cover the systems that the browse tree handles badly even when the companies themselves appear somewhere on the site.

The blind spot is structural, not literal.

The problem is not "the website has no page for these companies."

The problem is "the website does not classify the important system they belong to."

Thin, stale, awkward, sparse, or incomplete browse-tree page quality is not evidence by itself that this thread should claim the company.

## What this thread owns

### 1. Behind-the-shelf consumer infrastructure

Take companies whose real value sits behind the shelf, inside packaging, waste, contamination control, plant support, substrate flow, dosing, closures, or delivery support.

Examples:

- `Graphic Packaging`
- `Sonoco`
- `AptarGroup`
- `Silgan`
- `Clearwater Paper`
- `International Paper`
- `Packaging Corporation of America`
- `Waste Management`
- `Clean Harbors`
- `CECO Environmental`
- `Veralto`
- `Ecolab`

Why this belongs here:

- `AnnualReports` can place them in `Packaging & Containers`, `Paper & Paper Products`, `Waste Management`, or `Pollution & Treatment Controls`
- that still fails to show the behind-the-shelf consumer system they collectively control

### 2. Hidden connectivity and access-layer ownership

Take companies whose real value is owning scarce physical or orbital access beneath modern connectivity demand.

Examples:

- `American Tower`
- `Crown Castle`
- `SBA Communications`
- `Cogent`
- `Lumen Technologies`
- `Iridium`
- `Viasat`

Why this belongs here:

- the browse tree can scatter them across `REIT`, communications, or equipment labels
- that does not explain tower rent, route-density access, broader fiber-backbone ownership, resilient orbital coverage, or scarce infrastructure ownership

### 3. Control-layer and connectivity infrastructure

Take companies whose real importance comes from traffic control, trust governance, optical transport, installed-base network control, or connectivity-input supply rather than a plain sector label.

Examples:

- `Cloudflare`
- `Akamai`
- `Cisco`
- `Arista Networks`
- `Palo Alto Networks`
- `Ciena`
- `CommScope`
- `Corning`

Why this belongs here:

- `AnnualReports` can label them software, networking, communications, or hardware
- that still misses the control-layer and connectivity system they represent

### 4. Physical execution and embedded workflow systems

Take companies whose real importance comes from workflow embedment, access-over-ownership, procurement control, branch-density execution, or civic-failure-response support rather than a plain rental or distribution label.

Examples:

- `United Rentals`
- `Fastenal`
- `Ferguson`
- `WESCO`
- `Applied Industrial Technologies`
- `Core & Main`

Why this belongs here:

- `AnnualReports` can label them rental, distribution, or industrial-service names
- that still misses the embedded-workflow and physical-execution system they represent

### 5. Consumer behavior and cultural-demand systems

Take companies when the real research value is the recurring behavior or cultural pattern, not the formal product category.

Examples:

- comfort and casualization
- routine-use trust
- value-hunt and bargain discovery
- ritual and stimulation
- beauty, fit, and self-presentation
- fandom, play, and participation

Example names:

- `Crocs`
- `Deckers`
- `Birkenstock`
- `Ulta`
- `e.l.f. Beauty`
- `Hasbro`
- `Mattel`
- `Roblox`
- `Starbucks`
- `Monster Beverage`
- `Brown-Forman`
- `TJX`

Why this belongs here:

- the browse tree is product-led
- the archive needs behavior-led and culture-led interpretation

### 6. Relationship ownership, wallet, and coordination systems

Take companies whose real value is demand ownership, loyalty structure, marketplace coordination, multi-sided monetization, or participation control.

Examples:

- `Amazon`
- `Airbnb`
- `Booking`
- `DoorDash`
- `Instacart`
- `eBay`
- `Etsy`
- `Nike`
- `YETI`
- `Warby Parker`
- `Starbucks`
- `Costco`
- `American Express`

Why this belongs here:

- the browse tree does not classify who owns the customer relationship, wallet loop, status ladder, or coordination layer

### 7. Cross-sector built-environment systems

Take companies when the real system crosses `Financial`, `Real Estate`, `Industrial Goods`, `Services`, `Consumer Goods`, and `Basic Materials`.

Examples:

- `Prologis`
- `Equinix`
- `Digital Realty`
- `Home Depot`
- `Lowe's`
- `Builders FirstSource`
- `CBRE`
- `AECOM`
- `Dycom`

Why this belongs here:

- the browse tree splits one operating chain into legacy buckets
- the archive needs the full infrastructure and upkeep stack visible in one place

## What this thread should usually leave alone

Leave these to the ordinary sector threads unless the system-level explanation is clearly stronger:

- standard upstream materials and commodity names
- plain industrial backlog stories
- ordinary utilities
- generic banks and insurers
- straightforward healthcare packetization
- ordinary software names with no stronger control, access, coordination, or attention-system angle
- ordinary consumer brands whose only value is more category coverage

## Operating rule

Take the work here only if the better question is:

"what system is this really part of?"

Leave it to the normal sector thread if the better question is:

"what industry page does this belong to?"

Do not override that rule just because the industry page looks underbuilt.

Inside the mature infrastructure lanes, use the matching extension shortlist before taking another company so the next move is reuse-first and subtype-opening rather than generic infrastructure backfill.
