## Plain-English read

If the other threads are covering ordinary sectors and industries, this thread should not spend its time backfilling another standard `AnnualReports` bucket.

This thread should own the parts of the economy that the site does not really organize as first-class systems.

In other words:

- if `AnnualReports` already gives a company a reasonably clean industry home and another thread is covering that browse-tree lane, this thread should leave it alone
- this thread should claim only the names whose real value appears after breaking the browse-tree label apart

The blind spot is not "missing companies."

The blind spot is "missing system-level categories."

That means this thread should not claim a company merely because its ordinary industry page looks thin.

It should claim the company only when the system-level explanation is stronger than the browse-tree explanation.

The lane now has a practical first-pass routing model:

- screen the name first with the short ownership rule
- consumer and cultural blind spots should be tested first through the `consumer-behavior` core
- hidden-layer and infrastructural blind spots should be tested first through the `infrastructure` core

That default routing layer is now often more useful than jumping directly into a narrower lane memo.

## What AnnualReports does not cover well enough

The sections below describe the full blind-spot territory.

They do not mean every listed lane is default first-claim work for this thread.

The stricter current rule is:

- default first-claim work belongs to the four core infrastructure lanes
- broader consumer, platform, relationship, and built-environment lanes remain valid but should usually be conditional and subtype-driven rather than default first-claim work

### 1. Retail as a system

The site scatters retail-like companies across `Services`, `Consumer Goods`, `Retail`, and `Technology`.

This thread should therefore own:

- cross-format retail comparison
- inventory versus marketplace retail
- membership retail
- off-price and discovery-led retail
- project-led and replenishment-led retail

### 2. Relationship ownership

The site does not classify companies by who owns the customer relationship.

This thread should therefore own:

- DTC versus wholesale control
- distributor dependence versus direct demand ownership
- thick versus thin relationship systems
- relationship migration from transactional to recurring

### 3. Loyalty, wallet, and status capture

The site does not group companies by stored value, points currency, membership fees, or status ladders.

This thread should therefore own:

- wallet systems
- membership systems
- partner-funded loyalty systems
- low-friction frequency conditioning

### 4. Marketplace and coordination systems

The site can label a company software, services, travel, or retail without explaining whether the real engine is coordination, trust, participation, or second-layer monetization.

This thread should therefore own:

- marketplace systems
- local-demand coordination
- trust marketplaces
- ecosystem-control and multi-sided monetization

### 5. Recurring attention and cultural-distribution systems

The site does not separate businesses by whether they monetize recurring attention through subscriptions, advertising, creator supply, devices, or franchise-linked content ecosystems.

This thread should therefore own:

- paid attention systems
- ad-funded attention systems
- mixed subscription and advertising systems
- creator and audience-distribution systems
- device, operating-system, and content-bundle attention control

### 6. Consumer behavior and cultural demand systems

The site classifies product categories better than it classifies recurring behavior.

This thread should therefore own:

- comfort and casualization
- value-hunt and bargain discovery
- routine-use trust and replenishment
- beauty, fit, and self-presentation
- ritual, occasion, and stimulation
- fandom, play, and collectible participation

### 7. Hidden consumer infrastructure

The site does not naturally connect visible consumer demand to the infrastructure behind it.

This thread should therefore own:

- packaging and substrate systems
- waste and environmental-service systems
- merchant-enablement and delivery-layer systems
- consumer-adjacent plant and route-density infrastructure

The archive now has a cleaner delivery-layer split available through [C.H. Robinson Versus UPS Versus DoorDash Boundary Comparison](/home/manishmehta/ui-projects/annual-report-research/extracted/themes/ch-robinson-vs-ups-vs-doordash-boundary-comparison-2026-08-10.md), which separates freight orchestration, parcel-network density, and local merchant-demand coordination rather than flattening them into old service or software labels.

### 8. Modern built-environment systems

The site splits one real operating chain across `Financial`, `Real Estate`, `Services`, `Industrial Goods`, `Consumer Goods`, and `Basic Materials`.

This thread should therefore own:

- logistics-property systems
- digital-capacity property systems
- shelter affordability and upkeep systems
- water, comfort, and civic-flow systems
- ownership versus intermediation versus execution comparisons

### 9. Control-layer and connectivity infrastructure systems

The site can call these companies internet, hardware, or networking names without showing which ones control edge trust, enterprise network governance, optical transport, or upstream optical-content supply.

This thread should therefore own:

- edge-control and application-governance systems
- enterprise-trust and cyber-standardization systems
- optical-transport and data-movement systems
- upstream optical-content and connectivity-input systems

### 10. Hidden connectivity and access-layer ownership systems

The site can call these companies communications real estate or specialty real estate without showing that they are monetizing the scarce physical access layer beneath mobile and bandwidth dependence.

This thread should therefore own:

- global tower-ownership systems
- domestic tower simplification systems
- pure-play tower-rent systems
- adjacent route-density or private-infrastructure connectivity structures when they materially sharpen that split

### 11. Physical execution and embedded-workflow systems

The site can call these companies rental, industrial-supply, or contractor-distribution names without showing that the real value comes from workflow control, embedded presence, and access-over-ownership.

This thread should therefore own:

- access-over-ownership execution systems
- embedded procurement and replenishment systems
- branch-density and contractor-simplification systems
- engineering-heavy MRO or utility-specific workflow systems only when they materially sharpen the existing split

## What this thread should usually leave alone

Unless a company clearly sharpens one of the systems above, leave these to the standard sector threads:

- ordinary materials or commodity producers
- plain industrial backlog stories
- standard utility packetization
- generic bank and insurer coverage
- straightforward healthcare packetization
- routine consumer-sector backfill with no stronger behavior or relationship angle

## Operating rule

The selection question for this thread should be:

not "which industry page is still thin?"

but "which important system still has no useful home inside the AnnualReports browse tree?"

The practical default should be strict:

- covered browse-tree work belongs to the other threads
- this thread owns the residual systems that the browse tree cannot really express
- a company should move into this lane only when the system-level explanation is stronger than the industry-page explanation
