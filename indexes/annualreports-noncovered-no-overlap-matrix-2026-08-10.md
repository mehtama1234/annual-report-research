## Absolute default

Leave the company to the normal sector thread unless the blind-spot explanation is clearly stronger than the formal `AnnualReports` industry explanation.

The test is not whether the company exists somewhere on the site.

The test is whether the site classifies the real system well enough that another thread should own it.

Do not treat a thin, stale, awkward, or sparsely populated browse-tree industry page as evidence by itself that the blind-spot thread should own the company.

Use the short [AnnualReports Noncovered Ownership Rule](/home/manishmehta/ui-projects/annual-report-research/indexes/annualreports-noncovered-ownership-rule-2026-08-10.md) first when a worker needs a fast screen before using this stricter overlap table.

## Two-route gate

Before assigning a company here, force it through one of these two routes:

- `Consumer behavior`: `comfort`, `value`, `routine`, `ritual`, `identity`, `participation`
- `Infrastructure`: `control`, `access`, `workflow`

If neither route is strong, do not keep the company in this lane.

## Fast handoff rule

If a company could plausibly be covered by both this lane and a standard sector lane:

1. keep it here only if the system memo gets stronger without relying on ordinary category language
2. otherwise push it back to the normal sector thread

In shorthand:

- weak browse-tree system explanation means keep it here
- good enough browse-tree industry explanation means send it back
- thin browse-tree page quality alone means nothing

Inside the mature infrastructure lanes, use the matching extension shortlist before keeping another company here so the next move is subtype-opening rather than generic packet accumulation.

## Practical result

This thread is not the cleanup crew for uncovered industries.

It is the owner of the system-level categories that the `AnnualReports` browse tree cannot express cleanly.
