## Core test

If the best question is "what sector is this in?", leave it to the normal sector thread.

If the better question is "what system is this really part of?", it likely belongs here.

## Negative screen against the live browse tree

Do not take a company for this lane just because its page sits inside one of the ordinary live `AnnualReports` browse buckets such as:

- `Consumer Goods`
- `Services`
- `Technology`
- `Financial`
- `Healthcare`
- `Industrial Goods`
- `Real Estate`

That by itself proves nothing.

The company belongs here only if the live browse-tree label is materially weaker than the real operating system, behavior loop, relationship model, or hidden infrastructure role.

## Claim this lane only if at least one is true

1. The `AnnualReports` label is a weak explanation of the real operating system.
2. The real story is cross-sector behavior, not sector membership.
3. The key signal is relationship ownership, loyalty, participation, attention, marketplace structure, or embedded infrastructure.
4. The company materially sharpens an existing blind-spot comparison or opens a genuinely new one.

## Leave it to other threads if most of this is true

- it is mainly ordinary sector coverage
- the `AnnualReports` industry label is basically good enough
- it only adds another example of an already-proven pattern
- the main reason to take it is ease of filing collection
- the argument for ownership is only that the current industry page feels thin or outdated

## Core blind-spot ownership buckets

This lane should mainly own, by default first-claim priority:

- behind-the-shelf consumer infrastructure
- control-layer and connectivity infrastructure
- hidden connectivity and access-layer ownership
- physical execution and embedded-workflow systems

The following remain valid blind-spot lanes, but they should usually be conditional rather than default first-claim work:

- platform, marketplace, and ecosystem control
- fandom, play, participation, and collectibles
- recurring attention and cultural-distribution systems
- beauty, identity, fit, and self-presentation demand
- DTC, channel control, and owned-demand systems
- loyalty, wallet, membership, and status systems
- value-hunt and bargain-discovery behavior
- comfort, casualization, and practical-wellness demand
- routine-use trust and replenishment behavior

## Shortest operating version

- other threads cover `AnnualReports` categories
- this lane covers the categories `AnnualReports` cannot explain well
- thin browse-tree pages alone are not a claim reason

## Practical routing instruction

Before claiming a company, ask:

1. does it improve a system map?
2. does it improve a boundary comparison?
3. does it correct a weak source-taxonomy label?

If the answer is no to all three, leave it.

If the company lands inside one of the mature infrastructure lanes, use the matching extension shortlist before treating it as the next blind-spot move.
