## Core rule

This thread should own areas, not ordinary sectors.

If the best reason to add a company is only that its formal `AnnualReports` industry is still uncovered, another thread should usually take it.

This thread should claim a company only when the company strengthens one of the blind-spot systems below.

## Read this map as lane ownership

Other threads may keep filling ordinary `AnnualReports` sector and industry holes.

This map is for the areas that are still not really covered even when those ordinary packets exist.

In practical terms:

- the other threads can own standard sectors
- this thread should own the missing analytical systems
- company selection should follow lane quality, not just source availability

The current best first pass is now:

- screen the name with the short ownership rule
- route consumer and cultural names through the `consumer-behavior` core
- route hidden-layer and infrastructural names through the `infrastructure` core
- only then select a narrower lane or direct comparison

The retail lane now also has a concrete three-way subtype split through [Costco Versus Kroger Versus TJX Boundary Comparison](/extracted/themes/costco-vs-kroger-vs-tjx-boundary-comparison-2026-08-10.md), which separates paid utility membership, replenishment-and-basket-data retail, and treasure-hunt discovery.

The practical question is not "which source industry is still uncovered?"

It is "which important system still has no useful home inside the `AnnualReports` browse tree?"

## Structural blind spots in the source taxonomy

The underlying problem is not merely that some companies are missing.

The deeper problem is that the live `AnnualReports` browse tree is weak at organizing the economy by:

- recurring behavior systems rather than product aisles
- recurring attention and cultural-distribution systems rather than legacy media or software labels
- relationship ownership rather than formal source industry
- membership, loyalty, wallet, and status mechanics
- platform coordination and ecosystem control
- identity, participation, and cultural signaling
- real-estate and infrastructure chains that cross old sector lines
- hidden infrastructure beneath visible consumer demand
- hidden connectivity and access-layer ownership
- control-layer and connectivity governance
- physical execution and embedded workflow control

It is also weak at showing how much of the customer relationship is actually owned and monetized versus merely supported by price, convenience, or format strength.

That means this thread should mainly claim companies when they sharpen one of those blind spots rather than when they just fill an uncovered sector slot.

## Areas this thread should usually not own

Unless they clearly strengthen one of the fifteen lanes above, this thread should usually not spend time on:

- standard upstream materials names
- ordinary industrial backlog and capex stories
- conventional utilities
- generic bank packetization
- plain pharma or medtech packetization
- normal one-industry coverage whose main value is just completeness

That means this lane should usually leave ordinary uncovered sector pages to the other threads, even when the website still looks incomplete.

The blind-spot lane is not a residual bucket for random uncovered names.

It is the correction layer for what the source taxonomy cannot explain well.
