## Core rule

If a company is mainly useful because it fills a normal `AnnualReports` sector gap, another thread should own it.

This thread should claim work only when the main analytical value comes from something the source taxonomy does not express well.

In practice, this means the thread owns systems that are poorly described by the browse tree, not residual sector holes.

Thin, stale, awkward, or sparsely populated industry pages do not change that rule by themselves.

Operational translation:

- if another thread is filling a normal `AnnualReports` sector or industry page, let it
- if the real economic or cultural story still has no clean place in that taxonomy, this thread should claim it
- this thread should think in systems first and company packets second
- if a company can be explained well enough by its browse-tree page alone, it is not this thread's job

## What this thread owns

### 1. Cross-sector pattern mapping

Own the files that connect companies across legacy sector walls.

Examples:

- retail system types
- DTC versus wholesale and channel control
- platform and ecosystem control
- loyalty, wallet, and membership systems
- marketplace subtype hierarchies
- retail subtype hierarchies
- thick, mid-thickness, and thin relationship systems
- built-environment and infrastructure chains
- control-layer and connectivity infrastructure
- hidden connectivity and access-layer ownership
- physical execution and embedded workflow systems

### 2. Consumer, cultural, and societal behavior layers

Own the work that asks what recurring behavior or identity system is behind the reported numbers.

Priority behavior lanes:

- comfort and casualization
- value-hunt and bargain discovery
- routine-use trust and replenishment
- beauty, fit, and self-presentation
- premium ritual and occasion spending
- fandom, play, and collectible identity
- practical health and wellness signaling
- recurring attention, cultural-distribution, and paid versus ad-funded habit systems
- hidden infrastructure and embedded workflow systems where the real value sits below the visible product or software layer

### 3. Business-model overlays

Own the work that groups companies by monetization structure rather than by source taxonomy.

Examples:

- marketplace versus owned inventory
- direct relationship ownership versus channel dependence
- advertising, seller-services, or loyalty as second monetization layers
- IP licensing and franchise monetization
- thick versus thin relationship monetization

### 4. Taxonomy-breaker cases

Own the companies where the source taxonomy clearly hides the real operating system.

Examples already visible in the archive:

- `Airbnb`
- `eBay`
- `Birkenstock`
- `Kenvue`
- `Carnival`

### 5. Explicit blind-spot claim areas

If the other threads are covering the ordinary sector map, this thread should mainly own by default first-claim priority:

- behind-the-shelf infrastructure that sits inside visible consumer systems
- control-layer and connectivity infrastructure that sits between apps and the physical network
- hidden connectivity and access-layer ownership where bandwidth dependence becomes recurring site rent
- physical execution and embedded workflow systems where the real value is access-over-ownership, onsite embeddedness, and contractor simplification

The following remain valid blind-spot claim areas, but they should usually be conditional rather than default first-claim work:

- creator-merchant and enthusiast marketplaces
- travel-interface systems where the real split is orchestration versus loyalty-thickened intermediation versus decentralized trust
- local-commerce aggregation and delivery-coordination systems
- delivery-layer systems where the real split is freight orchestration versus parcel-network density versus consumer-interface-led last-mile coordination
- DTC relationship ownership and channel-control economics
- loyalty, wallet, membership, and status systems
- value-hunt, closeout, and discovery-led retail behavior
- identity, fandom, participation, and culturally activated demand
- recurring attention, cultural-distribution, and mixed monetization systems
- modern real-estate and infrastructure chains that cut across old sector walls

## What this thread should avoid

Do not take companies just because they are available or because their annual reports are easy to collect.

Avoid:

- plain sector backfill with no cross-sector angle
- another near-duplicate of an already-covered pattern
- packet work whose only value is ordinary industry coverage
- company additions that do not strengthen a blind-spot memo or pattern lane
- generic hardware, telecom, or industrial names whose best explanation is still their formal category

The default assumption should be that uncovered sector pages belong to other threads unless the company is a strong blind-spot case.

The stronger operational version is:

- this thread is not a cleanup bucket for the leftovers from `AnnualReports`
- this thread is the system-level lane for what the taxonomy hides
- if another thread is already covering the formal category, prefer not to duplicate the packet here
- weak page quality alone is not a reason to pull the company back into this thread

## Areas to leave to the other threads

If another thread is already covering standard `AnnualReports` sectors, let it keep:

- ordinary materials, energy, and utility packetization
- standard machinery, transportation, and industrial backlog stories
- plain bank and insurer coverage with no stronger wallet, loyalty, or infrastructure angle
- straightforward pharma, medtech, or hospital packetization unless the bigger trust, routine-care, or data-system angle is the main reason for inclusion
- normal consumer-staples backfill unless the company sharpens a routine-use, beauty, wellness, or relationship-control blind spot
- ordinary networking, telecom, or industrial names unless the bigger hidden-infrastructure, access-layer, or embedded-workflow angle is the main reason for inclusion

The default question for this thread should be:

not "is the industry uncovered?"

but "does the source taxonomy hide the real system here?"

## Company-selection test

This thread should normally take a company only if at least one of these is true:

1. It strengthens a cross-sector memo that the normal taxonomy cannot produce cleanly.
2. It adds a missing behavior lane such as fandom, value-hunt, or ritual spending.
3. It clarifies a business-model distinction such as DTC control, marketplace economics, or second-layer monetization.
4. It is a taxonomy-breaker whose source bucket is materially weaker than its operating reality.

If none of those are true, another thread should usually own the company.

Inside the mature infrastructure lanes, use the matching extension shortlist before taking another company so the next move is reuse-first and subtype-opening rather than generic packet accumulation.

## Operating rule for deliverables

This thread should usually produce one of two outputs:

1. a cross-sector memo or map
2. a company packet that clearly strengthens one of those memos

If a company packet does not improve a blind-spot map, it is probably the wrong use of this thread.
