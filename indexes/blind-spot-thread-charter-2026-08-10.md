# Blind-Spot Thread Charter

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

This charter defines what the thread covering `AnnualReports` blind spots should own so it does not duplicate the standard sector-by-sector packetization work being handled elsewhere.

## Core rule

If a company is mainly useful because it fills a normal `AnnualReports` sector gap, another thread should own it.

This thread should claim work only when the main analytical value comes from something the source taxonomy does not express well.

## What this thread owns

### 1. Cross-sector pattern mapping

Own the files that connect companies across legacy sector walls.

Examples:

- retail system types
- DTC versus wholesale and channel control
- platform and ecosystem control
- loyalty, wallet, and membership systems
- built-environment and infrastructure chains

### 2. Consumer, cultural, and societal behavior layers

Own the work that asks what recurring behavior or identity system is behind the reported numbers.

Priority behavior lanes:

- comfort and casualization
- value-hunt and bargain discovery
- routine-use trust and replenishment
- beauty, fit, and self-presentation
- premium ritual and occasion spending
- fandom, play, and collectible identity
- practical health and wellness signaling

### 3. Business-model overlays

Own the work that groups companies by monetization structure rather than by source taxonomy.

Examples:

- marketplace versus owned inventory
- direct relationship ownership versus channel dependence
- advertising, seller-services, or loyalty as second monetization layers
- IP licensing and franchise monetization

### 4. Taxonomy-breaker cases

Own the companies where the source taxonomy clearly hides the real operating system.

Examples already visible in the archive:

- `Airbnb`
- `eBay`
- `Birkenstock`
- `Kenvue`
- `Carnival`

## What this thread should avoid

Do not take companies just because they are available or because their annual reports are easy to collect.

Avoid:

- plain sector backfill with no cross-sector angle
- another near-duplicate of an already-covered pattern
- packet work whose only value is ordinary industry coverage
- company additions that do not strengthen a blind-spot memo or pattern lane

## Company-selection test

This thread should normally take a company only if at least one of these is true:

1. It strengthens a cross-sector memo that the normal taxonomy cannot produce cleanly.
2. It adds a missing behavior lane such as fandom, value-hunt, or ritual spending.
3. It clarifies a business-model distinction such as DTC control, marketplace economics, or second-layer monetization.
4. It is a taxonomy-breaker whose source bucket is materially weaker than its operating reality.

If none of those are true, another thread should usually own the company.

## Current high-priority claim areas

The strongest areas for this thread as of 2026-08-10 are:

- fandom, play, collectibles, organized play, and franchise-IP monetization
- loyalty, wallet, rewards, status, and membership systems
- retail-system comparison across inventory, marketplace, membership, and discovery models
- DTC versus partner-channel control across consumer brands
- platform and ecosystem businesses with multi-layer monetization
- real-estate and infrastructure chains that cut across `Financial`, `Real Estate`, `Industrial`, and retail-linked demand

## Operating rule for deliverables

This thread should usually produce one of two outputs:

1. a cross-sector memo or map
2. a company packet that clearly strengthens one of those memos

If a company packet does not improve a blind-spot map, it is probably the wrong use of this thread.

## Current best example

`Hasbro` is a good fit for this thread because it does more than add another consumer-goods packet.

It strengthens the still-thin lane around:

- fandom
- play
- collectible identity
- organized play
- franchise and licensing monetization
- physical-plus-digital engagement

That is exactly the kind of work the standard `AnnualReports` taxonomy does not surface well on its own.
