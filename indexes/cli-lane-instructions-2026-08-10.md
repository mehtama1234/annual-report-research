# CLI Lane Instructions

Date baseline: 2026-08-10

This file defines the current high-ambition lane mandates for the `annual-report-research` workspace.

These are not company samplers. They are lane-opening research briefs intended to push the archive into genuinely new territory.

## Current Archive State

As of Tuesday, August 11, 2026, several of the archive's highest-value lanes already have frameworks, proof pages, comparison memos, and watchlists.

That means this file should not be read only as a blank-slate frontier-opening brief.

It should also guide strengthening work inside already-open lanes.

In practice, the best next run may do one or more of these instead of opening a fully new lane:

- fill a missing flagship role
- add the strongest contradiction or weak-link case
- sharpen the burden-versus-beneficiary split
- improve the next-filing break test

## Master pursuit goal

Build `annual-report-research` into a disciplined multi-lane research archive that does two things at the same time:

1. collects source-complete `2025` annual-report and latest-three-quarter evidence as of `2026-08-10`
2. turns that evidence into higher-level reads on consumer behavior, cultural shifts, industrial pressure, operating structure, capital allocation, and recurring cross-company patterns

The goal is not to accumulate PDFs.
The goal is to produce a usable archive of company packets, lane summaries, and cross-company signals that opens new research frontiers rather than repeating already saturated repo territory.

This work should deliberately widen the archive across:

- recreation, lifestyle, and participation demand
- healthcare frontier and recurring-care systems
- connectivity, telecom, and technical infrastructure
- capital structures, property vehicles, and conglomerates
- adjacent physical, technical, institutional, software, care-delivery, and lifestyle layers that make those lanes more complete

At the end of a strong run, the archive should be stronger in five ways:

- more source-complete flagship companies
- better lane-level comparison sets
- clearer thematic interpretation inside each company packet
- stronger cross-company pattern recognition
- a cleaner handoff for the next batch
- a broader lane footprint that makes the next extension obvious instead of forcing the next operator to restart lane selection from scratch

## End-to-end pursuit standard

Pursue each lane as a full stack of work, not as isolated document collection.

That means every run should aim to:

- identify `4` to `8` flagship companies that together define a lane when the lane has enough source depth and useful internal contrast
- at minimum, complete one coherent comparison set and leave the remaining exact targets staged if the full lane build is still in progress
- capture the right annual and quarterly evidence for those companies
- interpret what changed at the company level
- compare the companies inside a coherent industry or operating lane
- extract the larger social, consumer, cultural, industrial, financial, or technical patterns that repeat across them
- explain what those repeated patterns say about behavior, infrastructure, pressure, control points, and monetization beyond any one management team
- leave the lane in a state where another thread can continue without reconstructing the logic

The lane is not complete if it only proves document coverage.
It must also prove interpretation: what changed, what pressures are shared, where value accrues, what behaviors are shifting, and what adjacent names now matter.

The practical standard is that each lane should explain a lived system:

- what behavior is becoming normalized
- what burden is becoming unavoidable
- what control point or monetization system is strengthening because of that shift
- which companies absorb the mess and which companies monetize the dependence

The finished lane should read like a system map, not a folder tree:

- filings provide evidence
- packets explain what each company helps prove
- lane summaries explain the repeated reality multiple management teams are reacting to
- handoffs make continuation obvious without re-deriving the lane logic

Interpretation here means identifying the lived systems underneath the filings:

- what households, patients, enterprises, regulators, and capital providers are normalizing
- which burden stacks are spreading across the lane
- which companies are carrying those burdens and which are turning them into defensible monetization
- where the same reality appears under different labels because of industry vocabulary differences

This is not an optional layer added after source collection.
It is one of the main outputs the lane is supposed to produce.
Consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, and bigger-picture pattern detection are part of the definition of a complete run.

Required packet standard inside every lane:

- each flagship company must produce both a source-complete packet and a thematic interpretation
- each packet must say what bigger recurring pattern the company helps prove
- each packet must connect management language to the underlying consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, technical, reimbursement, or capital-structure pressure where relevant
- each packet must explain whether the company is carrying the burden stack, capturing the surplus stack, or doing both

Required lane standard:

- the lane summary must identify the strongest repeated consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, and operating patterns
- the lane summary must explain what multiple management teams are reacting to at once
- the lane summary must identify the deeper lived-system shift underneath those reactions
- the lane summary must separate structural signals from temporary cycle effects
- the handoff must name the best next companies to test or widen the established pattern

## What this archive is trying to become

This archive should become a middle layer between raw filings and broad strategy narratives.

It should be strong enough to answer:

- what the company actually reported
- what changed across the annual and recent quarters
- what management is reacting to
- what economic role the company plays
- what larger trend the company expresses
- which other companies belong in the same comparison set

In other words, the archive should connect:

- filing evidence
- company interpretation
- lane structure
- theme formation
- next-name selection

## Standard for every CLI lane

Your job is not to sample one company. Your job is to leave `annual-report-research` materially more explanatory than it was before the run, whether that means opening a genuinely new lane or materially strengthening one that already exists.

Cover the assigned `AnnualReports.com` industries end to end for `10` hours.
Aim to complete `4` to `8` flagship company packets in the lane when the lane supports that breadth.
Do not stop at the first narrow quartet if the lane can be widened with adjacent in-scope names that materially improve the explanation.
Use `2025` annual reports plus the latest three reported quarters as of `2026-08-10`.
Use `AnnualReports.com` for taxonomy, but company IR and SEC as authoritative when AnnualReports lags.
For every company, produce both filing coverage and thematic interpretation.
Do not update shared repo-wide indexes continuously during exploration. Update them at the end of a coherent batch or leave the batch ready for integration.

Before stopping, leave a handoff with:

- commit hash
- companies completed
- companies partial
- industry lane summary
- key themes
- strongest cross-company signals
- next recommended names

The handoff should not just list themes.
It should say which of those signals already look structural, which remain provisional, and which adjacent companies are the best next test.
It should also be backed by one or more coherent checkpoint commits if the run materially advanced.

## End-to-end goals

The work is not just:

- downloading `2025` annual reports
- collecting the latest three reported quarters as of `2026-08-10`
- filling folders with filings

It is also a structured pattern-finding system.

More explicitly, the work is also:

- identifying consumer trends
- identifying cultural and societal shifts
- identifying industrial and operating pressures
- finding the bigger-picture patterns that repeat across companies
- identifying participation, franchise, and IP monetization where those repeated patterns matter
- identifying where the real power sits in the lane: relationship ownership, physical infrastructure, workflow control, trust, distribution, reimbursement, or balance-sheet advantage
- identifying which companies carry the burden stack versus which companies capture the cleaner economics created by that burden

Each lane should surface:

- consumer trends
- cultural and societal shifts
- industrial and operating pressures
- capital-allocation behavior
- recurring business-model patterns
- the bigger-picture signals that repeat across companies
- the control points and leak points that explain where economics are compounding and where they are being competed away
- the deeper demand logic underneath management language: participation, identity, urgency, trust, convenience, compliance, reimbursement, technical dependency, or capital scarcity

This thematic and societal interpretation is not extra work.
It is one of the main outputs.

The required output is therefore not just a filing chain.
It is a lane-level explanation of what repeated demand, social, cultural, operating, and monetization patterns those filings reveal.
If a run cannot identify the bigger-picture patterns that repeat across companies, it has not yet met the operating standard even if the source folders are complete.
That includes the company level, not only the lane-summary level.
If a packet can collect and summarize filings but cannot state what broader adaptation pattern the company helps prove, that packet is still incomplete.

That includes the pattern of who bears the burden stack versus who captures the surplus stack.
The archive should keep asking whether the same demand wave is paying the operator, the landlord, the workflow owner, the trust intermediary, the toll collector, or the better-capitalized allocator.

Pass-fail test:

- if a run can list the annual and quarter chain but cannot state what several management teams are reacting to at once, the run is still below standard
- if a packet can summarize the filing chain but cannot say what bigger pattern the company helps prove, that packet is still below standard

Operational reading rule:

- read each filing as evidence of adaptation, not just disclosure
- translate management language into the underlying behavior or burden shift
- compare several companies until the repeated pattern is clear enough to name directly
- write the packet and lane summary so a reader can see both the documents and the system those documents reveal

## Meaty end-to-end goal to pursue

Treat every lane as an attempt to explain a living system, not merely a filing bucket.

The end-to-end goal is to leave behind a research package that can answer all of the following at once:

- what the flagship companies reported in the `2025` annual and latest three reported quarters as of `2026-08-10`
- what repeated consumer, patient, enterprise, regulator, and capital-provider behavior those filings point to
- what cultural, societal, and institutional normalization is making the lane more durable or more fragile
- what industrial, operational, technical, labor, reimbursement, compliance, or balance-sheet pressure is shaping management behavior across several companies at once
- what monetization systems keep showing up: participation, franchise, IP, subscription, workflow control, toll collection, trust intermediation, reimbursement capture, rent extraction, spread capture, or asset-turn leverage
- which companies bear the labor, capex, balance-sheet, or service burden and which companies capture the cleaner economics
- which signals already look structural and which still need one more adjacent company to test them

The practical interpretation standard is direct:

- societal, cultural, consumer, industrial, and bigger-picture pattern finding is not extra work
- participation, franchise, and IP monetization pattern-finding is not extra work where those systems matter
- recurring lived-system interpretation is part of what makes a packet source-complete in practice
- if the run stops at document collection, it has not completed the lane

The lane should therefore move through five linked layers:

1. Filing capture:
   gather the `2025` annual and the latest three reported quarters as of `2026-08-10`, with AnnualReports taxonomy confirmation and company IR / SEC authority when AnnualReports lags.
2. Company understanding:
   state what changed, what management emphasizes, what burden stack the company carries, and what monetization stack it captures.
3. Cross-company comparison:
   put `3` to `4` flagship names into a coherent comparison set first, then extend toward `4` to `8` when the lane supports it.
4. Theme formation:
   identify repeated consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, and operating signals across those companies rather than leaving them as isolated packet notes.
5. Forward handoff:
   leave exact next names, strongest proved signals, tentative signals, and the commit that contains the integrated state.

Across those layers, the archive should deliberately look for:

- consumer trend formation
- cultural and societal normalization
- industrial and operating burden transfer
- participation, franchise, and IP monetization
- workflow, trust, reimbursement, or installed-base control
- capital-structure advantage or refinancing fragility

The meaty end-state is not "we have four company folders."
The meaty end-state is "we can explain what this lane is becoming, why its pressures are changing, where the economics are concentrating, and which adjacent names are the best next proof points."

The same standard applies inside each packet:

- not just what happened

## Required output layers per run

Each coherent lane run should leave usable work at five levels:

### 1. Source layer

- `2025` annual report captured or authoritatively verified
- matching annual filing captured or authoritatively verified
- latest three reported quarters as of `2026-08-10` captured or authoritatively verified
- source-ledger clarity on what was saved locally versus only verified by URL or filing reference

### 2. Company packet layer

Each flagship company should end with:

- a source-complete packet or a clearly documented partial packet
- a concise read on what changed in the annual
- a concise read on what changed across the last three quarters
- an interpretation of the company's role inside the lane
- explicit links to broader cross-company patterns
- a concrete statement of which consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, technical, or capital-structure pattern the company helps prove
- a concrete statement of which burden stack the company bears and which monetization stack it captures

### 3. Lane layer

The lane should explain:

- what economic function this lane serves
- where the bottlenecks, moats, and fragilities sit
- which sub-areas are stable cash-flow systems versus cyclical build-out systems
- which names are best treated as operators, toll collectors, asset owners, intermediaries, or capital allocators
- what broader social, institutional, cultural, or industrial behavior shift is making the lane more important
- where the lane's profits are accruing relative to where its operational burden sits

### 4. Theme layer

The run should identify repeating themes such as:

- pricing power versus volume dependence
- capital intensity versus asset-light control
- regulatory burden versus franchise protection
- trust, distribution, and switching-cost moats
- participation, community, franchise, and IP monetization where relevant
- labor intensity, service complexity, or technical specialization
- balance-sheet strength versus refinancing exposure
- consumer or institutional behavior shifts that help explain why the quarter chain moved
- structural versus cyclical pressures that should carry into the next comparison set
- participation, loyalty, franchise, status, or habit systems where management is monetizing recurring engagement rather than one-time transactions
- broader consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, and institutional shifts that several management teams are plainly reacting to at once
- the difference between who bears the cost burden and who captures the economic surplus inside the lane
- the difference between baseline demand and the system that actually monetizes that demand

The run should also be explicit about:

- which patterns are visible in one company but not yet proven across the lane
- which patterns are repeated strongly enough across several companies to count as genuine archive-level signals
- which adjacent industries or names are the best next test for those signals
- which signals appear to be one-cycle noise versus long-duration social, operational, or capital-structure change

### 5. Handoff layer

The end of the run should make continuation obvious for another thread.
That means the handoff should clearly separate:

- finished work
- partially finished work
- strongest signals already established
- unresolved comparison gaps
- exact next companies to add

## How to choose a strong 3-to-4 company batch

A strong batch should not include four versions of the same operating model.

Prefer a batch that gives:

- one demand gateway or distribution owner
- one capital-intensive infrastructure owner
- one toll-collector, measurement, or enabling-technology provider
- one contrast case with a meaningfully different balance sheet, customer base, or monetization model

This makes the lane capable of producing actual themes rather than just summaries.
