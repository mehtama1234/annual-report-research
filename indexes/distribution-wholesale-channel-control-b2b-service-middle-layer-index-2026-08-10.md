## Strongest repeated patterns

1. Channel control is not abstract. The strongest packets show distributors and service intermediaries winning through default workflow position, route density, inventory access, and procurement leverage rather than through ownership of the underlying goods.
2. Institutional demand is one of the cleanest stabilizers in the archive. Foodservice, healthcare, and business uptime needs recur even when the end consumer is cautious.
3. The most valuable middle-layer operators increasingly attach services to product flow. Specialty healthcare support, project logistics, owner relationships, field-service coordination, and digital procurement tools all widen the moat.
4. Working-capital discipline remains central. Inventory timing, backlog conversion, fleet utilization, and cash conversion are not side metrics in this frontier; they are the operating core.
5. Cultural and societal shifts still matter here. Value sensitivity, healthcare complexity, project outsourcing, premium travel demand, and digital infrastructure build-out all show up indirectly through the middle layer before or alongside end-demand companies.
