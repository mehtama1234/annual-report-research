## Strongest repeated patterns

1. Channel control is not abstract. The strongest packets show distributors and service intermediaries winning through default workflow position, route density, inventory access, and procurement leverage rather than through ownership of the underlying goods.
2. Institutional demand is one of the cleanest stabilizers in the archive. Foodservice, healthcare distribution, office-based care procurement, provider-network management, delegated-risk infrastructure, larger provider-system throughput, hospital procedure demand, installed robotic-platform utilization, workplace safety, business uptime, recurring specialty-therapy delivery, and therapy-enabling product demand all recur even when the end consumer is cautious.
3. The most valuable middle-layer operators increasingly attach services or workflow dependence to product flow. Specialty healthcare support, practice workflow embedment, provider-network and care-enablement services, hospital workflow embedment, project logistics, owner relationships, inspection and monitoring, facilities uptime support, field-service coordination, route density, uniforms and safety routes, technical uptime support, and digital procurement tools all widen the moat.
4. Technology distribution belongs in the frontier as clearly as physical building-products distribution does. Arrow and Avnet show that component routing, enterprise-computing and electronics-channel ownership, supply-chain services, inventory-days discipline, and working-capital management are middle-layer infrastructure, not secondary detail.
5. Security hardening is now clearly part of the frontier. Motorola shows the technology-and-managed-services layer, Resideo adds the upstream low-voltage contractor-distribution and building-controls channel, Brink's adds the literal route-based protection and cash-logistics layer, ADT adds the recurring monitored household-and-small-business protection layer, Allegion adds controlled physical-access infrastructure, Brady adds workplace-identification and safety-compliance control, GEO adds the labor-forward outsourced secure-services and supervision layer, and CoreCivic adds the balance-sheet-backed secure-capacity and facility-monetization variant of that same institutional-security logic.
6. Working-capital discipline remains central. Inventory timing, backlog conversion, fleet utilization, cash conversion, and receivables intensity are not side metrics in this frontier; they are the operating core.
7. Cultural and societal shifts still matter here. Value sensitivity, healthcare complexity, project outsourcing, premium travel demand, climate-control dependence, immigration-linked field-labor tightness, institutional hardening, public-safety digitization, energy-and-process infrastructure strain, and digital infrastructure build-out all show up indirectly through the middle layer before or alongside end-demand companies.
