## Purpose

Use the short ownership rule before treating any name in this note as a default claim for this thread.

## The actual blind spot

The main blind spot is not simply "uncovered industries."

The deeper blind spot is that the `AnnualReports` industry tree is weak at explaining systems such as:

- hidden physical infrastructure behind digital life
- control layers that sit between internet usage and ordinary software categories
- execution and access models in the physical economy
- recurring attention and cultural distribution systems
- cross-sector business models that do not fit one ordinary industry label

That means this thread should prefer names that clarify those systems, even when they sit inside ordinary-looking sector buckets like `Real Estate`, `Technology`, or `Industrial Goods`.

### 2. Hidden connectivity and access-layer infrastructure

Best names already present in the repo:

- `American Tower`
- `SBA Communications`
- `Crown Castle`
- `Cogent`
- `Iridium`
- `Viasat`

Why this group matters:

- it explains the invisible site, route, and access layer beneath mobile usage, app usage, and AI-era connectivity demand
- it is a better blind-spot fit than another normal telecom or software packet
- it sharpens a system the browse tree does not present well: physical choke points that monetize digital dependence before revenue reaches apps or cloud platforms

What the current packets already show:

- `American Tower` is the broadest global tower-ownership and datacenter-bridge case in the repo
- `SBA Communications` is the cleanest pure-play tower-rent model in the repo and makes bandwidth dependence legible as recurring rent
- `Crown Castle` helps contrast a simpler U.S. tower story against a more complicated portfolio-reset path
- `Cogent` now adds the live terrestrial route-density and on-net private-connectivity branch where building access, IP and optical routes, and wavelength economics matter more than tower rent or orbital coverage
- `Iridium` adds the non-terrestrial, off-grid, and mission-critical coverage layer where towers and route-density assets cannot fully substitute
- `Viasat` adds the heavier-bandwidth orbital, mobility, and sovereign-access layer where aviation, maritime, defense, remote broadband, and backlog conversion matter more than the narrower assured-coverage logic visible at `Iridium`
- `Zayo`, if completed later, would likely extend the stricter private-fiber, dark-fiber, and buyout-ownership side of the route-density branch rather than only the tower layer

Best use:

- strengthen the hidden-infrastructure lane
- build direct comparison work across tower, route, resilient-coverage, and bandwidth-heavy orbital access structures
- reuse the current tower-versus-route-versus-orbital split before taking another name

## Priority order for this thread

If this thread is choosing the next high-payoff blind-spot move, the best order is:

1. the behind-the-shelf consumer infrastructure lane, now widened by `Veralto`, `Ecolab`, `International Paper`, `Republic Services`, `AptarGroup`, `Silgan`, and `Packaging Corporation of America` beyond the original packaging, waste, and plant-control anchors
2. `Cloudflare`, `Akamai`, `Cisco`, `Arista Networks`, `Palo Alto Networks`, `Ciena`, `CommScope`, and `Corning` as a control-layer and AI-connectivity cluster
3. `American Tower`, `SBA Communications`, `Crown Castle`, `Cogent`, `Iridium`, and `Viasat` as a hidden connectivity and access-layer cluster
4. `United Rentals`, `Fastenal`, `Ferguson`, `WESCO`, `Applied Industrial Technologies`, and `Core & Main` as a physical execution and embedded-control cluster

Why this ordering:

- the first lane now has the clearest live subtype map, with both the rigid-container branch and the regional-density waste branch now covered by `Silgan` and `Casella`
- the second cluster most directly fills a taxonomy gap that other sector threads are least likely to explain well
- the third cluster makes the invisible physical network layer legible
- the fourth cluster is now stronger because it has a live extension set beyond the original three-way workflow split, but it still overlaps with ordinary industrial packetization unless it is handled as a systems comparison rather than standalone company backfill

## What this thread should avoid

Do not treat this shortlist as permission to take any ordinary uncovered name in these sectors.

Avoid:

- another generic industrial packet with no stronger access, control, or embedded-workflow insight
- another generic telecom packet that does not sharpen the tower, route, edge, or optical-control distinction
- another ordinary software packet that does not materially improve platform, attention, or control-layer understanding
- plain sector backfill that the normal threads can cover without losing analytical quality

## Operating rule

Before claiming a company for this blind-spot lane, confirm:

1. it explains a system `AnnualReports` classifies badly
2. it improves a cross-company comparison, not just company count
3. another thread focused on normal sector coverage would probably miss the same value
4. the result will strengthen a memo, comparison layer, or taxonomy correction
