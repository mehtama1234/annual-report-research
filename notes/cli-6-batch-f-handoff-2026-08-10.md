## Industry lane summary

- what economic function this lane serves: it places risk, owns senior-housing and care-adjacent property, manufactures private credit and retirement-linked capital, and intermediates deposits and loans
- where the moats sit: distribution density and workflow control, aging-linked supply scarcity, insurance-funded origination and spread economics, and stable relationship-bank funding
- where the fragilities sit: integration and talent costs, labor and property capital intensity, credit and spread conditions, and classic bank-cycle exposure
- what changed most in the annuals: Gallagher reinforced acquisition-plus-fee compounding, Welltower pushed the senior-housing platform harder, Apollo expanded its fee-plus-spread model, and M&T strengthened profitability and credit discipline
- what changed most in the latest three quarters: Gallagher stayed clean, Welltower kept occupancy and NOI momentum high, Apollo kept compounding through fees and spread-related earnings, and M&T showed the more variable quarter profile of a quality bank
- which parts of the lane look recurring: Gallagher is the cleanest recurring system, Apollo is highly recurring but tied to credit and insurance conditions, Welltower has recurring demand with more operating burden, and M&T remains the most cyclical
- which parts look cyclical or stressed: M&T has the most direct rate and credit exposure, while Welltower remains the most exposed to care-operator and labor execution
