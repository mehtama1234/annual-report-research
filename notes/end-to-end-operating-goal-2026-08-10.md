# End-To-End Operating Goal

Date: 2026-08-10
Applies to: `annual-report-research`

## Packet Inputs Used

- the repo's company-packet standard built around the `2025` annual report, authoritative annual filing, and latest three reported quarters as of `2026-08-10`
- AnnualReports.com taxonomy and archive confirmation, with company IR and SEC materials as the authority chain when timing or filings diverge
- lane-level expectations for coherent flagship-company batches, comparison building, theme extraction, and continuation-ready handoff
- the broader instruction set requiring consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, infrastructure, commodity, and capital-burden interpretation when the evidence supports it
- the autonomy rule that if an assigned lane runs thin, the next strongest uncovered adjacent lane should be chosen without stalling the archive

## Mission

Build `annual-report-research` into a source-complete, synthesis-driven archive of flagship public-company packets across undercovered industries, business systems, and operating layers.

If assigned industries run thin, overlap, weaken, or stop producing strong candidates, the worker must choose the next strongest uncovered lane independently and keep the archive moving.

This is not a filing-download task. It is an archive-construction task and an interpretation task at the same time.

Every completed company should improve both:

1. document coverage
2. interpretive coverage

The archive should steadily become better at explaining:

- consumer behavior
- cultural participation and identity
- societal demand shifts
- industrial bottlenecks and workflow realities
- capital intensity and buildout cycles
- commodity exposure and spread sensitivity
- infrastructure dependence and transport constraints
- pricing power, margin structure, and utilization dependence
- financing sensitivity and balance-sheet pressure
- digital, software, and AI adoption when it changes real operations

The output is supposed to become a comparative operating map, not a pile of PDFs.

## Core Standard

For every company, the required evidence base is:

- the `2025` annual report
- the authoritative `2025` annual filing or closest equivalent primary filing
- the latest three reported quarters, or closest honest equivalent reported periods, as of `Monday, August 10, 2026`

For many calendar-year U.S. reporters, that trailing window will be:

1. `Q2 2026`
2. `Q1 2026`
3. `Q4 2025`

For foreign issuers, half-year reporters, miners with operating updates, royalty structures, or non-standard fiscal calendars:

- preserve the company’s actual reporting cadence
- use the closest three reported periods honestly
- explain the cadence and adaptation clearly inside the packet

Do not invent a fake quarter structure for the sake of uniformity.

## Source Hierarchy

Use a strict source hierarchy:

1. `AnnualReports.com`
2. official company `Investor Relations`
3. `SEC`

`AnnualReports.com` is for:

- taxonomy
- archive confirmation
- company-page verification

Official company IR and SEC are for:

- the actual `2025` annual report
- quarterly materials
- earnings releases
- supplements and presentations
- primary filings
- authoritative chronology

Non-negotiable rule:

- treat `AnnualReports.com` as taxonomy and archive confirmation
- treat company IR and SEC as authoritative when `AnnualReports.com` lags

If the IR chain is incomplete, use the SEC chain aggressively and document the limitation.

## What The Work Is

The work is not just:

- downloading `2025` annual reports
- collecting the latest three reported quarters around late `2025` and `2026`
- creating folders without interpretation
- drafting one-off packets without sector context

The work is also:

- identifying consumer trends
- identifying cultural and societal shifts
- identifying industrial and operating pressures
- identifying business-model and margin-structure patterns
- identifying capital-cycle and infrastructure dependencies
- identifying recurring signals that repeat across multiple companies

Examples of the bigger-picture signals this work should surface include:

- participation systems
- franchise and IP monetization
- identity-driven demand
- ritualized and routine-use demand
- value pressure versus premium resilience
- project timing and execution risk
- throughput and utilization dependence
- commodity sensitivity
- refining margin exposure
- transport bottlenecks
- fleet replacement and after-sales durability
- workflow complexity and labor intensity
- reimbursement or regulatory dependence
- software or AI that materially changes execution

The societal, cultural, consumer, industrial, and bigger-picture layer is not optional commentary. It is one of the main outputs.

The packet fields should also do explicit analytical work:

- annual takeaways + latest three-quarter chain
  - prove: what changed and whether the direction is strengthening, weakening, or persisting
- plain-English operating model
  - prove: what job the company really performs in the system
- strategy read
  - prove: how management is responding to the pressure or opportunity
- growth engine + economic lever
  - prove: what is really carrying the story and what actually moves the economics
- operating constraint
  - prove: where the system is strained
- exact supporting facts
  - prove: the claim directly rather than by implication
- burden-versus-beneficiary interpretation
  - prove: who gets cleaner economics and who absorbs the messy work
- thesis breaker + watchlist
  - prove: the work is falsifiable and ready for continuation

## Main Production Objective

Produce a steady stream of flagship company packets that are:

- source-complete
- thematically interpretable
- comparable across industries
- strong enough to anchor a lane
- useful for later cross-company synthesis

The target is not dozens of shallow starts.

The target is a smaller number of strong, auditable packets that expand the archive into new systems and make comparison quality better after every batch.

## Starting Scope And Expansion Logic

The initial operating map can start from explicitly requested lanes, such as:

- energy, materials, and heavy inputs
- mobility, vehicles, distribution, and freight
- play, hobby, recreation, and culture participation
- healthcare expansion beyond large pharma, hospitals, and devices
- retail, consumer-interface, and demand-system layers
- financial, insurance, and institutional operating infrastructure
- industrial buildout, contracting, manufacturing, and service layers

But the archive should not behave as if those headings are hard walls.

The real objective is to cover the economic stack underneath them:

- extraction
- processing
- transport
- storage
- distribution
- equipment and maintenance
- customer interface and demand capture
- recurring service and replacement
- capital providers and risk carriers
- software, workflow, or operating-control layers

If a named industry list starts to fail, the worker should expand laterally or vertically on purpose.

Examples:

- if independent oil producers become repetitive, move into transport, refining, services, or metals that clarify the same commodity system
- if auto OEMs are already well represented, move into dealers, parts, aftermarket, trucking, shipping, or air-service infrastructure
- if toys become thin, move toward participation venues, collectibles, recreation equipment, entertainment, or adjacent leisure rituals
- if biotech is too binary or source-poor, move toward diagnostics, laboratories, specialty services, or recurring care workflows

The archive should be built like a connected map of operating systems, not like a checklist that ends when one category page looks exhausted.

## Coverage Model

Coverage should be built as a layered map of real economic systems, not as a flat list of stock tickers.

That means the work should keep expanding across:

- production
- inputs
- transport
- distribution
- retail or interface layers
- maintenance and replacement layers
- infrastructure operators
- financing-sensitive intermediaries
- software, workflow, or service layers when they materially shape the system

Whenever possible, build a lane with internal contrast.

Examples:

- upstream production versus transport versus refining
- OEMs versus parts versus dealers versus freight
- toys/IP owners versus participation venues versus retail access points
- diagnostic platforms versus drug-risk platforms versus recurring-care platforms

The point is not just to cover a sector name. The point is to make the operating stack legible.

## Selection Discipline

Prefer companies that are:

- large enough to matter
- clean enough to source
- current enough to fit the reporting window
- distinct enough to add a new signal
- representative enough to explain an industry system
- legible enough to compare against adjacent packets

Avoid:

- weak disclosure chains when a cleaner flagship exists
- repetitive names that add no new interpretive value
- already-covered names unless a real gap remains
- scattered starts that are unlikely to reach packet completion

Prioritize:

- scale
- source quality
- thematic distinctiveness
- clean filings
- comparative usefulness

## Self-Directed Expansion Rule

If you ever lack assigned industries, obvious targets, or clean candidates, pick the next strongest uncovered industry and company on your own.

This is not a minor fallback. It is part of the operating model.

That means:

- do not stop because a checklist runs out
- do not wait for a fresh assignment if adjacent territory is clearly valuable
- do not stay trapped in a weak lane when a nearby stronger lane is visible
- do not preserve a bad company choice just because it was mentioned first

Instead:

- choose the next strong uncovered area yourself
- extend into the nearest adjacent or highest-value lane
- prefer the company that best combines scale, source quality, and thematic distinctiveness
- keep moving toward broader archive coverage and better comparison quality

Good reasons to self-expand include:

- the assigned lane has become repetitive
- the remaining candidates are source-poor
- filings are too messy to produce a strong packet
- a neighboring industry offers a cleaner flagship
- an emerging theme needs a second or third comparator
- the archive is missing an important operating layer behind an already-covered sector

When self-expanding, prefer the option that most improves one or more of:

- industry breadth
- business-model breadth
- operating-pressure coverage
- capital-cycle coverage
- consumer or cultural pattern coverage
- commodity, infrastructure, transport, or workflow coverage
- comparison quality for an already emerging theme

Self-direction should be active, not passive.

If coverage momentum depends on waiting for a perfect instruction list, the workflow is too brittle.

The correct behavior is:

- notice where the archive is still thin
- choose the next best flagship yourself
- explain why that company strengthens the archive
- complete the packet to the same standard as assigned work

## Required Output Per Company

Every completed company must produce both:

1. a source-complete packet
2. a thematic interpretation

Minimum artifact set:

- raw source collection under `raw/`
- extracted packet materials under `extracted/`
- `company-profile.md`
- `company-packet.md`
- `source-ledger.md`
- source verification notes when needed for `AnnualReports`, official IR, and SEC

The company packet must be reusable by another worker without reconstruction.

## Packet Obligations

Each packet should make these things clear:

- what the company is
- why it belongs in the chosen lane
- what the `2025` annual report says about the current business
- what the latest three reported periods say about the current trajectory
- what the real reporting cadence is
- where the core revenue, cost, margin, and capital drivers sit
- what the major assets, segments, channels, geographies, or molecule flows are
- what risk structure or dependency structure matters most
- what broader theme the company confirms, complicates, or disproves

Every packet must answer two questions:

1. what happened at this company
2. what larger system does this company help explain

It should also be obvious from the packet whether the company is best understood as:

- a demand signal
- an infrastructure signal
- a capital-cycle signal
- a margin-structure signal
- a workflow or bottleneck signal
- an identity or participation signal
- a regulatory or reimbursement signal
- a spread, commodity, or utilization signal

## Theme Obligation

The thematic layer should shape:

- company selection
- source collection
- extraction priorities
- packet writing
- lane synthesis

Do not reduce the theme layer to a decorative paragraph at the end of the packet.

Each company should explicitly contribute to one or more recurring tracks, such as:

- trust-led demand systems
- routine-use and ritualized demand systems
- fandom, collectibles, and participation systems
- infrastructure-embedded operators
- commodity-price and throughput-driven models
- high-capex buildout systems
- recurring service, maintenance, and replacement systems
- financing-sensitive distribution layers
- science-platform versus recurring-care models
- transport, bottleneck, and export-dependent systems

The goal is not isolated company summaries. The goal is a comparative system map.

## Required Cross-Company Questions

Every company packet should help answer a set of larger questions that remain comparable across sectors.

Those questions include:

- what kind of demand system is this: habitual, identity-led, project-based, compliance-driven, emergency-driven, fleet-driven, export-driven, or participation-led
- where does pricing power really sit: brand, scarcity, regulation, switching cost, embedded workflow, logistics position, or asset control
- what actually moves margins: volume, spread, utilization, labor productivity, mix, reimbursement, financing cost, throughput, or cost deflation
- what kind of capital structure governs the business: reserve replacement, fleet age, store base, installed base, project backlog, laboratory footprint, or digital platform scale
- whether the company is mainly a signal of demand strength, infrastructure dependence, technology transition, financing pressure, regulation, or commodity exposure
- whether the surrounding system behaves like a durable habit, a cyclical purchase, a replacement cycle, a social ritual, a bottlenecked buildout, or a speculation-sensitive market
- which adjacent completed packets does this company confirm, challenge, or sharply differ from

These questions should be answered implicitly or explicitly in the packet and then carried upward into lane synthesis.

## Interpretation Standard

For every company, move from facts to interpretation in a disciplined way.

Do not stop at:

- revenue moved
- margins changed
- guidance increased or decreased
- volumes were up or down

Translate the evidence into what it means about the surrounding system.

Examples of the type of interpretation expected:

- whether a consumer category behaves like habit, aspiration, ritual, fandom, value-seeking, or identity expression
- whether an industrial operator is really exposed to volume, price, spread, utilization, financing, labor, or regulation
- whether a healthcare name depends on binary innovation outcomes, recurring workflow demand, or reimbursement continuity
- whether a mobility or freight name is driven by affordability, fleet age, financing cost, maintenance intensity, or throughput constraints
- whether an energy or materials operator is best read through buildout intensity, molecule flow, reserve life, project timing, refining margins, export dependency, or transport bottlenecks

Interpretation should be anchored in the filings, not invented on top of them.

## Lane-Building Standard

Do not think only in terms of isolated company completion.

Think in terms of building lanes that become stronger after the second and third company.

A good lane should reveal:

- what is stable versus cyclical
- what is volume-led versus price-led
- what is asset-heavy versus brand-heavy
- what is recurring versus event-driven
- what is local versus globally exposed
- what is controlled by infrastructure versus by demand generation

The lane becomes useful when a later reader can see why apparently similar companies are actually governed by different economic engines.

## Working Method

Work company by company to completion.

For each company:

1. confirm industry fit and lane value
2. confirm the authoritative `2025` annual report
3. confirm the latest three reported periods as of `2026-08-10`
4. save `AnnualReports`, official IR, and SEC artifacts locally
5. record source-authority and verification notes
6. extract the key financial, operating, strategic, and capital-allocation facts
7. write `company-profile.md`
8. write `company-packet.md`
9. write `source-ledger.md`
10. write the thematic interpretation inside the packet
11. update the relevant lane or sector synthesis once the packet is coherent
12. defer shared repo-wide index updates until the end of a coherent batch

Do not continuously churn shared indexes during exploration.

While exploring, keep notes on:

- why the company was selected
- what adjacent names were rejected and why
- where `AnnualReports.com` lagged
- what the authoritative reporting chain actually was
- what theme the packet most clearly strengthens

## Execution Loop

Run the workflow in this order:

1. choose the best next flagship
2. confirm the evidence window
3. collect and save the source chain
4. extract the critical facts
5. write the packet artifacts
6. write the thematic interpretation
7. compare the signal against adjacent completed companies
8. update lane synthesis after the packet is real
9. update shared indexes only at the end of a coherent batch
10. commit a clean checkpoint with a handoff-quality summary

If any step breaks, fix the weakest part rather than lowering the standard.

Examples:

- if the company is thematically useful but source-poor, switch to a stronger flagship
- if the source chain is strong but the company adds no new signal, switch to a more distinctive name
- if the lane is becoming repetitive, move into the nearest adjacent area that improves comparison value
- if the assigned list runs thin, keep going by choosing the next uncovered area yourself

Do not confuse motion with progress.

The workflow is failing if it produces:

- many raw folders with no packets
- packets with no thematic view
- themes with no source support
- repeated company choices that all say the same thing
- constant index churn without real lane completion

## Batch Standard

A batch is coherent when it adds a meaningful lane, comparison set, or thematic cluster rather than one isolated file.

At the end of a coherent batch:

- update the relevant sector or lane synthesis
- update shared indexes
- make the new pattern legible
- record any partials honestly
- leave a clean handoff path for the next worker

If a batch cannot support a clean synthesis paragraph about what changed in the archive, it is probably not coherent yet.

## Definition Of Done

The work is not done when files are downloaded.

The work is done only when:

- the source chain is collected and auditable
- the `2025` annual report is confirmed
- the latest three reported periods are confirmed
- the packet files are written
- the thematic interpretation is explicit
- the lane synthesis reflects the new company
- the batch is coherent enough for later integration
- another worker could continue without rebuilding context

If a company does not produce both source completeness and thematic clarity, it is not done.

If a lane does not become more legible after the packet is added, the integration is not done.

Partial work is acceptable only when it is explicitly labeled and leaves a useful restart point.

Useful partials include:

- raw source chains that are already collected and named cleanly
- quarter chronology that is already verified
- an identified archive lag between `AnnualReports.com` and official IR
- a nearly complete packet with only one missing primary document

Useless partials include:

- random downloads without a lane decision
- folders with no source ledger
- company notes with no authoritative filing chain
- a theme claim that cannot be tied back to the annual report or latest quarter set

## Run-End Requirement

Every working run should end with a handoff-quality checkpoint.

That checkpoint must say:

- commit hash
- companies completed
- companies partial
- industry or lane summary
- next recommended names

The lane summary should state what the newly completed work changes in the archive.

It should explain, in plain terms:

- what broader system became more legible
- what recurring pressures showed up again
- what new contrast was added versus already covered companies
- what the next best comparators or adjacent names are

The point of the closeout is not administrative neatness by itself.

The point is to make each run immediately usable by the next worker without forcing them to reconstruct:

- source authority
- reporting-window logic
- thematic meaning
- remaining gaps

## Non-Optional Analytical Deliverable

The archive is supposed to accumulate more than company facts.

It must accumulate evidence-backed explanations of:

- consumer behavior
- cultural participation
- societal demand shifts
- industrial operating pressure
- margin structure
- capital-cycle discipline
- commodity, transport, throughput, or infrastructure dependence

This means the analytical layer is not a bonus paragraph.

It must visibly shape:

- which companies get picked
- which facts get extracted
- how the packet is written
- how the lane is summarized
- how the next company is chosen

If the work ends with organized files but no clearer explanation of the surrounding system, the job is not finished to standard.

## End-Of-Run Reporting Requirement

Every run should end with:

- commit hash
- companies completed
- companies partial
- industry lane summary
- next recommended names

If no commit is made, state why.

## Archive-Level Objective

The broader objective is only being served when:

- multiple flagship companies across undercovered lanes are fully packetized
- each company has both source completeness and thematic interpretation
- sector and lane synthesis notes reflect recurring patterns across companies
- indexes are updated in coherent batches
- the repo stays handoff-ready
- each run leaves a usable git checkpoint

This workflow should produce a durable research machine, not a one-time scrape.

## Autonomous Continuation Rule

Assume the work should continue unless there is a real blocker.

Real blockers are narrow:

- the source chain is unavailable
- the reporting window cannot be honestly established
- the candidate is too messy to support a flagship packet
- there is a direct conflict with already assigned overlapping work

Most other situations are not blockers. They are selection problems.

When that happens, solve it by picking another company or another adjacent industry yourself.

The archive should keep advancing even when the original lane list is incomplete, exhausted, or low quality.

## Final Operating Instruction

When in doubt, optimize for:

- archive quality
- thematic distinctiveness
- clean evidence trails
- comparison value
- forward motion

Never reduce the assignment to document gathering alone.

The baseline evidence requirement is always:

- the `2025` annual report
- the latest three reported quarters, or the closest honest equivalent reporting periods, as of `Monday, August 10, 2026`
- `AnnualReports.com` for taxonomy and archive confirmation
- company IR and SEC materials as the authoritative source chain when `AnnualReports.com` lags

The baseline interpretation requirement is always:

- what the company says about its own business
- what the company says about the larger system around it
- what recurring pattern this adds to the archive across consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, commodity, infrastructure, capital, or operating themes

Different claim types also need different proof burdens:

- consumer claim
  - show: what behavior changed and which facts prove it
- cultural or societal claim
  - show: which real-life pressure is creating demand and why it is broader than one company
- industrial or operating claim
  - show: where the strain sits and what happens economically when that pressure changes
- technical or infrastructure claim
  - show: where software is the control layer and where physical bottlenecks still decide outcomes
- capital or balance-sheet claim
  - show: who must carry property, inventory, debt, or financing burden to keep the system working
- cross-company pattern claim
  - show: exact support from at least three companies and what evidence would weaken the broader pattern

If assigned industries run out, the next move is not to stop.

The next move is to choose the strongest uncovered adjacent lane yourself and keep building.

If you ever lack industries, lack clear targets, or inherit an incomplete lane list, pick the next strong area on your own and continue to the same standard.
