# Final Wrap-Up Goal And Success Criteria

Date baseline: `2026-08-11`
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Purpose

This note defines the final end-to-end goal for wrapping up the current archive push.

It is not a blank-slate build brief.

It assumes the archive already has:

- real company packets
- lane summaries
- proof pages
- cross-company themes
- handoff notes
- repeated-signal analysis

The remaining job is to close the archive honestly rather than to keep expanding it loosely.

## The Meaty End-To-End Goal

Take the current archive from:

- strong but uneven

to:

- interpretation-rich
- evidence-grounded
- continuation-ready
- honest about what is fully proven versus only directionally strong

In one sentence:

Finish `annual-report-research` as a plain-English, evidence-backed archive that lets a skeptical reader move from company filings to lane economics, cross-company patterns, consumer and societal shifts, operating pressure, and exact next watchpoints without depending on vague claims, missing flagship roles, or misleading evidence references.

## What “Wrap This Up” Actually Means

Wrapping this up does not mean:

- downloading every possible filing
- starting unlimited new packets
- chasing every uncovered company
- making the repo look cosmetically complete

Wrapping this up means finishing the highest-value remaining closure work across four layers at once:

1. `lane closure`
2. `proof quality`
3. `writeup quality`
4. `reference quality`

The final state should let a reader answer:

1. `What changed in this part of the economy?`
2. `Which companies prove it?`
3. `Who benefits and who carries the burden?`
4. `What patterns repeat across lanes?`
5. `What remains uncertain?`
6. `What exactly should the next filing reader watch?`

## The Four Final Workstreams

### 1. Lane Closure

Close the thinnest requested sub-lanes so the archive no longer feels like a few strong islands surrounded by thin edges.

The main unresolved lane-role gaps are:

- recreation:
  - second food-wholesale and owner-versus-brand comparison depth
- CLI 4:
  - second payer
  - second hospital
  - stronger long-term-care or scaled-care contrast if needed
- CLI 5:
  - stronger explicit system / endpoint / healthcare-information extension cluster
- CLI 6:
  - second life-insurance anchor
  - mortgage REIT anchor
  - cleaner savings-and-loans or thrift analog if feasible

This does not require dozens of new names.

It requires enough exact additions to remove the most obvious missing-role holes.

### 2. Proof Quality

Upgrade the weakest evidence chains so the archive can distinguish:

- `proven`
- `qualified but usable`
- `not yet canonically repaired`

This means:

- closing the highest-priority quarter-window ambiguities
- tightening source-authority language
- repairing misleading packet or ledger claims
- upgrading the best qualified cases where possible

The key standard is:

- do not claim stronger evidence than the current repo actually proves

### 3. Writeup Quality

Raise the remaining weaker writeups toward the newer proof-page standard.

The main requirement is:

- every important claim should feel concrete, causal, and backed by named examples

That means the writeups should not stop at:

- "aging creates demand"
- "AI increases infrastructure needs"
- "loyalty matters"
- "trust is valuable"

They should show:

- which exact company facts led to that conclusion
- why that pattern repeats across multiple companies
- where the burden sits
- what would weaken or break the conclusion

### 4. Reference Quality

Normalize the evidence-reference model so the repo points to evidence in an intentional, comprehensible way.

Because raw offload to Google Drive is intentional, the real remaining reference problem is not "raw is outside Git."

It is:

- many packets still point directly to sibling worktrees instead of:
  - the current repo
  - or the documented offload / manifest / resolver model

The final repo should make it obvious whether a source is:

- local in the current repo
- intentionally offloaded and recoverable through the manifest layer
- inherited and not yet canonically normalized

## The Success Criteria

The wrap-up is successful only if all of the following are true.

### A. Lane Success

- each major active lane has a coherent explanation, not just scattered packets
- the thinnest high-priority role gaps have been closed or explicitly deferred with exact next targets
- the archive no longer depends on one single flagship name to carry an entire requested sub-lane

### B. Interpretation Success

- the main lane writeups and proof pages explain:
  - what changed
  - why it changed
  - who benefits
  - who carries the burden
  - what repeats across companies
- major claims are supported by concrete company examples rather than only theme language
- older weakly evidenced notes have either been upgraded or clearly superseded by stronger proof pages

### C. Evidence Success

- the highest-priority thin-lane packets have explicit `2025` annual plus latest-three-quarters logic
- source-authority ordering is clear where AnnualReports lagged
- misleading packet or ledger claims have been removed
- the repo clearly distinguishes `proven` from `qualified`

### D. Reference Success

- the repo no longer casually relies on arbitrary sibling-worktree paths without explanation
- the evidence path for important packets is either:
  - local and inspectable
  - intentionally offloaded and documented
  - or explicitly marked as inherited and not yet canonically normalized

### E. Handoff Success

- the final continuation layer tells another worker:
  - what is complete
  - what is qualified
  - what is still partial
  - what the next exact targets are
  - what commit defines the wrap-up checkpoint

## The Ending Criteria

Stop only when the archive can honestly pass this ending test:

### 1. Lane-ending test

A skeptical reader can open each major lane and see:

- flagship companies
- lane summary
- repeated signals
- burden split
- next questions

without feeling that a key requested bucket is still obviously missing.

### 2. Proof-ending test

A skeptical reader can tell, for the most important packets:

- which cases are fully proven
- which are qualified
- why they are qualified
- what exact evidence is still missing if they are not fully proven

### 3. Writeup-ending test

A skeptical reader can read the main proof pages and say:

- "I see the exact companies"
- "I see the facts"
- "I see the causal chain"
- "I see why this is a bigger pattern"
- "I see what would break the claim"

### 4. Reference-ending test

A skeptical reader is not forced to guess whether a broken-looking local path is:

- intentional offload
- stale sibling-worktree dependence
- or a missing file

The repo should tell them.

### 5. Handoff-ending test

Another worker can continue from the repo without reconstructing:

- which lanes are actually done
- which packets are only qualified
- which gaps still matter most
- how the evidence-storage model works

## What Does Not Need To Be True Before Stopping

Do not wait for impossible perfection.

The wrap-up does not require:

- every packet in the archive to be fully proven
- every historical note to be rewritten
- every external dependency to be physically moved into Git
- every possible sub-lane to have maximum depth

The right standard is:

- the major insights are real
- the major lanes are coherent
- the major evidence claims are honest
- the remaining uncertainty is explicit

## The Concrete Final Sequence

The highest-yield order for finishing is:

1. close the biggest remaining lane-role gaps with a small number of high-value names
2. upgrade or clarify the highest-priority qualified packets
3. raise the weakest still-important writeups to the proof-page standard
4. normalize the most important non-canonical evidence references
5. leave one final wrap-up handoff that states:
   - completed
   - qualified
   - partial
   - next exact names
   - reference model
   - commit hash

## Bottom Line

The final goal is not to make the archive look busy.

It is to make the archive trustworthy, legible, and strong enough that:

- the analysis is concrete
- the evidence claims are honest
- the lanes feel genuinely opened and mostly closed
- and the next worker does not have to reverse-engineer what is real, what is partial, and how the evidence is supposed to be found

That is the wrap-up target for `annual-report-research` as of `2026-08-11`.
