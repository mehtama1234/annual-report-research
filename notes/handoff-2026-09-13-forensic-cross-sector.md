## Combined end-to-end objective

Turn the annual-report archive into a repeatable cross-sector investment-
research system. For each priority company, start with the economic control
point and macro/durability context, translate the narrative into operating and
valuation numbers, reconcile reported earnings to cash and required capital,
test accounting presentation and management incentives for financial-
engineering risk, and finish with evidence-graded conclusions, valuation
scenarios, and explicit filing-based thesis breakers. The governing lenses are
Lyn Alden's durability/liquidity lens, Damodaran's narrative-to-numbers,
reinvestment, lifecycle, and valuation lens, and our forensic framework for
earnings quality, cash conversion, capital burden, incentives, and falsifiers.

The consolidated operating specification is captured in the [combined
end-to-end forensic research system](../analysis/cross-sector/combined-end-to-end-forensic-research-system-2026-09-13.md).
Use it as the definition of done for the next company or lane: control point,
narrative-to-numbers bridge, GAAP-to-cash and burden stack, incentive test,
price-implied expectations, and filing-based breaker.

## Current cross-sector signals

1. The cleaner economics often sit with the party controlling a repeated
   workflow, standard, network, or customer relationship.
2. The control point is not sufficient: economic profit depends on cash after
   inventory, labor, maintenance, service, quality, financing, and capex.
3. Backlog, AUM, installed systems, market share, pipeline, and adjusted EPS
   are leading indicators, not proof of shareholder value.
4. “One-time” charges, tariff benefits, acquisition growth, working-capital
   releases, marks, and future-phase opportunities must be bridged to GAAP,
   cash, and return on incremental capital.
5. The best contradiction cases are companies that control an important layer
but either carry heavy physical burdens or fail to convert visibility into
cash.
