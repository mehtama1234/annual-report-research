# Insight Completion Rubric

Date: 2026-08-11
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Purpose

Use this page to judge whether a company packet, lane run, or proof memo is actually finished.

This is not a style checklist. It is a completion test.

If a writeup sounds smart but cannot pass this rubric with exact evidence, it is still intermediate work.

## The Core Rule

The archive is not done when it has documents, summaries, or company descriptions.

It is done when a reader can move from:

`source document -> exact fact -> company meaning -> lane meaning -> cross-company pattern -> economic consequence -> watch metric -> thesis breaker`

without having to guess.

## What "Done" Means

A finished insight artifact must answer all of these in plain English:

1. `What is happening?`
2. `How do we know?`
3. `Why is it happening?`
4. `Which exact examples prove it?`
5. `Who benefits economically?`
6. `Who carries the burden?`
7. `What would weaken or break the claim?`
8. `What should we watch next?`

If any one of those is missing, the work is not fully done.

## Scoring Scale

Use a four-part completion scale:

- `0 = missing`
- `1 = started but weak`
- `2 = present but still generic`
- `3 = strong and evidenced`

Minimum standard:

- flagship company packet: no section below `2`, at least `24/30` total
- lane summary: no section below `2`, at least `21/27` total
- proof memo: no section below `2`, at least `24/30` total

If an artifact fails the minimum, it should be treated as partial even if the file exists.

## Company Packet Rubric

Score each flagship company packet on these ten dimensions.

### 1. Source Coverage

`0`: missing filing chain

`1`: annual only or quarter chain incomplete

`2`: annual report, annual filing, and latest three reported quarters are all covered

`3`: same as `2`, plus source ledger and any AnnualReports lag or fiscal-cadence adaptation are explained cleanly

### 2. Real Business Explanation

`0`: only names the sector or product category

`1`: describes the business loosely

`2`: explains what the company is really selling and what repeated need creates demand

`3`: explains the real business model so clearly that a reader can distinguish it from a same-category peer

### 3. Exact Evidence

`0`: broad claims with no numbers

`1`: a few numbers but not tied to the conclusion

`2`: annual and quarter facts support the strategy read

`3`: exact metrics, segment facts, and quarter progression directly support each major claim

### 4. Growth Engine

`0`: no clear growth explanation

`1`: management language repeated without mechanism

`2`: names the real growth engine and the metric that best tracks it

`3`: also explains whether the growth engine is structural, cyclical, mix-driven, price-driven, capacity-driven, or partner-driven

### 5. Burden Stack

`0`: no burden discussion

`1`: mentions costs in passing

`2`: identifies the real burden stack such as labor, reimbursement, inventory, debt, property, compliance, capex, routing, or field execution

`3`: shows which burden most constrains profit and which metric would reveal improvement or deterioration

### 6. Strategy Interpretation

`0`: mostly summary

`1`: some interpretation but generic

`2`: explains what management is trying to change and why

`3`: distinguishes between stated strategy and what the numbers suggest is actually working

### 7. Bigger Signal

`0`: no broader meaning

`1`: vague trend language

`2`: ties the company to at least one consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, technical, or capital-structure signal with exact support

`3`: makes the company materially useful as evidence for at least one cross-company pattern and clearly states who benefits and who carries burden

### 8. Thesis Breaker

`0`: none

`1`: weak generic risk list

`2`: names what would weaken the company read

`3`: names the exact metric, event, or competitor behavior that would break the thesis

### 9. Watchlist

`0`: none

`1`: vague next steps

`2`: gives next-quarter metrics and questions

`3`: watchlist is specific enough that another thread could continue immediately

### 10. Comparison Value

`0`: isolated packet

`1`: peer names listed only

`2`: explains which peers clarify the company

`3`: explains what distinct role the company plays inside a lane or proof memo

## Lane Summary Rubric

Score each lane run on these nine dimensions.

### 1. Flagship Set Quality

`0`: no coherent set

`1`: names collected but roles overlap too much

`2`: 3-8 companies with distinct roles

`3`: comparison set covers the lane from multiple angles without becoming shallow

### 2. Economic Model

`0`: no lane economics

`1`: lane described by sector label

`2`: explains where demand comes from, where money is made, and where margins get lost

`3`: also separates recurring, cyclical, policy-driven, and capital-intensive parts of the lane

### 3. Relationship Owner Versus Burden Carrier

`0`: missing

`1`: implied but not stated

`2`: identifies who owns the customer and who carries the hard burden

`3`: shows how that split changes economics across the flagship names

### 4. Evidence Quality

`0`: mostly company labels

`1`: some reported facts but sparse

`2`: lane claims are supported by exact packet facts across multiple companies

`3`: the lane could be audited quickly because each major claim has exact examples and the packet inputs behind the comparison are named explicitly

### 5. Operating Pressures

`0`: none

`1`: generic macro mention

`2`: identifies the main labor, reimbursement, policy, inventory, property, network, capex, or workflow pressures

`3`: distinguishes which pressures are lane-wide and which are company-specific

### 6. Bigger-Pattern Value

`0`: no broader meaning

`1`: vague theme language

`2`: lane teaches at least one bigger consumer, societal, industrial, technical, or capital-structure pattern

`3`: lane clearly strengthens one or more repo-wide proof themes and explains the beneficiary-versus-burden split inside the lane

### 7. Metric Glossary And Watchlist

`0`: absent

`1`: weak metric list

`2`: gives the metrics that should be tracked across the lane

`3`: tells a future thread exactly what would strengthen or weaken the lane thesis

### 8. Next Targets

`0`: no next names

`1`: random next names

`2`: recommended names are tied to a specific gap

`3`: next names are prioritized by what they would prove, sharpen, or disprove

### 9. Handoff Quality

`0`: no handoff

`1`: handoff exists but is vague

`2`: completed names, partial names, key themes, and next steps are clear

`3`: commit hash, source gaps, raw-blob status, and exact next action are all recorded

## Proof Memo Rubric

Score each proof memo on these ten dimensions.

### 1. Plain-English Claim

`0`: no clear claim

`1`: claim exists but is too abstract

`2`: clear claim in simple language

`3`: clear claim with scope and limits stated honestly

### 2. Multi-Company Evidence

`0`: one-company anecdote

`1`: several companies named but thin evidence

`2`: at least three companies provide exact support

`3`: multiple companies play distinct roles in proving the pattern and the memo states which packet sections supplied the proof

### 3. Causal Chain

`0`: no mechanism

`1`: implied mechanism

`2`: explains why the pattern exists

`3`: connects customer behavior, cultural or societal context when relevant, operating burden, and economic outcome in one chain

### 4. Concrete Examples

`0`: none

`1`: examples are generic

`2`: examples include reported metrics or actions

`3`: examples are concrete enough that a skeptical reader can see how the conclusion was reached

### 5. Beneficiary Versus Burden Split

`0`: missing

`1`: hinted at only

`2`: states who captures clean economics and who absorbs the hard work

`3`: compares more than one version of that split across the evidence set

### 6. Disconfirming Evidence

`0`: none

`1`: weak caveats only

`2`: names what could weaken the claim

`3`: includes either a contradictory company, a boundary condition, or a metric that would clearly disprove the pattern

### 7. Watch Metrics

`0`: none

`1`: broad watchlist only

`2`: identifies exact next metrics or events to follow

`3`: watchlist directly maps to the thesis and disconfirming tests

### 8. Cross-Repo Usefulness

`0`: standalone essay

`1`: weakly connected

`2`: clearly connects to packets, lane summaries, or adjacent proof memos

`3`: materially improves the repo's ability to reuse evidence across multiple lanes

### 9. Aha Value

`0`: obvious statement

`1`: mildly interesting but familiar

`2`: gives a useful reframing

`3`: creates a real "I had not connected that before" moment grounded in evidence and concrete examples

### 10. Reader Handoff

`0`: dead end

`1`: some next ideas

`2`: gives next targets or questions

`3`: gives exact next companies, exact next metrics, and the best way to extend the memo

## Skeptical Reader Test

Every strong lane summary or proof memo should let a skeptical reader answer all four of these without guessing:

1. `What is the exact fact?`
2. `What does that fact mean?`
3. `Why does it matter beyond one company?`
4. `What would change the conclusion?`

If the artifact fails any one of these, score it down even if it sounds polished.

## Fast Failure Tests

If any of these are true, the artifact is not done:

- it uses company names as evidence
- it uses sector labels as explanation
- it makes a broad claim without exact filing support
- it has numbers but does not explain why they matter
- it never says which packet inputs supplied the lane or memo conclusion
- it names a consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, technical, or capital pattern without saying how the filing evidence supports it
- it says a trend exists without naming who benefits and who carries burden
- it says a theme is interesting without naming what would disprove it
- it sounds polished but another thread could not continue from it

## Review Questions Before Commit

Ask these before calling the work complete:

1. Can a skeptical reader trace each major conclusion back to exact facts?
2. Does it say which packet inputs produced the lane or memo conclusion?
3. Does the writeup explain mechanism instead of repeating management language?
4. Does it identify the burden stack, not just the revenue opportunity?
5. Does it say what would break the claim?
6. Does it tell the next thread what to watch and where to go next?

If the answer to any one of those is no, keep working.

## Short Version

The real test is simple:

`Could a smart reader understand what changed, why it changed, how we know, who gets paid, who carries the burden, and what to watch next without adding their own guesswork?`

If not, the artifact is not finished.
