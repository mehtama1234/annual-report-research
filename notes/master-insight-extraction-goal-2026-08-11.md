# Master Insight Extraction Goal

Date: 2026-08-11
Repo: `annual-report-research`

## Mission

Build `annual-report-research` into a source-grounded insight engine, not a filing warehouse.

The goal is to turn `2025` annual reports, annual filings, and the latest three reported quarters into a clear map of:

- what each company is really doing
- how each industry lane works
- which operating pressures repeat across companies
- which consumer, cultural, societal, industrial, technical, and capital-structure shifts are becoming visible in filings
- which claims are proven by exact evidence
- which claims remain early, weak, or in need of next-quarter confirmation

The finished archive should let a reader move from source documents to company strategy, industry economics, cross-company themes, aha moments, watchlists, and thesis breakers without guessing.

## Meaty End-To-End Goal

The end-to-end goal is to extract how the economy is changing from company filings, using companies as evidence rather than anecdotes.

Every lane run should start with a concrete business system and finish with a plain-English explanation of that system:

- who the end customer, patient, household, enterprise, institution, or government buyer is
- what recurring need, anxiety, habit, occasion, workflow, or physical constraint creates demand
- which companies own the relationship
- which companies carry the hard burden: labor, property, inventory, capex, regulation, reimbursement, debt, power, supply chain, or field execution
- which exact metrics prove the demand is real
- which exact metrics prove the pressure is real
- which strategy management is using to adapt
- which companies show the same pattern from a different angle
- which companies contradict or weaken the pattern
- what should be checked in the next filing window

The work is complete only when the archive can answer four reader questions without vague language:

1. `What is happening?`

   State the trend in plain English.

   Example: `Healthcare demand is moving into repeated-care networks outside the hospital.`

2. `How do we know?`

   Use exact filing-window evidence from multiple companies.

   Example: `DaVita shows dialysis treatment frequency, Option Care shows alternate-site infusion revenue, AdaptHealth shows home equipment delivery volume, Quest shows diagnostic testing growth, and Cencora shows drug-distribution scale.`

3. `Why does it matter economically?`

   Explain who gets paid, who carries burden, and which metric shows whether the system is improving or breaking.

   Example: `The patient need is recurring, but the profits depend on reimbursement, staffing, routing, documentation, product availability, and payer behavior.`

4. `What should we watch next?`

   Name the exact metrics and companies that would prove, sharpen, or disprove the claim.

   Example: `Watch treatments per day, organic testing growth, infusion revenue, equipment delivery volume, medical care ratio, labor cost, and drug-distribution operating margin.`

This means the output is not just a packet, a summary, or a theme name. The output is a connected evidence chain:

`source fact -> company strategy -> customer behavior -> operating pressure -> industry economics -> cross-company pattern -> aha moment -> thesis breaker -> next target`

Every company packet should contribute at least one useful insight to that chain. If it does not, the packet is unfinished or the company was the wrong target.

## Core Standard

No broad claim without concrete filing evidence.

No company name as evidence.

No sector label as explanation.

Every serious insight must connect exact packet facts to a clear business conclusion.

The minimum evidence chain for each flagship company is:

- AnnualReports.com taxonomy and archive confirmation
- `2025` annual report or closest equivalent annual report
- `2025` annual filing or authoritative annual filing equivalent
- latest three reported quarters as of the run date
- official company IR and SEC as authoritative when AnnualReports.com lags
- source pointers that make the evidence auditable

If a company has a non-standard fiscal year or reporting cadence, use the closest honest equivalent and explain the adaptation.

## The Four Required Insight Layers

Every meaningful lane run should produce four insight layers.

### 1. Company-Level Strategy

Explain the company itself.

Required questions:

- What business is this really in?
- What repeated customer, patient, enterprise, institutional, or societal need does it serve?
- What is the real growth engine?
- What metric matters most?
- What is management trying to change?
- What constraint blocks the strategy from becoming clean profit?
- Is the company carrying the burden stack, capturing the cleaner economics, or doing both?
- What would break the thesis?
- What peer comparison makes the company easier to understand?

Required output:

- source-complete packet
- company-level strategy read
- exact annual evidence
- exact three-quarter evidence
- key operating metrics
- management message
- operating constraints
- thematic interpretation
- thesis breaker
- next filing watchlist
- recommended peer comparisons

Example standard:

Do not write only: `Costco is a value retailer.`

Write: `Costco is a membership-funded warehouse system. Fiscal 2025 membership fees rose to $5.323B, net sales grew 8.1% to $269.9B, Q3 2026 net sales grew 11.6%, and digitally enabled comparable sales rose 21.5%. The strategy is to convert trust in low-price access into repeated household traffic and paid renewal behavior.`

### 2. Industry-Level Economics

Explain how the lane works.

Required questions:

- What customer need creates demand?
- How does the lane make money?
- What drives margin quality?
- What are the key operating metrics?
- Which companies own the customer relationship?
- Which companies carry assets, labor, inventory, debt, reimbursement, or regulatory burden?
- Which parts are structural and which are cyclical?
- What would weaken the lane thesis?

Required output:

- industry lane summary
- 4-8 flagship company comparison set when enough clean candidates exist
- economic model of the lane
- metric glossary for the lane
- company comparison table
- operating constraints
- regulation and policy risks
- capital intensity and cash-flow profile
- next recommended names

Example standard:

Do not write only: `Healthcare is growing because people are aging.`

Write: `Healthcare outside the hospital is a repeated-care logistics lane. DaVita shows treatment frequency through about 91,650 U.S. treatments per day in Q1 2026. Option Care shows alternate-site infusion with Q2 2026 net revenue of about $1.442B. AdaptHealth shows home-equipment logistics through about 38,500 equipment and supply deliveries per day. Quest shows diagnostic information flow with Q2 2026 organic growth of 10.0%. Cencora shows product-flow infrastructure with fiscal 2025 revenue of $321B.`

### 3. Cross-Company Themes

Identify repeated patterns across companies.

Required questions:

- What consumer behavior is changing?
- What cultural or societal shift is becoming normal?
- What industrial pressure appears across several management teams?
- What technical dependency or infrastructure constraint is spreading?
- What trust, compliance, reimbursement, labor, tariff, power, financing, or capacity burden is rising?
- Which companies monetize the shift cleanly?
- Which companies absorb the burden?
- Which adjacent industries should be tested next?

Required output:

- cross-company theme memo
- exact evidence table
- causal chain
- stronger plain-English conclusion
- disconfirming evidence
- watchlist metrics
- next questions

Required theme types to extract when evidence supports them:

- AI physical capacity
- hidden operating infrastructure
- selective consumer behavior
- membership, habit, and repeat relationship systems
- fandom, identity, play, participation, franchise, and IP monetization
- healthcare outside the hospital
- aging as operating reality
- trust as paid product
- relationship owner versus burden carrier
- regulation and policy as business model
- real estate split between needed and troubled space
- commodity chains rather than broad commodity buckets
- media split between attention owners and attention helpers
- taxonomy blind spots

Example standard:

Do not write only: `AI benefits infrastructure.`

Write: `AI demand shows up in physical order books. WESCO Q2 2026 backlog rose about 60% and data-center sales rose about 45% to $1.5B. Vertiv 2025 backlog reached $15.0B and Q4 organic orders rose 252%. Trane Q2 2026 backlog reached $12.1B. Duke Q1 2026 data-center ESAs reached 7.6GW. The conclusion is that AI is creating demand for power, cooling, electrical distribution, grid capacity, and skilled field execution, not only chips and cloud software.`

### 4. Curiosity, Watchlist, And Thesis Breakers

Turn findings into an investigation system.

Required questions:

- What surprised us?
- What pattern looks early but not proven?
- What exact metric should be checked next quarter?
- What would disprove the claim?
- Which company is the best next test?
- Which company is most likely to contradict the theme?

Required output:

- aha moments
- curiosity questions
- weak signals
- metric watchlist
- thesis breaker index
- next filing checklist
- next target roster

Example standard:

Do not write only: `Beauty is strong.`

Write: `Beauty may be becoming a data and media business. Ulta's packet records UB Marketplace with more than 200 brands and 5,000 SKUs, plus UB Media built from loyalty-member behavior. e.l.f. FY2027 Q1 net sales grew 36% and gross margin reached 83%. Watch Ulta comparable sales, inventory growth, marketplace/media progress, and e.l.f. gross margin, debt, and acquired-brand contribution. The thesis weakens if both the discovery retailer and accessible brand owner slow at the same time.`

## Required Company Packet End Sections

Every flagship packet should end with these sections or their equivalent:

### Company-Level Strategy Read

Plain-English answer:

What is the company trying to become, and what exact evidence proves it?

### Operating Model In Plain English

How does the company make money, what has to go right, and who pays?

### Strategy Evidence From The Filing Window

Use exact facts from:

- annual report
- annual filing
- latest quarter
- quarter minus one
- quarter minus two

### Customer Or Institutional Behavior

What behavior is the company responding to or trying to shape?

Examples:

- value-seeking
- identity spending
- home-based care
- participation and fandom
- workflow outsourcing
- security and compliance fear
- power-density demand
- repair and replacement behavior

### Cultural, Societal, Or Industrial Signal

What bigger reality does the company reveal?

Examples:

- aging into repeated care
- trust and proof becoming paid infrastructure
- AI creating physical bottlenecks
- beauty becoming discovery and media
- consumer pressure moving upstream into packaging
- distributors becoming procurement workflow owners

### Burden Versus Surplus

State whether the company:

- owns the clean relationship
- carries the heavy asset/labor/inventory/regulatory burden
- does both
- sits between the burden carrier and the customer

### What Could Break The Story

State the exact metric, event, or filing signal that would weaken the interpretation.

### Next Filing Watchlist

List the next metrics to check.

## Insight Types To Extract

### Real-Business Insight

What the company really is.

Examples:

- Ecolab is embedded hygiene, water, foodservice, pest, life-sciences, and compliance-service infrastructure, not just specialty chemicals.
- Veralto is quality, water, monitoring, coding, marking, and product-control workflow infrastructure, not just pollution controls.
- Roblox is participatory social and creator-economy infrastructure, not just internet software.

### Growth-Engine Insight

What is actually driving growth.

Examples:

- Ulta growth is stores plus salons, loyalty, digital, marketplace, media, Space NK, and beauty discovery.
- WESCO growth is data-center, utility, electrical, communications, and grid-service demand moving through distribution.
- Quest growth is diagnostics volume, advanced testing, consumer access, automation, and payer/clinical demand.

### Constraint Insight

What prevents demand from becoming clean earnings.

Examples:

- Brookdale demand depends on occupancy, staffing, resident affordability, and care intensity.
- AdaptHealth demand depends on documentation, payer routing, delivery cost, collections, and resupply execution.
- Graphic Packaging demand depends on volume, price/mix, debt, capex, plant utilization, and customer affordability.

### Management-Reaction Insight

What management keeps doing or repeating.

Examples:

- General Mills is reinvesting in value, product news, advertising, innovation, and brand relevance.
- Dow is using a weak chemicals cycle to push cost reduction and footprint simplification.
- Ferguson is using acquisitions, branch density, non-residential exposure, and technical distribution to offset residential softness.

### Economic-Lever Insight

The metric that explains the company better than revenue alone.

Examples:

- Costco: membership fees and adjusted comparable sales.
- DaVita: treatments per day and reimbursement.
- Ventas: SHOP same-store cash NOI.
- WESCO: backlog and data-center sales.
- Netflix: engagement, ad revenue, operating margin, and free cash flow.
- Freeport: realized copper price, unit cash cost, production, and capex.

### Burden-Stack Insight

Who carries the hard cost.

Examples:

- Marriott owns brand and loyalty while hotel owners carry property burden.
- Marsh owns advisory and placement workflow while insurers carry underwriting risk.
- Disney owns fan relationships but also carries content and parks burden.
- Utilities benefit from data-center demand but must carry regulated capex and rate-recovery risk.

### Cultural Or Societal Insight

What human behavior or social expectation is becoming visible.

Examples:

- Beauty is self-presentation, discovery, creator influence, and data.
- Comfort footwear is normalized identity, not just functional footwear.
- Aging is not a demographic sentence; it becomes housing, dialysis, diagnostics, home care, equipment, and drug distribution.
- Participation platforms turn consumers into players, creators, fans, and members.

### Industrial Pressure Insight

What operating pressure repeats.

Examples:

- AI creates power, cooling, skilled labor, grid, and equipment pressure.
- Industrial distribution is shaped by tariffs, working capital, supplier breadth, and branch density.
- Fertilizer is shaped by gas costs, plant uptime, geopolitics, and crop economics.
- Packaging is shaped by consumer affordability, capital projects, plant closures, and debt.

### Thesis-Breaker Insight

What would disprove the interpretation.

Examples:

- AI infrastructure thesis weakens if backlog does not convert into profitable revenue.
- Selective-consumer thesis weakens if Costco, Ulta, e.l.f., Crocs, and Deckers all slow together.
- Healthcare outside hospital thesis weakens if reimbursement and labor absorb all volume growth.
- Trust-as-product thesis weakens if major trust failures cause customer losses or if proof layers become commoditized.

## Lane Selection Standard

A lane is worth pursuing when it can produce:

- 4-8 flagship companies
- clean source chains
- internal contrast
- at least one undercovered economic role
- at least one cross-company pattern
- a useful next-target list

Prefer lanes that explain a system, not just a category.

Examples:

- `Healthcare outside the hospital`: DaVita, Option Care, Addus, AdaptHealth, Quest, Cencora, Brookdale, Ventas.
- `AI physical capacity`: NVIDIA, Cisco, Vertiv, Eaton, Trane, Comfort Systems, Duke, WESCO.
- `Selective consumer`: Costco, Ulta, e.l.f., Crocs, Deckers, General Mills, Graphic Packaging.
- `Trust and proof`: S&P Global, Marsh, CrowdStrike, Quest, Ecolab, Veralto, DoubleVerify, CME.
- `Commodity chains`: Freeport, Nucor, CF, Dow, West Fraser, Graphic Packaging.

If assigned targets run out, choose the next strongest uncovered adjacent lane and continue. Do not stop simply because the first list is exhausted.

## Raw Blob Rule

Raw PDFs, HTML filings, large downloaded artifacts, and blob-heavy source payloads should not be pushed blindly into Git.

Use Git for:

- packets
- profiles
- ledgers
- source summaries
- indexes
- manifests
- notes
- analysis pages
- site pages

Use Google Drive or an agreed raw offload path for:

- large raw source archives
- bulk PDFs
- large HTML captures
- local-only evidence bundles

Every raw offload should leave:

- manifest
- checksum
- Drive path or link
- explanation of which raw paths were offloaded
- Git pointers or notes that let another thread recover the evidence

## Definition Of Done

The work is done only when the archive has moved from documents to evidence-backed explanation.

A packet is not done if it only says what the company reported.

A lane is not done if it only lists completed companies.

A theme is not done if it only names companies.

The correct finished state is:

- exact evidence is collected
- company strategy is explained
- industry economics are explained
- repeated patterns are identified
- social, cultural, consumer, industrial, technical, and capital-structure implications are stated plainly
- weak points and thesis breakers are named
- next filing metrics are listed
- next company targets are clear

The archive should become a map of how the economy actually works, using filings as the evidence base.
