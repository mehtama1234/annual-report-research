What It Covers

  • AI is making physical infrastructure more important.
  • Hidden support companies may be closer to the center of the economy than they look.
  • The consumer is not weak everywhere. The consumer is selective.
  • Habits, memberships, loyalty, and repeat use are major business assets.
  • Fandom, play, identity, and participation are real economic forces.
  • Healthcare is becoming a repeated-care system outside the hospital.
  • Aging creates concrete operating needs, not just demographic language.
  • Trust is becoming something companies sell through proof, safety, measurement, and reliability.
  • The best economics often sit between the customer and the heavy burden.
  • Regulation and policy are part of the business model, not background noise.
  • Real estate is split between needed space and troubled space.
  • Commodity businesses must be read by chain, not by broad sector label.
  • Media is splitting into attention owners and attention helpers.
  • AnnualReports taxonomy is useful, but the best insights often cut across it.
