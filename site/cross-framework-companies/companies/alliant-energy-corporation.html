Annual Report Takeaways

  • Alliant is a useful fourth utility because it brings a smaller, more regional Midwest regulated-utility profile into the set. That makes it a cleaner test of whether the power-demand story is spreading beyond the largest utilities and power-platform franchises.
  • AnnualReports currently classifies Alliant as `Utilities / Diversified Utilities`, which is a useful taxonomy wrinkle relative to the `Electric Utilities` labels attached to AEP, NextEra, and Duke. The underlying operating story is still electricity-plus-gas regulated utility service rather than a merchant-power model.
  • The official `2025` annual report describes Alliant as a Midwest U.S. energy company with approximately `$4.4B` of annual operating revenues. The company says it serves approximately `1,010,000` electric and `435,000` natural-gas customers through Interstate Power and Light and Wisconsin Power and Light, and it has around `3,000` employees. The more current year-end `2025` corporate fact sheet pegs the customer counts at `1,012,286` electric and `433,344` gas customers, peak-hour demand at `5,465 MW`, total assets at `$25.0B`, construction and acquisition expenditures at `$2,483M`, and employees at `2,948`.
  • The annual framing says `2025` was a pivotal year because demand for reliable energy is accelerating and because agreements signed in `2025` will significantly accelerate future energy demand.
  • Alliant’s annual story is more measured than Duke or NextEra. It is not framed as a giant national buildout platform. It is framed as a disciplined regulated growth utility using Iowa and Wisconsin regulatory tools, generation connections, and customer-specific structures to convert data-center and industrial demand without blowing up affordability.

Signal Map

  • Sector: Alliant is an important control case because it shows that the utility-growth story is not only a story about giant scale platforms. Smaller regional regulated utilities are also being pulled into the same power-demand cycle.
  • Industrial: this is still an industrial-buildout story. Alliant’s demand conversion depends on site development in Iowa and Wisconsin, new gas and wind generation, resource-planning flexibility, and customer-specific rate structures.
  • Consumer: the consumer angle is central. Alliant repeatedly argues that growth is being handled in a way that protects affordability, keeps Iowa base electric rates stable through the decade, and keeps retail electric rates below national averages.
  • Cultural: Alliant shows how data-center growth is changing local and regional utility identity. The story is no longer only commodity utility service; it is community growth, industrial siting, and local power-system readiness.
  • Regulatory: Alliant is a strong example of regulation as a growth-enabling tool. The packet is full of rate structures, ICRs, advance ratemaking, and project-specific approvals rather than broad merchant-market optionality.
  • Technology: this is another second-order AI and cloud-infrastructure read. The growth is not in software revenue; it is in the physical power demand created by large-load customers such as QTS, Google, and Meta.
