Annual Report Takeaways

  • The `2025` annual report shows record revenue of `$72.2 billion`, net income of `$10.8 billion`, and EPS of `$15.38`, with full-year billed business of `$1.67 trillion`.
  • Management attributes the result to the `Membership Model`: premium card products, merchant and partner relationships, a global closed-loop network, and rich first-party data used for personalization, fraud controls, and product design.
  • The company keeps leaning into premium customers and fee-paying products. It acquired `12.5 million` new proprietary cards in `2025`, with more than `70%` of new accounts on fee-paying products.
  • Younger cohorts are central to the growth thesis. Millennial and Gen Z consumers represented roughly `65%` of new global consumer account acquisitions and became the largest share of U.S. consumer spending.
  • The annual narrative is unusually direct that AI may change how commerce itself happens: management discusses AI-powered agents discovering products, making decisions, and completing payments, with AmEx trying to be embedded in that future flow.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: revenue of `$19.6 billion`, net income of `$3.1 billion`, and EPS of `$4.53`.
  • Management message: AmEx raised full-year `2026` revenue-growth guidance to `10%` while reaffirming EPS guidance of `$17.30` to `$17.90`.
  • Structural themes: billed business stayed strong, U.S. consumer spend rose `11%`, international card services growth remained in the low teens, and the company kept extending the travel, dining, and merchant-partnership ecosystem.
  • Credit and capital tone: credit metrics remained stable, provisions declined year over year, and AmEx kept CET1 within its `10%` to `11%` target range while returning capital to shareholders.
  • Results: revenue of `$18.9 billion`, net income of `$3.0 billion`, and EPS of `$4.28`.
  • Management message: AmEx opened the year with double-digit revenue growth and strong spending engagement across consumer, commercial, and international segments.

Signal Map

  • Sector: AmEx shows that Financial is not only a balance-sheet and capital-markets story; it is also a premium-spend, card-fee, merchant-network, and travel-lifestyle demand story.
  • Consumer: the company is one of the clearest reads in the archive on higher-income consumer resilience, dining and travel demand, and youth-driven premium-card adoption.
  • Business culture: membership, status, convenience, experiences, and embedded benefits matter economically, not just as branding language.
  • Technology: closed-loop data, AI-driven personalization, mobile experience, and agentic-commerce positioning are central to the moat.
  • Credit: unlike the banks, AmEx pairs consumer exposure with a relatively strong recent credit tone and a customer base management repeatedly describes as high-spending and high-credit-quality.
