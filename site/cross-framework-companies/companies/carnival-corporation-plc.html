Business Model Read

Annual Report Takeaways

  • Carnival is the right next travel packet because it adds the cruise and destination-vacation layer that was missing from the archive. Delta gives an airline loyalty-currency model, Hilton and Marriott give branded lodging networks, Booking gives the distribution and trip-orchestration layer, and Caesars gives a gaming-and-wallet loop. Carnival adds the multi-day destination operator that bundles transportation, lodging, entertainment, food, and onboard wallet behavior into one product.
  • The aggregator and operating classifications diverge here in a useful way. AnnualReports labeled Carnival `Consumer Goods / General Entertainment`, while the SEC currently describes the business as `Water Transportation`. That mismatch is analytically helpful because it shows how consumer-facing vacation systems can sit awkwardly across traditional sector taxonomies.
  • The `2025` annual materials show a business that moved from recovery into record profitability. Full-year revenue reached `$26.6B`, operating income reached an all-time high of `$4.5B`, net income reached `$2.8B`, adjusted net income reached `$3.1B`, and adjusted EBITDA reached `$7.2B`.
  • The operating detail matters. `2025` passenger ticket revenue was `$17.419B`, onboard and other revenue was `$9.202B`, onboard and other revenue was `34%` of cruise revenue, occupancy reached `105%`, passengers carried reached `13.6M`, and customer deposits ended the year at a fourth-quarter record of `$7.2B`.
  • Carnival is one of the clearest examples in the repo of demand funded in advance. Customer deposits were `$6.831B` in current liabilities at `2025-11-30`, and total customer deposits were `$7.246B`, which reinforces that the company collects substantial cash before the voyage and then layers onboard monetization on top.
  • The year-end `2025` materials also flagged a major structure change that became real in `2026`. Management proposed simplifying the dual-listed-company framework and completing legal incorporation in Bermuda. That became effective on `2026-05-07`, when Carnival unified under Carnival Corporation Ltd.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: Q2 `2026` net income was `$537M`, adjusted net income was `$569M`, diluted EPS was `$0.39`, adjusted EPS was `$0.41`, and adjusted EBITDA was a record `$1.6B`.
  • Demand and growth: revenues were a record `$6.7B`, booked position for the remainder of `2026` was ahead of the prior year at historically high prices, and the company said demand for `2027` and beyond continued to exceed prior-year levels.
  • Deposit and wallet signals: customer deposits reached an all-time high of `$9.0B`, while management also highlighted higher onboard revenues and increased pre-cruise onboard sales.
  • Management message: Carnival said it overcame extreme geopolitical headwinds and nearly `30%` higher fuel costs through commercial execution and stronger cost efficiency.
  • Structural themes: this quarter reinforces Carnival as a destination-and-wallet system, not just a transport operator. The key signals are booking curve length, onboard spend, pre-cruise sales, deposit float, and pricing integrity across a controlled vacation bundle.
  • Results: Q1 `2026` net income was `$258M`, adjusted net income was `$275M`, diluted EPS was `$0.19`, adjusted EPS was `$0.20`, and adjusted EBITDA was a record `$1.3B`.

Signal Map

  • Travel lane: Carnival broadens the travel packet from airline, hotel, and booking-interface logic into a bundled vacation operator with a heavier destination and onboard-spend component.
  • Consumer interface: cruise is not a daily-use interface, but it is still a powerful consumer relationship surface built on deposits, loyalty, itinerary choice, exclusivity, and pre-booked ancillary spend.
  • Cultural: Carnival is a direct read on experience consumption, vacation aspiration, and the normalization of larger prepaid leisure purchases.
  • Payments and wallet logic: Carnival does not run a card-like loyalty currency the way Delta does, but its deposit float, onboard sales, and pre-cruise sales make the model meaningfully wallet-like.
  • Destination control: the company is a useful bridge between hotels and gaming because it does not only sell access to a room or a casino floor. It increasingly sells controlled environments, private destinations, and bundled in-trip spending opportunities.
  • Capital intensity: Carnival is a strong counterpoint to Booking and the asset-light hotel names because the economics sit on a much more capital-heavy fleet and destination base.
