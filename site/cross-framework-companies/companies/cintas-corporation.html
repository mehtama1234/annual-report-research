Annual Report Takeaways

  • Cintas gives the Services sector a fifth company and broadens the archive into route-based outsourced uniforms, facility services, first aid, safety, and fire protection rather than more transport or consumer-home service.
  • The `2025` annual package showed revenue of `$10.340 billion`, operating income of `$2.360 billion`, net income of `$1.812 billion`, diluted EPS of `$4.40`, cash flow from operating activities of `$2.166 billion`, and free cash flow of `$1.757 billion`.
  • The annual filing matters because it shows that roughly `95%` of revenue comes from route servicing at customer locations. This is a dense, recurring workplace-services network with pricing power, retention, and local execution as the key earnings levers.
  • The AnnualReports taxonomy should be treated carefully here. The site tags Cintas as `Services / Apparel Stores`, but the actual filing evidence shows an outsourced workplace-services and safety-compliance platform.

Signal Map

  • Consumer: the direct read-through is weaker to household demand and stronger to commercial occupancy, small-business formation, employment density, and workplace-normalization behavior.
  • Cultural: the cultural layer is workplace readiness, safety compliance, clean-and-professional presentation, and outsourced operational reliability for employers.
  • Technology: the technology layer is route planning, sales productivity, service density, processing efficiency, and customer cross-sell rather than flashy digital products.
  • Labor: labor matters heavily because the model depends on route productivity, plant throughput, retention, and field-service execution across thousands of delivery routes.
  • Capital allocation: Cintas is a strong cash generator with continuing buybacks and dividends, and now has a large strategic acquisition question through UniFirst.
