Annual Report Takeaways

  • The `2025` `10-K` defines Core & Main as a specialty distributor focused on water, wastewater, storm drainage, fire protection products, and related services sold to municipalities, private water companies, and professional contractors across municipal, non-residential, and residential end markets.
  • The most important annual point is that this is a replacement-and-upgrade infrastructure distributor, not a simple cyclical building-products name. The filing says its products are used primarily in the maintenance, repair, replacement, and new construction of water, wastewater, storm drainage, and fire-protection infrastructure.
  • Management also lays out a clearer margin strategy than many distributors do. The annual filing explicitly points to private-label expansion, sourcing optimization, data-driven pricing strategies, and an expansion of value-added products and services as the route to sustainable margin expansion.
  • The full-year numbers were solid. Fiscal `2025` net sales were `$7.647B`, gross profit was `$2.059B`, gross margin was `26.9%`, diluted EPS was `$2.31`, adjusted EBITDA was `$931M`, and operating cash flow was `$650M`.
  • The annual filing shows why Core & Main is a useful channel-power read. It says the company operates in large and highly fragmented markets, estimates its addressable market at about `$50B`, and describes itself as one of only two national distributors with this scale. That combination of fragmentation and density is central to the moat.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Net sales were `$1.910B`, gross profit was `$520M`, gross margin was `27.2%`, net income was `$113M`, diluted EPS was `$0.57`, adjusted EBITDA was `$226M`, and operating cash flow was `$82M`.
  • The topline was essentially flat year over year, but the composition mattered more than the headline. Management said lower volume overall was offset by acquisitions, while fire protection and smart-utility products showed better demand characteristics.
  • The quarter commentary is especially valuable because it sharpened the infrastructure thesis: management highlighted continued customer demand for integrated treatment-plant and smart-utility solutions that support aging water infrastructure.
  • Capital allocation was active too. The company repurchased `$88M` of shares during the quarter and another `$37M` after quarter-end while also opening five new greenfield locations.
  • Guidance remained constructive. Core & Main reaffirmed fiscal `2026` net sales of `$7.8B-$7.9B`, adjusted EBITDA of `$950M-$980M`, and operating cash flow equal to `60%-70%` of adjusted EBITDA.
  • The fourth quarter alone looked softer on reported sales because of one fewer selling week, but the daily-sales trend was still positive. Net sales were `$1.581B`, down `6.9%`, while average daily net sales increased `0.9%`.

Signal Map

  • Core & Main is far from consumer glamour, but it sits close to public necessity. Water reliability, wastewater handling, storm drainage, fire protection, and smart metering are basic civic functions that become socially visible only when they fail.
  • The larger societal pattern is that aging U.S. infrastructure is turning previously boring distribution categories into durable demand channels. When municipalities defer replacement too long, distributors like Core & Main become important because they are the logistics and specification bridge between manufacturers and field work.
  • There is also a civic-tech angle here. Smart metering and treatment-plant solutions show that infrastructure modernization is not only about digging trenches. It also includes measurement, control, leak detection, and utility visibility.
  • Core & Main still faces meaningful exposure to residential and non-residential construction cycles, even if the water-replacement thesis gives it more resilience than a generic building-products wholesaler.
  • Municipal infrastructure spending and the timing of federal appropriations remain direct risks, and the `10-K` names both explicitly.
  • The margin model depends on disciplined purchasing, pricing, and supplier relationships. The annual filing also flags supplier rebates, supplier-term changes, and the loss of exclusive or limited distribution rights as real risks.
