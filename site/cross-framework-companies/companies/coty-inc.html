Annual Report Takeaways

  • Coty's 2025 annual materials give the consumer-goods set something it did not have before: a beauty-heavy personal-products business with real discretionary and trend exposure. The business is centered on Prestige and Consumer Beauty, with fragrance, cosmetics, skincare, and body care rather than household essentials.
  • The 2025 10-K is explicit about where management still sees growth: premium fragrance, fragrance mists, social-led Consumer Beauty improvement, and a longer-build skincare opportunity. This is a much less defensive category mix than P&G, Kimberly-Clark, Colgate, or PepsiCo.
  • Coty also brings a different retail and brand structure into the sample. No retailer accounted for more than `10%` of global net revenues in fiscal 2025, and even top retailers Walmart and A.S. Watson were only about `4%` each at the global level.
  • The annual materials make clear that the company has strong assets but more execution risk. Management language centers on innovation, brand-building, e-commerce, fragrance leadership, and portfolio shaping, but also acknowledges sustained weakness in mass beauty categories.
  • This is useful because it stress-tests the broader consumer-goods thesis. If the staples names look operationally defensive, Coty shows what happens when the portfolio is more exposed to trend shifts, promotional pressure, and category softness.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: FY2026 Q3 net revenues were `$1,281.6 million`, down `1%` reported and `7%` LFL; reported and adjusted gross margin were `61.8%`; adjusted EBITDA was `$127.0 million`; and adjusted loss per share was `$0.03`.
  • Guidance: management said profitability came in ahead of guidance, but the overall quarter was still below potential.
  • Management message: Coty said beauty demand remained resilient overall, especially in fragrance and cosmetics, but the conflict in the Middle East materially weighed on results and Consumer Beauty remained weak.
  • Structural themes: this is the clearest proof point yet that the consumer-goods sector is not one thing. Beauty can still have attractive subcategories, but it is much more vulnerable to geopolitics, portfolio weakness, and execution gaps than oral care or household essentials.
  • Results: FY2026 Q2 net revenues were `$1,678.6 million`, up `1%` reported but down `3%` LFL; Prestige revenues were `$1,133.6 million`; Consumer Beauty revenues were `$545.0 million`; adjusted EBITDA was `$330.2 million`; and adjusted EPS was `$0.14`.
  • Guidance: on February 5, 2026, Coty withdrew prior FY2026 guidance for EBITDA and free cash flow and shifted to quarter-specific Q3 guidance.

Signal Map

  • Sector: Coty is the first strong counterexample inside the consumer-goods set. It shows that not all consumer-goods categories currently behave like staples.
  • Consumer: fragrance demand remains resilient, but mass beauty and broader Consumer Beauty are weaker and more promotional. The consumer signal here is selective spending, not broad resilience.
  • Cultural: beauty is more trend-sensitive, status-sensitive, and channel-sensitive than the other names in the set. Social commerce, ultra-premium fragrance, fragrance mists, and China prestige momentum matter more than household necessity language.
  • Regulatory and geopolitical: the Middle East conflict had a direct measurable impact in FY2026 Q3. This makes Coty more vulnerable to external shocks than the essentials-heavy names.
  • Labor: labor is not central to the filings; strategy, leadership transition, portfolio review, and execution are the main management themes.
  • Technology: technology matters mostly through e-commerce, digital marketing, and social-led brand building rather than through productivity or science-based routine efficacy alone.
