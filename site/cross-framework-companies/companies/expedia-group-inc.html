Annual Report Takeaways

  • Expedia is the right next Services name because it adds the second major travel-intermediary and marketplace layer beside Booking. Booking is the cleaner global merchant-and-direct distribution surface. Expedia adds a more explicit three-part stack: flagship consumer brands, the largest B2B travel business, and a premier advertising network.
  • AnnualReports is again only partially useful. As of `2026-08-09`, it still showed the `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K` and left sector and industry blank. That makes Expedia another clean example of why the archive cannot rely on the aggregator for current-year placement or taxonomy.
  • The `2025` annual filing frames Expedia as `the global travel marketplace` and makes the platform breadth explicit. At year-end `2025`, the company had about `3.6M` lodging properties available, including about `2.4M` online-bookable alternative accommodations through Vrbo and about `1.2M` hotels and alternative accommodations through its other brands.
  • The annual materials also show why Expedia matters to the broader consumer-interface project. The company kept rolling out `One Key` as the unified loyalty layer across Expedia, Hotels.com, and Vrbo, allowing cross-earn and cross-redeem behavior across hotels, alternative accommodations, flights, packages, cars, cruises, and activities.
  • Fiscal `2025` results show a scaled but still improving marketplace rather than a thin referral business. Full-year revenue reached about `$14.733B`, gross bookings about `$119.590B`, operating income about `$1.871B`, net income attributable to Expedia Group about `$1.294B`, and adjusted EBITDA about `$3.501B`.
  • The business mix is analytically useful. In `2025`, B2C gross bookings were about `$83.867B`, B2B gross bookings about `$35.723B`, B2C revenue about `$9.474B`, B2B revenue about `$4.842B`, Expedia Group advertising revenue about `$758M`, and trivago third-party revenue about `$417M`.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$4,315M`, gross bookings were `$33,928M`, operating income was `$800M`, net income attributable to Expedia Group was `$878M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$1,119M`.
  • Demand and growth: booked room nights rose to `111.5M`, total gross bookings grew `12%`, B2B gross bookings grew `21%`, B2C gross bookings grew `8%`, and revenue grew `14%`.
  • Mix and guidance signals: Q2 `2026` B2B revenue rose `23%`, and management raised full-year `2026` guidance to `$129.5B-$130.8B` of gross bookings and `$16.05B-$16.22B` of revenue, with adjusted EBITDA margin expansion of `+1.5` to `+1.75` points.
  • Capital allocation and structural read: Expedia repurchased about `880k` shares for `$200M` in the quarter and maintained the `$0.48` dividend, which reinforces that this is a high-cash-generation marketplace model rather than a capital-hungry asset owner.
  • Structural themes: Q2 is the clearest proof that Expedia belongs in the archive as a thick travel-relationship platform where B2B scale, consumer-brand performance, and advertising-network economics all expand together.
  • Results: Q1 `2026` revenue was `$3,426M`, gross bookings were `$35,530M`, operating income was `$251M`, net loss attributable to Expedia Group was `($6M)`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$542M`.

Signal Map

  • Travel marketplace versus travel operator: Expedia is a travel business where the main economic surface is workflow ownership rather than asset ownership. That makes it one of the strongest contrasts in the repo against airlines, hotels, cruise lines, and gaming operators.
  • Loyalty and wallet logic: `One Key` matters because it turns three consumer brands into a single cross-earn and cross-redeem surface. That is a much thicker relationship layer than a normal OTA rewards program.
  • B2B infrastructure: Expedia is one of the cleanest examples in the repo of a consumer-facing company whose moat may increasingly sit in a partner-distribution layer that ordinary end users barely notice.
  • Advertising network economics: the explicit `EG Advertising` and trivago advertising lines show that travel-intent businesses can turn attention and first-party data into a media business, not only into bookings.
  • Cultural read: Expedia is monetizing more than travel purchases. It is monetizing the desire for easier planning, smoother repeat travel, cross-brand confidence, and increasingly personalized trip management.
  • Taxonomy lesson: AnnualReports currently leaves Expedia unclassified and stale on the annual package, which is a useful reminder that the archive needs a defensible local taxonomy rather than a passive aggregator mirror.
