Annual-Report Starting Point

Ferguson adds the branch-dense plumbing HVAC waterworks and contractor-infrastructure layer to Industrial Goods because the annual and quarter materials show a channel business monetizing local availability technical support acquisitions and recurring repair-and-replacement demand even while residential conditions stay pressured, making it a strong bridge between Builders FirstSource WESCO and other contractor-facing distribution models.

Analysis Stack

Business Model Read

Ferguson is not a consumer brand in the ordinary sense, but it still sits under consumer behavior. When housing affordability is poor, households delay discretionary remodeling and some replacement work. That softness shows up in Ferguson’s residential RMI commentary before it shows up in more obvious retail channels.

Annual Report Takeaways

  • The annual filing chain shows a large, asset-light but operationally dense channel business. The fiscal-`2025` `10-K` said Ferguson served a `$340B` North American construction market, worked with about `37,000` suppliers, operated `1,746` branches as of `2025-07-31`, and employed about `35,000` associates. The transition-period `10-KT` updated that to `1,729` branches, about `6,200` fleet vehicles, and the same approximate associate count as of `2025-12-31`.
  • The basic business model matters because it explains why this name belongs in an infrastructure batch. Ferguson says residential and non-residential each account for roughly half of net sales, while roughly two-thirds of net sales tie to repair, maintenance and improvement rather than new construction. That means a large share of demand is recurring and failure-driven rather than purely cyclical new-build demand.
  • The transition-period and calendar-`2025` chain also shows how much this company depends on market fragmentation. Ferguson explicitly frames itself as bridging a fragmented supplier base and an even more fragmented customer base. That is the core moat: local inventory, product breadth, service, and workflow simplification in markets where many contractors still operate close to home and need rapid fulfillment.
  • The most important annual signal is mix quality. The `2026-02-24` calendar-`2025` results release reported sales of `$31.3B`, gross margin of `31.0%`, adjusted operating margin of `9.6%`, operating cash flow of `$2.2B`, eight acquisitions with annualized revenue above `$300M`, and net debt to adjusted EBITDA of `1.1x`. Management emphasized double-digit non-residential growth and continued market-share gains despite a weak residential backdrop.
  • The category mix inside the `10-KT` is also useful for comparison work. As of the transition-period disclosure, Waterworks was `24%` of U.S. sales, Ferguson Home was `21%`, Commercial/Mechanical `15%`, Residential Trade Plumbing `15%`, HVAC `11%`, Industrial `7%`, Facilities Supply `4%`, and Fire & Fabrication `3%`. That is a wide contractor-economy cross section rather than a single-product vertical.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Ferguson reported results on Monday, August 10, 2026. Sales increased `4.6%` to `$8.751B`, reported operating profit increased to `$893M`, adjusted operating profit reached `$932M`, operating margin improved to `10.2%`, adjusted operating margin was `10.7%`, and diluted EPS increased `6.9%` to `$3.43`.
  • The most important change was not just the headline growth rate. Management said residential growth returned even though the residential market backdrop remained challenging, while non-residential revenue increased `8%` and large capital project activity stayed healthy. That is a strong signal that Ferguson was still taking share rather than just floating on macro demand.
  • Guidance also improved. On August 10, 2026 management moved `2026` net-sales guidance from low-to-mid-single-digit growth to mid-single-digit growth and raised the low end of adjusted operating-margin guidance from `9.4%` to `9.5%`, while keeping the top end at `9.8%`. Capital-expenditure guidance also increased to `$375M-$425M`.
  • The operating message is that pricing is normalizing but not collapsing. Price inflation was in the low single digits and gross margin slipped `20` basis points because the prior-year quarter had been temporarily elevated by supplier-price timing. Even with that, operating leverage stayed positive because service density and cost discipline held.
  • The strategic layer sharpened again with M&A. Ferguson completed five acquisitions in the quarter and signed the deal for FloWorks, a distributor of highly technical valves and flow-control solutions. That pushes the company further into industrial-flow and higher-complexity channels rather than only ordinary plumbing and HVAC distribution.
  • On `2026-05-05`, Ferguson reported sales of `$7.472B`, gross margin of `31.0%`, reported operating profit of `$612M`, adjusted operating profit of `$647M`, operating margin of `8.2%`, adjusted operating margin of `8.7%`, and diluted EPS of `$2.13`.

Signal Map

  • Ferguson is not a consumer brand in the ordinary sense, but it still sits under consumer behavior. When housing affordability is poor, households delay discretionary remodeling and some replacement work. That softness shows up in Ferguson’s residential RMI commentary before it shows up in more obvious retail channels.
  • The company also sits inside a broader cultural move toward resilience and reliability. Water systems, HVAC systems, and commercial mechanical systems are not aspirational purchases. They are failure-response and uptime purchases. That makes Ferguson a good read on how cost-of-living pressure and infrastructure neglect shift spending from optional upgrades toward essential fixes.
  • There is also a labor-and-complexity pattern here. Specialized contractors increasingly need product availability, technical support, and simplified procurement because jobs are more fragmented and labor remains constrained. Ferguson’s value proposition is built around reducing job friction rather than merely shipping boxes.
  • Residential weakness remains a real drag. Across the quarter chain, Ferguson repeatedly ties softness to weak new residential activity, weak permits, and soft RMI. The `Q2 2026` improvement did not remove that pressure; it only showed that Ferguson could outperform it.
  • Supplier pricing still matters, but the quality of margin matters more. Gross margin around `31%` was supported by disciplined execution, while some quarter-to-quarter movement came from the timing and extent of supplier price increases. That means simple margin comparisons can overstate or understate underlying operating strength.
  • Large capital projects and non-residential bidding are now central to the growth story. Waterworks and commercial/mechanical exposure repeatedly drove the stronger side of the business, which makes Ferguson more tied to infrastructure modernization and industrial project flow than a surface read of "plumbing distributor" would suggest.
