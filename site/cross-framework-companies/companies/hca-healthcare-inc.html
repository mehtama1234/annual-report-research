Annual Report Takeaways

  • HCA is the first large provider and hospital-system operator in the healthcare archive, which materially improves the sector sample.
  • Fiscal `2025` ended with revenue of `$75.600B`, net income attributable to HCA Healthcare of `$6.784B`, diluted EPS of `$28.33`, and Adjusted EBITDA of `$15.566B`.
  • The annual filing shows the scale of the platform at December 31, 2025: `190` hospitals, `121` freestanding ambulatory surgery centers, and `31` freestanding endoscopy centers across `19` states and England.
  • The annual report message is explicit that growth is being funded and defended through hospital-capacity expansion, new outpatient facilities, artificial intelligence, and a new electronic medical record platform.
  • This is a useful healthcare addition because it shifts the analysis from payer and product economics toward provider throughput, service mix, reimbursement architecture, payer mix, and capital intensity.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: revenue of `$20.230B`, net income of `$1.699B`, diluted EPS of `$7.62`, adjusted diluted EPS of `$7.59`, and Adjusted EBITDA of `$4.027B`.
  • Volume signal: same-facility admissions rose `2.5%`, and same-facility equivalent admissions rose `2.7%`.
  • Strategic read: the quarter shows provider earnings being pulled in opposite directions by policy and mix. HCA estimated about `$400M` of pretax pressure from exchange-related coverage loss and about `$400M` of incremental Medicaid Supplemental Payment Program benefit, primarily from Florida.
  • Results: revenue of `$19.109B`, net income of `$1.620B`, diluted EPS of `$7.15`, and Adjusted EBITDA of `$3.802B`.
  • Volume signal: same-facility admissions rose `0.9%`, and same-facility equivalent admissions rose `1.3%`.
  • Structural read: seasonal provider demand proved less stable than a simple defensive-sector read would imply. Respiratory-related admissions were down `42%`, respiratory-related ER visits were down `32%`, and a winter storm also affected volumes.

Signal Map

  • Sector: HCA gives healthcare a provider and hospital-system operating model that neither the payer nor pharma names capture.
  • Demand: the business shows that provider demand is real but economically filtered through payer mix, service mix, respiratory severity, surgical volume, and directed-payment timing.
  • Competition: scale, network density, local-market position, and capital availability matter directly in hospitals because capacity, staffing, and outpatient adjacency are operating weapons.
  • Culture and business model: HCA ties healthcare delivery to infrastructure, workflow, and trust. AI, the electronic medical record platform, and outpatient expansion are framed as practical operating tools rather than abstract innovation language.
  • Watch item: exchange coverage loss, Medicaid supplemental approvals, and surgical-mix softness can all move earnings quickly even when admissions look healthy.
