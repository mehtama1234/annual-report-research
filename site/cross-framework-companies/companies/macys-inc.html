Annual Report Takeaways

  • Macy's is a useful archive addition because it gives the consumer-facing work a weaker-middle department-store operator that is trying to repair the business through portfolio mix, digital tools, media, marketplace, and luxury and beauty adjacencies rather than through a simple store-recovery story.
  • AnnualReports.com was only partially useful here. On Monday, August 10, 2026, it still showed `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, even though Macy's official investor-relations stack already exposed the `2025` annual report.
  • The `2025` annual and year-end materials show that the company is not just one department-store banner. The economic story now runs through Macy's, Bloomingdale's, Bluemercury, credit-card revenues, Macy's Media Network, and Macy's Marketplace inside the broader `Bold New Chapter` repositioning effort.
  • Full-year `2025` net sales reached `$21.8B`, comparable sales returned to positive growth at `1.5%`, other revenue reached `$857M`, credit card revenues net reached `$669M`, Macy's Media Network revenue net reached `$188M`, and the company ended the year with `$1.2B` of cash and cash equivalents.
  • The strategically important point is that Macy's is trying to turn a pressured middle-format retailer into a wider multi-banner and media-enabled relationship system. Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury are growing faster than the Macy's nameplate, while marketplace, media, and digital tools are being used to thicken the customer relationship.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: Q1 `2026` comparable sales increased `3.0%`; go-forward comparable sales increased `3.1%`; net sales were `$4.7B`, up `1.8%`; total revenue was `$4.892B`, up `2.1%`; GAAP diluted EPS was `$0.23`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.13`; and adjusted EBITDA was `$290M`, or `5.9%` of total revenue.
  • Operating posture: Bloomingdale's comparable sales increased `10.2%`, Bluemercury comparable sales increased `6.4%`, Macy's nameplate comparable sales increased `1.6%`, credit card revenue was `$172M`, other revenue was `$210M`, and Macy's Media Network revenue was `$38M`.
  • Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows the repair effort working through a portfolio rather than a single format. Macy's core comps improved, but the better read is that luxury, beauty, financial-adjacent revenue, and newer interface layers are carrying more of the quality.
  • Results: Q4 `2025` net sales were `$7.6B`; comparable sales increased `1.8%`; go-forward comparable sales increased `2.0%`; Bloomingdale's comparable sales rose `9.9%`; Bluemercury comparable sales grew `1.3%`; GAAP diluted EPS was `$1.84`; adjusted diluted EPS was `$1.67`; and adjusted EBITDA was `$840M`, or `10.6%` of total revenue.
  • Operating posture: full-year `2025` net sales were `$21.8B`; comparable sales increased `1.5%`; other revenue reached `$857M`; credit card revenues net reached `$669M`; Macy's Media Network revenue net reached `$188M`; and the company returned `$448M` to shareholders.
  • Structural themes: this is the anchor quarter for the packet because it closes the year with the clearest proof that Macy's is no longer only defending the old core. The portfolio is being widened through luxury, beauty, media, marketplace, and better disclosure around go-forward comparable sales.

Signal Map

  • Sector: Macy's widens `Consumer Goods` with a direct department-store and portfolio-repair case instead of another staple, beverage, off-price, or beauty-pure-play operator.
  • Consumer: this is a useful read on the pressured but still functioning middle of discretionary retail. The customer is not disappearing, but the stronger spend is showing up disproportionately in luxury, beauty, and better-run go-forward stores.
  • Cultural: the company matters because the Macy's nameplate alone is weaker than the broader portfolio. Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury suggest consumers still respond to premium contemporary, luxury beauty, and more curated shopping environments even while the classic department-store middle remains under pressure.
  • Channel and interface: Macy's is a useful bridge case because the relationship now includes marketplace, media, AI-assisted shopping through `Ask Macy's`, digital and store integration, and a meaningful credit-card revenue layer.
  • Business model: the key signal is that a legacy department-store operator can widen beyond merchandise margin alone, but the extra layers work best when they sit on top of improving category and portfolio execution rather than on top of a still-deteriorating core.
  • Competitive positioning: Macy's is most useful next to `Kohl's`, `Ulta`, `Bath & Body Works`, `Ross`, and `Burlington`. It is weaker than the strongest value and beauty systems, but it is much more structurally interesting than a plain department-store decline call because luxury, beauty, media, marketplace, and financial-adjacent revenues are all visible at once.
