Annual Report Takeaways

  • Marriott is the right next Services name because it turns the travel-status lane into something broader than one airline plus one hotel operator. Delta gives the archive a loyalty-currency system, Hilton gives it an asset-light lodging compounder, and Marriott adds the largest branded stay network in the set.
  • AnnualReports still lagged here as of `2026-08-10`, showing only the `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, while Marriott IR already exposed the `2025` annual report. That makes Marriott another direct example of why the archive cannot rely on the aggregator alone.
  • The `2025` annual materials frame Marriott as a scale distribution and fee engine built on franchising, management, licensing, and Marriott Bonvoy. The system ended `2025` with `9,805` properties and `1,779,936` rooms across `145` countries and territories, while the development pipeline reached approximately `4,100` properties and nearly `610,000` rooms.
  • Marriott Bonvoy is central to the business strategy, not a side feature. In `2025`, about `75%` of U.S. hotel room nights and `68%` of global hotel room nights were booked by loyalty members, and co-branded credit cards in `11` countries remained a significant funding source for the loyalty program.
  • The full-year `2025` earnings release said diluted EPS was `$9.51`, net income was `$2,601M`, adjusted net income was `$2,742M`, adjusted EBITDA was `$5,383M`, and worldwide RevPAR increased `2.0%` for the full year.
  • Marriott also matters because it combines repeat travel behavior with owner demand. Gross room additions were nearly `100,000` in `2025`, net rooms grew more than `4.3%`, and the company returned more than `$4.0B` to shareholders in the year.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: Q2 `2026` diluted EPS was `$2.90`, net income was `$766M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$1.592B`.
  • Demand and growth: worldwide RevPAR increased `3.4%`, U.S. and Canada RevPAR increased `5.0%`, system size reached `10,082` properties and `1,813,698` rooms, and the pipeline reached approximately `629,000` rooms.
  • Management message: Marriott framed the quarter around broad U.S. demand strength, continued room growth, and new multi-year U.S. co-branded credit card agreements with `JPMorgan Chase` and `American Express`.
  • Structural themes: this quarter reinforces Marriott as a brand-system, distribution, and loyalty platform. The key signals are RevPAR, room additions, co-brand economics, pipeline depth, and the ability to keep signing owners even while the Middle East conflict pressured international results.
  • Results: Q1 `2026` diluted EPS was `$2.43`, net income was `$648M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$1.398B`.
  • Demand and growth: worldwide RevPAR increased `4.2%`, system size reached `9,926` properties and `1,795,808` rooms, nearly `15,900` net rooms were added, and the pipeline reached nearly `618,000` rooms.

Signal Map

  • Sector: Marriott deepens Services into global lodging, meetings, travel, and repeat-stay behavior rather than only transport, retail, or business services.
  • Consumer: this is a direct read on status-oriented and branded travel demand, with a stronger explicit loyalty concentration than many other travel names disclose.
  • Cultural: Marriott adds a broad branded-hospitality and experience layer where trust, familiarity, program status, and destination behavior all matter.
  • Payments and wallet logic: Marriott is not as pure a wallet case as Delta or Caesars, but the co-branded card economics and loyalty-program funding make the relationship much closer to a payments-linked consumer system than a simple hotel brand set.
  • Capital light versus capital heavy: Marriott is analytically useful because it shows how a global hotel platform can scale through fees, owner signings, and loyalty without owning most of the real estate.
  • International: Marriott is one of the best global travel-demand reads in the repo, but Q2 `2026` also shows how geopolitical disruption can break the international picture even while U.S. demand stays strong.
