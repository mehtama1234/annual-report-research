Business Model Read

Annual Report Takeaways

  • McKesson’s `2025` annual report shows a company still anchored in scale pharmaceutical distribution but increasingly oriented toward higher-value healthcare services.
  • Fiscal `2025` revenue reached `$359.1 billion`, up `16%`, and diluted EPS reached `$25.72`.
  • The segment mix clarifies the economic architecture:
  • U.S. Pharmaceutical remained the dominant engine at `$327.7 billion` of revenue.
  • Prescription Technology Solutions reached `$5.2 billion`.
  • Medical-Surgical Solutions reached `$11.4 billion`.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: Q1 FY2027 revenue was `$105.4 billion`, GAAP EPS was `$5.15`, and adjusted EPS was `$9.93`, up `20%`.
  • Segment signal: North American Pharmaceutical revenue reached `$86.8 billion`, Oncology & Multispecialty reached `$14.2 billion`, Prescription Technology Solutions reached `$1.6 billion`, and Medical-Surgical Solutions reached `$2.8 billion`.
  • Management message: leadership highlighted strong core distribution performance, continued growth in oncology and multispecialty, and solid execution in biopharma services.
  • Structural meaning: McKesson is no longer only a bulk distributor story. Specialty, oncology, and provider-facing solutions are increasingly central to earnings quality and strategic narrative.
  • Results: Q4 FY2026 revenue was `$96.3 billion`, adjusted EPS was `$11.69`, and full-year FY2026 revenue was `$403.4 billion` with full-year adjusted EPS of `$39.11`.
  • Operating signal: full-year cash flow from operations reached `$6.2 billion`, and free cash flow reached `$5.4 billion`.

Signal Map

  • Healthcare dependence: pharmacies, providers, oncology practices, and health systems rely on intermediaries like McKesson to keep treatment and dispensing systems functioning.
  • Institutional replenishment: this is one of the clearest institutional resupply businesses in the archive. Demand is recurring, operationally necessary, and tied to regulated care delivery.
  • Cultural: treatment is moving toward specialty, multispecialty, and alternate sites of care, which raises the value of distributors and service platforms that can manage complexity.
  • Technology: prescription technology and access solutions show that software-like workflow infrastructure is increasingly fused with physical distribution.
  • Portfolio strategy: McKesson’s actions around Medical-Surgical Solutions and the Apollo transaction show the company trying to separate lower-fit or differently-valued assets while sharpening its healthcare-platform focus.
  • Capital allocation: large buybacks, dividend growth, and transaction discipline sit alongside growth investment, which is typical of strong middle-layer companies with durable cash generation.
