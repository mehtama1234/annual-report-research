Annual Report Takeaways

  • Northrop adds the missing defense-and-deterrence layer to the industrial set. It broadens the archive away from machinery, automation, electrical infrastructure, and HVAC into military aerospace, strategic systems, and national-security backlog.
  • The 2025 annual report is explicit that the company sees a more complex global security environment, rising allied spending, and sustained demand for advanced systems. That makes Northrop a clean read on geopolitical and budget-supported industrial demand.
  • Full-year 2025 sales were `$42.0 billion`, diluted EPS was `$29.08`, operating cash flow was `$4.8 billion`, free cash flow was `$3.3 billion`, and backlog ended the year at a record `$95.7 billion`.
  • Northrop is also useful for theme work because it ties industrial demand to deterrence platforms and capacity scaling, not only to commercial capex. The annual materials center the B-21 Raider, Sentinel, and other next-generation programs as anchors of future growth.
  • The annual report also highlights about `$2.5 billion` of R&D and capital investments in 2025, which reinforces that this is an investment-heavy production-scaling story rather than a mature defense-cash-harvest story.
