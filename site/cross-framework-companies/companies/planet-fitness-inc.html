Annual Report Takeaways

  • Planet Fitness is one of the cleaner blind-spot fits in the current archive because the AnnualReports page effectively misclassifies the operating system. On `2026-08-10`, the saved company page still showed `Consumer Goods` and `--None--`, even though the actual business is a recurring-fee, membership-conditioned, low-friction wellness service system.
  • AnnualReports also lagged on current-year coverage. The saved page still pointed to `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, so the usable current annual anchor had to come from the fiscal `2025` SEC filing chain and official-site verification rather than from the aggregator page itself.
  • The fiscal `2025` filing and Q4 release show a scaled but unusual access business. Full-year revenue increased `12.1%` to about `$1.3B`, net income attributable to Planet Fitness, Inc. reached `$219.1M`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$551.6M`, and system-wide same club sales increased `6.7%`.
  • The `10-K` makes the model legible in a way the taxonomy does not. Planet Fitness ended `2025` with about `20.8M` members and `2,896` clubs, of which `2,604` were franchisee-owned and `292` were corporate-owned. The quarter releases simplify that same point by stating that about `90%` of clubs are owned and operated by independent business owners.
  • The relationship layer is also clearer than the source labels imply. The company is not just selling fitness access. It is selling affordable, recurring self-maintenance inside a branded `Judgement Free Zone`, with the standard Classic Card for new members starting at `$15` per month and the PF Black Card for new members at `$24.99` per month.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: `Q2 2026` total revenue increased `7.1%` to `$365.2M`, net income attributable to Planet Fitness, Inc. was `$67.1M`, adjusted net income was `$68.4M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$152.8M`.
  • Membership and network posture: system-wide same club sales increased `1.7%`, system-wide sales increased `$66.6M` to `$1.4B`, `23` new clubs opened in the quarter, and total clubs reached `2,930` as of `2026-06-30`.
  • Capital and outlook posture: the company repurchased about `4.0M` shares for `$200.0M`, ended the quarter with `$544.4M` of cash and marketable securities, and reiterated a `2026` outlook of about `1%` same-club-sales growth, about `7%` revenue growth, about `6%` adjusted EBITDA growth, `150` to `160` new equipment placements, and `180` to `190` system-wide new club openings.
  • Structural themes: this quarter matters because it shows the company operating as a membership-repair and retention story, not only as a unit-growth story. Management explicitly said it was working to reignite sustainable member growth, refreshing marketing, and testing pricing, member experience, and retention.
  • Results: `Q1 2026` total revenue increased `21.9%` to `$337.2M`, net income attributable to Planet Fitness, Inc. was `$51.6M`, adjusted net income was `$59.4M`, and adjusted EBITDA was `$139.9M`.
  • Membership and network posture: system-wide same club sales increased `3.5%`, system-wide sales increased `$88.0M` to `$1.4B`, total membership reached about `21.5M`, and total clubs reached `2,909` as of `2026-03-31`.

Signal Map

  • Sector: Planet Fitness should strengthen Services, but the more useful analytical frame is not just recreation. It is fee-based access, routine-use behavior, and low-friction wellness participation.
  • Consumer: the demand system is built on affordable self-maintenance, low-intimidation access, recurring habit, and a branded environment that lowers social friction for mainstream exercise participation.
  • Cultural: this is one of the cleaner proofs in the archive that body-confidence and wellness demand do not always look premium, elite, or overtly athletic. They can also scale through friendliness, affordability, and non-intimidation.
  • Societal: the packet reflects a broader normalization of paying a modest recurring fee to preserve mobility, appearance, energy, and self-respect without joining a high-commitment or prestige-coded fitness culture.
  • Operating model: the important move is not just membership count. It is the combination of franchise-heavy expansion, recurring dues, annual fees, equipment placements, national advertising contributions, and retention-sensitive pricing architecture.
  • Competitive positioning: Planet Fitness is most useful next to `Costco`, `Target`, `Starbucks`, `lululemon`, `Birkenstock`, `Crocs`, and `Victoria's Secret`, not just next to conventional leisure or recreation names.
