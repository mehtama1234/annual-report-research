Annual Report Takeaways

  • Primoris is the right next contractor addition because it keeps the industrial lane anchored in field execution, but adds a different mix: utilities, gas operations, communications, renewables, natural-gas generation, and heavy civil work.
  • The official `2025` annual report shows a very strong base year. Revenue reached `$7.575 billion`, net income reached `$274.9 million`, diluted EPS reached `$5.02`, adjusted EBITDA reached `$531.1 million`, and year-end backlog reached `$11.9 billion`.
  • The annual letter makes Primoris a direct read on the same power-demand and AI-linked buildout seen elsewhere in the archive. Management explicitly tied demand to replacing aging infrastructure, hardening the grid, expanding power delivery, building natural-gas generation, scaling solar and battery storage, and communications builds tied to data-center development.
  • The annual package also shows a real labor and execution story. Primoris said it increased its labor force by more than `2,800` people in `2025`, highlighted tighter markets for certified journeyman linemen, and emphasized the advantage of self-performing the vast majority of its work.
  • Primoris is not a generic heavy-construction company. The annual materials divide the business between `Utilities` and `Energy`, with Utilities spanning power delivery, gas operations, and communications, and Energy spanning renewables, industrial construction, heavy civil, maintenance, and pipeline services.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: Q2 `2026`, for the quarter ended `2026-06-30`, delivered revenue of `$1.688 billion`, net loss of `$24.2 million`, diluted loss per share of `$0.45`, adjusted net loss of `$14.6 million`, and adjusted EBITDA of `$11.4 million`.
  • Demand and backlog: total backlog reached a record `$13.9 billion`, including `$8.2 billion` of master service agreement backlog, and management highlighted roughly `$1.4 billion` of natural-gas power-generation awards during the quarter.
  • Management message: the company said challenged renewables projects were still hurting earnings, but also said it had delivered record bookings and the highest total backlog in company history.
  • Structural themes: Primoris is now a useful counterexample inside the industrial set. Demand and bookings stayed strong, but project redesign, sequencing, labor productivity, sub-surface conditions, and weather were enough to flip the quarter into an operating loss.
  • Results: Q1 `2026`, for the quarter ended `2026-03-31`, delivered revenue of `$1.560 billion`, net income of `$17.4 million`, diluted EPS of `$0.32`, adjusted net income of `$32.2 million`, and adjusted EBITDA of `$60.5 million`.
  • Demand and backlog: Utilities improved year over year, but Energy margins weakened sharply, and management updated full-year `2026` guidance to reflect solar-project revenue and margin pressure plus the PayneCrest acquisition.
