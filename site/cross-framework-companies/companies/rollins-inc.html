Annual-Report Starting Point

Business Model Read

Sector: Rollins broadens the Services sector beyond transport and logistics into residential and commercial field services.

Annual Report Takeaways

  • Rollins gives the Services sector a third company and, more importantly, a different service model from CHRW and UPS. This is not freight or parcel logistics. It is a recurring residential and commercial field-service network built around pest and termite control.
  • The `2025` annual package showed revenue of `$3.8 billion`, operating income of `$726 million`, diluted EPS of `$1.09`, and operating cash flow of `$678 million`.
  • The annual and Q4 chain matter because they show a service business with a large recurring base, steady acquisition support, and route-density economics rather than shipment-volume exposure. Management said recurring business plus ancillary services represented more than `80%` of revenue and grew more than `7%` organically in both Q4 and full-year `2025`.
  • Rollins also matters for theme work because it adds a consumer- and housing-adjacent service operator where local demand generation, pricing, staffing, weather, and route efficiency are the key earnings levers.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: Q2 2026 revenue was `$1.1 billion`, diluted EPS was `$0.30`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.32`, operating income was `$201 million`, and net income was `$144 million`.
  • Guidance and tone: on `July 22, 2026`, management did not present a formal numeric guidance reset in the earnings release, but it explicitly said growth had slowed in parts of the residential business that rely more on digital leads and inbound calls.
  • Management message: management said relationship-based channels such as home builders and door-to-door sales remained stronger, while consumer-initiated demand softened through the quarter before improving again toward the end of June and into July.
  • Structural themes: Q2 is the most revealing quarter because it shows that even a high-recurring services compounder is still exposed to consumer lead generation, channel mix, and cost alignment. This is not recession-proof demand; it is resilient demand with visible pockets of softness.
  • Results: Q1 2026 revenue was `$906 million`, diluted EPS was `$0.22`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$0.24`, operating income was `$145 million`, and net income was `$108 million`.
  • Guidance and tone: on `April 22, 2026`, management emphasized a strong peak-season setup and said the company exited March with about `12%` total growth and more than `8%` organic growth.

Signal Map

  • Sector: Rollins broadens the Services sector beyond transport and logistics into residential and commercial field services.
  • Consumer: the company gives a direct read on household and small-business maintenance demand, digital lead flow, home-builder channels, and the resiliency of recurring home-service spending.
  • Cultural: the cultural layer is execution discipline at the branch and route level rather than brand storytelling. Channel mix, local accountability, and customer retention matter more than product launches.
  • Technology: the technology layer is modernization, pricing tools, demand-generation systems, and local operating visibility, not headline AI. The company is using systems to improve execution rather than to define the product.
  • Labor: staffing, route density, sales productivity, and local service execution are central earnings variables.
  • Capital allocation: Rollins is acquisitive but disciplined, pairing steady operating cash flow with recurring dividends and modest leverage.
