Annual Report Takeaways

  • Stryker adds the orthopaedics, robotics, capital-products, and hospital-procedure layer that the healthcare archive still lacked even after adding Abbott and Boston Scientific.
  • Fiscal `2025` ended with net sales of `$25.116B`, reported EPS of `$8.40`, and adjusted EPS of `$13.63`.
  • The annual and quarterly trail show an operator still positioned to grow at the high end of medtech, but with a more obvious dependence on procedural volumes, hospital capital budgets, and product execution than the broader healthcare packets.
  • This matters because Stryker sits in a different earnings lane from the payer, provider, pharma, diagnostics, and cardiovascular-specialist packets already in the archive.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: net sales of `$6.6B`, reported EPS of `$3.30`, and adjusted EPS of `$3.69`.
  • Segment signal: MedSurg and Neurotechnology grew `9.7%` reported and Orthopaedics grew `9.1%` reported.
  • Strategic read: this is the rebound quarter. Management framed the result as meaningful recovery from the cyber incident, with regained momentum, broad procedure demand, and strong execution across both major segments.
  • Results: net sales of `$6.0B`, reported EPS of `$1.93`, and adjusted EPS of `$2.60`.
  • Segment signal: MedSurg and Neurotechnology grew `5.0%` reported while Orthopaedics grew only `0.1%` reported; organic growth still held at `4.1%` in Orthopaedics.
  • Structural read: this is the disruption quarter. The cyber incident materially affected operations, and the quarter also introduced the Ortho Tech recast that combined Mako, enabling technologies, and orthopaedic instruments into a more explicit surgical-platform grouping.

Signal Map

  • Sector: Stryker adds the missing orthopaedics, robotics, and capital-equipment medtech lane to Healthcare.
  • Demand: demand here depends on procedure volumes, replacement cycles, hospital capital willingness, surgeon adoption, and product-platform execution rather than payer pricing or drug launches.
  • Competition: this is not just a diversified devices story. Competitive position is tied to procedure-room presence, installed workflow relevance, robotics and enabling technology, and category execution in orthopaedics and MedSurg.
  • Culture and business model: Stryker reflects a healthcare model where hospitals keep buying into tools, implants, and enabling platforms that are embedded in procedures and surgeon workflow.
  • Watch item: the company is strong, but the trail shows a real operational vulnerability to disruption and a meaningful dependence on keeping procedure and capital momentum intact.
