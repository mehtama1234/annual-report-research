Annual Report Takeaways

  • Take-Two is a strong blind-spot fit because `Technology / Application Software` is a weak description of the operating reality. The annual and trailing-quarter materials show a franchise-driven interactive-entertainment system built on sequel cadence, live services, recurrent consumer spending, mobile scale, and release-calendar anticipation rather than on software-seat or generic app-license economics.
  • The source mismatch is useful on its own. As of `2026-08-10`, AnnualReports.com still only showed a `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, while the local official IR and SEC chain already supported the current annual anchor and the in-scope `FY2027 Q1`, `FY2026 Q4`, and `FY2026 Q3` set.
  • Because Take-Two's fiscal year ends on March `31`, the right annual anchor for this August `2026` review window is the fiscal `2026` annual package even though the archive's wider scope year remains `2025`.
  • Full-year FY`2026` net bookings reached `$6.721B`, above guidance and about `$750M` above the initial May `2025` outlook. Full-year FY`2026` GAAP net revenue reached `$6.656B`, and recurrent consumer spending grew `17%`.
  • The May `2026` investor presentation makes the platform structure clearer than the label does: FY`2026` net bookings were `49%` mobile, `40%` console, and `11%` PC and other, while the company framed the market around `3.6B` global players, nearly `149B` annual mobile downloads, and major owned-IP systems led by `Grand Theft Auto`, `NBA 2K`, `Red Dead Redemption`, and `Zynga` titles.
  • This is analytically different from both plain software and toy-only comparisons. `Hasbro` and `Mattel` show physical-IP and brand-extension systems. `Roblox` shows creator-built digital worlds. Take-Two adds the scaled premium-publisher and live-services layer where fandom, recurrent spending, content cadence, and launch anticipation all compound.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: FY`2027 Q1` net bookings were `$1.39B`, slightly above guidance, while GAAP net revenue was `$1.53B`.
  • Mix and monetization: recurrent consumer spending accounted for `84%` of both net bookings and GAAP net revenue.
  • Profitability: GAAP net loss was `$34.1M`, including a `$43.4M` impairment charge tied to an unannounced third-party title that the company chose not to continue developing.
  • Management message: management reiterated fiscal `2027` net bookings guidance of `$8.0B` to `$8.2B` and tied the year directly to the scheduled `2026-11-19` launch of `Grand Theft Auto VI`.
  • Structural themes: this quarter is useful because even with year-over-year net-bookings pressure, the business still reads as a recurrent-participation system rather than a one-shot hit business. The dependence on live services, sports franchises, mobile titles, and major launch timing is explicit.
  • Results: FY`2026 Q4` net bookings were `$1.58B`, above guidance, and GAAP net revenue was `$1.68B`.

Signal Map

  • Sector: Take-Two strengthens Technology, but the better read is as a franchise-IP and recurrent-participation system that source taxonomy flattens into `Application Software`.
  • Consumer: the demand is not just software usage. It is repeat participation in sports franchises, open-world entertainment, live services, mobile games, and culturally loaded release events.
  • Cultural: the company is filing-backed proof that franchise identity and game-world attachment remain major spending systems, especially when supported by sequel cadence, multiplayer persistence, and large release moments.
  • Societal: Take-Two reflects the normalization of gaming as one of the largest mass-entertainment categories across console, PC, and mobile, with engagement tied to global fandom, digital add-on spending, and long-duration title lifecycles.
  • Business model: the important move is not selling a disc or one-time download. It is combining premium launches with recurrent consumer spending, live operations, mobile scale, licensing, and franchise anticipation.
  • Competitive positioning: Take-Two is most useful next to `Hasbro`, `Mattel`, `Roblox`, `Electronic Arts`, `Netflix`, and `Disney`, not next to generic enterprise-software issuers.
