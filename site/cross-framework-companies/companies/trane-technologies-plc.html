Annual Report Takeaways

  • Trane adds a missing layer to the industrial set: HVAC, building controls, energy services, and climate infrastructure. It broadens the archive beyond heavy equipment, automation software and controls, and electrical hardware.
  • The 2025 annual report is explicit that demand for sustainable and resilient infrastructure is accelerating because of the data economy, rising energy use, and the need for reliable systems. That gives the industrial work a cleaner bridge from AI rhetoric to cooling, controls, and facility systems.
  • Full-year 2025 reported revenues were `$21.3 billion`, organic revenue growth was `6%`, adjusted continuing EPS grew `16%` to `$13.06`, adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to `20.1%`, and free-cash-flow conversion was `98%`.
  • The annual report and 10-K also make Trane useful at the theme level because they explicitly call out data-center facility controls, data-center HVAC systems, data-center liquid cooling, and data-center services.
  • This is also a high-quality backlog story: full-year 2025 bookings were `$22.6 billion`, book-to-bill was `106%`, and year-end backlog reached about `$7.8 billion`, up `15%` versus 2024.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: Q2 2026 reported revenues were `$6.354 billion`, adjusted continuing EPS was `$4.31`, organic bookings were up `37%`, and backlog reached a record `$12.1 billion`.
  • Guidance: on July 30, 2026 Trane raised full-year 2026 guidance to about `11.5%` reported revenue growth, `9%` organic revenue growth, GAAP continuing EPS of about `$15.00-$15.10`, and adjusted continuing EPS of `$15.20-$15.30`.
  • Management message: management highlighted exceptional bookings strength, especially in Americas Commercial HVAC, where bookings were up `50%`, and said momentum was carrying into the second half of 2026.
  • Structural themes: this quarter makes Trane look like a cooling-and-controls infrastructure platform riding the same AI and power capex wave that shows up elsewhere in electrical equipment and power systems.
  • Results: Q1 2026 reported revenues were `$4.969 billion`, adjusted continuing EPS was `$2.63`, organic bookings were up `24%`, and backlog reached a record `$10.7 billion`.
  • Guidance: on April 30, 2026 Trane raised full-year 2026 guidance to about `9.5%` reported revenue growth, `7%` organic revenue growth, and EPS of about `$14.75-$14.95`.

Signal Map

  • Sector: Trane shows that industrial strength is also running through building systems, cooling, controls, and service-heavy retrofit demand, not only through machinery, automation, and electrical gear.
  • Consumer: consumer demand is secondary here. The meaningful demand drivers are commercial buildings, data centers, institutional projects, residential replacement, cold-chain logistics, and infrastructure efficiency spending.
  • Cultural: Trane turns sustainability language into equipment, controls, services, and thermal infrastructure. It is one of the cleaner examples of climate and efficiency rhetoric translating into concrete industrial demand.
  • Regulatory: energy-efficiency rules, refrigerant transitions, building codes, and public-sector decarbonization incentives matter much more here than they do for some other industrial names.
  • Labor: technician availability and installation capacity matter because backlog conversion depends on project execution, service capability, and field labor, not just factory output.
  • Technology: the relevant technology layer is building controls, digital energy management, data-center HVAC, liquid cooling, and connected service systems.
