Annual Report Takeaways

  • Union Pacific adds the rail-and-supply-chain layer to the industrial set. It broadens the archive beyond industrial equipment and systems suppliers into actual freight flows, network utilization, and logistics efficiency.
  • The 2025 annual filing shows a railroad that improved safety, service, and operating metrics while still managing volume, mix, and fuel-surcharge noise. That makes Union Pacific a good counterweight to backlog-heavy industrial names.
  • Full-year 2025 operating revenue was `$24.5 billion`, net income was `$7.1 billion`, diluted EPS was `$11.98`, and the reported operating ratio improved to `59.8%`.
  • The annual filing is especially useful because it highlights the combination of core pricing gains, `1%` volume growth, and operating-efficiency gains, while also showing where lower fuel surcharge revenue and mix pressure can dilute the headline story.
  • Union Pacific also matters for theme work because it turns industrial demand into freight intensity. Domestic intermodal, grain, coal, industrial chemicals and plastics, and automotive all provide a broad read on physical economic activity.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: Q2 2026 net income was `$2.0 billion`, diluted EPS was `$3.36`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$3.41`, and operating revenue reached a record `$6.9 billion`.
  • Guidance: on July 23, 2026 Union Pacific improved the 2026 EPS-growth outlook to high-single digit while affirming pricing above inflation, operating-ratio improvement, strong cash generation, and the `$3.3 billion` capital plan.
  • Management message: management emphasized strong execution, volume growth, record freight revenue, and readiness to meet rising customer demand with strong service.
  • Structural themes: this quarter makes Union Pacific look like a pricing-and-productivity logistics franchise with upside from better volumes, but still exposed to fuel and business-mix effects.
  • Results: Q1 2026 net income was `$1.7 billion`, diluted EPS was `$2.87`, adjusted diluted EPS was `$2.93`, and operating revenue reached a record `$6.2 billion`.

Signal Map

  • Sector: Union Pacific shows that industrial strength is also expressed through freight networks, modal efficiency, intermodal flows, and price/mix discipline, not only through selling equipment into capex cycles.
  • Consumer: consumer demand matters indirectly through intermodal and merchandise flows, but the more useful lens is supply-chain throughput across agriculture, industrial chemicals, automotive, energy-related freight, and imports.
  • Cultural: the cultural layer is operational excellence and national supply-chain efficiency rather than technology spectacle. This is a system-performance story.
  • Regulatory: rail regulation, safety standards, labor dynamics, merger review, and Surface Transportation Board oversight matter directly.
  • Labor: labor matters because railroad productivity, service reliability, and terminal performance depend on workforce efficiency and crew availability.
  • Technology: the relevant technology layer is network optimization, fuel efficiency, locomotive modernization, and operating-system discipline rather than software or hardware product cycles.
