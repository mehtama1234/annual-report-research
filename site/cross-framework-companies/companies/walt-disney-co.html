Annual Report Takeaways

  • Disney is the right next Services name because it expands the archive from retail and pure subscription operators into a consumer-cultural system built on franchise IP, streaming, sports, parks, and merchandise demand.
  • The annual-report sourcing gap is itself useful. As of `2026-08-09`, AnnualReports.com still showed Disney's `2024 Annual Report and Form 10K`, while Disney's own investor-relations stack already exposed the `2025` annual report and `Q3 2026` quarterly materials. That makes Disney a clear example of why the archive cannot rely on the aggregator alone.
  • The `2025` annual and year-end materials show a large multi-engine company rather than a narrow media asset. Full-year `2025` revenue was `$94.4B`, income before taxes was `$12.0B`, total segment operating income was `$17.6B`, diluted EPS was `$6.85`, and adjusted EPS was `$5.93`.
  • The segment mix matters. At year-end `2025`, Entertainment full-year segment operating income rose to `$4.7B`, Sports stayed a major advertising and distribution engine, and Experiences delivered record full-year segment operating income of `$10.0B`.
  • Disney is especially useful to the archive because the same fan relationship shows up across digital subscriptions, theatrical releases, sports viewing, park attendance, and consumer-product demand. That makes it stronger as a consumer-system case than as a simple streaming or legacy-media case.

Latest Quarter Chain

  • Results: Q3 `2026` revenue was `$25.2B`, income before taxes was `$3.6B`, and total segment operating income was `$5.6B`.
  • Operating detail: Entertainment revenue reached `$11.3B`, Sports revenue reached `$4.5B`, and Experiences revenue reached `$10.0B`. Entertainment segment operating income increased to about `$1.68B`, while Experiences segment operating income increased to about `$3.02B`.
  • Management message: management framed the quarter around deep fan connections, strong experiences demand, ESPN viewership gains, and continued confidence in the full-year setup. The company also raised its fiscal `2026` share repurchase target to at least `$9B`.
  • Structural themes: this quarter reinforces Disney as a system that monetizes consumer attention across both digital and physical channels. The useful read is not only quarter growth, but the way IP, streaming, sports audiences, parks demand, and capital returns all move together.
  • Results: Q2 `2026` revenue was `$25.2B`, income before taxes was `$3.4B`, and total segment operating income was `$4.6B`.
  • Segment detail: Entertainment revenue was `$11.7B`, Sports revenue was `$4.6B`, and Experiences revenue was `$9.5B`, all up year over year.

Signal Map

  • Sector: Disney broadens Services beyond logistics, restaurants, and live events into IP-driven entertainment infrastructure with recurring digital, advertising, and destination economics.
  • Consumer: Disney is one of the archive's strongest examples of consumers repeatedly paying to stay inside an ecosystem rather than making isolated one-off purchases.
  • Cultural: the cultural signal is unusually strong. Disney monetizes attachment to franchises, characters, stories, sports brands, and family rituals across multiple channels.
  • Loyalty and membership: Disney is directly relevant to the archive's loyalty work because management is explicitly trying to evolve Disney+ into a broader membership ecosystem rather than leaving it as a standalone streaming product.
  • Physical plus digital: Disney is a high-value case for how digital engagement and physical presence can reinforce one another. Parks, streaming, sports, and theatrical releases all feed the same relationship system.
  • Advertising and audience reach: Disney is also useful because subscription economics are only part of the picture. Advertising demand and audience scale, especially around ESPN and broader streaming inventory, remain central.
