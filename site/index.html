Start Standard

New work should start from packet evidence, not from theme labels. The expected chain is source coverage, annual and quarter facts, company packet interpretation, lane comparison, repeated pattern, plain-English conclusion, watch metric, and thesis breaker.

A strong writeup should let a skeptical reader answer four questions without guessing: what is the exact fact, what does it mean, why does it matter beyond one company, and what would change the conclusion.

  • Use annual takeaways plus the latest three quarters to show what changed and whether the direction is strengthening or weakening.
  • Use the plain-English operating model and strategy read to explain what job the company really performs and how management is responding.
  • Use the growth engine, economic lever, and operating constraint to show what is really carrying the story and where the system is strained.
  • Use exact supporting facts plus burden-versus-beneficiary interpretation to show who gets paid and who absorbs the messy work.
  • Use the thesis breaker and next-filing watchlist to show what would weaken the read and what needs to be watched next.

Claim Standard

  • Consumer claims should show what behavior changed and which company facts prove it.
  • Cultural or societal claims should show which real-life pressure is creating demand and which companies are reacting to it from different positions.
  • Industrial or operating claims should show where the strain sits and what happens economically when that pressure changes.
  • Technical or infrastructure claims should show where software is a control layer and where physical bottlenecks still decide outcomes.
  • Capital or balance-sheet claims should show who must carry property, inventory, debt, or financing burden to keep the system running.
  • Cross-company pattern claims should use exact facts from at least three companies and say what evidence would weaken the broader pattern.
