The Algorithm as Buyer: AI Agents Taking Over Commerce Discovery

The barrier between search and purchase is collapsing as AI agents become the primary interface between consumers and products—bypassing storefronts entirely.

Instead of people browsing product pages or marketplaces, AI agents will soon discover, compare, and execute purchases on behalf of users. This shifts the economic game from attracting eyeballs to ownership of merchant relationships, payment infrastructure, and agent-routing logic. Companies that embed themselves into the agent workflow before the shift completes will own the new layer; everyone else becomes a commodity supplier.

The mechanism

For two decades, the consumer internet has been built around discovery-to-purchase funnels: search engines, app stores, marketplace listings, product pages, shopping carts, checkout. That entire funnel assumed a human making conscious decisions at each step. AI agents collapse this funnel because they can perform every step—discovery, comparison, negotiation, payment, logistics—without human involvement.

The old model rewarded companies that owned the gateway: Google Search, Amazon, Instagram, TikTok. Traffic acquisition cost, engagement metrics, and conversion rate were the game. The new model will reward companies that own the agent layer: merchant routing, payment orchestration, trust signals, and preferred-vendor logic. This is why Shopify invested in Catalog and Sidekick (merchant-facing AI), why Etsy built agent-checkout integrations with Google, Microsoft, and Stripe, and why American Express is talking about embedding itself into agentic-commerce workflows.

The immediate pressure is on merchants. Shopify's $378.4B in GMV and 65.6% Shopify Payments penetration means merchants are already accustomed to letting Shopify see transaction data and user behavior. But if an AI agent from Google or Microsoft or ChatGPT becomes the discovery and purchase interface, merchants now depend on being picked by the agent. That shifts bargaining power sharply. Merchants will need tools (Shopify's Catalog, Etsy's seller rankings, AmEx's merchant-data partnerships) to make themselves visible and preferrable to agents. The payment layer becomes even more critical because agents will choose payment providers based on friction, trust, and data integration.

For payment and financial-services companies, the payoff is existential. American Express explicitly stated that management discusses AI-powered agents discovering products, making decisions, and completing payments, and AmEx is trying to be embedded in that future flow. If agents choose how to pay, closed-loop networks like AmEx (which own both card-holder and merchant data) have a structural advantage over open-network payment processors. Airbnb's 45% resolution rate for AI-assisted issues suggests the interface will be conversational and real-time—meaning payment needs to flow seamlessly inside the agent interaction, not after the user has left the conversation.

Who pays · who profits

Who pays
Merchants and smaller platforms pay first, through higher platform fees and reduced leverage with agents. If you sell on Etsy and your products aren't surfaced by the Google or ChatGPT agent, you disappear. Shopify merchants will need to invest in Catalog and data quality to get picked by agents. Traditional e-commerce players without agent integration—particularly mid-market brands without the infrastructure to integrate with multiple agent platforms—face margin pressure and visibility collapse. Payment providers without agent integration lose transaction routing logic to those that have it.
Who profits
The companies that control agent routing, merchant data orchestration, and payment embedding will capture disproportionate value. Shopify's Catalog and Sidekick aren't just features; they are bids for merchant defensibility when agents take over discovery. American Express's closed-loop network becomes more valuable because agents will prefer payment paths that don't require friction (leaving the conversation, entering card data, confirming an address). Etsy benefits because its peer-to-peer model and seller-trust data become inputs into agent matching—human-made goods and seller reputation are exactly what agents need to differentiate in a commodified product set. Microsoft, Google, and OpenAI profit as the agent infrastructure layers, but the real economic rents accrue to whoever sits between the agent and the merchant.

The tension

The winning scenario for consumers (frictionless agent-driven discovery and purchase) is the losing scenario for independent merchants and smaller platforms. If Shopify merchants only get discovered through Google or ChatGPT agents, Shopify loses control of the funnel. Etsy sellers face the same risk. Payment networks fragment: agents will route to the payment provider that integrates cleanest into their workflow, creating winner-take-most dynamics in payments, not the open-network competition we have today. Regulatory scrutiny will also increase because agent-driven commerce creates new points of control and potential bias. If Microsoft's agent prioritizes Microsoft or partner sellers, that is a new form of search manipulation.

The surprising part

The most underestimated dynamic is that merchant data quality and platform infrastructure become more valuable than raw traffic volume. Shopify's GMV and Shopify Payments penetration matter not because the company owns the storefront, but because the company now owns the data layer that agents need to make routing decisions. Etsy's strength isn't its design or cultural positioning—it is its seller-trust data and the fact that agents need authentic, human-made goods to compete with commodity Amazon listings. American Express doesn't benefit from more card-member spend; it benefits from being embedded in the agent's decision logic at the moment of payment. This is a quiet inversion: the platform with the richest merchant or customer data, not the platform with the flashiest interface, will own the agent layer.

Why it matters

This affects how much choice and diversity survive in commerce. If agents prioritize merchants that integrate with Shopify's Catalog or Stripe's payments infrastructure, then only merchants with resources and technical sophistication get discovered. Small sellers, artisans, and niche producers disappear from agent visibility. Etsy's 5.6M sellers and Airbnb's 5M hosts—most of whom are small operators—face margin collapse if they cannot compete in an agent-routed world. At the same time, if agents route correctly, consumers get faster access to exactly what they want without wasteful browsing and decision fatigue. The tension between accessibility (keeping small sellers viable) and efficiency (agents finding the best match) will define whether agent-driven commerce concentrates wealth or democratizes it.

When AI agents replace human shopping, the businesses that sold products to people will become back-office suppliers to platforms that sell visibility and payment rails to merchants.
