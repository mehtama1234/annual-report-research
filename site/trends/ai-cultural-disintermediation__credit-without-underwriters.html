Credit Without Underwriters: The Loan Officer Becomes a Parameter

Upstart grew loan originations 86% in 2025 while headcount rose only 18%, and now runs 90%+ of loans with no human in the loop. This is not a faster loan officer. It is the deletion of the loan officer as a job category.

Consumer credit is the cleanest live case of AI erasing a human expert class rather than assisting it. Upstart fully automates more than 90% of its loans across 100+ bank and credit-union partners and grew originations 86% in 2025 against 18% headcount growth, a labor-productivity gap no human underwriting shop can match. Affirm goes one step further with its AdaptAI and BoostAI systems, which do not just approve or decline but design the offer itself, merchant by merchant and consumer by consumer, so that even the human product-design and rate-setting function disappears. The value that used to be distributed across loan officers, credit analysts, and risk committees now accrues to whoever owns the model parameters.

The mechanism

The loan officer's job was to read a borrower — income stability, debt patterns, intent — and price the risk. Upstart has compressed that judgment into a model and runs it with more than 90% of loans fully automated across 100+ bank and credit-union partners. The proof that this is replacement rather than assistance is in the labor ratio: Upstart grew originations 86% in 2025 while headcount grew only 18%. A human underwriting operation cannot decouple volume from staffing like that, because each additional application requires additional human attention. The model does not, so the same 1,497,149 loans and $11.0B of 2025 originations run without a proportional back office.

The model is not just cheaper; it is getting sharper as it scales. Upstart's conversion rate rose to 19.4% in fiscal 2025 from 15.1% in 2024 — meaning more of the same applicant traffic converts to funded loans as the model touches more data. Auto and home originations each grew about 5x in 2025, and 70% of Q4 2025 auto-and-home funding came from 11 different partners with another 13 signed. The human function being deleted here is not a single loan officer but an entire distributed apparatus of regional credit analysts and committees, replaced by one software surface that banks plug into rather than staff.

Affirm shows the deeper cut: the AI no longer just approves or declines, it designs the financial product. AdaptAI and BoostAI decide, at the checkout moment and for each merchant, whether to present 0% APR, Pay-in-X, or interest-bearing monthly installments, and on what terms. In Q2 2026 over 65% of approved U.S. loan applications were presented with a 0% APR offer, and the number of merchants funding those offers more than tripled to over 40,000 by Q1 2026. Affirm's own 'Big Nothing' 0% APR event lifted participating-merchant GMV about 5% overall and about 27% when promoted on Affirm surfaces — evidence that the model's offer design is what moves demand, not a human financing team's annual plan. The rate-setter, the product designer, and the promotions strategist are all now parameters.

Why now

The inflection is fiscal 2025 into 2026, when both platforms crossed from pilot into production infrastructure with the economics to prove it. Upstart returned to GAAP profitability in Q2 2026 with $4.2B of quarterly originations, 90%+ automation, and an all-time-high contribution profit — while applying for a national bank charter, a sign it intends to own more of the stack rather than remain a wrapper. Affirm posted net income of $100M in Q3 2026 on $1.039B of revenue with adjusted operating income at 27% of revenue, its tenth consecutive quarter of 30%+ GMV growth, and said its average annualized cost of funds hit the lowest level in three and a half years. The specific shift that dates this trend to now is the move past approval automation: Q1 2026 was the quarter Affirm reported that merchants funding 0% APR offers more than tripled to over 40,000, meaning the AI had begun designing the product at scale, not just deciding yes or no on a product a human designed.

Who pays · who profits

Who pays
The human intermediary class first — loan officers, credit analysts, underwriting managers, and risk committees whose work is now a model. Upstart's 86%-vs-18% growth-to-headcount gap is a direct measure of jobs not created. Partner banks and credit unions pay by ceding the origination relationship and its margin, becoming funding pipes rather than credit decision-makers. Borrowers who fall outside the model's training distribution pay through silent exclusion: a decline from Upstart or Affirm has no loan officer to appeal to and no human discretion as a backstop. And consumers broadly pay in data — the model's edge depends on capturing behavioral and transaction signals at the moment of checkout, which is why Affirm frames richer transaction data as the thing that deepens merchant dependence on its surface.
Who profits
Upstart and Affirm, and the AI layer that now sits between consumers and institutional credit. Upstart booked $53.6M of net income in fiscal 2025 and returned to GAAP profitability in Q2 2026 with an all-time-high contribution profit while funding growth without new equity. Affirm earned $100M of net income in Q3 2026 at 27% adjusted operating margin. Both capture origination economics that used to be distributed across bank loan officers and committees. The secondary winners are the merchant platforms that embed these systems and get frictionless financing without building underwriting or funding infrastructure themselves.

The tension

Algorithmic credit is only 'better' when the training data is representative and the model is calibrated. A human loan officer can exercise discretion on an edge case; the model approves or declines on parameters it cannot easily explain, and any bias in the training data gets systematized at scale rather than committed one loan at a time. The structural fragility is funding: neither Upstart nor Affirm owns the balance sheet the way Synchrony or American Express do — they orchestrate partner and capital-markets funding. Affirm touted its lowest cost of funds in three and a half years, which is a tailwind that can reverse; in a credit contraction, when partners tighten, both face immediate pressure and there is no human-judgment fallback to keep credit flowing to millions of borrowers. Accountability also thins: a loan officer can be held responsible for discrimination; a model can claim it is 'just following the parameters.'

The surprising part

The restructuring is being driven by cost reduction in origination labor, not by smarter credit assessment. The single most telling figure is not an approval-accuracy metric — it is that Upstart grew originations 86% while growing headcount only 18%. The AI's advantage that actually reshapes the market is that it is cheaper and faster per loan, not that it is wiser. Affirm's ability to deploy thousands of distinct 0% APR offers across 40,000+ merchants in real time is something no human team could do, but the value capture flows to Affirm through higher volume and lower per-loan operating cost, not primarily to the consumer or the merchant. The credit world is being rewired by deleting a labor cost structure, which is a less headline-friendly story than 'AI reimagined lending' but a more accurate one.

Why it matters

Credit is shifting from a relationship mediated by a human with discretion, accountability, and an appeal process into a computational output tuned for the platform's revenue and risk. If Upstart or Affirm declines you, there is no loan officer to appeal to; your access is a function of model parameters optimized for the platform, not your circumstances. For workers, this is the disappearance of an entire white-collar profession — credit analysis and underwriting — with the 86%-vs-18% growth gap showing how few new jobs replace it. For the financial system, credit decisioning is consolidating into a handful of model-owning platforms that do not own their funding; if those platforms hit a capital crunch, credit access for millions can contract faster than a diversified bank system would allow, with no human backstop.

AI underwriting has turned the loan officer from a person who reads a borrower into a parameter inside a model, and the companies that own the parameters — Upstart and Affirm — now capture the margin and the relationship that used to belong to thousands of bank credit officers.
