Who Owns the Algorithm Owns the Relationship: Where the Value Goes

When AI absorbs a professional's judgment, the money does not stay with the merchant, the bank, or the host — it migrates to whoever owns the model that made the decision.

AI disintermediation is not about cheaper service; it is about where economic power comes to rest. The company that builds and controls the model layer absorbs the margin, the data, and the recurring contact that a human loan officer, agent, or advisor used to own. Upstart's $11B annual originations across 100+ banks prove this: the company runs credit decisions that banks used to own. Affirm's 515K merchants means the checkout conversion layer belongs to Affirm, not to the bank or merchant. Airbnb's 45% of issues resolved by AI means customer relationships once mediated by agents are now mediated by the platform. Shopify's statement that 'AI is lowering business-creation friction while increasing platform dependence' is the entire thesis in one line.

The mechanism

Upstart is the clearest case where AI is not a feature but the entire product. It connects millions of consumers to 100+ banks and credit unions; more than 90% of loans are fully automated with approval, pricing, conversion, and fraud screening all running through one model layer. The origination fee that once compensated a loan officer now flows to Upstart. FY2025 originations reached $11.0B, and the company grew originations 86% and revenue 64% while adding only 18% headcount. The detachment of growth from labor is the signal that economics no longer live with the human intermediary.

Affirm demonstrates the same migration inside checkout. The company explicitly states its moat 'increasingly sits in data-driven approval and offer optimization rather than commodity loan origination.' AdaptAI and BoostAI select which financing offer to show and design merchant programs across 515K active merchants (up 44% in Q3 2026). The underwriting margin that used to sit at the bank now sits at the platform, with 26.8M active consumers transacting 6.7 times per year (Q3 2026) locked into the Affirm surface.

Airbnb shows the pattern on the cost side. Nearly 45% of Q2 2026 support issues that begin with the AI assistant resolve without a human agent, and support cost per booking fell 16% year-over-year. That labor savings on a $12.2B annual revenue business compresses into $4.6B of free cash flow. The customer-service relationship formerly owned by a call-center worker is now owned by Airbnb's model, and the dollars that would have paid that worker are retained as platform margin.

Shopify and Stagwell reveal the structural logic. Shopify explicitly states 2025 was about 'laying the rails for a new era of AI commerce' where products are 'discovered, compared, recommended, and potentially purchased through AI systems and agentic workflows rather than only through traditional browser sessions.' Its own language that 'AI is lowering business-creation friction while increasing platform dependence' proves the thesis: as AI makes merchants' jobs easier, it makes merchants more dependent on the layer that owns the AI. Stagwell's Marketing Cloud grew net revenue 230% year-over-year by racing to become the AI layer for marketing before it is disintermediated itself.

The unifying mechanism: a human intermediary's value was bundled—trust, judgment, data, customer contact all lived in one person and could not be separated. AI unbundles and re-bundles at the model layer. Whoever owns the model owns the approval decision, the transaction data that improves the next decision, and the customer relationship that produces the next transaction. The merchant keeps the activity; the platform keeps the compounding asset.

Why now

The inflection is Q2 2026 combined with FY2025 filings: Upstart returned to GAAP profitability (Q2 2026) and posted record $193M contribution profit at 62% margin—the model layer is now demonstrably self-funding at scale. Affirm reached net income of $100M and adjusted operating income of 27% of revenue (Q3 2026) while its cost of funds hit a 3.5-year low, proving the AI-driven underwriting layer is economically durable. Airbnb quantified AI support resolution at 45% for the first time (Q2 2026) alongside a 16% cost-per-booking decline. These are not forward forecasts or aspirational claims. They are reported margins showing up in current cash flows across three different industries, all tied to AI-powered disintermediation.

Who pays · who profits

Who pays
The professionals whose judgment the models absorbed: bank loan officers (100+ institutions outsource their entire underwriting to Upstart), call-center agents (45% of tickets at Airbnb never reach human hands), merchants (who gain convenience but face 'increasing platform dependence' per Shopify's own disclosure), and consumers (whose trust in opaque models replaces trust in interrogable people).
Who profits
The platform that owns the model. Upstart captures origination fees across a network it does not staff. Affirm captures underwriting margin at 515K merchants. Airbnb retains the support-labor savings as free cash flow atop $91.3B gross booking value (FY2025). Shopify pulls Payments penetration to 68% of $378.4B GMV as merchants deepen dependence. In each case the recurring economic relationship and the data that compounds it concentrate at the layer that controls the algorithm.

The tension

The value concentrated at the model layer is extracted from the network the platform depends on. Upstart's 100+ banks are trained to trust outsourced credit logic; the day a regulator or competitor builds an equivalent model, the spread evaporates. Affirm's 515K merchants are one competing checkout button away from switching. Airbnb's AI does not have proprietary claim on the support problem it solved. The platform's power is real but contingent on participants finding rebuilding the model harder than paying the toll.

The surprising part

The most valuable capture is not the fee—it is the data exhaust that makes the next decision better, and no individual intermediary could ever accumulate. Upstart's conversion rate climbing from 15.1% to 19.4% between Q4 2024 and Q4 2025 is the model teaching itself across every partner's loans simultaneously. A single bank's loan officer only ever sees one bank's applicants. The AI layer wins not because it is smarter per decision but because it sees every decision, and that vantage point is structurally impossible for the human it replaced.

Why it matters

The judgments that shape your life—whether you get the loan, what price you are shown, how your complaint is resolved—are migrating from people you could argue with to models owned by companies whose incentive is to keep the relationship, not to explain it. When the decision layer consolidates at a handful of platforms, the terms of your access to credit, commerce, and service are set by an algorithm you cannot see, owned by a company you do not deal with directly.

AI does not just do the intermediary's job—it moves the intermediary's paycheck, data, and customer relationship up one layer to whoever owns the model, and keeps them there.
