CBRE Group Inc.

Critical infrastructure services (data center, power, telecom, cooling) jumped from 3% of core EBITDA in 2021 to 14% in 2025, proving brokerage and facilities-management intermediary benefits directly from AI-linked infrastructure demand

Standout figure
Full-year 2025 revenue of $40.6B, Q2 2026 revenue of $11.2B, core EPS guidance raised from $7.30-$7.60 (Feb 2026) to $7.80-$7.90 (July 2026), 2025 free cash flow of $1.7B, critical infrastructure now 14% of core EBITDA (up from 3% in 2021)
Watch next
Whether critical infrastructure growth continues to accelerate as data-center demand and AI infrastructure buildout intensify, and whether CBRE can maintain service-intermediation margins as competition from REITs and direct-operator capabilities increases.

Why It Is Distinctive

CBRE is not a landlord or developer; it is a services-and-intermediation platform sitting between tenants, landlords, lenders, investors, and infrastructure operators. The rapid rise of critical infrastructure (data centers, power, cooling, telecom) from 3% to 14% of EBITDA shows real estate is being reshaped by non-traditional demand. CBRE monetizes brokerage, facilities management, project execution, and transaction services rather than owning assets. This is the clearest proof that real-estate cash flows increasingly flow through service intermediaries.
