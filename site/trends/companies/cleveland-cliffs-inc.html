Basic Materials · Steel & Iron

Cleveland-Cliffs Inc.

Vertically integrated from iron ore to automotive flat-rolled steel, CLF struggled through weak automotive demand and contract lag—Q2 2026 shows recovery is underway but contract friction remains a structural drag.

Standout figure
Adjusted EBITDA swung from -$21M loss in Q4 2025 to $286M in Q2 2026 (13x improvement), yet full-year 2025 adjusted EPS still fell to -$2.48; contract structure delays margin realization.
Watch next
Automotive production in North America through 2026 Q3-Q4 and whether new multi-year contracts achieve better pricing than legacy slab arrangements. Working capital and liquidity ($3.1B at Q2 2026) are adequate but not abundant.

Why It Is Distinctive

CLF is not a simple commodity proxy. Fixed annual contract pricing on ~35-40% of shipments creates lag and protection at once. The real earnings driver is automotive OEM production schedules, contract realization timing, and whether multi-year automotive contracts can restore margin. Integration from ore→pellets→DRI→steelmaking→finishing also creates higher fixed costs and slower margin swings.

Evidence Trail

The Mini-Mill That Doesn't Need Iron Ore

  • 2025 adjusted EBITDA of $37 million on $18.6 billion revenue (0.2% conversion), demonstrating how integrated ore-dependent model struggles to convert gross margin into earnings when automotive demand is weak
  • Q2 2026 GAAP net loss of $134 million despite sequential EBITDA recovery of $191 million, showing that even operational improvement cannot overcome fixed-cost structure of integrated mills

What Breaks: Complexity Failure and Integration Implosion

  • Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $95M included an approximately $80M one-time energy cost from extreme cold; full-year 2025 adjusted EBITDA was just $37M on $18.6B revenue with a $1.4B GAAP net loss. Adjusted EBITDA swung $191M from Q1 ($95M) to Q2 2026 ($286M), showing how a vertically committed cost base amplifies operating friction.

The Integration Tax: Workers, Communities, and Captive Customers

  • Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA included approximately $80 million one-time energy-cost impact from extreme cold weather, showing exposure of fixed-asset base to operational disruptions
