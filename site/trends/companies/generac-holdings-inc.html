Industrial Goods · Electrical Equipment & Supplies

Generac Holdings Inc.

Data-center and hyperscale backup-power demand is transitioning the company from residential standby generator maker into mission-critical enterprise resilience infrastructure provider.

Standout figure
Q2 2026 C&I external net sales up 29% to $556M; data-center backlog expanded to ~$1.6B excluding additional committed volumes; management signed global supply agreement with leading hyperscale operator and secured second hyperscale agreement.
Watch next
Whether capacity expansion can keep pace with hyperscale demand, whether residential demand stabilizes as outage environment normalizes, and whether large-megawatt systems profitability sustains.

Why It Is Distinctive

Generac is shifting from selling one-time resilience machines to households toward becoming embedded infrastructure for data-center operators, telecom networks, and hyperscalers. The installed-base is becoming more software-attached through Mobile Link monitoring and grid-services participation. Tariff-refund benefits in Q2 2026 (~$71M) show margin optics need care, but underlying momentum is real.

Beyond the Generator: Cummins and the Power-Systems Platform

  • C&I (Commercial & Industrial) external net sales rose 29% in Q2 2026 and 28% in Q1 2026, far outpacing residential growth; data-center momentum described as accelerating across both hyperscale agreements and backlog expansion
  • Expanded data-center backlog to approximately $1.6B excluding additional committed volumes by Q2 2026; signed first global supply agreement with leading hyperscale operator in Q2 2026, followed by second hyperscale agreement on June 24, 2026
  • Q2 2026 C&I net sales $556M with adjusted EBITDA margin of 24.8% for the quarter, reflecting premium pricing on large-megawatt backup-power systems versus residential equipment
  • Management stated goal to double C&I product sales in the years ahead and is dramatically increasing capacity for large-megawatt generators; integrating Allmand and Enercon acquisitions, expanding Belvidere facility

Generac's Bifurcation: When the Same Company Sells to Homeowners and Hyperscalers

  • Q2 2026 net sales rose 11% to $1.17 billion; residential external net sales fell 2% to $617 million while C&I external net sales rose 29% to $556 million
  • Company signed global supply agreement with leading hyperscale operator and secured second hyperscale agreement on 2026-06-24; data-center backlog expanded to approximately $1.6 billion excluding additional committed volumes
  • Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $291 million or 24.8% of net sales; $71 million pretax tariff refund materially boosted gross margin and EBITDA
  • Full-year 2025 net sales fell 2% to $4.21 billion; residential product sales fell 7% to $2.27 billion; C&I product sales rose 5% to $1.46 billion
  • Company is dramatically increasing capacity for large megawatt generators and expects data-center opportunities and related investments to help double C&I product sales in years ahead

When the Lights Became a Business Risk

  • 2025 full-year net sales $4.21B, C&I product sales $1.46B (up 5%), residential sales down 7% to $2.27B
  • Q2 2026 net sales $1.17B (up 11%), C&I sales $556M (up 29%), data-center backlog ~$1.6B excluding additional committed volumes
  • Q1 2026 was final stages of vendor approval with multiple hyperscale customers with expanded backlog; guided to mid-to-high teens sales growth
