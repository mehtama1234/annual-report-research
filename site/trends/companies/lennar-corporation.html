Lennar Corporation

Land-light model with asset ratio <5% and 12.9% incentive spending in Q2 2026 maintained 20.5k unit deliveries despite elevated mortgage rates, proving affordability engineering via standardization and cycle speed matters more than headline volume

Standout figure
Q2 2026 revenue of $7.9B, net earnings of $305M (diluted EPS $1.24), new orders down 4% YoY to 21,749 homes, deliveries up 2% to 20,519 homes, backlog of 16,818 homes valued at $6.6B, ASP of ~$391k, incentives at 12.9%
Watch next
Whether the land-light model and incentive-driven margin compression can sustain long-term returns, and whether the pending Millrose spin (luxury homes) materially impacts the core builder's scale and execution velocity.

Why It Is Distinctive

Lennar is not waiting for mortgage rates to fall; it is engineering affordability through inventory turns, land-light capital structure, standardized product, and dynamic pricing. The company keeps describing production efficiency (122-day cycle time, 2.5x inventory turn) and operating margin architecture rather than headline demand strength. This is an operating-model story, not a volume story. Even with new orders down, deliveries stayed strong through faster throughput and lower prices.
