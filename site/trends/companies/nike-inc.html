Retail · Apparel, Shoe & Accessory Stores

NIKE, Inc.

Fiscal Q4 2026 revenue down 1% reported (-4% currency-neutral) despite $0.52/share tariff-recovery benefit; direct channel down 7–9% while wholesale up 4%—DTC ownership is strategic, not automatic margin win.

Standout figure
FY2026 revenues $46.4B with gross margin 42.9%; Q4 2026 NIKE Direct $4.1B (down 7% reported), wholesale $6.6B (up 4%); tariff-recovery benefit ~900 basis points in Q4, inflating reported margins.
Watch next
NIKE Direct momentum reversal and whether profitability gains from tariff recovery can persist. Inventory and portfolio cleanup are ongoing; if wholesale demand softens, the channel-balance thesis would test the growth path.

Why It Is Distinctive

NKE is not a DTC-first victory narrative. The company owns ~38% of revenue through direct channels ($17.7B FY2026), which is strategically important, but wholesale reaccelerated while NIKE Direct contracted. The brand carries real cultural power, but that has to translate into traffic, conversion, and repeat purchase. Tariff policy, wholesale channel health, and regional demand balance all matter as much as direct-channel strategy.

Evidence Trail

NIKE Loses the Middle While HOKA Owns the Top

  • NIKE Direct revenues declined 7% in Q4 FY2026 reported (down 9% currency-neutral), despite $46.4B in annual revenues
  • Wholesale revenues up only 4% in Q4, down from 8% in Q2 and 5% in Q3, signaling recovery concentrated in value channels
  • Gross margin at 42.9% for FY2026, compressed despite wholesale recovering, indicating margin pressure from wholesale mix shift to value
  • The company emphasized in filings that wholesale reacceleration is happening 'primarily in value channels rather than full-price partners'
