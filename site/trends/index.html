Bottom-up trend analysis

What 519 companies are actually telling us

Trends surfaced bottom-up from 519 company annual reports and the latest earnings — open-coded, clustered, and written up in depth. 20 distinct dynamics, 168 deep-dive pages, every figure traced to a filing.

societal · demographic · 9 pages

The Gray Wave Dividend

Medicare is paying for the shift from nursing facilities to living rooms — and the operators who own the pharmacy, the aide, and the equipment deliver are capturing non-discretionary recurring revenue on 465,000 patients a day that will not shrink in a recession.

economic · technological · 9 pages

The AI Infrastructure Toll

Hyperscalers are paying premium rates to a small tier of specialty contractors, quarry operators, and component makers who control inputs that cannot be imported, automated, or stockpiled — and who can grow only as fast as their licensed labor does.

societal · cultural · 9 pages

The Outage Anxiety Economy

The grid hasn't gotten dramatically worse — but the cost of losing power for an hour has tripled, and the companies that sell backup electricity have figured out how to charge for the fear gap between those two facts.

economic · societal · 8 pages

The Cash Harvest

In energy, healthcare, consumer staples, and financial services, companies with captive customer bases are raising prices on people who cannot easily leave, then routing every dollar of excess cash to shareholders instead of workers, communities, or long-cycle assets. The discipline looks like financial virtue. The distribution of its costs is not.

industrial · economic · 8 pages

The Spread Machine

The most durable profit engines in the 2026 economy are not innovators but intermediaries — companies that sit between producers and consumers at chokepoints so expensive to replicate that pricing power has nothing to do with service quality and everything to do with the cost of building around them.

industrial · economic · 9 pages

The Vertical Integration Ratchet

Industrial platforms bought their supply chains between 2022 and 2024 when debt was cheap and commodity exposure was untenable. Now the leverage is fixed and the moats are structural—but so are the failure modes, and the companies that look strongest are not the biggest integrators but the ones with input advantages their competitors cannot buy.

societal · cultural · 8 pages

The Trust Market

When AI dropped the production cost of false content to near zero, institutional credibility became the asset class nobody expected—and the companies that spent decades accumulating it are now raising prices for access.

societal · cultural · 9 pages

The Addiction Dividend

The most durable moat in modern capitalism is not a patent or a network—it is a daily habit that users have stopped noticing they are performing. The companies that engineered that invisibility are now disclosing it openly to investors as their core competitive advantage.

cultural · economic · 9 pages

The Presence Premium

Algorithmic content has made on-demand video a commodity — and made being in the same room at the same time genuinely scarce. The companies that control fixed-capacity live experiences are converting that scarcity into deferred-revenue moats and operating margins that infinite-content businesses cannot touch.

economic · societal · 8 pages

The Affordability Ceiling

Consumer spending is bifurcating not by preference but by hard constraint — Bloomingdale's comps at +10.2% while Macy's nameplate comps at +1.6%, HOKA grows 23.6% while Nike Direct contracts 9%, and the middle shelf is being emptied from both ends at once.

societal · economic · 8 pages

The Workers' Invisible Tax

When companies face labor scarcity, the choice is wages or automation. Across mining, landscaping, construction trades, and packaging, profitable companies are choosing automation — and the gains flow to shareholders while workers and their communities absorb the cost without offset.

societal · cultural · 9 pages

The Screening Revolution

Medicine is moving from treating disease to finding it years before symptoms appear — and the companies that lowered the friction to participate are printing growth numbers that look nothing like healthcare.

societal · cultural · 9 pages

The Local News Collapse

Gannett reported its first positive annual net income since its 2019 merger—and total revenue still fell. That is the structural trap: digital progress is real, print collapse is faster, and no one has cracked a profitable model for covering city hall.

cultural · economic · 8 pages

The Ritual Economy

When consumers compress discretionary spending, the habits tied to daily identity and life-stage milestones hold — pet care, morning coffee, oral hygiene, bridal jewelry — while aspirational and hedonic categories collapse first.

cultural · technological · 8 pages

AI Cultural Disintermediation

AI is not just doing the work of human experts — it is absorbing their authority. The platforms that own the AI layer are capturing the trust, the data, and the economic relationship that credentialed intermediaries spent careers building.

cultural · demographic · 8 pages

The Pet Economy's Mirror

Pet care spending is growing volume and margin simultaneously while most consumer categories choose between them — because pets have crossed a threshold from expense to dependent, and identity-category spending does not respond to affordability pressure the way discretionary spending does.

industrial · economic · 8 pages

The Grid's New Landlords

Gas pipelines and regulated transmission operators are extracting structural economic rents from the AI buildout — tolling electrons and molecules that hyperscalers cannot route around — while bearing none of the technology risk that defines every other layer of the AI stack.

economic · cultural · 8 pages

Membership or Nothing

Across mobility, media, pet food, and financial services, the best prices and fastest delivery now sit behind a fee gate — and the companies that built that gate are using non-member revenue to subsidize member pricing, creating two parallel economies under one brand.

economic · industrial · 8 pages

The Commodity Whiplash Transfer

When nitrogen prices spike or cocoa costs surge, integrated giants no longer absorb the pain — they pass it downstream through contracts engineered to shift risk onto the next tier. The farmer, the packager, the small contractor, and the household bear the hit so CF Industries, Glencore, and Freeport-McMoRan do not have to.

economic · regulatory · 8 pages

The Private Capital Colonization

Alternative asset managers have absorbed trillions in insurance float, pension obligations, and retail savings since 2022 — capturing fee structures 20x higher than regulated banks for identical economic risk, while remaining invisible to the regulatory safety net built to handle the next crisis.
