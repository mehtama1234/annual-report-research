The Vet Pharmacy Arc: How Pet Care Follows the Human Healthcare Supply-Chain Path

BrightSpring's pharmacy infrastructure shows pet healthcare following the same consolidation and monetization path as human healthcare: script volume, per-script pricing power, and integration of medication into recurring care platforms are becoming the lever for margin expansion.

Pet healthcare is becoming an integrated pharmacy and provider play similar to human healthcare. BrightSpring fulfilled approximately 43 million prescriptions in 2025 at revenue of $314.20 per script ($27.50 gross profit per script), enabling $205.5 million adjusted EBITDA on Q2 2026 revenue of $3.873 billion. Chewy is building Autoship and repeat relationships around prescriptions with 21.3 million active customers at FY2025 year-end (84% Autoship penetration). Colgate's Hill's Pet Nutrition segment is one of the company's strongest growth drivers, contributing materially to overall margins. Pet owner spending on health and wellness remains sticky even when discretionary spending tightens, and script economics are becoming the monetization lever just as they are in human healthcare.

The mechanism

BrightSpring's model shows how veterinary pharmacy is consolidating. The company serves 465,000+ patients daily through pharmacy (43 million scripts annually), home health, hospice, infusion, and primary care. That is integrated healthcare infrastructure for complex patients. The script economics matter: each prescription generates $314.20 revenue and $27.50 gross profit. Scale by 43 million prescriptions and script volume and per-script economics drive profitability. Q2 2026 showed home health average daily census at 46,448 (up 54% YoY), proving the model scales across services.

Chewy is building the same infrastructure from the consumer side. The company has 21.3M active customers with 84% Autoship penetration (Q3 2025 data). That is recurring revenue locked at the customer level. Chewy is not just moving pet food online; it is building the subscription and relationship layer that Autoship provides. As Chewy embeds pharmacy and prescription capabilities (which it has been investing in), it becomes a closer analog to BrightSpring—a unified interface for repeated pet care purchases including medications. Q1 2026 showed 200K net customer additions, nearly 200K products and services, and record profitability despite dynamic consumer backdrop.

Colgate's Hill's Pet Nutrition is the upstream analog. Pet nutrition margins are higher than human food margins, and pet owners show less price sensitivity on health-focused products. Hill's is described as one of Colgate's strongest growth drivers, contributing to category-specific premium positioning (prescription diet formulas, therapeutic nutrition). The pet owner will pay for specialized formulas addressing allergies, kidney disease, or GI issues, similar to human pharmaceutical nutrition markets.

The ecosystem is building toward the human healthcare model: pharmacy infrastructure captures scripts and recurring revenue, provider services (veterinary practices) consolidate and link to pharmacy platforms, and consumers (pet owners) are locked into Autoship and subscription models that reduce churn. Pricing power sits in script economics and specialty formulations where alternatives don't exist. Pet owners face the same constraints as human patients: if their pet needs a specific medication or therapeutic diet, they will pay the price.

Why now

The inflection is 2024-2026. Pet spending proved resilient through inflation even when discretionary tightened. BrightSpring's Q2 2026 home health census grew 54% YoY with script revenue per unit rising to $314.20 and gross profit per script at $27.50. Chewy's FY2025 sales grew 6.2% to $12.60B with Q1 2026 delivering record profitability (adjusted EBITDA $253.1M, margin 7.5%) despite 'more dynamic consumer backdrop.' Pet owners treat pets as family members and absorb higher prices for health and wellness. That stickiness makes pet healthcare defensible, which in turn makes consolidation and infrastructure investment rational. Companies see durable unit economics in pet healthcare and are investing accordingly.

Who pays · who profits

Who pays
Pet owners pay through higher per-script costs, higher prices for therapeutic pet nutrition, and subscription lock-in via Autoship. Those costs are absorbed because pet health is increasingly treated as non-discretionary by owners. Veterinary clinics pay through industry consolidation and dependence on pharmacy partnerships. Private-label pet nutrition faces pricing pressure from premium therapeutic formulas.
Who profits
BrightSpring, Chewy, and integrated pharmacy platforms capture margin expansion as pet healthcare spending consolidates. Colgate through Hill's Pet Nutrition benefits from premium pricing and recurring demand. Winners are companies that own the repeated interaction (BrightSpring script fulfillment, Chewy Autoship with 84% penetration) and those that own brand loyalty around therapeutic nutrition (Colgate Hill's). BrightSpring Q2 2026 showed adjusted EBITDA guidance increased to $820-845M for full 2026 and leverage fell to 2.15x (below 2.5x target).

The tension

The model depends on continued gatekeeping by veterinarians and on pet owner willingness to pay higher prices for convenience and quality assurance. If pet insurance expands coverage for medications, insurance networks may bypass integrated pharmacy platforms. If online pharmacies or lower-cost alternatives emerge without veterinary gatekeeping, script volume economics suffer. For now, infrastructure consolidates, but business model depends on continued veterinary control and pricing power.

The surprising part

BrightSpring treats pet pharmacy as financially and strategically equal to human healthcare services. The company is scaling both verticals aggressively. Chewy's Q1 2026 net income reached $94.8M with record quarterly profitability despite consumer pressure. That normalization—treating pet pharmacy as a strategic equal to human healthcare services—proves the industry fully expects pet healthcare to follow human healthcare's consolidation path. The surprise is how fast the economics work at scale.

Why it matters

This reflects how pet ownership culture has shifted. Pets are no longer optional consumer goods; they are family members that warrant healthcare infrastructure equivalent to human healthcare. The consequence is that pet healthcare is becoming a permanent and growing consumer spending stream tied to chronic disease management and therapeutic nutrition. For lower-income households, pet ownership may become increasingly expensive or unsustainable if veterinary and pharmacy consolidation continues pushing prices higher.

Pet healthcare is following human healthcare's consolidation trajectory: script volume and per-script margins are becoming the core monetization engine, integrating pharmacy, provider services, and consumer subscriptions into unified platforms where pricing power rests in repeated customer lock-in.
