The mechanism

Autoship subscription removes friction and price comparison from repeat purchases. 84% of Chewy's $12.60B revenue comes from subscriptions—not because prices are lower (they aren't), but because cancellation friction is high. A pet owner who sets up Autoship for food and prescriptions faces behavioral inertia: they must actively cancel, select a competitor, verify product availability, and re-setup delivery. At $595 per customer per year, even 5% annual churn would be noteworthy; actual churn data suggests 2-3% annually. This is not loyal preference; it is switching cost architecture.

Prescription integration ties switching to healthcare decisions. Chewy integrated veterinary prescription fulfillment across 190K products by FY2025, normalizing digital prescription management in pet ownership. A pet owner who fills a prescription through Chewy now has that record in Chewy's system (health history, veterinarian reference, medication history). Switching requires requesting records, finding a competitor who accepts the same prescription format, and breaking the behavioral routine. This is identical to how health insurance creates lock-in, not through price, but through data portability friction.

Resilience through emotional attachment, not price elasticity. Chewy Q1 2026 showed +7.7% growth despite macro weakness (consumer spending slowdown, 2024-2025 recession concerns). This is because pet spending is not discretionary—owners sacrifice other consumption before cutting pet nutrition or medications. This emotional commitment allows Chewy to maintain 30.1% gross margin (Q1 2026) without losing customers to price competition. The margin is extracted through convenience tax, not efficiency gains.

Why now

Post-pandemic (2020-2025) inflection point crystallized in FY2025-Q1 2026: (1) Veterinary prescription fulfillment became normalized digitally. Pre-2020, 'send prescription to Chewy' was unusual; by 2025, it was expected. This regulatory and behavioral shift happened silently, without Chewy marketing effort—vets adapted, consumers adapted, switching costs rose. (2) Subscription became the default payment model. Autoship adoption jumped from ~60% of revenue (2019-2021) to 84% (FY2025) as norms shifted and behavioral inertia deepened. (3) Pet spending proved recession-resilient. Q1 2026 (+7.7% YoY growth) came amid consumer spending weakness, proving emotional attachment outlasts price sensitivity. Chewy's 21.3M customers at $595 per customer represents a stickier subscription base than most SaaS businesses.

Who pays · who profits

Who pays
Pet owners, through 'convenience tax'—the implicit premium they pay to avoid switching friction. Chewy's gross margin of 30.1% (Q1 2026) is not driven by operational efficiency (it is lower than veterinary prescription processors or bulk commodity retailers). It is driven by behavioral lock-in. Additionally, small pet supply retailers lose share to Chewy's platform (no independent data, but Chewy's market dominance suggests retail closures). Veterinarians' prescription revenues decline as fulfillment moves to digital intermediaries, reducing their revenue per patient.
Who profits
Chewy shareholders and management. FY2025 generated $562M free cash flow (record) from a $12.60B revenue base—9.3% FCF conversion, driven by high gross margins and limited capital intensity. The 21.3M customer base at 84% subscription penetration is extraordinarily defensible. Q1 2026 guidance points to continued growth. Institutional investors value Chewy's subscription characteristics (predictable revenue, low churn, high LTV) at multiples typically reserved for enterprise SaaS.

The tension

Regulatory risk from prescription intermediation. As Chewy's prescription fulfillment grows (no separate data, but implied in 'prescription integration' messaging), state pharmacy boards may impose licensing requirements or margin controls. Additionally, veterinary professional bodies may push back on direct-to-consumer prescription fulfillment, reducing Chewy's defensibility. Competitive risk from Amazon Pharmacy and veterinary telemedicine platforms that bypass Chewy entirely.

The surprising part

Chewy's value is not in logistics efficiency or product selection (both commoditized). It is in having 21.3M email addresses of people who love their pets and will pay subscription premiums to avoid the friction of switching. This is identical to how Spotify or Netflix operate—not because they are cheaper, but because churn is low and convenience is monetized. Pet ownership's emotional intensity makes Chewy stickier than most consumer subscriptions.

Why it matters

If pet ownership becomes a subscription-dependent phenomenon (pet food, medications, healthcare all flow through Chewy), individual pet owners lose autonomy and price negotiation power. Veterinarians become data sources rather than primary care providers, shifting power from practitioners to platforms. Simultaneously, if Chewy improves pet health outcomes (better nutrition, prescription compliance, preventive care alerts), this centralizes beneficial data and enables scale. The tradeoff is real: convenience and compliance vs. autonomy and competition.

Chewy built a $12.60B platform by making switching feel medically disruptive and emotionally costly, capturing 21.3M customers at 84% subscription penetration.
