The Screening Revolution: Who Profits from Earlier Detection

Guideline changes, payer coverage expansion, and the normalization of at-home and low-friction screening drove a scaling wave in diagnostic testing 2024-2026. Exact Sciences, Guardant Health, Labcorp, and Quest Diagnostics all benefited from higher test volumes, mix improvement toward advanced diagnostics, and recurring customer relationships. Exact Sciences Q4 2025 revenue $878M with screening revenue $695M. Guardant Q2 2026 revenue $335M with screening revenue $52.9M and Shield tests reaching 66k. Labcorp Q2 2026 revenue $3.73B with 6% growth and 15.8% operating margin. Quest Q2 2026 net revenues $3.043B with requisition volume up 13.1% and requisition growth up 13.0% organically. The profits flowed to companies that controlled the front door of screening behavior (Exact, Guardant), ran the laboratory backbone (Labcorp, Quest), or offered convenience-first access platforms (Exact's Cologuard, Guardant's Shield, Quest's direct-to-consumer services).

Earlier, more convenient, and more acceptable screening will drive volume growth and open new recurring-revenue relationships. Companies that own the screening entry point, offer low-friction test formats, and expand into companion diagnostics and monitoring will outperform those dependent on aging, high-barrier-to-entry testing methods.

The mechanism

Guideline changes (USPSTF colorectal-cancer screening age lowering, broader multi-cancer early detection recommendations) and payer coverage (Medicare/Medicaid, United Healthcare, Cigna) created policy tailwinds for screening volume. Guideline inclusion drives physician orders and payer reimbursement.

Exact Sciences' Cologuard (stool-based colorectal screening), Cologuard Plus, Oncodetect (post-treatment monitoring), and new blood-based CRC screening through Freenome partnership all competed on convenience and acceptability. Lower friction drives participation among patients who avoid invasive screening.

Guardant's Shield multi-cancer early detection test and oncology-plus-screening platform addressed a different vector: blood-based screening appeals to patients who prefer convenience but also to health systems seeking earlier detection at less cost than advanced staging.

Labcorp and Quest provided the laboratory backbone and direct-to-consumer access (Quest's physician-office and consumer channels, Labcorp's central-lab scale and biopharma services). They benefited from volume, mixing toward higher-value advanced diagnostics, and enterprise embedment.

Payer coverage and insurance reimbursement were critical gates. Exact Sciences' acceleration in Q2-Q3 2025 correlated with expanded Medicare coverage and Quest collaboration. Guardant's Shield momentum in Q1-Q2 2026 included UnitedHealth and guideline-inclusion wins.

Why now

The inflection was 2024-2025. Major guideline changes (USPSTF colorectal screening age lowering in May 2021, but implementation and payer coverage matured in 2024-2025) created a structural floor for volume. Simultaneously, company product launches accelerated: Exact's Cologuard Plus (Q3 2025), Cancerguard (Q3 2025), blood-based CRC (Q4 2025). Guardant's Shield expanded into colorectal and broader populations. Coverage wins cascaded: Medicare added Oncodetect (Q3 2025), UnitedHealth added Shield (Q1 2026). By Q2 2026, volume trends were undeniable. Exact raised full-year guidance repeatedly; Guardant raised 2026 guidance to 32-34% growth; Labcorp and Quest both raised full-year guidance. This was not a one-time spike. It was structural volume expansion driven by policy, product, and payer alignment.

Who pays · who profits

Who pays
Patients (time, discomfort, travel for testing), insurance plans (test reimbursement, treatment cost offset if earlier detection improves outcomes), and self-pay consumers (out-of-pocket for direct-to-consumer tests).
Who profits
Exact Sciences (owns Cologuard brand and colorectal franchise), Guardant (owns Shield multi-cancer platform and oncology relationships), Labcorp and Quest (laboratory volume, throughput, and margin mix), companies offering specimen collection (Quest's physicians, Exact's retail partners), and new genomics companies capturing early-detection opportunity.

The tension

The tension is between volume growth and margin sustainability. Payer pressure on per-test reimbursement could squeeze margins if volume growth slows. Additionally, if too many screening tests proliferate without proven outcomes benefit, payers may tighten coverage or demand evidence of improved outcomes rather than mere volume growth. Finally, if screening volume accelerates faster than follow-up testing and treatment infrastructure (endoscopy, oncology) can handle, patients and providers will face delays.

The surprising part

Exact Sciences and Guardant both pivoted heavily toward consumer-facing and direct-to-consumer language in 2025-2026, despite traditionally being physician-order-focused diagnostics companies. Exact talked about 'screening more people than ever' and emphasized retail partnerships and Quest collaborations. Guardant launched direct-to-consumer campaigns for Shield and spoke of 'colorectal cancer screening awareness' via influencers. This marketing shift showed that both saw volume growth as dependent not only on physician adoption but on consumer acceptance and awareness. This inversion—diagnostic companies marketing to consumers—reflected the consumerization of healthcare testing.

Why it matters

Earlier cancer detection and prevention through screening are among the highest-leverage public-health interventions. Broader screening access could reduce cancer mortality and treatment costs. However, if screening proliferates without outcomes evidence or if healthcare systems cannot absorb the testing volume, false-positives and unnecessary follow-up could harm public health.

Guideline changes, payer coverage expansion, and low-friction test formats scaled earlier detection into high-volume recurring behavior, benefiting companies that owned the screening entry point and laboratory infrastructure.
