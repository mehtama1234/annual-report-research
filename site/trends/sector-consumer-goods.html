Sector synthesis

Consumer Goods

Consumer Goods in 2026 bifurcates cleanly: identity-attached brands with direct-to-consumer control (Philip Morris, e.l.f., Tapestry, Burlington) capture share and pricing power while staples incumbents grind through tariffs, private-label competition, and affordability pressure. The gap is structural, not cyclical.

The state of the sector

The Consumer Goods sector is not cyclical anymore — it is permanently splitting into winners and losers based on brand heat, channel control, and geography. Philip Morris International leads with smoke-free now 41.5% of revenues and 43% of gross profit (FY2025), reaching 43M+ consumers across 109 markets with smoke-free volumes up 12.8% and adjusted EPS at $7.54. e.l.f. Beauty delivered its 30th consecutive growth quarter at 83% gross margin with 7 years of market-share gains, growing net sales 36% to $479.4M in FY2027 Q1. Tapestry's Coach grew 31% in Q3 2026 at 75% gross margin while acquiring 2.2M new customers with 35% Gen Z. Burlington accelerated store expansion to 40 new stores in Q1 2026 while hitting +6% comp sales. Crocs brand passed $1.0B quarterly revenue in Q2 2026 with 52.1% direct-to-consumer mix. Victoria's Secret posted +13% comps in Q1 2026 after years of brand repair.

The other half is trapped. Kraft Heinz saw organic net sales down 3.4% in 2025 despite committing $700M to brand reinvestment. Coty withdrew full-year guidance in February 2026 after Middle East geopolitical shock hit beauty and Consumer Beauty segment continued declining. Whirlpool is cutting dividends while North American appliance demand sits at recession levels. Campbell's is guiding negative organic sales for FY2026. General Mills flagged weakness in core categories. The pattern is structural: consumers at mid-market and below are trading down to private label and off-price formats faster than incumbent brands can offset with volume. Staples brands cannot generate pricing power without brand equity.

Tariffs are the visible accelerant masking the real story. Funko received $25.4M in tariff refunds Q1-Q2 2026, Spin Master $37.9M, JAKKS Pacific $11.1M, Build-A-Bear $7M — accounting tailwinds that disappear when refunds stop flowing. Tapestry absorbed 180 basis points of tariff headwind in Q3 2026 without margin erosion because of pricing power. Hasbro and most small-to-midcap hardware makers absorbed costs without equivalent cover. China-sourced supply chains face a permanent cost floor.

The winners share four traits: (1) identity attachment that survives affordability pressure (smoke-free nicotine, accessible beauty, off-price discovery, licensed IP); (2) direct-to-consumer channel control providing margin and first-party data (Levi's at 49% DTC with 62.7% gross margin, YETI at 60% DTC, Crocs at 52.1% DTC); (3) geographic diversification with emerging-market momentum (Coca-Cola unit cases flat FY2025 but emerging markets carrying growth, Lululemon Americas down 5% comps Q1 FY26 but international up 13%); and (4) either product format innovation (Philip Morris, Hasbro multiformat IP) or recurring revenue adjacencies (Carnival deposits at all-time high $9.0B, Hill's Pet Nutrition outpacing core Colgate categories). The companies that survive and compound own the customer identity and have a recurring reason to return beyond commodity replenishment.

Sub-trends inside the sector

Smoke-free category migration as recurring revenue replacement

Philip Morris is executing the clearest large-scale consumer-category migration: shifting nicotine demand from combustibles (which grew 9.5% in net revenue) to IQOS, ZYN, and smoke-free pouches (up 12.8% in volume). Smoke-free already 41.5% of revenue and 43% of gross profit with 43M+ consumers across 109 markets as of Q2 2026. The business model depends on addiction stickiness maintaining pricing power during the transition — adjusted EPS nearly doubled from $4.52 (FY2024) to $7.54 (FY2025) despite volume shifts.

Philip Morris International

Accessible beauty as compounding share-gain machine

e.l.f. Beauty, Tapestry, and Victoria's Secret each prove that accessible or aspirational-adjacent brands compound market share through identity alignment and community engagement. e.l.f. delivered 30 consecutive quarters of growth at 83% gross margin with FY2027 Q1 net sales $479.4M (+36%) and 7 consecutive years of market-share gains. Tapestry's Coach acquired 2.2M new customers (35% Gen Z) at 75% gross margin in Q3 2026. Victoria's Secret hit +13% comps in Q1 2026 after years of brand repair. The common thread: brand as cultural signal, not commodity purchase.

Off-price and closeout retail acceleration

Burlington and Ollie's Bargain Outlet grow comp sales while accelerating store openings in an environment where traditional retailers contract. Burlington hit +6% comps in Q1 2026 while opening 40 new stores in the quarter (1,242 total). Ollie's Army membership grew 12% to 17.5M without subscription fees. The macro is their tailwind: consumer affordability pressure and private-label penetration push traffic directly into off-price formats where brand-name discovery at reduced prices is the entire proposition.

Burlington Stores, Ollie's Bargain Outlet

IP-driven franchise flywheels outpacing physical toy retail

Hasbro and Mattel pivot from toy-box dependence to IP-led franchises that monetize across tabletop, digital, film, and collectibles simultaneously. Hasbro's Wizards of the Coast and Digital Gaming grew 45% in FY2025 driven by Magic: The Gathering +59% with Monopoly Go! contributing $168M annually. Mattel's Masters of the Universe movie released Q2 2026 with action figures +35%. Physical product margins are tariff-pressured; the real upside is IP licensing and digital monetization where recurring revenue compounds.

Hasbro, Mattel, Spin Master

DTC channel as margin and data engine replacing wholesale

Across apparel (Levi's, YETI, Crocs), direct-to-consumer is the margin expansion lever and first-party data capture. Levi's DTC reached 49% of revenue with Q2 2026 gross margin at 62.7%. YETI's DTC is 60% of sales. Crocs' DTC reached 52.1% of sales with Q2 DTC +12%. The shift captures customer data, purchase patterns, and repeat-order economics that wholesale can never provide — owning the customer relationship is the moat.

Staples brand repair under affordability pressure proves difficult

Kraft Heinz, General Mills, Kimberly-Clark, Campbell's, and Colgate-Palmolive face organic growth stuck in low single digits or negative. Kraft Heinz saw organic sales down 3.4% in 2025 despite committing $700M to brand reinvestment. Campbell's is guiding negative organic sales for FY2026. Coty withdrew full-year guidance in February 2026. The pattern is structural: private-label is an adequate substitute for mid-market and below consumers, and they are making the substitution in real time. Pricing defense cannot substitute for brand equity that was allowed to erode.

The tension

The sector's deepest fault line is identity-versus-commodity, accelerated by tariffs that function as accounting noise masking structural cost floors. Every company absorbs material tariff and commodity inflation; those with brand heat in identity-coded categories (accessible luxury, smoke-free nicotine, off-price discovery, licensed IP) can route the cost through channel mix or pricing power. Staples incumbents with household-necessity positioning cannot: private label is adequate, and consumers are making the substitution in real time. The second fault line is tariff accounting distortion: Funko $25.4M, Spin Master $37.9M, JAKKS Pacific $11.1M, Build-A-Bear $7M in Q1-Q2 2026 refunds mask soft underlying operating models. When refunds stop, the real cost structure becomes visible. Third fault line is geographic bifurcation: Coca-Cola FY2025 unit cases flat despite $47.9B revenue; Lululemon Americas comps down 5% Q1 FY26 but international up 13%; PepsiCo international organic growth 7% in Q2 2026 vs North America softness. Emerging markets are where growth lives.

Who wins · who loses

Winners are identity-attached demand survivors (e.l.f., Tapestry, Philip Morris, Crocs, Deckers, Lululemon international), DTC channel operators (Levi's at 49% DTC with 62.7% gross margin, YETI 60% DTC, Crocs 52.1% DTC), off-price retailers capturing trade-down (Burlington, Ollie's), and IP-holding franchises monetizing across physical and digital formats (Hasbro, Mattel). Losers are mid-market staples brands that eroded brand equity (Kraft Heinz -3.4% organic FY2025, Campbell's guiding negative FY2026 organic, General Mills flaging weakness), discretionary categories dependent on housing-cycle sentiment (Whirlpool cutting dividends), and single-channel hardware manufacturers with heavy China exposure and no DTC buffer to absorb tariff pass-through. Department store middle is structural loser: Nordstrom went private to escape pressure; Macy's surviving on media-network and credit-card revenue rather than merchandise margin.

Outlook

Consumer Goods bifurcation is permanent. Private-label penetration earned during affordability pressure tends to stick even when sentiment improves. Companies with brand identity, direct customer relationships, and recurring touchpoints will continue gaining share regardless of cycle. The next 12-18 months clarify which staples incumbents complete brand repair (Kraft Heinz's $700M bet and General Mills' FY2027 'step change in remarkability' are tests) and which face secular decline. Tariff refunds fade and real cost structures become visible. Philip Morris smoke-free reaching 50-55% of revenue by 2027-28 becomes the most compelling long-duration narrative in the sector: the company transforms its demand base without losing the cash engine. Companies that survive compound through three criteria: (1) own the customer identity (Philip Morris smoke-free identity, e.l.f. accessible beauty community, Tapestry Coach Gen Z heat); (2) control the distribution (DTC 50%+ of mix with 60%+ gross margins); (3) recurring reason to return beyond commodity replenishment (subscription adjacencies, loyalty data loops, licensed IP). The retail middle and passive staples incumbents are permanently squeezed.
