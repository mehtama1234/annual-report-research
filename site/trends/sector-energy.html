Sector synthesis

Energy

U.S. and Canadian energy is not transitioning away from hydrocarbons — it is engineering them into a hard-asset export and infrastructure machine. Producers lock gas into 20-year contracts; midstream operators build fractionators, LNG terminals, and gas-to-power plants; refiners run at 94%+ utilization; oil sands mines reach record output at breakevens below C$22/bbl. The shift from commodity producer to infrastructure operator is complete.

The state of the sector

The 2025-2026 energy story is discipline compounding on scale. Upstream producers use M&A to hit critical size, then immediately shift to deleveraging and buybacks: Expand Energy (7.5 Bcfe/d, 0.5x leverage) signed a 20-year LNG export deal; EQT cut net debt from $7.69B to $5.54B in a single quarter while generating $1.832B free cash flow in Q1 2026 alone; Devon/Coterra merged to 1.359 million Boe/d and disciplined costs below $11/Boe. Canadian Natural Resources hit a record 624,754 bbl/d oil sands mining output while capturing C$22.19/bbl all-in costs and an average US$8.37/bbl SCO premium.

Midstream transformed from toll road to margin engine. Energy Transfer (140,000 miles of pipe) posted Q2 2026 EBITDA of $5.07B, up 31% YoY; Enterprise Products Partners (14.7 MMBPD equivalent) set a $1.8B net income record and credited Q2 gains explicitly to monetizing both volume growth and global shipping disruption simultaneously; Targa Resources ($1.603B Q2 EBITDA record) spends $4.5B annually on growth capex while distributing $5.00/unit and guiding to $5.7-5.9B 2026 EBITDA; ONEOK (acquisition-layered, $2.1B quarterly EBITDA) and Williams ($8.4B 2026 EBITDA guidance, up $200M) build fractionators, LNG export terminals, and gas-to-power infrastructure. The Waha basis problem (which touched -$9.52/Mcf in Q2 2026) became an infrastructure problem solved through transport, not a hedge problem.

Services and equipment companies reinvented themselves as industrialized infrastructure plays. Baker Hughes IET (Industrial Energy Technology) hit a record $37.1B remaining performance obligations in Q2 2026 on LNG, turbomachinery, and power systems orders; SLB's Data Center Solutions revenue grew 80% YoY to $186M in Q2 2026, with digital ARR above $1B; TechnipFMC holds $16.4B backlog across subsea and offshore with $30B delivered over three years. Refining utilization held at 94% globally; Suncor achieved record upgrader utilization of 99% and record refining throughput of 480,000 bbl/d in Q2 2026.

Sub-trends inside the sector

LNG export as permanent infrastructure, not optionality

U.S. gas is locking into decade-long export contracts at scale. Expand Energy signed a 20-year deal for 1.15 million tonnes per annum at Delfin; EQT holds 0.5 MTPA of 5-year and 10-year LNG export SPAs starting 2028 plus a 325,000 Dth/d premium power supply agreement; Cheniere completed Corpus Christi Stage 3 trains 1-4 in 2025 and exported a record 670 cargoes. Domestic gas is now priced against global LNG markets, not just Henry Hub.

Cheniere Energy, EQT Corporation, Expand Energy, Baker Hughes, Williams Companies, TechnipFMC

Permian and Appalachian consolidation for infrastructure access

Basin consolidation is ending; winners are now selected by infrastructure reach. Permian players (Diamondback, Devon, Coterra, Permian Resources, Matador) complete 190+ bolt-on transactions per quarter; Appalachian players (EQT, Expand, Antero, CNX, Range) differentiate on LNG exposure and NGL premiums, not drilling quality. The value shifted from 'best rock' to 'best market access.'

Diamondback Energy, Permian Resources, Devon Energy, EQT Corporation, Expand Energy, Antero Resources, Range Resources

Midstream margin transformation

Energy Transfer, Enterprise Products, ONEOK, Targa, and Williams are building dense fractionation, LPG export, and gas-to-power infrastructure while distributing record cash. Energy Transfer's Q2 2026 EBITDA hit $5.07B (31% YoY growth); Targa self-funds $4.5B annual capex while guiding to $5.7-5.9B 2026 EBITDA and $5.00/unit distributions; Enterprise monetized Q2 simultaneously on volume growth and global shipping disruption. The capacity buildout is compounding and self-funding.

Canadian oil sands as precision industrial operation

Canadian Natural Resources, Suncor, Cenovus, and Imperial Oil run integrated upstream-to-retail systems at record throughput with breakevens now irrelevant to the economics story. Canadian Natural hit C$22.19/bbl all-in costs and captured US$8.37/bbl SCO premium; Suncor achieved 99% upgrader utilization and record 480,000 bbl/d refining throughput. These are 30+ year reserve bases managed for cost discipline and shareholder distributions, not volume growth.

AI power consumption is routing back into natural gas infrastructure. Williams raised 2026 EBITDA guidance $200M to $8.4B on Blackstone power-innovation JV and data-center projects; Enterprise traced new pipeline demand to AI and data-center load growth; Baker Hughes received $1.4B+ in power systems orders in Q1 2026 alone. This structural demand layer did not exist in prior cycles.

The tension

The sector's fault line is the gap between contracted LNG/power demand visibility and physical basis constraints. When Waha basis traded at -$9.52/Mcf in Q2 2026, producers with firm transport captured value while others curtailed. The 10-20 year LNG contracts that EQT, Expand, Antero, and Range are signing lock domestic supply to global markets — brilliant if global LNG holds, catastrophic if demand shifts, policy reverses, or Europe accelerates renewables faster than expected. Parallel tension: every midstream expansion and oil sands growth plan assumes hydrocarbon social license and regulatory footing through the 2030s while ESG pressure and energy-transition policy remain manageable. Companies run both narratives simultaneously (carbon-management positions, renewable diesel platforms) but physical capex flows overwhelmingly into hydrocarbon expansion.

Who wins · who loses

Winners: integrated platforms with LNG access, NGL fractionation density, and low-cost basin positions (Cheniere, Enterprise Products, Expand Energy, EQT, Targa, ONEOK, Suncor, Canadian Natural Resources) plus services companies pivoted to industrial infrastructure (Baker Hughes $37.1B IET RPO, SLB $1B+ digital ARR). Losers: standalone pure-commodity producers without contracted takeaway or scale (operators exposed to full Waha basis without firm transport, Appalachian producers without LNG linkage); standalone refiners without midstream buffering (PBF Energy post-Martinez fire); North America oilfield-services exposure (Halliburton activity recovery slower than infrastructure-oriented peers).

Outlook

2027-2030: three concurrent forces drive the sector. First, LNG export capacity reaches full utilization as Stage 3 trains complete and new SPAs activate. Second, NGL fractionation and Gulf Coast export infrastructure handle Permian production growth. Third, gas-to-power from data centers becomes structural baseload, not incremental. Canadian oil sands continue compounding at low cost with multi-decade lives. Risk: commodity price collapse from synchronized global demand slowdown, European LNG destruction, or policy reversal exposes 2025-2026 expansion capex as over-investment. Base case: infrastructure already built is too embedded and contracted to unwind fast, so cash generation continues in softer commodity environments; winners are operators whose economics are driven more by throughput, utilization, and contract coverage than spot prices.

American and Canadian energy is being engineered into a hard-asset export machine — LNG trains, NGL fractionators, and gas-to-power systems compounding cash returns for whoever owns the connective tissue between wellhead and global demand.
