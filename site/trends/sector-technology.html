Sector synthesis

Technology

AI infrastructure has split technology into two economies: companies wired into the buildout are compounding at triple-digit rates, while those outside are defending against rapid structural decline.

The state of the sector

The 2025-2026 technology sector is defined by AI capital expenditure as the organizing force: companies are either benefiting, adapting, or being bypassed. At the infrastructure layer, Broadcom crossed $10.8B in AI semiconductor revenue (143% YoY growth) with $16B expected in Q3 2026. Micron scaled from $13.6B to $41.5B in quarterly revenue across three quarters as HBM became mandatory. Arista crossed its first $3B quarter at 49.9% non-GAAP margins on AI-fabric networking. Microsoft's Azure exceeded $100B annual revenue with $627B in commercial remaining performance obligations locking multi-year contracts. These are not incremental — they are structural step-changes driven by hyperscaler AI capex compounding across silicon, memory, networking, and cloud.

A second tier monetizes the AI wave indirectly but durably: AppLovin generates $800M+ quarterly free cash flow from AI-driven ad optimization; Snowflake repositions as the governance layer for agentic AI; CrowdStrike crossed $5B ARR while recovering from the July 2024 outage, validating platform consolidation over product-switching risk; Adobe's AI-first ARR tripled YoY exceeding $500M. These companies capture the productivity and monetization layer above infrastructure. Meanwhile, ad-tech mid-tier (Trade Desk, Taboola, Teads) fights fragmentation and AI-mediated discovery displacing intent-based buying.

The fault lines are stark. Intel generated flat revenue at $52.9B in FY2025 while the AI boom raged around it, betting survival on an 18A foundry ramp without anchor customers. EchoStar disclosed going-concern doubt with $7.3B current debt. Forrester's revenue fell from $432.5M to $396.9M as AI eroded legacy analyst content value. Companies that didn't wire themselves to infrastructure buildout or software governance are compressing fast — and the compression is structural, not cyclical.

Standout companies

  • Broadcom Inc. — Custom AI accelerator revenue reached $10.8B growing 143% YoY, with Q3 2026 guidance at $16B. The company has become the dominant supplier of AI silicon to hyperscalers preferring custom chips over NVIDIA commodity GPUs.
  • Micron Technology, Inc. — Revenue scaled from $13.6B to $41.5B across three consecutive quarters in 2026 as HBM became mandatory in AI systems. The most dramatic revenue step-change in the sector, backed by 16 strategic customer agreements locking future demand.
  • AppLovin Corporation — Generates $800M+ quarterly free cash flow from pure AI-driven advertising infrastructure. After divesting gaming assets, the company runs no consumer products and captures only auction economics of mobile app monetization at 53% revenue growth.
  • Microsoft Corporation — Azure crossed $100B annual revenue while $627B in commercial remaining performance obligations locks multi-year contracted revenue. Converted the enterprise AI transition into a multi-year backlog of extraordinary scale.
  • Arista Networks, Inc. — Crossed its first $3B quarter with 49.9% non-GAAP operating margin — proof that AI-fabric networking is rapid-growth, high-margin. Driven by 150M cumulative port shipments creating ecosystem lock-in.
  • CrowdStrike Holdings, Inc. — Crossed $5B ARR while recovering from the July 2024 global outage. The incident tested customer defection and ARR trajectory shows customers did not defect, validating platform consolidation as more durable than product-switching risk.
  • Intel Corporation — Flat revenue at $52.9B in FY2025 while the AI boom generated record profits for everyone around it. Executing a foundry bet without external customer demand — the outcome determines whether x86 remains relevant in the next decade.
  • Snowflake Inc. — Repositioned from data warehouse to 'control plane for the agentic enterprise' with $9.2B RPO, NRR above 125%. Cortex Agents and MCP Server framing position data governance as AI infrastructure nobody else controls.

The tension

The sector's core fault line is the divergence between AI infrastructure winners compounding at 30-143% and legacy platforms managing structural decline — with inflection happening faster than balance sheets absorb. Deeper tension: what if AI model commoditization collapses infrastructure pricing power? If foundation models become cheap utilities, marginal value of custom silicon, HBM, and AI networking compresses rapidly. Broadcom and Micron's windfalls depend on hyperscalers continuing to compete on AI capability rather than simply buying commodity inference capacity. Second tension is capital: Microsoft $80B+, Meta $72B, Amazon/Google comparable — the largest coordinated capital deployment in technology history — assumes AI monetization materializes before rate pressures force discipline.

Who wins · who loses

Winners are companies embedded in AI infrastructure supply chains that cannot be bypassed: custom silicon designers (Broadcom), HBM suppliers (Micron), AI-fabric networking (Arista), process-control equipment (KLA, Lam Research). Software layer winners are platforms with high switching costs and AI expansion narratives increasing ARR per customer: CrowdStrike, Snowflake, Zscaler, Adobe. Losers are companies dependent on traffic patterns AI disrupts — content recommendation platforms facing 3%+ quarter slowdowns (Trade Desk), legacy enterprise IT without AI narrative (Intel, HP Inc.), analyst intermediaries being disintermediated by AI-generated content (Forrester). The structural loser is mid-tier that cannot demonstrate either infrastructure necessity or sticky subscription economics — compressed on both margins and multiples.

Outlook

AI infrastructure buildout has minimum two to three more years of compounding capex intensity before supply-demand curves balance. Hyperscaler commitments already booked through 2027. The question is not whether AI hardware demand continues but whether software monetization layer catches up fast enough to justify infrastructure spend. Microsoft Copilot (30M seats) and GitHub Copilot (20M users) are early validation, but revenue per seat must scale significantly. Next battleground is agentic AI governance: who controls the data layer, identity layer, and policy enforcement when AI agents execute workflows autonomously. Snowflake, Zscaler, CrowdStrike, and Microsoft each position as control plane. Legacy telecom/cable (AT&T, Charter, Comcast) face parallel transition — fiber buildouts yield recurring broadband eventually, but capex absorption is multi-year and video decline accelerates faster than fiber acquisition fills gap.

AI infrastructure spending has created the most concentrated wealth transfer in technology history — from enterprise IT budgets into silicon, memory, networking, and platform companies — and companies outside that transfer are discovering that structural decline is faster than they planned.
