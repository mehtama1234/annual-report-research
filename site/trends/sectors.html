Sector-wise synthesis

What's happening inside each sector

Bottom-up synthesis of each sector's own dynamics, standout companies, and fault lines — from the coded filings.

Sector

Basic Materials

Copper scarcity, gold windfall, and domestic steel protection are bifurcating Basic Materials into asset-rich winners and commodity volume losers: do you own scarce, irreplaceable physical infrastructure?

Sector

Consumer Goods

Consumer Goods in 2026 bifurcates cleanly: identity-attached brands with direct-to-consumer control (Philip Morris, e.l.f., Tapestry, Burlington) capture share and pricing power while staples incumbents grind through tariffs, private-label competition, and affordability pressure. The gap is structural, not cyclical.

Sector

Energy

U.S. and Canadian energy is not transitioning away from hydrocarbons — it is engineering them into a hard-asset export and infrastructure machine. Producers lock gas into 20-year contracts; midstream operators build fractionators, LNG terminals, and gas-to-power plants; refiners run at 94%+ utilization; oil sands mines reach record output at breakevens below C$22/bbl. The shift from commodity producer to infrastructure operator is complete.

Sector

Financial

Finance in 2025-2026 consolidates into platform empires: mega-operators absorbing insurance float into private credit engines while active managers bleed $97B+ in annual outflows and regional banks escape spread compression through fee diversification.

Sector

Healthcare

Healthcare is splitting into workflow-embedded and commoditized layers. Embedded platforms—distribution (McKesson, Cencora), diagnostics (Labcorp, Quest, Natera), robotics (Intuitive), recurring device systems (Insulet, DexCom), dialysis (DaVita)—generate recurring revenue and pricing power from institutional lock-in. Commoditized layers—generic pharma, home health labor, fee-for-service payers—face margin compression. The macro is GLP-1 volume pull through distribution and device infrastructure while squeezing distributor gross margins; reimbursement policy remains the sector's binding constraint.

Sector

Industrial Goods

Industrial Goods has bifurcated into an AI-infrastructure execution race and a residentially-tied collapse. Contractor backlogs at Quanta ($53.4B), Comfort Systems ($14.06B), and Vertiv ($15.0B) are record-setting—but demand is now completely outpaced by execution capacity. The constraint is craft labor, not orders. Meanwhile, housing-tied equipment distributors and builders are drowning in affordability gravity.

Sector

Industrials

Mueller Industries (MLI) runs a copper-fabrication spread business where prices float with raw materials, generating $958.5M in operating income on $4.179B in sales while residential construction stayed soft — proof that replacement demand, repair cycles, and HVAC mix can outpace housing starts. CECO Environmental runs project-engineered pollution control and industrial water treatment, posted $1.819B in backlog post-Thermon but negative $(15.7)M free cash flow in Q1 2026 even as orders surged 97% — the fault line between backlog visibility and cash conversion. Both companies are being re-rated the same way: electrification, grid infrastructure, data-center cooling, and emissions compliance are turning their old products into essential equipment again.

Sector

Real Estate

Real estate has cleaved into two industries: infrastructure-grade compounders riding AI, aging demographics, and supply scarcity at 20% NOI growth; and rate-sensitive yield vehicles grinding through macro headwinds they cannot control.

Sector

Retail

Retail is fracturing into two irreducible tiers: Amazon's compounding logistics and advertising flywheel pulling $200.6B in Q2 2026 revenue, versus Nike and Bath & Body Works fighting for relevance with worn-out channel strategies and eroding membership trust. The DTC era is over; wholesale rebalancing and brand transformation are the only stories.

Sector

Services

Services companies are splitting into two economics: those who own recurring-relationship infrastructure (loyalty currencies, subscription flywheels, franchise fee engines) earning 30%+ margins, and those still running legacy formats whose cost structure is harder to fix than the format is to disrupt.

Sector

Technology

AI infrastructure has split technology into two economies: companies wired into the buildout are compounding at triple-digit rates, while those outside are defending against rapid structural decline.

Sector

Utilities

Utilities are executing the largest capital buildout in decades — data-center load, climate hardening, aging-pipe replacement — but regulatory approval gates every dollar of earnings. The fault lines are between who locked in cost recovery before politics shifted and who still needs approvals they may never get.
