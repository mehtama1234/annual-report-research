Sector-wise synthesis

What's happening inside each sector

Bottom-up synthesis of each sector's own dynamics, standout companies, and fault lines — from the coded filings.

Sector

Basic Materials

Copper scarcity, gold at record prices, and AI-driven demand for physical infrastructure are splitting Basic Materials into winners and laggards along a single fault line: do you own the right ore body in the right place?

Sector

Consumer Goods

Consumer Goods in 2026 is a sector split down the middle: staples brands grinding through tariffs, affordability pressure, and private-label competition while identity-driven companies — in smoke-free nicotine, accessible beauty, off-price retail, and franchise IP — are growing with real pricing power and volume momentum.

Sector

Energy

The U.S. and Canadian energy complex is not transitioning away from hydrocarbons — it is industrializing them: building out LNG export trains, NGL fractionation networks, gas-to-power infrastructure, and midstream integration at a scale that makes the sector look less like a commodity patch and more like a permanent supply machine wired to global demand.

Sector

Financial

Finance in 2025-2026 is a platform arms race: scale operators are absorbing insurance float, private-markets AUM, and AI-powered workflows while traditional spreads compress and active managers bleed assets to index.

Sector

Healthcare

Healthcare is splitting into two economies — an infrastructure layer printing cash on scale and recurring use, and a clinical innovation layer where franchise concentration, reimbursement politics, and commercial execution risk are colliding at once.

Sector

Industrial Goods

The AI buildout has rewired Industrial Goods from a cyclical machinery play into an infrastructure execution race—every contractor, distributor, and equipment maker that touches power, cooling, fiber, or physical site development is running at a pace the sector has never seen.

Sector

Industrials

Two companies, two different models — commodity pass-through and engineered compliance infrastructure — both being re-rated because AI, electrification, and reshoring have made their old-economy products essential again.

Sector

Real Estate

Real estate has fractured into two separate asset classes: infrastructure-grade winners (towers, logistics, senior housing) compounding at double digits, and everything else managing for survival.

Sector

Retail

Retail in 2026 is a three-speed sector: Amazon's flywheel compounds at 20% growth, Nike fights for direct-channel relevance while wholesale rebounds, and Bath & Body Works runs a brand repair program with 40 million members who aren't buying enough.

Sector

Services

Services companies are bifurcating between operators who own recurring relationship infrastructure — loyalty currencies, subscription flywheels, franchise fee engines — and those still running legacy formats whose structural decline accelerating automation and digital substitution can no longer mask.

Sector

Technology

AI infrastructure demand has split technology into two economies: companies touching the AI buildout are compounding at historic rates, while those outside it are defending against obsolescence or quietly imploding.

Sector

Utilities

Utilities are in the middle of the largest synchronized capital build in decades, driven simultaneously by data-center load, grid hardening, climate liability, and aging-pipe replacement — but every dollar of that build rides on regulatory approval, and the fault lines are widening between who captures the upside and who absorbs the risk.
